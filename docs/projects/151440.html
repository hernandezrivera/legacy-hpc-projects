Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to enhance the coordination and management of IDP camps in Adado and Dhusamareeb district. This project will target 20,000 beneficiaries (10,000 Adado and 10,000 Dhusamareeb). Most of the IDP camps are manned by gate keepers. The relationship to and with the IDP community is similarly diverse but in the long absence of the Government providing IDPs with assistance and protection, informal managers have often become the primary go to person and provider of support and, to a certain extent, protection, in particular since communities of IDPs are often marginalized and discriminated against because they belong to minorities or are separated from the protection of their clans. Aid diversion has been allegedly high in informal settlements, but the extent of it remains unclear. SSWC intends to establish new community structures and strengthen the existing structures in collaboration with the local authorities in Adado and Dhusamareeb districts. SSWC will focus on creating coordination structures and mechanisms that help the beneficiaries’ access different services in their settlements. Communication channels will be enhanced to foster information sharing in the camps with other service providers in the targeted areas. The project will develop service mapping mechanisms to address the population movements, trends of new arrivals, exiting individuals and returnees in the IDP camps. This will be enhanced by engaging focal points in the camps, the focal points will conduct service mapping and record all the essential needs/gaps in the camps.

Needs

Protection issues remain a core of the humanitarian crisis diverse range of protection issues in Somalia, including violations of international human rights and humanitarian law. At least 1.1 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) and other vulnerable people, notably women, children, minorities, the disabled, child and female headed households, survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation (particularly children), or older persons without support structures, are exposed to protection risks, such as forced evictions, discrimination based on status, child rights violations and child labour, family separations and gender-based violence (GBV), such as rape and sexual assault, due to conflict and protracted displacement as well as natural hazards. According to the interagency assessment report there has been increased family separation due to migration of the pastoralist, women, children and elderly were forced to move to IDPs settlements for assistance.According to SSWC assessment more women live in the IDP camps and in their location they have few or no functioning latrines with no lighting systems and they are compelled to go for long distances to collect firewood and fetch water as well go for open defecation this therefore put them at risk of GBV related incidents.Settlements are often ungoverned or governed by gatekeepers, overcrowded and displaced people have limited access to protective shelter and other basic needs. Women in the camps are prone to exploitation from their male counterparts due to the deplorable housing conditions in their settlements. Above this there is poor coordination of the camps by the village relief committees or the camp leaders; they lack the capacity to managing the population in the camp thus making some of the residents not getting better services. According to SSWC assessment report an estimated 20,000 people were displaced affecting four major camps in Adado( Badbado,Karama,Kulmiye and Daryel)

Activities

The project will have varied activities in the camps. These activities will be site management, capacity building of the camp leaders, conducting community forums, service mapping in the targeted IDP camps and providing effective structure for practical collaboration and coordination of humanitarian interventions.SSWC wil strengthen the existing coordination mechanisms and in sites where there no existing structures SSWC will establish active structures which will be governed by Standard Operating Procedures(SOPs). The structures will be identified through camp leaders and existing projects staffs.This will help in the identifying needs and duplication of services. SSWC will ensure atleast 16 community structures exist in the vast IDP settelements in Cadado and Dhusamareeb with specific sector task assigned to each structure. 10 groups comprising of 8 members will be trained and strengthened as part of the camp structures establishment. Establish/reinforce community mechanisms for communication channels (centres of information/centres of communication/centres of coordination)SSWC will develop communication centres in all the targeted IDP camps for essential information recording and sharing. The centres will allow the existing and new IDP arrivals to access information of the various services offered at the camps.The centres will also provide a linkage between the SSWC and the IDP residents whereby SSWC will further coordinate with other services providers in the IDPs. Its expected 5 communication centres be established in different IDP settlements in Cadado. Creation of IM product providing information on population movement and numbers (new arrivals, exits or returns, population by site, identification of gaps, monitoring of services, mapping of or establishment of focal points) after Inventories existing tools and identify gaps in information SSWC will conduct to service mapping and monitoring.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is linked to durable solutions since it has the basis of closely working with the community in the IDP camps. The leaders together with the local authorities will be trained on the management and coordination of proper structures for their settlements. This will in turn help the community form committee members that will continue working in the camp even after the end of the intervention. More so the local authorities will be engaged through the community forums and they will be part of the participants during the training and will be invited during decision making processes.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risk in this intervention will be displacement of the most vulnerable persons in the IDP camps particularly women and children. SSWC will closely talk to the government authorities to lease land for longer period of more than 10 years. Eviction could also be a risk in this intervention this is can be overcome by settling the internally displaced persons in a permanent land.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The culture and traditions of the Somalia does not propagate women and youth in involving in decision making they might be a risk of exclusion, to mitigate this SSWC will set proper criteria of beneficiary selection considering all gender and age groups. The other mitigation measure will be creating awareness within the communities to include women to a pivotal role in taking part in the intervention and to develop feedback and complaints response mechanisms so as to get information from the beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project will take into educate the youth on the effects of joining terror groups and creating violence.SSWC to organize forums to sensitize on the peace building and shunning conflicts to avoid violence.
