Project Summary

This project is designed to link food assistance to early recovery livelihoods activities and compliments NPA’s emergency projects and the NPA Rural development projects in Awerial, Yirol East and West. NPA plans to provide an appropriate package of emergency and early recovery assistance by distributing livelihoods inputs to vulnerable farmers in Awerial , Yirol East and West. This project is integrated with NPA rural development project as the first phase of assistance as project participants wait for the long term sustainable assistance under the rural development programme.
South Sudan has numerous challenges as a result of the protracted crises. In response, NPA is now planning and implementing more sustainable projects, which link emergency food security interventions with early recovery and livelihood activities. In this project NPA is proposing to distribute fishing kits to 2500 households (HHs) with vegetable kits and tools , 2500 H/H with fishing kits and crop seeds to 3 000 HHS. This would follow by training 1500 farmers on crop and vegetables will also receive training on improved agronomic practices and 500 farmers will be trained on fish preservation marketing and group dynamics. The trained farmers will be expected to pass on their knowledge to other vegetable farmers and fishermen. The farmers will undergo training on vegetable production, crop production and this knowledge will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks. These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetables, crops and fish will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and widened dietary diversity options.
Beneficiaries of this project are household members who are extremely food insecure individuals (i.e. population in IPC 4 and 5) spread across 3 project locations. NPA will follow a community-based approach, whereby the community take up the role in the selection of eligible households particularly returnee and internally displaced households, while also including vulnerable members of host communities. A vulnerability selection criteria will be developed where communities also participate in discussing and endorsing the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection criteria will also be comprised of both status and proxy indicators, such as: i) Displacement status and duration, ii) Vulnerability status (female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed households, and large households), iii) Economic situation (income, debt and living conditions). Other issues are households with malnourished children under 5 years and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW).
Beneficiaries will therefore be the prime movers of the implementation process through their continuous engagement in the project. During periodic community meetings and Post Distribution Monitoring assessments NPA will ensure that the voices and concerns of all groups are captured. In addition, beneficiaries will actively participate in project implementation through mechanisms such as Food Management Committees. Targeting criteria will also be verified and refined through active participation of the beneficiaries. Further, to promote accountability, NPA will continue using the community based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide platforms for beneficiaries' active involvement in the project and avenues to voice any issues or concerns.

Needs

According to the IPC, it is projected that Yirol East and West will have an estimated caseload of 20,000 people at IPC Phase 5 in the coming months in no assistance is provided, primarily a consequence of inter-communal violence. According to the current IPC, Yirol East and West and Awerial are in IPC phase 4 .According to the September 2018 IPC analysis, about 6.1 million people (59% of the total population) in South Sudan would face crisis or acute food insecurity during the July to August 2018 peak lean season period (IPC , Sep 2018). The overall food and nutrition security situation in South Sudan is expected to slightly improve from September to December 2018, with the population in crisis phase reducing to 43%. However, the outlook for January to March 2019 indicates that the lean season will start earlier than expected, and 49% of the population is expected to face crisis or worse acute food insecurity. Regionally, acute malnutrition in most of the areas in South Sudan has also reduced. Due to the earlier than expected beginning of the lean season in 2019, the GAM rates are expected to deteriorate, especially during the first quarter. In Lakes state, about 44 % of the population, mainly from the counties of Yirol West, East , Awerial are expected to be in crisis phase (moderately and severely food insecure) in the first quarter of 2019 (IPC , Sep 2018). Insecurity still contributes to the underlying causes of low agricultural production in the Awerial, Yirol east and West counties (Reach,2018) . Yirol East has been witnessing a large influx of IDPs from Rumbek North since early May 2018 as a result of land disputes and community tensions in Oakam community. In most cases women and girls are affected the most by the violence when undertaking daily survival tasks, such as collecting firewood and water (HRP , 2018). Although civilian disarmament was conducted, there still exist on-going inter-clan conflicts, which are perpetual by revenge attacks among various communities in Lakes State (Reach: 2018) . The insecurity incidents cause displacements, destruction to properties, deaths, and disruption of livelihoods activities. These continue to hinder efforts to early recovery process and resilience building among communities. The communities are also faced with crop failures due to limited and erratic rainfalls, fall army worm infestation, limited access to food and basic services with escalating (more than tripled) food, inflation and fuel prices. Consequently, there is increased inaccessibility to some areas and farming lands/gardens, poor or low food production, limited health services, poor water sources and dysfunctional markets.The combination of above factors resulted in high percentages of food insecure populations that critically require both food assistance and early recovery form of food assistance to not only enhance their recovery from the stresses and future shocks, but also build their resilience. Previous experience of working with the communities in Awerial, Yirol East and West has shown that usually that apart from the food assistance the IDPs needs emergency livelihood assistance to help them settle and cover their immediate needs in terms of agricultural inputs. In these areas, there is a huge gap in responding to emergencies. Activities

1. Project launching (staff mobilization).NPA will conduct grants opening meeting with staff to explain the objectives of the project and discuss on the implementation plan and how the project is going to be implemented. This will be attended by emergency staff implementing the project and support staff 2. Community mobilization, beneficiary’s identification, verification and registration. NPA will conduct regular meetings with local authorities, key stakeholders and communities in these locations, providing updates on objectives, implementation modalities, targeting and selection criteria, as well as present distribution plans and a strengthened communication/feedback mechanism in the targeted location. NPA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and solicit feedback from community members. The PMCs will be trained and empowered to raise concerns of their communities to NPA and key actors in each area. Beneficiary selection will be done in consultation with the community using agreed developed selection criteria 3. Sourcing of livelihoods inputs and delivery to field locations.NPA will procure 2500 fishing kits , 2500 vegetable kits and tools and 5000 crop kits and tools and send them to Awerial, Yirol east and West 4. Formation and training of PMCs.NPA will facilitate and encourage the formation of project management committees which will help in the implementation of the project 5. Distribution of livelihoods inputs NPA will distribute 2500 vegetable kits and tools , 2500 fishing kits and 5000 crop kits and tools to vulnerable households in Awerial, Yirol east and West. NPA will also do a post distribution monitoring 6. Formation of farmers and fisher folks groups and conducting trainings. NPA will encourage the formation of farmer groups and fisher forks group so as to encourage working together and encourage peer to per exchange 7. Monitoring and reporting.NPA will ensure that the required reports are submitted in time. NPA will also conduct post distribution monitoring and distribution process monitoring 8. Crop yield (harvest) assessmentsA crop yield harvest assessment will be done to determine the crops yields after harvesting period NPA through the guidance of the gender and protection coordinator will ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed throughout the project activities

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

NPA recognizes the essential role of women in improving household food and nutrition security, which in South Sudan has become even more accentuated as the number of women-headed households increases as a result of the conflict. For this reason, this projects’ interventions will focus as much as possible on women to ensure families, and above all women and children, reap the benefits. The project shall thus put in place mechanisms to ensure that women, men, boys and girls participate and benefit from all interventions. Needs assessment, project design and implementation are done through a gendered lens as this crisis has affected women and men in different ways. For instance distribution will take into account safety of women and ensure that women are not inadvertently exposed to additional risk. Both women and men will be part committees equally as per the standard operation procedures, where they are able to discuss associated concerns. NPA recognizes the essential role of women in improving household food and nutrition security, which in South Sudan has become even more accentuated as the number of women-headed households increases as a result of the conflict. The interventions will focus as much as possible on women to ensure families, and above all women and children, reap the benefits. The planned activities seek to assist women to manage the consequences of crisis and food insecurity and malnutrition, providing opportunities for income generation, increasing access to nutritious food sources and reducing exposure to gender-based violence. Women are taking an increasing share of the agricultural responsibilities in the current situation, and will thus be the main target group for the voucher schemes and distribution of inputs, particularly, vegetable kits. The distribution of agricultural inputs will reduce women’s dependence on others for inputs and allow for more consistency in farming. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A protection focus can lead to improved food security by integrating and addressing the protection concerns of beneficiaries into project activities. Protection means carrying out activities in ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance. NPA will look at the contextual risk, programmatic risk an institutional risk. The project design takes into account do-no-harm principles. NPA has in place security management systems and works closely with community leaders and local partners to monitor security situation, detect changes in tension levels and make appropriate decisions that ensure that staff in insecure locations are evacuated before the situation becomes untenable.
