Project Summary

This project design will support 410 (60% girls and 40% boys) unaccompanied and separated children and 500 (60% boys and 40% girls) children formerly associated with the non-state armed groups with integrated individual and intersectional case management and referrals/linking of children at risk to support services in Jere, Bama, Lamurde, Guyuk, Yunusari, and Yusufari LGAs in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states respectively. These are locations where GSF has existing child protection structures and has built a relationship with the community based stakeholders.
Haven hosted the co-leadership of the child protection area of responsibility for three years, this intervention shall replicate the knowledge gained to improve the already existing LGA based coordination structures of the Protection Sector / Child Protection Area of responsibility coordination efforts to ensure functioning referral mechanisms in the project locations.
GSF shall facilitate child safeguarding and Psychosocial support including life-skill based support to 1,346 (40% boys & 60% girls) and recreation for 7,580 younger children and their families in humanitarian intervention through Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) in Jere, Bama, Lamurde, Guyuk, Yunusari, and Yusufari, targeting IDP and Host community and returnee population. GSF shall inculcate the MHPSS minimum service package to guide the intervention throughout the project duration.
GSF has been engaging community based child protection mechanisms to deliver context friendly added value and protection to children, families, and the community, this intervention will prioritize already established child protection committees in areas where GSF is operating, and establish new structures in areas where they are non-existent. These CPCs shall setup in coordination and collaboration with other LGA based child protection actors to strengthen the system through training and monthly meetings.
Through the participation of key stakeholders, GSF shall link livelihood and vocational skills for 200 (60 girls, 40 boys) adolescents at risk, out of school children and 200 (80 women, 20 men) caregivers based on the outcome of economic opportunity mapping on livelihood Programmes in the target LGAs.
The project will also support advocacy on the implementation of the child safeguarding policy across formal and informal learning centres, as well as implementation of the domestication of the child rights act in Borno states. GSF will organize a capacity building workshop to 30 child protection committees on prevention and response to family separation that requires family tracing and referrals across the two states. GSF shall print and distribute the already existing child protection AoR IEC materials in local languages to promote awareness on child protection and carry out door to door sensitization activities to 6,500 persons through the CPC and community based volunteers including on commemorative events (16 days of activism, International Women’s Day, International Day of the Boy Child, International Day of the Girl Child, World Children’s Day, and Day of the African Child).
GSF through this project, shall participate in protection monitoring activities to support intervention to support the understanding of protection risks in line with the Protection Analytical Framework (PAF) developed by the Global Protection Cluster, which focuses on the four pillars of 1) context (harmful, discriminatory, protection contextual factors), 2) current threats to the population (violations and abuses), 3) threat’s effects on the population (priority effects on population), and 4) existing capacities to address protection threats (capacities and response coverage and gaps). The GSF protection monitoring activities shall also integrate human rights analysis by linking protection risks to violated rights and duty bearers’ commitment under International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and International Humanitarian Law (IHL).

1 - Needs

According to the Child Protection HRP 2023 guidance, approximately 2.1 million people need child protection services due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, out which 1.3 million children need child protection services, The AoR aims to reach 1.5m Persons in Need (1,050,000 children, 53% girls and 450,000 caregivers, 53% women). Based on the CP AoR priorities in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe (BAY) with the highest severity ranking of child protection needs as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview. To ensure that all children boys and girls are free from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, trafficking, harmful practices and are safe /thriving to their full potential, GSF will focus on prevention and response with a systems strengthening approach in in the highest severity areas of Borno (Jere, Mafa, Ngala, Bama), Yobe (Tarmua, Yusufari, Yunusari, Geidam) and Adamawa (Lamurde, Guyuk, Yola North) based on consideration of the humanitarian and development nexus as well as the evolving situation in the Northeast.According to the Multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in 2023, Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, high poverty rates and relocations of IDPs etc. Priority needs are case management services for victims of abuse, neglect and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children, addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labor, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities. Alos, there is need to strengthen the child protection system, through setting up building blocks for the same, the initial focus will look at capacity building of social welfare workforce, community-based child protection committees, strengthening coordination at LGAs, focus on data and information systems for evidence based programming and advocacy, partners’ institutional capacity support through trainings, mentorship and coaching as part of the localization efforts within the CP AoR.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the project activities. The project team will conduct risk analysis exercises prior to activities and periodically thereafter to understand the vulnerabilities, capacities, threats, power holders, gender analysis and service availability in the areas to identify what risks might exist or arise. When the risk is identified, either pre-existing or potentially due to GSF activities, specific mitigation measures are assigned (time-bound with a responsible person identified), and the risk itself is measured by the community against the positives of the activity proposed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GSF will work to ensure accountability to the affected populations through various activities. information sharing about the project activities through meetings with community-based stakeholders, gender-balanced staff speaking local languages, and message dissemination through community leaders, will ensure that GSF interventions have clear, relevant, and timely information required to make informed decisions and choices. To ensure community members are able to deliver complaints in a safe and unbiased way, there will be a series of approaches to receive feedback on the CRM system which include complaint boxes strategically located in the community and an open hotline for Managers. GSF will proactively encourage open feedback from beneficiaries via the community-based structures. CRMs will be designed with the participation of community members to ensure that they are located and managed in such a way as to ensure the highest level of engagement by all members of the community, including those with limited literacy, mobility, and access to technology, as well as those facing heightened marginalization.Community-based facilitators will support generating the data needed to ensure responsiveness and accountability in programming through documenting protection issues/concerns including instances/trends in violations, gaps in service provision, lack of information, and other barriers to service access. This data collection will ensure that these barriers and rights violations are identified and prioritized and that information is analyzed and processed so as to allow effective response by the GSF team and other duty bearers in reaction to the feedback obtained.By maintaining an ongoing dialogue with beneficiaries, acting upon those perspectives, and explaining why certain decisions have been taken, GSF will gain their trust and thus promote greater transparency and accountability towards them.
