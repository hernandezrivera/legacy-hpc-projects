Project Summary

Our HRP proposed response is aligned to the Health Cluster’s aim to improve access to and utilization of essential health care services to reduce excess morbidity and mortality amongst 60,000 IDPs including host populations and Returnees.
LiveWell will provide health services that are adapted to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention while continuing to provide essential lifesaving health care. Community and County Health System reliance will be promoted through bringing synergies and optimizing use of limited resources in the World Bank Projects LiveWell is currently implementing in Bor South, Twic East, Duk, Pibor, Pochalla in Jonglei State and the current Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA). We will also collaborate with CUAMM in Lakes states during the implementation of SSHF emergency projects in Awerial, and Yirol East respectively.
Our proposed budget for 7 counties to implements 3 Health Cluster’s clinical package is at US$ 2,000,000 for 60,000 targeted beneficiaries. This was calculated using HC’s cost per beneficiary of $37.7. The benefit will aim at supporting the following 12 health facilities based on the following level.
• 7 Mobile Clinics (Gumuruk, Okadi, Padiet, Wangulei, Machuor, Awerial, Bor islands) in Pibor, Pochalla, Duk, Twic East, Yirol East, Awerial, and Bor South counties 40,789 IDPs (13,754 people), returnees (11,074 people) and Host communities (20,000 people)
• 2 PHCUs (Tinagau, Yangtikyom) in Yirol East county to reach 18,125 targeted vulnerable people.
• 3 PHCC (Pibor, Pariak, Kolnyang) in Pibor, Pochalla and Bor South counties to reach 30, 000 people

1 - Needs

The people of South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, COVID-19 pandemic, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. Our health system heavily relies on humanitarian support and is still unable to cope and respond to humanitarian emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services to the vulnerable people. Government contributions to health services was just 1.9% of 2021 national budget allocations with the addition of an underperforming economy also contributing to poor health outcomes. (HC’s HNO 2022). According to National Health Cluster data (2021): there are huge inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country: 8% of 1,869 health facilities provided the full package of BPHNS, 58% of the Bomas do not have Boma health teams (Community Health system). 59% of the country’s population can access health facilities in one hour’s time while 80% reported barriers in accessing health services. There is low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3 coverage), making children more vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases. UNFPA South Sudan (2021) reported South Sudan with MMR at 789 per 100,000 live births (one of the highest in the world). ANC at 39% women during pregnancy, and institutionalized delivery at 12.3% as well as skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3%. Access to BEmONC is very low, Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6% with modern methods at 5%, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP 2020). Recent mapping in 37 of the 79 counties revealed that leprosy is still endemic in all surveyed counties (indicating high burden of Neglected Tropical Diseases). Only 20 health facilities are providing treatment in 17 counties nationwide. Access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate. Research study shows 41% of probable occurrences of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) in Six states. Attack on health care workers/facilities continues to negatively impact provision of and access to health services. Recent South Sudan IDSR reports (2021/2022), malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64%) and mortality (45%). Malaria is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality, accounting for 52.6% of all morbidities and 58.2% of all mortalities in 2021. Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 1,800 cases with 11 deaths and still remained above epidemic thresholds in 2021. Vaccine Derived Poliovirus 2(cVDPV2), (59 cases reported since the outbreak began in 2020 of which 42% are female) and the threat of infectious disease outbreaks like COVID19. The humanitarian situation across Jonglei State continued to deteriorate as a result of floods, cattle raiding, child abduction and food insecurity (IPC 4 & 5). A range of compounding natural, man-made, and macroeconomic shocks have resulted in mass displacement and have limited access to crops, livestock, and markets. This is evidenced by SMART surveys conducted in April 2020 by Action Against Hunger in Duk County. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of children in Duk, GAM was estimated at 22%. (REACH_SSD_ Situation Overview _ Jonglei_ Sept 2020). With limited humanitarian access in Lakes State, information gaps exist on the severity of these needs and the locations of vulnerable populations. Yirol East is at IPC 4 whereas Awerial and Yirol West Counties are in IPC 3. High levels of malaria and low access to medication across Lakes reportedly drove health needs in September. (REACH_SSD_ Situation Overview _ Lakes_ Sept 2020). LiveWell is therefore seeking your approval to recommend our concept note “Improve equitable access, scale-up response and strengthen the capacities of health facilities to provide lifesaving essential health care services to the IDPs in Jonglei, and Lakes” for HRP 2022.

2 - Project activities

 Conduct epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including investigation and response. Conduct immunization for routine, mobile and outreach services Provide IPC WASH materials, protocol and refreshers training to prevent disease outbreak & COVID-19 Conduct mass and routine COVID-19 Vaccination campaign to protect health workers and the population at risks. Increase access to Boma Health initiative by providing ICCM and IMNCI guidelines  Provide lifesaving essential health care services such as out-patient, in-patients, mobiles services. Procure essential drugs and supplies to run the lifesaving services Train health workers on IEHK kits, rational use of antibiotics, IPC & WASH in health facilities etc Provide reproductive health services such as ANC, skilled deliveries, adolescence health, family planning, abortion care and PNC Conduct BEMONC & CEMONC services in PHCCs and county hospital Provide vitamin A supplementation and deworming tablets for children under 5 years Conduct mass measles and cholera vaccination Give PMTCT & advance HIV/AIDs management and care Distribute LLIN/ mosquitoes nets in hard to reach locations. Conduct community consultation meetings to provide feedback and also ensure compliance Treatment and management of mental health conditions  Provide psychosocial support and psychological first aids for survivors of rape Provide M & E tools for mobile and static health facilities. Clinical management of a rape and associated sexual infections Treatment of malaria, pneumonia and AWDs using IMNCI guidelines Conduct health education, promotion and preventions at community level. Conduct personal and environmental hygiene for schools and households. Implement mobile and outreach clinical activities in hard to reach locations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedbacks will be used in the design to amend the PSEA policy, guidelines and also be adapted in the new projects to further protect beneficiaries against SEAs. These feedbacks will form part of the project performance reporting to the LiveWell and will help guide the fine-tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

At the point of services, meetings, groups dialogue, project report, raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition LiveWell will develop a culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own it. Stakeholder workshops will be organized in the location identified for implementation of the response. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Youth groups, religious leaders and representatives of beneficiaries
