Project Summary

The nutrition needs of children and women of Syria continue to rise, year after year. The socioeconomic status has deteriorated further, in addition to the February earthquake that exacerbated the needs and amplified vulnerabilities. The erosion of service capacity continues, with water and sanitation systems, health systems, and social protection systems under immense strain. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024, Syria faces a concerning escalation in the prevalence of wasting, stunting, and anemia affecting children under the age of five. Elevated levels of anemia for women of reproductive age, particularly among adolescent girls (15–19 years). The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition has witnessed a substantial threefold increase over the past five years, surging from 1.7% in 2019 to 4.7% in 2023 with a total of 508,892 children urgently requiring life-saving treatment for Acute Malnutrition, out of which 109,642 children suffer from severe acute malnutrition (SAM), a 44% increase from 2023. Stunting rates have consistently risen across Syria, increasing from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023, with an estimated 650,000 children under the age of five in Syria being stunted. 6-23 months of age children’s access to nutritious food is concerning, with only 6% of them meeting their minimal adequate diet. Consequently, 1 in 2 children suffers from anemia, posing additional risk for child growth and cognitive abilities.
Iron deficiency anemia and poor maternal nutrition during pregnancy and sub-optimal breastfeeding practices contribute to an increased risk of preterm delivery or low birth weight, leading to stunted children later in life. 1 in 10 women is wasted, and one in four women aged 15–49 years suffers from anemia, with an even higher prevalence observed among teenage girls, where 1 in 2 girls is anemic.
UNICEF through its implementing partners (IPs), both through fixed and mobile clinics, will provide a minimum package of preventive and curative nutrition services for children 6-59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women based on the area’s severity, with a special focus on the first 1,000 days of life. UNICEF and its IPs will scale up the early detection of acute malnutrition and the provision of treatment for SAM. In addition, UNICEF will provide children and their mothers with preventive nutrition supplies for the prevention of all forms of malnutrition, including multiple micronutrient supplementation (MMS) and small-quantity lipid-based nutrient supplements (SQ-LNS). UNICEF will continue its support to optimize the infant and young child feeding practices. In addition, UNICEF in Syria will introduce MMS for adolescent girls 15-19 years to tackle anemia and break the vicious cycle of maternal and child anemia. Furthermore, UNICEF will improve children’s and mothers’ access to safe and nutritious food through strong integration of social protection activities into the nutrition program.
In addition to those activities, UNICEF will work with its implementing partners to set up a national nutrition information system that enables timely and quality data, increases the efficiency of activities, and prevents duplication. UNICEF will also prepare a national nutrition strategy and update the national CMAM guidance in alignment with the new global WHO guidance, and consequently, UNICEF will build the capacity of its national partners on the CMAM program as well as IYCF.
UNICEF works closely with its sister agencies for complementarity on the ground. UNICEF coordinates with WFP for provision of lipid-based nutrient supplement for children 6-23 months of age to ensure continuation of services and prevent duplication of efforts. Under the CMAM program, UNICEF works with WFP and WHO to provide treatment for all types of acute malnutrition, including MAM and SAM with complications, which are the responsibility of WFP and WHO respectively.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

MEAL Plan: UNICEF through its field offices and 3rd party facilitators will conduct regular visits to its implementing partners for monitoring of nutrition activities. All data will be collected and reported by partners to UNICEF using the 4Ws reporting system. MEAL teams will enable program beneficiaries to share feedback on quality of services, accessibility, and available services.UNICEF and the Nutrition Sector will carry out both sectoral and project-level protection risk analyses to demonstrate thoughtful and safe mitigation measures in all planned nutrition interventions. These protection risks include:Gender-Based Violence. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: (1) support equality in the participation of women, adolescent girls, and other at-risk groups as staff and leaders in nutrition activities, and (2) encourage men and boys’ participation in awareness raising services. 3) Provision of and equitable access to nutrition services for vulnerable groups. Analyzing and disaggregating all data by sex, age, and disability with a special focus on the needs of vulnerable and minority groups, throughout the program cycle with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision. The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries persists especially for IDPs, and women-headed families. Mitigation measures: 1) Enforce UNICEF PSEA policy for all implementing partners with zero tolerance, with a signed documented commitment from all implementing partners. 2) Establish community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance mechanisms and referral systems. 3) Awareness-raising on SEA among the staff of implementing partners with continuous training on PSEA & regular monitoring through field visits and reports 4) conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns. Risk of Lack of or inadequate information provision, feedback and participation of affected communities. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: Accountability of the Affected Persons (AAP) is implemented during the program cycle starting from the planning phase, where beneficiaries will be provided information about the project and where it will be implemented. Community participation during awareness creation of the project on criteria of eligibility. Also, the affected people will have various options for feedback mechanisms such as a hotline for information and suggestions boxes to collect community feedback and ways to address that information collected will be put in place. There will be different types of feedback mechanisms - complaint boxes, interviews, surveys, field monitoring, and other techniques where applicable - that allow the beneficiaries to join UNICEF in deciding to provide better responses corresponding to their needs.
