Project Summary

After nine years of conflict, WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity where large numbers of IDPs overburdened the system, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and effects of climate change and poor water resources management. WASH infrastructures and service provision thus require significant investments, repairs and operational support. Though affecting the overall population, individuals with little immunity such as children, pregnant women and elderly are particularly at risk as dysfunctional or lack of WASH facilities increase the risk of widespread waterborne diseases and directly threaten their health.
Through the proposed intervention, SIF will directly contribute to SO1 through improving access to safe water for drinking and domestic purposes through the rehabilitation of sewage networks in some of the highest severity ranking sub-districts of Duma (Rural Damascus), As-Saan and Muhardeh (Rural Hama) and Eastern Kweires and Jebel Saman (Rural Aleppo). The intervention will mainly consist in replacing sewage pipes, excavating manholes and connecting sub-sewage lines to the general sewage network. Moreover, SIF will contribute to SO4 through rehabilitating WASH facilities in 8 public schools in Shafuniyeh (1), Qahtanieh (1), Karnaz (1), Mourek (1), Eastern Kwaires (2) and Jabel Saman (2) sub-districts. The intervention will consist in rehabilitating existing WASH facilities, supplying new toilet blocks and fountains and providing toilet blocks for students with disabilities including ramps and steel handrails. These localities were selected based on their level of accessibility and the severity of needs as per 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), SIF field assessments and consultations with local authorities and municipalities of each targeted sub-district.
This intervention is based on SIF’s experience over the past 8 years implementing WASH projects in Syria. In 2019, SIF reached a total number of 56,401 beneficiaries through various WASH interventions such as rehabilitation of water and sewage networks, distribution of water tanks and rehabilitation of boreholes across Rural Damascus, Hama, Dar’a and Aleppo governorates.
Regarding sewage network rehabilitation, the intervention will directly benefit:
- 1,500 individuals in Rural Damascus (50% men, 50% women), including 550 children, 600 adults and 350 elderlies;
- 12,750 in Rural Hama (50% men, 50% women), including 3,825 children, 5,100 adults and 3,825 elderlies;
- 8,500 in Rural Aleppo (50% men, 50% women), including 2,550 children, 3,400 adults and 2,550 elderlies.
The above-mentioned figures are based on preliminary estimations and could change during the implementation. Besides, upgraded WASH facilities in public schools, including improved access to water supply and sanitation facilities, will benefit to an estimated number of 3,200 children (50% men, 50% women), with approximatively 400 children per public school. Regarding GBV/PSEA, SIF will pay specific attention to children by providing them with quality gender and disabled friendly WASH facilities taking into account menstrual needs and including locking doors and lighting in public schools. SIF will also ensure to provide WASH facilities accessible for children with disabilities. This integrated project will be implemented in close collaboration with Education, Shelter and Protection sectors, and will aim at rehabilitating private houses and public schools, and providing non-formal education, psychosocial support (PSS) and child protection awareness sessions to children and adolescents inside public schools. This WASH project will thus participate in fostering returnees’ movements towards their home town through providing quality sewage services and improving children access to education through providing quality WASH facilities inside public schools.

4 - Needs

All the targeted locations have been selected following SIF needs assessment conducted in January 2020 and the intervention designed accordingly. As part of this assessment, SIF coordinated with relevant local authorities, representatives from the local communities and SARC. In Duma, As-Saan, Muhardeh, Eastern Kweires and Jebel Saman, WASH infrastructures – such as water and sewage pipes – were severely damaged leading to raw sewage to mix and pour either into the water sources or flood in the streets, thus contaminating soil and the overall water resource management. These deteriorated conditions pose significant health risks by fostering the development of harming waterborne diseases, and forced people to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as households spending significantly more of their income on purchasing water. This is also leading to environmental pollution caused by the degradation of those systems. Moreover, hostilities caused important damages to public schools in Shafuniyeh, Qahtanieh, Karnaz, Mourek, Eastern Kwaires and Jabel Saman thus affecting child access to adequate WASH facilities, especially for children with disabilities. Among the population of concern, children under 2 years, young girls and women age and people with disabilities are the most affected. Lack of door locks and adequate lighting, lack of gender segregated facilities, lack of privacy and lack of access to sanitary products are considered as the main concerns for girls and children with disabilities in public schools where WASH facilities are not functional. The bad smell in streets is another problem caused by the condition of damaged sewage networks.

5 - Implementation of activities

SIF will coordinate with the relevant local authorities such as the general water foundation, municipalities, SARC and WASH sector to identify the localities of intervention and avoid any overlapping or duplication with other NGOs. SIF will also select beneficiaries, obtain the necessary approvals and issue official acceptance certificates for handover. SIF will rely on a contractor to rehabilitate sewage networks and WASH facilities in public schools. Contractor selection will be based on SIF Standards of Procedures (SoPs) in order to ensure transparency and accountability of the selection process. Composed of skilled and experienced engineers, SIF WASH technical team will supervise contractor teams in order to ensure the quality delivery of rehabilitated sewage systems. Contractors will also be asked to provide a period of guarantee in order to ensure the sustainability of the works. The activities will be monitored on a daily basis by SIF WASH technical team through field visits, data collection and reporting to SIF coordination, HQ and donors to report progress, highlight areas of concerns and ensure good completion of the works. Through monthly project and budget follow-up, SIF will ensure that standards of project implementation are followed and fund resources are being effectively used. Participatory approach, through regular structured dialogues with groups of different ages, gender and backgrounds, particularly those with specific needs on WASH issues (e.g. persons with disabilities and elderly), will ensure that any protection concerns highlighted are discussed and resolved. MEAL team will also set up a mechanism for complaints and appeals, ensuring that men and women are both comfortable with it.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. The existence of unexploded ordnances in the locations where the project will be implemented. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: conduct a thorough assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the sites that are aimed to be rehabilitated; coordinate the interventions with local authorities that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood; ensure that unexploded ordnances’ clearance is conducted if needs be; check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encountered. 2. Workers injured while performing activities. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: ensure that the contractor respects safety measures before signing the contract, regular field visits by SIF WASH team to ensure that security rules are respected. 3. Girls and female adolescents are at risk of GBV in the newly rehabilitated WASH facilities in public schools. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: WASH facilities will be rehabilitated according to Protection principles through including required locking doors, lighting and segregating WASH facilities by gender.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Not getting approvals from national security to enter the area. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF will target easy-to-reach areas and establish a constant dialogue and follow-up with the authorities. 2. Risk of increased prices due to increase of the inflation rate. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: SIF has already included the inflation rate in the budget; in case of continuous inflation SIF will adjust the BoQs accordingly. 3. The security situation deteriorates and prevents the good running of the activities. Likelihood: low. Impact: Mitigation: regular monitoring of the security situation by SIF Logistics and Security Manager; contingency plan in place at field and coordination level; preparedness of SIF team. 4. Works achieved by the contractor are not of good quality and are not sustainable. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: selection of contractors based on SIF previous experience; capitalization of NGOs over their past experience with contractors in the areas; coordination with local authorities (i.e. MoLAE, Governorate and municipalities); provision of a guarantee period; inspection of the works by provisional and final acceptance committees (composed of certified engineers) to validate the good quality of the achieved works. 5. The activities have a negative environmental impact. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: all executed activities will comply with the global safety and protection measures and SIF will make sure that environment-friendly materials and products are included in the BoQs.
