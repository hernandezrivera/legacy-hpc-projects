Project Summary

As South Sudan enters its 7th year of conflict and protracted crises, 4.5 million people are still in need of humanitarian protection assistance. Bor South, Pibor and Duk counties in Jonglei and Awerial county in Lakes are among the top 10 severity counties by protection index. (Protection Cluster HRP 2020 Strategy). Poor protection parameters exposing people to multiple vulnerabilities and threats include rampant intercommunal violence, flooding, roads inaccessibility and pockets of crimes and access constraints to humanitarian actors which together have led to an increase in GBV against women and girls, separation of families, lack of information about availability of essential services and protection information, breakdown of protection support structures and mechanisms, and destruction of livelihoods. The most affected people are children, women, elderly, persons with disabilities, and pregnant and lactating women. This project will target 56,000 persons of concern (26,000 women, 20,000 men, 6000 girls, 4000 boys: with 5% PWDs of total beneficiaries) with integrated protection services including Mobile and static protection assessment and monitoring; Protection by presence and protective accompaniment; Comprehensive case management inform of individual protection assistance with cash-based component; and Safe referrals to services including information dissemination on available services; Psychosocial support programming; Legal assistance and counselling for survivors integrated into case management. To maximize protection outcomes, CINA will undertake Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging; Community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation; Housing, land and property programming and promote Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence; with possible CBI for durable solutions programming. CINA will use static programming in the state/county headquarters while deploying mobile and outreach teams to deep field locations. General protection will be integrated by enhancing inclusive access to critical cross-sectoral services, community-based protection and resilience initiatives, timely protection monitoring and assessment to inform Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approaches to other sectoral programming and response and by ensuring measures in place for protection mainstreaming and centrality of protection, accountability to affected populations and prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation to beneficiaries. To avoid duplication of activities with other protection actors in the proposed areas, especially in Pibor, CINA in collaboration with other partners on ground will prioritise locations that have limited access to protection services including Lekuangole, Gumuruk and other areas at the periphery of Pibor, to be accessed through both static and field-base roving response. The project will be coordinated with Protection Cluster, OCHA and protection partners on ground to build synergies for protection response and coordination. CINA will require $ 300,000 to meet the protection needs of the affected persons of concern in the priority counties by 31 December 2020.

1 - Needs

Protection assessments to hard-to-reach locations by REACH in September 2019 revealed that Duk is among top 5 counties where insecurity impedes people’s access to health and education services. Bor South, Pibor, Duk, and Awerial are among counties facing acute food insecurity (IPC 4) moving into 2020 (IPC report September 2019). South Sudan situation report by OCHA on 11 October 2019 indicated that a severe flooding hit Pibor, Duk, Twic East and Bor South among other locations in South Sudan, affecting drinking water sources, impeding access to schools and health facilities, destroying shelters and limiting humanitarian ability to move across affected areas. As 2019 ends, the political processes leading to formation of unity government are unpredictable although return of IDPs and refugees is expected to increase in areas already affected by protracted crises and protection needs will be heightened. When conflicts and crises ensue, the most vulnerable people including children, the persons with disabilities, elderly, pregnant and lactating mothers, etc are usually left behind during flights where they suffer greater risks and abuses. The persons with specific needs usually lack access to information, livelihood opportunities, food, medical care, shelter and NFIs and psychosocial support. Tensions over HLP issues and other community resources compound existing forms of intercommunal violence. GBV against women and girls become rampant. The child survivors of violence (including unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children) usually lack appropriate responsive services to survive and preventative and resilience services to cope. This protection environment coupled with decline in community protection support systems and structures and existing protection caseload from ongoing protection response calls for immediate and unhindered protection intervention to provide critical protection services to affected persons and at-risk populations.

2 - Activities

The proposed interventions in this project will include: 1) Life-saving protection services through: Mobile and static protection assessment and monitoring; Protection by presence and protective accompaniment; Comprehensive case management inform of individual protection assistance with cash-based component; and Safe referrals to services including information dissemination on available services; Psychosocial support programming; Legal assistance and counselling for survivors integrated into case management. The IPA will follow existing CINA’s approaches including using a vulnerability criteria (or as will be agreed with PC), IPA cases (including of: SGBV, murder, disappearance/family separation, forced recruitment, underage recruitment, unlawful detention, land disputes and documentation issues, and cases of extreme vulnerability related to illness, poverty, number of dependents, etc) will be prioritized for IPA case management services. IPA case package will include NFIs and cash assistance (for some selected cases); Safe referral pathways will be developed during protection monitoring and assessments and will be maintained. Beneficiaries will be provided with protection information and referred appropriately for services especially targeting survivors of violence including CMR services forSGBV survivors, legal aid for unlawful detention cases, referral for food assistance for those in extreme need, NFIs for the elderly and persons with disabilities. 2) To maximize protection outcomes, CINA will undertake Protection awareness raising and prevention messaging; Community-based protection preparedness and risk mitigation; Housing, land and property programming and3) Promote Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence; with possible CBI for durable solutions programming, especially targeting youth and returnees.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The rights of beneficiaries to Complaints and getting feedback is enshrined in CINA's Code of Conduct (CoC). All community-based awareness raising activities will be integrated and rights of specific groups or individuals will be highlighted and recognized including rights of women, children, persons with disabilities, elderly and other people with special needs. CINA project team will recognize the basic rights of host community, IDPs and Returnees and their freedoms of expression to participate fully in the project activities. CINA will encourage the engagement of all beneficiaries and disseminate information on available services without discrimination on basis of their gender, cultural, social, economic, religion among others. The rights to decision making and active participation will be ensured through all phases of project management cycle.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The internal displaced people or communities will be targeted as part of the beneficiaries during the project implementation. Mixed community caseload will be targeted to benefit from the Community-Based protection Mechanism and risk prevention messaging and Community awareness activities. The displaced affected-communities will be given equal opportunity to access the services rendered based on their vulnerability.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to Government policies related to Returnees, IDPs from Awerial to Jonglei (Bor South, Pibor and Duk) and host community as well as Returnees from Kenya and Uganda. With expected formation of unity government and repatriation of cattle camps from Equatorial region and ongoing peace initiatives between Boma state and Jonglei State Governments, there will be an influx of people returning to the places of origin. The project will engage community systems such as Community based protection networks, youth clubs and women groups.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to National Refugee and Returnees Framework and Country peace building processes for instance, South Sudan National Dialogue. These measures are expected to increase the population returning to their places of origin and scale up the scope of humanitarian response in 2020 given the peace environment and stability that will likely be a prospect for interventions in terms of protection programming, safety and security, settlement of land disputes among other key issues.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable for Returnees, IDPs and host community although there is vulnerability caused by insecurity, flooding and intermittent cattle rustling in Bor South, Pibor, Duk (Jonglei) and Awerial (Lakes). CINA project teams will work hand in hand with the local authorities on security updates and safety issues in the respective locations. CINA will also work together with partners who are implementing peace building and conflict resolution activities to curb the inter-communal conflicts.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

This project will be implemented using community structures such as Community-Based protection Networks which involved youth leaders, women leaders, religious groups leaders, chiefs, local government administrators and community based organizations (CBOs) who will be supported to develop community action plans. These community structures will also mentor and sustain their initiatives after the end of the project. They will also be in a better position to take over from CINA after project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Women and girls will be consulted and included as IPA beneficiaries to benefit from CBI component, especially the GBV survivors, the pregnant and lactating women and child-mothers. They will be consulted to make decision about type of IPA services relevant to them and how they will use cash to prevent further occurrence of risks. Youth also will be consulted and involved as stakeholders for community-based protection initiatives. They will be guided to design local protection initiatives and implement them. CINA will use its existing community-based complaint mechanisms using the complaint box, banners, and hotline. The CBM focal point (project manager) collects feedback on monthly basis and synthesizes it and get back to respective beneficiaries through leaders of CB protection networks. Safe referral pathways will be developed by CINA and maintained in the project locations and beneficiaries will be provided with information dissemination about availability of services. Information will be provided by community mobilisers in local languages, with simple and precise messages. Banners will also be displayed at community points (distribution sites, schools, churches, child friendly spaces) about availability of services and who to contact in case of emergency.
