Project Summary

This intervention aims at contributing to the improved health and hygiene status of women, girls, men, boys, the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs) through timely and equitable access to safe, potable and adequate water in targeted communities in Maiwut and Ulang counties.
The proposed WASH interventions will be implemented in close coordination with SSAID’s health, nutrition, protection and FSL programming. Women and girls are the primary users of WASH services, the intervention is designed to be gender-sensitive and prevent GBV. Activities will include provision of safe water supply; upgrading of existing boreholes to solar powered water supply system, pipe line extension from the existing solar powered water supply system and distribution of water purification chemical (PUR), (for those beneficiaries who are depending on river or surface water) , the rehabilitation of water points (boreholes) with secure location and access within the community and nutrition facilities with a focus on construction of flood resilient boreholes for which the apron and flood protective wall will be raised by at least 1m. The designs of the structures will be considered so that people with disabilities (PWDs-both male and female) as well as older people can easily access and collect water. Further, household latrine construction, WASH NFI distribution, and the training of Water Users Committees (WUCs) and pump mechanics will be carried out. SSAID will encourage selection and participation of female in the WUCs. Each of the WUCs will consist of at least 50% female membership. These WUCs will be responsible for ensuring that sustainable and viable operation and maintenance of these systems are in place at the community level. SSAID will ensure the quality of water through water quality testing which will be done before and after rehabilitation/construct on of water points.
For the sanitation component, the project plans the implementation of flood resilient communal and institutional ventilated improved pit latrines which will be equipped with hand washing facilities. The design of the structures will be done considering all the segments of the society especially PWDs and older people and ensure that they can access them easily without any barrier. The latrines will be gender inclusive and will have a lock on the doors for protection purposes. The location will also not be far from the beneficiaries’ home and will therefore be easily accessible by women and girls during day and evening time. The construction of latrines will be complemented by the delivery of hygiene promotion messaging on safe excreta disposal to improve the sanitation practices. SSAID hygiene promoters and trained home health promoters (HHPs) will conduct weekly monitoring visits to at community, nutrition and household level to assess the usage and cleanliness of the latrines and document any challenges or feedback from the household. The HHPs will disseminate information on the five essential hygiene and sanitation practices (fecal–oral route transmission or the ‘F-diagram’), Safe water handling, Safe excreta disposal, Handwashing and personal hygiene, Environmental hygiene and baby WASH messages. SSAID will continue using its internal hygiene promotion training module as well as the Care Group Manual to train HHPs for the Healthy Practices, Safe Communities Project.
To improve, the WASH condition of the caretakers of SAM/MAM which contributes reduction of malnutrition, WASH NFI kits will be distributed. The integrated approach will address the high levels of malnutrition by targeting the families of SAM and MAM children for WASH interventions and providing access to drinking water, thereby acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver of poor health and malnutrition. The WASH intervention in these three counties will be done carefully without creating any conflict among the beneficiaries.
Data will be disaggregated by sex and age wherever possible.

1. Needs

An estimated 6 million people’s living standards and wellbeing will be impacted in 2024 by lack of access to adequate safe water and sanitation. WASH conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states including Upper Nile. Despite replenishment of major waterways during the rainy season, only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide. This dropped to 34% in rural areas. In Upper Nile, 33% of households travel for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water. Persons with disabilities (PWDs) are most vulnerable to this situation. Access to safe water as well as extremely low coverage of water infrastructure makes households rely on surface or unimproved water. In Upper Nile, 71% of households reported using rivers or puddles as their main water source (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023). The WASH needs in the targeted areas of Maiwut, Nassir and Ulang therefore remains high. According to the WASH cluster 2023 severity / Vulnerability mapping these three counties have been classified under WSC-4 with Maiwut county falling under priority one and Ulang counties falling under priority two. The existing inter-communal conflict, regular flooding and mobility of the community have exacerbated the WASH needs of the communities. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2024, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition. The lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera and AWD. According to the national WASH cluster strategy, the main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure. Maiwut and Ulang counties share those underlying, common drivers. The low capacity of the community and poor management of the system contributes to the continuous damage of the WASH structures. The absence of government WASH department in these counties exacerbates the situation as this department is supposed to give technical support to the community WASH services. According to the Inter-agency Rapid Needs Assessments conducted in May, August and September 2023 in Maiwut and Ulang counties, by agencies operating in these counties, the WASH needs are as follows: • Water points were flooded and contaminated; people are forced to drink and use contaminated river, swamp and stagnant water. • Limited water treatment practices including use of PUR. • The primary water collectors are women and girls have to walk long distance to fetch water and are exposed to protection risks (GBV, harassment). • Women and girls collecting water have to wait in long queues for long periods • Open defecation is prevalent where latrines are not functional, especially in rural areas, except in households where latrine construction was supported by INGOs. • Unsafe hygiene practices prevail as does lack of handwashing facilities in homes and institutions. Children are particularly prone to water borne diseases since they play in open areas and bathe in stagnant water. • Sanitation facilities in health and schools are not functional as they lack regular maintenance, resulting in deteriorating sanitation and environmental health amongst patients, health workers and pupils. • Poor hygiene practices aggravate health issues and heighten the risk of disease outbreak (including cholera and typhoid). Frequent and recent flooding, displacement has resulted in lower food productivity affecting the nutritional status of communities, lack of access due to poor road conditions and conflict which prevents rehabilitation of existing water supply structures. • Lack of awareness, prevention and control measures for prevailing Covid-19.

2. Project activities

SSAID has engaged the communities during needs assessment and will continue this engagement throughout the implementation to encourage community participation and sustainability of the program. SSAID will conduct assessments and baselines to assess current WASH needs and key trends in target communities and technical and sanitary surveys of targeted water points / sources for upgrading or rehabilitations. SSAID will also carry out distribution of famine prevention minimum package (WASH –NFIs kits) comprising buckets, PUR Sachets / Aqua tabs, soap and filter cloth to ensure safer water consumption and handling. Distribution of WASH NFI kits for caretakers of SAM/MAM children who are receiving treatment at the nutrition facilities. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated according to gender and age. Safety audits at distribution sites will be done in collaboration with the SSAID protection team. SSAID will upgrade existing boreholes to flood resilient solar powered water supply systems, pipe line extension and construction of water collection points with six faucets for the community and from the existing nearby solar powered water supply system to the nutrition facilities, identifying locations through consultation and risk analysis with women, girls, boys and men – 6 BHs, conduct safety audits and focus group discussions. Rehabilitation and repair of flood resilient boreholes at community and nutrition facility level. WUC and local pump mechanics training, regular monitoring and supervision. SSAID will encourage at least 50% female WUCs membership. These WUCs will be responsible for ensuring that sustainable and viable operation and maintenance of these systems are in place at the community level. Water quality testing will be done before and after rehabilitation of water points to ensure safe water provision, prevent WASH related diseases. SSAID will undertake distribution of water treatment chemical/PUR for SAM/MAM care takers. For the sanitation component, SSAID will construct flood resilient ventilated improved pit latrines at community nutrition and health facilities, each of 5 stances with a 3m depth pit lined with brick wall and super structure iron sheet equipped with permanent hand washing facilities and soap. The design of the structures will be done considering all the segments of the society especially PWDs and older people and ensure that they can access them easily without any barrier. SSAID will make special emphasis on WASH interventions for PWDs. At least 10% latrines constructed/ rehabilitated will ensure enough space for PWD to enter and use comfortably (providing enough space to enter in to the latrines with wheelchair). All elevated water and handwashing stations will have a ramp for easy access. SSAID will conduct house to house verification for HH latrine construction; PWD identified will be provided with seating arrangement or ramp arrangement for their latrines depend on the disabilities. The latrines will be gender inclusive and will have a lock on the doors for protection purposes. The location will also not be far from the beneficiaries’ home and will therefore be easily accessible by women and girls during day and evening time. Latrine construction will be complemented by the delivery of hygiene promotion messaging on safe excreta disposal to improve the sanitation practices. Trained HHPs will conduct weekly monitoring visits at community, nutrition and household level to assess the usage and cleanliness of the latrines and document any challenges or feedback from the household. The HHPs will disseminate information on the five essential hygiene and sanitation practices (fecal–oral route transmission or the ‘F-diagram’), Safe water handling, Safe excreta disposal, Handwashing and personal hygiene, Environmental hygiene and baby WASH messages.
