Project Summary

"Provision and promotion of comprehensive primary healthcare services for FDMNs and affected host community through equitable access to and effective & efficient utilization of primary and secondary healthcare services, with special focuses on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support; and non-communicable diseases.
At the same time, project will response significantly to any potential outbreak of diseases and emergency response. One of the important objective of the program is to maintain liaison with GoB and other stakes."

Objectives

"1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support; and non-communicable diseases. 2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to communicable disease risks including diseases with outbreak potential, and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone 3. Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behavior and utilization of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender and age considerations and vulnerable groups .4. Strengthen health sector coordination, information management and monitoring towards achieving rational, standardized and accountable health service delivery."

Needs

"The FDMN community as a whole is a vulnerable group, however, women, children, elderly, and people with disabilities, are more vulnerable and at risk of harm. The majority of the FDMN population are women and children (52% are women and girls; 55% are children under 18). Women and Children are seen to be specially at risk of sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) within the camp setting. They have limited access of health care services as their movement is restricted by male, in addition to that, superstition is very much prominent in this community. As a result, women are getting deprived from their essential health care services, specially, during pregnancy period and delivery. Currently, only 72% pregnant mother receiving at least one ANC service and only 43% of deliveries are being conducted in health facilities. Still a large percentage (28%) of mother are not taking any ANC services and huge number of deliveries are taking place in un-hygienic condition, that should be addressed by proper motivation and ensuring quality services at service centers. Coverage of Pentavelent 3 is only 67% among in under 1 year children of FDMN community. And the target is > 95%, that should be prioritize with immunization program.According to EWARS, in FDMN community major infectious diseases with highest proportional morbidity reported are; acute respiratory infection (18.6%), acute watery diarrhea (4.6%), suspected varicella (4%), unexplained fever (3.9%) and other diarrhea (2.6%). These situation further worsen due to any potential disaster and outback of disease. A qualitative assessment focused on mental health and psychosocial wellbeing was conducted by IOM in 2018 with 229 purposely selected adults and children in three refugee sites. Many adult respondents reported feeling always sad (74%), always tense (64%) and always nervous (48%). Similar concerns were found among children: always sad (50%); always tense (50%); always nervous (58%). This finding indicates that, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) is a burning issue that should be addressed properly.Regarding Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs) burden, hypertension is the largest contributor to the NCD disease burden in this population, followed by COPD and DM (although the difference between these two is not large). Gaps in NCD service provision include availability of insulin, laboratory capacity to diagnose NCDs, knowledge gaps among health care providers, and systematic registering and recording of NCD cases at the health facility level."

Activities or outputs

"1. Provision of essential primary health care services: Following facilities will provid health services:● 2 Comprehensive Primary Health Care Centers providing 24/7 health services ● 1 new Comprehensive Primary Health Care Centers will be constructed and go into operation by 2020 to provide 24/7 health services ● 9 Health Posts providing services during day time (9 am - 4 pm)The facilities will provide health care services according to the essential primary health care services package (ESP) with special focus on sexual & reproductive health, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health, EPI, nutrition counselling to pregnant women and mother of under 5 children, family planning, referral services for complicated cases, information management, waste management and infection control, tuberculosis and malaria control screening and treatment, Screening and treatment of non-communicable diseases (NCD) like; hypertension and Diabetic mellitus. In addition to these, Primary health care centers will provide normal delivery facility, observation of diarrhoeal patients and minor injuries and comprehensive mental health and psychosocial services. In the health facilities, there will be separate space for women, children and person with disabilities. Project will collect reports according to age and sex aggregation of service recipients. 2. Health facility infrastructures upgradation: To ensure durability, better infection prevention and control (IPC), and quality service delivery in the camp setting during extreme situations such as monsoon, cyclone, and other potential disasters BRAC started transforming all primary health care centers and health posts from bamboo made temporary structures to more durable structures approved by the government of Bangladesh. 3. Community health outreach program: BRAC has been operating integrated community health components which aims to increase awareness and health service seeking behavior from community and facility level health centers among FDMN. 200 trained Female Community Health Workers (CHWs) have been conducting regular home visits to 28,000 households in their catchment areas, providing basic health services such as identification of eligible couple; pregnant women; malnourished children; adolescents & women of reproductive age; suspected TB cases; disable people; including health education and referral to nearest health facilities. 4. Capacity Building: Ensure the continuous capacity building of the health team on different subjects with special focus on SRH, adolescent health and newborn and child care, NCDs, MHPSS including but not limited to workshops, training, capacity building activities, OJT, mentorship programs and providing technical assistance to ensure high quality of care in the facilities and community.5. DRR (Including Monsoon Preparedness): To mitigate the risk of outbreaks and the potential disruption of health services during the potential disasters BRAC has been upgrading the health facilities to be more disaster ready; formed mobile medical teams and trained them on first aid, mass casualties, and psychological first aid; also BRAC will ensure the appropriate requisition of drugs and medications, and will also create oral rehydration therapy corners in every health facility in case of a potential AWD outbreak.6. Linkage and referrals: Established and maintain intra (BRAC-Nutrition, Protection, WASH, Education, Food) and inter-sectoral linkage (Health sector, CiC, etc.).7. Information management, Monitoring & Evaluation: To ensure an effective information management, reporting and monitoring mechanism we have digitized the MIS integrating DHIS2, EWARS, HIS, HERAMS into the system. Frequent field monitoring and evaluation visits complement the system through data triangulation. Exit interview will be conducted with different age and sex group to ensure the quality of health care service."
