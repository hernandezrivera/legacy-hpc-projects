Project Summary

Project Summary:
Child Learning Center for Rohingya Children
Objective: The objective of the project is to provide early childhood development and learning opportunities for 47774 children including 2213 girls and 2561 boys, which is 46% girls and 54% boys, (age group 5-17 years) from the Rohingya and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, where they live in refugee camps.
Activities: The project will maintain and upgrade 49 existing and 6 more new centers for Rohingya community and 5 Centers for Host community , which will offer the following activities:
• Quality education: The project will follow the Myanmar curriculum for Rohingya and national curriculum for host community and provide age-appropriate and context-relevant learning materials and methods for the children. The project will also provide basic literacy and numeracy skills for the out-of-school children and adolescents.
• Indoor games and recreation: The project will provide various indoor games and recreational activities for the children, such as puzzles, board games, arts and crafts, storytelling, and music. These activities will help the children to develop their cognitive, social, and emotional skills, as well as to have fun and enjoy their childhood.
• Awareness sessions: The project will conduct awareness sessions for the children and their caregivers on various topics, such as child rights, child protection, gender equality, health and hygiene, disaster risk reduction, and peace and tolerance. These sessions will help the children and their caregivers to gain knowledge and awareness on these issues and to influence their community positively.
• Participatory events: The project will organize participatory events for the children and their caregivers, such as cultural festivals, sports competitions, community dialogues, and advocacy campaigns. These events will help the children and their caregivers to express their views and opinions, to celebrate their diversity and culture, and to engage with their community and stakeholders.
• Psychosocial counselling: The project will provide psychosocial counselling for the children and their caregivers who have experienced trauma, violence, or stress. The project will also train the staff and volunteers on how to identify and refer the cases that need specialized mental health support.
Outputs: The project will produce the following outputs:
For Rohingya Children
• 65 learning centers maintained and upgraded with adequate facilities and equipment
• 3824 children including 1738 girls and 2086 boys, which is 45% girls and 55% boys, (age group 5-17 years) enrolled and attended the learning centers regularly
• The children are received quality education, indoor games, recreation, awareness sessions, participatory events,
• Safe and inclusive spaces ensured for the children and their caregivers in case of disaster
• 90 teachers and facilitators appointed including 65 male and 25 female, which is 72% male and 28% female to teach and supervise the children
For Host Community Children
• Along with 3824 Rohingya children, 950 host community school going children ( 50% girls and 50% boys) will be being benefited with 5 LC and 20 teachers ( 10 female and 10 male).

Outcomes: The project will achieve the following outcomes:
• Improved learning outcomes and life skills for the children and adolescents
• Enhanced psychosocial well-being and resilience for the children and their caregivers
• Increased protection and empowerment for the girls and boys
• Strengthened social cohesion and harmony between the Rohingya and host communities
• Reduced vulnerability and increased preparedness for the natural disasters
The project is a project of Education Development And Services (EDAS), a local NGO that have experience and expertise in education and humanitarian sectors. The project will also coordinate and collaborate with the education sector and other relevant sectors.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Identified Needs of the Project:Child Learning Center for Rohingya ChildrenThe project is based on the recognition that education is a basic and universal human right, which should be enjoyed by all children, regardless of their circumstances. Education is also a key factor for the development, growth, and prospects of the children, especially those who are affected by natural or man-made disasters.The project addresses the specific needs and challenges of the Rohingya children and the host community children in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, where they live in refugee camps. The Rohingya are a persecuted ethnic minority who have fled from Myanmar to Bangladesh, where they face multiple hardships and risks. The host community children also suffer from poverty and lack of opportunities.Some of the identified needs and challenges of the children in the camps are:• Lack of access to quality education: The children face barriers to access education, such as distance from learning facilities, protection issues, restrictions due to household responsibilities, and lack of sufficient education kits and WASH facilities. The children also lack a structured curriculum, skilled and well-trained teachers, and a social and emotional learning atmosphere. As a result, the children have low learning outcomes and life skills, and are at risk of dropping out of school.• Lack of psychosocial support: The children have experienced trauma, violence, and stress due to the displacement, persecution, and living conditions in the camps. They also face risks related to SGBV, prostitution, trafficking, early or forced marriage, sexual exploitation, child labor, abuse, and extremism. The children need psychosocial counselling and support to cope with their situation and to enhance their resilience and well-being.• Lack of protection and empowerment: The children, especially the girls, face discrimination and exclusion based on their gender, age, and ethnicity. They also face safety issues inside and outside the camps, and lack of basic personal hygiene and dignity. The children need protection and empowerment to exercise their rights, to participate in decision-making, and to influence their community positively.• Lack of social cohesion and harmony: The children, especially the adolescents, face social and cultural norms that limit their interaction and integration with the host community. They also face hostility and resentment from some segments of the host community, who perceive them as a burden and a threat. The children need social cohesion and harmony to foster mutual understanding, respect, and tolerance between the Rohingya and the host communities.• Lack of preparedness for natural disasters: The children, along with their caregivers, face the risk of natural disasters, such as cyclones and floods, that can worsen their already vulnerable situation. The camps are in areas prone to landslides and waterlogging, and the learning facilities are made of temporary materials that are not durable or resilient. The children need preparedness for natural disasters to reduce their vulnerability and to increase their access to safe and inclusive spaces in case of emergency.The project aims to address these needs and challenges by providing early childhood development and learning opportunities for 4774 children (5-17) from the Rohingya and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The project will also ensure safe and inclusive spaces for the children and their caregivers in case of disaster.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Our project is aligned to the Rationalization Principles in the following ways:1.Adopt an integrated approach: Our project adopts an integrated approach by addressing the multiple and interrelated needs and challenges of the children in the camps. The project aims to provide education, psychosocial support, protection, empowerment, social cohesion, and disaster preparedness to the children. The project targets both the Rohingya and the host community children, promoting a holistic and inclusive response that benefits both groups. Our project also coordinates with other sectors and working groups, such as the Health Sector and the GBV Sub-Sector, to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication of services.2. Ensure predictability and continuity: Our project ensures predictability and continuity by providing early childhood development and learning opportunities for the children throughout the year. The project follows a structured curriculum and a standardized monitoring and evaluation system. Our project also ensures that the learning facilities are safe and accessible for the children and their caregivers. The project has contingency plans and emergency kits in case of natural disasters. Our project also builds the capacity of the teachers and the community members, enhancing their skills and knowledge to sustain the project activities and outcomes.
