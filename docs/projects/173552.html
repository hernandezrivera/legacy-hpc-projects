Project Summary

The project aims to provide timely equitable access to emergency WASH services for 40,000 IDPs/host communities affected by conflict in Borno and Yobe States of Nigeria, including the areas of return. The intervention will focus on the repair and construction of water points, training hand pumps mechanics, provision of hygiene and dignity kits, water treatment materials as well as hygiene and COVID19 awareness-raising campaigns for communities to reduce both the spread of water-borne diseases and of COVID19. The affected population will also be supported in the construction of emergency latrines specifically targeting the most vulnerable and newly displaced people. The response is aligned with the 2020 HRP Strategic Objectives and WASH Sector Objectives as well as with WASH Sector response to COVID-19 guidance and priorities.
The protracted humanitarian crisis in the North-East BAY States (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) has left 7.9 million people (23% women, 57% children) in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020 (HRP Nigeria 2020). According to the HRP 2020, an estimated 2.2 million people are in need of early recovery assistance (31% women, 28% men, 41% children, 15% people with disability).
The 33rd round of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM – August 2020), estimated the number of IDPs and returnees to be 2,118,550 (78% women and children) and 1,714,682 (82% women and children) respectively. Out of the total number of displaced people, 1,566,011 IDPs are currently in Borno, 213,467 in Adamawa and 142,425 in Yobe, while 708,527 returnees are in Borno, 819,148 in Adamawa and 187,007 in Yobe.
COOPI’s CBPP and majority of KIIs indicated that access to water is a key constraint to productive livelihoods, health, and is a potential driver of conflict. Water quality is a concern: a cholera outbreak, declared by the Yobe State Ministry of Health, has resulted in large number of cases since 2018. Access to adequate supplies of safe water will reduce morbidity and mortality from water-borne diseases in target communities.
The proposed project is designed also to use cash-based assistance to support basic WASH needs without undercutting local businesses and markets. This will build on COOPI’s strong experience in CVAs and market systems development (MSD) for better market-driven programming in crises and build resilience - going beyond cash, towards assistance that meets the immediate needs of affected people, while at the same time enabling them to take increasing control of their own coping and recovery through local systems and resources.
In order to ensure localisation, local CSOs will be trained and engaged for training modules and training-of-trainers will be provided, and local CSOs will conduct outreach training to target communities using a curriculum adapted for local contexts

1 - Needs

The Nigerian Humanitarian Crisis, largely triggered by a regionalized armed conflict, is one of the most severe protection crises in the world, with civilians bearing the brunt of ongoing hostilities, malnutrition, food insecurity, displacements and dire conditions including over-congestion and severe shortages of shelters across camps and communities hosting IDPs. Social safety net and socio-economic resources are depleted with most populations depending on meagre and overstretched humanitarian services. As per HRP-2020, a total 4.5 million people are in need of emergency Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) services in North-East Nigeria as an effect of the crisis over the past ten years.The deterioration of the health system due to insecurity and physical damage to health and WASH facilities has impacted the disease preparedness and services system resulting in an increase in morbidity and mortality related to chilldhood communicable diseases especially those that are preventable. Mostly displaced populations and host communities in North-East Nigeria face challenges associated with limited access to safe and adequate water, hygiene and sanitation services. Scarce and inadequate WASH services are the main cause of faecal transmitted infections including cholera and diarrhoeal disease (acute water diarrhoea and bloody diarrhoea), which remains the second leading cause of morbidity and mortality among children under the age of five, and the leading cause of death in the BAY States. Cholera remains a threat to public health in Nigeria with many cases recorded yearly and a peak during the rainy season between June and September. One of the national priorities for Nigeria government is to tackle the cholera and diarrhoea as stated in WASH Sector emergency technical guidance.Moreover, the outbreak of the COVID-19 in Nigeria, like many other countries of the world, continues to be a great focus of government at all levels in both preparedness and response approaches. Maternal and child malnutrition is a significant public health issue, as well as a social and economic problem in Nigeria. About one-third of children under 5 years are stunted (more than half in the north of Nigeria). Each year an estimated one (1) million Nigerian children die before their 5th birthday (NDHS Survey Report) and malnutrition contributes to nearly half of these deaths. Borno and Yobe remain among five (5) states where GAM is above the warning threshold and SAM is above WHO’s SAM crisis threshold. Underlying contextual factors contributing to deteriorating WASH services in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States include the vulnerability of the populations to climate shocks, ongoing conflict dynamics putting communities vulnerable to epidemic outbreaks. More immediate causes are primarily related to poor WASH, health behavior associated with malnutrition, including poor infant and young child feeding practices amongst caregivers, poor household water treatment and poor adherence to handwashing practices. COOPI being a member of Yobe Cholera Task Force, and based on the Disaster Risk Reduction DRR assessment conducted by COOPI, IMC and ALIMA conducted in December 2019 in Yobe state, it has appeared that the majority of areas of targeted LGAs do not have a cholera and other epidemics and disasters preparedness structures to meet the current and future needs. Coupled with the deteriorating nutrition situation in the States, there is need to scale up WASH coverage with improved and sustainable quality of assistance.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The proposed project offers a context-appropriate holistic and blended package of WASH interventions complementing other EU funded Yobe and Borno package early recovery efforts which meets early recovery needs and enhances social protection in the immediate term while strengthening individual, household, and community capacities, improved nutrition, and their supporting in the long-term. Furthermore, COOPI is committed to apply the humanitarian-development nexus approach to support Yobe State and ensure coherence between political, security, development and humanitarian actors. The proposed action will focus on meeting the immediate needs of conflict-affected and vulnerable groups in Yobe and Borno States, while addressing some of the underlying system failures and drivers of conflict and building the capacity government agencies to build trust and better respond to the WASH needs of communities through provision of WASH infrastructures, capacity building and supplementing other FSL and nutrition programs being carried out in same locations. The proposed project activities also aligns with the Yobe State government’s identified intervention and targets for development.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project also fits with Protection mainstreaming purpose. Protection is mainstreamed in the key components of the design and the implementation of each step of the project. COOPI will closely monitor the respect of its application.A strong sensitization will be carried out among the beneficiaries. They will be informed of their right, and how to report any breach. WASH NFIs distribution and construction of latrines and water facilities will be conducted in locations that are within easy reach for the target beneficiaries and during day time to ensure that beneficiaries get to their homes before dark as well as enough lights available. The planning awareness session and times will be done in a manner that will not interfere much with other community daily activities and programs targeted at children, pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls. Special consideration for pregnant and breast feeding women and girls, children and people with specific needs will be made to ensure that priority is given. To this purpose, prioritization on queues and easy access to distribution sites, as well as possible home delivery for heavily pregnant women and beneficiaries with disabilities will be ensured. Furthermore, COOPI will commit not to engage children in the management of the distribution and other intervention sites. The staff on site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI staff involved will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistleblower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM coordinate each other, so that at the programming level, protection is mainstreamed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will build on the previous experience of monitoring and evaluation of emergency and early recovery programs to ensure that the proposed project adapts its strategy to respond to actual context-specific needs as well as to demonstrate changes in outcomes to which the programme contributes. The monitoring system will include regular site visits, quality checks by the management team and/or technical staff, and community feedback and accountability mechanisms, and will be used to inform periodic management meetings in which the team will review data, identify trends, and propose changes to interventions when necessary.COOPI will establish a community feedback mechanism and project management committees in alignment with guidance provided by WASH Sector, UNICEF and donors. COOPI will ensure regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to capture their preferences, their respective priority needs and document barriers they face in accessing safe and meaningful assistance and information. Dedicate protection and AAP officers will be recruited that speak the local languages and are gender balanced to facilitate communicating with communities. Dedicated Community Feedback assistants will be recruited for the help and feedback desks. They will be equipped with computers/tablets to reduce the risk of data mismanagement/breaches in data protection.
