Project Summary

WFP will provide early recovery and resilience to shocks will be promoted through asset creation and preservation and, increased livelihoods Joint multi-sectoral assessments and Emergency Food Security Analysis will be conducted regularly to monitor the nutrition, food security, and market situation in crisis-affected states.
A gender-transformative approach that reflects the needs of women, men, girls, and boys will be adopted. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. Training and capacity building of staff and partners in the protection and humanitarian principles will include on-the-job coaching. WFP will ensure that affected populations (women and men) will be consulted throughout the programme cycle. Beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be tailored to their preferred means of communication. Beneficiaries will continue to be empowered with knowledge on their food assistance entitlements and their rights, including WFP’s zero-tolerance policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be embedded in M&E tools, complaint feedback mechanisms, and training of partners and staff.
WFP will continue the collection of key information on beneficiaries and assistance provided as part of its M&E activities. Monthly monitoring will include distribution and activity site management; beneficiary and entitlement verification; and ensuring adequate protection and gender measures. Lessons will be regularly drawn from monthly process and biannual outcome monitoring and reporting, and periodic reviews and evaluation to inform strategic and operational decisions.
WFP will further consolidate partnerships to enhance national and state-level emergency preparedness and response systems, including food security and nutrition assessment and monitoring, early warning, protection, gender analysis, targeting, and registration processes, safety nets, cash-based transfer capacities, and supply chain.
Where security and access to land allow, WFP plans to increase livelihood activities to support access to productive assets, income-generating activities, and self-reliance in the long term. Assistance will be coordinated with the local government, to ensure food assistance is complementary and fills gaps.

1 - Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns, and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women, and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education, and basic services. , The continued assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihood support, access to livelihood opportunities, labor market, and productive assets, remain limited. The recent Government plan to resettle IDPs who were in camps enhances the need to provide sustainable livelihood support as a means to reduce prolonged dependency on humanitarian support. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production, disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, food security, and nutrition situation remain extremely fragile. WFP regularly conducts protection analysis including Do No Harm, protection risk assessments, and protection monitoring within the context of the provision of food assistance. Protection and gender-specific questions have also been integrated into vulnerability food assessment tools and rapid intersectoral assessments to ensure that protection and gender are captured in food security assessments.

2 - Activities

Provide conditional transfers to food-insecure persons, including women, young people, and smallholders. In 2024, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 47,325 beneficiaries through non-agricultural livelihood activities with activities that aim to increase livelihood opportunities, creation and preservation of assets that improve livelihoods, and natural resource management activities in the B.A.Y States. To continue focused support to the most vulnerable conflict-affected communities and LGAs, WFP leverages evidence from the Cadre Harmonise and other supporting information such as the EFSA and market and livelihood assessments carried out by WFP. The activities undertaken shall build resilience and shock responsiveness and are in the range of WFP’s livelihood interventions. The selection of the activity type shall be informed by contextual analysis and community-based participation. Targeting through a community-based approach shall enable further prioritization of the most vulnerable. The type of businesses for which cash grants are targeted is informed by seasonal livelihood programming that entails an analysis of the seasonal activities that are unique to the operational context and thereby, appropriate forselection. In short, it forms the basis for various businesses that are appropriate for the context. The activity selection process, targeting and prioritization criteria, and transfer modality selection ensure social cohesion aligned with the “do no harm” principle. The selection of both IDPs and host communities to receive resilience-building shock-responsive support assures overall community integration in the end. The selection of activities and transfer modalities are informed by WFP’s operational context, beneficiarychoice, and community-based targeting/prioritization. Gender and protection analysis also informs the above-mentioned selection/approach. The appropriate geographical targeting is also informed through the FSS and the CH results, complemented by WFP food security assessments and IOM DTM. Targeting and prioritization of beneficiaries and activities are collectively derived through the participation of Partners and the communities to avoid duplications and minimize assistance gaps. WFP collaborates with IOM through a data-sharing agreement that assures a harmonized registration approach via electronic data exchange.
