Project Summary

The Project aims to address the urgent protection needs of Migrant Domestic Workers (MDWs) in Lebanon. Migrant Workers’ Action (MWA) and Egna Legna Besidet (ELB) are organisations with unique profiles and unparalleled trust amongst MDW communities in Lebanon. ELB is the biggest and most recognised MDW-led, owned, and run organisation in the country that provides holistic, protection-based support for MDWs and capacity development for MDW community groups. All of its executive members are former MDWs or the children of MDWs. ELB focuses on women MDWs in prisons and detention centres, and undocumented MDWs, especially single mothers, who face legal and protection challenges, including those whose houses and livelihoods were destroyed by the August 2020 explosion in the Port of Beirut. MWA is an international organisation focusing mainly on live-in MDWs in Lebanon, mostly women. MWA uses a strategic multi-layered approach to assist MDWs in Lebanon through emergency relief, legal action, employment mediation, research and advocacy at the local, regional and international levels.
This project aims to contribute to Outcomes 1, 2, and 3 of the ERP by continuing the operations of MWA’s safehouse for MDWs who have left abusive households and need temporary secure accommodation and protection services before returning home.
The deteriorating economic situation in Lebanon has contributed to the overall worsening of the situation of MDWs. Our work has noted a drastic increase in MDWs leaving their employer’s homes or being kicked out and unable to meet their basic needs, facing increasing psychosocial stress and acute health concerns. MDWs who have recently left houses in which they were exposed to severe abuse and who now live in Lebanon without legal status, therefore, need both multisectoral assistance to meet their immediate needs and support to undertake the often arduous process of putting together the required documentation and resources to leave the country. As a result, many shelter and temporary accommodations providers have been at total capacity and/or have tightened their criteria due to the high demand in referrals for accommodation by MDWs needing to return to their home countries. Additionally, the increase in dollar-based inflation has left some providers with no choice but to close down. More specifically, in recent months, MWA has seen an increase in referrals and the need for temporary accommodation support for severe cases, including gunshot, burn, stabbing, cancer, and multiple perpetrator rape victims.
This project will support activities undertaken by MWA to provide 10 MDWs per month
with temporary accommodation in the safehouse and medical and PSS support, including for severe or complex trauma.
It will support weekly recreational therapy sessions with the women in MWA’s safehouse, with techniques including holistic, complementary, and creative art therapies. The project will also include the provision of basic necessities and amenities for service users (SU). Finally, the project will support the return and reintegration process for women to return home.
ELB targets over 200 vulnerable MDWs who face legal and protection challenges. The client selection criteria will be based on their vulnerability and need for support, and the targeting will be done by case workers, field staff, and community volunteers.
The intervention will provide legal awareness workshops to all 100 migrant domestic workers to educate them on their rights and the legal framework governing their work and residency. ELB will provide legal assistance to 25 migrant domestic workers facing legal challenges related to their work and residency. Multipurpose cash-based support will be provided as $90 USD per person one-off cash-in-envelope assistance for up to 200 migrant domestic workers. Finally, emergency return services will be provided to 60 MDWs who urgently need assistance.

Outcome 3 Activities: 3.1-Provide AVRR assistance for 30 safehouse participants and 60 vulnerable MDWs.MWA will assess needs, develop aftercare and reintegration plans, and deliver workshops and training for women in the safehouse, linking at least 30 SU with pre-arrival livelihood and support services in their home countries and enroling at least 30 SU in programs to improve reintegration.ELB will assist in the return of at least 60 vulnerable MDWs, covering fees for administrative procedures and travel tickets.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

ELB and MWA have established strong risk assessment protocols and mitigation measures, and staff are equipped to respond to those challenges holistically and comprehensively. PSEA policies are implemented throughout the organisation aiming to respond to issues effectively as outlined:Provide professional and holistic support using best social practice to engage SU with professionals including a wide range of practical assistance, social work intervention, 1-1 keywork sessions, advocacy and liaison with agencies and assessing, managing and working effectively with risk.Carry out needs based risk assessments and support plans with SU assessing mental and physical health needs. Safehouse protocols and sexual health guidelines are in place to prevent risks of sexual exploitation. Training on sexual health and abuse for both staff and SU. A risk mitigation strategy will be adopted to ensure flexibility and adaptability for more impactful, relevant, and effective cash assistance.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Both organisations’ systems and processes ensure the provision of a holistic service with a service user-led approach. With this approach, MWA tries to ensure that those affected by the issues can tailor the service to their needs by retrieving information in 1-1 keywork sessions, daily conversations and case closure forms. MWA and ELB have fully equipped teams of practitioners that work effectively with risk while maintaining safeguarding protocols, continuous professional development and respecting the dignity and rights of the service user. Project partners maintain professional inter-agency relationships and share information when necessary with ongoing consent from service users. Service users maintain the right to make formal and informal complaints and are supported to take the appropriate steps and procedures. As a stakeholder-led organisation embedded in the community, ELB ensures AAP through MDW participation in program design, regular consultations, and accessible feedback.
