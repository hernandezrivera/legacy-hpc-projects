Project Summary

ACTED is proposing a nine-month intervention that supports women, girls, boys, and men meet their basic shelter and NFI needs; therefore, promoting improved resilience and urgent assistance in areas facing limited livelihood opportunities and suffering from public and private infrastructure destruction. This intervention will target Jere, Monguno, and Kukawa LGAs.
The proposed project suggests to procure 1,500 emergency shelters and 1,500 NFI kits for distribution to new arrivals in target areas, which expect to receive a new influx of displaced families due to the ongoing military offensives. 800 ESKs and 800 NFIs will be procured, prepositioned, and distributed by ACTED’s Shelter Team to new arrivals; while 700 ESKs and 700 NFI Kits will be prepositioned between Maiduguri and Monguno Logistics Sector Warehouses to contribute to the Shelter/NFI Sector pipeline. The prepositioned humanitarian items will be available for Sector partners for distribution, enabling rapid response to new onset of needs.
Both items are expected to be provided to new arrivals based on needs and vulnerability criteria, specifically targeting newly arrived families that have been unable to receive assistance from any other implementing partner, women- and child-headed households, the elders, persons with disabilities, etc. The intervention will target 15,000 individuals (assuming five members per household). Through the response, ACTED expects to promote the safety and dignity of families affected by the conflict.

Needs

Due to large displacements across Borno State, as well as large-scale refugee and IDPs return movement into LGA capitals which already host a large number of IDPs, shelter capacities have become even more inadequate than before. It is estimated that 2.1 million people require shelter and NFI assistance, including 1.6 million IDPs, 265,000 people in host communities, and 270,000 returnees. 50,000 returnee households in Borno state live in inadequate shelters, according to the HRP 2018. Most of the affected population in newly accessible areas and areas of return live in makeshift shelters, partially destroyed houses, unfinished buildings, overcrowded public buildings, and outdoor areas. Overcrowding, insecurity, and exposure to the external elements represents a major concern, especially in the light of a conflict that has seen an increase in the number of attacks against civilians, in addition to cases of exploitation and abuse. Serious protection concerns arises for women and girls who are often living in squalid conditions in sites and host settings characterized by overcrowding and limited access to safe, sanitary, and dignified accommodation; further increasing risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and harassment. An estimated 55% of people in need of shelter and NFI assistance are women and girls. Among the IDPs, people with special needs (PSN) including people with disabilities and the elderly, require accessible shelters with accessible latrines and ablution facilities to prioritize hygiene, safety and dignity. Due to the distance to nearest functional markets, the disruption of livelihoods and both sudden and multiple displacements; many people have no means to purchase essential non-food items. According to the latest Shelter and NFI Sector Needs Analysis, two million people are in need of shelter/NFI assistance in Borno state. The multiple displacements and ongoing conflict has forced the populations of concern to flee with little more than they could carry, resulting in urgent needs for life-saving NFIs.
