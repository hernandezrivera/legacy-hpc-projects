Project Summary

The project objective is to;
1: Contribute to the protection of newly displaced people, refugee returnees, and people affected by natural disasters including floods, fires, droughts and evictions. SYPD will achieve this through the distribution of emergency shelter kits and non-food items to affected people.
2. Improve the living conditions of the protracted internally displaced persons. SYPD will support the achievement of this objective through provision of sustainable transitional shelter with improved security of land tenure, NFIs distribution, site planning and mapping of basic services.
3. Facilitate access to durable solutions for IDPs who are willing to locally integrate or return to their place of origin. SYPD will achieve this by providing safe, habitable permanent shelter, site planning, secure land tenure and disaster resilient mainstreaming.
Under the shelter and NFIs project, SYPD will promote linkages with camp coordination and camp management, and durable solutions, and promote local building culture and building back safer. The project will support vocational trainings and ensuring linkages with the private sector by supporting cash transfers and voucher systems where markets are functional.

Needs

Internally displaced persons continue to face the brunt of the humanitarian emergencies in Somalia. In 2017 and 2018 drought and conflict continued to displace thousands of people into overcrowded settlements. Over 2 million the number of internally displaced persons in Somalia. According to UNHCR estimates, about 1.1 million Somalis or approximately a tenth of the population are affected by protracted displacement while additional newly displaced persons have added to the case load. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) continue to live in deplorable conditions in protracted unplanned displacement settlements with no access to basic services. About 70 to 80 per cent of the IDPs are women and children who are also exposed to protection concerns. The environment within which they live exposes them to various risks and hazards. IDPs continue to face risks to their lives due to lack of protection from weather hazards, physical safety, lack of access to basic services, insecurity and a life without dignity. IDPs are disproportionately at risk of gross abuses of human rights, especially women and unaccompanied children. Sexual and gender-based violence is widespread, even in areas of Somalia enjoying relative security. Many IDPs from minority clans suffer pervasive discrimination since they often lack vital clan protection and connections.The shelter needs for over 2 million protracted internally displaced persons and newly displaced people remain alarming. Data collected through inter-agency assessments in Afgoye corridor, Baidoa and Belet Weyne depicts an alarming situation with over 60 per cent of the IDPs do not have adequate shelter, NFIs, access to housing and property rights and durable solutions.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The building of resilience of vulnerable individuals, households and communities in Somalia to future shocks remains of paramount importance to tackle the key risks and address the underlying causes of vulnerability for IDPs and vulnerable host communities. SYPD will work towards tackling the self-reliance of, and working towards comprehensive solutions for, forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs, by ensuring a more active engagement of development actors, government authorities and other donors such as the Somalia Stability Fund and the World bank.Even in the context of protracted displacements, the environment in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region, middle Shabelle still presents opportunities for durable solutions and resilience building. SYPD will work with the community leaders, relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure, capacity building and income generation activities and hand over mechanisms for community based projects. During the project implementation, SYPD will build coordinate closely with the Shelter cluster and integrated response across other clusters to achieve a comprehensive response package that guarantees resilience building.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Internally displaced persons in Somalia and particularly in Bay, Lower Shabelle, Middle Shabelle, banadir, and Hiraan face the greatest risk of exclusion due to absence of protection mechanisms, legal frameworks such as housing and property rights and discrimination. Minority clans, youth, women and girls are particularly excluded due to cultural norms and community structures mainly dominated by men.SYPD will incorporate inclusion considerations into project design by establishing and strengthening community project committees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. The beneficiary registration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion. SYPD will establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable groups have a voice in the project implementation. Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through established community mechanisms. The project implementation will mainstream capacity building on all dimensions of exclusion with proper hand over mechanism to ensure that the mainstreaming is maintained throughout the project implementation and handover. SYPD will work with government authorities at the national and local level to strengthen protection frameworks, legal redress and secure land tenure and property rights for IDPs. Each household will be granted a secure occupancy of land for at least five years or as appropriate based on the prevailing circumstances.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender and age discrimination is a major challenge to the attainment of durable solutions and appropriate response for IDPs. Women, girls and youth and especially the poor or minority groups are often excluded in projects design and thus project outcomes due the structure of decision making process which are often male dominated. Land and property rights are often within the realm of dominant clans and male domain. This risks locking women, and girls into a cycle of destitution. To break this challenge, SYPD will mainstream age and gender into the project design, implementation and monitoring. SYPD will form project implementation committees and protection mechanisms such as age and gender balanced community project committees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. The beneficiary registration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion. SYPD will also establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable and especially women and girls have a voice in the project implementation. Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through established community mechanisms.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Armed conflict remains the main drivers of displacement in most parts of Somalia. The correlation between conflict and poverty is now well documented globally. Due to poorly developed skills especially among IDPs, limited access to credit and capital assets and lack of jobs, Somali youth, especially young women, face difficulties in securing livelihoods. In a shattered economy, many youth and especially in IDP settlements have no options but to remain idle or search for alternatives such as migration or illegal activities. To mitigate the effects of armed conflict and violence in the target locations SYPD will implement an assistance package complemented with capacity building particularly in shelter construction, settlement layout and site planning as a way of creating self-reliance and a road map to return. Where appropriate, SYPD will use cash and voucher system to promote local economy growth and income generation.
