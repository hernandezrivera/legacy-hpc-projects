Project Summary

According to the Aleppo and Idleb Governorates SMART survey conducted in August 2023, the prevalence of acute malnutrition based on WHZ in both Aleppo and Idleb governorates is comparatively higher than the previously observed GAM prevalence in the survey conducted in Northwest Syria at the same time in 2022; 4.4% (3.1-6.1% C.I) and 3.8% (2.7-5.2% C.I) respectively. According to 2018 WHO/UNICEF classification of acute malnutrition is considered low (2.5- I). The two survey areas recorded high stunting rates of 22.5% (19.7-25.7% C.I) and 22.2% (19.3-25.3% C.I) in Aleppo and Idleb respectively, with very slight increases compared with last year at 22.3%, but it is still over 20% which is considering as a Critical/ Extreme according to the GNC severity scale.
Despite the notably high proportions of children ever breastfed in Aleppo (92.9%) and Idleb (87.8%) only 38.8% and 44.7% of the children 0-23 months in Aleppo and Idleb respectively were initiated to the breast within the first hour of birth, with the proportion being lower in both governorates than the one recorded in NWS (2022) at 54.3%. Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) rates for 6 months were low in both governorates at 35% and 37.8% in Aleppo and Idleb respectively, compared to the 56.3% EBF rate recorded in NWS (2022). The proportion of infants 0 to 59.9 months of age who were fed formula and/or animal milk in addition to breast milk during the previous day receiving mixed feeding were 15.1% and 18.8% in Aleppo and Idleb respectively.
For the Maternal situation, the acute malnutrition situation between the WRA and PLW showed a slight decrease where the prevalence of wasting (MUAC < 230 mm) for WRA was 9.2%, 10.3%, and for PLWs was 9.8% and 10.1% in Aleppo and Idleb respectively. However, more than half (52.4%) of the women in Aleppo are likely to be anemic, while about a third (33.5%) of the women in Idleb are affected as well, - these are severe and catastrophic levels.in addition high prevalence of anemia among adolescent girls 48% in Aleppo and 32% in Idleb. Women who are underweight and anemic during pregnancy are more likely to have stunted children, perpetuating the inter-generational transmission of stunting.
Intervention and implementation of activities. SAMS will facilitate the prevention and treatment of malnutrition through the following measures:
Nutrition centers in Health Facilities will offer screening, diagnosis, treatment, referral, and follow-up for Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases. Nutritionists will conduct necessary measurements (including edema, Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC), weight, length, and Z-score), calculate the Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) or Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) dose based on standardized criteria, and provide detailed instructions on their utilization, along with dietary recommendations. They will also assess the child's condition in consultation with a doctor to identify any medical complications, referring cases to stabilization centers if necessary. Mothers will receive guidance on the importance of nutritional complementarity and sustained breastfeeding for children under two years.
Support for Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) will be offered through both group and one-on-one counseling sessions. Group sessions for PLW will be conducted at the HFs to provide awareness sessions on nutrition behaviors and best practices. CHW teams will conduct edema and MUAC screenings for children under 5 years old and PLWs during household visits. Suspected cases identified by CHW teams will be referred to nearby nutrition centers for confirmation of diagnosis and subsequent treatment follow-up. These measures, implemented by SAMS, aim to effectively address malnutrition and promote healthy nutritional practices within the communities served.
