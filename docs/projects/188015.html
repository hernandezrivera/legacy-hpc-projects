Project Summary

Non-attendance is highest in Idleb at 28%, in Aleppo at 26%, and in Deir-Ez-Zor at 25%. In addition, most schools are not flexible enough to accommodate children who work to support their families. Walking to school is high-risk for children, which can limit access to educational opportunities. Students travel about 20 minutes to get to school, with 49% in rural Damascus, 38% in Deir-Ez-Zor, and 27% in Idleb. The poor condition of education facilities, where about 11,989 schools are damaged, used as shelters, or inaccessible, and the limited availability of functioning ECE centers. 213,219 education workers
need to be supported through capacity building, half of whom are female. In November 2021, the informal exchange rate of the Syrian pound weakened by 0.3 percent to reach SYP 3,486/USD. (WFP, November 2021) and severe winter conditions, with temperatures ranging from -2C to +10C, have led to a significant increase in the price of clothing in the markets and 75% of people are unable to cover their winter supplies. In addition, the income of most families is between 75-100 thousand SYP per month, compared to the minimum need of 600-800 thousand SYP without rent.
One in three communities in Syria is believed to be contaminated and one in two people-about 10.3 million-are at risk from explosive hazards (UNMAS October 2021). By 2022, there were 50,821 confirmed cases of COVID-19 with 2,956 deaths (WHO January 2022).
The goal and mission of the project is to provide safe and equitable access to education services over a two-year period (2022-2023) to effect more profound change than would be possible with short-term projects for 369,379 (50% high displacement rates with severity 3-5) children in 14 sub-districts and adults of displaced persons, returnees, and overburdened hosts in the highly neglected sub-districts of:
Sabe Byar, Ghizlaniyyeh/Rural Damascus
Saraqab, Ariha/Idlib
Deir Ezzor, Tabni, Abu Kamal, Ashara/Deir Ezzor.
Atareb, Maskana/Aleppo
Farqalas/Homs
Hama, Ziyara/Hama
Dar’a/Dar’a
According to the assessment conducted and the HNO 2022-23, RSRP is planning to intervene to cover education needs. Through two years the intervene includes implementation of ECE (3100 children from 3 to 4 years old), non-formal education (19900 children from 5 to17 years old), BTL campaign (188000 children from 3 to 17 years old), reconstruction of damaged schools, provision of gender and disability washing facilities for children. Providing school supplies to 46000 students (3-17 years), improving social protection by distributing winter packages to 23000 students (3-17 years). 23800 teachers are trained and upgraded in MHPSS and 8000 students receive LSCE training. 3000 children in 9thand 12th grade will receive transportation support. Awareness sessions for 46000students on Covid19-mine risk education. In addition to establish 25 PTA committees to for active participation of local communities.
The total of 153469 targeted beneficiaries for 2022, will receive education assistance as following:
Total of (3-17yrs) students 151,936
Total of (25-59) teaching personnel 1,533
Total number of persons with disabilities 10,173
It is worth mentioning that the project is in line with the HNO 2022-23 and the Education Sector Strategic Objectives and Priorities (SO2 and SO3):
Strategic Objective 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, IHRL and though quality, principled assistance
Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.
The intervention areas may be increased according to the increase in the areas that will be under control in the years 2022-2023

4 - Needs

According to the assessment conducted and the HNO2022-23, Deir Ezzor, rural Damascus, Idlib, Aleppo, Homs, Hama and Dar'a score3-5on the sectoral severity scale and an estimated6.3 million children and 7% children with disabilities have problems accessing education services, while63% do not attend classes. The figure is28% in Idleb,26% in Aleppo and 5% in Deir-ez-Zor, due to the poor condition of educational facilities. 11,989 schools are damaged, used as shelters, or inaccessible, and there are few functioning ECE centers. Children work to support their families, and children must also walk an average of 20minutes to reach an accessible school. In addition, teacher displacement has led to a severe shortage of qualified teachers who are able to teach. Over 84,218 teachers are in need of pedagogical support. Needs are implementation of ECE (3100 children from 3 to 4 years old), non-formal education(19900 children from 5 to 17 years old), BTL campaign (188000 children from 3 to 17 years old),reconstruction of damaged schools, provision of gender and disability washing facilities for children. Providing school supplies to 46000 students(3-17 years), improving social protection by distributing winter packages to 23000 students(3-17 years).23800 teachers are trained and upgraded in MHPSS and8000 students receive LSCE training.3000 children in 9th and 12th grade will receive transportation support.Awareness sessions for46000students on Covid19-mine risk education and PTA committees

5 - Implementation of activities

RSRP is implementing the project directly only and serve the northern and northeastern regions through its main office in DZ as a permanent presence and currently implementing ECW for 2021-22 with UNICEF. Following the assessment, RSRP plans to expand services to geographic areas accessible to RSRP. The activities are consistent with the Education Sector Response Strategy, which takes into account the needs-based approach, identification of children ages 3-17 and adults with PIN under 2022-23HNO at the sub-district level, and HRP2022 Strategic Goal SO2&3. MEAL has critical points to ensure quality outcomes: - All activities implemented are monitored by staff/teachers before, during and after implementation to monitor/evaluate and find gaps. In the quality assessment of the RSRP, the instruments are structured according to the following aspects: conceptual framework, openness and transparency, cultural appropriateness, robustness of the methodology, validity, reliability and conclusiveness. The MEAL Department identifies cases to refer them either to protection services, another education service, or to the public school.-Creation of different communication channels for beneficiaries' feedback and complaints.-Consideration of COVID-19 measures.All activities will be carried out in coordination with the sectors, the MOE, and local authorities. In addition, RSRP will coordinate with the WASH sector to rehabilitate schools / learning spaces that will include WASH facilities

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1:Learning facilities unable to put into place and maintain Covid-19 safety measures including positive behavioral changes. Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigation Measures: RSRP will ensure that learning facilities should have Covid-19 safety protocols in place and protocols should include identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health center. These protocols include no-touch thermometers, hand washing and hand-sanitizer stations, face masks.2:Insuficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages. Likelihood:High Impact:High.Mitigation Measures: 1Suficient water and sanitation facilities should be provided and maintained.2WASH facilities should be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. 3:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully students. Likelihood:Low Impact:High.Mitigation Measures:RSRP learning facilities to have an enforced code of conduct covering school; staff, aid workers or contractors 4:Learning facilities are structurally unsafe or protected due to damaged infrastructure. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures:Learning facilities improved to ensure that buildings are safe and protected including stairs, walls, roofs, electric, water,-RSRP to ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PR1: Misconduct by humanitarian workers while realizing the activities. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure: 1. Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection and develop strategies to address the risks of GBV and PSEA during all project-stages. 2. Selection of competent and professional hygiene volunteers and signing of a code of conduct by all humanitarian workers. 3. Establishment of an effective feedback and complaint mechanism. 4.Assigning PSEA focal point to deal with all PSEA cases that may occurred. PR2: Targeted communities including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in RSRP being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigation Measure:1. Train staff on PSEA policy and procedures. 2. RSRP will work on raise awareness of targeted communities about PSEA and how to report misconduct.
