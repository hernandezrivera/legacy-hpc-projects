Project Summary

Children in the Syrian Arab Republic continue to face one of the most complex emergencies in the world. Over two thirds of the population require assistance because of a worsening economic crisis, continued localized hostilities, mass displacement, devastated public infrastructure and effects of the earthquakes. The 15.3 million people in need include 4.5 million women, 7 million children (3.2 million girls), 2.6 million people with disabilities and 5.3 million internally displaced people (IDPs). Over half of the displaced people are in NW Syria.
Populations in Syria experience widespread gender based violence (GBV) in its different forms, including partner and family violence, technology facilitated violence, sexual violence and harassment, early and forced marriage, and incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Safety and security concerns continue to be a gendered issue as women and girls are disproportionately affected by various forms of violence. The MSNA shows that female-headed households are more likely to express concerns around safety and security at home (18 per cent), and discrimination on the basis of race, political beliefs, religion, class, age, sex, marital status and disability (20 per cent). Female-headed households are also more likely to report safety and security concerns related to displacement (nine per cent), and threats of exploitation and abuse (including sexual) (seven per cent), further highlighting the dangerous nature of the risks they are likely to face.
After the earthquake, many women and girls have been subject for violence. It was noticed that they are not aware of their rights and they can’t access the needed services. The project aims to have women and girls living in dignified safe environment where their needs can be met and their rights are known. At the end of the project women and girls should be able to understand their rights and advocate them to others in the community. The target group will have the knowledge on whom to approach in cases of violence and whom to seek in case of consultations.
SOS will support women through providing GBV, and MHPSS sessions to women and girls 14 years or older as an activity of protection and prevention through using the national manuals developed through mobile team and family visits. Through the collaboration with other partners and the sector, SOS will be able to target participants as per the selection criteria. SOS will also target communities affected by the earthquake and the war that reported the need for such intervention. A committee from these empowered women will be created to share the knowledge, communicate challenges and needs, and provide feedback in a two way communication form. SOS through these sessions will identify participants that are victims of GBV and in need for case management and refer them to the correct services either internally or externally and will follow up with them. Those who are in need for legal consultations and psychotherapist will be referred to local organizations that provide the services in good quality. However, will pay fees for legal support for those who need civil documents. Women and girls who are within the criteria for vocational and literacy trainings will be enrolled. The participant enrolled in the vocational training will get a women dignity kit to motivate them at the end of the training as a reward for their attendance. In addition, SOS will target the community (such as community leaders, schools, colleges, local authorities) through conducting FGDs, GBV trainings, data collection and feedback handling mechanisms training, and through the recruitment process. When women are being subjected to different types of violence this would affect their ability to provide parental care. This might lead to family separation or might affect the children physical and mental health.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

SOS CV Syria will support the eligible women through providing a course of parental care skills depending on the national manual developed by UNICEF. Some major risks that might affect the program and are based on the sector risk matrix are as following:-Risk: Child and Adult Safeguarding risks that might affect the participantsLikelihood: High, Impact: highMitigation: Continuous training and application of Child Safeguarding (CS) Policy and Sexual Misconduct Regulation, Feedback and Complaints mechanism in place, CS Focal Point actively consulting project plan and implementation.-Risk: Participants are asked for a sexual or other favors in exchange of humanitarian assistanceLikelihood: Low, Impact: HighMitigation: SOS ensures all staff are trained on PSEA and they are aware of the SoPs and code of conduct. SOS has zero tolerance with such cases.-Risk: Participants are not able to reach SOS feedback mechanisms or are afraid of reporting.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation: SOS will ensure the different channels of the feedback mechanism that take into consideration the confidentiality and the protection of the reporter, as well as seeking the advice from the community on the preferred and most suitable mechanism. Ensuring the participation of participants in all phases.-Risk: Content of messages related to the sessions is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organization.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation: The community briefings and FGDs will support to understand the community. In addition to depending on national manuals and expert people in the field with the support of the sector.-Risk: Exclusion of some groups due to the choice of the locationLikelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation: SOS CV Syria will coordinate with the community key people to ensure the inclusion of all groups and will design the programme and the location based on the targeted group. The AAP assistant will ensure the inclusion of all groups in all the programme phases.-Risk: Lack of a appropriate intervention due to inadequate service provision and inadequate capacity, high turnover and loss of capacity lack of specialized and well-trained staff/service provider in these particular areas of expertiseLikelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigation: Depending on HNO data, assessments, community FGDs, and sector needs to determine the services needed in the community. In addition to adjust salaries to the market value, identify consortium partners and local NGOs to support the implementation and provide continuous trainings for the staff.-Risk: Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providersLikelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigation measures: Case management conducted by trained personnel only with adequate and adaptable policies in place.
