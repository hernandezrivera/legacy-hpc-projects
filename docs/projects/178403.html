Project Summary

The proposed project focuses on Education and Food security and Livelihoods sector interventions.
Under the education sector, FH proposes to create and renovate more inclusive learning spaces for children including children with disabilities, improve the quality of education through teacher capacity building, promote inclusive learning through provision of gender segregated WASH facilities and improvement of the learning environment to cater for children with disabilities, building the capacity of SMC/PTA on school management/governance and psychosocial support, Sensitizing children and guardians on COVID 19 and how to encourage out of schools children including those with disabilities to enroll and continue learning. FH will integrate and collaborate with nutrition, protection and FFE partners through the involvement of CNV in agriculture training programmes, school feeding and setting up of kitchen gardens at nutrition centres. FH will ensure some of the targeted schools host demonstration plots which can be used for instruction. Under education, FH will target schools and pupils directly affected by the flooding, and those who have been pushed out of school.

1 - Needs

Two consecutive years of flooding affecting already vulnerable populations experiencing low productivity from the impact of protracted conflict and violence. Unpublished reports from a recent Girls Education South Sudan (GESS) assessment on pregnancy in the Country indicate that many school girls got married or became pregnant during COVID-19 lockdowns. The government made efforts to ensure learning continued through radio lessons but its only limited to areas with radio reception and radio reception is not available in most rural locations. Following the flooding in 2020, multi-sectoral rapid assessments were conducted between December 2020 and January 2021 to ascertain the impact of the flooding and determine priority needs. In the four FH priority counties under this proposal (Upper Nile State –Panyikang, Ulang Counties; Jonglei State – Fangak and Pigi/Canal Counties), the following was recorded; a) 135,163 people were affected with 91% of them displaced, b) 27 schools suffered physical infrastructure damage, including the destruction of both teaching and learning materials, and learning spaces for 6,034 children (56% of them girls)). In Ulang and Panyikang county, 70.8% of the population and in Fangak and Pigi/Canal, 84.7% of the population is estimated to require humanitarian assistance at the peak of the lean season, May to July 2021 (ibid). Inadequate food intake and low dietary diversity have caused a rapid increase in malnutrition rates with all targeted FH counties exhibiting IPC Acute Malnutrition IPC 4 outcomes (GAM rates between 15% and 29%) in October 2020 They are expected to deteriorate further as the food security situation worsens. Schools on higher ground are now hosting IDPs and are no longer used for school activities (Rapid Fangak, Pigi/Canal, Ulang and Panyikang Counties IRNA assessments - January 2021). Panyikang has increasing number of returnees with no partner supporting EiE. Teachers in Panyikang, Fangak and Pigi didn’t benefit from IMPACT teachers’ incentives. IRNA reports estimates that 7,100 (4,000M 3,100F) are out of school. The rapid assessments identified the following needs: Education;• Inadequate learning spaces and WASH facilities for IDP children.• Inadequate TLM and hygiene supplies to facilitate quality and safe learning• Volunteer teachers with limited or no teaching skills and skills to appropriately deal with children experiencing• psychological stress • Hunger which has forced children not to attend school • Demotivated teachers who are not receiving a salary from the government.

Print and distribute Information, Education and Communication materials on COVID-19 to 160 schools and awareness. Sensitize 16 schools’ communities to encourage enrolment of out of school children and children with disabilities and how to prevent the spread of COVID 19.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

FH has a robust protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy. FH will ensure this policy protects target communities through raising awareness and reporting provisions within the policy. In all project related gatherings, FH will take time to explain its PSEA policy, emphasizing the right of the community to report any SEA cases and the available reporting mechanisms. FH will also produce banners in English and in local languages, with basic information on its PSEA policy and available reporting options. The banners will be displaced at all community meetings, at the gate of the respective FH field offices and in public spaces and schools. There are existing child protection clubs in each of the targeted schools. Through this proposed project, FH will reinforce training on reporting of suspected SEA cases through the toll free phone numbers and the suggestions boxes. FH already has suggestion boxes in each of the 25 target schools which is managed by a member of the SMC and FH staff. The SMC members will receive a refresher training on PSEA and the available referral pathways. FH will work closely with protection clusters/partners to provide support and referral of PSEA victims.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As part of the activity planning process, FH will separately engage groups of PWD or their representatives, women, girls and the elderly, to understand their special needs/barriers that can prevent them from participating in the project. FH will also conduct security and safety audits before the project starts to understand how vulnerable groups can safely participate in the project without being exposed to risk. This will include but is not limited to, agreement on places to hold activities like distributions, registrations and training, days and time for conducting project activities that do not interfere with other livelihood or social activities, and modifications necessary to improve access to project activities especially for PWD and the elderly, (for example, expanding the gate into the demonstration gardens for easy wheelchair access. Through the GESS 2 project, FH, with technical support from Leonard Cheshire (FH disability technical partner), is involved in sensitizing the families with children with disabilities (CWD) on how they can support them at home and what it takes to enroll them into school and so they can continue learning. Plans are underway for FH, in partnership with Leonard Cheshire, to administer the Washington Group of Questions (WGQ) to schools/communities. This tool will collect information and data forintervention planning and implementation that involves the participation of CWDs. Quarterly, FH will Vulnerable groups to review address and modify the approach to project activities based on their feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Inception meetings will be held at the county, payam and school levels with government representatives, stakeholders and community representatives at that level. Inception meetings will also be done at the Boma level with traditional leadership, village heads and youth, elderly, women and PWD representatives. After that, a series of community mobilization and sensitization meetings will be conducted at the start of the project with the community to ensure everyone has enough information about the project including, objectives, targeted population, caseloads, and the role of different community actors in the project. During registrations and distributions, FH will display banners showing beneficiary entitlements. At the school level, FH will hold quarterly meetings with the SMCs members to plan, implement and monitor each school’s activities. FH has an established complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM), which is part of its accountability system that ensures targetpopulations have access to safe and reliable reporting mechanisms. The CFM includes establishing a properly constituted help desk at every community meeting, suggestion boxes at strategic locations in the county identified together with the community, anonymous complaints email option, an international toll-free number, and a local reverse charged mobile number with a WhatsApp option that is managed by the Human Resources Manager at Juba Level. A local accountability committee with representation of women, elderly, PWD, will be established to continuously interact with the project and capture their feedback. These reporting details are displayed during all community meetings and at public places and schools across the counties. FH also encourages the community to speak with FH staff on the ground or seniormanagement staff when they visit project sites regarding any complaints they might have towards project staff or the community. When senior project staff visit the project areas they have confidential FGDs with separate groups of school children including girls and boys, men, women, the elderly and PWD and get any complaints and feedback directly. All complaints are treated with confidence and all FH staff must act accordingly with due diligence on any complaint raised to them by the community. All Project Managers and field supervisors are responsible for ensuring e that the feedback loop is closed by giving feedback to the community on all issues raised through the CFM. In the school, children will be trained on how to report suspected child protection cases anonymously through the suggestion boxes placed in each of the 25schools.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All complaints/feedback received will be logged on to a complaints/feedback log sheet. If the complaint/feedback is something that can be addressed by the supervisor at the field level, he/she has an obligation to adapt activities basedon the feedback. For instance, if a complaint of an inclusion error is reported, the field supervisor will raise the complaint with the Project Management Committee (PMC), and a verification household visit will be conducted. If the inclusion error is confirmed, the beneficiary is removed from the list and a replacement is identified and verified with active involvement of the PMC. If several inclusion and exclusion errors are reported, FH will call for a community meeting to refine the targeting criteria. If complaints cannot be dealt with at the field level, they are escalated to the FH Office in Juba for action by themanagement team or the senior leadership as appropriate. The feedback loop is only closed when it signed off by a senior member of FH staff as having been satisfactorily resolved. At the school level, the complaints/feedback raised by learners will be discussed during monthly SMC meetings and addressed. In case the SMC body is not able to resolve the issues, they will be escalated to the Payam education supervisors and the PTA members for further advice.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

● Risk of interference from external stakeholder. FH will hold inception meetings with all stakeholders to ensure all are aware of project objectives, target and targeting criteria. FH will use a community participatory process to refine targeting criteria to be context specific and acceptable to all stakeholders. FH will ensure representatives of vulnerable groups are incorporated in all project structures and that they are consulted throughout the project cycle. FH will ensure continuous awareness raising of its CFM and will react promptly to feedback raised through the CFM. FH will do ongoing stakeholder and local power analysis and mitigate negative interference.● Risk that marginalized groups, will not participate in project. Before project start, FH will conduct an induction training for all project staff, SMCs, PTAs, County and Payam Education authorities on disability awareness. Facilitated by Leonard Cheshire (FH disability technical partner), the training will focus on disability awareness, improving the physical activity environment, and packaging and disseminating information in alternative forms compatible with PWD. FH will: Identify groups of PWD, organizations for PWDs, the elderly, youth, women and girls to inform them about the project, identify those interested, and collect data of PWD interested using the WGQ (for PWD); Budget more time for activities to accommodate PWD and the elderly who may be slow in accomplishing tasks; Ensure activities begin with confidence building for PWD who may lack self-assurance due to prolonged stigma and exclusion; Ensure the environment for all activities is safe for PWD and the elderly to perform tasks without risk of injury of fatigue; Arrange for family member support where needed to ensure the PWD and elderly can be supported during the activity and at home; In consultation with Leonard Cheshire, support PWD, elderly and other frail individuals with basic adaptation of the home environment to enable them to work at home; Simple modifications may include raising garden beds; creating more space between garden beds to enable wheelchair movement; Siting gardens close to water source and closer home; creating sitting/resting places around the garden. - Link PWDs with organisations that provide appropriate assistive devices; School authorities trained on disability and inclusion will act as advocates to ensure schools’ infrastructure is inclusive for all physically and mentally challenged children, teachers and family members. For example, advocating for building classrooms that are wheelchair accessible by CWD. The Community volunteers will conduct house-to-house sensitization of the IDPs/returnee community to ensure CWD and other disadvantages children are supported to enroll and attend lessons regularly.● Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using CFM.FH will; Set up the CFM in consultation with communities so that the CFM is accessible to all (women, elderly, PWD); Offer more than one feedback option so beneficiaries can choose a mechanism comfortable to them; Ensure confidentiality and closure of feedback loop to build confidence and trust among the target communities; Periodically review and modify the CFM with active participation of beneficiaries, especially vulnerable group.● Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence.FH will; Conduct a safety audit as part of the inception process to identify potential safety and security concerns of the targeted communities, particularly speaking with vulnerable groups (elderly, PWD, women, girls and boys); In consultation with key informants and vulnerable groups, identify and map safe and accessible zones that can be used for project activities; Endeavor to deliver services as close to the communities as possible to reduce the distance travelled by beneficiaries to access assistance. Community Mobilization Volunteers will track any suspected violence and FH will work closely with the protection cluster.
