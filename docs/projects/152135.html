Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen timely, coordinated and integrated food security response through collaborative approaches that enhance local capacities and coordinate with the Food Security Sector partners, stakeholders and other sectoral interventions. It targets at least 85 organizations - FSS partners, national institutions, donors, other stakeholders.

Needs

Around three million people in the BAY states are projected to be food insecure and considered to be facing crisis or emergency situations. This represents a significant improvement from 3.7 million (November 2017) due to the delivery of food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihoods support; improved security conditions which has allowed for farming and market activities in some locations; and favorable climatic conditions for farming. Nevertheless, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile particularly in Borno State, and IDPs state that food is their largest unmet need. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food needs, including cooking energy, which particularly increase protection risks for women and children. In spite of some improvement compared to last year, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets and livelihood activities remains limited. Market and trade routes remain disrupted, restricting trade flows and activities. This is further exacerbated by route disruptions during the rainy season and sporadic hostilities. Vulnerable people arriving from hard-to-reach areas are in critical need of access to nutritious foods. Meanwhile, people who remain in hard-to-reach areas have not been assessed, and thus not yet assisted by any humanitarian intervention.The following are the specific needs:• Food assistance through the most appropriate modalities • Assistance to support safe access to fuel-efficient technologies through the most appropriate modalities• Agriculture livelihood support including provision of inputs/assets through the most appropriate modalities, community-based productive asset creation/enhancement activities, provision of agriculture extension services, entrepreneurship, technology transfer, value addition and processing capacity building (Agricultural livelihoods refers to crop production, livestock, fisheries, forestry and natural resources management.)• Coordination among all stakeholders with evidenced based FS analysis and advocacy to inform the response• Protection integration/mainstreaming in FS responseTo effectively deliver the above planned activities, it is crucial that robust and effective coordination mechanisms with strong information management capacities remain in place at the federal and state level. This will help to strengthen synergies and effectiveness to ensure timely, appropriate, collaborative and integrated food security response to people affected by the crisis in the northeast Nigeria.

Activities

Activity 1: Support the Food Security Sector coordination at federal and state levels - including support payment of sector coordination personnel; conduct regular FSS, SAFE and Agriculture & Livelihood Taskforce meetings at state and federal level; undertake and lead sector planning, strategy development, emergency preparedness and monitoringActivity 2: Support information management and food security analysis for the northeast crisis - including support payment of sector IM personnel; support data collection and information analysis, and develop IM tools and maintain FSS website;Activity 3: Strengthen local capacities including the national sector leads at state and federal level to lead and support FS coordination - ensuring the involvement of authorities through "learning by doing" approach; strengthen state-level coordination mechanisms built on existing structures; provide logistics, equipment and materials to build local capacities. Activity 4: Conduct joint protection monitoring missions with protection partners, protection integration capacity building and develop related localized sector-specific guidance/checklists. In line with the Centrality of Protection strategy, the sector aims at continuous advocacy/sensitization among partners to ensure minimum 5% of a project budget to be used towards protection mainstreaming activities as well as integration of commitment to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in any FSL project design. Ex: Sector partners' minimum standard: each partner should have Code of Conduct that addresses PSEA issues and staff are committed to it. Activity 5: Continue working with other stakeholders (e.g. camp management) and key partners to strengthen and streamline existing complaint and feedback mechanisms and the referral pathways among partners. Activity 6: At least one Sector/Cluster Coordination Performance Monitoring survey will be conducted to monitor and evaluate the sector's performance based on stakeholders' feedback.
