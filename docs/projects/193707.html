Project Summary

The Gaza Strip is facing immense challenges related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), which pose significant health risks to its nearly two million residents and constrain the socioeconomic development of the Palestinian people. The ability of the service providers to ensure satisfactory operation of over 500 WASH facilities has been undermined by funding gaps, also, more than 290 WASH infrastructure were affected during May 2021 escalations, constraining access to water and sanitation services for more than 1.2 million Palestinians. This situation has reduced households access to adequate water and sanitation services, weakening Gaza households’ capacity to maintain their health, livelihoods, social and hygiene status, of particular concern in light of the ongoing pandemic. Women, children, elders and Persons with Disabilities (PWD) are the most affected by this shortage in WASH services. Women in particular are struggling to maintain minimum hygiene and ensure the availability of drinking water. In line with the WASH cluster objective #2: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians”, this project proposes to provide emergency WASH assistance and capacity building support to the female-headed households in Rafah governorate in the Gaza Strip. Specifically, the project will improve access to water for drinking and domestic use and will complement the assistance they receive from Islamic Relief Palestine (IRPAL)through its family empowerment programme. The project will target 100 of the most vulnerable female-headed households (75 females, 55 males, 60 boys, 60 girls), that are critically underserved in relation to WASH services as revealed in the Rapid Needs Assessment carried by IRPAL and its partners in June 2021. Based on the findings of the rapid needs assessments, IRPAL and partner have identified the following locations for 2022 interventions: Rafah Camp, Alshabwra, Tal Sultan and Rafah Algharbiah. IRPAL intends to respond by providing an appropriate, integrated WASH response for the most vulnerable households through improving access to water for drinking and domestic use, sustaining access to basic sanitation services and promoting personal and environmental hygiene among the targeted Households. IRPAL is the WASH cluster focal point in Rafah, and it has the staff, experience and practical presence in the area, thus creating a conducive environment for successful operations. Throughout the project life cycle, IRPAL will coordinate with WASH cluster and partners, the local authorities and other stakeholders including affected community, and will depend on a local partner on ground for implementing the activities ensure maximum utilization of resources. The proposed interventions ensure protection mainstreaming as all activities are guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability, as well as participation and empowerment. Also, accountability will be ensured through applying IRPAL’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism.

1 - Needs

WASH sector in Gaza Strip face systematic challenges and particular vulnerabilities driven by the longstanding Israeli occupation and Gaza blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, the COVID-19 outbreak, and most recently the May’s escalation, along with the fiscal and socioeconomic consequences of these factors. People in the Gaza Strip are exposed to public health risks associated with poor water quality, poor wastewater collection and treatment, lack of storm water infrastructure and lack of proper hygiene practices. The ability of WASH authorities, partners and service providers to upgrade, maintain and operate at full capacity more than 500 water and sanitation facilities have been undermined by significant funding gaps, the Israeli import restrictions on ‘dual-use materials and equipment”, and the poor governance of the WASH sector as well as the limited technical and financial capacities. Moreover, more than 290 WASH infrastructures in the Gaza Strip were affected during the May 2021 escalation, constraining access to water and sanitation services for more than 1.2 million Palestinians, yet the WASH service providers are still struggling to fully restore and maintain the functionality of WASH services in the Gaza Strip. According to HNO -2022, over 1.35 million Palestinians across the oPt are affected by public health risks associated with insufficient access to WASH services and will be in immediate need of humanitarian assistance in 2022.When adequate and appropriate water, sanitation and hygiene are not available, major health hazards can result. However, simply providing water and sanitation facilities will not by itself guarantee their optimal use or impact on public health. Understanding vulnerabilities and social relations is absolutely essential in assessing, designing and implementing an appropriate WASH programme that is effective and safe and restores the dignity of the affected population. In its Rapid Needs Assessment Report, which was conducted by the sponsorship programme at IRPAL in cooperation with its local partners in June 2021, 1,000 female-headed households from the five governorates of Gaza Strip to assess their multi-sectorial needs and priority areas of interventions. Among the 140 households participated in the survey from Rafah governorate, it was found that 65 households are not using safe bathing or latrine facility as bathrooms should be repaired/reconstructed with disproportionate impact on women and girls, diminishing their sense of physical security and privacy. Also, 51 houses are with inadequate pipelines, networks and HH connections and/or inadequate/damaged sanitation facilities and networks. Also, most families are in need for assistance for accomplishing their basic hygienic needs in a modest and acceptable way as well as promoting their personal and environmental hygiene. This situation has reduced household access to adequate water and sanitation services for these families and for some of them who are not able to mitigate the interruption of services, they are adopting negative coping mechanisms such as decreasing their water consumption and discharging wastewater and dumping solid wastes to open areas. This condition has been worsened by the last hostilities as many families lost essential items such as water tanks, or have their sanitation facilities and networks damaged, exacerbating the stresses among the families’ members and the needs for specialized WASH interventions to alleviate their suffering.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to critical needs of the female-headed households which are affected by the loss of sole breadwinners and the protracted humanitarian and pandemic crises, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities in order preserve the dignity of vulnerable beneficiaries through increased access to safe, equitable and adequate water, sanitation and hygiene promotion and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children. IRPAL will maintain close coordination with public service providers, WASH cluster and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. The targeted locations have been identified and the local partner will be responsible for the collection of data, implementing the project activities and conducting community consultation meetings to get feedbacks on design, implementation and monitoring of activities to ensure gender and age specific WASH interventions as well as integration of safety and mitigation of protection risks especially for females. IRPAL will lead efforts with key stakeholders, linked with the provision of the following WASH interventions: (1) The provision of PE water tanks, and (2) The rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities: IRPAL and implementing partner will select the HHs in need, conduct technical assessment and provide cash assistance for the targeted HHs to conduct the rehabilitation and upgrading of HHs sanitation facilities in 50 housing units to meet SPHERE standards (Cash Modality: Self-help). This includes maintenance of toilets, kitchens and in-house sewage connections. The maintenance works will address women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. Activity (3) The provision of standardized household hygiene kit, in which all family members: women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities and elderly’s specific needs are considered, to helps HHs maintaining a healthy and safe hygiene environment, IRPAL and partner will distribute family hygiene kits to the target families (Modality: Cash). Activity (4) Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns: IRPAL and the implementing partner will conduct the hygiene promotion activities will include home visits and delivering awareness messages to HHs with poor hygiene situation. These hygiene promotion activities are to enhance safe hygiene and water management practices (personal hygiene, hand washing especially, and safe water handling), and will consider the specific needs of different target groups (Modality: Service Provision). The targeted HHs will be actively engaged in the design, planning, implementation and evaluation of activities. IRPAL will put in place a beneficiary feedback mechanism, whereby beneficiaries can communicate and express their feedback, suggestions or complaints about services, including about the possible negative consequences of its programming. IRPAL MEAL department will collect, record, analyse and respond when relevant, to all beneficiary feedback.

3 - Indicators

IRPAL implements an extensive framework of tools for the monitoring and evaluation of the activities conducted. The planned MEAL activities will assess bottlenecks, barriers, challenges and lessons learned, and staff will monitor progress against agreed WASH cluster indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. The WASH cluster indicators are designed in order to closely monitor the meeting of the project objectives, especially the WASH cluster objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians. The following are the expected results and indicators of the project: The outcome indicator for Activity (1) and Activity (2) is # of people benefiting from improved access to safe drinking and domestic water services, while the output indicator for Activity (1) is # of PE tanks provided and the output indicator for Activity (2) is # of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation, solid water and hygiene practices and the output indicator is # of households sanitation facilities rehabilitated. The outcome indicator for Activity (3) and activity (4) is: # of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation, solid water and hygiene practices while the output indicator or Activity (3) is # of household hygiene kits provided and the output indicator for Activity (4) is # of WASH awareness campaigns conducted. The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted 50HHs (including 250 people: 75 females, 55 males, 60 boys, 60 girls), through improving their access to basic WASH services. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the indicator “% of project beneficiaries (male/female) reporting that humanitarian WASH assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured by IRPAL.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The project adopts the cash modality (when applicable) in the rehabilitation/maintenance works, purchase of water tanks and maintenance/operation tools and materials, and for the purchase of hygiene kits. This modality proves to be efficient and it maintain the dignity of target households when reflected in using self-help approach that empowers households to resume their capacity in leading the solution to their concerns rather than being considered as a mere recipient of support, especially when the approach is complemented with mentoring and training on the use of cash. The hygiene promotion awareness activities will be delivered through service delivery modality.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

IRPAL has an effective and reliable complaint and feedback mechanism that will be applied to provides a chance for all beneficiaries especially the most vulnerable to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention has been developed with comprehensive understanding of the protection principle, especially “Do No Harm”. The project design had considered protection risks and mitigation measures. During the verification and final selection of the pre-identified target facilities, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform them about the provided assistance and services. The project will prioritize marginalized/economic vulnerable communities that do not have sufficient resources/capacities. The project includes effective use of referral pathways for specialist support, in areas related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and psychosocial support. Accountability to the affected population has been well mainstreamed in the project design, implementation, and monitoring. A set of mechanisms will be established to ensure accountable implementation of the project activities. The rehabilitation inside HHs will improve the hygienic-sanitation conditions and ensure privacy and safety, to contribute to reducing the risk of protection-related risk. The provision and installation of new water storage units will reinforce the beneficiaries' ability to survive and prevent or alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity. In addition, the provision of dignified hygiene kits and the hygiene promotion awareness activities will enhance positive hygiene practicing through encouraging beneficiaries thus protect public health HHs’ members, particularly children and women. All these activities will facilitate proper access for WASH services, and thus contribute to increase access to human right and ensure beneficiaries dignity through improving their life conditions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

IRPAL seeks to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. The project directly improves access for people with specific vulnerabilities in terms of socioeconomic and protection to basic WASH services. A participatory approach with local partner and communities was used to identify the needs, to develop a risk mapping and to decide together on the implementation. The targeted communities are the most vulnerable population which is reflected in IRPAL’s needs assessment. The beneficiaries' selection process is needs-based and will consider transparent and accountable processes including the feedback and complaint mechanism. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level.
