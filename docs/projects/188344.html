Project Summary

The purpose of the Shelter and NFI intervention is to provide life-saving and life­ sustaining shelter and NFI support, and contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure in Syria. The different components of NRC's Shelter project are intended to contribute to HRP Shelter & NFI Sector Objectives 1 and 2.
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities, IDP and refugee returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the conflict. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators. The needs of the different genders, elderly and PWD will be considered during design, implementation and monitoring processes.
Based on the needs in Syria for 2022 and 2023 and in line with the NRC’s Syria Strategy, the Shelter programme will include emergency and recovery response options. These options are not intended to be "standalone" and may be delivered in conjunction with one another.
In NES and NWS – camp and informal sites settings, activities will be implemented through cross-border; where NRC will be predominantly focused on provision of essential winterization assistance (using winter labeled cash or in-kind, where appropriate) under Activity (1) and rehabilitation of community infrastructure i.e. roads under Activity (2). Shelter and NFI activities will be delivered as a fully-integrated joint programme where relevant. The implementing modality will be defined based on access constraints and NRC’s capacity and will include cross-border emergency (unconditional cash, in-kind winterization assistance), transitional (rehabilitation and construction of roads, lighting and other camp infrastructure) and recovery where relevant.
In Government of Syria controlled areas, NRC will provide core NFIs (in kind and CVA modality) to the newly displaced and support with the rehabilitation and maintenance of collective shelters as well as with the shelter kits. Seasonal NFIs will be provided to households using in kind or CVA modalities. Rehabilitation of damaged houses will be done using holistic approach incorporating WASH (rehabilitation of common areas and community infrastructure by ensuring sufficient water storage, connection to water and sewer networks, etc.). Whenever possible rehabilitation of buildings will include training on shelter repairs rather than using contractors. In 2022 NRC will explore possibilities on linking housing and infrastructure with solar energy (already incorporated in WASH projects, schools and medical centers rehabilitation) and further pilot these types of projects in 2022/2023. Other community infrastructure such as markets, schools and medical centers will also be rehabilitated in coordination with relevant sectors. Shelter and WASH activities will be delivered as a fully- integrated joint programme where relevant. The implementing modality will be defined based on access constraints and NRC capacity, transitional (roads, lighting and other settlement infrastructure) and recovery (housing rehabilitation) where relevant.

4 - Needs

2022 HNO estimates 5.92 million people need shelter support, and 38% of the population live in substandard shelter. The main challenges are access to basic services (water, sanitation and energy), protection from elements and physical safety. The deteriorating economic situation (90% of population living below the poverty line) also has negative impact on the ability to carry out shelter repairs as well as on the access to essential and seasonal NFIs, with more than 4.9M people in need of NFI assistance (top needs are related to energy and clothing). In camps settings 67% of the population are unable to afford NFIs available in the marketplace. The top items reported as available yet unaffordable are Such items are crucial, particularly during harsh winter seasons, when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving. Mainly within camps settings in Northwest and Northeast Syria, 2.02 million people live in sites of last resort often with lack of crucial infrastructure and rely on emergency shelter solutions and continued humanitarian support. Females, children, PwD and elderly are the most affected and are forced to adopt harmful coping mechanism, such as keeping children out of education, selling assets, etc. Out of camps settings, the main challenges are access to basic services (water, sanitation and energy), protection from elements (especially challenging during the winter months) and physical safety.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will contribute to Specific Objective 1 through: (a) provision of non-fuel core and essential NFIs ; (b) Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs using in kind and cash modalities; (2) provision of timely life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support (medium­ term/temporary shelter) to crisis-affected households. This will include the provision of emergency shelter (e.g. tent installation in camps, transit sites and spontaneous settlements with associated infrastructure, rehabilitation of collective shelters (also incorporating WASH facilities and services). NRC will contribute to Specific Objective 2 through: (a) provision with adequate and sustainable housing stock to targeted crisis affected households are; (b) Rehabilitation of community infrastructure i.e. road repair and construction; (c) rehabilitation of damaged and unfinished buildings; (d) supply and installation of HH level solar energy systems (piloted in 2022/2023); (d) enhancement of tenure security with support of NRC Capacity building program (included in Protection Sector submission); (e): rehabilitation of common areas and activity; (f): rehabilitation of community infrastructure (special focus on WASH services and linkage with Education and ERL).(c) rehabilitation of collective shelters (incorporating WASH facilities and services).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Centralized distributions can create security risks for beneficiaries and staff. In-kind NFI distributions might lack appropriate items for women, girls, and boys and/or the quality/type. MM: Prioritize household-level distributions where feasible; Risk assessment and alternative distribution methods; Market-based approaches in place; Complaints and Feedback mechanisms in place.2) Lack of adequate understanding of Housing Land, and Property causes risk of eviction and lack of security of tenure. MM: Due-diligence carried out to ensure understanding of rights and responsibilities and strengthen security of tenure; Complaints and feedback mechanisms in place; community awareness3) Barriers to identification of targeted areas/beneficiaries constitute a risk of selection bias, unintended marginalization of neediest communities, and/or fueling tensions between neighboring communities. MM: Needs assessment include FGDs with community members; verify beneficiary list; Train staff on Core Humanitarian Standard and accountability; ensure targeting different groups.4) Limited options for adequate distribution sites and bureaucratic and administrative impediments contribute to compromising accessible, organized and tension-free distributions. MM: Feedback and complaint mechanism; Safe and Inclusive Programming, PSEA training to staff; learn from outcome monitoring findings; consider Cash and Voucher modalities in areas with functioning markets to improve dignified outcomes.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are mainly identified during the beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities as well as confidential complain and feedback channels available to them and makes sure gender balanced teams register and deliver assistance to communities whenever possible. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM started and expected to conclude in 2022.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. NRC staff are injured, killed, or suffer psychological trauma due conflict: Mitigation: Security briefings, HEAT and HEIST trainings, briefings, more support from UNMAS.2. Fraud/corruption/bribery. Mitigation: enforcing NRC's policy of zero tolerance through strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the "whistle-blowing" policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. 3. NRC is unable to timely operate due to the impact of internationals sanctions. Mitigation: request for derogation, looking for different banking channels, advocacy.4. NRC is unable to timely implement due to delays in approvals from Government/local authorities. Mitigation: Advocacy plan in place, set the lead time for approval submission to be 2 weeks with a close follow.5. NRC is unable to timely deliver the planned activities due to rapid inflation and change of prices. Mitigation: review budget and targets on a quarterly basis, communication with the donors, find more convenient payment terms to suppliers.6. Donor policies, including overly restrictive redlines and perceptions, affect the ability to implement and scale-up programs. Mitigation: Engaging with donor, Joint advocacy efforts with DINGOs, Intensify the negotiations with local partner and MOUs, Long terms programs and reconstructions, media coverage in private and public advocacy.C-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during C-19; adapted implementation modalities,
