Project Summary

Despite conducting elective surgeries by UHWC to patients scheduled on the MoH waiting lists through the implementation of 2 projects funded by OPT-HF, one of these projects was implemented in 2018-2019 & targeted 827 patients & the other is currently being implemented targeting 1,474 patients. Despite implementing such projects by other international & local institutions, the need still exists & the No. of patients increase steadily as a result of the increase in the severity of the accumulated crises faced by the MoH where the most recent was the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, upon which the MoH allocated all its hospitals to deal with emergency & COVID-19 cases.
According to HNO 2021, the elective surgeries waiting list reached 11400 surgeries.
In order to contribute to bridging the gaps & responding to the humanitarian needs of those patients, through this project 2000 electives surgeries will be conducted at al Awda Hospital – Jabalia & Al Awda Health center Rafah targeting women, men, girls, boys & PWD from all Gaza Strip.
The surgical operations will cover the following No. & specialties:
- 1000 ENT
- 500 General
- 200 urology
- 150 Orthopedic
- 150 pediatric
600 minor
800 moderate
600 major
Through this project, 12 from UHWC staff will attend PSEA training
A partnership will be continued through this project with the Central Blood Bank Society (CBBS) in order to provide the necessary blood units for patients to save their lives. As through this project, 2000 blood units will be collected and provided to Al Awda Hospital & other hospitals in the Gaza Strip
In order to achieve the principle of coordination & complementarily, during the design of this proposal coordination has been done with the HC coordinator & MoH who emphasized the need & the importance of this project. As well As, coordination has been done with Al Ahli Arab Hospital in order to enhance complementarily & to avoid duplication in the provided services. Moreover, the idea of the project meets Health Cluster obj. #1 related to strategic obj. 2.
Believing in the principle of community participation in identifying their most important problems, needs & priorities, 50 calls were conducted with individuals from the local community from the Northern & Gaza governorates where the majority of them confirmed the importance of alleviating the suffering of patients who are registered on the MoH waiting list, especially who needs major & medium operations, as the delay in conducting the surgical operations pose a threat to the lives of patients & increase their suffering adding to the possibility of having complications.
Another 50 phone calls will be conducted with the project beneficiaries to monitor the project services at the mid of the project. While 100 questionnaires will be filled to measure the beneficiaries' satisfaction about the provided services at the middle & end of the project.
Noting that the UHWC adopts hotline, email & complaint boxes to receive complaints, the project beneficiaries will be informed about this mechanism through radio & social media, including sign language for PWD.
To achieve the principle of protection, the project will target different age groups, including men, women boys & girls, as well as PWD without discrimination.
To respond to gender needs, medical staff of both sexes (M&F) will be available. Moreover, pediatric surgeons will be available to respond to the age needs.
In order to protect patients & medical staff from the transmission of COVID19 infection, several preventive measures will be followed, including the initial examination (IGG, IGM) for the patients before entering the hospital, while PPE will be distributed to the medical staff & patients inside the hospital.
To reach accurate & easily accessible information, HIS is in place & used at all UHWC facilities that desegregate data with gender, age & PWD.

1 - Needs

The HNO 2021 indicated that there are 11400 patients in need of surgical operations scheduled on the waiting lists at the MoH while many of them are expected to suffer from deteriorating in their health status. Although thousands of elective surgeries were performed to patients who referred from the MoH to NGOs hospitals in the Gaza Strip, through funding from the OPT HF – ECHO UNRWA and others, the need still exists in light of the deterioration of the health system in the Gaza Strip, as the MoH reports indicated that the number of those registered on the waiting lists reached 11400 patients in September 2020. (HeRAMS). As the MoH suffers from a series of crises related to the severe shortage of health resources, including the shortage of essential medications that reached 47% and medical disposables 33% (MoH report 8/2020). This is in addition to other crises, such as the cut off power for long hours and shortages of fuel needed to run the electrical generators as the cut of electricity reached 13 hours daily (OCHA Sept. 2020), in addition to the disruption of medical devices and the difficulty of repair, adding to the crisis of human resources resulting from the early financial retirement imposed by the Palestinian Authority on public sector’s employees in the Gaza Strip. Hence, this situation has been worsen after March 2020, with regard to the spread of COVID-19 in the Gaza strip, where the MoH uses all its resources to respond to this emergency situation, and that affect the increase in the number of registered patients on the waiting lists. As there are in average appox. 2000 elective surgeries added per month to the waiting list The UHWC has conducted 50 phone calls with population in Gaza and North Gaza Strip governorates targeting different gender and age groups in order to identify the most important priorities of the community in the health issues. The majority of participants of these calls stressed that surgical operations of patients scheduled at the MoH waiting list is the important priority as the increase in waiting time for patients in need of surgical interventions increases their suffering and pain, beside the likelihood of having complications due to delay in conducting the needed surgical operations, especially on the category of children with ENT health problems, as the delay in conducting such surgical operations will contribute to educational difficulties for school children due to their reduced hearing level. It is worth to mention that, the idea of the project was discussed with the Health Cluster Coordinator and MoH who stressed the importance of this intervention as it meets a vital need in the health sector. As well as coordination has been done with Al Ahli Arab Hospital in order to enhance complementarily & to avoid duplication in the provided services.

2 - Activities

The project will include the following activities: Activity 1: Conducting elective surgeries to 2000 patients free of charge including men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and PWD, according to the lists received from the MoH on a monthly basis. The surgical operations will be conducted in Al Awda Hospital – Jabalia and Al Awda Health Center - Rafah. The surgical operations services include: • Medical examination by specialists. • Pre and post-op investigations • Surgical operations. • Hospitalization. • Post-op Follow up. Activity 2: Procurement process to purchase all needed material and medical disposable for the CBBS. Activity 3: Collecting and distributing 2000 blood units Activity 4: Conducting 12 hours training course through ZOOM platform for 12 of UHWC staff in Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) Activity 5: Raising the awareness of the community about UHWC complain mechanisms and PSEA through 2 radio spots and 5 facebook advertisements, knowing that these advertisements will be adapted to the sign language of the PWD. Activity 4: Conducting 50 phone calls with beneficiaries form the project (40% of whom are females in addition to PWD) will contribute to the project’s monitoring. Activity 5: Filling out 100 satisfaction questionnaires to measure beneficiaries’ satisfaction about the quality of provided services at the midterm and end of the project. Activity 6: Documenting the project’s activities through: Photography & audio-visual report by ensuring respect of the beneficiaries’ privacy and dignity. In order to contribute to limiting the spread of the Corona pandemic among Al Awda Hospital and Al Awda Health Cetner's staff and patients, prevention and safety measures will be followed in accordance with the MoH and WHO protocols, as they will be provided with PPE in addition to conducting initial examinations (IGGM & IGM) for all patients before conducting surgical operation, provided that the suspected cases are referred to the central MoH lab.

3 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through the achievement of the following indicators, knowing that MEAL plan will be prepared including data collection methods and desegregations of these data, tools, frequencies, responsibilities and reporting: # of patients benefitted from provision of elective surgery # of blood units collected and distributed # of trained staff in PSEA # of people from the community aware about the UHWC complaint system and PSEA. % of patients satisfaction about the provided services at Al Awda Hospital – Jabalia and Al Awda Health center - Rafah # of patients participated in the project monitoring and evaluation through phone calls and questionnaires. Monitoring and Evaluation: UHWC will follow up and monitor the project activities through the MEAL plan which will be prepared by the UHWC MEAL Officer to ensure the progress of activities according to the action plans. Once the project approved, the project coordinator will conduct a meeting with UHWC project staff through ZOOM platform, to preserve physical distancing in connection with the infection conditions of the COVID19 pandemic, where he/she will present the project activities, goals, indicators and results to the project staff to prepare action plan and to distribute responsibilities and duties. The project coordinator will follow up and monitor activities on the ground on a daily basis through direct contact with the project team. UHWC project coordinator will conduct regular monitoring zoom meetings and field visits to the project locations to insure continuous flow of information on the progress of activities, and to see work on the ground. Reports and statistics will be sent from the field to the project coordinator where all statistics will be desegregated according to age, gender and PWD. The Project coordinator will compare the financial plan with the real expenses as well as action plan with the achieved activities in order to find out if there is a deviation in the implementation of activities according to the time plan and the financial cost. UHWC project department will coordinate with the donor for issues related to the implementation of the project. The UHWC and CBBS project team will also document the project activities through photos and audio-visual report that will be published at the UHWC website, facebook and YouTube channel at the end of the project. The beneficiaries will also participate in the monitoring and evaluation of the project, as 50 phone calls will be conducted with the project beneficiaries to monitor the project services at the mid of the project where necessary modification will be made to the project activities if necessary. While 100 questionnaires will be filled to measure the beneficiaries' satisfaction about the provided services at the midterm and end of the project.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Within the policies and the principles of UHWC in involving the local community to participate in all stages of its programs and projects that are implemented, UHWC adopts complaints and feedback system, which collect the data, complaints and any suggestions from the beneficiaries and the community members through using the following tools: 1- The complaints boxes, which are located in all UHWC's health and community facilities.2- Generic email3- Hotline.4- Paper-based or electronic questionnaires, which are distributed to measure the beneficiaries ’satisfaction about the provided services in the UHWC’s facilities. Complaints management mechanism:Complaints are managed through the following mechanism:- Enable receiving complaints from affected population.- Responding to complaints.- Accountability and lessons learned In order to engage the local community and encouraging them to file complaints, UHWC announces complaints mechanisms and tools through social media. Moreover, the receptionists in each of UHWC's health facility inform the public face-to-face about the complaints mechanisms in the event that any violations of the beneficiaries are being addressed while receiving services. Through these methods, the beneficiaries' satisfaction can be measured in addition to the most important problems they face while receiving services from the UHWC’s facilities. However, the feedback is taken by the beneficiaries to determine their suggestions to improve the quality of the services in terms of both quantitative and qualitative. Whereas, beneficiaries can use e-mail to file more sensitive complaints about sexual exploitation while receiving services. Special committee from UHWC manage these complaints, as the committee send reports about the beneficiaries' complaints to the Executive Director to solve them and to use them in designing future intervention strategies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The UHWC adopts the principle of equality and impartially in providing its services to beneficiaries, especially marginalized groups that need humanitarian interventions to improve their health and humanitarian status. UHWC provides its services without any discrimination on the basis of age, sex, religion, ethic, race or political affiliation. Knowing that through this project the UHWC will receive the lists of beneficiaries from the Ministry of Health, and to achieve the principle of impartiality in the provision of services UHWC will provide the surgical services to all those mentioned in the lists on the basis that each patient has its individual characteristics and special needs, especially individuals with disabilities, children and women as the most vulnerable in the society and who need special attention and protection depend on Human dignity, confidentiality and non-discrimination. Where the UHWC provides its services according to the slogan (health service is a right to whoever needs it). As well as, CBBS services will also be provided based on the patient’s need and without any kind of discriminations.Access to the project services will be easy according to the following: Accessibility: The Union of Health Work Committees will provide the project services through two health facilities (Al Awda Hospital in the North and Al Awda Health Center in the south governorates of the Gaza Strip). The geographical locations of these centers are characterized by their presence in the middle of the governorates, which is easily accessible and is known to the public since thier establishment. The project services will be provided to the target groups including disability in a safety and dignity manner, and without any discrimination. Access to information: Based on the principle of transparency with the local community, the project will be announced through UHWC and CBBS facilities. The project banner will be located at the waiting areas of the UHWC health facilities. The project will also be announced through the UHWC website and social media. Availability: Health services are provided in Al Awda Health Center from 7:30 am to 8:30 pm, where they provide all medical services and medical support services in addition to awareness services for the families. While Al Awda Hospital services are provided 24 hours, 7 days a week. Affordability: All the project services will be provided totally free of charge in link with the project's objectives on the one hand and the needs of the beneficiaries on the other hand. Acceptability: All the services that the project will provide express an urgent need for the beneficiaries, making it more acceptable as a priority of the community and does not conflict with the values, principles, ethics and customs of the community. Note that the project team consists of both sexes (male and female), which contributes to its social acceptance.
