Project Summary

The goal is to provide portable drinking water, gender sensitive latrines, wash hand facilities and sanitation pads through the construction of bore holes, gender sensitive latrines, wash hand facilities and the distribution of sanitary pads to support the vulnerable, IDP’s and their host community, and the government to increase access to drinking water and sanitation and strengthen the sectors managerial capacity. It falls under the UN action plans whose ultimate goal is to save lives and alleviate suffering, support affected population to meet their basic needs, and strengthening vulnerable populations and resilience, by age group and gender, and support national actors to prevent and deal with shocks. It also falls under the sectoral objectives to improve sustainable access to basic sanitation and safe drinking water, gender sensitive latrines, hand washing facilities, sanitary pads and sensitization on hygiene and sanitation to vulnerable people, reduce the risk of poor hygiene related-morbidity and mortality of affected population in the crisis and reinforce the capacity of local actors to improve WASH services and resilience of affected population.
The project covers two divisions namely Menchum and Mezam Divisions of Cameroon. The main expected outputs of the this project includes the construction of three bore holes to increase the access, quantity and supply of portable drinking water, 06 latrines, 06 hand washing facilities, sanitary pads and sensitization workshops to the Bafut Annex IDP students and the host institution PSS Mankon, Baba II and Zha-Biya village. It also entails trainings and rising of awareness for beneficiary communities on drinking water use, sanitation and hygiene, and capacity building for sector management structures.
The project will raise the drinking water access and the sanitation access within the project area. This project is also expected to generate a change of altitude and behavior with respect to hygiene and health within the beneficiary communities and the average prevalence rate of diseases associated with lack of safe drinking water, latrines and sanitation will decline .Thus the project will meet the drinking water needs of host communities and through IEC, will bring a prompt change of behavior with regard to health and hygiene. The school, councils, community interest groups and women associations will also benefit from the project. The project will create about 50 non- permanent jobs during the implementation and 25 permanent jobs upon completion in the maintenance, repair and management of the water.
The estimated cost of the project is $94661.7657045. The project will be financed by donors and self funds. The lesson learnt from this project will enhance preparation of a wider program and ensure better quality at entry.
Knowledge management will be implemented through monitoring /evaluations components to be executed by the project monitoring and evaluation expert. The feasibility studies will generate a considerable knowledge base that will lead to preparation of future strategies and operations.

Besoins / Needs

There is a general low level of awareness on sanitation and hygiene. Out of the 200 households assessed in the three sub divisions only about 30 households have adequate latrines. This is mostly in Bamenda II. It should be noted that in Zhoa open defecation is the general practice. The toilets facilities in all the host communities assessed are insuﬃcient, hence open defecation has remains a normal practice except in Bamenda II. Waste is disposed in the open ﬁeld and this amount to poor sanitation practices for in the Host communities. Use of sanitary pad is low among adolescent girls and women. Out of 100 adolescent girls interviewed only 27(27%) confirmed they use sanitary pads during their periods. The rest (73%) rely on orthodox practices. As concerns hand washing, only 21% of the 100 persons interviewed could cite the five critical moments of hand washing. Finally access to safe water is a very critical challenge for the IDP’s and Host communities as many trek for hours before having access to water and the number of boreholes currently available cannot cater for the need of the IDP’s without queuing. The population of Zhoa (Menchum) and Baba II (Mezam) complained of water having color and smell.
