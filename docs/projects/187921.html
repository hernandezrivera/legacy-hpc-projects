Project Summary

With an estimated 18 per cent of school age children not attending any form of learning , availability of and access to quality education services remains a challenge within Syria that requires an effectively coordinated needs-based response amongst humanitarian education actors.
The complexity of the humanitarian crisis in Syria and the systemic gap between education needs and the education response, demands larger investments in strengthening the existing coordination structures to support its membership to deliver a predictable, efficient, effective and inclusive response that can meet needs and enable evidence-based donor decision making.
UNICEF and Save the Children co-lead at the WOS and Gaziantep Cluster, UNICEF leads the Education sector, with the Ministry of Education as the co-lead in Damascus and Save the Children leads the Education Working Group in NES.
UNICEF and Save the Children, as co-leads of the Education Sector, will continue to support all aspects of the humanitarian programme cycle, advocacy and resource mobilization efforts, promote synergies and information sharing among members at the hub level and information between the hubs and focus on key strategic activities related to the six core coordination functions, namely:
• Support service delivery based on needs;
• Inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis;
• Plan and implement cluster strategies;
• Monitor and evaluate performance;
• Build national capacities;
• Support advocacy efforts
Given that the majority of the response is carried out by local organizations, coordination efforts will be approached through a localization lens.

4 - Needs

Based on an analysis conducted by the Education Sector, it is foreseen that in 2022/2023, education services will continue to be fragmented across the country, accessibility and availability of education services will continue to be limited and the quality of services will continue to not meet expectations. These issues will be further exacerbated by the trajectory of the COVID-19 pandemic, economic duress, insecurity and displacement. At the response level, coordination in Syria is carried out through three main response centres in Damascus, Gaziantep and NES. These three centres respond to different education landscapes through different operational theatres and are brought together through the Whole of Syria (WOS) approach. In 2021, there were over 200 Education Sector/Cluster members across the three hubs. The vast majority (150) of the members are national NGOs, with many international NGOs and most UN agencies working through national NGOs. Through a lens of localization, the Education Sector will continue to prioritize strengthening the capacity of humanitarian actors to deliver a high-quality response and sustain previous education investments at scale. The sector also promotes the response to better integrate the priorities, preferences, solutions and feedback of children, caregivers and educationists. Additionally, the sector will continue to coordinate with local education authorities and, where possible and relevant, build their technical capacity.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented through the WOS Education coordination structure with three response centers under the overarching leadership of Whole of Syria (WOS), with linkages to other sectors (WASH, Health, Protection, Shelter/NFI, Camp Coordination and Camp Management, Early Recovery and Livelihoods). This project covers staff costs related to the coordination functions, including dedicated information management and coordination capacity and the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: (a) support service delivery; (b) inform strategic decision-making; (c) plan and implement cluster strategies; (d) monitor and evaluate performance; (e) build national capacities; and (f) support advocacy efforts. At the WOS level, and under the cluster co-leadership of UNICEF and Save the Children, concerted efforts will focus on enhancing the effective coordination in the three hubs and at the WOS level through: 1. Ensuring the management, provision and use of accurate response information; 2. Developing and disseminating timely needs, response and funding related information;3. Strengthening evidence-based advocacy on attacks on education and other key issues; 4. Promoting exchange of best practices and technical guidance and standards; 5. Representing and advocating on behalf of the sector, including key donors and stabilization actors amongst others;6. Facilitate capacity building with focus on localization

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (high) : Children in insecure areas are denied their right to education due to lack of capacity and ability of education members to respond appropriately and with accountability. Mitigating measures: Members and donors are encouraged and provided relevant information, tools and guidance to actualize a needs-based approach that enables the most severe needs and the most underserved populations to be provided humanitarian education support. Risk (high): Child safeguarding measures are not sufficiently in place and/or not sufficiently operationalized to ensure a safe learning environment. Mitigating measures: Members and local education authorities are trained and provided tools to ensure child safeguarding measures are operationalized in the learning spaces they support and/or are responsible for. Risk (high): Organizations who are members of the Education Sector/Cluster/Working Group do not have sufficient duty of care measures to keep their employees sufficiently safe from threats or to make informed decisions. Mitigating measures: In relevant fora Sector coordinators encourage aid organizations to have Human Resource policies that enable sufficient duty of care. The sector will have limited influence to mitigate this risk as duty of care policies are decided at the organizational level and the sector interacts with education officers and other technical personnel of our members.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by aid workers to the people in the communities they interact with.Mitigation: The Sector will continue to champion that Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. The sector informs members of the importance of PSEA policies and informs members and local education authorities of any available hub level SEA reporting mechanism. The sector will have limited influence to mitigate this risk as PSEA policies are decided at the organizational level and the sector interacts with education officers and other technical personnel of our members.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

It is not clear if the UN cross-border resolution will be renewed, regardless, the sector will continue to advocate for renewal and work with all efforts to ensure that with or without a resolution coordination is effective and efficient. Given the high numbers of NNGOs a key benefits of sector membership are access to Humanitarian Fund (HF) funding. The HF is one of the only pools directly funding NNGOs of the Damascus and Gaziantep hubs and historically does not sufficiently prioritize education. The sector will continue to advocate OCHA to increase HF education funding and find ways to provide access to HF-like funding for NES. The sector will also continue to advocate donors to prioritize unmet needs, underserved populations and frame education services and objectives as longer-term investments. The risk of conflict and displacement remains high in some parts of the country. The sector will support education members with timely and accurate information on needs, provide tools and guidance and carry out ad hoc funding raising efforts for sudden and acute education needs. Attacks on schools continue to kill and injure students and school personnel. The sector has limited influence on preventing attacks. The sector will focus its efforts on promoting mitigation of the impact of attacks and a better response to the consequence of attacks in collaboration with other sectors and engage with MRM4Syria.
