Project Summary

The primary objective of our initiative is to bolster early recovery efforts for civilians by promptly refurbishing essential infrastructure damaged by the February 2023 earthquake and ongoing military confrontations in northwest Syria. Furthermore, the project is dedicated to fortifying protection mechanisms through the enhancement of early warning systems to elevate civilian safety levels. Since its inception in 2013, the White Helmets (WH) have been pivotal in delivering a spectrum of services aimed at augmenting public safety, fostering stability, recovery, and fortifying community cohesion and resilience in host communities and among internally displaced persons (IDPs). These services, traditionally rendered by local, provincial, or national authorities—now either defunct or ineffective in northwest Syria—cover the rehabilitation of roads, health and educational facilities, water and sanitation systems, utility networks, and waste management and disposal services. The region's essential infrastructure has suffered extensive damage due to the protracted conflict, recent seismic activity, and intensified military strikes, further complicated by the resultant debris, posing additional safety hazards.
The WH has a proven track record of successfully rehabilitating a broad range of public civilian infrastructure in northwest Syria, such as the enhancement of road connectivity between Sarmada and Kafr Deryan, thereby improving the mobility of people and goods. Building on this foundation, our project seeks to extend these efforts by leveraging existing resources and acquiring additional equipment. This will enable teams of WH volunteers to mitigate safety risks and restore public infrastructure functionality, thereby ensuring the provision of crucial services and support across 12 priority sub-districts in the Aleppo and Idleb governorates. In collaboration with regional early recovery and protection clusters, as well as local organizations and communities, our endeavor will facilitate emergency road repairs, the restoration of safe water, sanitation, and utility services, and the refurbishment of hospitals, health facilities, and schools. Additionally, it includes the collection, removal, and management of debris and solid waste, benefitting up to 580,000 inhabitants.
Our project is distinguished by its holistic approach to gender equality and social inclusion. We emphasize gender-specific needs and social inclusivity in needs analysis, adapted assistance, and adequate participation. Activities are designed to be gender-sensitive and proactive in addressing gender disparities. For instance, female volunteers play an active role in mine action activities and the early warning system, challenging traditional gender roles in professional contexts. Moreover, our infrastructure rehabilitation efforts, including water systems and health centers, are consciously designed to meet the specific needs of both women and men, embodying the principles of gender-sensitive aid.
Additionally, our project is committed to engaging underrepresented groups, particularly the elderly and persons with disabilities (PWDs), to ensure their perspectives shape project design and implementation. Strategies include enhancing physical accessibility, fostering inclusive decision-making, building capacity on the rights and needs of PWDs and the elderly, and promoting diverse recruitment. Through community consultations and collaborations with specialized organizations, we aim to guarantee inclusive participation and address specific needs, adhering to the principle of 'nothing about us, without us'.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project will support three types of activities under two clusters. Firstly, the project will support emergency rehabilitation of roads, restoration of safe water, sanitation, and utility services, rehabilitation of a number of hospitals, health facilities, and schools, as well as the collection, removal, and management of debris and solid waste across 12 sub-districts. These activities will benefit a population of up to 580,000 people. Secondly, the project will support the continued operation of the WH’s early warning system (EWS), which monitors and alerts users to natural and man-made disasters, including military attacks. It will also deliver training to local communities and humanitarian actors on evacuation routes. Additionally, under this component, the project will support mine action activities to ensure the safety and resilience of the communities. These two complementary elements aim to empower communities with the knowledge and resources needed to respond effectively to crises, ultimately saving lives and reducing vulnerabilities
