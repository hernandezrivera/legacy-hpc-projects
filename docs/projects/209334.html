Project Summary

Since the eruption of armed violence on April 15th, 2023, WASH assistance was highlighted as a priority need in conflict areas and states that have received significant IDPs. Most of the country’s WASH needs are ranked between 3 and 5 on the severity scale (HNRP). In the zones of interventions under this action, the severity of the needs is deep. Pockets in Blue Nile, Sennar, Gedaref, and West Darfur are in level 5 while Al Jazeera, White Nile, Red Sea, River Nile, Khartoum, and Central Darfur are comprised between levels 3 and 4 (HNRP). In addition, the cholera outbreak in the country has impacted greatly the Sudanese. With around more than 8,980 cases as of January 2024, cholera has claimed around 245 lives (WHO). The pressure on available WASH resources has lead to a cholera outbreak (more than 8,980 cases and 245 lives, WHO) due to increased consumption of water from unprotected sources. Since the onset of the crisis, Acted has been conducting both static and mobile WASH in IDP sites and with national partners in conflict hotspots. In 2024, Acted will continue this action to improve the quality of life of newly arrived IDPs in official sites. In coordination with local authorities, other humanitarian responders including Acted’s partners and in accordance with Acted’s assessments and SPHERE standards, Acted will evaluate the existing water supply system, hygiene environment and sanitation infrastructures of each selected IDP sites in the states of River Nile, Red Sea, Gedaref, Al Jazeera, Sennar, White Nile, Blue Nile, Central and West Darfur. With a team of trained WASH experts and engineers, Acted will either construct or rehabilitate WASH assets in official IDP sites to improve access to water, hygiene and sanitation for all IDPs. Typically, Acted will rehabilitate durable toilets and showers, while constructing additional emergency ones to respond to all the needs of newly arrived IDPs. Acted will improve general hygiene of the camps by constructing handwashing stations when they are lacking. In parallel, hygiene promotion will be ensured through the distribution of water kits to the most vulnerable households (including chlorination tabs) as well as individual hygiene and dignity kits. This will ensure that displaced individuals and households have access to clean drinkable water and personal hygiene items, thus reducing the risk of cholera spread. Hygiene promotion conducted with partners and established WASH committees will enable to spread key hygiene messages to the whole camp population which will enable to improve sanitation and hygiene l conditions in the camps. These awareness-raising sessions will follow a social behavior-change approach to maximise its positive impacts. At the same time, Acted will ensure adequate water supply to the most at-risk individuals through water trucking to locations hosting displaced persons that lack safe access to water points. In conflict hotspots, Acted will provide technical and financial support to its national partners that are the frontline responders in states like Khartoum or Al Jazeera. Acted will provide sufficient financial means and technical supervision to rehabilitate WASH assets and ensure water supply. In addition, Acted will provide a financial grant for these frontline responders to distribute necessary hygiene and dignity kits to conflict-affected populations.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs). Program Implementation Phase: - Acted mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during community sensitization and trainings. - waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites. Accountability: - Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Beside its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design. Human resources: - Acted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. - For the intervention, Acted will set up a gender- balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, Acted will promote an equal gender participation.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.
4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. The creation of WASH and Water Committees will also enable the communities to voice their concerns and provide feedback on the activities. These established community-based structures will be included in the decision-making process whenever possible to ensure community members' feedback inform the activity implementation.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment: - Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. - Training program related to safeguarding issues. - Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7,000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning and during implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available to support individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
