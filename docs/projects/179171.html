Project Summary

The IRC will target some of the most vulnerable families in NW Syria through multi-purpose cash assistance. The IRC will aim at supporting families meet their most urgent basic needs through a one off cash assistance of a 100 USD, that is the full amount of the SMEB value. The IRC will monitor local markets pre and post project implementation to ensure the functionality of local markets as well as post distribution monitoring to ensure meeting project objectives or families’ meeting their most urgent needs.

4 - Needs

Protracted displacement, the depletion of productive assets and savings, limited economic opportunities and the widespread destruction and contamination of agriculture-related infrastructure have had a profound socioeconomic impact on the population. More recently, this has been compounded by the significant depreciation of the Syrian pound (SYP). In some cases, chronic levels of deprivation have contributed to adoption of harmful coping strategies, such as reduced food consumption; deferment or delay in seeking necessary medical care; reduced hygiene practices, which in turn increase public health risks; the spending of savings and the accumulation of debt. Consultations with communities seeking to sustain or rebuild their lives indicate that access to income and livelihood opportunities and basic services are among their primary concerns.3 These unmet needs lead people to resort to increasingly exploitative and hazardous activities as their financial and material resources are exhausted, especially after displacement. Such negative coping strategies are likely to disproportionately affect the most vulnerable individuals, including IDPs, female headed households, pregnant/lactating mothers, child-headed households, elderly people, and PWDs

5 - Implementation of activities

Multipurpose cash assistance targeting vulnerable IDPs and host community families to enable them cover some of the most urgent basic needs. The IRC will give out a one off cash assistance of a 100 USD based on the harmonized SMEB value. The IRC will conduct a pre and post market assessment to ensure basic commodities are available and at a reasonable price in the local markets. The IRC will conduct exit interviews at the site of distribution and PDMs after 2-4 weeks of cash assistance with the selected head of households to ensure accessibility, appropriateness of the activity, check on any inclusion or exclusion errors, and monitor any taxation cases.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

One of the risks that was outlined in the Protection Risk Analysis (PRA), is the perception of biased beneficiary targeting and selection. IRC has found this individuals especially believe either host, or IDPs, are favored during humanitarian activities. During project design and planning, IRC considers do no harm principals and conflict sensitivity concerns, including a solid understanding of the number of IDPs in a targeted location, vulnerabilities of both IDPs and host community, and the level of integration they have in the community and markets. IRC does not target activities solely for host or IDPs, but instead included and encourages both host and IDPs in every project. Anecdotal end-line data shows that this is very beneficial to IDP’s integration in a community. To address the perception of bias, IRC conducts hoise to house registration, and conducts secondary and community verification for cases that lack sufficient identification documents. Selection of beneficiaries is conducted by staff in Amman, not in the field, to ensure transparency. Information sessions conducted during registrations clearly outline the eligibility for a certain project, and the beneficiary feedback mechanism establishes a redress process for any individual (who was part of a registration, or just a community member) who feels there was bias. The IRC has identified the risk of beneficiary exploitation, particularly related to perceived power that would-be beneficiaries think local authorities have over registration and selection processes. Exploitation can include sexual exploitation by local authorities or staff. The IRC has conducted PSEA trainings for all staff, including field staff.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The IRC has identified the risk of beneficiary exploitation, particularly related to perceived power that would-be beneficiaries think local authorities have over registration and selection processes. Exploitation can include sexual exploitation by local authorities or staff. The IRC has conducted PSEA trainings for all staff, including field staff. Confidential and trust worthy reporting mechanisms have been shared with all staff, and both staff and beneficiaries are encouraged to use the IRC’s beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism. When a complaint regarding protection concerns, including sexual exploitation is found, this complaint is handled and investigated sensitively per best protection practices

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks to project implementation include loss of access to project areas due to conflict (inter armed groups or due to Government of Syria offensives). As mitigation, IRC conducts pre-project security assessments that includes risk of future offensives. IRC will have ‘exit plans’ for all locations and planned measures to implement to ensure staff safety and duty of care, building on lessons learned from the South Syria offensive in summer 2018. Another high risk is interference by armed groups or local councils, who may attempt to control or influence projects by requesting or demanding IRC to take certain actions. IRC adheres to humanitarian principles and the established Joint Operating Procedures for northwest Syria. IRCs well-resourced humanitarian access team will conduct principled negotiation and conflict resolution in order to continue project plans and meet the identified needs of a community. IRC has seen success in leveraging local communities to put pressure on armed groups to drop their demands. When no negation is possible, in order to abide by humanitarian principals, IRC will withdraw from the area. There is a risk of local authorities or armed groups targeting IRC beneficiaries for taxation or exploitation. IRC holds door to door registration for the MPCA projects and conducts community and secondary verification for individuals who lack identification documents.
