1 - Needs

• The most vulnerable HHs are using negative/unsustainable coping strategies like using investment loans for food consumption, selling productive assets, cutting wood in remaining forests to produce charcoal, eating seeds put aside for the following agricultural season, harvesting and eating immature crops, using forecasts of future agricultural production as loan collateral, and joining gangs (men/boys). • The least crisis- and disaster-resilient households have no or few economic assets. They do not have savings and access to credit, no income-generating activity (IGA), and/or live in an “ajoupa”, precarious structures made from local materials like branches and leaves. • Men are typically less vulnerable in the face of crisis because they are more able to secure loans, experience less insecurity, more able to migrate alone to seek sources of income elsewhere, and have more diversified IGA options. • Particularly vulnerable members of the targeted population are people with disabilities, single-headed households, especially girl/woman-headed households, elderly women and men, female widows, and adolescent pregnant girls.

2 - Activities

Goal: Increase HH and community ability to cope with hazards, shocks, and stresses of current and future crises/disaster. Beneficiary numbers: PARC will target 25,045 women and 10,734 men from HHs who have moved into or at high risk of moving into Phase 4. Livelihood Development: This project proposes to support livelihood development through VSLAs created by CARE since 2009 and that are now regrouping over 75,000 and 15,000 members, respectively in Grand’Anse and the North-West. Key activities include (1) building the capacities of existing VSLA groups in financial literacy, business planning, and negotiation with value chain stakeholders; (2) supporting VSLA-led business development and market analyses of resilient livelihoods; (3) providing financial and technical support to support new livelihoods. Market Systems Strengthening: Agricultural markets operate efficiently when critical resources, notably technical training and support to farmers, access to credit and financial products, and established relationships between farmers and farm product traders, are readily available. However, these resources are not readily available as the problem statement above notes. Building Community Awareness/Mobilization: As part of the DFID-funded resilience project, CARE has developed the capacities of CDRMCs whose immediate and efficient responses were highly praised after Hurricane Matthew.
