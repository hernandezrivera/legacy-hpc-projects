Project Summary

The poor humanitarian situation in Rubkona, Koch counties in unity state and Fangak in Jonglei state is largely driven by influx of returnees and refugees running away from fighting in Sudan, worsening acute food insecurity, economic deterioration and effects of floods causing risks of disease outbreaks. The health situation remains poor as communicable disease burden is still high with malaria accounting for more than 45% of all morbidity, ARI 19% and diarrheal diseases 10%. Gunshot wounds from sporadic localized fights and accidental injuries are reported and floods have displaced reptiles leading to increase of snake bites, difficulties of access and major constraints to the referral system to secondary health facilities as roads are muddy/damaged or whole communities are isolated by flood waters. The tough economic situation South Sudan faces has weakened the ability of State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County health department (CHD) to support direct health services delivery including monitoring and supervision of quality of care. Moreover there are many health facilities not incorporated into the HSTP under UNICEF which will continue to experience shortages of essential medicines and medical supplies, nonpayment of staff salaries and operation support for these health facilities and if no humanitarian support is provided will likely return to non-functional status.
Women, children, elderly and people with disabilities (PLWD) are more vulnerable and worst affected by humanitarian crisis. World relief proposed interventions are developed to contribute directly to the health cluster/health sector strategic objectives of improving access to life saving essential health care services to these crises affected vulnerable groups in order to reduce morbidity and mortality. The project will provide services that in summary will include Outpatient Department (OPD) consultations to treat common morbidities and injuries, reproductive health services covering antenatal care/delivery services/post-natal care, childhood immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, integrate SGBV/CMR/PSEA and mental health, pharmaceutical and medical supplies support, community health promotion and risk communication, detection and response to outbreaks through surveillance and support strengthen the referral system to secondary health care. A multi-sectoral approach integrating health, nutrition and cross-cutting SGBV/CMR/PSEA/mental health while complementing access to other services is critical part of this project in addition to upholding localization, capacity building and working closely with local county health departments/authorities, beneficiary communities at County and Payam levels. All activities will be complimentary and working keenly to ensure that duplication is eliminated as much as possible and contribute to optimized use of available resources across the health sector.
While working with the communities, the project will establish Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms. World Relief will sensitize the communities on the services available including their rights to safe, equitable and quality services. The project will provide channels for the communities to provide feedback and launch complaints. This will include suggestion boxes provided in each facility and outreach site, telephone services where project participants can call and launch their complaints. Regular meetings will be conducted within the health facilities and outreach sites to receive and respond to complaints.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to struggle with a severe health crisis. South Sudan Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (HNRP) 2024, estimated that 6.3 million people will need health services of which 3.2 million will be targeted requiring a budget support of $ 116.4 million. This crisis and humanitarian needs are largely driven by influx of returnees and refugees fleeing the fighting in Sudan, acute food insecurity, economic deterioration, effects of harsh floods with the resultant risk of disease outbreaks. A combination of these multiple emergencies has eroded the coping capacities of the communities leading to the increasing humanitarian needs. The health situation remains poor with malaria the leading cause of illness and death affecting half of the country’s population, low measles vaccination rates leading to frequent outbreaks and persistent cholera and Hepatitis E cases. Infant and child mortality is high as well as one of the highest world maternal mortality ratios of over 789 per 100,000 live births. In the three counties of Koch, Fangak and Rubkona, Communicable diseases remain burdensome with malaria accounting for over 45% of all morbidity, followed by ARI 19% and diarrheal diseases 10%. Local county health cluster coordination meetings and IRNA 2024 assessment reports indicate increasing cases of snake bites, sporadic gunshot wounds from localized conflicts and accidental injuries . This challenging scenario is further compounded by difficulties of accessibility worsened by floods, challenging referral for severe illness cases to secondary care facilities due to damaged and muddy roads plus poor state of local canoes with risk of drowning especially in Fangak county. With the current South Sudan economic meltdown leading to nearly doubling of prices, the State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County health department (CHD) capacity to support health services delivery is further weakened. Boats, canoes, and walking are the only means of transportation in some counties like Fangak. Inability to procure fuel for speed boats or hire canoes severely affects CHD capacity to implement monitoring and supervision, hinders last mile transportation of supplies to facilities and referral of patients with severe medical conditions. With a number of these health facilities not incorporated into the HSTP under UNICEF, shortages of essential medicines and medical supplies, ability to pay staff salaries and operation support for health facilities will be practically difficult for CHD to provide in order to keep the health facilities delivering the required basic health services in-line with ministry if health standards

2. Project activities

- In Rubkona, Koch and Fangak counties, World Relief will provide support to a total target of 129,852 people with health services. Sixteen static health facilities will be supported, and five mobile outreach teams will provide mobile integrated outreaches to locations isolated by floods and distances. Nine static facilities in Rubkona, 5 static facilities and two mobile teams in Fangak and 2 static facilities and 2 mobile teams in Koch County- In collaboration with the CHD will hire healthcare workers meeting SMOH staffing standards to ensure quality of care provision. Coaching of staff on-the-job, refresher training to enhance their capacity will be done utilizing MOH guidelines. Timely payment of health workers salaries for a motivated work force. - Climate resilient health service delivery: World Relief is looking at climate adoptive health services delivery in areas of operation given the persistent flooding for years in these locations. (1) Areas isolated by floods, bad roads and distance, mobile health outreaches will be adopted. To improve access for the elderly and disabled, pregnant and lactating women and young children, WRSS will deploy mobile outreach teams to take integrated services closer to the people. (2) Mobile accommodation-WR will procure small camping tents for mobile team members to sleep in while they spend more days/nights out in the distant outreach locations. (3) World relief intends to procure one speedboat ambulance to support referral in Fangak county and one vehicle ambulance to support referrals in Rubkona IDP Camp. (3) Supported facilities at risk have dykes around to protect them from flood water and that health facilities are maintained on highland areas where flood water will not easily reach to destroy them. This will reduce the cost of repeated heavy rehabilitation needs ensuring longevity of the supported health facilities. - Disease surveillance to monitor/detect/report outbreak risks through the South Sudan EWARS/IDRS in collaboration with CHD and support by WHO technical focal persons. - Community health education and surveillance that will be conducted by home health promoters (HHP) who are part of the Boma health initiative workers (BHI-W) government strategy to ensure dissemination of key health messages, create demand for health. This HHPs are selected from within the beneficiary community and will always remain to help in the community even if WRSS exits programming in those locations- Essential health care services support. The health facilities and mobile teams will be supported to provide essential life-saving primary health services as per the Essential Package of Health and Nutrition Service of South Sudan. This will include consultations and treatment for communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases, first aid support and treatment of injuries. Maternal-child health services and immunization against vaccine preventable diseases will all be provided. - Interagency Emergency Health Kits (IEHK) and Reproductive health Kits (RHK) will be sourced through WHO and UNFPA core pipeline support. World Relief will also seek to procure additional medicines and supplies with donor support which will enable World Relief to mitigate the risk of stock ruptures ensuring that facilities and mobile teams are well prepared to respond to health needs within their areas of coverage. - World relief will integrate basic SGBV services-CMR/PSEA awareness, and Mental health & Psychosocial support into routine health delivery process. World Relief will integrate Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention and response activities focusing on the safety and needs of women and girls.
