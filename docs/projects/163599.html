Project Summary

The proposed project’s objective is to enable conflict-affected populations to meet their basic needs and provide vulnerable individuals with support when they experience harm, while promoting a transition towards early recovery and resilience-strengthening in the medium term. This is the year 3 of a project which started in 2018 and its strategy is aligned with the signature outcomes of the IRC Strategic Action Plan.
Project background (Year 1 and 2) - Since 2018, the project took a staggered approach across the two implementation zones: early recovery programming was first piloted in Mayo Sava, and then rolled out in Logone et Chari by the 2nd year. During year 1, the IRC focused its activities to ensure that people are meeting their basic needs and avoid using negative coping strategies via Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance. In year 2, the IRC transitioned to the generation of income and assets through a labor market-based intervention (entrepreneurship trainings, facilitation to self-employment).
Proposed project (Year 3) - In order to respond to the most urgent needs, the IRC Cameroon programme will prioritize life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to under-served, crisis-affected areas, including hard-to-reach areas in the Far North and Southwest (SW) & Northwest (NW) regions. In the Far North, the IRC will program in three most affected departments – Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga, but will prioritize its response according to changing needs and zones with populations that are most impacted by armed group incursions and violence targeting civilian populations and, so, will remain flexible to shift its programming in these three departments.
In order to achieve the 1st signature outcome “People meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategies”, , and linked outcomes below, the IRC will implement cash-based interventions to support households to meet their immediate basic needs, re-establish their livelihoods, and avoid the use of negative coping strategies. In terms of water, sanitation and hygiene (sub outcome 4), IRC will seek to protect people from WASH-related diseases by ensuring they can meet their essential water needs through cash-based programs and rehabilitation of non-functional water infrastructure, have access to needed hygiene items, have knowledge of safe hygiene practices, and access to critical sanitation infrastructure.
To achieve the 2nd signature outcome “People are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm” and related outcomes, activities will focus on provision of critical information, reduction of protection risks through community-based protection activities, supporting access to basic services for persons with specific needs, including provision of GBV prevention and response services.
The intervention will target IDPs, host community members, returnees and unregistered refugees. IRC will use both an individual targeting approach (through case management, PSS, legal assistance) and a community-based approach.

Besoins / Needs

The project will primarily be based on the two outcomes that support IRC Cameroon’s two Signature Outcomes: People meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategies and People are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm. Activities will ensure the following sub-outcomes: - Women, men, girls and boys have their social services and urgent protection needs met - Community members protect themselves and each other and follow the rules of law - State and non- State actors have access to information about and know the protective rules and laws - Service providers construct or rehabilitate water points that fulfill the needs of people who use them - Women are substantially represented in water management committee and involve in water system design and management - Individuals know their entitlements regarding access to clean water and hold service providers accountable - Health facilities have necessary facilities and materials to improved hygiene behaviors - People have the knowledge and are motivated to practice improved hygiene behaviors - Pupils, parents have a knowledge to improve sanitation behaviors. - Households construct and maintain hygienic latrine. For the WASH component the IRC will work on rehabilitation of water sources in community, health centres and schools to ensure provision of safe water used and/or construction of new one wherever necessary according sphere standards. IRC will work to reduce the open defecation practices by family latrines construction. IRC will include the WASH voucher modality response the WASH voucher modality response for a period of 6 months to ensure that households have means for hygienic practices. The protection support will be provided to persons at risk including victims of Gender Based Violence (GBV) identified through case management. Based on comprehensive individual’s protection assessment, most vulnerable will receive cash and/or vouchers to address risks and barriers to safely access services and meet their basic needs. Moreover, the IRC will continue to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable women and girls by facilitating IRC’s Economic and Social Empowerment (EASE) 3 discussion groups within the Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) as well as support for the start of Income Generating Activities Analysis (IGAs) of youth. The intervention will target IDPs, host community members, returnees and unregistered refugees. IRC will use both an individual targeting approach (through case management, PSS, legal assistance…). A community-based protection approach will be implemented to strengthen existing mechanisms, by building the capacity of community volunteers and protection committees on various protection-related topics (case management, international guidelines to prevent and respond to GBV, referral tools and pathways, and psychological first aid (PFA)). Case management and psychosocial support services to person at risk including survivors of GBV and legal assistance will be offered in safe spaces and through mobile response. This protection intervention will enable IRC to respond and protect in a sustainable manner the most vulnerable person in the most affected communities who continue facing violence, forced displacement, GBV related violence and many other rights violations. Risk reduction initiatives will be targeted at all community members and towards humanitarian actors operating in these regions targeted with a focus on improving safe and equitable access to life-saving services for all vulnerable persons, and highlighting specific protection concerns.
