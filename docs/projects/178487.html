Project Summary

This project is a hope to improve the lives of more than 111744 IDPs (Men: 18624, Women:
19830, Boys: 35892, Girls: 37398) stayed camp cluster in northern Idlib in the last six
months, which poses an additional burden on the area. The targeted 85 random camps in Aleppo and Idlib governorates are either an extension for pre-established camps or new camps established by the IDPs in haste to find a place to stay and start a new life, the camps were added in ISIMM database, and the project will establish an effective camp management system to support the humanitarian efforts and organize the work by an effect. The camps are the last resort of the people who left their homes and assets searching for a safer place away from the danger to the lives of their children, women, and the elderly.
SECD is aiming by the support of the donors to put an end to the threats to 111744 IDP’s
lives and dignity by implementing complementary activities to improve the targeted
camps' infrastructure, ensure provide protection for the women, children, and elderly with
focusing on the inclusion mainstreaming for the people with disabilities through the
following key activities:
-Establishing camp management and training them on the necessary knowledge to targeted camps
-Prepare a fire response plan in the targeted camps by establishing of 121 fire point
extinguishing points and training for a fire safety plan.
- Provision of comprehensive support to existing or establishing fire mitigation and response system
in IDP sites.
-Developing fire warden systems, creating/supporting fire committees, which will be responsible for providing awareness sessions for IDPs on fire hazards and how to respond to incidents.
- Provision of fire response resources (such as fire extinguishers, fire blankets, fire extinguisher tools, and first aid kits) about 22000 fire banket will be distributed
-Provision of integrated care and maintenance through the inclusion of camp management to basic
infrastructure in IDP sites.
- The camp management respective committees will be supported and equipped to conduct cleaning and/or small maintenance for drainage systems, reinforcing areas of the site impacted by flooding or other incidents through small scale intervention, ensuring access to facilities (latrines, showers) for women and children, mitigating protection risks by improving and maintaining lighting, fixing fence, organize camp gate in sites and refilling fire extinguishers and replenishment of other fire response
-Provision of relevant vocational training and tools to camp management committees to
participate in the infrastructure upgrade/repairs in the sites. This would also potentially
contribute building a skilled-workers portfolio for Cash-for-Work initiatives.
All the activities will be done based on the needs, and SECD with coordination with the CCCM cluster will choose the camps of the urgent need for a response.
The intervention targeted the first priority camps in need for the proposed activities according to the respective cluster recommendation and this was supported by the in-depth need assessment conducted by our MEAL department
This intervention is saving lives considering the protection and focusing on a cost-effective and sustainable solution to be provided to the displaced people who are living under the danger of being infected by the fire incidents, the flood, and the inability to access the basic services

4 - Needs

On March 04th, 2021, the MEAL department at Syrian Engineers for Construction and Development (SECD) conducted a needs assessment in the self-settled and planned camps in Jisr Ash-shughur district (Badama, Janudiyeh, Darkosh, and Jisr Ash-shughur) sub-districts and in Jebel Saman district (Daret Azza and Atareb) sub-districts to assess the most challenges in terms of camp management and fire point plans’ services and determine gaps and the main needs of IDPs living in the camps. therefore, identify the overall activities related to the mentioned sector and the proper intervention to mitigate the effects of displacement on the people. The assessment is at KIIs level and the total sample size is 146. KEY FINDINGS 1- In March, CCCM reported on wind incidents that impacted 168 sites as well asfires in 18 sites and floods in 15 sites.2. In regard to camp committees, analyzed data show that 86% of camps have no committees at all however 14% of assessed camps have committees according to the KIIs.3- According to the KIIs, 98% of the assessed sites in Jebel Saman reported that they do not have any fire points while 1.6% of them mentioned they have. The in Jisr Ash-shughur about 92% mentioned they have fire points while 8% said no.4- According to the KIIs, 98% of the assessed sites in Jebel Saman reported that they do not have contingency plan while 1.6% of them mentioned they have. in Jisr Ash-shughur in general overview about 89% mentioned they have fire points while 11% said no5-There are no warehouses and management offices at 98% of the assessed camps in Jebel Saman’s camps, the result in Jisr Ash-shughur is 96%.

This would also potentiallycontribute building a skilled-workers portfolio for Cash-for-Work initiatives.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

SECD will adhere to the following elements in its implementation of the project: 1 –Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: by considering this important concept in the design of the project, SECD is supporting the people in need in a dignified manner and the intervention is to support the people in need without resulting and negative effects on the targeted beneficiaries and communities.2- Meaningful Access: SECD will consider that access is a priority especially for the PWD, women, and girls 3- Accountability: SECD will establish a compliance mechanism in order to allow the beneficiaries to share their feedback and concerns about the project ( WHATSAPP hotline- FGD – email – field visits of the M&E teams – compliant boxes) 4- Participation and empowerment: this proposal is a result of FGDs with the women, girls, men, and PWD in the targeted areas, and this community engagement approach will be a fundamental factor in all the project stages, SECD is committed to mitigating the following risks 1- The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse during the shelter rehabilitation: All SECD staff are receiving regular training on the code of conduct and PSEA policy and they signed on these policies, The contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration with a specific clause on PSEA with an emphasis on mitigation measures and sanctions in case of abuse by an employee, Beneficiaries will receive information on the CRM and the expected behavior of SECD staff and its contractors with an emphasis on how to report any SEA incidents. A woman staff is present during all visits to women and child led-households

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to the targeted locations will be still available.- The people in the camps acceptance for the project.-The border between Turkey and Syria still open.-The targeted locations are secure and accessible.- The assumption is that humanitarian principles are applied and adhered to for all stakeholders.-The needed materials are available in the local market. Risks and mitigation (Mitigating Measure): Risk (1): Outbreak of conflict prevents access to project locations, Likelihood of Risk Occurring: low, Effect on Expected Results: Very High, Risk Management Strategy/Mitigating Measure: Lesson learned from 2013 operations found that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days. A cautious approach that plans for these types of delays is expressed in the project work plan. Risk (2): Application of humanitarian principles: The risk of interference and aid diversion by armed groups exists, Likelihood of Risk Occurring: LowEffect on expected Results: Very High.Risk Management Strategy: high coordination with the AWG, signing MOUs with the local council and As a mitigation measure, SECD will monitor and report any allegations or instances of interference or aid diversion. SECD also engages with AWG to share information on access- and risk-related developments in NWS. Risk (3) : COVID-19 Outbreak in the target location. Likelihood: Low - Impact: High:Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid COVID-19 outbreak during implementation, social distancing at least 2 meters, providing masks and gloves for all persons involved in project activities (field staff, vendors, drivers..etc).
