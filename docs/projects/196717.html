Project Summary

In collaboration with the partner Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) and the municipality of Qteilbiyyeh, the distribution of food vouchers is expected to guarantee food security, support and improvement of agricultural production according to indications of the Food Security and Agriculture cluster and a professional training program. The initiative will strengthen the resilience of about 15% of the local population selected from among the most vulnerable people, favoring the economic recovery of this rural area 50 km from Lattakia.

3 - Needs

The rural sub-district selected according to the ABA (Area Based Approach) criteria is Qteilbiyyeh, 50 km from Lattakia, has 29.048 inhabitants of which 14.255 need food and agricultural safety aid, with a UN severity scale FSA of 3 (severe, HNO 2023). Furthermore, the severity scale for agricultural production is now equal to 5 (the worst) and that for the increase in local prices is equal to 4. At the local level, the population survives mainly from family farming (90%), from state or parastatal jobs (50%) and from small businesses / crafts (30%). However, the hyperinflation of recent years, the serious fires that hit the area in October 2020 (15% of the land still unusable), the difficulty of being able to afford adequate seeds or agricultural equipment, the lack of basic services (eg. electricity), etc. it has led the population to neglect their agricultural plots, to sell agricultural goods to survive by entering a vicious circle that no longer allows them to live in dignity. Even those who have a state job are no longer able to meet basic needs, becoming in fact a "poor worker" who needs help. At the level of local supply of water for agriculture there do not seem to be any problems since the district has artificial streams and lakes.

4 - Implementation of activities

Increase the protection of the Syrian population most at risk and strengthen their resilience, with particular reference to food security. # 300 families (1800 beneficiaries) who received regular (8 months) additional food support, through food vouchers. At least 10% will be families with disabled people. The value planned is 300.000 SYP a month for an average family of 5 members (as per Recommendations for Food Assistance cluster FSA). Among these 300 families at least 30 will be households with one or more members with disability. # 500 families (3000 beneficiaries) were trained on sustainable agriculture # 500 families (3000 beneficiaries) who received agricultural inputs (seeds, equipment, nutrient for the land, etc.) through specific agricultural vouchers (one-off distribution). # 125 people including young people (18-25 years), disabled (at least 10%), female heads of households and the unemployed were trained in specific technical skills for access to work. # 80% have increased their professional technical skills by at least 30% # 120 people (at least 60% attendance at the courses and positive pre-post test) received voucher grants to buy professional kits to start a professional activity.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection risks are the ones related to food and agriculture inputs distribution processes, with women and girls particularly exposed, and possible tensions between IDPs or returnees and the host local community. For this reason, we selected beneficiaries based on needs, i.e. to be food insecure or low income household, or women-headed household, and we will put in place specific mechanisms (already in use in the current similar project in East Ghouta) like an hot line number distributed with an informative leaflet of the project. Moreover, AVSI together with SARC will use a monitoring questionnaire as community back mechanism to receive complains or suggestions. Finally, all activities are directed and tailored to be inclusive and accessible for women, elderly and persons with disabilities and to follow the principle of do not harm. For this last reason all the necessary measures against Covid-19 of personal protection during training, distribution and counselling will be take in place.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AVSI is ensuring that all staff and volunteers involved in this program are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassments (PSEAH), Child Safeguarding Policy, Code of Ethics and the internal reporting mechanisms for CP and SEA cases, through the mandatory annual trainings and the signature of the code of conduct and the SG bulletin. AVSI has worked on enhancing its internal PSEAH minimum standards through participation in trainings provided by UNICEF in 2020. For this project AVSI will reinforce field monitoring, outreach activities, awareness among personnel, beneficiaries and local populations regarding PSEAH, limitations of the risks through officers' field constant presence and work (including reporting and referrals).


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The situation in the area of intervention is relatively stable. AVSI is in continuous contact with its main partner SARC, the cluster FS and UNDSS and it receives periodic specific reports about the situations in the country. AVSI has specific Security Guidelines and Procedures for Syria and specific measures are in place for Covid-19 crisis and for possible field missions (use of PPE, specific AVSI driver and car; security check during the trips, Whats App updates). Thanks to the collaboration with SARC, the access to the beneficiaries is guaranteed, the identified areas are selected according to needs of the population. The improper use of livelihood and agricultural inputs donated is a risk considered: in this case, a fundamental part is played by the daily mission on the field planned by AVSI and SARC, by the strict contacts with the community leaders, by explaining to the beneficiaries during the training the procedures and objective of the project and the availability of a reserve list which will allow us to substitute any intentional or unintentional misuse of the inputs. HR is managed by a specific AVSI HR officer to avoid delays and to face internal queries and guarantee staff well-being. Finally, from a financial point of view, all the expenditures related to the project are sent on a monthly base to HQ and to the regional office to enable the revision of the accounting.
