Project Summary

Following our assessment and the studies that conducted by specialized engineering teams and technical staff to understand the situation of the proposed camps in northwest Syria and the main challenges faced by the displaced people in these camps. Several suggestions were set for each camp, which are considered real solutions for these challenges, and alleviate suffering of the displaced people in the suggested camps. Nasaem Khair will select the IDPs who are in a desperate need of support and living in the worst camps in NW Syria. In the suggested camps, Nasaem Khair will ensure shelter is provided to the newly displaced families in an accountable manner as part of the response to the emergencies in the existing IDP sites.
As a part of the contingency planning, Nasaem Khair will also establish a solid fire fighting system including fire extinguishing points to extinguish fires if they break out in the camps, and items to be distributed to the IDPs to prevent and fight fires incidents that occurring due to multiple causes such as the misuse of heating materials. We will also install many tents with dimensions of 20*10 to be used as warehouses and to store all types of assistance in addition to the camp assets.
Furthermore, Nasaem will allocate teams of experts to periodically evaluate the situation in the affected camps, thereafter, Nasaem will systematically follow up by providing care and maintenance services for just to name a few for the destructive drainage network and water collection areas on the sides of the roads to manage the flooding, as well as damaged tents' ground insulation, open spaces and roads affected by the floods due to heavy rain. These activities will be made in line with the recommendations provided by the CCCM cluster to protect the tents from flooding and rainwater entering into them. Nasaem Khair will also backfill the excavated holes in roads and opens spaces to facilitate movement of IDPs and implementation all the activities with no obstacles in the targeted camps. In addition, we will set many tents 6*4 that will be used as classrooms for elementary students in the camps. Nasaem will also fix the impairments in the gates and fences of the targeted camps to ensure all the protective measures are in place. Nasaem is planned to regularly inspect the status of segregated latrines to ensure the privacy of the residents, and to equip them with all the necessary equipment. The maintenance services will include monitoring of the solar energy system for lighting and water distribution. Also, bathroom blocks with a solar water heating system will be monitored and maitained with the necessary equipment.
Moreover, We will provide training to the Camp Management Committee on the effective management of the camp and PSEA issues to continue managing the camps after the end of the project.
Nasaem will select the people most in need among IDPs to ensure that the project will meet the needs of the most vulnerable using the following criteria:
- Female headed households caring for children
- Child headed households
- Elderly and disabled headed households.
- Families have people with chronic diseases.
- Single headed household.
- Family has a large number of individuals with no income.
The selection of those beneficiaries will be done by Nasaem gender-disaggregated MEAL team, beneficiaries will be selected according to these criteria where a prioritization will be given according to vulnerability criteria then beneficiary's registration list will be established and verified.
Nasaem will closely coordinate with CCCM cluster and all the NGOs/agencies working in the same geographical areas to avoid any duplication/overlapping in implementation of the project. These activities will provide a dignified shelter for IDPs in the targeted camps to ensure long-term solutions set in place for IDPs who suffer from protracted conflict.

4 - Needs

As the conflict in Syria reaches the 10th year, many people are exposed to protracted as well as multiple displacement. Some 56 per cent of IDP households in the north-west have been displaced for five years or more, while a further 31 per cent have been displaced for one to five years. North-west Syria witnessed a complex pattern of displacement, as in addition to the needs of protracted IDPs, there were large numbers of new displacements. CCCM tracked over two million movements from January to the end of October 2020, a large percent of whom are women and children. The top three priority needs reported for newly displaced IDPs in 2020 was shelter, cash and voucher assistance, as well as safety and security. Many fled their homes within the space of hours without any belongings while others managed take with them what they needed to survive, believing that they would not be coming back. The number of displaced people increased significantly, with many IDPs forced to seek shelter in planned camps, informal camps or settlements, transit/reception centres. The ongoing conflict has forced families from their homes, with not even the most basic of necessities available to them. Of the four million people in northwest Syria, an estimated 2.7 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. However, within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is in fact deteriorating amidst and increase in people in need in addition to significant overcrowding in Idleb and north-west Aleppo. Furthermore, needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newly displaced IDPs requiring core, supplementary and seasonal assistance.

Nasaem will Set a solid fire fighting system to prevent and fight fires incidents that occurring due to several causes such as the misuse of heating materials. Furthermore, Nasaem will allocate teams of experts to periodically evaluate the situation in the affected camps, thereafter, Nasaem will follow up by providing care and maintenance services for the damaged tents' ground insulation, open spaces and roads affected by the floods due to heavy rain. These activities will be made in line with the recommendations provided by the CCCM cluster to protect the tents from flooding and rainwater as possible. Nasaem will also backfill the excavated holes in roads to facilitate movement of IDPs and implementation all the activities with no obstacles in the targeted camps. In addition to setting many tents 6*4 that will be used as classrooms for elementary students in the camps. Nasaem will also fix the impairments in the gates and fences of the targeted camps to ensure all the protective measures are in place. Nasaem is planned to regularly inspect the status of segregated latrines to ensure the privacy of the residents, and to equip them with all the necessary equipment. The maintenance services will include monitoring of the solar energy system for lighting and water distribution. Also, bathroom blocks with a solar water heating system will be monitored and maintained with the necessary equipment. Nasaem will train the camps' managers and leaders in the targeted camps about effective management of the camps to maximize benefiting of the existing resources and meeting the required standards to manage the camps in a good manner.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: A breach of ethics. Mitigation: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, and ask them to sign them. Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. Mitigation: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the area based on the updated service map. For the establishment of the new camps, Nasaem will ensure that the sites of the camps are safe as much as we can and shouldn't be proximal to frontline areas. It might be a risk of inter-community tension between the old and recently arrival IDPs due to varied assistance packages across persons, Nasaem will promote the effective contribution of communities’ actors in the camps and will coordinate closely with the cluster and use the CCCM cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content to avoid the tension that may arise. If different to recommend standard, we will identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Nasaem will ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Nasaem will make its best to inform all the targeted households by multiple tools. There might be a risk of occurring fire in the camps. Nasaem will ensure to set adequate fire fighting points in the targeted camps as well as all the required procedures are taken place to mitigate the risks in case of emergency cases. Risk: Occurance of PSEA cases. Mitigation: Nasaem will train and follow up the camps' management and provide close monitoring and technical support to to increase their awareness of PSEA cases.
