1 - Needs

DRC WASH department was set up in Tripoli over the spring/summer 2020 as part of DRC’s COVID-19 response and is carrying out a portfolio of programmes of approximately USD 1.2 millions. DRC ongoing WASH programming is focused on the provision of essential WASH services in underserved communities in Tripolitania. Moreover, the DRC WASH team is also carrying out rehabilitation works in around 17 health facilities and schools, in priority areas hosting large numbers of IDPs, refugees and migrants. Through the ongoing WASH programming in Tripolitania, DRC is building an understanding of the WASH needs on the ground, mapping the key WASH local duty-bearers and testing the room for manoeuvre to implement WASH infrastructural improvements serving informal settlements and occupied buildings.Due to the challenges people face in identifying accommodation in Libya as well as their limited financial capacity, many IDPs, migrants and refugees reside in impoverished and derelict locations in Tripolitania urban areas, which tend to present the most critical needs in terms of WASH services. Neighbourhoods hosting large numbers of IDPs, refugees and migrants living in informal settlements and unfinished buildings are therefore the priority areas for DRC integrated response. According to the WASH sector HRP exercise for 2021, the overall target of the WASH Sector includes around 265,000 individuals. The Mantikas of Tripoli and Benghazi are hosting the highest number of PIN (People in Need): 40,672 and 37,064 respectively. The same HRP exercise has identified Tripoli and Benghazi as the Mantikas hosting by far the highest numbers of migrants in need of critical WASH assistance. During the 14 months of armed conflict, intense clashes had advanced to densely populated suburbs of southern Tripoli including Ain Zara, Abu Salim, Salahuddin and Qasr Ben Gashir, leading to severe damage of critical WASH infrastructure. The main water supply infrastructure for the Western mantikas (the Man Made river) was severely affected by years of looting, lack of maintenance and used as a military target during the conflict. According to Abdullah El-Sunni, head of the Tripoli-based water authority, as of July 2019, 101 of 479 wells on the western pipeline system had been dismantled. Moreover, the water flow to Western Libya had dropped from 1.2 million cubic meters per day to about 800,000 cubic meters per day in July 2019 (33% decrease), due to sabotage and lack of funding and maintenance. According to the preliminary results of REACH MSNA conducted in 2020, the Western Mantikas are highly dependent (>80% of the Libyan respondents) on bottled water for drinking purposes. A comprehensive assessment conducted by UNICEF on the water supply system in Benghazi has shown that the water supply scheme of the city has been significantly affected by the conflict, with around 38% of the wells destroyed, one third of the chlorination systems non-operational and only two thirds of the 36 pumping stations in the city functioning at their full capacity. The distribution network, completed in the 1980s, does not cover large parts of Benghazi’s urban sprawl which developed in the last 40 years, and is now depending on illegal rudimentary connections, private wells and water trucking. The preliminary results of REACH MSNA conducted in 2020, found Benghazi among the 6 mantikas, out of 22 assessed, with the highest percentage of non-Libyans reporting no access to drinking water, and around 40% of Libyans families with inconsistent access to the public network in the last 7 days. The number of COVID-19 cases has exponentially increased in Libya throughout August/September 2020, with Benghazi, Tripoli and Misrata being the largest epicenters of infection. According to the preliminary results of the COVID-19 assessment conducted by Voluntas, the lack of access to water is the primary barrier to handwashing for around 80% of the Libyan and non-Libyan population.

2 - Activities

DRC WASH response will include: • The provision of the basic humanitarian WASH assistance for marginalized communities of migrants, IDPs and refugees living in informal settlements and unfinished buildings. The full WASH package includes desludging, waste collection, temporary sanitation facilities, hygiene promotion and provision of hygiene items.• The rehabilitation of the water system and main water sources, as well as the extension and rehabilitation of the water network to ensure safe water supply in underserved neighborhoods and priority areas, is of the outmost priority in the context of the ongoing COVID-19 response. Strengthening of the local WASH duty-bearers through the provision of essential supplies, to ensure the sustainability of the intervention. The rehabilitation of sanitation facilities for key areas will also be considered whenever feasible.• Interventions to increase access to safe water, functional sanitation facilities and dignified hygiene standards for children in their learning environment. • COVID-19 awareness raising and provision of COVID-10 PPE in prioritized areas.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.2) The improvement of WASH services should include the surrounding host community, if similar levels of critical needs are identified. An area based approach is being developed by DRC to mitigate this sort of risks.4.4) Distributions of hygiene items and awareness sessions are organized to ensure COVID-19 preventive measures are in place.4.8) The limited focus of the WASH Sector strategic objectives and activities for the HRP 2021 on durable solutions will increase the likelihood of this risk. DRC will advocate for more durable solutions to be adopted and include this type of programming in its fundraising strategy.4.13) As mitigation risk, DRC includes GBV considerations into the design of WASH facilities and services. As an example, the location of sanitation facilities should be safely accessible, gender separated and provided with locks for privacy and safety. GBV considerations are factored through inclusive programming, referrals and close coordination with DRC Protection departments.4.16) Strong coordination mechanisms with other actors; including early warning systems, rapid needs assessments to highlight advocacy needs.4.17 and 4.18) The provision of basic WASH services in illegally occupied buildings is often obstacled by the lack of formal rental agreements. On a case by case basis, DRC seeks engagement with the municipality to advocate for at least basic services to be provided in informal settlements and occupied buildings and clarify land tenure issues. 4.20) MRE is part of DRC/DDG overall ability to respond; outreach messaging will be conducted to increase awareness of target populations of risks and identifying markers of ERWs/UXOs. Coordination internally with DRC/DDG mine action for information about potential contaminated areas, and increased coordination with other mine action actors.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

DRC is currently in discussion with WFP on signing the CFM (Common Feedback Mechanism) agreement. Parallel to the CFM, DRC has put in place a strong internal complaint and feedback mechanism accessible in all areas of intervention, allowing for the participation of the targeted group in the services and assistance delivery.The DRC CFM is managed by the MEAL department and includes a helpline of which the number is systematically communicated to targeted populations. DRC registers and addresses all requests and regularly reviews and highlight learnings from received feedback throughout the project cycle. Learnings inform current and future programming and allow for increased engagement with the supported groups.In addition, an internal accountabiltiy framework is under development, to allow the increased participation and engagement with beneficiaries and community groups into the design, implementation, and feedback on proposed activities. These include regular assessments, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews, throughout the project cycle.
