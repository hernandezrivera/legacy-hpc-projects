Project Summary

The on-going conflict between the SAF and RSF in Sudan has resulted in massive damage to public infrastructure and basic amenities with approximately two-thirds of clashes during the first weeks of fighting were in cities with populations exceeding 100,0004. Neighborhoods, critical buildings, and essential infrastructure in Khartoum and other major cities have experienced devastating consequences, including the airport, presidential palace, ministries of defense, energy, education and health, and several hospitals. Fighting outside Khartoum has centered on urban areas along major roadways.
Several cities have faced heavy bombardments and clashes, and associated unexploded ordnance, causing hundreds of civilian deaths and casualties, as well as damage to critical water, health care and power infrastructure. The fighting has also reached West Darfur, South Darfur, North Darfur, South Kordofan, North Kordofan, Blue Nile, Kassala and the Red Sea. Since 24 April 2023, hostilities between SAF and RSF have ignited intercommunal violence in West Darfur, particularly in the state capital of Ag Geneina, resulting in numerous casualties and displacements, and widespread looting.
The conflict, like many others have generated significant amount of debris and waste that impedes movement of people and the provision of life-saving assistance including passage of emergency supplies. In addition, the mass displacement of huge number of people into already vulnerable communities creates a huge amount of waste and debris as many seek shelter in schools, public buildings, and other community shelters. In addition, public buildings have been occupied by military and armed elements including schools, healthcare facilities that have been devastated and need to be restored for civilian use soon as possible.
UNDP follows the disaster waste management guidelines developed by the Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB), which define debris as “a mixture of building waste and rubble typically arising from damaged buildings and their demolition. This waste stream can include natural materials such as clay and mud, trees, branches, bushes etc.
Leveraging UNDPs experience in emergency debris management in several countries, this project aims at improving the food and livelihood security of conflict-affected population by proding emergency of cash or vouchers through cash for work in debris removal, enabling vulnerable people, including IDPs, returnees, farmers and herders to access markets and purchase food and other essential goods and services such as agricultural inputs, livestock to enable them restore food production. It will target about 10,000 people of which at least 45% are women and girls. Of these at least 3000 will be vulnerable host community members; 3000 IDPs and 3000 returnees.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project contributes to the Strategic Objective 1 of the FSL Sector. It aims at improving the food and livelihood security of conflict-affected population through the provision of emergency of cash or vouchers through cash for work for debris removal, enabling vulnerable people, including farmers and herders to access markets and purchase essential goods and services as well as restore agriculture production. Activities for this project will include the following: 1. Carry out infrastructure damage assessment and make recommendations for the demolition of the severely damaged ones in order to reduce the risk of causing further damage to civilian population. 2. Launch Cash for work activities for the clearance, removal, and disposal of debris from major streets and public facilities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This Project will mainstream environmental protection in all activities to ensure that the removal and disposal of debris have no negative impact on the environment. UNDP will also apply a conflict sensitive approach to planning and implementing the project, ensuring the expansion of agriculture production and other livelihood opportunities do not create and or exacerbate existing natural resource-based conflicts.UNDP follows the disaster waste management guidelines developed by the Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) and the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB).

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention will mainstream protection concerns in cash for work activities in order to address, mitigate and reduce risk to particular vulnerable groups especially women. UNDP will prioritize safety, dignity of vulnerable groups and avoid causing harm. UNDP will also ensure a transparent selection criteria and participatory processes, promote equitable and impartial access for all groups, enhance accountability. UNDP will also specially target and promote the participation and empowerment of the most vulnerable groups especially the Youth and women and take steps to address structural obstacles to their meaningful participation. The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of IDPs and refugees to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce social tension and promote social cohesion. Capacity building trainings of community institutions that will be established to management the project such community management committees (CMCs), beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, refugee rights, gender equity, SGBV, rights of people living with disabilities among others. The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need will be incorporated in all stages of project implementation. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. FSL activities will carry out a conflict-sensitivity analysis by understanding the impacts of assistance on the socio-political schisms that cause, or have the potential to cause, destruction or violence between groups. The Do-no-harm analysis will help to understand the complexity of the conflict environments in all project locations so that we can see how FSL decisions we make affect inter-group relationships.
