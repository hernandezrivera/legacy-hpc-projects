Project Summary

In alignment with the Shelter Cluster (SC) 2019 strategy, objective 2, to decrease vulnerable households (HH) exposure to harsh weather and protection concerns, Development and Peace (D&P) Catholic Relief Services (CRS) and Bayader for Environment and Development Association (Bayader) propose urgent shelter rehabilitation and NFI assistance for 300 HH over 12 months. Completed works will translate into decreased reliance on yearly assistance and a reduced caseload for the humanitarian community.
Focusing on the distinct needs of women, girls, persons with disabilities (PwD), and the elderly, this intervention uses an approach of accompaniment to promote gender equality and full participation of all household members. The project features an easily scalable approach that emphasizes shared household decision-making and adoption of safe, environmentally responsible shelter practices.

Results from the 2018 Islamic Relief (IR) Community Vulnerability Mapping Study show that Khan Younis governorate has the highest percentage of HH (33%) living in vulnerable localities among all governorates in the Gaza Strip. More specifically, the data on economic vulnerability—that considers living conditions, including types of housing, average area of the home etc.—shows that Khan Younis has the greatest number of localities deemed economically vulnerable. CRS will thus target Khan Younis governorate, focusing on the most vulnerable HH living in deteriorated shelters and prioritizing female headed households (FHH), HH with PwD, and the elderly.
To fully engage all HH members, particularly women, girls, PwD and the elderly, local partner Bayader will recruit and train a gender-balanced intern team comprising fresh graduates in engineering and social work to accompany households. Intern teams will provide technical guidance and facilitate discussions to ensure rehabilitation plans reflect all HH members’ needs. These discussions aim to promote gender equality through shared decision making. Additionally, interns will gain valuable, real-world professional experience, thereby improving their livelihoods and that of their family, while increasing their future employability.
The intervention will build on over 5 years of CRS’ experience in shelter programming in Gaza, emphasizing high engagement of females, PwD and the elderly, as well as improved environmental, health and safety practices. Future learning will be shared with the SC, with the aim of greater adoption of gender and environmentally-responsive approaches by other shelter actors.
With the option of multi-year funding, CRS would endeavor to influence behavior change by working with other shelter actors to establish best practices in gender-responsive and environmental-responsible approaches relevant to the Gazan context.
This project is a partnership between D&P, CRS, and local partner Bayader. The collaboration between D&P and CRS is rooted in the global Caritas network, where both organizations are partners and work in a consortium providing global humanitarian assistance. Bayader is an experienced D&P and CRS partner with ongoing shelter project activities in Gaza, with demonstrable success.
• D&P’s primary responsibilities will include donor reporting, compliance and program oversight.
• CRS will be responsible for program quality, beneficiary selection, beneficiary accountability, monitoring and evaluation, liaising with the SC, adherence to protection principles and overseeing environmental, health and safety standards.
• Bayader will be responsible for program implementation, including community engagement, project monitoring and managing the livelihoods internship component.

Needs

According to the Shelter Cluster (SC), an estimated 23,300 households (HH) are living in high to severe substandard housing and need rehabilitation; the highest percentage (29%) located in Khan Younis. Approximately 8700 HH are at risk of exposure simply due to inadequate climate protection. In an August 2017 rapid assessment CRS identified over 9,000 HH living in substandard housing units and disaster-prone areas, and provided 7,153 HH with NFI in the form of e-vouchers. These HH fall within the SC vulnerability criteria and their housing units meet fewer than half the SC minimum standards, posing significant health and protection risks, including seasonal displacement. Conditions in these homes include exposure to flooding, leaking roofs and uncovered windows. A family suffering from seasonal flooding would benefit from simple rehabilitation measures such sealing off entrances to water intrusion, repairing roofs and covering windows. These measures would provide long-lasting relief and reduce the risk of displacement including seasonal movement. For poor families, responsive measures such as these are beyond their means yet completing them could mean numerous gains; and for the humanitarian community, a reduced caseload. Further, some 92.5% of HH in Gaza are subject to shelter vulnerability. Basic provisions such as mattresses, blankets, tarps and gas cylinders (NFIs) would enhance emergency preparedness and reduce their exposure to harsh climatic conditions while preventing seasonal movement. The shelter needs of men women, boys, girls, persons with disabilities (PwD) and the elderly, are unique and distinct. CRS’ shelter experience and community consultations reveal that: • Women prioritize kitchens, bathrooms, and privacy • Men prioritize electricity, insulation, and high ceilings/roofs • Children/Adolescents desire space to play, locks on doors for privacy and adequate lighting for homework • Elderly and PwD require accommodations such as ramps, handrails and wider doorways to facilitate access and mobility These differences emphasize the importance for rehabilitation plans to reflect shared decision-making to meet each members’ needs. Because substandard housing disproportionately affects women, girls, PwD, and the elderly—as they spend most of their time at home due to cultural norms or limited mobility—their input is vital in shelter-related decisions. In CRS’ current programming emphasizing female engagement, over 95% of women and girls report that shelter improvements reflect their distinct needs. A recent SC study shows that vulnerable groups such as widows, FHH, PwD have limited knowledge of humanitarian services, which frequently results in unequal access to assistance. A recent CRS-led gender analysis confirmed that men are perceived to be the most appropriate gender to procure humanitarian assistance. FHH and PwD must often rely on others, particularly male relatives, to access assistance. This finding further underscores the importance prioritizing these groups. As such, BNF selection will include:1. Preliminary lists from MoSD/MoPWH/CRS’ existing database – prioritizing FHH, PwD and elderly2. Local committee input 3. Shelter technical assessment (physical conditions-as per SC minimum standards)4. HH social assessment (vulnerability/protection criteria-as per SC guidelines)5. HH Scoring (All scores weighted based on shelter conditions and vulnerability criteria, as per SC vulnerability criteria and minimum standards. Priority given to FHH, PwD, elderly.)6. Cross-check with SC7. Final BNF selection

Activities

CRS will rehabilitate 300 substandard shelters; prioritizing FHH, PwD, and elderly and providing NFI options inclusive of the assistance package. 1. Climate Comfort: Sealing entrances and windows, securing doors, roof repair. Avg/HH $2000 USD. NFIs up to 10% of assistance.2. SC Minimum Standards: Wall and roof repair, fixing locks, mounting privacy walls, installing handrails for PwD and elderly. Avg/HH $4000 USD. NFIs up to 5% of assistance.Experience from current programming confirms that HH need ~$4000 USD to safely and responsibly rehabilitate homes up to SC minimum standards. Completed rehabilitations will translate into decreased reliance on yearly assistance and a reduced caseload for the humanitarian community. Activity 1: Community Consultations & NFI Set-Up CRS will conduct sex-segregated community consultations to identify the most relevant NFIs. Building on CRS’ electronic voucher program, staff will pre-vet vendors to maximize competition and allow beneficiaries (BNFs) to negotiate prices. This scalable modality promotes emergency preparedness for BNFs and prepositions CRS to respond immediately in an emergency. To mitigate selection errors, sex-segregated community committees will review preliminary BNF lists and provide feedback. As part of CRS’ protection mainstreaming strategy, each HH will be scored based on a technical and social assessment. Scores will be weighted based on housing conditions and vulnerability criteria—prioritizing protection-related cases (i.e. FHH, PwD, elderly). Activity 2: Orientation.Sex segregated orientation for both spouses covering: • Climate comfort options• Protection rehabilitation options (securing windows, privacy walls, handrails)• Environmental power options • Rehabilitation modalities (self-help/contractor)• NFIs • Feedback mechanism NFI orientation for BNFs opting for NFIs covering:• How to use e-vouchers • Rights & responsibilities of the card user • Feedback mechanism HH headed by PwD and elderly will have the option of a home visit for the orientations.Activity 3: Internship Program: CRS will include a 9-month livelihood opportunity for Gazan youth. Local partner Bayader will train a gender-balanced intern team of engineer and social work graduates on protection, gender and GBV. Interns will:• Accompany HH to develop rehabilitation plans that reflect shared decision-making and consider all HH members’ needs• Advocate on behalf of BNFs to receive fair market prices • Advise families on protection, environmental, health and safety practices• Promote CRS’ feedback mechanism A gender balanced team ensures females have someone they can work with directly, respecting cultural norms and facilitating women’s full participation. Activity 4: Rehabilitation & NFIs Staff will review plans to for alignment with SC minimum standards and inclusion of protection, gender and environmental measures. After validating plans, BNFs will choose between 2 options: 1.Self Help: Beneficiaries manage rehabilitation; 2.Contractor: Pre vetted contractors manage rehabilitation. Providing a contractor approach provides FHH, PwD with more options and is part of CRS’ protection mainstreaming approach. Project staff will conduct regular monitoring visits to ensure rehabilitations meet quality and safety standards, and that FHH and PwD are sufficiently supported to manage the rehabilitation process. BNF HH opting for NFIs will receive e-voucher cards enabling them to purchase what they need to manage harsh weather and protection concerns. Activity 5: Learning: Over the last 5 years of shelter work in Gaza, CRS gained experience engaging women and girls in a meaningful way and is leading the way for environmentally-responsive approaches. With the possibility of multi-year funding, CRS would seek to influence other actors by developing best practices, documenting lessons learned and promoting behavior change and greater adoption of environmental, health, and safety practices.
