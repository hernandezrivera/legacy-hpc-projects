Project Summary

Since 2018, the security situation in Burkina Faso drastically deteriorated with the expansion and intensification of activities of non-state armed groups (NSAGs), exploiting pre-existing inter-communal tensions. A further escalation was observed in 2020, with a greater toll on human lives and on vulnerable populations. The non-state armed groups (NSAGs) are fragmented, with multiple internal players; and there are increased external players linked with the regional dynamics (i.e. Central Sahel countries). This has led to massive population displacements and has had a severe impact on already vulnerable populations.
As of November 2020, over 1 million persons have been displaced and new displacements are reported daily, marking a 250 percent increase in over a year. The security situation has had repercussions on the overall food and nutritional security of the country. Food security and nutritional situation has been impacted by the growing crisis affecting vulnerable populations. Particularly, the food security situation has observed an almost ten-fold increase in number of people in Cadre Harmonise / IPC Phase 3+ - therefore, people in need of food assistance.
The unprecedented humanitarian emergency has left 2.9 million persons in need of humanitarian assistance – according to the draft revised Humanitarian response plan. Health services, schools and markets have been closed due to growing insecurity in several parts of the country, seriously affecting the population’s ability to sustain healthy lives. Across the country, over 95 health centers have closed and 199 are operating with reduced services, affecting over 1.2 million people. No less than 2,512 schools were forced to shut down, depriving over 350,000 children of education. COVID-19, and measures taken by the Government from March 2020, added another layer of vulnerability.
Humanitarian access to vulnerable populations in need of assistance is restricted by threats posed by non-state armed groups, counterterrorism operations, the presence of Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) planted on roads and the damage or destruction of key road infrastructure (bridges targeted in attacks in the Sahel region for instance). In addition, access is hindered by restrictive counter-terrorism legislation preventing negotiation with NSAGs. Many humanitarian actors have left areas which remain impeded by serious access constraints. To safeguard neutrality and the perception of it, humanitarian workers in Burkina Faso do not use military escorts, particularly since the national Security Forces (FDS) remain the primary target of armed groups.
In order to support the planned humanitarian response and enable access to the populations in need and project implementation sites, UNHAS aims to provide a safe, effective and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescent movement, donor organizations and diplomatic missions.
Upon the need and decision of the Steering Committee, UNHAS currently offers three weekly flights rotations - Dori, Gorom-Gorom, Arbinda; Djibo, Titao, Ouahigouya; Fada N’Gourma, Gayéri – and special flights all departing from Ouagadougou airport. The service is accessible to all humanitarian actors upon registration transporting personnel or cargo aimed to support targeted populations. A cost-recovery mechanism will be developed in 2021. Orientatively, the service will aim to transport over 3,500 passengers and 60 mt of cargo.

2 - BESOINS, ACTIVITÉS ET RÉSULTATS ATTENDUS
2.1. Décrivez les besoins, activités et résultats attendus

Considering the deterioration of the security situation, UNHAS aims to provide a safe, effective and efficient passenger air service to the entire humanitarian community and their implementing partners, principally NGOs, UN agencies, red cross/crescent movement, donor organizations and diplomatic missions. Considering the evolving situation in Burkina Faso, the United Nations Humanitarian Air Service was launched officially in late 2020, with a first flight in October following the acquisition of the necessary flight authorizations from Civil Aviation Authority. This came as a process started in March 2020, with an assessment, from WFP Aviation security, to assess the feasibility of a potential service in Burkina Faso, upon Humanitarian Country Team request. This determined the intention to deploy a helicopter, as it would allow for more direct approach to the centre of interest, not requiring any special airstrip infrastructure nor large use of the armed forces to ensure the security of the area. The use of a helicopter allows improved access during the rainy season compared with fixed wing or road transport; serviceability of the helicopter is less impacted by sodden ground conditions during the rainy season. As a result, a MI-8 MTV - 3mt with 19/20 pax capacity was deployed in August 2020. The service allows to mitigate the humanitarian access constraints faced by humanitarian community in accessing hard-to-reach of the country. Populations in these areas are suffering from a multifaceted emergency – insecurity causing massive displacements of populations and natural/climate-related shocks affecting those most vulnerable. Food and nutritional situation is critical in a country, where approximately 5 percent of the population has been forced to displace. Over 2.9 million people are in need of a comprehensive humanitarian assistance as access to basic services is limited or non-existent. Two platforms have been put in place to manage the service:- UNHAS User Group Committee (UGC) meetings composed of NGOs, UN agencies and Donor Representatives. The role of the UGC is administrative, provides feedback to UNHAS' Chief Air Transport Officer (CATO) to help ensure high service quality and maintains a service-oriented approach to supporting the partners. The UGC are used as a forum where the users of the service can voice their views on operational matters, including suggestions with regard to destinations or other changes to the weekly flights schedule.- Periodic Steering Committee meetings chaired by the Humanitarian Coordinator and composed of some representatives from INGOs, UN Agencies and Donors to define operational requirements and air transport priorities, also to discuss topics of a strategic nature such as funding, and give guidance on UNHAS policies.Upon the need and decision of the Steering Committee, UNHAS currently offers three weekly flights rotations - Dori, Gorom-Gorom, Arbinda; Djibo, Titao, Ouahigouya; Fada N’Gourma, Gayéri – and special flights all departing from Ouagadougou airport. The service is accessible to all humanitarian actors upon registration. A cost-recovery mechanism will be developed for 2021 operations based on decision from the Steering committee. Furthermore, partnership is ensured at local, regional and national level through the different platforms. For each serviced location, a partner is identified locally to serve as a focal point for security-related aspects. Relevant analysis and consultations is ensured in advance and during flights to ensure that a location is accessible.
