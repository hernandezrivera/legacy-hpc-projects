Project Summary

Integration and institutionalization of C4D/Communication with Communities (CwC) approaches and interventions into health, nutrition, WASH, education, and child protection for sustained long-term and at-scale efforts. Strengthening the role of religious leaders towards increasing community ownership and participation in host and camps. Continued support to strengthen community accountability through information feedback centers and closing the community feedback loop. Promoting social cohesion and confidence-building in the host communities through community engagement interventions in Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub-districts. Supporting local governance structures to improve results for children at the community level through the Country Programme Local Governance for Children (LGC) program.

Objectives

Needs

As of April 2019, 40 percent of refugees lacked information about more or better food; financial support, water; aid registration; general information about events around the camps, what was happening in Myanmar; and long-term options for their and their children’s futures. The UNICEF 2018 Knowledge, Attitude, Practice and Behaviour (KABP) baseline survey confirmed the importance of face-to-face communication. Eighty-eight percent of refugees described Mahjis as a source of information. Community mobilizers were the second most common source of information (28 percent) while 20 percent of refugees get information from religious leaders. Ukhia and Teknaf are highly vulnerable upazillas with poor living conditions and coverage of basic services. Livelihood activities and markets have been significantly affected by the refugee influx. Eighty-five percent of participants in a recent survey did not feel safe having Rohingya refugees nearby. They perceive that the influx has led to an increase in the cost of living (79 percent), traffic accidents (74 percent), and crime (24 percent) . Empowering Rohingya refugees and host communities to provide feedback is key to enable a relevant, conflict-sensitive and localized response. Using established feedback systems supplemented with additional feedback through targeted outreach and engagement, such as perception surveys and focus group discussions, will help provide data that can be consolidated and can support sectors’ in decision-making.To address the needs and gaps in integration of gender, age and disability perspectives into the response, UNICEF will: continue to collect disaggregate data by sex, age and disability; ensure gender-balanced teams (staff and volunteers); use mechanisms and create opportunities to engage community members and ensure equal access of women, girls, boys and men in the design, implementation and evaluation of integrated programs. Although a majority of refugees state that they are finding it easier to access information about the basics of services (who, what, where and when) and navigate their way around their camp, there are still many information gaps. For example, the elderly, those living with disabilities, adolescent girls, and women who are unable to attend a listening group or walk around the camp are still failing to get access to the basic information about the delivery of services. It is essential to maintain and sustain community engagement interventions at the camp level through community mobilization volunteers. Language-appropriate radio and offline audio programs and pictorial IEC materials have addressed some of the communication gaps in information provision but continued to be produced for 2019. Accountability will remain a key UNICEF priority in the years to come as well. UNICEF partners to collect feedback, grievances or complaints and respond to them with effective and timely data sharing and evidence generation with other agencies/sectors. While there has been some communication, accountability mechanisms are in place e.g Betar Sanglap with the host communities, there are still information gaps on key issues that will also be addressed in the 2019 UNICEF plan.

Activities or outputs

"i) CAPACITY DEVELOPMENT: - Identify, engage and train community volunteers and staff of partner organizations on IPC and CwC techniques and methods. ii) COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT: - Raise awareness to targeted population (children, women, men) on key behavioral and social change messages on health, nutrition, education, hygiene, sanitation, and protection through the door to door visits, community consultations & community dialogue for sustained behavior change.- Identify community and religious leaders including Imams, Majhis, children and adolescents who have the potential to influence their immediate community; conduct training/ orientation with local community leaders e.g., maji, imams, women leaders, children and adolescent on key Community engagement approaches and messages; and engage local community leaders in designing, development, and implementation of community engagement activities. To mobilize communities on DRR, environment and social cohesion through Interpersonal sessions and wider community consultation. Development of gender-sensitive materials and ensuring sex-disaggregated data is collated and synthesized through online platforms.- Facilitate issue-specific mobilization/outbreak response campaigns to reach a minimum of 75% (each round) of the settlement population with life-saving information. iii) COMMUNICATION RESOURCES: Develop age-appropriate and culture-sensitive local media program i.e IPT show, folk song and Tarana shows, development of public service announcements, partnership with the ministry of information/Bangladesh Betar, Bengali Creative Media & community Radio Naf. iv) ASSESSMENT/ RESEARCHES/STUDIES: Multi-sectoral baseline KAPB study on health, nutrition, WASH, education and protection issues (aligned to the RTM); development of program/ field guidance briefs, development of Language glossary & development of case studies and good practices. v) INFORMATION & FEEDBACK SERVICE CENTERS/FACILITIES: Train staff/volunteers managing IFCs especially in handling feedback and complaints; record and respond to the complaints, queries, and feedback from the affected population; and analyze regularly and share with affected population and management summary progress on feedback/complaints made. Leading and coordinating the Info Hub sub-working group"
