Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide life-saving and essential health and nutrition services for conflict and disaster affected people in need in East Darfur and Gedaref States. The targeted localities are Adila, Abu Jabrah and Bahr Al Arab in East Darfur; East Galabat, West Galabat, Gala Alnahal, and Al Guraisha in Gedaref.
The project will directly support 14 health facilities with the deployment of health workers, provision of essential supplies and standardized service delivery, and scale-up of nutrition services in the targeted localities to mitigate and prevent morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition, Medical Teams International (MTI) will focus on the immediate life-saving nutrition needs that include Out-patient treatment and in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition, treatment of moderate acute malnourished under-fives and global acute malnutrition among Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in prioritized localities. Preventive nutrition Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, emergency supplementary feeding programme (e-BSFP) for under-fives and PLW, within the crisis-hit populations, Food based prevention of malnutrition (FBPM) for under 2 children and PLW at risk, provision of micronutrient powders to children under 5 years (Home Fortification) and salt iodization, treatment and prevention of anaemia, Vitamin A supplementation, and capacity building. To provide life-saving health and nutrition services in difficult-to-reach locations, it will also deploy 4 mobile health units. This project will directly help 307,200 beneficiaries in total. All demographic groups, IDPs, host communities, women, men, individuals with disabilities, and children are among the beneficiaries. The services would be specifically designed to encompass preventive, promotional, and curative actions. The approach is comprehensive including the linkage of services from community outreach to secondary health care ensuring a continuum of care at all levels. The project will also contribute to improving the capacity of local communities and MOH towards enhancing prevention and health care-seeking practices, mitigating, and responding to disasters and outbreaks. This humanitarian intervention will be carried out in close cooperation with MOH, UNHCR, COR, the local community, and other partners. To make sure that beneficiaries are getting high-quality services that fit their needs and ensure their active participation and engagement through the process, the project will include
accountability measures such as feedback channel sat all levels and KPC surveys priority localities. The project will also rely on core humanitarian principles and common humanitarian standards. The project activities and indicators are aligned with the overarching humanitarian framework and objectives. Robust monitoring and quality assurance mechanism will be laid out to ensure the success of this project.

1 - Needs

Accessibility to health services is impacted by the recurring patterns of flooding, drought, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, localized conflicts, and the current economic crisis. According to the Sudan Health Cluster, in 2023 there will be an estimated 10.1 million people in need (PiN). Medical Teams International (MTI) gathered information collected from the state and federal ministries of Health, implementing partners, cluster, and relevant health & nutrition reports. HNO 2023 estimates that 0.77 million people in the Gedaref state are in need, with 70% of them in severe need. In East Darfur, there are 0.61 million individuals in need, 10,000 of whom are in severe need. Without any humanitarian aid, the locals' health and nutritional issues will only get worse, resulting in higher rates of morbidity and mortality. The health systems remain weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health and nutrition services, especially in the localities affected by floods and conflicts, Close to 349,000 people were affected in 16 out of 18 states in 2022 and significant damage to infrastructure including health infrastructure. In addition to that, the country experienced multiple disease outbreaks, including, dengue fever, chikungunya, Monkeypox and malaria and the health system is unable to timely and effectively respond to these health and nutritional crises. Approximately 11 million people are residing in high-risk areas for vector-borne diseases, and a total of 63,280 confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported across Sudan with 4,961 associated deaths. The COVID-19 case fatality ratio (CFR) of 7.7 is one of the highest globally and has been so throughout the pandemic with the vaccination coverage at around 10 per cent of the total population. The country is expected to have outbreaks of endemic water-borne and vector-borne diseases such as hemorrhagic fever and malaria in 2023. There is unequal distribution of health workers in the country. 70% of health professionals work in urban, with 38% of these working in Khartoum, the country's capital. Additionally, there is a substantial gap in the provision of basic primary health care since 67% of the staff provide secondary and tertiary care. Most facilities lack the skills and resources necessary to identify and address trauma, mental health issues, and non-communicable diseases (NCDs). There is a significant gap in Sexual reproductive health (SRH) services, with unnecessary deaths because of inadequate reproductive health services, and the prevalence of contraception is still quite low. The lack of basic SRH services, such as antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), and family planning (FP), means that pregnant and lactating women (PLW) lack access to information and health services at all stages of pregnancy. According to the Nutrition Cluster, in 2023 there will be an estimated 4 million PiN. More than 76% of the population is under 5 years, and 23% of PLW require life-saving care and nutritional interventions. According to the latest S3M (2018) survey, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence among children under five remained at 13.6 per cent. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates in Gedaref are estimated above the 15.4% , emergency threshold.Tentative SENS survey results conducted in Camps reveal GAM rates of 7.9% (MAM-6.4% & SAM-1.5%) & 11.6% (MAM 7.5% & SAM -2.6% in Babikiri and Umrakuba Camps respectively.Despite the high prevalence of acute malnutrition, geographical and treatment coverage for nutrition services is still below 60 per cent. These high GAM rates reflect a serious situation given the vulnerability of refugees and host communities and thus call for costant attention and provision of CMAM programming.
