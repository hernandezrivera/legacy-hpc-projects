Project Summary

The malnutrition situation in Sudan remains dire with an expected increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition due to the deteriorating nutrition determinants as result of the ongoing catastrophic humanitarian crisis. The nutrition sector estimates that 4.7 million children under five and pregnant and lactating women are acutely malnourished and in need humanitarian assistance in 2024.
Through this project, UNICEF aims to (1) increase equitable access to an integrated lifesaving and preventive nutrition services focusing more on the “1000- day window”- as a critical opportunity to prevent child wasting and stunting among under five children, (2) improve early detection of acute malnutrition (3) improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treat severe acute malnutrition among under-five children. UNICEF will target all states in the country with more focus on hotspots and IDP hosting states through scaling up integrated lifesaving nutrition preventive and curative services.
The preventive package includes iron-folate supplementation for pregnant and lactating women, maternal, infant and young child feeding nutrition (MIYCN) counseling and support for pregnant and lactating women. The package for under five children includes the provision of micronutrient supplements, Growth Monitoring and Promotion, as well as deworming. Overall, 1.4 million pregnant and lactating women, and over 4.9 million under five will be targeted with the preventive package. Additionally, UNICEF will support the provision of cash transfers to 150,000 of the poorest pregnant women and caregivers of children under-two to help eliminate the financial barriers that women and children may experience in accessing nutritious diets and essential nutrition services. UNICEF will ensure more focus on IYCF-E interventions as part of emergency preparedness and response, with intensive advocacy and orientations to mobilize humanitarian partners to protect and support infant feeding in the emergency context.
UNICEF will strengthen early detection and referral of children suffering from wasting. The key strategy will be large-scale integrated nutrition campaigns through house-to-house modality to significantly scale up the early identification, referral, and management of acutely malnourished children, in addition to integrating it with other preventive services such as micronutrient supplementation for children and women. Through this, 4.9 million children under five will be targeted with early detection of wasting and referral activities.
UNICEF will work toward the expansion of the geographical coverage of the treatment programme for severe acute malnutrition through the establishment of fixed and mobile sites to reach the planned target of 539,858 children under five suffering from SAM, representing 75 per cent of children under five with SAM in 2024. As part of this, UNICEF will continue working closely with WFP and WHO to ensure the complementarity and continuum of care among the three levels of management for acute malnutrition program. Different strategies will be implemented including (i) maintaining functionality and expanding coverage of OTPs to deliver lifesaving nutrition services and (ii) supporting mobile clinics to reach displaced, hard-to-reach communities with lifesaving nutrition services, and (iii) Expanding active case finding. Additionally, UNICEF will support evidence generation through implementing SMART surveys.
To support the implementation of integrated preventive and curative nutrition services UNICEF will:
•Secure the pipeline of all essential preventive and curative nutrition supplies to prevent and treat children with malnutrition.
•Expand the partnerships with various stakeholders across the country.
•Strengthen the health system through efforts to strengthen supply chain management, nutrition information systems, monitoring, frontline nutrition workers payment and capacity building.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection are one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. UNICEF has zero-tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and harassment. UNICEF has trained and sensitized its staff and implementing partners to report any incident of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence: both incidents related to staff and incidents related to beneficiaries. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners on their sensitivity to the matter, including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy on protection (prevention and response):  The children, women and men UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse.  UNICEF has zero-tolerance for (sexual)exploitation, abuse nor harassment. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors.  All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment.  All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone, or tolerate behaviour that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, and harassment:  An organizational culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero-tolerance for sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented.  Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights.  Swift and credible investigation and sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations.  Survivor-centred response: Provide survivor-centred assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced.  Engaging and equip partners in the fight against SEA and harassment.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Accountability to affected populations is one of UNICEF’s core commitments in humanitarian actions. UNICEF is consistently encouraging its partners at national and sub-national levels to design and implement beneficiary-centered interventions. UNICEF is keen to improve the quality of humanitarian assistance provided through different implementing partners as well as to protect the beneficiaries and ensure that they are receiving basic social services in a safe and participatory manner. Therefore, the establishment of two-way communication channels between the service providers and the targeted beneficiaries is encouraged and highlighted as a priority within UNICEF-supported interventions (taking into consideration that there are variations among the humanitarian actors in terms of the practice and use of different complaints and feedback mechanisms). UNICEF will continue advocating and encouraging different implementing partners to adopt feedback mechanisms appropriate to the context aiming to optimize the ownership and engagement of the communities to UNICEF-supported interventions. Several feedback mechanisms will be adopted such as hotlines, complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbooks, patient exit interviews, and focus group discussions. All received complaints will be documented and verified by UNICEF staff through field visits to the sites to ensure the implementation of the appropriate corrective actions.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Accountability to the affected population is one of UNICEF core commitments in humanitarian actions. UNICEF is consistently encouraging its partners at the national and sub-national level to design and implement beneficiary-centred interventions. Efforts will be made to advocate for establishing efficient feedback and complaint mechanisms at implementing partners project sites to ensure that targeted beneficiaries have suitable means and methods to raise their voice and reflect their ownership of the services provided. Different modalities will be used, such as establishing hotlines, dissemination of a mobile number of focal points for feedback and complaints, provision of complaint boxes at health facilities, community feedback logbook and patient exit interviews. UNICEF is also an active member of theinteragency platforms and is committed to making significant-quality contributions to it. At the global level, the UNICEF’s Executive Director has assumed the role of Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Champion for the prevention of SEA and SH for one year. As part of this role, UNICEF has led the development and adoption of an IASC Champions Strategy on the protection from and response to SEA and Sexual Harassment.
