Project Summary

The project is aimed at providing life saving support to vulnerable IDPs and Host Communities families living in makeshift shelters & with limited access to basic non-food items in Gedo & lower Juba. The project will target those who have continuously been affected by either, the prolonged flooding, displacements due to conflict, drought, forced eviction & the inability to repair or replace their shelters owing to their poor economic situation.
The proposed intervention will support 24,000 vulnerable individuals (4,000 HHs with average size of 6) including 18,000 IDPs (75%; 3,000 HHs) & 6,000 individuals from the host communities (25%; 1,000 HHs) (out of the 62,304 individuals targeted by the cluster (CLSHL/CO1) in the country), as they are also in dire need of timely life saving & life-sustaining emergency shelter support since they don't have ability to repair or replace their shelters. This will be realized by ensuring their access to adequate and dignified shelter solutions through an in-kind response modality where 4,000 basic NFI kits and 4,000 cluster standardized Emergency Shelter Kits will be provided. The target beneficiaries will include; 7,200 men-30%, 10,080 women-42%, 3,360 girls-14% and 3,360 boys-14%. In addition, the beneficiaries selection exercise will try to ensure that about 60% of the targeted HHs will be women/child-headed, 17% has elderly people & 15% of them will have at least one persons living with disability.
The project will adhere to the cluster recommendations to ensure the provision of a habitable covered living space that provides a healthy living environment, enabling the beneficiaries’ to have & enjoy privacy, dignity & protection. The intervention will mainstream gender issues & ensure that the needs of women, men, girls & boys are taken into account especially during critical decision making levels from the initial consultations stage on the needs to the designing & construction/rehabilitation of the shelters.
To promote community participation, HAPEN will ensure active & meaningful engagement of community groups in all aspects of the project cycle from needs assessment, beneficiary selection, response planning & monitoring. This will promote an informed implementation approach that will enable reaching the most vulnerable & needy individuals. While to promote inclusivity of all the affected groups, HAPEN will engage representatives from all the minority groups during key activities such as beneficiaries identification and selection. Accountability to the affected population will be ensured by providing the target beneficiaries with a platform of airing their views & perception with regards to the project. This will be achieved through rolling out a comprehensive feedback, complaints & response mechanism which will be easily accessible to all beneficiaries. This will include among others, a complaint Hotline number that will be displayed on project display materials and also awareness-raising sessions to ensure target population are informed on how they can voice their concerns to project management & raise any positive or negative suggestions.
Accountability to resources will be ensured by developing a comprehensive M&E plan which will entail a baseline survey, technical tracking sheets for distributions, post distribution and construction monitoring, weekly/monthly progress reports, monthly financial reports and a project completion report. After the provision of the NFIs to the targeted population, a PDM exercise will be conducted to inform and assess the effectiveness & efficiency of the intervention. Furthermore, a PCM will also be conducted to assess the quality & standard of the shelters constructed. The M&E plan help to ensure the effective implementation of project activities, assess if the required quality standards are met and help in monitoring the progress in line with agreed milestones and targets.

1. - Projects activities

• Supporting 18,000 vulnerable beneficiaries from 3,000 IDP HHs with the distribution and installation of 3,000 cluster recommended standardized Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) in their existing camps. •Supporting 6,000 vulnerable beneficiaries from 1,000 HC HHs with the distribution and installation of 1,000 cluster recommended standardized Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) in their existing villages. • Distribution of 4,000 NFI kits to the targeted 3,000 IDP HHs (18,000 individuals) and to the 1,000 HC HHs (6,000 individuals). Each NFI Kit to contain a kitchen set, jerry can, solar light, ropes, and plastic sheets. • Awareness raising activities focusing on containing the spread of COVID-19 pandemic and promoting healthy hygiene practices targeting the 24,000 individuals. • Establishment of an effective Feedback, Complaint & Response mechanism.• Conducting a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) within 2 to 4 weeks after completion of NFI kits distribution. This will allow capturing beneficiary satisfaction on the provided support, assessing quality against agreed standards and monitoring progress as per agreed workplans. •Conducting a Post Construction Monitoring (PCM) within 1 to 2 months after completion of all construction work on Shelters. This will help in probing whether the implementation was in line with agreed milestones and targets and will enable the project team to assess beneficiary satisfaction on the provided support.• The above-outlined seven activities will observe the underlying shelter solutions principles and standards in the following ways: - The shelter designs will ensure considerations in relation to climate, topography, hazards and environmental risks, national and international minimum standards, livelihoods, and the local availability of resources i.e. materials, skills and infrastructure. The project will also ensure minimal possible impact on the natural environment. Basic site planning will be considered to reduce congestion and increase adherence to Shelter Cluster and SPHERE Standards relating to provision of essential services, access, drainage, site planning, fire safety, common areas, etc. - To ensure cost-effectiveness and supporting local businesses, the project will prioritize locally available materials to the extent possible, to adequately reflect on the cultural preferences and traditional lifestyle of the target persons of concern. - Promoting active community engagement throughout project implementation to empower target population in building their own shelters with the necessary organizational and material support. HAPEN will empower the beneficiaries to undertake construction of their shelters and repair them in future through onsite training and owner-driven approach by engaging the recipients in constructing emergency shelters.- Engagement of relevant stakeholders including Regional Shelter Cluster, Local Authority, Camp Leaders and Ministry of Humanitarian & Disaster Management.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.4 Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. - to mitigate against this risk, the project will; promote the safety & dignity of target beneficiaries by ensuring that all sites are based in safe locations, are free of hazards and are clearly humanitarian spaces. - Ensure easy access of the services offered by establishing distribution locations that are accessible to PWDs and are located in close proximity to where the beneficiaries reside. - The distribution points will be central with beneficiaries not having to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. 4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area - to mitigate this and avoid potential eviction of the 3,000 IDP families to be supported in their present camps, HAPEN will maintain a strong collaboration with all relevant stakeholders including Shelter & CCCM clusters, relevant line ministries, community leaders and land owners where possible and acquire an MOU or land tenure. HAPEN will ensure securing necessary Land Certificates from relevant local municipalities before project implementation is initiated, to ensure sustainability of the infrastructure and to prevent potential eviction of communities supported through the project.
