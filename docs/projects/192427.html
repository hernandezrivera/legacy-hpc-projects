Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving access to quality, affordable and comprehensive primary health services for vulnerable populations in Lebanon. Given the current deteriorating economic situation in Lebanon – vulnerable populations have become more at risk of being unable to access healthcare in Lebanon. These populations include Lebanese, Palestinian refugees residing in Lebanon, and migrants that are becoming increasingly unable to afford or access healthcare in Lebanon. This compounded with a potential fourth wave of the COVID-19 on the horizon – immediate intervention is required in order to ease the health impact on beneficiaries as well as ensure continuity of operations for Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCCs).
For health activities, IMC will work with 4 PHCCs: Al Moasat PHCC (South), Al Amir Medical Center (Akkar), Nahda Bedawi (Tripoli) and Bar Elias PHCC (Bekaa). These centers have been selected due to the high density of vulnerable Lebanese as well as a considerable size of migrant populations in the areas. Our data has shown a 9% increase in demand for IMC services by vulnerable Lebanese in July 2021 in comparison to the same month the previous year. The support to these 4 PHCCs will include: increasing the hours of operations for PHCCs by 4 hours a day 5 days a week by providing salary support for physicians, nurses, data entry clerks, and other personnel and running costs, secondly, donating essential acute medications as per the Ministry of Public health (MoPH) essential medication list, lastly, IMC will be providing PPE’s for the healthcare providers at the PHCCs in order to be able to provide high-quality services in a safe manner for both the health staff and the beneficiary. IMC will be recruiting and training Lebanese HOVs from the target population in order to disseminate and provide health awareness on IMC services to vulnerable Lebanese communities and migrants - as well as identify cases requiring primary healthcare and referring them back to the PHCC.
IMC Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) team currently support 20 Case Management (CM) teams based in 20 PHCCs across Lebanon aiming to strengthen the integration of MH into PHCCs. CM teams consist of full-time case managers, part-time psychotherapists and psychiatrists. MH services including PSS with case managers and consultations with psychotherapists and psychiatrists are provided to beneficiaries free of charge to beneficiaries from all nationalities and age groups. Cases that require hospitalization are referred to MH psychiatric wards. Under this project, IMC is proposing to complement MHPSS provided at the 20 centers by procuring additional psychotropic medications to be provided as part of MH case management services to the MH beneficiaries from the target population group including Lebanese and migrants as well as assisting coverage of inpatient care when needed. No additional budget is needed for either specialized services or running costs and these medications will be used to complement the services provided for free. Case managers will be able to conduct closed group awareness raising sessions for the target community (which will also target PwDs) to decrease stigma and promote accessibility to MHPSS services.
IMC is planning to procure acute and psychotropic medication which will be donated to the PHCCs. IMC will coordinate with MoPH to complement quantities and try to plug gaps in the MOPH supply chain. IMC patients (14,400) will also benefit from MoPH medications. IMC will run a detailed assessment, quantities will be shared with MoPH for coordination of numbers and specifying the number of PHCCs to be covered as well as the duration of coverage and timeframe. At the PHCC level, medications will be dispensed free of charge and only upon medical prescriptions. IMC has partnered up with agencies that can help with international procurement and will be actively using this chain of supply rather than rely on local markets.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

IMC will procure acute medication and psychotropic medications from the local market to be donated to PHCCs. - Salary support for physicians, nurses, and running cost to allow the 4 PHCCs to operate for additional hours beyond their existing schedule. - Patients will benefit from subsidized consultation at 3000 LBP and will receive medication free of charge. If the patient requires diagnostics (Lab/Imagery) then he/she will be covered under other projects. - 2 Health Outreach Volunteers will be recruited from each targeted area (8 total). HOVs will be trained on key health messages and will provide health awareness in the community and refer cases back to PHCC. - The community health club aims to raise awareness around positive health and wellbeing through a series of awareness sessions and activities conducted at the field level. - Awareness raising activities aim to increase participants’ knowledge on mental health and help participants self-identify or identify others with mental health disorders. Participants attend four sessions that lasted 2-3 hours each. Each awareness session cycle included 10 participants, who are actively involved in the selection of the eight different topics. - Following our discussions with IMC psychiatrists, vulnerable beneficiaries who are in need of inpatient psychiatric care report challenges in covering the hospital charges, even when there is support by other financial entities such as MoPH and NSSF that covers up to 85% of the fees.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

IMC pays attention to ensure that all groups can access to health and mental health services at all of its program sites. At health facilities, IMC circulated new guidelines in 2018 to support protection of beneficiaries’ privacy and rights, particularly for women. As part of its safeguarding policy, IMC developed a safeguarding/protection risk assessment and mitigation measures in order to ensure that all its facilities and programs ensure meaningful access, accountability, participation, and safety of the community members, as well as respect of the do no harm principle. Staff are prone to experience burnout among other stressors as they are sharing the same challenges with the people they are providing services to. IMC aims on extending the already established staff care project, by covering a percentage of the staff care consultant providing individual and group support to IMC staff. Allowing the organization to better respond to the needs of the staff for a longer duration.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IMC Lebanon employs mechanisms and approaches in order to ensure the participation of the affected communities throughout the program cycle. To ensure beneficiaries’ feedback is received on a regular basis, the MEAL department conducts regular patient satisfaction surveys and post-distribution monitoring. Program teams conduct FGDs throughout program implementation in order to collect beneficiaries’ feedback concerning project. IMC Lebanon is currently implementing a Community Based Feedback and Response Mechanism (CBFRM), which is a set of clear, transparent procedures that provide affected communities with access to safe, timely and confidential means of voicing complaints related to IMC-supported services and activities. A series of focus group discussions were conducted with male and female beneficiaries of different nationalities across all areas in Lebanon. Moreover, FGDs were conducted separately for male and female beneficiaries to design the CBFRM based on different needs.
