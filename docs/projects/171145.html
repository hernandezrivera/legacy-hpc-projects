Project Summary

The Overall Objective of the proposed intervention is to support vulnerable communities in Area C of the West Bank by protecting the productive capacity of their agricultural activities affected by the deteriorating socio-economic conditions, Israeli violations against Area C communities and exacerbated by the COVID 19 crisis. The project will be implemented by Oxfam and its three local partners (ESDC, MA’AN and LRC). The project is in line with the country strategic objective three (SOP3): “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued: The project is in line with the Food Security Sector (FSS) objectives 1- “The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected”; 2“Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs” and 3 “FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, in fair partnerships among concerned parties, including civil society organizations, key national ministries and concerned donors’. The project will carry out the following interventions towards the achievement of these objectives:
• Activities that will address Objective 1: Support Area C farmers by enhancing and restoring productive capacities: includes rehabilitation of agricultural lands, agricultural roads, improving access to and decreasing water wastage for agriculture through installing irrigation networks, and carrier lines, rehabilitating water wells to obtain more efficient access to water using renewable energy, and rehabilitation of livestock sheds.
• Activities that will address Objective 2: Creating temporary cash for work opportunities for farmers and agricultural workers, and support small scale farmers/herders' cooperatives to access domestic markets including food parcels purchase and distribution to vulnerable households, and elderly centers.
• Activities that will address Objective 3: Provide legal awareness and legal support to
target communities, in full coordination with Ministry of Agriculture, Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission, land and water authorities, Ministry of Social Development and the Food security sector partners/ In addition to carrying out policy and advocacy
activities that will address the root causes.

1 - Needs

The proposed intervention builds on the current FS intervention implemented by the four partners and will continue to address prevailing needs of acutely vulnerable farming households. The consortium have done a rapid needs assessment RNA in prioritized localities (listed below) in September/October 2020, includes data collection over phones with village councils, field visits to community leaders, meeting, and small focus groups.The RNA in the prioritized communities through field evaluation and monitoring reviews conducted in July and august 2020 respectively under an ongoing projects 1) EU land and water funded project, being implemented by Oxfam and ESDC, RWDS and PFU, and 2) Land and Water Resources Management Program (LWRM 2013-2020 that LRC is engaged in) highlighted key critical interventions that are needed in several locations includes but not limited to: Halhul, Shuyukh al Arrub, Beit Ummar, Al Jiftlik, Az Zubeidat, Jericho, Marj al Ghazal, Marj Naja, Kafr al Labad, Shufa, Tulkarm, Beit Anan, Beit Duqqu, Beit Ijza, Beit Iksa. The priorities highlighted by the communities were as follows: (1) A pressing need to enhance access to their land through opening new roads, and/or roads rehabilitations; land, greenhouse; animal sheds; water wells and water carrier lines rehabilitations. (2)There is an increase in morbidity and mortality among livestock mainly new born livestock due to poor living conditions including poor feeding, insufficient veterinary services that worsened during the Covid-19 outbreak in addition to poor and old animal sheds. (3) The deteriorating economic conditions have led to depriving vulnerable farmers from securing their basic needs which has further deteriorated during the covid19 outbreak causing an increase of farmers’ debts and inability to protect their livelihoods, hence job creation through cash for work modality is a need, as well as food parcels to address the needs in the short and medium run.The meetings revealed that the communities’ needs have increased as a result of the continuous violations, restrictions, harassment and daily attacks practiced by Israeli forces and settlers which sharply increased during the Covid 19 crises "Demolitions and seizures of Palestinian property and humanitarian aid, including Palestinian homes, continue, despite previous commitments from Israeli authorities not to target Palestinian residential structures during the pandemic. According to the 2018 SEFSEC, 10.1% of Area C’s population is severely food insecure. 30% of the total number of severely food insecure households in the West Bank reside in Area C, and according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, the impact of the Covid19 crisis in the West Bank is expected to be fully reflected in the employment statistics covering July-December, as the World Bank forecasts that the share of poor households in the West Bank will more than double by the end of the year, from 14% to 30%. Moreover, for Area C, as reported in the HNO, since January and up to September 2020 at least 82 livelihood structures were destroyed or confiscated by the Israeli occupation, and a lot of trees were vandalized or destroyed by Israeli settlers. Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities are disproportionately affected due to their location in sensitive areas and their reliance on agriculture-based livelihoods, as their sole or main source of income. Vulnerable farmers in Area C continue to face settler violence, restrictions and intimidations. The poor infrastructure and limited access to essential services and support have been exacerbated by the COVID 19 crisis. During the current HF project of 2020, LRC, ESDC and MA’AN, have been in regular consultation with the MoA to coordinate and pre-select the target localities. It was agreed among partners to specify a pool of localities that will be prioritized at this stage (mentioned below), however, the final selection will be conducted at the beginning of the implementation following a set selection criterion which will include: The list of localities will be selected based on the HRP priority areas and the recommendations made by the MoA with regards to fair distribution of resources (filling the gaps in none targeted areas by other actors), criticality of the areas in terms of vulnerability of communities and their exposure to Israeli threats and reported attacks that are registered by the MoA at the Damage Documentation Department (DDD), and finally based on remaining gaps that are not addressed by the FSS partner organizations working in the areas. This consultation and coordination process will be followed by deeper needs assessment at the localities to select the beneficiaries who will be benefiting from the agricultural activities (rehabilitation of agricultural lands, livestock sheds, agricultural wells, irrigation systems) and the cash for work activities. In addition, Area C communities continue to face legal challenges that can be summarized by the following, a) absence/weak of a centralized administrative body providing legal counseling and response mechanisms to address military orders; b) limited knowledge about technical steps to prepare legal cases to challenge military orders, especially new military orders since many amendments to Israeli laws are made c) presence of several entities managing such cases, causing confusion, lack of accountability and mistrust of people receiving military orders towards service providers; d) continued and sharply increasing of IHL violations by Israeli authorities against Palestinian land and housing rights; e) loss of large areas of land, especially agricultural land, due to increasing Israeli stop work orders over the last five years; f) failure to address hundreds of Israeli military orders due to frustration, lack of knowledge and awareness, g) increasing costs of legal aid to defend the right to land and housing.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides wider access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive feedback either online, through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in-line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam has already started adapting its intervention accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19 outbreak. Oxfam’s helpline will be primarily used for reporting any incidents of misconduct fraud, exploitation or abuse which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Beneficiaries will be provided with the information on the available mechanisms and will be encouraged to use them whenever needed. Such information will be also made available in the MoUs that the beneficiaries will sign at the beginning of the project. Moreover, Oxfam will carry out a training on PSEA and safe programming to partners field team to ensure proper understanding on the use of these mechanisms is achieved.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential protection needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessment will take into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on them and will enhance the roles each member of the household fulfills and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers. Prior to the implementation of the activities project partners will be invited to attend a training session by Oxfam’s Gender and Protection Officer on the overall protection principles and on how to detect GBV, this should equip them to conduct risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures that will be taken into consideration in advance. All activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and in full engagement and consultation of the targeted communities. Examples of how Oxfam will ensure 'do no harm' principles are integrated in the project are as follows: 1- Active engagement with targeted communities including their representatives, (village councils, and DoA) and the farmers representatives (CBOs and local committees in the target localities) will be the basis for all steps of the project cycle, that will ensure meaningful representation of women and youth whenever possible to ensure project’s design is responsive to the different needs and concerns of the people we serve. 2- Supporting women in the identification of cash for work activities, and direct support for their farms (herders and agricultural farmers) will follow a quick risk assessment to take place that will identify any potential risks of women participating in the activity and identify means to support them within the boundaries of the project or beyond through a referral mechanism. 3- Cash for work activity will be aligned with child protection policy that will prohibit child labor. It will also promote women participation in the agricultural work that is typically un-accounted for, employing the equal access principle of protection and taking in consideration preserving the dignity of targeted women by tailoring the activity in accordance to what is most suitable for them in terms of timing, form, location, method of payment and consequence household and child care burdens.4- COVID19 precautionary measures and specific project SoPs will be integrated in the project design after carrying out project risk assessment to ensure mitigation measures and scenario planning for various situations of restriction on movement, change in outbreak rates among communities. This is to ensure safety to beneficiaries, project staff contractors, community representatives, and community members in general who are engaged with the project activities. For example, Oxfam requires as part of these SoPs when implementing projects under COVID19 for project staff, suppliers and volunteers to be trained in the use of Personal Protective Equipment and to sign a consent that they agree to engage in any project activity. Oxfam has offered this training to its local staff and partner staff involved in the past and ongoing projects. With regards to PSEA, Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, and confidential councilors who are trained on PSEA and intimidation and bullying where they are responsible for providing capacity building to partners on PSEA and ensuring compliance of all field staff with the Oxfam policy on PSEA. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

At the community level, project local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the intervention, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any bias in selecting beneficiaries and locations, targeting beneficiaries and service distribution. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting awareness sessions to inform targeted communities about services that can be accessed. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committee as well as further exploring women and people with disabilities needs.This will be integrated through 1- Orientation sessions on the project activities with the project community committees, 2- Dissemination of designated feedback mechanism dedicated by the project under the partners and Oxfam responsibility, 3- The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: such as the dissemination of the beneficiary's selection criteria, clarity and orientation of the selection process, announcement to community of activities and ways to receive benefit, etc.4- Project community committees will act as a key mechanism that will facilitate feedback from community members and key project stakeholders to project staff. PCCs will play a major role in the targeting and selection processes of beneficiaries that ensures fair and transparent selection and targeting of beneficiaries and minimizing unintended consequences on the overall community.
