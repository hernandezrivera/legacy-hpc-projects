Project Summary

In 2021, four of the 5 counties that CARE works in are projected to have proxy GAM levels above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. These are: Rubkona (25.00%), Pariang (19.59%), Koch (17.80%) and Abiemnhom (15.28%). The only exception will be Mayom which has a proxy GAM rate of 11.11%). In addition, the recently released IPC analysis indicates that between December 2020 and March 2021, the 5 counties will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) while between April and July 2021, Abiemnhom, Pariang and Rubkona will be in IPC 4 while Mayom and Koch will remain in IPC 3. This project will provide Integrated, life-saving emergency nutrition interventions for children under the age of 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in the above 5 counties. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and Returnees will be of special interest to this project because of their high levels of vulnerability. Number of beneficiaries targeted for the project will be 258,015 and the total budget for the project will be $ 1,272,961.

1 - Needs

The high malnutrition levels experienced in parts of Unity State and the Ruweng Administrative Area are as a result of multiple factors including but not limited to the high morbidity burden estimated to be 36% (malaria, watery diarrhea, pneumonia vaccine preventable diseases), the poor quality and diversity of food, elevated levels of food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition, intercommunal conflict, flooding and the COVID-19 related disruptions. The proxy GAM rates for 2021 indicate that all the 5 counties where CARE works (with the exception of Mayom which has a proxy GAM rate of 11.11%) have GAM levels above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. These are: Rubkona (25.00%), Pariang (19.59%), Koch (17.80%) and Abiemnhom (15.28%). According to the recently released IPC report: - Between December 2020 and March 2021: close to 6.35 million people (55.3% of the population) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Between April 2021 and July 2021, the food security situation will deteriorate with an estimated 7.24 million people (68.7% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. This unprecedented food insecurity is expected to have a huge impact on the malnutrition situation and therefore morbidity and mortality especially among children under the age of 5 and pregnant and lactating women in areas with a high burden of communicable diseases. The high burden of infectious diseases such as malaria, acute respiratory infections and diarrhea, which contribute up to 77% of all the OPD consultations (HMIS, 2012) will also have an impact on malnutrition of the under 5 children because, apart from food insecurity, poor health has been identified as the leading cause of malnutrition in South Sudan. All the 5 counties that CARE works in (Pariang, Abiemnhom, Rubkona, Mayom and Koch) will be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The improvement in the food security situation early in the period is largely attributed to availability of harvests, fish, and livestock products such as milk. With the onset of the dry season, market access and functionality are also expected to improve, even as harvests find their way into the markets and lead to some drop in the prices of staples. However, for the severely food insecure households, their dire food security situation is attributed to low crop production because of flooding events during the agricultural season, the continued effects of COVID-19 on their livelihoods and incomes, the ongoing economic crisis and currency devaluation that continues to erode their purchasing power, and limited access to milk and wild foods.- Between April 2021 and July 2021: 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Abiemnhom, Pariang and Rubkona will be in IPC 4 while Mayom and Koch will remain in IPC 3. Food insecurity in the above 5 counties during the lean season is driven by high reliance on markets at a time when cereal prices are at their seasonal highest and most households have depleted their harvest stocks, as well as reduced volumes of fish. The effects of COVID-19, the economic crisis, currency devaluation and reduced market access and functionality because of the rainy season are also expected to play a role in the deterioration of the food security situation. Incidents of insecurity related to inter-communal violence and cattle raiding are likely to be experienced during this period in Koch, Rubkona and Mayom, further contributing to the projected severe acute food insecurity. To mitigate the food consumption gaps, households are likely to rely on the availability of milk and other livestock products as animals return nearer homesteads, increased availability of wild foods, some access to fish, and delivery of planned humanitarian food assistance.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARE has ensured that the PSEA Focal Person is known to all staff and beneficiaries and that contacts are made widely shared. Additionally, CARE provides awareness-raising sessions on sexual exploitation and abuse for all staff and beneficiaries and other personnel in CARE on a regular basis. The Human Resource department makes appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing PSEA prevention efforts.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARE will use their presence at the community level to engage all sectors of the community and ensure that their voices are heard.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The opportunity for communities (both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) to provide CARE with their feedback and/or complaints, and in exchange to receive feedback or response from CARE, is an important part of being accountable. To this end, CARE will train all staff implementing the project to ensure that they understand and have the capacity to implement the Accountability to Affected Populations Mechanism

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CARE will use the beneficiary feedback to adapt the project and ensure that it is addressing the needs of the community. To this end, CARE will involve the community in all the implementation of the project including the target criteria for beneficiaries and mechanisms for referral. Regular project reviews will be conducted together with the affected population to ensure that the project is on track and is meting the expectations of the community.
