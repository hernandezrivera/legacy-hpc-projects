Project Summary

People affected by the conflict in Eastern Ukraine have improved access to sufficient quantity and quality of water. Adequate sanitation is ensured by improved access to water, provision of critical WASH-related supplies and increased awareness of water, sanitation and hygiene issues and services.

1 - Needs

In 2019 continued attacks cause damage to civilian infrastructure, injure personnel and cut off the power that keeps the WASH systems running in the region. In the first nine months of 2019, over 75 violent incidents have affected civilian water facilities: every four days on average. Frequent disruptions of water supply caused by these incidents, coupled with lack of access due to political issues or heavily mined areas, prevent communities from having access to safe water. These same dynamics also affect schools and hospitals where a lack of water and sanitation hampers treatment of injuries or disease. The need for WASH assistance is greater for rural households living within 20 km of the ‘contact-line’, particularly among certain vulnerable groups like poor people, elderly and persons with disability due to their physical immobility. A recent assessment found that more than one in four families on both sides of the ‘contact line’ reported the worsening situation of water supply over the past five years. Water stoppages, unavailability, inability to treat water and lack of money to afford water bills, sanitation and hygiene products are among the commonly identified critical problems.Access to water remains a key concern in both GCA and NGCA. Active conflict in communities close to the ‘contact line’ creates risks for population relying on centralized utility systems which often become the subject of shelling. There were more than 75 WASH incidents reported from January to September 2019 which is worse than 2018. Some pipelines have been damaged repeatedly, which increases the cost/complexity of such repairs, for example near Toretsk city. This also affects the resilience of water utilities to fix the consequences of such damages, and to continue working in locations between opposing forces. Within 20 kilometres of the contact line, the situation with access to water is considered worse than in the rest of the government-controlled territory (GCA): fewer households have access to centralized water supply (59% compared to 72% in the rest of GCA), bottled water (15% compared to 29%) and trucked drinking water (5% compared to 12%). Over a quarter of the respondents of the WASH cluster assessment study reported deterioration of the water situation since the beginning of the conflict.The quality of water in the conflict-affected areas is yet another concern. According to the recent WASH assessment, two thirds (63%) of households in GCA perceive drinking water quality as unsatisfied; 10% of households have the need to purify the water but cannot afford the costs of purification.14% of households in GCA and 13% in NGCA have unsatisfactory hygiene practices, including the use of unhygienic menstrual hygiene materials, poor water handling or lack of access to basic hygiene products such as soap, laundry detergent, toilet paper or shampoo. This problem is exacerbated for households in rural areas, poor households, as well as households with disabled and elderly people . Overall, about 26% of all households in GCA and 20% in NGCA need help with sanitation, including households that experience difficulties emptying their septic tanks, households which need help with garbage removal and those who do not have access to ‘improved sanitation’.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: Improved access to sufficient quantity of water • PIN will support the local administrations and utility companies on centralized, decentralized and private hot and cold water supply in public infrastructure and social institutions.• In case of necessity and feasibility, in locations where re-establishment of the existing water supply system is not possible and where water trucking is a constant need due to the damages of the main water supply lines, PIN will organize the development of a new water source, such as a new borehole or pipeline connection.• PIN will support social institutions with providing the reliable provision of sufficient water of an adequate qualityOutcome 2: Improved access to sufficient quality of water• PIN will provide drinking and technical water through water trucking. PIN will strive to provide people residing in the areas of PIN water trucking interventions with the amount of water which is the recommended for the total basic WASH needs according to the Sphere standards. • In the areas with no other possibility, PIN will procure and distribute bottled water on a very short term basis to people who have lost access to water due to the conflict or have difficulties accessing adequate amounts of drinking water.• PIN will provide household level or small-scale water treatment supplies or equipment• PIN will also install smaller water tanks in social or in private HH to facilitate the water trucking activities. These tanks will be also used for contingency water storage. Outcome 3: Improved access to adequate sanitation • PIN will support the local administrations and utility companies on centralized, decentralized and private waste water systems in public infrastructure and social institutions.• PIN will provide support to people with septic tanks emptying or latrines provision for improvements of sanitation at household level• PIN will support waste management activities in locations where solid waste has over-accumulated and is presenting a serious threat to public health • PIN's sanitation intervention in institutions will focus on improving the sanitary conditions in social institutions hosting/servicing vulnerable populations. • Besides, PIN is going to focus on the rehabilitation of heating systems that can include replacing radiators and pipes as well as replacing heating boilers, pumps, tanks. Outcome 4: provide critical WASH related supplies and/or awareness• PIN will organize the distribution of hygiene kits that take into account the gender and age specific needs of the most vulnerable (target population: sick and disabled, single mothers/fathers with children, etc.) – material donation, voucher and cash assistance. A certain quantity of hygiene kits and disinfectants will be kept as contingency stock.• Hygiene promotion targeted at the most vulnerable (children, elderly, pregnant & lactating women) on the topics of appropriate hygiene practices (personal hygiene, waste management, water consumption) to prevent water and sanitation related diseases will be conducted by PIN.• PIN will provide cash or vouchers for hygiene materials as one of the most efficient and appropriate modalities to provide life preserving subsistence assistance. PIN will collect information on age, gender and number of people with specific needs in project targeted communities. PIN will develop a plan together with community members in order to determine the most appropriate way to reach to people with specific needs, like people with limited mobility, elderly people, etc.; identify possibilities of joint actions with relevant humanitarian agencies like Help Age in such cases. Activities in 2020 form a part of a vision to continue providing WASH support to the affected area until the end of 2021.
