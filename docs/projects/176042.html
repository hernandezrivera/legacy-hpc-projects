Project Summary

This project will seek to address the growing need for NFI items in EES (Magwi, Budi, Lafon, Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East), CES (Kajo-Keji, Morobo, Lainya, Yei), Lakes (Rumbek East, Rumbek Centre, Rumbek North, Cuiebet) and Jonglei (Bor South). As a result of the large number of IDPs hosted in these areas, combined with the increasing number of returnees, many of the basic NFI needs of these groups are unmet as they have minimal assets and resources to support their households.
Key activities under this project include the distribution of emergency shelter and non food items plastic sheets, rubber ropes, poles, mosquito nets, sleeping mats, kitchen sets, blankets, kangas, and facemasks. Titi Foundation will also identify, track and refer cases of land disputes in each of its operational areas, with gender considerations incorporated. Titi Foundation will use Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality to respond to emergency shelter and basic NFI needs for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in Lakes, Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei. Titi Foundation also aims to focus on CVA (20%) in locations where security is more stable with functioning and accessible markets.
In taking a gender-sensitive approach to support safe and dignified shelter needs of vulnerable populations, Titi Foundation will seek to ensure that all of its activities under this project are inclusive of all community members.
Furthermore, to ensure that other vulnerable groups, such as people with disabilities, are included in the distribution of key items, Titi Foundation will work closely with Local Monitoring Groups, as well as community leaders, civil society groups, and partners on the ground like PAH and Spedp in CES and Jonglei and CCOSS and Plan international in lakes and DCA and Acted in EES to ensure that these community members are identified prior to implementation and the necessary accommodations are made.
TITI Foundation will engage the affected population through a community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach which will be employed throughout the project to ensure that the affected population and beneficiaries are informed and included throughout all stages of the project, particularly regarding the objectives and activities of the project. In addition, the CBPP will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community in each of the project locations, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. The CBPP approach will ensure that the project is accountable, accepted, and owned by the targeted population. In addition, the CBPP and Project Management Committees are representative of the different gender and age groups (male, female, youth, elderly), and specific information and messages are targeted towards specific groups.

1 - Needs

Since the peace agreement was signed in 2018, returnees to different counties have increased, according to IOM DTM’s Round 9 of Mobility Tracking, with a total number of 1,674, 668 returnees in the country. The needs of these returning population are compounded by the fact that there are still 1,615,756 IDPs in the country. In the coming years, more refugees and IDPs are expected to return to their places of origin, or re-settle elsewhere, with minimal resources to rebuild their lives and meet their basic needs. This ongoing population movement has also placed additional pressures on scarce resources and infrastructure within host communities, and has already been observed in each of the locations targeted in this project. To leave these needs unaddressed increases the risk of inter-communal tensions and violence, inhibiting the ability of local communities to meet their own needs and sustain their livelihoods and assets. To support communities during this time of transition, as they also grapple with the impact of natural disasters and inter-communal violence which lead to displacement and the destruction of assets and infrastructure, Titi Foundation will provide multi-sectoral support in the locations it currently has a static presence in, with S-NFI being a key component. Shelter situation is at its extreme odds in the rural setting due to access to housing, land, and property (HLP), an important issue during the years of conflict, is now critical as people are further displaced, the population grows, PoC sites start to transition and South Sudanese refugees return. The formalization of land laws and the creation of mechanisms of their implementation remains a gap and creates an obstacle for people who want to return. Widespread lack of literacy and insufficient legal ownership documentation made it difficult for many people to understand and effectively advocate for themselves. This issue contributes to attempts of improper land-grabbing that can drive further conflict and displacement. Women and people with disabilities disproportionately struggle to assert rights to housing and land. Given that Housing, land and property (HLP) rights continue to be a growing key protection issue across South Sudan, an estimated 1.5 million will be affected by HLP issues in 2021, in addition 743,000 children are indirectly affected of HLP issues. During the years of conflict, ownership claims and control over land and property has played a defining role which was likewise marked by large-scale destruction of housing, land and property. The danger of HLP is likely to worsen with the Spontaneous return of both IDPs and refugees who are expected to increase in 2021 as the government moves forward with the implementation of the R-ARCSS. TITI foundation will strengthen key community systems through a strong collaboration with its partners to avert further damage to the population that is deeply affected by the highest level of contamination which is predominantly located in the Equatoria, along the main routes and areas for returnees from Uganda. Explosive hazards inhibit civilians from collecting water or firewood, cultivating land, attending school, and receiving health care services, among other critical daily needs. They further hinder humanitarian organizations from accessing vulnerable communities or providing lifesaving assistance in conflict-affected areas. While substantial progress has been made in the monitoring and clearing of explosive contamination, landmines and ERW continue to pose a threat to civilians and humanitarians in South Sudan.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

In the locations selected for this project, where Titi Foundation already has a long-term and static presence, the majority of markets are functioning, and a few are in recovery from natural disasters or insecurity. Over the project duration of one year, it is anticipated that these markets will remain accessible and functioning to the communities in which they are located. As a result, CVA contributions are likely to succeed and benefit the targeted individuals and households, while simultaneously strengthening local market systems. Also Titi Foundation has been participating in joint market monitoring initiative and uses the data to inform its cash base interventions.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

A market assessment will be carried out at the start up process of implementation. The CVA component will represent 20% of support provided to communities with conditional and restricted to shelter and NFI assistance and 80% will be provided through in-kind assistance. Many of the locations targeted in this project have functioning local and accessible markets systems that can be supported and strengthened through CVA initiatives, and additionally the cash will provide opportunities for households to address gaps in needs that may not be filled through in-kind assistance. Cost effectiveness is also a fundamental aspect in the use of the cash or voucher due to the many challenges and dynamics in a community when it comes to transportation and local market destruction with in-kind commodities; Beneficiaries are able to make a dignified choice and meet their most pressing needs if the Cash and Voucher system is applied.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff are trained on, including those located outside of Juba. Additionally, whenever services or projects are implemented, communities are provided with information on the PSEA policy, awareness on what PSEA is, and the process for them to report any incidents. Beneficiaries are encouraged to come forward with any concerns or information they have to any member of Titi Foundation, and are also provided with information on referral pathways for each community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

All of Titi Foundations Local Monitoring Groups are inclusive of different population groups and hails from the communities, to ensure that the staff are receiving feedback from even the most vulnerable members of a community. The establishment of the complaint desk with a focal person allows for people who are not able to utilize the suggestions box to also provide feedback. The Local Monitoring Groups also consult with the broader community, and are able to advocate on behalf of their most vulnerable members. Titi Foundation staff also consult with communities to identify vulnerable members to ensure that they have access to the program’s activities and services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

In order to ensure effective records of feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up in each community, a suggestion box , hotlines and complaints desk is set up with a relevant focal person and we will ensure that the locations are accessible and that they are provided in places that beneficiaries feel comfortable with. At the beginning of each project or activity, beneficiaries are provided with information on the purpose and location of both the suggestions box, hotlines and complaint desk.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Titi Foundation has established Local Monitoring Groups in each of the locations that it works in, to ensure that in addition to M&E mechanisms, community members are able to provide regular feedback on project assistance. Titi Foundation values and takes seriously all of the feedback it receives through these mechanisms, and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible. This may include changing the location of activities to increase accessibility, assisting in the identification of referral services needed, or other mitigating measures to reduce risk or harm to beneficiaries, while also ensuring all projects are inclusive and accessible. Any complaints made against Titi Foundation’s staff or programming will also initiate an internal review and investigation, in alignment with the organizations policies and code of conduct.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Medium risks • Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area – Titi Foundation operates in locations that are receiving IDPs, returnees and refugees. As a result, at times there is a risk of disputes over land ownership and access, particularly for those whose land was occupied prior to displacement. Increased population sizes may also contribute to pressure on resources such as land. Within its S-NFI activities proposed for this project, Titi Foundation will work to identify, track and refer cases where disputes have arisen, with particular consideration for women who face additional challenges in regards to land ownership to HLP partners in the areas of intervention like IOM.• Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources.Titi Foundation maintains strong ties to community leaders and members in all of the locations that it operates in, reducing the risks of tensions around the distribution of services. The establishment of Local Monitoring Group, which are inclusive committees constituted of community members, also reduces the likelihood of this occurring.• Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries Titi Foundation has digitized its data collection procedures for M&E protocols to ensure both data privacy and quality. All staff are trained on ethical protocols for data management. • Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms The locations of the suggestion box and the desk for the focal point to receive complaints was decided in conjunction with communities, reducing the likelihood of this risk. • Risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA – Titi Foundation has a comprehensive PSEA policy which all staff are trained on in all locations. The organization’s management works closely with field-based staff to ensure that they have the resources and support needed to address any issues that may arise in this area. Communities are also provided information at the beginning of each project to ensure that they are familiar with the mechanisms within Titi Foundation that are available to support them if they raise any concerns or complaints.• Risks of women, girls and boys without and with disabilities exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance – Titi Foundation meets with community members before implementation to outline the plan and discuss any concerns that community members may have, including risks they may encounter when accessing services. For community members that may encounter barriers or difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance, Titi Foundation works with the households and community members to identify safer alternatives, i.e. a household member can collect items on behalf of a person with disabilities, or Titi Foundation’s staff will deliver to their household while accompanied and monitored by community members. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.)Establishment of, and communication about, referral and protection services are communicated with each of Titi Foundation’s projects and services, to ensure that all community members have the information they need to access assistance. This is provided in all locations that the organization is working in.

Also the beneficiaries are trained on effective use of kitchen sets in meal preparations and the use of water containers as a safer way of storage. Hence all this will contribute to a hygienic way of meal preparations reducing food contamination and hence are key to promoting children nutritional conditions.
