Project Summary

The recent escalation resulted in additional 520,000 people forced to leave their homes, since 1st December 2019. As of 31 January, (WHO, 2020), at least 53 health facilities had suspended services due to insecurity, threats of attacks or the simple fact that entire areas have been deserted by civilians seeking refuge from violence and daily bombardments. An estimated 2.9 million people in Syria’s northwest are in need of health care (WHO, 2020), with women and children estimated to comprise 76% of this population (OCHA, 2020). The UN estimates that 25% of IDPs are women of reproductive age, and 4% are pregnant women that require sustained maternal health services, including emergency obstetric care. Outbreaks of measles, acute bloody diarrhea, typhoid fever, and leishmaniosis were reported in various areas of the country throughout the year (HRP Syria, 2019). Overstretched health services and low coverage of routine immunization has exposed IPDs and returnees to risks of infectious diseases. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to affect the lives of women and girls, with adolescent girls, women-headed households, especially those divorced and widowed (HNO Syria, 2019). Children, including those unaccompanied, separated or living with older/disabled caregivers, remain particularly vulnerable to exploitation and GBV.
Human Appeal (HA) is an active partner with the health cluster and was running a specialized healthcare facility for women and children and had to shift its operations to Sarmada town in the Idleb governorate. HA will continue to provide health care services to its beneficiaries in Sarmada with extended healthcare services including MHPSS, Trauma and CMR services for women and children in Sarmada town (HeRAMS code: G0703086!Sarmada Maternal and pediatric hospital)
Human Appeal will ensure the provision of vital healthcare to a total of 6,990 individuals with 15,430 healthcare services on a monthly basis. HA has provided 13,876 health care services to 5,328 individual beneficiaries (2968 Women, 1067 girls and 1293 boys) through its hospital and 1,522 individuals (241 men, 3,992 women, 2007 boys and 1,634 girls) through its mobile clinic in January 2020. Out of the 6.990 individuals, 3% of them will be people with various disabilities.
Aligned with HRP objectives the main health sector project interventions will include:
Provide essential primary and secondary health care, emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) and referral services which includes outpatient consultations, MHPSS services and referrals for advanced MHPSS and protection services, trauma consultations, CMR services through trained gynecologists, ANC and PNC care, attendance of vaginal and C-section deliveries by skilled gynecologists and midwives, minor and major surgeries to the beneficiaries, provide routine immunization services to all children <1 years of age, provide health facility with essential medicines and medical supplies to the health facility, strengthening the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services, establishing and maintaining women and child-friendly spaces at the facility, provide home-based medical services through outreach activities of mobile clinic for vulnerable (people with injuries and disabilities) households with limited access to health facilities, awareness sessions about family planning and reproductive health, health promotion and awareness-raising sessions by health care workers and CHW's in schools and in other social gathering places.
The project will ensure outreach and inclusion of vulnerable communities from IDP camps and remote locations with the provision of a Mobile Health Clinic offering pediatrics, gynecology and general clinic services and disseminating of relevant IEC/BCC messages.

4 - Needs

As of 31 January 2020, Northwest Syria Readiness and Response Plan has identified an outbreak of disease, increase in psychosocial and mental health concerns, and lack of gynaecological services as potential risks due to evolving humanitarian situation inside Syria. It has further noted that there is a lack of qualified health staff in the areas of displacements, particularly of gynecologists and midwives, as well as lack of reproductive health care supplies. There is a need for operational cost support and staff salaries at key priority health facilities. Sarmada is part of sub-district Dana and is on the route of existing movement of displaced population from Idleb. The total population of Dana sub-district is over 560,000 whereby 50% are children between 0-17. The Al-Iman Hospital (HeRAMS code: G0703086!Sarmada Maternal and pediatric hospital) is located at the entrance of Sarmada and serves as a major healthcare facility for the nearby rural populations with a catchment area of over 50,000 people. It is one of the few HF with 24/7, free-of-cost CEmOC and pediatric services.

5 - Implementation of activities

HA will maintain regular information sharing with beneficiaries on project activities ensuring that the project objectives are well explained in the local language simply and easily. Members of the host communities, IDP’s, and other relevant stakeholders will be able to share feedback with HA through the local council, Email, and WhatsApp number. Also, staff will ensure transparency on project activities and plans during meetings with community representatives and wider stakeholders. HA will promote participatory monitoring and evaluation and will assess the effectiveness of offered services based on regular feedback from beneficiaries. This will be done through HA’s CFM, enabling the beneficiaries and affected population with direct access for feedback and complaints with the M&E Coordinator, based in Gaziantep. Besides, HA staff will conduct regular field visits to monitor project activities and seek regular feedback on the satisfaction levels of beneficiaries.HA provides salaries based on salary scales defined its HR policy. Accordingly, HA pays salaries for unskilled workers 500-1199 USD, for officers 1200-1699 USD, coordinators 1700-2199 USD, managers 2200-2499 USD and for head of departments 2500-3000 USD for Turkey office staff only (excluding SGK and other legal expenses). Details of the staff are as follows; clinical staff; 11 doctor, 15 nurse and 7 midwife. Technical staff; 2 pharmacist, 3 lab and 3 anesthesia technician, 3 surgical assistant, 2 PSW, 2 CHW and 1 data entry. Facility staff; 1 medical and 1 admin manager, 7 security, 8 cleaner, 2 receptionist, 1 time monitor, 2 maintenance and 1 accountant. Field staff; 1 health coordinator and M&E officer as direct and HR, logistics, and finance officers as support. Support staff; health, HR and logistic manager, M&E and Finance coordinator and media officer are partly supported by this project. Mobile clinic staff (8) is covered through other sources.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. GBV and sexual exploitation risk: The project aims to ensure the dignity of all beneficiaries through the provision of health care services with a focus on maintaining the privacy of women. Mitigation measures: • Privacy in the examination room inside the ambulance vehicle, and confidentiality, through a secured database and code names for the survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). • Majority of the hospital staff, including doctors, are females which provides a comfortable and safe environment for women and children. • Maintaining a strong monitoring and evaluation system and providing beneficiaries with clear and various complaint channels.2. Inclusion risk: Persons with disabilities disproportionately lack access to health services in humanitarian contexts. This can lead to worsening health conditions and increased vulnerability. This proposal reflects the principles of disability inclusion and ensures adequate communication to these affected populations. Mitigation measures: • The staff will get trained on the rights of persons with disabilities provided by the HA protection team and the project coordinator. • Beneficiaries with physical impairments will be identified by CHWs in each location so that the MMU team can undertake home visits for these people. 3. Safeguarding risk: Mitigation measures: • All beneficiaries have equal rights to protection from abuse and exploitation. • Every HA staff member signs the PESA and Child Safeguarding policies when recruited. • Health staff will be trained by the HA protection team and the project coordinator on providing trauma-sensitive care, psychosocial first-aid, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks 1: Safety and security is the major and high risk across Syria.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation measures:a. The project has selected Sarmada as this location has remained safe from airstrikes due to its proximity with the Turkish border. Also, HA has signed an MOU with Idleb Health Directorate that provides security from local influences. Furthermore, the project will manage a warehouse outside the hospital in addition to the one based in the hospital to store life-saving drugs. Risk 2: Supplies of essentials, particularly drugs, are dependent on supply from Turkey and the access to cross border aid might be limited. Similarly, the continuous availability of professional staff on a long-term basis is low risk.Likelihood: Low, Impact: MediumMitigation measures:A. HA has a warehouse to store life-saving medicines in sufficient quantities. It also engages with other actors like MSF, Americares and WHO for the supply of medicines and other consumables on need-basis.Risk 3: The hospital needs continuous access to stable electricity, fuel connections. Such interruptions can cause problems in the incubation section as well as to interrupt surgical procedures.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation measure:Currently, electricity is covered through generators. However, there is always a risk of interruptions either due to fuel supply or generators going out of order. Risk4: Syria being a patriarchal society where men have dominant roles at the household and community levels. Women require cooperation and permissions from men to participate in the community therefore the project may not effectively deliver the intended services.The project will ensure the provision of all services in a culturally sensitive and traditionally acceptable manner through female staff and female CHW's.
