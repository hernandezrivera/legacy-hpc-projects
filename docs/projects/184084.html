Project Summary

Sudan continues to navigate through its transition phase, which is characterized by social, political, economic, recurrence of local conflicts, security, and governance challenges. On top of that, the country has a long history of hosting refugees and asylum seekers, who are seeking safety from violence, persecution, and other hazards in their countries of origin. A total of 1,111,329 refugee and asylum-seekers are estimated to be hosted in Sudan. 543,306 of this number are said to be in five states, namely South Kordofan, West Kordofan, White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref as per UNHCR’s Sudan population overview, 30 September 2021 edition.
The overall deteriorating situation in Sudan is affecting the welfare of the majority of the population including refugees. The refugees are highly vulnerable as they have limited capacities and assets to sustain their livelihoods and other economic opportunities. They are mostly located in rural settings where they face huge challenges in accessing energy for household meal preparation but also for lighting.. The majority of the registered households are female headed, who also have children and other vulnerable members to care for. The situation is precarious and needs a multi-sectoral interventions to address their vulnerabilities. Against this background, UNDP has put in place this response plan which aims at: 1) enhancing the self-reliance of refugees by promoting activities targeting households and individuals through conditional seasonal programmes linked to livelihoods and asset creation enabling access to livelihoods and lifesaving assistance to refugees as part of protection of refugees; and 2) improving access to clean sustainable energy sources by refugees. UNDP will link activities of this response plan to those of its ongoing community-based assistance and local peacebuilding interventions in the proposed localities in order to maximize impact, but also to demonstrate humanitarian-develop-peace nexus. UNDP will work closely with UNHCR, Commission for Refugees (COR) and other implementing Partners and actors in other sectors to implement this response plan.

1 - Needs

The deteriorating economic environment which is branded by high inflation and the devaluation of the Sudanese local currency, elimination of wheat flour subsidies, high transportation cost due to the liberalization of fuel prices, etc., have inevitably led to hike in the prices of basic food items and other essentials. This, in turn, has negatively affected the purchasing power of refugees and host communities. The lack of economic and livelihood opportunities has also negatively impacted the welfare of refugees who continue to face enormous challenges in accessing work permits irrespective of the skills they possess. The CRP 2022 Refugee Sector Response, Sector Strategies had quoted WFP’s Food Security Monitoring System (FSMS) report for Q3, 2021, noting that in Eastern and Southern Sudan, 42 percent of refugee households are food insecure. The FSMS results for Tigray crisis refugees indicate 59 percent prevalence of food insecurity. According to this quote, the main contributing factor is economic vulnerability, as 94 percent of refugee households are spending more than 65 percent of their total expenditure on food. 52 percent of refugee households adopted negative food-based coping strategies; the quote concluded. The situation, if not addressed, presents a huge risk and severe consequences to the livelihoods of the already-vulnerable populations who are net buyers of food and essential items. Hence, access to food-based livelihoods such as agriculture and livestock support, tied with other non-farm livelihoods such as seasonal labour, cash for work, small scale income opportunities, etc., is vital to addressing the welfare of refugees. Furthermore, access to energy remains a huge challenge for refugees. In South and West Kordofan States, refugees live in settlements within host communities while those in White Nile, Kassala and Gedaref live in camps – all settlements and camps are located in rural areas where electricity is either lacking or in limited supply or unaffordable. This, coupled with the lack of alternative source of energy for cooking and lighting, puts firewood as the main reliable source of energy for household meal preparation by refugees and hosts. While in search of firewood, women, and children, who are the primary users of forest resources face the risk of exposure to harassments and other hazards such as remnants of war especially in post conflict communities. This is in addition to the environmental degradation associated with firewood collection. The inefficient 3-stone-stoves mostly used by refugees are a source of hazards including carbon monoxide which is detrimental to the health once inhaled. The availability of sustainable energy source for lighting and household meal preparation such as solar stoves will have a significant positive impact on the lives of refugees and reduce the exposure to the risks associated in search for alternative sources of energy in forests.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Livelihood related activities will focus on the following:• Lifesaving seeds and tools distribution and asset creation• Cash-for-work as live saving intervention• Distribution of small ruminants (goats and sheep) as live saving intervention• Agricultural training and extension services using farmers field method.• Training in small ruminants including identification of symptoms of basic diseases• Vaccination of small ruminants before distribution• Training in financial literacy and entrepreneurship including coaching and mentoring support • Provision of small-scale business grants and start-up capital • Facilitate access to markets and financial services• integrate livelihood interventions with other sectors to improve access to productive assets Energy and Environment related activities will focus on the following: • Distribution of solar cooking stoves• Training of refugees in the fabrication, use and maintenance of solar cooking stoves.• Installation of solar lights in areas where refugees are prone to protection risks during night periods• Conduct awareness raising on the sustainable use of forest resources, environmental protection and management of woodlot.• Training on other livelihoods skills such as the collection and processing of non-timber products from local forests such as mushrooms and medicinal products. • Provision of small-scale income opportunities such as service provision in equipment maintenance, land preparations, crop harvesting, storage and aggregation Interventions will identify disadvantaged groups such as the disabled and prioritize them for assistance alongside other vulnerable groups. Depending on the disability, UNDP will seek further assistance (mentor/community supporter, e.g.) to help them actually utilize seed/tool, etc. given to them.The distribution of solar cooking stoves will replace the inefficient 3-stone stoves that are commonly used by refugees for cooking. The interventions will train the youth among the refugees on how to fabricate, use and maintain solar stoves using locally available materials. The provision of solar stoves will not only reduce the time spent by women and children to collect firewood but reduce the protection and environmental risks associated with firewood collection. Solar streetlights will be installed in areas where refugees are prone to protection risks during night periods. This will help to illuminate the area for the protection and safe movement of women and children. Sensitization on the sustainable use of forest resources, environmental protection and management of woodlots will increase the awareness level of refugees on the sustainable use of forest, timber and non-timber resources. In localities that face climate related shocks such as dry spells or occasional flooding, skills will be imparted on risk reduction – housing, drain management, water collection points. Interventions will demonstrate humanitarian-development-peace nexus by ensuring that refugees who receive seeds and tools are linked to the long-term economic ventures provided in communities by UNDP such as tractors, harvesters, irrigations schemes and access to markets. This will not only guarantee refugees’ access to water for irrigation farming, but also access to equipment such as tractors for land preparation and harvesting machines at no-cost as the community institutions and cooperatives which UNDP had facilitated establishment will apply the unhindered access to these assets by refugees. All of these activities will be anchored on the ongoing local peacebuilding initiatives in communities supported by UNDP and other actors to reduce tension, while promoting peaceful co-existence and social cohesion between refugees and host communities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During needs assessment carried out by UNDP in the proposed areas in 2019, it was discovered that unsustainably harvesting and ravaging local forests and other natural resources were among the coping mechanisms of refugees and vulnerable members of host communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled clearing and burning of farm sites, improper disposal of waste, (to name but a few) were among the environmental hazards observed in the proposed localities. Proposed interventions will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of project activities to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through training on NRM and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation. Proper waste disposal will be promoted among refugees, for example the creation and strengthening of waste disposal committees, waste collectors identification and use of disposal sites will be used as a livelihood activity. The use of solar stoves will be promoted especially among women who are frequent users of natural and forest resources. Soil and water conservation techniques such as minimum tillage, controlled burning of farm sites, mulching, the use of organic manure for backyard gardening, etc. will be promoted.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will contribute to Sudan’s protection environment by conducting awareness raising trainings and campaigns on civil, social and economic rights of refugees to increase self-reliance and opportunities for solutions. Focus will be on awareness and sensitization initiatives on basic rights with local authorities, host communities and service providers to reduce social tension and promote social cohesion. Capacity building trainings of CMCs, beneficiaries and key community members will include topics with focus on human rights, refugee rights, gender equity, GBV, rights of people living with disabilities among others. The project will apply the principle of equity and /or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions. Targeting criteria will be used throughout to ensure the most vulnerable are targeted. Protection specific activities have been incorporated in planned activities. The project will ensure vulnerable people have access to project assistance including sustainable livelihoods in addition to ensuring the use of a well-defined selection/identification criteria of beneficiaries for project assistance. The project will also ensure to share information on protection issues that the project will come across with humanitarian and protection partners working in the state. The project will also ensure stronger coordination with humanitarian actors, especially UNHCR to avoid missed opportunities for integrating protection information in refugee profiling for project assistance. The project will seek for cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be encountered during project implementation.
