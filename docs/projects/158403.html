Project Summary

In the aftermath of Cyclone Idai’s destruction, households and even entire communities were left without the necessary materials for survival, including shelter, bedding and clean water. Cyclone Idai caused dramatic damage to homes, with floodwaters sweeping away entire villages and thousands homes. Many families lost everything, and women in collective sites report that they fled with nothing more than the clothes on their backs. Those people with specific needs, including children, persons with disabilities, elderly people and child headed households face particular risks in accessing shelter, bedding and other basic necessities. Within such a context, the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse is present, as people become desperate for live-saving resources such as beddings, shelter and water. It is critical that the response reduces risk of exploitation and negative coping mechanisms in a crisis.
Objective 1: Affected populations have the necessary materials/domestic items to meet their basic physical needs
Output: Core relief items (including kitchen sets, sleeping mats and mosquito nets) are distributed to affected people.
Activities:
• Distribution of core relief like kitchen sets, cleaning kits, hygiene kits, sleeping mats to approximately 80,000 individuals (80,000 households) who have been displaced by the cyclone. Targeted assistance will be provided to identified people with specific needs (PSNs), in compliance with UNHCR standards of commodity distribution and providing due diligence in recording, monitoring and reporting processes.
Objective 2: Support early recovery through distribution of shelter kits coupled with technical shelter support to affected people.
Output: Access to shelter by the affected population improved through distribution.
Activities:
• Training of staff in shelter construction as well as protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) in line with the UN’s zero-tolerance policy in this area
• Provision of emergency shelter assistance to pockets of need to urgently support displaced families in accommodation centers and relocation contexts.
• Targeted distributions of shelter tool kit to rehabilitate damaged homes and shelters.
• Support of self-recovery through community and owner driven strategies (e.g. exploring training local builders in the community to become Technical Roving Teams on Build Back Safer for shelter repair), BBS messaging and sustained technical support and supervision. This will target 5000 people for the training
• Provision of coordinated shelter support and technical assistance to planned resettlements
