Project Summary

IYD has been working inside Syria since 2013 in different sectors and mainly in Food Security and Livelihoods. This experience has given IYD the opportunity to build good relationships and network based on trust and transparency with both the local authorities and beneficiaries. While implementing our projects inside Syria, we could realize the needs and gaps of the vulnerable people by conducting rapid needs assessments. The project aims to increase the resilience of ID Households who had to leave their houses due to the deterioration of the security status which have had intensified in the past four months, as well as targeting a percentage of the host communities who match IYD’s selection criteria. The project will provide food vouchers of 55 USD monthly value for six months to 2,000 displaced families settling in the districts of Al-Bab and Jarablus. Each displaced family will redeem this 55 USD voucher at the contracted vendors with IYD who will be selected on specific criteria. In this regard, a vendor assessment will be conducted to identify the best vendors. These food vouchers will be enough for five persons per month, and the list of the allowed items to be redeemed will be wide in order to cover the needed monthly K/cal for each family member including girls, boys, women and men.
Such projects are proved to preserve the dignity of the beneficiaries in an efficient way. To give an example, the beneficiaries’ needs who receive in-kind kits are not always met as the contents do not necessarily express their needs, as well as the longtime the beneficiaries have to wait in queues to get the in-kind rations. Moreover, the list of the permitted items to redeem in food voucher project is relatively long, which means that beneficiaries are not obliged to be limited with a number of items as it is usually the case with kits’ distribution projects.
Besides the distribution of 2000 food vouchers on monthly basis, IYD will target another 2000 additional families residing in the camps of Jarablus, and Al-Bab with food rations for four months as this modality is the preferred one by the families staying in camps because their access to the market would have some impediments such as the remoteness of the nearest market. IYD, throughout its long-standing experience in food rations’ projects, will ensure that studied selection and targeting criteria will be applied during the project implementation. To ensure it will increase their resilience, IYD will consult the whole targeted groups about the items which they prefer to be in the rations. These food rations will provide the needed monthly K/cal for each family member according to WFP standards which IYD has been applying during 4 years of partnerships with WFP. HNO 2017 has rated Al-Bab district by 3.00 on the severity scale, while Jarablus has been rated by 4.00. Based on that, targeting these districts will have an important impact on alleviating the suffering of the targeted people there.

IYD, throughout this proposed project, is looking forward to meeting the food needs of the targeted beneficiaries where many vulnerable families are either receiving inadequate food assistance or not receiving at all. IYD will coordinate with the active humanitarian actors in the targeted areas to fill the gap in their areas and avoid any overlapping might arise. When studying food status in the targeted areas,it has been found out that these areas are in dire need of assistance especially food where a very few people have the purchasing power of their food needs. In addition, the prices of the food items are increasing every other time as a result of the closure of cross borders which sometimes occurs. Based on that, the distribution of food vouchers will contribute to improving the nutrition situation for all the targeted categories; children, women, pregnant women, lactating, men and people with special needs

Needs

To have a factual and tangible gap analysis for the proposed project areas, IYD has conducted a needs assessment on mid-November 2018 covered all the project areas. The staff who worked on this needs assessment composed of two teams; the first team has two females and three males while the second one has two females and eight males. The total number of Key-Informant KI and HHS interviews was 505 divided by the intended project areas. The sources of information were community leaders, local relief committees, local NGOs, statistics offices, and normal HHs. When studying the results of the needs assessment, a food gap between a large number of surveyed communities is clearly evident. Along with the food gap, other needs were found out there. According to the need assessment, the food gap has effectively affected the studied communities especially the displaced families. In the same context, the studies HHS has reported that 86 % of the families have no food assistance for three months. As a result, the HHS especially women heading households found themselves obliged to face the harsh circumstances of the lack of assistance especially food one. The coping strategies of the studies families vary between relying on less preferred and less expensive food items, reducing the number of daily meals, buying food items on credit, sending the family members to beg, borrowing food, keeping the whole day with no food, and sending the children to eat with neighbours. Considering the security and physical constraints, 89 of respondents confirmed that intended areas are accessible and no outstanding constraints are available. In relation to the food items which the studied communities prefer are; cereals, bread, sugar, beans, oil, ghee, meat, egg, tea, coffee, fruits, and vegetables. Furthermore, food was the first priority of the studied communities, Fuel was the second, WASH was the third one, and shelter was the fourth. Link for the Needs assessment:

Implementation of activities

The first step of the project will be the recruitment of the staff. In order to achieve this, IYD will publish an announcement of recruitment through its social media channels. The process of recruitment will be conducted in a fully transparent manner, where all the candidates will be tested on their technical knowledge. After contracting the staff, IYD will hold training sessions, in order to get them fully ready for the implementation of the project. Next step will be the communication with the Local Councils in the project areas. Following this, IYD will select the beneficiaries using the eligibility and selection criteria which will be shared with the local community As the project will be based on the distribution of food vouchers and food rations, IYD will do an assessment of all the available vendors in the targeted project areas. This assessment for the vendors will be carried out to provide IYD with the full details of the capacities of these vendors including their stock, staff, speed of restocking etc.Following this step, the phase of food voucher distribution will commence, where 2000 food vouchers will be distributed to the beneficiaries. During the distribution, each beneficiary will receive a manual designed specifically to educate the beneficiary about his/her rights and duties during the distribution and how to maximally benefit from the food voucher, what are the allowed items to buy, the locations of the contracted vendors, redemption taboos and other useful information. IYD will give the beneficiaries 10 days of each month to redeem the voucher. During the redemption period, IYD, through its project team, will supervise the whole processes on the ground, in order to ensure that everything is running just as planned. IYD’s team on the ground will be following the vendors step by step to monitor if any mistake is made or any violation is committed. IYD team will also notify the vendors to correct any mistakes they made, and a WhatsApp vendors’ group will be specifically created so that IYD could respond to the vendors’ complaints and queries straight away. In parallel to that, IYD’s M&E team will be distributing complaints and suggestions notes to beneficiaries to identify if there is any troubles or obstacles taking place during distribution. From the very beginning, IYD’s M&E team will follow all the project activities including conducting Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), focus groups discussions (FGDs), vendor assessments, market assessment, monitoring the process of vouchers distribution and redemption, and the last activity M&E team will do is the Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). The PDM will be divided into two parts; the first part targets the vendors; while the second part targets the beneficiaries. Accordingly, the PDM aims to identify the satisfaction extent of the project, the quality of the presented services, as well as the benefit earned from the suggestions and feedback from both the beneficiaries and vendors.On the financial level, IYD’s finance team on the ground will be collecting the invoices, payment requests and other financial documents from the vendors. After receiving these invoices, they will be entered into the finance software that was specifically developed to serve this project. The next step is the disbursement of the vendors’ dues. On the other hand, the distribution of food rations will go through the following steps. At first, IYD will Identify and select the beneficiaries using the Beneficiaries Selection Tool, BST. Next, a tender for the purchase of food rations will be announced. Third, the food rations will be procured and stored within the warehouse. After that, the distribution of the food rations’ batches will begin. By the completion of food rations batches distribution, final reports and PDM will be shared with the donor, and the project will be closed out.

Protection Risk Analysis

- The risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers. IYD will mitigate this by establishing a confidential complaint mechanism and referral mechanisms, addressing the SEA complaints, training staff on PSEA and GBV, asking all staff to sign code of conduct, creating females and males’ queues separately, having female staff. Behaving with beneficiaries as do no harm and respecting their dignity. - Increased risk of inter-community tension between community residents and host communities due in part to unequal level of assistance given to community residents in comparison to host communities. IYD will mitigate this by ensuring that the community are all needs based, and that community residents are amongst the most vulnerable. - Sexual harassment from the beneficiaries because of the overcrowded distribution location. The mitigations are by creating females and males’ queues separately and having a committee for organizing the distribution process. - To avoid Gender exploitation. IYD will mitigate this by including all the age and gender needs accordingly within the project designing phase. Clarifying all the project objectives and beneficiaries’ selection criteria to all the local authorities. Asking the local authorities’ members, vendors and services’ providers to sign the PSEA code of conduct. - To avoid the gender discrimination, IYD will provide suitable and safe distribution environment such as: accessible ramps for people with special needs, drinking water, waiting seats, umbrellas, secure distribution center and placing it away from military installations or threats such as attacks. - Child labor risk, the mitigation is to make the service provider committing to the listed conditions which provided to not make any work with him if he was breaking the signed contract with IYD by practicing the child labor. - Child food ration receiving risk. The mitigation is by not delivering the food ration to the child and having a mobile distribution team to deliver the ration to this child house in case the householder is not able to come to the distribution center.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Full support from the local authorities and host communities to implement the project’s activities smoothly. Risk:Disputes between the host communities and the IDPs might take place in terms of the benefiting from the project’s activities.Cross border closure between Turkey and Syria.Steal or loss of food rations.Attempts of interference by military groups might take place. Mitigation:IYD will conduct FGDs on the selection criteria, as well as distributing leaflets give more details on the selection criteria.IYD will check for alternative from the local markets if the border closed for a long time.The food commodities will be stored in a well-placed, safe and guard warehouses. The warehouses will be locked and monitored 7/24.Work with Local councils will decrease the impact of the military groups interference to the minimum extent.
