Project Summary

In support of the ERL Sector (Strengthened Communities and Participation), UNICEF through this project in 2022&2023, will further support its Generation Unlimited initiative and No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), female and male and those with disabilities, to access social, civic and economic opportunities for skill development and engagement (including participation in social cohesion initiatives) with the objective of contributing to enhancing resilience and social cohesion. UNICEF will build on strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most vulnerable adolescents to strengthen skills development, employability and meaningful engagement (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches) through friendly spaces and mobile teams. The proposed project will support young women and men to have better access to civic, social, and economic opportunities for skill development and engagement and to unleash their potentials and increase their learning, leadership, and employability opportunities. UNICEF will promote the active and meaningful participation of young people brought together from different ages, genders, abilities and cultural and educational backgrounds to be agents of positive change in their communities. UNICEF will work in the following three pillars and related interventions: (1) Provide access to services and skills-building for adolescents and youth for enhanced employability. (2) Strengthen social, civic and economic engagement of adolescents and youth and foster volunteerism and social cohesion-building. (3) Support young entrepreneurs to start their own project projects through seed funding. UNICEF will continue strengthening the capacity of local partners including services providers, youth workers and young volunteers to improve the quality of provided services and enhance the evidence generation. UNICEF will support the scalable and sustainable approaches with national partners including integrated centers & platforms. In this project, UNICEF will give priority to adolescents and youth in the newly accessible areas in Dara’a. Since the end of September 2021, the humanitarian situation and access to Dar’a Al-Balad and towns in Dar’a have progressively improved. These will include Rural Damascus, northern Homs, southern Hama, east and northeast of Aleppo, Deer EZzour and Idleb. UNICEF will expand its support to young people in NES, focusing on returnees and displaced people in Raqqa and Hasakah, including those in camps, collective shelters and urban settings. During 2021, IDPs have begun returning from the camps to their places of origin in Raqqa. Projects engaging young people will be essential needs for their effective integration in their communities. In addition, since 2014, many young people have been staying in camps and collective shelters. They do not enjoy the freedom of movement and face other risks, thus offering them activities that will enhance their skills and allow them to catch up to their peers in terms of skills and non-formal education. UNICEF will focus on the most deprived areas in rural and urban areas. These will include camps (Hol, Roj, Newroz, Areesha, Mahmoudli) in Hasakah and Raqqa, the collective shelters in Haskah city, and the rural areas in Hasakh Qamishli, Malikia, Yaroubia, and Raqqa Governorate. The programme will coordinate with and support other UNICEF programmes to ensure interventions are tailored to the particular needs of young people. The programme will implement activities for young people affected by, or at high risk of, emergencies or the socio-economic situation, to acquire the right skills, become empowered and resilient community members who help their communities to cope with emergencies. Life-skills and citizenship education for these adolescents will equip and empower them to act as both participants and catalysts of change in their community as volunteers and peer influencers.

4 - Needs

Changing demographic trends in Syria make it important for the country to promote development and participation of adolescents and youth, to enable them to develop to their full potential and transition to adulthood responsibly. However, external factors such as the severe economic situation during years of conflict and imposition of sanctions, limited employment, and livelihoods opportunities, as well as social norms related to gender, inhibit their transition into productive and engaged citizenship as adults. Adolescents and youth in Syria are facing additional critical issues that increase their vulnerability, decrease their resilience and limits the realization of their rights, including a critical weakness in basic services, particularly those responsive to the specific needs including with regards to education and learning, technical and vocational skills development, mentoring and leadership; weak institutional support to adolescents to develop social, civic and economic participation skills and to provide them with opportunities for positive participation; high unemployment rate among adolescents and youth, and it is significantly higher among girls. Adolescents and youth in Syria lack opportunities for skills development, engagement, and participation. Many adolescents and youth are poorly skilled, and do not acquire adequate educational and professional assets to successfully navigate the important transition into adulthood and sustainable livelihoods.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will support the 300,000 vulnerable adolescents and youth, girls, and boys, to social, civic, economic, and digital engagement services and opportunities. This includes(1)youth led- initiatives will aim to improve the overall well-being of young people (individually and collectively and enhance their contribution to resilience and social cohesion in their communities as well as to address their issue of concerns for instance; support adolescents-led community initiatives/actions including hygiene promotion campaigns and menstrual hygiene management, climate change, COVID-19 response activities, harassment, and abuse.(2)Social cohesion activities include promotion of harmony through cultural and sports for development activities at community level, youth-led community dialogue, volunteer action. The project will support the youth-led research and evidence to collect and analysis data which will support them to conduct their own actions.Young people will access skill-development activities to empower them for butter social and economic engagement including entrepreneurship, leadership skills.The project will support the employability of young people and provide them with opportunities for economic self-empowerment. These include creating fora for adolescents and youth to generate economic or social value for themselves as individuals, or for their peer groups and communities. Initiatives also include seed funding to support youth to run their social and business project.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Over a decade of crisis, young Syrians have experienced tragic events that affected their resilience and led many, especially girls to adopt negative coping mechanisms (dropping out of school, addictions, child labour and early marriage). Community resistance in many locations due to social norms leads to preventing girls or people with disabilities from participating in the sport/cultural/engagement activities. Also, COVID-19 and the challenging economic situation has further magnified these risks. UNICEF continue to mainstream the principles of safety, dignity and do no harm as well as the inclusive and meaningful access of the most marginalized boys and girls especially those who live with disability to make sure that no one is left behind. UNICEF will focus on assessing, identifying, and responding to medicate those risks through its need-driven and gender-sensitive programmes to address the most challenging concerns and social norms. UNICEF will increase the safe access to skills development, social cohesion, awareness-raising and engagement opportunities. UNICEF will prioritize developing the capacity of the implementing partners through providing technical support in the fields of Child Safeguarding, Code of Conduct and PSEA. UNICEF will work with parents & local leaders to ensure equitable access to services and participation of boys and girls in addition support the enabling learning environment including trusted centers by community.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF has its PSEA policy in place and ensures establishing mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio pf protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance networking for the benefit of people at risk and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in dignified way is integrated in project activities. Consequently, as per-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plan are guaranteed.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1) Deterioration of economic situation and barriers to access the centers due to lack of transportation as a result of the current challenging situation of fuel has a high likelihood and impact on the young participants. UNICEF will support mobile interventions and outreach approaches to reach the most affected people and support the centers which are in the areas where vulnerable adolescents live.2)Potential conflicts between IDPs and host communities as well as the instability of security situation in some areas have a medium likelihood.UNICEF will continue to promote social cohesion service and youth-led initiative and awareness-raising to mitigate such risks. 3) Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when delivering assistance and service or in case access to services or information are linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. measures: Raise capacities of staff and volunteers for implementing partners and third-party monitors, as well as targeting communities with awareness about SEA risks. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff and third-party monitors. Design complaint mechanisms and ensure adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality. Assumptions: Integrated and joint approaches with other sections (CP and education) are supported including system strengthening, scalable and sustainable approaches. Youth participation across the country is promoted.
