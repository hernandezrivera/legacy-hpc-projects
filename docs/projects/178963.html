Project Summary

The project aims at reducing vulnerability and improving physical and mental well-being of crisis-affected population with particular attention to specific vulnerabilities linked to Disability, Gender, and Age (DGA). The organization’s action focuses on providing multi-disciplinary health services tailored to the needs of people with physical and functional limitations and their caregivers through direct intervention or through the support of local partners in North-West, North-East and GoS-controlled parts of Syria. The intervention includes provision of multidisciplinary physical rehabilitation services (including the provision of assistive mobility devices, prostheses & orthotics and education sessions for caregivers) and the psychosocial support services to physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and their caregivers. Furthermore, to ensure continuity of the tailored treatment plans Tele-rehabilitation/Tele-PSS sessions in case of access restrictions, due to COVID-19. Improving access to services through referral and accessibility work into health structures. These services help in recovery of physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and support the caregivers in a holistic approach in order to alleviate stress, fatigue and other psychological impacts related to their responsibilities. The organization mainstreams COVID-19 Key messaging in the activities package according to WHO Guidelines.
In GoS- controlled areas and North-west Syria the intervention include the provision of Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to people suffering from psychological distress without having physical rehabilitation needs. MHPSS services include psychological first aid, psych education, and individual and/or group session therapy. Noting that in GoS- controlled areas, the organization focuses on developing rehabilitation services in areas where services are not available.
Concerning the DGA mainstreaming, all activities and delivered services are prepared based on a rapid assessment to study the sensitivity of the context and ensure inclusion of Persons according to DGA. This includes the promotion of equal opportunities for people with different needs and abilities. Conditional cash for transportation is also provided to patients and caregivers according to individual assessed needs based on pre-defined criteria. HI will closely coordinate with the Cash working group in the targeted hub to align the common standards and guidance. conditional cash will be provided to patients and/or caregivers based on individual assistance and HI criteria taking into account socioeconomic, travel, clinical factors.
The health staff assess the needs case by case based on fixed criteria. The cash is conditioned to the attendance of patient to the treatment sessions aiming to ensure the finalization of the treatment plan. In parallel, the organization provides support to its local health partners through technical, financial and managerial support and the capacity building tailored to the needs of partners, to ensure the quality of the delivered services and the sustainability of impact. While the outreach teams reach out to IDPs; and underserved communities; as well as persons who are incapable of reaching the service centers.
In addition to the rehab and inclusion specialist support to the health clusters, by guiding through several session on how to implement DGA inclusion principles and how to identify vulnerability risks. In addition to trainings related to understanding needs of persons with disabilities mainly women.

4 - Needs

Health and protection needs have been aggravated by continuity of hostilities in 2020; persistence of localized security incidents in NES; Spread of COVID-19 increased the stress on the health system aggravating affected people vulnerability. 12.4M of people are need of health assistance according with HNO/HRP2021.Key health infrastructures have been damaged as well as limiting access to lifesaving services. From HNO/HRP 2021 , 28 attacks of health facilities have been reported and 50% of healthcare workers estimated to have fled the country. In addition to the issue of the non-functional facilities which is particularly alarming in Idleb, Deir Ezzor , Homs, Rural Damascus and Aleppo. As a result, PWDs face difficulties accessing rehabilitation services due to absence in functional health facilities. Only 5% of partially and fully functional facilities offer Physical Rehabilitation Services. The INGO data (Aug 18- Dec 20) indicates that 42% and 58% of the beneficiaries are respectively people with recent Injuries and with long term disabilities. Access to health was reported among the top priorities by 60% of households with at least one family member with disability. Only 62% of them reported having access to health. Main barriers to access health services included lack of existing services, or available but too expensive, or lack of finances to cover transportation. For mental health, crisis continues to affect psychosocial well-being of those affected by displacement, exposure to violence, loss of income and reduced access to basic services, touching the youngest in particular. The psychosocial support has been reported as the 2nd protection priority need by 50% of people in NWS and 43% in NES.

5 - Implementation of activities

Provide primary and secondary health care service including Trauma care. Persons in needs of services are identified through other services (i.e.presence in health facilities, coordination fora, partners) and through outreach teams. The INGO will provide identification and needs assessment of persons with injuries and/or disabilities in hospitals, rehab centres, IDP camps, communities. The INGO will deliver multidisciplinary rehab services adapted to needs of each beneficiary, through gender balanced teams, including: physical and functional rehab sessions, provision of assistive devices, production and provision of prosthetic and orthotic devices, education sessions and/or complementary PSS sessions (also to caregivers) and provision of orientation and PSS sessions to caregivers. The INGO will provide MHPSS who suffer from psychosocial and psychological issues caused by conflict, displacement and multiple losses. The health service will be provided with the support of 6 rehab centers in GoS, 5 Hospitals in NES, and 7 hospitals and 2 rehab centers in NWS. In addition to 2 mobile teams in NES and 15 in NWS. And 10 community workers in NES and NWS. The INGO will strengthen the national technical capacity through technical support specially on the identification of barriers/discriminatory practices, production of inclusion assessment report with practical recommendations and action plan, training, coaching and follow ups. In aim to improve the inclusiveness of their services. Along the MEAL role in monitoring the quality of implementation through regular field visits and remote monitoring tools and Knowledge, attitude and practices (KAP) and satisfaction surveys. In addition to Feedback and Response Mechanism.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Protection of field staff and partners, the INGO keeps low visibility to protect the identities of its staff. In case of an attack on health care, the INGO activates its Duty of Care policy and provide the field staff with psychological support to prevent burn out.•PSEA, the INGO applies PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. The INGO also raises awareness on organization’s policy on PSEA and its complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. As part of inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to the organizations’ staff and its partners to highlight specific vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has a protection task force, which ensures protection mainstreaming is optimized. Additionally, referral task force also works towards improving protection referrals and its processes.•Healthcare Provision for vulnerable groups and equitable access to health services; staff is regularly trained on Humanitarian principles and how to interact, communicate and engage with people with various disabilities and needs. •Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data. •Working through implementing partners; before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted to the context. •Lack of local community acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with the community. The INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and always make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in its programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure the satisfaction of the community with its services. •Loss of funding is mitigated by the organization’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding. •The eventual spread-out of COVID-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this risk, the organization and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available in targeted locations; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging including DGA ; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster•The available budget reduction due to exchange rate loss is managed by the organization continuous use of USD value for money transfer and payment as well as, close monitoring the local market flow.• Logistic and fund transfer issues in addition to supply chain interruption, mitigated by assessing transfer partners and enhancing coordination with INGOs and prepare contingency stock.• Mismanagement for cash mitigated:1) individual monitoring of beneficiaries, 2) FRM and anti fraud corrupt ion policy.
