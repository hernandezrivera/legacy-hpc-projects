Project Summary

The project aims at alleviating the negative effects of the country multi-level crisis on the accessibility to and quality of learning opportunities for Lebanese, PRL and migrants children. The action encompasses community-based activities to ensure children continue, return or are integrated in education opportunities, formal or non-formal. OOEC, children at risk of drop out, children with learning loss and with special education needs will be identified through a needs assessment at HH, schools and NFE centers’ level in Bourj Hammoud and Sin El Fil (El-Metn district), building on CESVI and JRS Lebanon presence in the areas with education, protection and livelihoods since 2014. The assessment will include a protection risk analysis and will lead to the identification of beneficiaries to be targeted under each activity. 700 individuals outreached will be engaged in BTS awareness and engagement activities and referred to public formal education or Multiple Flexible Pathways, supporting – in line with MEHE National Plan – the transition towards the formal system. In these regards, CESVI and JRS will ensure close coordination with public school principals within Bourj Hammoud and Sin El Fil for the continuity of/reintegration into formal education. 220 parents will be targeted with awareness raising activities, on education and protection: CESVI will lead a “know-your-rights campaign” providing information regarding available services and referral mechanisms with key messages on the right to an inclusive and fair education; JRS will provide parents with protection and MHPSS awareness sessions to promote the children self-protection capacities and to manage potential shocks caused by the crisis). 300 children will receive regulated retention support through Remedial Education and Homework Support, in JRS education center. Classes will include English, French, Science and Mathematics as well as homework support. In addition, remedial education will serve as a safe space for children at risk of sexual and economic exploitation. Hygiene kits will be provided to vulnerable Lebanese and migrants children enrolled in the Retention Support Program. Hygiene kits will include items such as shampoo, combs, toothbrushes, toothpaste, soap, deodorant, and menstrual pads. JRS will be complementing the Retention Support Program by providing psychosocial support sessions (PSS) to 80 students, equipping them with tools for the management of stress, grief and loss, depression and anxiety, trauma, and for healthily express emotions. Life skills messages will be mainstreamed throughout sessions. 12 teachers of the NFE Center run by JRS will be trained on both education and child protection topics to strengthen their skills and therefore to meet the needs of their students. To improve and facilitate access to more inclusive education opportunities, 50 assistive technologies and devices will be provided to most vulnerable children with disabilities: the items will be directly provided to the HHs when personal use only is advisable, to NFE center when they can be shared among several children attending education and/or retention support – to maximize the impact. Adequate training will be provided to guarantee a correct use of the devices. Unrestricted CA to cover education related costs will be provided to HHs for 300 children engaged in retention support. The amount of 20 USD*child is aligned with the cost calculated by UNICEF for covering education-related costs. The activity led by CESVI, who has extensive experience in conducting CA programs, intends to foster children retention by reducing economic barriers. The activity aims at targeting children attending retention support. Targeting approaches and transfer modalities will be coordinated with the CfE Task Force, which CESVI is regularly attending (ideally, assistance will be disbursed in USD, thought OMT, with whom CESVI has an already existing agreement).

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The following activities will be conducted in Bourj Hammoud/Sin El Fil al area. OUTCOME 1: 1.1) Back-to-School campaigns for intensive advocacy and communication efforts targeting 700 children – the activity includes the conduction of a need assessment to identify out of education Lebanese and migrants students, including children at risk of drop out. 1.2) 300 children are supported with cash for education in order to return to or remain in school – the activity will be targeting students in need receiving retention support from JRS. 1.4) 300 children receiving regulated retention support through Remedial Education and Homework Support. 1.5) children with disabilites provided with assistive technology and devises in formal and non-formal education. 1.6 220 parents/caregivers engaged in awareness sessions aiming to improve their involvement in the education of children. 1.7) Provision of hygiene kits to the students attending retention support in the NFE center of JRS. OUTCOME 2: 2.1) 12 teachers of JRS education center are trained on education and protection-related topics. 2.3) 80 children receiving PSS and life skills training.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CESVI and JRS will adopt the DNH principle as outlined in each organization's Code of Conduct and policies including, but not limited to, Child Safeguarding and PSEA. Partners will jointly conduct a project dedicated safeguarding risks analysis at the inception phase as per available tools aimed at identifying safeguarding risks, activate effective preventive measure and monitor effectiveness of such measures in the implementation of activities. The program will adopt a gender-sensitive approach in all phases (stakeholder analysis, needs assessment, sampling, selection, implementation, MEAL). Gender and protection cross-cutting issues (differentiated impact, opportunities, threats, capacities, coping mechanisms and other protection issues), are mainstreamed through all the phases of PCM.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

A project Complaint & Feedback mechanism, inclusive of child friendly tools, will be put in place. The structure of the CFM system will be shared with relevant stakeholders to ensure that it is built based on a participatory approach and that multiple communication channels are accessible and in place. The information on CFM will be provided verbally by project team during activities and by sharing leaflets (inclusive of child friendly version). Affected people and stakeholders will be enabled to provide feedback and complaints through accessible formats, languages and channels, such as a dedicated hotline, a CFM email, CFM boxes placed in strategic locations, and verbally to project team. Frontline workers will be trained to guarantee a transparent and safe sharing of information with regards to the assistance provided (duration, value, frequency, eligibility…). Referrals will be done through the Inter Agency system, noting that the accreditation of CESVI on RIMS is ongoing.
