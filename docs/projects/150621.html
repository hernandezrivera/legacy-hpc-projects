Project Summary

This intervention is an immediate response to protect women, children and men from external shocks affecting their food security status and livelihoods. The targeted beneficiaries are vulnerable small-scale farmers who base their income on agricultural activities and the viability of these livelihoods are currently threatened by the rapidly degrading economic context.
This project has been developed to contribute to the achievement of the three FSS objectives (O1) Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced, (O2) Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs, and (O3) FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, and partnership with key national ministries is strengthened.
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

Needs

From July to August 2018, AAH conducted 3 participatory context assessments in Rafah, Khan Yunis and Deir Albalah Governorates, as well as a desk review of 2018 secondary data sources. The analysis confirmed a severe degradation of the humanitarian situation in the Gaza Strip, with increased HH debt levels, reduced cash flow and increased expenses for small business owners. These exacerbate the protracted crisis already felt by the continued blockade and put at extreme risk already vulnerable HHs. Said assessments analysed socioeconomic data from existing livelihoods beneficiaries from both female and male-headed HHs, as well as qualitative information from small-scale farmers across the 5 Gaza governorates. The analysis of these assessments took into account sex and age disaggregated data and found that the average monthly income of sample profile was 546 NIS. Of the sample, 30% have started to sell HH assets; 40% are receiving assistance from family/friends; 87% have debts; and 72% declared debts for food. This evidence of deepening poverty has impacted HH capacity to create jobs and improve their production capacity. Interviewed stated that they have estimated a reduction of their cultivated area by 30% in 2018, which directly impacts the number of workers that can be hired. Those workers are also farmers who don’t have agricultural assets and depend on wages to meet their basic needs. Only 2% of interviewees had a temporary job during the assessed period and 80% of the interviewed farmers indicated that despite the need for workers they had to reduce the number of hired workers and working days by 50-70%. During the assessed period, farmers stated that agricultural workers are earning no more than 5 NIS per hour (30NIS/Day= 600NIS/month). A reduction of 47.9% according to PCBS minimum wage. According to the Labour Force Survey (PCBS, 2018), the average daily wage for wage employees in Gaza Strip in the 2nd quarter 2018 is 62.6 NIS. Business transactions are conducted by debt, due to a lack of cash on the market, resulting in farmers obtaining agricultural inputs (fertilizers, seeds, etc.) by contracting debts with suppliers who request shorter repayment periods (reduced from 6 to 3 months). Depending on the timing of the agricultural season, it becomes too risky to continue business and many small-scale farms start to fail, leaving fallow unproductive lands. In some cases, suppliers refuse to accept credit, and this reduces the obtainable agricultural inputs by up to 40%, reducing productivity. The 4 hours of electricity per day available from the power grid reduces water availability to agricultural land, as power is necessary to operate water wells and irrigation systems. The impacts experienced are: (i) dryness which delays crop development, (ii) up to 40% loss of crops, (iii) plant diseases (white root, late blight, powdery mildew) and (iv) up to 30% reduction of the planted area. 15% stated that they tried to improve the electricity shortage by using generators, but the financial burden for them is too high and not sustainable (3 litter/15NIS/day). In urban areas, where small or micro-businesses fall into three main economic sectors: production, commercial and services, the main needs observed in 2018 include less liquid cash circulating in the market and increased unemployment. This has directly caused profit to decrease by 55%, due to a reduced capacity to buy products and services. Subsequently, 95% of the businesses from the AAH assessment suffer from more frequent credit transactions – both buying stocks and selling goods – which in many cases results in customers defaulting on payments. Finally, to compound the impact of reduced profits, businesses have seen an increase in expenses related to electricity. In order to prevent vulnerable households from falling into deep poverty, support is required to increase productivity for rural farming households and stimulate cash flow.

Activities

The planned activities under this project were developed to be implemented/delivered to the most vulnerable households (or affected people) in three packages of support: (i) Immediate relief responses to alleviate immediate needs by supporting vulnerable households to react and cope with shocks that are affecting their food security status; and therefore enable households to protect their livelihoods, increase productivity and improve their food and nutrition security status (immediate relief responsive actions=70%). (ii) Activities that enhance people’s medium-term livelihood protection (high impact remedial actions=20%), while ensuring adequate food security and livingconditions, restoring people’s dignity, preventing secondary shocks and risks, with a particular focus on preventing the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. (iii) Create and improve a consolidation of structural changes in the described context of vulnerability (long-term actions for environment building=10%). Actions under this part will conduct a change in household attitudes while addressing the underlying causes of humanitarian vulnerabilities. A1: Immediate/urgent relief actions to rehabilitate existing and not fully functioning key productive assets. This activity will target 210 vulnerable households (145 male-headed and 65 female-headed) in Rafah, Khan Yunis, Deir Albalah and Gaza Governorates. Small scale farmers that are facing the consequences of the current lack of economic resources in Gaza Strip which has squeezed their capacity to repair or conduct good maintenance for their key agricultural infrastructures such as productive lands, water ponds, irrigation systems, etc. The electricity shortage is affecting their productivity and their capacity to create agricultural jobs. Because of that, under this activity, the selected families will be supported to rehabilitate 280 dunums of agricultural open lands (150 farmers: 120 males and 30 female), 30 water ponds (30 farmers: 25 males and 5 females), and 60 solar units (30 of those will be installed in the rehabilitated water ponds) will be installed to reduce expenses and mitigate livelihoods impact from the electricity shortage affecting poultry farmers and improve the irrigation capacity of the beneficiaries. Additionally, farmers will be also provided with an emergency package of agricultural inputs such as fertilizers, pesticides, seeds and tools. A2: Providing support to enhance people’s medium-term livelihood protection while ensuring adequate food security and living conditions. Under this activity, a range of cash transfer options will be established to support economically vulnerable households in the targeted area to help them to prevent secondary shocks and risks, with a particular focus on preventing the adoption of negative copings mechanisms. Thus, this activity will target 275 economically poor households (225 males and 50 females) particularly encouraging the involvement and ownership of women and girls to be supported in order restore livelihood actions that are affected by the current dire economic situation. The typology of actions to be implemented will be de ned in participation with the families. Based on the context analysis conducted to prepare this project and the consulted families’ suggestions the actions are Cash for Work opportunities and cash to expand the type of sources of household’s income and production level. A3: Enhancing the nutritional practices of the families in the targeted areas through ongoing community awareness campaigns addressing the speci c needs of women, men, boys, and girls (approximately 1500 HH). Under this activity, selected HH will bene t from technical training sessions on agroecology and organic farming. Additionally, Community awareness campaigns will be conducted to improve practices and increase knowledge to nutrition.

Indicators

The proposed intervention will provide immediate relief responses to support vulnerable HHs (women, children, and men) so they can react and cope with shocks that are affecting their food security status; therefore enable to protect their livelihoods, increase productivity and improve their food and nutrition security status. HHs who based their income on agriculture activities and facing problems to provide maintenance and cannot rehabilitate their key productive assets. Farmers who are at risk of food insecurity, whose livelihoods were affected by the current situation, with family members unable to work (with physical or mental disabilities, medical cases), with a high number of dependents and do not have a stable source of income. Additionally, to do no harm and improve the overall protection of targeted communities. The expected results are the following: Improved production and productivity capacity for vulnerable farmers: rehabilitation of agricultural open lands, water ponds, installation of alternative source of electricity, and provision of an emergency package of agricultural inputs. Increased capacity of families to prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms: Cash transfers options in order to support economically vulnerable households in order to develop CfW opportunities, cash to expand the type of sources of household’s income and production level. Increased awareness, knowledge and practices, improved appropriate nutritional practices, agroecology and organic farming: The core promotion messages will be technical training on organic farming, management of sustainable agroecosystems, reduction of using chemicals in the crop production, nutrition to reduce the need for responsive activities in the future. All the above mentioned technical trainings will follow the priorities set by the MOA in Gaza. Through FSS and the local partner, the project will establish a communication channel to ensure full coordination and with the relevant contact in MoA. Base line and end line surveys and general project monitoring and evaluation will be carried out throughout the entire project in coordination with other agencies operating in the area; AAH will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community and to be used for advocacy purposes. Expected indicators and targets are: • 280 dunums made productive or with improved production capacity (full and partial land rehabilitation, piping and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, etc.) • 1200 m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided • 180 kW of renewable power supply systems installed. •. • 485 farmers, herders, breeders, receiving inputs and services (Individuals, based on HH average size: Gaza Strip 5.6 unless specific data available.) • 20 localities improved their capacity to react and cope with shocks affecting their food security status through a timely access to immediate relief responses. • Living conditions for 11,166 women, men, girls and boys are improved. To measure the improvement of the living conditions of the BNFs, AAH will conduct a baseline and endline survey. Both exercises will track and compare the following core proxy indicators: (i) Coping Strategy Index (CSI), (ii) Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS), (iii) Household Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS), Food Consumption Score (FCS) and Minimum Dietary Diversity for Women (24-MDD-W). In order to ensure accountability, AAH has a Beneciary Feedback Mechanism (FBM) as well as a PSEA mechanism, allowing beneciaries to provide their comments and complaints on the activities and is designed to increase their protection, in compliance with sensitive data management and adhering to “Do No Harm”. A phone number will be distributed to the target communities enabling BNFs to report feedback or complaints relating to project implementation.
