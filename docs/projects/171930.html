Project Summary

The aim of the project “Food and Winterization Assistance for Conflict-Affected People along the ‘contact line’ in Donetska and Luhanska NGCA” is to cover basic food and winterization needs of conflict-affected populations in the NGCA areas along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine.
The project will be implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine directly (in case of accreditation) or through local partners, which will be identified in the first half of 2021.
The food component of the project aims at addressing the most urgent food needs of the conflict-affected populations by providing food sets to 1000 persons during the project duration (July-December 2021). There will be two caseloads, each receiving 3 months of assistance. Whereas Caseload 1 (500 people) will be supported during July-September 2021, Caseload 2 (500 people) will be supported during October-December 2021.
The winterization component of the project aims at addressing the most urgent winterization needs of the conflict-affected populations by providing winter clothing sets to 1000 persons for the winter season of 2021-2022.
By providing food sets and personal insulation, the proposed project will contribute to the FSL Cluster Objective 1 “Ensure immediate access to food for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict and/or СOVID-19” and Shelter/NFI Cluster Objective 1 “Address gaps in essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency”.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. As identified by the FSL Cluster, immediate access to food is needed by approximately 210,700 people in both GCA and NGCA. Out of them, 78,700 people reside in in the 0-20 km zone along the ‘contact line’ in Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA (58,200 and 20,700 persons respectively). According to the estimate by the FSLC, the food needs are the most severe in NGCA of Donetska oblast (severity scale 5).The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by REACH on NGCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in February 2020 identified 8% of urban households as food insecure (9% in Donetsk NGCA and 7% in Luhansk NGCA). Although women are identified as most vulnerable, men demonstrated poor scores in food security indicators. Thus, as for urban households in Donetsk NGCA, 22% of men in have poor or borderline Food Consumption Score (FCS), compared to 11% women. In Luhansk NGCA, on the contrary, women are more food insecure than men (10% and 3% respectively). According to the MSNA, food belongs to the main household expenditure in NGCA, with almost 50% of household income spent on food. The closure of the ‘contact line’ in relation to COVID-19 challenged the economic status of older people (estimated by REACH at approximately 500,000 persons), who rely on pensions from the Government of Ukraine. As identified by the Shelter/NFI Cluster, winterization assistance is needed by approximately 174,000 people in both GCA and NGCA. Out of them, 42,864 people reside in Donetska and Luhanska NGCA. According to the Shelter/NFI Cluster’s estimate for 2021, the most severe needs will be in the areas along the ‘contact line’, namely 5-km zone in the GCA and 20-km zone in the NGCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (severity scale 3 or 4). ASB will target primarily those beneficiaries who meet vulnerability criteria (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households) and/or those households who have low scores on food security indicators. The assistance will be provided based on the particular needs of men, women, boys and girls, including their food security and vulnerability status. The food assistance aims at the household level, so all gender and age groups will be covered. However, households of older men and women and persons with disabilities as well as female-headed households will be assessed for assistance in the first place.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Purpose: to meet food and winterization needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern UkraineOutput: vulnerable households along the ‘contact line’ received food and winterization assistance. Outcome: target households improve their food security and preparedness to winter.Activity 1. Distribution of food sets The beneficiary identification will be conducted in two rounds, summer and autumn 2021, in Donetsk and Luhansk NGCA. There will be two caseloads of 500 people, each receiving 3 months of assistance. Whereas Caseload 1 (500 people) will be supported during July-September 2021, Caseload 2 (500 people) will be supported during October-December 2021. Thus, 1000 people will be supported during the project duration. The composition of a food set will follow current recommendation of the FSLC. The caloric value of the food basket will cover a daily norm of 1900 kcal, with the cost of 996 UAH (approximately 36 USD) per person per month.The food sets will be procured in GCA and follow national quality standards. In line with the CARI methodology of the WFP, each household member will be provided with a food set. The duration of assistance (3 months) corresponds the minimal duration of assistance as recommended by the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster. The beneficiary identification will be done through the survey of the head of household on the food consumption patterns and coping strategies. Since female-headed households generally demonstrate high levels of vulnerability, women will particularly benefit from the project activities.The project foresees also home delivery of food assistance to older people with disabilities and/or homebound persons. Activity 2. NFI distribution to cover critical winterization needsSince the purchasing capacity of vulnerable households in NGCA is limited, also because of COVID-19 pandemic and travel restrictions, ASB will improve their preparedness to winter by providing them with personal insulation kits. The composition of the kit (winter jacket, felt boots, thick socks, woolen hat, woolen scarf and thermal underwear) will be in line with the current recommendation of the Shelter/NFI Cluster (129 USD per person). Thus, 1000 vulnerable persons will be provided with winter clothing sets in October-November 2021. The project foresees also home delivery of NFIs to older people with disabilities and/or homebound persons. Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the utilization of the aid provided. A feedback mechanism will be available throughout the project to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. All complaints will be recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation. PDM findings will be used to improve the programme.
