Project Summary

This project aims at providing access to temporary and sustained water supply, safe
sanitary facilities and increased awareness on safe hygiene practices to IDPs, host
communities and returnees affected by drought, floods and conflict in Middle Shabelle,
Hiraan, Kismayo, Baidoa and IDP settlements in Banadir. Water vouchers will be provided
to 20,500 people and 23 strategic water points will be constructed/rehabilitated. The
water sources will be located 30m away from latrines to avoid contamination. Dewatering
of 30 wells and application of shock chlorination will be done in flood-affected areas. To
strengthen community ownership and ensure sustainability of WASH assets 50 water
management committee members (50% women) will be trained on operation and
maintenance. The project staff and WASH committee members will also be trained on
DRR and protection mainstreaming. 97 gender-sensitive latrines with hand-washing
facilities, raised to avoid flooding and fitted with locks from the inside to curb GBV will be
constructed in IDP camps, schools and health/nutrition centers. CLTS approach will be
introduced where no latrines will be constructed. Community members particularly women
and girls will be consulted in the siting and design of WASH facilities. 5000 hygiene kits
will be distributed to targeted high-risk families i.e. families with malnourished children
and affected by AWD. 600 hygiene promoters will be trained in the PHAST method and
will carry out community hygiene campaigns. WOCCA will continuously monitor the
Shabelle river and disseminate early warning and hygiene messages through radio and
SMS when floods are imminent. Sensitization meetings will be held at the inception of the
project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them
about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary
entitlement and roles. Accountability to the affected population will be enhanced through
the provision of a feedback hotline to beneficiaries where they can provide feedback and

Project Activities

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement IDPs displaced by the recent floods, drought and the ongoing conflict in the targeted regions face serious protection risks particularly women, children, and marginalized groups. They are at risk of exclusion from accessing basic humanitarian services at the camps. Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries, discrimination from host communities, lack ofaccess to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement, IDPs lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. The project will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Using participatory beneficiary targeting and selection approaches cognizant of age, diversity, disability, vulnerability and gender-specific needs, to ensure the most vulnerable and minority groups are targeted 2. Recruiting individuals from both the host and IDP populations in the implementation of the project in order to benefit the local economy as well as to ensure a non-discriminatory recruitment process (Do no harm) 3. Conducting sensitization meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age Women, girls and other at-risk groups face an increased risk of sexual assault and violence while traveling to water points and sanitation facilities that are located farfrom homes or placed in isolated locations. Lack of lighting, locks, privacy and/or sexsegregated sanitation facilities can increase the risk of harassment or assault against women, girls and boys. In IDP camps tensions with the host communities may arise over water resources or during the distribution of hygiene supplies, this can lead to violence against women and girls who are often responsible for household water and hygiene. The project will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Ensuring that women, girls are consulted in deciding on the location and design of latrines and washing areas to maximize safety, privacy and dignity. 2. Constructing gender separated sanitation facilities, with lighting and equipped with lockable doors for privacy and safety 2. Establishing water and/or sanitation committees composed of 50% women, to be responsible for the operation and maintenance of the facilities .3. Ensuring water and sanitation facilities are centrally located for easy accessibility bywomen, girls, the sick, elderly and people with disability. Feedback and complaint mechanismWOCCA will establish a hotline that will be used throughout the project. The line will be operational from the beginning of the project used by an employee not directly involved in this specific project. The number will be shared with all beneficiaries, direct or indirect in the project area. This way a beneficiary has the option to inform WOCCA about project related issues whilst an obligation of WOCCA towards responding to the beneficiary is guaranteed.
