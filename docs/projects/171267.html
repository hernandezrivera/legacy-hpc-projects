Project Summary

The project aims to address the current deteriorating needs of farmers and herders and fisherfolks in Gaza and north Gaza Governorates suffering from livelihood asset depletion, increased challenges due to deteriorating socio-economic conditions resulting from the continuous blockade and the unfolding of the impact of the Covid-19 crisis. The project will address Country HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 3 “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.” and will be implemented in partnership with UAWC, a Palestinian local NGO that has a strong experience in implementing agricultural activities across the Gaza Strip and a prolonged experience in humanitarian assistance and livelihood support interventions. The project aims to enhance the productive capacities of 485 farmers, herders, poultry farmers and fisherfolks to sustain food security and increase their livelihood resilience (benefiting a total of 2668 individuals). Activities will include land rehabilitation, greenhouse rehabilitation, fishing boat rehabilitation, poultry farm rehabilitation, small ruminant shed rehabilitation, and installation of solar energy to water wells and to poultry farms. The project will utilize cash for work modalities as means to provide cash injections to vulnerable men and women (140, 100 men and 40 women) to enable them to protect their livelihood activities and secure their immediate basic needs. The project activities were coordinated with FSS coordinator and with the ministry of agriculture (MoA) and are designed following consultations carried out with FSS partners who have similar activities. Areas of intervention are khan younis, Deir El Balah, Gaza, and north Gaza governorates and were prioritized based on the FSS priority matrix and in initial consultations conducted are as follows: The project is in line with the FSS’s specific objective 1 “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced” and Specific Objective 2 “Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs”.
All proposed activities can be implemented under the COVID19 current crisis according to SoPs that will be developed based on the level of the outbreak during the implementation period and the associated risks. Oxfam and UAWC have been adapting their projects to fit COVID19 restrictions and implications ensuring safety of beneficiaries, staff and wider community.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip during the second quarter of 2020 increased and reached 49.1% from 45.5% since the previous quarter which is a record in the region. The poverty level in Gaza is four times higher than in the West Bank and female headed HH’s in Gaza experience a poverty rate 1.3 times higher than males. For livelihood groups, the continuous Israeli blockade on Gaza, restrictions on movement and on access to restricted areas affected fisherfolks, farmers and herders, damaged their livelihoods and exposed them to high risks of depletion and abandonment. Women continue to suffer the negative impact of the prolonged humanitarian crisis resulting in having their participation in the labor market in Gaza dramatically lower than men which is less than 15% participation rate in the second quarter of 2020, of which, 78%, are unemployed. Electricity cuts continue to affect farmers in accessing water to irrigate their land causing additional unaffordable costs for many small scale and vulnerable farmers. Herders in Gaza also continue to struggle limited grazing areas, and poor or non-existent animal shelters causing re-occurring deaths and illnesses to small ruminants in particular. Poor poultry farming environments and access to services mostly affect small scale poultry farmers – chicken specifically- who are already challenged by the market that is controlled by large scale farmers. Oxfam and UAWC have carried out consultations with FSS actors working in addressing these problems, and have initially coordinated with the MoA to support and facilitate the prioritization of activities and target areas. The MoA has provided UAWC with data on potential water wells that need rehabilitation and adaptation to solar energy and has also provided a list of priorities that are already identified in the target areas. The project activities are aligned with the FSS priority matrix of activities identified per location that were agreed by the FSS partners, and the MoA and MoSD during consultations conducted in the HNO discussions. Further assessment using focus group discussions and consultation interviews will be carried out at the start of the project to re-validate these specific needs. In the nominated areas and affected farmers, herders and fisherfolks will be requested to apply to receive project assistance through the project community committees and in close liaison with the MoA in the areas of Khan Younis, Gaza, Deir El Balah and North Gaza governorates.

2 - Activities

Project activities will address the complex vulnerability of livelihoods by addressing problems of poor infrastructure, depletion of livelihood assets and resources of vulnerable livelihood groups and increasing unemployment across the population. Activities include support to open land, and greenhouse agricultural activities and its related access to water resources and irrigation systems in addition to adapting to renewable energy when relevant (i.e water wells, and poultry farms) to respond to the power crisis. Activities will also include rehabilitation of poultry and small ruminant farms/sheds, and fishing boats. Fishing boats rehabilitation and land and greenhouse rehabilitation will be implemented through cash for work activities that will maximize benefit to the target population. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides wider access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive feedback either online, through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in-line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting procedures, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam has already started adapting its intervention accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19 outbreak. Oxfam’s helpline will be primarily used for reporting any incidents of misconduct fraud, exploitation or abuse which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Beneficiaries will be provided with the information on the available mechanisms and will be encouraged to use them whenever needed. Such information will be also made available in the MoUs that the beneficiaries will sign at the beginning of the project. Moreover, Oxfam will carry out a training on PSEA and safe programming to UAWC’s field team to ensure proper understanding of the use of these mechanisms

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential protection needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available. Technical assessment will take into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also keeping the protection of data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, which is important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on them and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers. Prior to the implementation of the activities project partners will be invited to attend a training session by Oxfam’s Gender and Protection Officer on the overall protection principles and on how to detect GBV, this should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures that will be taken into consideration in advance. Most importantly, activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and in full engagement and consultation of the targeted communities and in liaison with relevant authorities whenever needed and relevant. Specifically, the following steps will be implemented to integrate do no harm throughout the project: 1- Project risk assessment will be prepared at the project start ensuring identification of clear measures for mitigation any risks with beneficiaries, contractors, Oxfam and UAWC staff, community committees and the wider community by the project activities. 2- Active engagement with targeted communities including their representatives, through the project community committees that will include farmer representatives (women CBOs and local committees in the target localities).This approach will be the basis for all steps of the project cycle, that will ensure meaningful representation of women and youth whenever possible to ensure that the project’s design is responsive to the different needs and concerns of the project beneficiaries. 3- Supporting women in accessing the cash for work activities by providing safe and appropriate work opportunities in terms of the location and time. All activities will follow a quick risk assessment which will identify any potential risks related to women’s participation in the activities and identify means to support them within the project period or beyond through a referral mechanism. 4- Cash for work activity will be aligned with child protection policy that will prohibit child labor. It will also promote women participation’s in the agricultural work that is typically un-accounted for, employing the equal access principle of protection and taking in consideration preserving the dignity of targeted women by tailoring the activity in accordance to what is most suitable for them in terms of timing, form, location, method of payment and implications of household and child care burdens5- Activities will be adapted to COVID19 related precautionary measures. COVID19 related SoPs will be integrated in the project design. For example, Oxfam requires as part of implementing projects under COVID19 for project staff, suppliers and volunteers to be trained in Personal Protective Equipment (PPEs) and to sign a consent that they agree to engage in any project activity. Oxfam had offered this training to its partner staff involved in current and pervious projects implemented under COVID19. At the community level, project community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the intervention, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing any bias in selecting, targeting beneficiaries and service distribution. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing on the participation of women in the project’s committees.This will be integrated through - Orientation sessions on the project activities with the project community committees - Dissemination of designated feedback mechanisms designed under this project as part of the partners and Oxfam’s responsibility, - The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: such as the dissemination of the beneficiaries selection criteria (that will focus on scoring the most vulnerable and affected groups such as small scale farmers, having livelihood activity as the sole source of income, HH protection concerns, etc), clarity, orientation of the selection process, announcement to community of activities and procedures to receive benefits, etc.- Developing further the Oxfam's existing feedback mechanisms to improve accessibility and appropriateness of these mechanisms to the specific target groups- Carrying out safe programming training to partner field and project staff that ensures continuous identification of risks and acting upon these risks throughout the project cycle.
