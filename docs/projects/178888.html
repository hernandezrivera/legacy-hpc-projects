Project Summary

Syria today is at a crossroad of the crises with alarming humanitarian needs with beyond expectation increases in the last year with 90% of the country living below the poverty line, HNO 2021stated that 1,716,776 in Rural Damascus are food insecure, an escalated socio-economic crisis, unprecedented currency devaluation, rising inflation, record unemployment and a Covid-19 pandemic captivating community who are trying to recuperate from a decade of conflict. Rescate based its interventions on the FSA activities and indicators per HRP objectives: Support rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities, including bakeries. The current project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities. Rehabilitation of the stopped production line will add production of 14000 bread bundles on daily basis. Direct beneficiaries in the adjacent location who will benefit from the production line in Sbeineh and 8th March compound will reach 11000 HHs.
In close coordination with local stakeholders including Directorate of Bakeries, results show that due to lack of funding, the Ministry of Internal Trade and Customers (MITC), is unable to support the urgent need for refunctioning the 2nd production line at Tishreen Auto Bakery in Sbeineh. However; the ministry will provide raw materials and personnel to run the bakery after repairs. The bakery after rehabilitation will serve the Sbeineh area and five surrounding neighborhoods total population 150,000 citizens. Site assessment results show that one production line is functional yet needs light maintenance, while the 2nd production line needs total rehabilitation due to lack of maintenance since 2016. In response to the FSS Sector SO. 3.1 and 3.2, this interventions aims to provide; Support light rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructure through appropriate modalities, including Rehabilitation of essential economic infrastructure and facilities, such as, bakeries, Cost efficiency is perceived as recommended in the sector's guidelines. A detailed BOQ is prepared in coordination with the Bakery Management and costs are amended on basis of market research. The BOQ now includes 15% contingency for the heavy inflation rates.
Rescate plans to provide light rehabilitation, eventually maintenance for the 2nd production line to meet population lifesaving demands. This intervention will enable inhabitants to have better access to bread at affordable prices in the local market under the critical economic situation. Noting that people are depending on bread as main nutrition substance with the minimum amount of cheese, jam, or yoghurt to feed their children.
Rescate remains committed to submit 4Ws and reports to cover progress and achievements, M&E, financial and narrative reports to the sector and donors.

4 - Needs

Rescate performed several assessments in Rural Damascus, the team found that crisis affected families are facing serious difficulties obtaining sufficient bread to meet their daily requirements. There is severe lack in the supply of bread as the limited number of functioning bakeries have very little capacity in addition to increased bundle price. Relying on public bakeries in other geographical areas is restricted due to transportation costs increase. Women reported protection risks when they walk for long distances at night to reach bread distribution points. As coping mechanism, some families started producing bread at home, however the costs of bread flour and fuel or gas are very high. This additional economic burden is negatively impacting the lives and well-being of families whose livelihoods have been severely affected by the conflict. The field assessment showed that the current production of Tishreen Bakery doesn’t meet the population needs. Private bakeries in Sbeineh, Bwedah, and Hajeera are not operational yet due to rehabilitation high costs. Bardeh, 8 March Compound, Kuwaiti buildings, & Hosh Blas don’t have any bakery, therefore; they depend on Sbeineh bakery for bread/daily food. Rescate focus on the importance to maintain jobs for the bakeries’ workers and their families under the current deteriorated economic situation and limited job opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Based on Rescate’s approach on developing resilient livelihoods, which integrates humanitarian relief, socioeconomic development, and strengthening market system, and in line with the FAS sector log frame, Rescate proposes to implement the following activities: Support rehabilitation of relevant economic/productive infrastructures through appropriate modalities, including bakeries. Rescate in Syria has developed a Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism (FCRM)to allow for lodging complaints or make suggestions to the organization. The mechanism functions under the Project Manager/ team to resolve complaints in a timely and consistent manner. The project will complement primary data where possible with the use of information from secondary sources (e.g. UN agencies, local authorities, SARC, etc.) to inform ongoing situation and needs analysis and to triangulate monitoring data.Activities such as, claim mechanism will be introduced. A dedicated M&E officer will be with the team to monitor the implementation and to prepare reports about progress, maintain track of works vs. budget and action plan, and about beneficiaries’ segregation (gender, age) and social impact. A questionnaire will be prepared to assess beneficiaries’ satisfaction during and after the project implementation. ONG Rescate maintains a schedule for financial and narrative reporting to the donor and shares information under the FAS 4Ws.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Occurrence of gender-based violence, sexual exploitation & abuse including of minors: Rescate staff, volunteers and partners are child protection gender and GBV aware; GBV is strongly mainstreamed in this project; Safeguarding accountability and reporting mechanisms are in place in Rescate and partners. • Women may be harassed to get access to the rehabilitated community infrastructure: during the project implementation Rescate will conduct awareness sessions on GBV and the role of women in their communities, besides to the extent possible Rescate will ensure women participation in the management of the rehabilitated infrastructures • The project could be perceived as taking a side in the conflict due to favoring specific communities or social groups: for every project Rescate will perform conflict sensitivity analysis in order to make sure that the project is enhancing connecting factors and minimizing dividing factors in the targeted locations.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of sexual exploitation: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: All Rescate staff and partners, and contractors, are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. All PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Mission will scale the issue to ONG Rescate Headquarters in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During the implementation of the project unattended people may feel discriminated and tensions arise. There is a mid-risk that this could happened, and the impact again would be grave. For mitigation measures, Rescate will: 1) do follow up visits to the site of intervention, in the visits will remind the targeted population, and local stakeholders about the selection of the location rationale being highly populated and serving other adjacent areas as well; As soon as Rescate learns of any tension that may be arising, Rescate will immediately take action according to the situation, informing MOITC and donor. Assumption that Covid-19 will not disrupt the project. It is difficult to assess beforehand the probability for this to happen and its consequences. Rescate will take prevention measures since the project launch to avoid contagion: PPE will be distributed for the staff & workers at all times. Assumption is that there will be stability of prices for the project implementation, there is a high risk that this will not be the case, and could have grave consequences. Mitigation measures will be to pay down-payment to the local contractor to purchase all the needed materials and equipment on the project launch. In terms of support, the assumption is that the MOITC, the local authorities and governorate maintain a strong collaboration with Rescate. There is a low risk this would not be the case, as previous projects have been successfully implemented under this line. In case of a lack of support, the impact would be grave. For mitigation measures, Rescate will inform at all times to the public authorities of the results being achieved, will keep the targeted population aware & informed & will clarify any question they have.
