Project Summary

This project will ensure that the most vulnerable populations affected by conflict or natural disasters with specific needs have timely access to Emergency Shelter (ES) and Non-Food Items (NFI) assistance. This project contributes to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objective 1: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity; and Strategic Objective 2: Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services.
IOM proposes to purchase, preposition, and distribute 20,000 NFI kits and 20,000 Emergency shelters as a preparedness measure for addressing the needs of newly displaced and vulnerable people with special needs from the protracted displaced populations in 14 different states. Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items assessment missions will utilize Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) data to select the most vulnerable households to be assisted ES/NFI kits. IOM will also transport the emergency shelters and non-food items to the distribution locations. Kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable populations in selected states through close coordination with ES/NFI sector lead and partners as well as government bodies to ensure consistency in provision of assistance and to mitigate assistance duplication. As a final stage IOM will undertake post distribution monitoring missions to monitor usage of the distributed items after 2 to 4 weeks of the distributions.

1 - Needs

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) identifies 1.5 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), 445,000 returnees and 394,000 vulnerable residents as having shelter and NFI needs, driven by the multitude of conflict-related, ecological, and economic factors, further exacerbated by the still looming threat of COVID 19. The sector will target 1.34 million people with most severe needs (56% of the People in Need (PiN)) in 77 localities across the Darfur states, South and West Kordofan, Blue Nile and other flood-affected states, by delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced people as well as addressing mid to longer-term needs of returnees and vulnerable communities living in protracted displacement at risk of secondary displacement due to conflict or hazards. In fact, with 30,000 people displaced by conflict in 2020 and half a million displaced by conflict in 2021, we have actually seen a sharp increase in both conflict and lifesaving needs (55% increase) with recurrent displacement such as in West Darfur, and sporadic inter-communal conflict in eastern Sudan among the Bani Amer and Nuba tribes. The estimate of the number of IDPs was revised upward to 2.87 million in 2022 – including newly accessed populations not yet registered with estimates on displaced people in non-governmental controlled areas and Sudan also continues to host over 1.16 million refugees. Majority of the populations are affected by floods annually, with 2020 being the worst rainy season in a century causing damage to infrastructure, service facilities, and destroying family homes and properties. Overall needs have also grown considerably due to the severe economic crisis, which have been further exacerbated by COVID-19, and flooding that affected 0.75 million people across Sudan. Ongoing macro-economic challenges have resulted in high inflation rates and devaluation of the Sudanese pound, impacting on the shortage of fuel and cash throughout the country. The increased price of essential goods and medical supplies have especially impacted vulnerable and affected communities. IDPs including those who are either newly displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, returnees and refugees are still in need of emergency shelters and NFIs including plastic sheeting, plastic mats, jerry cans, blankets, and cooking sets. These items help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks and provide some privacy and security to those in need. More sustainable shelters for returnees and protracted IDPs affected by recent conflict but also natural hazards need to be addressed. It is estimated 43 localities (22 per cent of the total caseload in Sudan), across eight states are currently hosting IDP and returnee populations with acute humanitarian needs- ranked at extreme levels (severity 4) and catastrophic levels (severity 5), and they require assistance. In eastern Sudan and many parts of central and north Sudan the severity remains low (severity 1). However, populations in Khartoum, Blue Nile, Gedaref, Gezira, Kassala, Red Sea and Sennar states remain vulnerable with potential to be affected by hazards such as floods. Heavy flash floods also affected North and East Darfur and other states such as River Nile in 2020 and 2021. An estimated 40 per cent of flood affected people in Sudan reside in some 34 localities across 15 states with severity 5 ranking. Given the current context, including man-made and natural disasters, the socio-economic situation, the lack of critical services and an increase in urgent humanitarian needs, will force populations to flee their homes in search of assistance and are likely to require a far greater response from the ES/NFI Sector.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Based on DTM data provided through MT and EET information conduct ES/NFI needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable households within each community including people with specific needs (PSN) and include gender considerations related to the participation of men and women in the distribution process. 2. Provide training on construction techniques including participation of men and women in technical trainings and information campaigns, ensuring that constructed structures will be sustainable in target camps and communities with high rates of displacement or return. 3. Train beneficiaries on hazard mitigation, especially flood and fire related hazards, to support community preparedness, disaster risk reduction, and early recovery response, while raising awareness on environmental impacts and mitigation and use of environmentally friendly alternative materials. 4. Purchase, preposition and distribute 20,000 ES for approximately 100,000 of the most vulnerable newly displaced, protracted IDPs, and returnees based on assessed needs, and PSN criteria utilizing environmental-friendly material and locally acceptable shelters in line with the ES/NFI sector standards. 5. Purchase, preposition, and distribute 20,000 NFI kits, which is in line with ES/NFI sector standards and contains (plastic sheets, sleeping mats, jerry cans, blankets, kitchen sets and mosquito nets) to benefit 100,000 crisis and disaster affected IDPs and vulnerable persons, prioritizing women, children, and the elderly. 6. Conduct post distribution monitoring (PDM) in areas where NFI and emergency shelters have been distributed (2-4 weeks after the distributions). The PDM will include household to household interviews on the usage of distributed materials, beneficiary satisfaction, and complaints on the quality and quantity of received materials.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IOM will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will follow applicable international requirements. IOM will ensure that the proposed project will have a positive environmental impact by encouraging the beneficiaries to be involved in hazard mitigation activities such as awareness raising sessions on materials used and safe construction of shelters, as well as environmental impacts related to flood and fire hazards and protecting their shelters and best handling of NFIs for longer-term use. The training sessions will also include promoting alternatives to firewood such as fuel efficient cooking and livelihood options to preserve the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM will ensue to strengthen gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations as they link with the “do no harm” principle and are in line with IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises. The needs for such individuals will be reflected in NFI assessments, programme design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring. This project will prioritize female, child, and elderly headed households to receive ES/NFI assistance. Another group that will be prioritized is households assessed to be vulnerable and in dire need of ES/NFI assistance. All members of the community including unaccompanied minors, people with special needs, women and girls will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in the consultation, distribution, and post-distribution processes. Beneficiaries will also be involved in assessing their access and safety around accessing services, e.g., identifying the locations of distributions points, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure do no harm. Staff will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately and using the referral pathways and provide essential support to community members to access the referral pathways. IOM will work with Staff and suppliers to strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising and in line with IOM PSEA guidelines.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

All IOM staff are carefully trained in codes of conduct and PSEA, prior to any engagement with affected communities. Trainings will be refreshed on a frequent basis throughout the year. The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase - registration of returnees, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. IOM has developed a PDM format that will track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from ES/NFI beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will include interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on ES/NFIs quality, appropriateness, and utilization as well as any complaints including alleged SEA which will be reported using the appropriate pathways. The system has also been developed to assess the short-term effects of ES/NFIs in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (household interviews, focus group discussions. and observations by key stakeholders), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and dissemination procedure. Methodologies used will include a desk review prior to the field exercise being conducted.
