Project Summary

Latest November 2023 IPC report indicates at least 7.1 million people (56.3% of the population) likely to experience Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with consequent high malnutrition levels more so among children and pregnant women. 2024 Pilgrims of Hope's intervention will utilize an integrated approach to address the food insecurity and livelihoods needs of the population in Central Equatoria -Kajokeji, Lainya, Terekeka and Juba counties. The main overall objective of the interventions will be to reduce food insecurity of the above selected Counties sustainably and having as main targets food insecure communities classified in IPC 4 and 3 with special attention to single mothers, the now high numbers of returnees and vulnerable households. FSL service delivery will integrate emergency to reliance interventions, aiming to strengthen the productive bases of agricultural communities in order to enhance overall production. while targeting the most vulnerable by integrating them into active roles within more-developed groups and production systems. More importantly, this model also does use the “model-farmers” approach, where by successful local farmers set the example for other community members. Activities will include capacity building on agronomic functional theories and practices and awareness with a focus on access to improved agricultural inputs and services.
At the institutional level [county and local authorities], the interventions will focus on creating and strengthening monitoring systems during implementation so that the targeted meets the expectations. POH will ensure that, activities focus on their integration/interaction through agriculture activities, enhanced access to quality and improved agricultural inputs (seeds and tool kits, capacity building in agriculture using the Farmers Field School (FFS) approach. Essentially, the diversification of livelihoods activities will be supported (eg. vegetable production, agro-forestry among others). Special attention will be given to climate change adaptation and smart agriculture practices with the aim of fostering a sustainable increment of production with constant attention to preservation of natural resources and restoration of lost resources especially trees planting. With the lessons learned from compounding effect of climate change to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches shall be adhered and implemented in 2024 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services especially to the vulnerable returnees HHs. The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programming with other relevant sectors such as nutrition, WASH, health, protection and food security through effectives coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to multi-sectoral basic service which will further reduce food insecurity and increase resilience. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programming of FSL services approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs accordingly.

1. Needs

More than ten years down after gaining independence and after signing the most recent peace agreement, the Country continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Conflict, sub-national violence, public health challenges and climatic shocks that have severely affected people’s livelihoods and hindered access to essential services. According to the World Bank Data, World Development Indicators 2023). South Sudan’s life expectancy is 55, people spend just 5.5 years in school on average and earn USD 768 a year. And an estimated 9.4 million people in South Sudan, including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children and 337,000 refugees, are projected to need humanitarian assistance and protection services in 2023 – reflecting a 76 per cent of the country’s population, a 5 % increase from 2022 (South Sudan HRP OCHA 2023). Latest November 2023 IPC report indicates at least 7.1 million people (56.3% of the population) likely to experience Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity with consequent high malnutrition levels more so among children and pregnant women. Conflict, severe flooding and insecurity, fueled by sub-national and intercommunal violence, crime and wide-scale impunity, continue to be among the main drivers of humanitarian needs in South Sudan. The conflict in Sudan has already affected South Sudan's economic outlook, particularly for northern States that rely heavily on imports from Sudan, leading to an increase in food prices and fuel costs. This could potentially exacerbate the protection risks and vulnerabilities of the population of the northern States but also returnees arriving from Sudan, Kenya, and Uganda, and increase food insecurity, negative coping mechanisms and acute humanitarian needs. This is happening amid the protracted conflict and frequent natural disasters including flooding in South Sudan which has resulted into displacement, increased food insecurity and exposure of the population to the risk of asset loss and destitution. Highly vulnerable returnees are settling into areas of return that are already facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity and a shortage of available livelihoods due to years of compounding shocks and stressors among them a prolonged dry spell talk of Terekeka County (IRNA Report). The majority South Sudanese returnees have endured long, arduous journeys, are traumatized, and many have experienced GBV. People are arriving in South Sudan in extremely bad health, nutrition, and physical conditions, almost all have demonstrated food insecurity and the inability to meet basic needs. Host communities are strained by increasing demand for resources and services by returnees, whereby many have exhausted their food items and there are limited livelihood opportunities as well as basic services in the Payams, making life very difficult, especially for women, children, and the elderly and with disabilities.

2. Project activities

Provision of vegetable and crop livelihood kits in the main season targeting 12,000 HHs – these will be prepositioned through UN-FAO pipeline. Training and providing of evidence base technical guidance to beneficiaries on working agronomic practices and training and setting of 50 kitchen/ demonstration plots at Payam level or OTP sites for nutrition sensitive agriculture. Training on post-harvest handling. Post distribution monitoring/ post-harvest assessments and marketing strategies for surplus. Natural Resource management and agro forestry - Formation and training of natural resource management committee. - Establishment of fruit tree seedling. - Conducting community level sensitization on natural resource mapping and utilization with minimal conflict. - Encouraging use of improved cooking stove reducing the amount of emission into the atmosphere and training on Agroforestry technics in response to climate change.
