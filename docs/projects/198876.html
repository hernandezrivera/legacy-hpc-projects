Project Summary

QRCS aims through the proposed project to ensure continuity and increase the coverage of life-saving preventive and curative nutrition services among children under 5 (boys and girls) and PLWs. QRCS has been supporting seven fixed OTPs centers and 6 mobile rapid response teams reported on monthly 4Ws in coordination with nutrition clusters, UN agencies, health directorates, and local stakeholders in which targeted areas are ranked as a high severity according to the HNO 2021 and nutrition cluster reports, at 29 communities including IDPs camps and host communities in Idleb and rural Aleppo.
QRCS will continue provision of quality curative and preventative nutrition services through running eight RRTs and deployment of community outreach activities in the targeted areas in Idleb and rural Aleppo, as well as QRCS, will continue to implementing programmatic adjustment in the context of COVID-19 to protect health care providers, mitigate the risk of infection and ensure continuity of life-saving nutrition services as the followings:
1-Continuity of life-saving nutrition services particularly CMAM, micronutrient supplementation, and IYCF-E under guidance for adaptations in the context of COVID-19 through the deployment of eight mobile rapid response teams and each RRT will visit 3-4 communities weekly.
2- RRTs will provide MUAC screening, refer complicated SAM cases and provision of therapeutic for SAM and MAM cases among children 6-59 months and malnourished PLWs in 29 communities including IDPs camps.
3- Provision of Maternal nutrition and appropriate IYCF-E counseling and messages for pregnant women and mothers.
4- Screen, refer children and PLWs with Medical problems.
5- 16 CHWs and one team leader will conduct community outreach activities promoting IYCF-E, Maternal nutrition, health, and WASH-related topics and massages, and Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs during Nutrition screening based on NWS nutrition cluster and TWG recommendations in the context of COVID-19 using additional platforms such as mobile text messages, radio, TV, and social media to protect, promote and support IYCF-E.
6-Distribution of micronutrient supplementary to PLWs and children under 5 based on SoPs and nutrition cluster recommendations.
7-Conduct training on IYCF-E and CMAM programming in the context of COVID-19 for RRTs staff as well as identify visual signs of abuse/violence against childU5/PLWs, referral pathway and COVID-19 pandemic.
8- Roll-out of a family MUAC approach for caregivers, PLW, and family members.
9- QRCS will conduct COVID 19 precautions during implementation by procuring protective personal equipment such as gloves, masks, and sterilizers, as well as staff, will receive training on IPC and precaution measures.
10- QRCS will coordinate with nutrition cluster, partners, and local councils and coordinate with WASH cluster through the regular coordination between Nut. and WASH clusters if needed.
11-QRC will conduct MEAL activities including PDM, Field monitoring visits, and BNFs satisfaction survey.
During the project -24 months- period, QRCS will provide supportive supervision and on-job monitoring and training through a community health leader, field project coordinator, and project management based in Turkey, as well as QRCS, will provide equitable access and quality nutrition services to the most vulnerable population among 57,395 children under 5 (28,655 boys and 28,740 girls) and 12,320 PLWs reaching 99,090 expected beneficiaries including (55,160 Female and 43,930 Male), with 1,413 disables people, 56,480 displaced population and overburdened and 42,610 host communities. All estimated caseloads and a number of targeted beneficiaries were determined based on the capacity of QRCS and previous project findings, achievements, and results which were reported to nutrition cluster via monthly 4Ws.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO2022, 5.5M mothers and children in Syria are in need of nutrition assistance 2022 in addition nearly 11% of mothers are malnourished in NW Syria. The 2022 HNO indicated increased PIN numbers across different malnutrition parameters since 2020. For instance, chronic malnutrition or stunting increased by 4%,SAM cases (increased by 29%), MAM cases (increased by 44.7%), and for PLWs, the number of PLWs in need of malnutrition treatment was approximately 265,000. According to the SMART survey conducted by PAC in July 2022, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months is 22.3 % in NWS in Aleppo and Idlib. The prevalence of GAM increased to 5%. Among PLWs, the prevalence of SAM across northwest Syria is 12.9%. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria, with Idleb more than Aleppo. For the IYCF indicators, as last survey, the deterioration in the Minimum Dietary Diversity, Minimum Dietary Frequency, and Minimum Acceptable Diet continued, the reasons for this deterioration could be the economic deterioration and the increase of food prices Only 53.4% of children under 6 months are exclusively breastfed whilst only 10.8% consume the MAD in NWS. The anemia rate is 36.18% among targeted WRA women of reproductive age (15 -49). For the Maternal situation, the acute malnutrition situation between the WRA and PLW showed some increases compared to 2021 results, the MAM PLW rate had increased from 12.4% in 2021 to 12.9% in 2022.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project is planned to provide life-saving nutrition services to the most vulnerable people in areas with high severity of needs at 29 communities, including IDPs camps, and enhance the resilience of affected communities most of them are women and children in high severity through provision through community engagement and outreach activities.This project will continue providing immediate and longer-term interventions to address the most vulnerable population's urgent needs and increase the coverage to the high severity and remote locations, including IDPs camps, as well as to increase equitable access to curative nutrition services through systematic and timely identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases for pregnant and lactating women, and boys and girls under 59 months:• Provision MUAC screening, refer complicated SAM cases, and provision of therapeutic for SAM and MAM cased among children 6-59 months and malnourished PLWs as in 29 communities including IDPs camps. • Provision of Maternal nutrition and appropriate IYCF-E counseling and messages for pregnant women and mothers.• Promoting and providing health and nutrition key education messages on IYCF-E, dietary diversity, hygiene promotion, and COVID-19 using appropriate IEC and promotion materials. As a slightly longer-term intervention • Provision of nutritional curative and preventative supplementations to targeted beneficiaries.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) affects accessing nutrition services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: medium, Mitigating Measures: Provide nutrition services in accessible easy-to-reach areas. Training of nutrition RRTs on Prevention and management of sexual violence. The presence of female staff in the nutrition teams increases access of women and girls to nutrition services. Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) of women. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating measures: Establish effective community-based complaints mechanisms, and awareness-raising on PSEA amongst nutrition team members, with continuous training on the topic. Risk: Inequitable access to nutrition services by Vulnerable Groups. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: Implementation of standard essential services package across the sector interventions, including improved quality reporting and robust monitoring through standardized SOPs. Addressing the specific needs of vulnerable groups through supporting dedicated nutrition subgroups groups IYCF, CMAM, and Capacity Building


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers or project contributors (risk of harassment) of women and girlsLikelihood: Medium, Impact: highMitigation measure: a confidential complaint mechanism was activated, QRCS staff was trained on PSEA complaints, and the team signed contracts, including the code of conduct and PSEA policies. New staff will train on PSEA mechanisms and regulations as well as sign a code of conduct related to internal PSEA policy. The human resources unit and MEAL staff in Turkey receive PSEA complaints through the hotline (WhatsApp number: +905362628125 and email address (complaints@qrcs.org.qa). MEAL team in the Turkey office and HQ have access to CRM email and WhatsApp to monitor and track CRM during implementation. QRC takes measures and penalties for guilty staff (if any) according to the approved QRC policy and regulations in this regard. On the other hand, QRC incorporated PSEA responsibilities into staff performance appraisals/evaluations, protecting persons where allegations of PSEA are reported involving our personnel; suspending staff under investigation, referring victims to Psycho-social support, and witness protection program if needed. Investigations on allegations will be conducted in a timely and professional way and protection staff will be actively engaged through reporting to protection clusters on violence incidents (if happened) to ensure that all the activities are implemented through a gender lens.
