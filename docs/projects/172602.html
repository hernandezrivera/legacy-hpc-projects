Project Summary

Rehabilitation of Shelters, distribution of essential NFI kits to enhance the living conditions and protection of PoC. DRC aims on supporting 2.135 individuals out of following groups: IDPs, Returnees, Non-Displaced, Asylum Seekers, Refugees and Migrants.
DRC aims on targeting the most vulnerable out of the target groups as stated above. The vulnerability status is identified through assessments, taking into account socio-economic factors. Persons belonging to the group of 'most vulnerable', as women/minor headed households, chronically sick, people with disabilities and elderlies are primarily selected due to their high level of social vulnerability.
DRC selected beneficiaries are engaged throughout the entire project from the project design (joint assessments, discussions on scope of work etc.) to providing feedback to the MEAL department in the beneficiary satisfactory survey after finalizing the work.

1 - Needs

Libya has been adversely affected by conflict and protracted political crisis since 2011, driving people into displacement, affecting their well-being and living standards as well as access to basic services. According to the 2020 HNO, out of 6.7 million people in Libya, 1.8 (26%) million people have been identified as affected. Overall, 595,000 people are facing critical issues related to minimum living standards. The conflict has affected the living conditions of people due to severe damages or destruction to housing and public infrastructure. A lack of adequate shelter options and rising demand in areas of displacement has led to housing shortages or increase in rental costs affecting people’s ability to afford adequate shelter, many being at risk of eviction.According to the PIN survey, released the 8.10.2020, in total 376.000 individuals are in need of Shelter/NFI assistance. 2 - Activities

1. Rehabilitation of shelters: for all rehabilitation activities DRC will conduct technical detail assessments at the facility to identify the material/items required to ensure dignified, healthy and protected living conditions after finalization of the work. Quantity and quality of such items will be in adherence with corresponding shelter guidelines as Sphere and with minimum requirements and other guidelines as stipulated by the Shelter Sector Libya. Already during the selection process DRC is providing an orientation on the project goals, selection criteria, feedback mechanism, implementation modality and involvement of the community/committee/beneficiary in the project. Beneficiaries/Committee members are participating in the technical assessment and discuss with the DRC engineer the scope of work and its limitations of the assistance. Different implementation modalities are possible in shelter interventions, working with contractors, community based, beneficiary based, voucher system, cash and in kind provision. Each project location gets assessed individually on the most feasible and most efficient modality, while high emphasis is laid on beneficiary engaging modalities combined with a voucher approach. Execution of work by external construction companies is the last resort and only foreseen under extraordinary circumstances. 2. Provision of essential NFI items: The same target group as identified under the rehabilitation work will receive standardized NFI items, if during the assessment those items are not available to the people. NFI assistance forms a complimentary activity to the shelter rehabilitation, therefore the NFI orientation to the beneficiary is done at the same time. DRC follows the recommendations and kit contents as elaborated and endorsed by the Shelter Sector, minor modifications might be necessary depending on the outcome of the assessment. Kits as determined by the Shelter Sector comprise of: standard NFI and seasonal kits.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Under point 4 the risks 4.1, 4.8, 4.15, 4.16, 4.17 and 4.20 are evaluated to be on a 'high' risk level. Following mitigation measures are planned:4.1 & 4.15, feedback mechanism:DRC has established a functional feedback mechanism which is incorporated into all DRC responses. This mechanism is introduced to all stakeholders of Shelter/NFI projects by hand outs (leaflets, business cards etc.), during orientation sessions to local authorities/governmental institutions, to community leaders/committee representatives, landlords and the beneficiary. During assessments face to face and/or via phone emphasis is laid on the feedback mechanism orientation as an integrated part of the sessions. DRC's feedback mechanism ensures confidentiality at all stages. DRC can be reached through a dedicated phone number and a dedicated mail address only trained DRC staff is having access to. 4.8:DRC´s shelter intervention is facilitating access to durable solutions for forcibly displaced populations, including through providing safe and dignified shelter conditions in displacement locations as well as in return areas. Local integration and dignified return are eased through the rehabilitation of shelters and provision of NFI items for displaced individuals and returnees. 4.16: a) Returnee/Non-displaced (ND) response: returnees/ND are not at risk of eviction if they own the shelter for rehabilitation. In case of tenancy, DRC is contacting the landlord to provide an orientation on the project. After obtaining approval from the landlord on the work, DRC, landlord and beneficiary are signing a tri-partite agreement stating a minimum tenancy period of 9 months after finalization of rehabilitation.b) Asylum Seekers/Refugees/Migrant response: if living under a private tenancy agreement, same modality as above applies. If they are residing in collective centres/unfinished buildings settings, DRC aims on signing an agreement with the respective landlord stating a minimum period of time the population has the right to remain within the premisses. If the land is owned by the government, similar agreements are envisaged, although is is recognized that such agreements will likely not find signature by the authorities. Therefore the risk of eviction in cases without agreement is higher to those having an agreement with DRC in place. 4.17: in all DRC shelter rehabilitation interventions the verification on the land ownership is mandatory. In private settings the claiming landlord needs to provide documents clearly stating that he/she is the righful owner of the premisses. In case he/she lacks of such documents DRC will consult village elders/sheik/community leaders to confirm such and to sign the agreement as a witness, confirming that the claiming landlord is the rightful owner.IN governmental owned settings such procedure is not necessary as public buildings used as collective centres by nature are verified to belong to the government. Such confirmation, i f needed, can get obtained from the local authorities. In addition, usually the inhabitants of such facility can confirm the ownership as well. 4.20:Safety and Security of DRC staff, the target group and other stakeholders is the upper most importance while delivering aid to the site. DRC Safety department is analysing the situation on the ground prior to the decision to execute any work in certain areas. Before team's departure from the office to the respective site Safety needs to provide clearance for the trip to take place. In Libya, DDG, an integrated unit within DRC, are programming HMA (Humanitarian Mine Action) and AVR (Armed Violence Reduction) activities across the country in Tripoli, Benghazi and Sabha. DRC will liaise directly with DDG and make use of their NTS (Non-Technical Survey) capabilities, which aid with the investigation and subsequent mapping of hazardous land/areas.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

DRC is currently in discussion with WFP on signing the CFM agreement. Parallel to the CFM, DRC has put in place a strong internal complaint and feedback mechanism accessible in all areas of intervention, allowing for the participation of the targeted group in the services and assistance delivery. The DRC CFM is managed by the MEAL department and includes a helpline of which the number is systematically communicated to targeted populations. DRC registers and addresses all requests and regularly reviews and highlight learnings from received feedback throughout the project cycle. Learnings inform current and future programming and allow for increased engagement with the supported groups. In addition, an internal accountability framework is under development, to allow the increased participation and engagement with beneficiaries and community groups into the design, implementation, and feedback on proposed activities. These include regular assessments, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews, throughout the project cycle.
