Project Summary

In addition to continued need to provide lifesaving assistance to the refugees (Women, boys, girls, men and alternative gender identity), IOM seeks to expand its scope of work to strengthen the health systems to the local communities. By October 2019, IOM and its partners is supporting: 04 Community Clinics, 08 PHC, 10 Health Post and 02 Upazilla Health Complex serving about 350,000 refugees-and will continue to do so in 2020. IOM will ensure adequate staffing, provision of essential medical supplies, and 24/7 emergency referral systems through 12 vehicle ambulances.
On coordination, IOM seeks to maintain support to the health sector coordination at camp level by continuing the deployment of 5 Camp Health Focal Persons
Overall, IOM supports 350 CHWs accounting for about 24% of the coverage. IOM will support local NGOS to implementing the Community Health interventions and ensure. Besides, IOM will also support local NGOs (under selection) to scale up access to Palliative Care services. In Camp 10, one HP is to be refurbished from bamboo infrastructure to semi-permanent sandwich pannels.
IOM requires additional funding to maintain delivery of lifesaving quality services through the networks of health facilities and CHW without which, over 25% of the population would be left without access to essential comprehensive primary health care services.

Objectives

SO1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases.SO2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to communicable disease risks including diseases with outbreak potential, and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone. SO3. SO4. Strengthen health sector coordination, information management and monitoring towards achieving rational, standardised and accountable health service delivery

Needs

In Cox's Bazar, the overal refugee health situation has improved given the wide coverage by the health infrastructure. The crude death rate (CDR) has declined to below 0.4deaths/10,000/day threshold by April 2018 (UNCHR). Although there are over 8 secondary care facilities, for the refugees, access to these lifesaving services is only on referral basis. As a single agency, IOM runs the largest emergency medical referral system in the camps with 12 vehicle ambulance and two 24/7 call centers. To date, 4,819 refugees have received lifesaving secondary health care services using the referral to Sadar district hospital or Chittagong divisional hospital. Referral systems need to be strengthened alongside expanded support to district hospitals and Upazila Health Complexes in order to ensure strong Secondary Healthcare. Initiatives for the inclusion of People with Disabilities (PWD), coherent care for People Living with HIV (PLWHIV), the adherence to quality assurance-adherence in treatment guidelines, scaling up access to Adolescent and Sexual Reproductive Health services are all gaps to be addressed. Palliative Care and Specialized services Mental Health and Psychosocial Support services are areas that IOM will pay attention due to lack of adequate coverage.By October 2019, IOM and its partners is supporting: 04 Community Clinics, 08 PHC, 10 Health Post and 02 Upazilla Health Complex serving about 350,000 refugees. IOM supports 350 CHWs accounting for about 24% of the coverage. Gender: Although the health services is universally accessible, there is need to ensure programs specifically endavours to adress the health needs of young boys and girls and women. Socioculturally, young girls are restricted to homes with movements only accompanied by a male/older figure. Such norms limits the particpation of adolescent girls (12-17) who constitute 6.9% of the refugee population. Adolescent girls have diminshed access to SRH information and services, women who suffer SGBV are faced with stigma, lack of skilled/specialized service providers. This project, through its SRH activities will increse access to safe delivery, FP, treatment of STD/Is, clinical management of rape all of which seeks to enhance gender and human rights of this vulnerable group
