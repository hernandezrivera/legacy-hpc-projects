Project Summary

The proposed project is intended to deliver a mix of static primary health care and outreach services to 40,000 beneficiaries (2,550 males under five years of age, 2,600 females under five years of age, 17,870 males above fives years and 16,980 females above five years of age) to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Kajo keji, morobo, lainya and yei Counties, and generally improve the health of the population in the area.
The project will complement ongoing interventions by South Sudan Health Association(SSUHA) by supporting health service provision through provision of essential drugs and equipment to support the growing numbers of returnees in 18 static health facilities and 10 mobile sites and responding to the effects of COVID-19 pandemic. The proposed project will be implemented through the already existing Government -MoH systems to enhance sustainability (durable solutions) through strengthening of the health system to offer quality health services. The project will support the existing health facilities through capacity building for health facility staffs, provision of additional essential drugs and equipment as well as provision of additional staff. The static health facilities will be supported with additional essential drugs in order to conduct mobile outreaches and take services nearer to the communities. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health National Health Policy, 2016-2026). Proposed interventions will directly contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3.
South Sudan Health Association will seek to reduce excess morbidity and mortality as a result of climatic shocks and conflicts. Proposed activities will seek to contribute to improvement in the physical and mental wellbeing of 40,000 beneficiaries including 50 persons with MHPSS services (28 females and 22 males) affected by conflict and displacement as well as 50 persons with disabilities (20 males and 30 females) and responding to 50 cases of SGBV. It further, seeks to improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for 40,000 crisis-affected population, 18 Static facilities and 10 mobile outreach teams will be set up and strengthened through the provision of medical supplies and the improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be recruited and trained. Among the activities that SSUHA will conduct includes; supplemental vaccination campaign to under 5 children (2,550 boys and 2,600 girls) and Women of childbearing age (WCBA); Provision of fuel to facilitate the health staff conduct mobile outreach since there ready 4 vehicles supported under HPF. Provision of antenatal and post-natal care to pregnant and lactating mothers, strengthening physical and mental healthcare system. Treatment and control of communicable diseases targeting 2,500 boys, 2,600 girls, 2,547 PLWs, 14,433 WCBA, and 17,870 men; Strengthening of referral system between Primary Healthcare facilities, the community (remote villages) and the regional hospitals; Establishment of 10 mobile clinics; Creating Disease Surveillance and Response plan; Training of 50 health workers on clinical management of raped survivors, IMCI, Basic Emergency Obstetric Care and disease surveillance and response. Training will be conducted for community health workers in integrated community case management on communicable diseases such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malaria.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. These situations are worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operation interference, public health challenges such as the effects of Covid-19 as well as climatic shocks like flooding and localized droughts which has had severe impact on people's livelihood, access to water, sanitation and hygiene as well as Health services. The humanitarian communities in South Sudan estimates 8.9 Million people in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2022). Flooding, sub-national violence and COVID-19 continues to strain access to the limited services. South Sudan's health system relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance to provide basic essential life saving services. Limited health facility functionality and capacities and poor coverage have made it difficult to meet the additional health needs. South Sudan has an extremely low immunization coverage with 45% penta3 coverage. Destruction of WASH facilities and lack of access to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions with communities at risk of water borne diseases such as diarrhea, Cholera, Hep E. (HNO 2022). Kajokeji County has only 11 functional health facilities of which 4 are supported by health partners, while the other 27 healthcare facilities have been vandalized and remain nonfunctional. Lainya with a total of 23 health facilities only 10 were supported under the current HPF3 funding, however due to funding constraints, HPF will support only 6 of the 10 health facilities in its 4th year. Yei with 39 health facilities, only 10 were supported by the current HPF project, however only 7 will be supported in the current 4th year of HPF3, making a gap of 3 Health facilities that will not have any support from partners. Of the 14 facilities that were active in Morobo County, only 4 remains functional; the other 10 have been destroyed and non functional, roads are impassible during the rainy season, cutting off large areas from the rest of the country. This proposed project will support the existing facilities with essential drugs and capacity building of staff. These counties, with an estimated population of 343,400 (approximately 13,736 children under five years of age and 78,982 pregnant and lactating women) are in great need of increased access to healthcare services due to the large number of returnees. For instance, many people living far away from health facilities must walk for several hours to access medical services. For this reason, the population is more likely to try and solve health problems through traditional medicine, which usually causes further complications. The proposed project is intended to deliver a mix of static primary health care and mobile/outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard to reach populations and is expected to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Kajo keji, morobo, lainya and yei Counties, and generally improve the health of the population in the area. The project will complement ongoing interventions by South Sudan Health Association(SSUHA) supporting health service provision and responding to the effects of COVID-9 pandemic. The proposed project will be implemented through the already existing Government -MoH systems to enhance sustainability (durable solutions) through the strengthening of the health system and capacity building to 50 staffs to offer quality health services. This, combined with emergency preparedness and response, will ensure service continuity and will play a crucial role in achieving the Health Sector Development Plan’s overall objective of “increasing access, utilization and quality of health and nutrition services, with emphasis on maternal and child health and with attention to effectiveness, efficiency, and equity” (Ministry of Health National Health Policy, 2016-2026)

2 - Project activities

South Sudan Health Association will provide life saving health interventions which are in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health National Health Policy 2016-2026 which conforms with the health cluster strategic objectives. The outreach/mobile clinic will reduce the excess morbidity and mortality as well as improve the quality of life of 40, 000 beneficiaries (2,550 boys and 2,600 girls under five years of age as well as 17,870 men and 16,980 females above 5 years of age) by providing access to health services in remote and conflict affected hard to reach areas particularly access to maternal child health, general consultation, screening and information on COVID-19 prevention and treatment of diseases. The main activities will include; prevention, control and treatment of communicable and non communicable diseases including epidemic-prone diseases in 18 static health facilities and 10 outreach/mobile sites. • Support reporting for communicable disease surveillance and Response Early Warning and Response Network including COVID-19 •Capacity building for 50 health facility staffs on Essential package of Health Services (EPHS) core services including disease surveillance, infection prevention and control, clinical management of rape and emergency obstetric care •Provision of integrated primary health care services through medical screening, consultations and referral of critical cases both at outreach and fixed health facilities to 40,000 beneficiaries. • Providing adequate environment for urgent medical consultations including timely treatment of survivors of GBV and delivery of regular primary health care services for returnees, displaced as well as host communities. •Providing early detection, diagnosis, and treatment of emergency and/or life threatening injuries and other health conditions such as diarrhea, fever, respiratory infections, malnutrition among others. • Support national vaccination campaigns in addition to routine immunization services for under fives, and pregnant and lactating mothers at 18 health facilities and 10 outreach/mobile sites and facilitate access and utilization of the immunization services. •Provide risk communications and community engagement for COVID-19 through a network of Boma Health workers and community committees • Focus on the special needs of especially vulnerable groups (children, pregnant or post partum women, newborns, the elderly, mental illnesses and the critically injured). • Facilitate medical referrals including survivors of GBV and transfer of patients through ambulance to secondary or tertiary health facilities as needed. • Procurement of essential drugs and other pharmaceutical equipment to support both the static health facilities and mobile sites to support the growing number of returnees •Conduct 10 monthly outreach/mobile clinics services in targeted locations in Kajo keji, Lainya, Morobo and Yei counties to increase access and utilization of health services by community members. • Provide focused antenatal care and postnatal checkups to attending mothers during outreach and in static health facilities. • Conduct normal deliveries in all the 9 PHCCs and refer complications for comprehensive maternal health services in the nearest hospital. • Provide family planning services and awareness raising in all the established facilities. • Provision of equitable access to ANC services, skilled facility delivery and post natal care services at 18 static and 10 mobile sites • Provide Basic management of survivors of gender based violence (PEP, CMR, treatment for STI, wound care etc) • Provide training to 30 health workers on Psychological Support and Psychological First Aid for people affected by the conflict (Children, women, and people of special needs). • Conduct RRT training for 50 health workers on integrated Health, WASH, Protection and nutrition response. • Train 30 health workers including county health department on emergency preparedness and response.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Accountability to the Populations will be emphasized and implemented to ensure affected individuals and communities play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions, and ensuring transparency throughout the project development and implementation while encouraging Inter-agency feedback and complaints mechanisms as part of an integrated M&E process. SSUHA already has a Robust feedback mechanism which is regularly employed to get feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholder using a variety of methods including: Live chat, Emails, Telephone calls, media, written documents, surveys, FGDs and meeting with community leaders and other project beneficiaries.The above mechanisms will regularly be deployed to inform the project as well as incorporate beneficiaries feedback in order to provide better services

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSUHA will include persons with disabilities, women and girls as well as boys and men to ensure that their unique needs are covered. Age, sex, and vulnerability criteria disaggregated data will be used to track inclusion, promote accountability and reduce exclusion of those who qualify for assistance. SSUHA will identify barriers to accessing assistance and services for women, men, boys, girls and persons with disabilities including individuals who are not literate and will design interventions that are contextually and culturally appropriate to their needs. SSUHA will ensure that specific groups are considered e.g PLWs, persons living with disabilities, unaccompanied minors, the elderly etc with relevant mitigation measures to prevent risks and harm. The project will incorporate measures to proactively include persons with disabilities and elderly including individuals who are not literate in the program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Beneficiaries' safety and security, particularly women and girls, persons with disabilities including individuals who are not literate will be regularly monitored through feedback and accountability mechanisms and field visit reports. SSUHA will ensure that facilities working hours and setting do not pose risk for vulnerable groups. SSUHA will strengthen its feedback and accountability measures to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of how to report and will strengthen the management systems to respond to such complaints if they occur. Feed back mechanisms will include; toll free hotline numbers, use of GBV focal points to identify and report cases of assaults' and regular community sessions with relevant groups

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection will be mainstreamed through prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm by delivering services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities are culturally appropriate and avoid any unintended negative consequences. Ensuring equitable access according to needs and not based on age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance. Services and assistance will be provided in an adequate balance, within safe and easy reach to all including People Living with Disabilities, and those with mental disorders. SSUHA will ensure zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse and all project staff are expected to sign the organization’s Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy before commencement of work. To ensure ethical compliance in implementation, project staff will also sign the SSUHA staff code of conduct, child-safeguarding policy in addition to other organizational policy documents. The project will take into consideration inclusive guidelines without regard to disability, gender, social/economic status, and ethnicity to ensure all members of the affected communities benefit from our activities. Implementation of the project will also involve the participation of a proportional number of qualified women (50%-50% of the total workforce) to challenge the notion of discriminating against women in the work place.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Implementing feedback mechanism is a standard procedure in SSUHA programming as one of our systems to monitor quality, promote participation and improve accountability. Before the onset of a project, inception meetings are conducted with the county authorities, community leaders, elders, chiefs, women representatives as well as the youths to introduce the scope and mandate of the project and communities are informed of the channels for reporting feedback/complaints. SSUHA uses different tools adapted to the language, context and culture to avoid barriers preventing the usage and facilitate the access. We also follow strict confidentiality protocols in order to assure the privacy of the informant and the information is processed by the correct staff. Actions to inform about the feed back mechanism existence are taken. Before implementing the system, the pertinent committee is trained on how to compile, share and process the information and afterwards lead/follow up actions of response. The information compiled is a valuable resource to monitor the project, better adapt it to the needs of the population, improve current and future intervention and open a fundamental participation channel with the population.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

South Sudan Health Association (SSUHA) will provide refresher training to all staff involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/protection mainstreaming and ensure that they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. SSUHA has a safeguarding policy in place to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on sexual exploitation and abuse of the beneficiaries. Boma Health Workers, community volunteers and health/nutrition workers will strive to identify passive cases in the community and report using the existing reporting mechanism and also continue dissemination of behavior change communication key messages on sexual violence and exploitation. SSUHA shall carry out in-depth research and analysis to identify the different risk factors for the project and strategies to address these risks. Research suggests “the most effective strategies are the result of indigenous movements that advocate for the overall well-being of women and girls and target all members of the community. SSUHA will continue to work closely with the local government structures, community leaders and reinforce good relationship on the ground to ensure ownership of the project, SSUHA will maintain contact with UNOCHA and South Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (SSRRC) coordinators to discuss and mitigate issues like forced taxation of humanitarian assistance, recruitment etc that may arise and affect the project implementation. SSUHA will also maintain active participation in the United Nations Department of Safety and Security briefings where project staffs will be informed of areas with UXOs like landmines, unexploded bombs etc. These areas will be avoided until when its declared safe by UNDSS for project activities. SSUHA will also conduct integrated mobile outreach to take services nearer to the communities with no health facility structures, this will reduce the distance patients have to move to access health services in the nearest health facility, also reduces the risk of exposure to violence as a result of traveling long distance on the road and in areas which are bushy, this is especially risky for women and girls.
