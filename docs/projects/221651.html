Project Summary

Malnutrition remains a public health problem in South Sudan especially among the most vulnerable members of the population mainly children under five years and women of reproductive age. The high burden of acute malnutrition is attributed to elevated levels of food insecurity, high prevalence of diseases; poor sanitation conditions; sub-optimal maternal, infant and young child feeding practices and limited access to healthcare.
Alight’s nutrition response will focus on preventive and treatment nutrition interventions in Morobo and Aweil West Counties. The response will primarily target children under five years both boys and girls as well as Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women and Girls (PBWGs) among the host community, IDPs and returnees. The proposed interventions include prevention of acute malnutrition through social behavior change communication to improve MIYCN practices in the community and blanket supplementary feeding programme; prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years; community management of acute malnutrition through the stabilization centre, outpatient therapeutic programme and targeted supplementary feeding programme and community mobilization where the community nutrition volunteers will be engaged to identify and support enrolment of children and PBWGs with acute malnutrition before their condition deteriorates. In addition, Alight will work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, FSL and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition and to enhance recovery of beneficiaries undergoing nutrition rehabilitation. The proposed interventions seek to reduce morbidity and prevent mortality associated with acute malnutrition and subsequently improve maternal and child health. The proposed interventions are in line with the nutrition cluster objectives which seeks to:
(a) Increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents and women.
(b) Increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection
and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBWGs.
(c) Enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information.
(d) Strengthen coordination and integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women by the end of 2025.
The project aims to reach 87,261 persons i.e. 62,821 persons in Aweil West and 24,440 persons in Morobo. The proposed interventions will be implemented in accordance with the South Sudan Ministry of Health's nutrition guidelines. Alight will make efforts to ensure the work that the organization does is sustainable through capacity building of the County Health Department (CHD) by working closely in every phase of the project design and implementation as well as working within the health facilities to strengthen health and nutrition programming. This will ensure the CHD is able to continue the project or use the knowledge and skills they would have acquired. Alight will build the capacity of the County health director, nutrition officer and M&E officer on nutrition service delivery, human resource management, nutrition information system, management of nutrition supply chain, finance, and leadership as well as governance. On the same note, Alight will build the capacity of the health facility staff including nutrition assistants under the Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP) as well as Boma health workers in nutrition interventions. Alight will also continue to ensure meaningful involvement of the communities in the implementation of the nutrition project to foster ownership.

1. Needs

Aweil West County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State: Aweil West has had better political stability as compared to other parts of the Country; however, it has been significantly affected by the consequences of protracted conflict in the Country which has resulted in severe economic crisis and subsequently high inflation. Moreover, erratic weather conditions in the County i.e., severe floods and dry spells have resulted in poor harvests. High inflation and poor harvests have adversely contributed to food insecurity and subsequently the high burden of acute malnutrition. This is consistent with the results of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report released in November 2024 where Aweil West is projected to experience critical acute malnutrition situation (IPC Phase 4) from October 2024 to June 2025. In addition, Aweil West is projected to experience worse acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) between December 2024 to March 2025 and the situation is projected to worsen from April to July 2025 where the population is likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Poor infant and young child feeding practices have also contributed to the high prevalence of acute malnutrition in the County. This is supported by the 2024 IPC that reported Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) for children at 23.2%, minimum meal frequency at 9% and minimum acceptable diet at 1.1% in NBeG. Other major drivers of acute malnutrition include lack of safe water, inadequate hygiene and sanitation, high prevalence of illnesses and limited access to quality healthcare services. It is worth noting that Aweil West continue to receive returnees and refugees fleeing the Sudan crisis who are likely to overstretch the existing basic services and food reserves in the host community already at high risk of acute malnutrition. Morobo County in Central Equatoria State: Morobo is among the most affected Counties in South Sudan by the continued localized conflicts that have led to massive displacement of the population within the County and to the refugee camps in the neighboring Countries, however, recently, the number of returnees has increased owing to the improved security situation in South Sudan following the formation of the revitalized transitional government of national unity, testified reduction of food ration in refugees camps, unemployment and livelihood opportunities, and limited access to basic services in the Countries of refuge. Though, Morobo is classified as IPC AMN Phase 1 (Acceptable) from October 2024 to June 2025, the population in the County is exposed to the risk of acute malnutrition due to food insecurity attributed to recurrent conflicts that has disrupted normal livelihoods which is consistent with the November 2024 IPC report that indicated the population in Morobo is facing worse acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) and it is projected that the same situation will continue up to July 2025. Furthermore, other aggravating factors that contribute to acute malnutrition in the County include the high burden of diseases with limited access to healthcare services, poor access to proper sanitation facilities, and suboptimal child feeding practices. The 2024 IPC reported a high prevalence of illness at 49.6% and MDD at 19% among children in CES. Malnutrition can have long-term negative effects particularly during the first 1,000 days of a child’s life, from pregnancy through to two years of age, therefore, prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five years as well as PBWGs through an integrated multisectoral response programming remains a significant need in the targeted Counties. Furthermore, the protracted conflict has over the years weakened the government infrastructure and systems and consequently the ability to respond to the burden of acute malnutrition thus the need to build and strengthen the capacity of the County health department to take over the delivery of nutrition services.

2. Project activities

To prevent acute malnutrition, Alight will implement BSFP targeting children aged 6 to 23 months and PBWGs, conduct MIYCN individual counselling and group education sessions at the nutrition facilities and community, carry out cooking demonstration sessions to mothers/caregivers of children aged 6 to 23 months, establish kitchen demonstration gardens and facilitate Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs). In addition, Alight will carry out Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years and provide iron folic supplements to adolescent girls to prevent micronutrient deficiencies. To treat acute malnutrition, Alight will operate stabilization centre to treat severely malnourished children under five years with medical complications, OTP to treat children 6 to 59 months with SAM without medical complications, and TSFP to treat children 6 to 59 months with MAM as well as PBWGs with acute malnutrition. Nutrition screening targeting children under five years and, PBWGs in the community will be carried out through CNVs and BHWs besides family led MUAC screening to enable early detection of malnourished cases and referral for treatment at the nutrition facilities. Home visits for children and PBWGs enrolled in CMAM programme will be conducted by CNVs. To enhance the quality of services provided, Alight will conduct routine support supervision and monitoring of nutrition activities to identify capacity gaps and subsequently conduct on-the job training and mentorship, conduct exits interviews with project’s beneficiaries to establish their satisfaction, obtain feedback and areas for improvement on the services delivered, and establish complaints and response mechanism to provide feedback on nutrition interventions and promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery. Furthermore, health and nutrition workers, CNVs and BHWs will be trained on MIYCN/malnutrition prevention and management of acute malnutrition. Alight will also participate in the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Surveys at the County level. MIYCN barrier analysis will be conducted to determine barriers to optimal uptake of maternal, infant and young child feeding and care practices. Monthly nutrition reports will be collected, compiled and submitted through the Nutrition Information System and DHIS2, and 5Ws through the Activity Info platform. Alight will strengthen coordination, collaboration and integrated programming with other sectors:• WASH cluster: Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM and promotion of optimal WASH practices in the community and nutrition facilities. • Health cluster: Management of children with diarrhoea, provision of LLITN to children and PBWGs with acute malnutrition, testing and treatment of malaria among OTP and TSFP beneficiaries, training of nutrition workers on IMNCI in standalone nutrition sites to build their capacity in providing services on IMNCI, and BHWs on identification of acute malnutrition and referral for treatment according to the national nutrition guidelines. Nutrition assistants under HSTP will be engaged and trained on nutrition interventions.• FSL cluster: Establish and maintain kitchen demonstration gardens at nutrition facilities and community through MtMSGs, CNVs and beneficiaries discharged from CMAM programme. In addition, beneficiaries discharged from CMAM will be linked with FSL projects for support. • ECD: Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM by equipping the nutrition sites with child play materials.• Education sector: Raising awareness on optimal nutrition practices including the importance of kitchen gardens in schools.• GBV cluster: Training nutrition workers on GBV basics including how to respond to GBV disclosures safely and appropriately as well as conducting GBV safety audit in nutrition sites to identify and address protection/safety needs.
