Project Summary

This project aims at promoting and strengthening access to education opportunities for internally displaced people in Meconta, particularly, to 2,983 boys and girls, including CWD, and 67 female and male teachers, totaling 3,050 beneficiaries. These beneficiaries are those affected by the crisis and deprived from the right of education due to conflict by providing learning materials and opportunities informal and formal learning. The schools teachers will be provided with teaching and learning materials, including psychosocial support.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed project will aim to ensure children have access to safe and protective learning spaces and are able to resume learning as quickly as possible. The project aims at promoting and strengthening access education opportunities for internally displaced people in Meconta, particularly, to 3,050 of boys, girls and CWD 5-19 affected by the crisis and deprived from the right of education due to conflict including teachers. World Vision will support the beneficiaries to ensure children quickly regain access to quality education and restore a sense of normalcy to improve their wellbeing. The support will be materialized by providing learning materials and opportunities, both formal and informal learning and support 67 teachers, male and female with teaching and learning materials, including the necessary psychosocial support.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

World Vision priorities will be providing access to safe, protective and inclusive learning spaces in the identified communities in Meconta district. This will be done by 1.1: Procuring and installing …. temporary learning spaces (TLS) to accommodate the children affected by the conflict; these will be accompanied by trained community volunteers. 1.2 Purchasing 5 kits of toys for age appropriate groups, (5-10), (11-13) and (14-18) to be used TLS to help children with set aside their traumatic experiences and focus on rebuilding their lives. 1.3: Recruit and train …. child friendly spaces volunteers in children’s play monitoring and basic occupational therapy. 1.4 Provide subsidies to volunteers working with children in temporary learning spaces; 1.5 Organize campaigns for parents and caregivers for children’s enrolment and retention in temporary learning spaces. This will offer children an immediate safe place to learn, a sense of normalcy, and a place to receive much needed mental health and psychosocial support. 2.1 Procure and distribute learning materials (students books, notebooks, pens, pencils and rubbers) for different learning levels (ECD, primary and secondary school levels). This will allow enrolled girls and boys to benefit from students’ kits to overcome any financial barriers and support their return to learning.2.2 Additionally, and through close collaboration with existing World Vision Child Protection teams working in a similar project in Meconta district, there will be established community-based referral mechanisms to ensure girls and boys have access to specialized services in line with their needs.2.3 Recognizing the gendered implications of this crisis and the ensuing response and the potentially newly arising gendered risks for children, a protection component will also be integrated within the TLS to ensure all children feel safe in learning spaces and schools.2.4 To support children’s enrolment and retention in both temporary learning spaces and existing schools, World Vision will conduct community awareness and engagement activities, including back to school campaigns.2.5 Train children and adolescents on peer to peer support. 3.1 Identification and analysis of teachers and their situations; provision of psychosocial support so that they can return to work. 3.2 Procure and distribute teachers Kits ( illustrative boards, markers, flip charts, glue tapes and colour cardboards.3.3 In collaboration with the district education services provide orientation sessions upon the delivery of supplies and regular support, including through WhatsApp teachers’ circle groups.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Gender diversity is considered during the design of projects, with a focus on ensuring that women and girls have equal access to humanitarian support and services by addressing the barriers. Gender will also be considered at all levels of the project including recruitment and orientation of staff on gender sensitivity. World Vision will deliberately encourage participation of women and girls in decision-making in the implementation of the project. 48% of young Mozambican women aged 20-24 years are married before the age of 18, while 14% are married before the age of 15. Child marriage is associated with a significantly higher likelihood of leaving school early. Girls are more likely than boys to be pulled out of school in crises and less likely to return. Therefore, there is a higher risk posed to girls who have stopped going to school because of the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado and the related spillover into neighbouring provinces than there is to boys.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Child marriage and child labour might increase as a negative coping mechanism of families during the period the period that access to schools is restricted because of displacement. These are factors that will be taken into consideration in the project design. Promotion and protection of rights are core principles for the implementation of all projects. In humanitarian crises, child safeguarding and protection tend to get disrupted and/or compromised leading to high likelihood of child protection violations and incidents. To mitigate this risk, where possible protection principles will be incorporated into all activities including ensuring meaningful, non-discriminatory access to project services, prioritising the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and ensuring meaningful participation. The project will align with Sphere, Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS), Inter-agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and other relevant standards to ensure that the right to receive humanitarian assistance is safeguarded impartially for all affected populations especially children. Communities will be sensitized about protection concerns as well as reporting and referral mechanisms.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

World Vision will be intentional in considering the inclusion of persons with disability in the project. A survey that will include questions that contribute to the identification of persons with disabilities and their needs will be conducted. The adoption of inclusive interventions will be prioritised, not only to ensure protection of people especially children with disability, but also to address barriers to their access to assistance. With appropriate training of staff and on the job support, the project will build on successes and lessons from the previous projects so that persons with disability can participate in and benefirt from planned interventions. In cases where temporary infrastructure needs to be installed such as learning spaces or handwashing stations, the project team will be sensitised to promote inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

WVMoz is aware of environmental considerations and will act appropriately to implement activities that are not harmful to the environment. These efforts will include working together with other partners to ensure issues to do with the environmental protection are properly addressed and good practices promoted at school and community levels.
