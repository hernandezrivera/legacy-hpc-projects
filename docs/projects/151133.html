Project Summary

The project targets Baidoa districts affected by crisis in South west State. READO in response to conflict and environmental related crisis and other emergencies affecting the larger Somalia has proposed this project to mainly keep 2004 learners and 61 teachers and 30 CECs at school while looking at multisectoral approach in solving the case of high school dropout rate following the 2017 drought, Floods and conflict. Flood Response Plan by OCHA, May 2018 has indicated that most schools in flood prone areas has been affected and destroyed thus the schools will need temporary learning spaces and rehabilitations. Through provision of water, food, trainings on resource management, systems strengthening and supply of teaching and learning materials, READO will manage to address Education with reference to Education cluster HNO2018, Food Security and Protection concerns like equity, impartiality, accountability, and safety in and around schools, through the schools as the entry point to aid provision. This is hoped to reach some 2098 (1203 boys, 801 girls, 60 men and 34 women) through a period of 12 months with the assumption that the region will remain stable.

Needs

While the Gu rains are expected to ease the impact of severe drought conditions spanning over four consecutive poor rainy seasons, flooding is compounding an already fragile humanitarian situation with an estimated 2.4 M children of school going age in need, 1.05 million who require urgent assistance due to displacement (HNO,2019). In Bay in South West State, the situation continued to worsen with nearly 8000 learners affected by flash and river flooding. Flash flooding also affected access to roads and washed away farmlands. Some 2800 children of school going age moved to upland areas of Awdinle village in Baidoa district. Limited road movement due to flooding is hindering access to some of the affected areas. Baidoa district is among the worst affected areas in the SWS. The road connecting the two districts is almost impassable, (OCHA Bulletin July, 2018); conflict, insecurity, forced displacement, flooding, drought and other emergencies remain significant impediments that have severely disrupted education opportunities in Somalia. READO statistics show that most schools don’t have a feeding program in place which is a pull factor for quality education for girls and boys (5-17 yrs), lack of Community Education Committee training and lack of trained teachers. Further the schools also lack recreational materials that limit both teachers’ and students’ engagement in physical education activities which are essential for their wellbeing, especially in the times of stress. While CECs and Regional Education Officers exist in the area, their capacity to understand and carry out their duties in school supervision and management has been limited by lack of financial and technical support. Their roles in resource management and enforcement of Code of Conduct for Teachers are largely lacking. Learners lack child to child clubs and gender sensitive latrines to encourage retention of learners while paying special attention to needs of girls through provision of sanitary pads.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Despite substantial progress on mainstreaming durable solutions in national development frameworks (ESSP FGS), the context of ongoing insecurity and drought means that safe and dignified durable solutions (return, local integration or settlement elsewhere) to displacement is limited to access to learning opportunities and humanitarian assistance, and diversion of assistance. As a result, many are forced to move from their communities and some also move preemptively for fear of violence being deliberately imposed on them. This has had negative effects on learners who largely depend on structures (schools) that are largely fixed. Insecurity has led to occupation of schools, destruction of learning materials and loss of learners and teachersThe project aims to build the capacities of learner to be resilient to shocks faced during crisis through promotion of child to child clubs at school level, train Ministry officials to supervise schools and develop policies that can ensure sustainable development of schools based on the already established ESSP and MDGs in the South West State of Somalia and train CECs to initiate discussions and eventual projects that can support school by generating income. The choice of activities has also been carefully done to ensure sustainability and durability of programming.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and cities. Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. In IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision with little or no land to erect classrooms. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing education services, or from the sheer absence of education services due to poor targeting of settlements. This project will target most vulnerable learners and teachers with keen attention to IDPs who are able to continue education even in emergencies.Baidoa has the second largest IDP population besides Banadir. READO having worked with IDPs in this locations on other programs including education SHF Education integrated project will adhere to its organizational policies, principles and guidelines as well as humanitarian principles in ensuring inclusivity of all vulnerable and marginalized groups from the project design stages through the full cycle of implementation and monitoring with feedback mechanisms enhanced to promote quality response to needs of the affected populations. Biasness shall at all times be discouraged through set out controls to check on the protection of the community most at risk. Learners from minority groups/clans and those with special needs will be looked at with special lenses especially in response so that they too can have safe and protective learning environments to access basic quality education.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Displacement affects individuals of all ages and sexes, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and needs may evolve over time. Many IDP learners, whether recently displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, are compelled by a lack of access to education assistance, to resort to negative coping mechanisms with serious protection repercussions. For instance, children of school going ages (5-17) are at a heightened risk for recruitment into fighting forces, child labor, early marriage and dropping out of school. Schools serves as a safe space for boys and girls and is critical to ensuring that children displaced by or affected by drought and conflict do not only have access to education but are better able to cope, survive and recover from the crisis impacts and contribute to longer term peaceful development of Somalia.To mitigate on these issues READO will strive to implement the Safe Schools policy recently developed by the FGS and focusing on mainstreaming protection in Education through activities like; Prioritizing safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm; Promoting equitable and impartial access to all; ensuring accountability to affected populations and Strengthen participation and empowerment of women and girls at levels of Education management/programming.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Despite previous issues of insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks on schools including through improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and aerial bombardments, as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between the non-state armed actors, the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) and supporting actors have done alot to ensure return to normalcy in the newly accessible areas. Baidoa and surrounding districts are now very accessible with most IDPs flooding the district due to its relative calmness. However, assumptions are that risks such as eruption of new conflicts and emergencies may still arise thus eroding the little gains already made in ensuring stability and continuity of learning processes in emergencies.READO will strive to implement the recently ratified Safe Schools Policy to ensure safety of learners and learning processes in schools and on the roads leading to schools. READO will always adhere to humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality and do no harm so as not to be seen to be involved in the political/social dimensions of the communities.
