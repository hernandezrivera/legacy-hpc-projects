Project Summary

To ensure the most vulnerable conflict-affected households (HHs) in Cabo Delgado can meet their basic household needs through access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene facilities through the construction of key infrastructure.
People continue to be seriously affected by the ongoing conflict in northern Mozambique. The impact of armed conflict, violence and insecurity in the region, combined with successive climate shocks and disease outbreaks, have led to recurring waves of internal displacement in the province, in search of safety and basic means of survival. According to the International Organization for Migration's (IOM) DTM Mobility Tracking Assessment, the region has a total of 834,304 internally displaced people and 420,200 returnees in 221 locations. The majority of this group is made up of women and children, with 79% of current internally displaced people and 76% of returnees being women and children (under 18). Although many communities have decided to return to their place of origin, the situation remains very uncertain with security risks in many districts, making it difficult to implement long-term interventions in some areas. In this unstable scenario, families are unable to access the most basic services in several sectors and assistance does not reach several districts. Assessments conducted by CARE and partners revealed that WASH interventions are among the most urgent needs, as described below:
WASH: Access to water continues to be one of the biggest challenges in Cabo Delgado. An RNA in Macomia revealed that there are no water points in the internally displaced areas, forcing people to pay or travel long distances to access water. Schools also face a lack of water and adequate sanitation, creating barriers to school attendance, particularly for menstruating girls who often miss school during their cycle. Furthermore, the lack of a latrine was reported in all assessments consulted. Most households and settlement sites do not have latrines or the latrines are damaged and do not function.
Therefore, CARE proposes to focus on WASH interventions in Quissanga and Macomia districts to benefit both IDPs and returnees. Because both districts are hard to reach and underserved, CARE will combine emergency assistance with long-term solutions. This will allow families to benefit from long-lasting interventions.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

To ensure the most vulnerable conflict-affected households (HHs) in Cabo Delgado can meet their basic household needs through access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene facilities through the construction of key infrastructure. This project targets internally displaced people, returnees and host communities.Due to the traumatic experiences and the losses, all conflict affected IDPs are considered vulnerable. However, certain groups such as women (particularly pregnant women, lactating mothers, female heads of household and widows), children, elderly persons and persons with disabilities (PWD) are especially at risk because they face additional challenges. The lack of resources, accessibility and decision-making power make it difficult for these groups to meet their basic needs. Data reveals that the majority of IDPs in the northern of Mozambique are women (29%) and children (50%) (IOM). Women and girls are typically in the frontline of crisis as first responders, caregivers to their communities, and rebuilders of livelihoods. Yet they are also at greatest risk of GBV and the negative health and economic consequences of gender inequality. The difficult living conditions in both resettlement sites and host communities create especial risks to women and girls’ safety. The assessments revealed that many families share the same room or tent, and the sharing latrines are located outside the building zones, exposing women and girls to high GBV risks. Pregnant women and other people with special needs reported that they have limited access to adequate health care services.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CARE has established contingency measures to protect mission personnel and assets operating in emergency response. All field activities are suspended during the critical periods. As part of the security measures for staff, satellite phones have been made available to CARE staff in the Pemba office so that they can stay in contact even if the telephone networks were damaged. This guarantees agile communication between the teams during the emergency response activities. Furthermore, CARE’s Safety and Security Manager will provide real-time information on the security context, guiding the team on the implementation. At the time of the proposal, interventions planned for districts are limited to the WASH sector, as shelter interventions would require a higher exposure of the team in the district increasing security risks. Those assessments will be conducted throughout the project period, allowing CARE to provide life-saving interventions while maintain safety of the staff. Furthermore, CARE has attached the main risks and mitigation strategies identified for the project.CARE has a robust safeguarding policy in place which is managed by the Gender and Protection, MEAL and an accountability team. Safeguarding Risks, including SEA, are identified, managed, and reduced using a risk reduction approach. Safeguarding staff will support project accountability holders through induction and training on SEA, safeguarding, child abuse and code of conduct to staff, partners, suppliers, volunteers and all who encounter children during the project who will be required to sign the Code of Conduct. Training also enables the team to identify the first signs of abuse and report accordingly. A due diligence check is also done for all new employees before recruitment. In addition, in all CARE’s emergency response implementation locations there are designated PSEA focal points. Furthermore, CARE will continue to circulate relevant materials on the PSEA to beneficiary communities as part of the awareness-raising. Community members will be made aware of the PSEA 6 Principles, CARE’s Code of Conduct, and the mechanisms to report any violations. Potential SEA risks in program delivery settings will be examined and mitigated accordingly by gender-balanced staff. Reporting mechanisms counts with help desk, suggestion box, compliant hearing committees and a toll-free line (linha verde). All complaints are reviewed and any case involving Government staff is referred to the appropriate government and staff. . By offering different communication channels for feedback and complaints, CARE takes into account different communication accessibility requirements of community members, depending on their age, gender, literacy, preferred language of communication, and any disability they may have.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

the development of gender-empowering WASH activitiesthe development of WASH activities with gender empowerment, with integration in the conduct of the project with a minimum of 50% inclusion of men and women, in the responsibility of activities and the creation of committees.As a gender-focused humanitarian relief and development agency, CARE has designed this project placing women in the centre of the implementation. CARE and Ophavela staff working under the project will receive a gender training and orientation to understand gender dynamics, challenges and their responsibility while working within the affected communities. Across project delivery, CARE will prioritise women headed HH for the WASH and shelter interventions. Under the shelter sector, CARE will train and involve women builders to replicate a durable and dignified shelter `type c` which consists in a vernacular traditional design using local materials. The shelter training will promote safer shelter construction prioritizing women participation. Under the WASH sector, all the interventions will follow the minimum commitments for the safety and dignity of affected people. CARE will tailor its awareness messages for female groups. Below are the key measures to ensure protection and gender mainstreaming across the project’s interventions: • WASH facilities constructed/installed under this project, including water points, latrines and handwashing stations, will be strategically located within communities to mitigate SGBV risks. • Latrines built under this project will be equipped with locks to mitigate SGBV risks and will be accessible to people with limited mobility. • Gender Mainstreaming into WASH training for Water Point Committee (WPC) and community mobilisers. • Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) prevention messaging will be embedded in community hygiene promotion campaigns. • Any distributions will feature gender awareness sessions with targeted messaging to encourage the increased participation of women, redistribution of care work, GBV and SEA prevention, and Feedback and Complaints Mechanisms (FCM).• Providing IEC material with images about gender, child protection, PSEA and safeguarding to support

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Solid waste management in all project execution processes, from design and implementation, inclusion of environmental and solid waste management applicability standards in all WASH project processes.In addition to this, CARE recognises the importance to implement sustainable practices during project implementation to minimise the risks of doing no environmental harm. Under both Shelter and WASH sectors, CARE uses the environmental checklist provided by the clusters to guide the choice of materials for the interventions and promotes the use of government approved raw materials such as wooden poles and bamboos. With regards to the shelter environmental technical working group, CARE has submitted a request for participation in November 2022, however, group is not active. In addition, CARE will give preference to local suppliers and businesses, especially those from disadvantaged communities or underrepresented groups, to foster economic development and resilience. Whenever possible, CARE chooses products or services with minimal environmental impacts, making use of reliable and effective labeling schemes. CARE will encourage suppliers to reduce packaging waste through measures such as properly sized packaging, removing unnecessary packaging layers, or using recycled content. Furthermore, CARE’s procurement process is electronic, opting for soft copies to reduce paper trail.
