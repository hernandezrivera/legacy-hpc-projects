Project Summary

IOM CCCM activities plan to contribute to all the four CCCM Sector objectives for 2020.
The project aims to address the Objective 1 (Provide and coordinate streamlined humanitarian lifesaving, multi-sectoral assistance and strengthen the basic infrastructure support in IDP sites) through:
• Maintaining and managing Reception Centers ;
• Maintaining and managing one Planned Camp ;
• Establishing and managing new Planned Camp and/or Reception Center in Idleb and/or Rural Aleppo; and
• Collecting information about IDP sites in Idelb and Rural Aleppo, including multi-sectoral gap analysis, and reporting to Cluster members under the Sites Monitoring Team (SMT) project.
The project plans to meet the Objective 2 (Improve the management quality in and accountability of IDP sites) through:
• Enhancing camp management capacity of camp management of informal settlements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo under the Site Management Support (SMS) project; and
• Enhancing camp management capacity of NGOs through the provision of blended remote CCCM trainings for humanitarian workers based in Syria.
The project would contribute to the Objective 3 (Strengthen household and communal coping mechanisms in IDP sites) through:
• Enhancing communal capacity of informal settlements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo to address fire risk through the provision of fire safety measures and training under the SMS project.
Finally, the project aims to meet the objective 4 (Inform the CCCM response of cluster members) through:
• Collecting information on IDP movements and displacements in Idelb and Rural Aleppo and reporting to Cluster members under the SMT project.
The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with CCCM Cluster Coordinator, by IOM Turkey cross border team based in Gaziantep. IOM will directly implement the blended remote CCCM training, and for the rest, IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced local NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the CCCM Cluster.

4 - Needs

According to the CCCM cluster, there are an estimated 739,462 IDPs living in last resort camps, informal settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorate. Furthermore, among the estimated populations of 6.15 million IDPs in Syria, 12% are young children (<5), 32% are children (5-17), 51% are adult (18-59), and 5% are over 59 years old (Elderly). In 2019, the CCCM cluster tracked 1,446,401 population movements across northern Syria. Furthermore, due to the recent hostilities, 586,215 IDPs have been newly displaced since 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020. During this period, nearly one quarter of IDPs sought shelter in IDP camps, informal settlements and collective centers in north-west Syria. To cope with sudden displacements, the CCCM cluster has actively promoted the establishment and management of Reception Centres for sudden displacements in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. These centres have hosted 66,740 IDPs (monthly cumulative sum), including over 32,119 new arrivals, since their establishments in late 2017 until the end of 2019. The planned camp IOM manages in Mareet Elkhwan, Idleb, also has received 5,506 individuals (1,241 families) in total from congested and vulnerable IDP sites since its opening in November 2018 until the end of 2019, contributing to alleviating the burden on already overstretched IDP hosting capacity in the region. Compared to formal camps, collective centres, and reception centres (which do receive some form of relief assistance), informal settlements have intermittent or non-existent basic service provision, as well as poorer standards of sanitation and housing infrastructure. Therefore, there are urgent needs of providing multisectoral humanitarian assistance to displace populations in reception centers and planned camp as well as to improve living conditions and management quality of IDP sites, especially informal settlements, in Northwest Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with CCCM Cluster Coordinator, by IOM Turkey cross border team based in Gaziantep with technical and administrative support from IOM sub-office in Gaziantep. IOM will directly implement the blended remote CCCM training, and for the rest, IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced local NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the CCCM Cluster. IOM recognizes that remote management poses several challenges for implementation inside Syria. To address these challenges, IOM developed a Whole of Syria Monitoring Strategy which aims to guarantee rigorous and effective monitoring of all projects implemented inside Syria. Stipulated in the strategy is a two-pronged M&E approach: The first approach is to strengthen the M&E capacity of IPs to monitor the project and be the gatekeepers of accountability. The second approach consists of IOM monitoring activities conducted through TPM. Regular monitoring activities, which include (but is not limited to) beneficiary assessment, distribution monitoring and post-distribution monitoring, will be conducted by IPs while construction verification, post-distribution monitoring activities, satisfaction survey will be conducted by third parties. IOM will also engage all different groups of target community through regular meetings, transparent information management and throughout implementation. IOM will apply the IOM AAP frameworks, including community feedback mechanism, focus group discussion and joint community-IOM post service monitoring, to all project activities and will work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP throughout the project lifespan. Based on feedback from the affected population, the project is modified as much as possible to better address needs of camp residents. Gender and age are key vulnerability criteria for beneficiary selection of some activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Risk 1: Continued operations of reception centers, planned camp, and any possible coordinated relocation assistance will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. Linked to this is the risk the Reception Centers will become ad-hoc camps if IDPs perceive it to be a long-term solution. • Risk 2: Needs assessments and/or Intentions surveys will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs living in camps and contribute to assessment fatigue. • Risk 3: Tacking IDP movements and analyses displacement trends will encourage IDPs to flee to areas where CCCM members are present.• Risk 4: The management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not truly representative of the IDP communities.• Risk 5: Equipping and training emergency responders and IDP committees in IDP sites will let individual residents over-rely on the trained responders and committees for responding to disasters and shirk individual responsibility. • Mitigating measure 1: i) Maintain the CCCM guidance and position on camp expansion and establishment and camp support ensuring that camps are the last-resort; • Mitigating measure 2: i) Ensure that the intention surveys and needs assessments are as light as possible and inform operational guidance; • Mitigating measure 3: i) Contribute to the collective IDP tracking; and ii) Cover areas that have not been assessed when mapping IDP concentrations during sudden displacements.• Mitigating measure 4: Ensure that any management structure is representative of the entire community with strong selection criteria. • Mitigating measure 5: i) Incorporates general awareness raising focused on the individual capacities of men, women, and children; ii) ensure that partners work in line with humanitarian principles and follow the principles of Do No Harm; and iii) apply the IOM AAP frameworks and will work to build the capacity of project partners in AAP throughout the project

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Staff mobility and access restrictions to affected population: Active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance. o IPs are encouraged to report any security and access-related issues and to develop and update their evacuation/contingency plans. o GPS coordinates of project sites will be shared with OCHA for de-confliction.o Contingency areas in other locations will be pre-identified with the CCCM cluster during project design if possible. o No UN staff will cross into Syria, which will lower the profile of the activities and ensures the safely of both UN and IP staff. ‘Duty of care’ strategy in place for local partners.• Aid diversion by armed groups. o IPs are encouraged to report any incidents of aid diversion and interference incidents by the armed groups. o IOM utilizes third party monitoring mechanisms to conduct warehouse verification, distribution and post-monitoring distribution activities to mitigate the risk. o Targeting of most vulnerable beneficiaries will also be a key under this action. Recruitment of female staff will be promoted. • HLP disputes on project sites. o HLP Due Diligence Guidelines has been/will be used to minimize the risks associated with land ownership. All partners will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land, and discuss with owners and available site management.
