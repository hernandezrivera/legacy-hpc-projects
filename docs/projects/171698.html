Project Summary

At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10-14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1 in 2019 Deyr, and 13.8% in 2019 Gu).
Despite the serious nutrition situation, some livelihoods have critical rates of malnutrition due to food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin A supplementation and poor child care and suboptimal feeding practices. The Somalia 2020 Micronutrient Survey shows that there is an increased susceptibility of mothers and children to malnutrition. It indicates that around 40.2% of non-pregnant women and 47% of pregnant women are anemic and similarly around 43% children below five years are anemic, and both these indicators reveal a serious public health challenge. The same survey shows that the situation of Infant & Young Child Feeding practices is well below the acceptable standards. The prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding is at a low level of less than 20%.
In the 2020 post Gu food and nutrition assessment, pockets of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15 percent were reported in 9 out of the 37 areas surveyed:East Golis, Bosasso IDPs (Bari), Garowe IDPs (Nugaal), Galkacyo IDPs (Mudug), Beletwein Urban, Shabelle Riverine, Mogadishu IDPs (Banadir), Juba Riverine, Elberde pastoral). Morbidity among children was high (≥ 20percent) in 11 out 37 population groups surveyed.
The proposed interventions will cover treatment (Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program for malnourished children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women - PLW) and prevention (Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program for children 6-23 months and PLW, Mother, Child Health and Nutrition (MCHN) for children 6-23 months and PLW in the districts where GAM (Global Acute Malnutrition) is above 15%.An estimated 849,900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021, including 162,000 who are likely to be severely malnourished.
The World Food Program is the leading UN agency in Somalia in fighting hunger. In Somalia, WFP together with the Ministry of Health, nutrition cluster and the partners supports nutrition interventions in the treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) ,prevention of acute and chronic malnutrition and treatment of HIV/TB malnourished individuals. Treatment of MAM prevents children from mortality and deteriorating to Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), which requires extensive medical support; prevent deterioration of maternal nutrition status and subsequent low birth weight.
WFP collaborates with UNICEF and MOH to offer an integrated package of nutrition and health. This program follows the Ministry of Health guidelines for treating acute malnutrition in Somalia.
WFP also implements preventive program: Seasonal Blanket Supplementary Feeding (BSFP) a seasonal program implemented in areas with high rates of acute malnutrition and food insecurity. The purpose of the program is to reduce the incidence of malnutrition. The target group is non-malnourished children 6-23 months and PLW.
Mother and Child Health and Nutrition Program (MCHN) aims at promoting growth by focusing on the first 1,000 days of life which contributes to prevent acute and chronic malnutrition and break the inter-generational cycle of malnutrition. Pregnant mothers also receive an incentive if they deliver their babies at health facilities ensuring safer deliveries under the supervision of a midwife/or skilled health worker. The program also aims at increasing the utilization of antenatal and post-natal services as nutrition services are offered within the health centres. The target population is children 6-23 months and PLW in the districts where Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is more than 15%.Worldwide 50% of people living with HIV/TB are malnourished, WFP aim to promote adherence and uptake of treatment.Nutrition and health messaging is integrated across all WFP nutrition programs.

Mitigation Measures

Protection of civilians to ensure communities are not forced to leave their residence to access humanitarian aid.WFP has a large presence in Somalia and works with local partners to provide assistance in all regions of the country that are accessible to humanitarian partners. WFP supports a network of 256 MCH centers providing preventive care and 593 sites providing treatment support under the TSFP programme. Up to a third of the TSFP sites are mobile, enabling WFP to reach populations in remote locations. This means that targeted women and children do not have to walk great distances to receive WFP nutrition assistance and reduces the risks associated with such movement.The use of cash-based transfers and SCOPE cards also allows beneficiaries to access their entitlement at locations most convenient to them. Protection of women, girls and boys from sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse ( SEA) when accessing humanitarian assistance.WFP has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse which focuses on acts committed by WFP employees or others associated with its work against the people it serves. WFP’s CPs are required to abide by WFP’s regulations and to adhere to its commitments against harassment and abuse. WFP has a Somalia-specific GBV referral protocol and is continuously working with protection partners such as UNICEF and the GBV sub-cluster to update referral pathways to ensure coverage in locations with gaps in service provision. WFP has provided training on PSEA and GBV to its local partners and organises regular refreshers sessions for the CPs . Protection that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project .At the beginning of each project, WFP carries out consultations with all stakeholders at the community level, discussing all aspects of the project design and implementation. This includes various community members, religious leaders, clan leaders, youth leaders and political leaders. Government stakeholders and other nutrition partners are also consulted through the nutrition cluster. This engagement serves to secure community buy-in and ownership of the project and avoid any backlash that could lead to violence against recipients of assistance.To mitigate against exclusion and marginalisation, WFP employs community-based targeting principles and processes through which the most vulnerable and under-represented members of the communities, including IDPs, are able to participate. Communities are consulted on the validation of vulnerability criteria, and formation an all-inclusive selection committee for the identification of most vulnerable household at the village level. When carrying out community consultations and targeting, specific attention is paid to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation from across the community. WFP supports the participation of both men and women as well as other vulnerable groups by providing safe spaces for these groups to provide feedback (e.g. women-only focus discussion groups).Accountability to affected population WFP has a robust complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) that handles complaints and feedback from all beneficiaries and all stakeholders involved in the assistance process. The CFM not only aims at strengthening accountability to communities but also to identify a range of protection, fraud and diversion issues as well as other concerns related to delivery of WFP assistance. Complaints can be made through a well-established Somalia-based toll-free hotline, directly to WFP monitoring staff and cooperating partner staff at programme implementation sites, through telephone calls and email or short messaging to WFP offices, and through local authorities and local leaders. Information on WFP’s hotline numbers is widely available through posters at food and cash distribution points and is printed on the SCOPE card.

Please provide further explanation

Many IDP, urban and host populations struggle to meet their adequate nutrition needs in times of crisis or even after crisis subsided. Most IDP face chronic food insecurity due to lack of reliable livelihood opportunities and exposure to shocks over many years. In response to this, WFP Somalia Country office has moved towards multi-year, comprehensive Interim Country Strategic Plan – ICSP (2019 – 2021) that enables WFP to forge the long-term multi-stakeholder partnerships that are central to humanitarian, development and peace objectives. In the framework of ICSP, WFP address the root causes and better support longer-term sustainable solutions (through nutrition and resilience and nutrition sensitive programming) while effectively addressing immediate humanitarian needs. In Somalia ICSP, WFP has adopted a multi-sector approach, based on the comparative advantage of bringing together agriculture, livelihoods, health, food assistance, nutrition and WASH as the most effective of reaching those furthest behind. WFP had secured rolling yearly funding like KFW (German fund) and RBA (Rome Based Agencies) that demand the participation of the governments playing a crucial role in laying the foundation for resilience and longer-term development. Similarly, WFP has embarked on strengthening the working relationship with national counter parts to build governments’ resilience and adaptive capacities to address malnutrition. This makes WFP nutrition programmes well placed to not only contribute to both humanitarian and development objectives, but also leverage its presence, tools and dual mandate to enhance its contribution to peace. The ICSP aligns with National Development Plan priorities to support a nationally led development and peace process and supports the capacity of the government at Federal, regional and local levels to provide durable solutions for the citizens. The SUN initiative supported by WFP, UNICEF and other UN agencies and has created a multi-sector platform to address malnutrition in Somalia. Likewise, WFP and UNICEF supports the Ministry of Health in the harmonization of the Community Health Workers tools and training. The outreach component is a critical part in the Essential Package for Health Services that cuts across all the levels of health service delivery. Under the leadership and primary responsibility of the Ministry of Somalia at the Federal, State and local level, harmonised outreach strategy ensures involvement of the populations of concern in health and nutrition programs. This approach fosters community based and participatory approaches allowing the affected population to participate in the planning, ownership and management of durable solutions to resolve the health and nutrition problems affecting them. WFP will continue collaboration with the government on programme implementation and capacity strengthening on policy development and implementation and monitoring of the nutrition activities. The government has taken the leadership of nutrition services delivery both at the Federal and at the federal member state level. WFP will work together with the government to support the holistic approach to nutrition programming.
