Project Summary

The intervention aims to improve safe and adequate access to sanitation services for the most unserved and underserved vulnerable people in Southern governorates at Gaza strip (Khan Younis and Rafah), which is in line with HRP 2023 strategic objectives, WASH Cluster objectives and comply with HNO 2023 analysis. The communities selected in full coordination with PWA, CMWU, Khan Younis Municipality, WASH cluster coordinator, and Khan Younis WASH area focal point Action Against Hunger (AAH) as the project targeted locations. PEF will contribute in enhancing accessibility to safe and adequate sanitation services for directly 8,300 (6500 in Al Satar, 1800 in Al Furqan) most vulnerable people (1,982 men, 1,969 women, 2,222 boys, 2,127 girls) living in Al Satar and Al Furqan localities in Khan Younis and Rafah respectively, who are currently unserved or not connected to any sewer collection systems and rely on old cesspits/percolation pits, which are not well managed or carefully handled due to peoples’ vulnerable economic situation. Throughout the year, these pits are subjected to frequent flooding and continuous accumulation of wastewater, creating unsafe and unsustainable conditions, and exposing people to dangerous health risks, particularly diarrhea and skin diseases in children. The environment was also subjected to severe deterioration due to the seepage of wastewater into subsurface soil and pollution of the groundwater. In addition, the pests, vectors, and rodents’ outbreaks were frequent phenomena in this area. PEF will formulate gender- balanced communities’ committees to be engaged in the project lifecycle and participate in decision making process. PEF plan to construct sewer collection networks and household connections in targeted locations that are the most efficient, adequate, and affordable solution, according to the Gaza context, to improve people’s access to proper sanitation services and decrease their dependence on expensive or environmentally unfriendly solutions, such as in-open wastewater dumping and cesspits. The installation works of sewer collection networks in the targeted community will include 1) construction of different sized sewer pipelines and manholes, and 2) closing and filling of cesspits and septic tanks and reconnecting the households to the sewer collection system, which will be connected to the public wastewater networks up to the wastewater pumps’ stations to treatment plants as planned by PWA and CMWU through the developed master plans. The installation works will be implemented by highly qualified and expert contractors contracted based on a competitive tendering process to ensure high-quality works are delivered. PEF is an active member in WASH cluster, with broad experience in implementing humanitarian projects in the Gaza Strip. This extensive expertise has gained the communities’ trust and cooperation and resulted in an extensive network of local partners. The action will also address critical safety, health, hygiene, water and wastewater situation, water conservation, protection, safeguarding, GBV, women empowerment and environmental issues and maintain shared responsibilities and total behavioral change practices through awareness community sessions at HHs level including IEC materials. Additionally, PEF will promote community ownership, women empowerment, accountability, and participation throughout the project and community engagement activities, and in-place complaint and feedback mechanism.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) remains in a protracted crisis, where millions of Palestinians, who are still struggling to meet their basic needs, will be highly vulnerable to humanitarian shocks driven by multiple political and socio-economic factors in 2022. In Gaza, more than half (60%) of the people live in poverty, and it is estimated that (29%) people will require humanitarian assistance in WASH sector (oPt Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 - Analysis). The humanitarian situation in Gaza is very fragile due to the prolonged concurrent blockade imposed by Israel since 2007, internal Palestinian political divisions, and the impact of destructive recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups. During the May 2021 escalation, there were more than 100 incidents, causing damage to water and sanitation infrastructure affecting more than 1.2 million people in Gaza, as measured by OCHA Flash Appeal (May 2021) and Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) Preliminary Analysis Report (June 2021). In the context of the increasingly deteriorating humanitarian situation in Gaza, about 648,596 people are at risk of damage to their health, mainly due to insufficient access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, as stated by the oPt Humanitarian Needs Overview - Analysis (HNO) 2023. Moreover, water and sanitation services to the local communities have been severely impacted. This has been exacerbated by recurrent military conflicts, rapidly deteriorating infrastructure, strict limitations on the import of construction materials and water pumps, diminished and unreliable energy supply, consequences of the COVID-19 outbreak, as well as the rise in prices of materials due to the conflict in Ukraine along with sanctions imposed on Russia. In addition, the limited resources have diminished the capacities of the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU) and local municipalities, the primary WASH service providers. In May 2022, PEF in partnership with Mercy Corps conducted a rapid needs assessment to identify urgent needs and priorities to improve people’s access to WASH services and safe conditions. The needs assessment was conducted in four locations, Al Manara, Jorat Allout, Alstar, and Albatn Alsamin in Khan Younis. The assessment consisted of six focus group discussions (FGDs) with locals (three male and three female) in Al Manara, Jorat Allout and Alstar, as well as five key informant interviews (KIIs) with local leaders, existing neighborhoods community committees’ members, representatives of water and sanitation departments in Khan Younis Municipality and CMWU in all four locations. The results showed that people living in these areas were not being served by any sewer collection system and rarely depended on new septic tanks, relying on old cesspits/percolation pits instead, which are not well managed or carefully handled due to peoples’ vulnerable economic situation. Throughout the year, these pits are subjected to frequent flooding and continuous accumulation of wastewater, creating unsafe and unsustainable conditions, and exposing people to dangerous health risks, particularly diarrhea and skin diseases in children. The environment was also subjected to severe deterioration due to the seepage of wastewater into subsurface soil and pollution of the groundwater. In addition, the pests, vectors, and rodents’ outbreaks were frequent phenomena in these areas. In addition, Al Furqan neighborhood is one of area in Al Sultan locality in Rafah, which also suffering from wastewater accumulation and absence of safe drainage, as some international agencies and organizations intervened through construction of wastewater collection system. According to CMWU needs assessment, 120 HHs (1,800 individuals) are remaining in need and depend on cesspits as a coping mechanism which require continuous sweeping to avoid flooding that is costly and increase the burden on those vulnerable communities.Urgent response is necessary to these communities’ needs, especially women who are enforced to reduce the amount of water used inside their homes and even reduced the amount of water from the existing storage tank of the building, stop using the toilet and kitchen and depending totally on the jerricans for washing, cleaning, showering, urination and defecation and disposing its content at different locations, at the near open agriculture area and sometime at the road. Moreover, it increased the burden on women as caregivers to children and elderly through using coping actions toward reducing water usage as a main source of hygiene. These are affecting directly applying of personal and public hygienic practices which lead to deterioration of public health of elderly and children and safe environment.PEF had identified these assessed locations/communities in coordination with the PWA, CMWU, Khan Younis Municipality, WASH cluster coordinator, and Khan Younis’ WASH area focal point Action Against Hunger (AAH).PEF will intervene in construction of wastewater collection network, households’ connections, closing of existing cesspits with all associated works. Moreover, the intervention will work to increase the targeted HHs awareness regard hygiene, water and wastewater situation in Gaza strip, women empowerment in management water resources and protection.The proposed action will contribute to alleviating health risks experienced by vulnerable groups, especially women, children and elderly, due to the widespread use of unsafe and unsustainable solutions for handling domestic wastewater, such as cesspits/percolation pits that are frequently subjected to flooding and wastewater accumulation. The action will contribute to protecting the environment by providing environmentally-friendly solutions for wastewater collection and management, such as wastewater collection networks and households connection and closing the existing cesspits.

2 - Activities

Toward achieving the project outcome “Improved safe and adequate access to sanitation for unserved communities and people are suffering from wastewater flooding and accumulation”, two main outputs planned to be achieved. Construction of the sewer / wastewater collection system (6500 L.M) with households’ connection (Output 1); and conducting awareness sessions for targeted communities at HHs’ level (720 sessions) (Output 2). In details, several activities are planned to achieve the intervention results. Launch the Project, Establish Coordination, formulation of gender-balanced communities’ committees and Conduct Baseline Assessment (Activity 1.1.1) as PEF will conduct a grant-opening meeting with all program and operational staff, followed announce the project in the targeted areas, conduct detailed areas’ mapping, and establish/update the coordination mechanism with PWA, CMWU, Khan Younis and Rafah Municipalities, WASH cluster coordinator, WASH cluster areas focal point AAH & IRPAL, and other relevant cluster members. In targeted communities, PEF will establish or reformulate existing communities’ committees to be gender-balanced committees in coordination with Khan Younis and Rafah Municipalities and CMWU and will conduct orientation sessions for community committees’ representatives to ensure community engagement in project management and decision-making process, accountability, and transparency. The project team, with the participation of gender-balanced community committees and in consultation with the Khan Younis and Rafah Municipalities and CMWU, will prepare and finalize the baseline and technical assessment tool, which will be used to collect technical and social baseline information to update the indicators’ values to compare against the end line data when closing the project to measure the action’s feasibility and impact. All women need, thoughts, priorities, perspectives and concerns will be considered strictly in project activities’ verification and updating.PEF will activate its complaint and feedback mechanism in the targeted locations, which will be accessible during the project implementation for dealing and solving any complaint according to the transparent and accountable mechanism that PEF practice in all its projects. PEF will introduce its Child Safeguarding, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and Gender Equality guidelines and standards to all project staff, community committees, community leaders, and targeted groups during the orientation sessions and community involvement activities to ensure all complaints and feedback are received, addressed, and efficiently closed. In addition, any urgent cases will be referred to the relevant cluster and relevant active actors.Construct Sewer Collection Networks and Household Connections (Activity 1.1.2), PEF will be responsible for the overall project management and installation of sewer networks and household connections in Al Satar (4500 L.M pipes) and Tal Elsultan/Furqan areas (2000 L.M pipes). The pre-technical assessment indicated that the sewer system would include laying about 6,500 m of different sized UPVC pipelines, constructing different diameter manholes and HH connection, and closing existing cesspits. PEF, in close coordination with the CMWU and Khan Younis and Rafah Municipalities, will update the Bill of Quantities (BoQs) and conduct new market surveys for updated material prices, and develop tender dossiers, which includes engineer drawings, calculation sheets, general and specific conditions, technical specifications, and all other relevant supporting documents. An open and competitive tendering process will be initiated according to PEF procurement policies starting from the tender dossier development and announcement, ending with the closing stage and handing over to CMWU, Khan Younis and Rafah Municipalities. The contracted contractor will start the installation works of the dedicated sewer collection networks and HH connections under the direct supervision of PEF, Municipalities and CMWU engineering teams. Upon completion of the contractors’ works, the sewer collection networks and household connections will be ready to operate. Conduct awareness sessions (Activity 1.2.1), PEF will design and develop the implementation plans for community awareness promotion campaigns, addressing critical health, hygiene, water, wastewater, environmental issues, gender and protection issues in the targeted area in full consultation with gender-balanced communities’ committees. These will focus on preserving and promoting the local ownership of the sewer collection networks, household connections, and the overall value of service installed and delivered. The awareness promotion campaign aims to maintain the shared responsibilities and total behavioral change practices among targeted communities and people. PEF health promoters, in consultation with community committees and stakeholders will design and prepare awareness-raising messages for the community and households. Key messages include but are not limited to personal health and hygiene, handwashing, body cleaning, environmental protection, and cleaning and maintaining the sewage collection networks, household connections and water conservation, including design and print of suitable IEC materials. Information about complaint and feedback mechanism, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), gender equality, and child safeguarding will be part of the hygiene awareness messages.Conduct End-line Assessment, and Project Handover and Close-out (Activity 1.2.2) PEF will work to close the project by conducting an impact assessment at the end of the project, ensuring that the sewer collection networks and HH connections are installed in the targeted area as planned in the project proposal, ensure that women need and concerns were taken and addressed through the intervention.

3 - Indicators

The intervention is in line with HRP STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE 2 “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”, WASH Cluster Objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians", and HRP outcome "The vulnerable population in the West Bank and Gaza improved their access to equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed WASH services ". For Outcome (1) number of people benefiting from improved access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services will be considered as an indicator. For Output (1.1) Construction of the sewer / wastewater collection system with households’ connection, PEF will consider number of meter length of sewer network installed/ rehabilitated (Indicator 1.1.1), number of people with improved access to functional and safe sanitation facilities and services (Indicator 1.1.2), Percentage of HHs’ women confirm enhancement of daily hygiene practices (Indicator 1.1.3), and Percentage of HHs’ women state noticeable reduction in children skin diseases (Indicator 1.1.4) as indicators. For Output (1.2) Conducting awareness sessions for targeted communities at HHs’ level, PEF will consider number of WASH awareness campaigns conducted (Indicator 1.2.1), number of HHs received awareness sessions in the planned topics (Indicator 1.2.2) and Percentage of HHs’ women knowledge improvement in the planned topics (Indicator 1.2.3) as indicators.PEF is committed to genders-sensitive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) across all its programs and projects. PEF has developed a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability, which forms the basis of M&E system for all PEF projects. In case of emergency, PEF’s minimum standards for MEAL and gender in emergencies will be applied. Throughout the project MEAL will be undertaken in accordance with comprehensive M&E plan which will track performance against indicators (outputs and outcomes), timeframe, and encompass key criteria for baseline/end-lines and other related studies. Data collected will be gender sensitive and disaggregated by sex for both qualitative and quantitative in nature using different tools such as questionnaires, Key Informative Interviews (KII), and Focus group discussions (FGDs). PEF teams will coordinate with projects manager, partners, local community committees on regular basis to ensure the implementation of M&E actions based on projects plans and in consultation with OCHA.PEF team will be engaged in data collection activities such as baseline/end-line survey, interim monitoring review, beneficiaries’ satisfaction FGDs, and other M&E actions. For this project, monitoring will be at two levels including adequate monitoring tools:• Monitoring implementation in the field: day to day monitoring of activities is the responsibility of PEF and project field staff. This will include but not limited to: daily progress follows up sheet, technical tracking of construction work, follow up and monitoring sheets, and weekly follow up meetings with municipalities and local communities’ representatives.• Data collection plan for project indicators and general project impact will be further developed and expressed in the Logical Framework Analysis. The project impact will be assessed by baseline, and end-line surveys. Output indicators will be assessed using the following data collection tools: questionnaire, observation checklists, and community FDGs. As MEAL will prepare and produce MEAL Plan, MEAL Framework, Baseline/Endline reports field visits report. The end-line survey will measure the status of planned indicators and achievements of project implementation on people’s lives in addition to people’s satisfaction with the quality of provided support and performance of PEF for accountability and learning purposes. Both quantitative and qualitative methodologies will be applied in data collection and its analysis to ensure case studies are captured.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PEF has its own feedback and complaints mechanism and policy that will be established and announced through the intervention, mainly in orientation sessions with communities' committees, communities and HHs through awareness visits. All complaints and feedbacks will be received by PEF MEAL Officer (except complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, GBV, PSEA, or fraud, will be referred to PEF executive manager and board trustees as confidential reporting) and classify these complaints and feedbacks based on type of each complaint such as - but not limited to -: request for benefit, objections, suggestions, needs, protection, safeguarding ... etc. These complaints and feedbacks will be studied through a specialized committee based on the type of received complaints and feedbacks, and deliver their recommendations and actions to PEF management. PEF management will consider the specialized committees’ recommendations in decision making process through project implementation and in future interventions if could not be maintained through the project duration and allocated budget. On another hand, it is worth to mention that women complaints and feedbacks will be considered strictly and managed in confidential manner; their suggestions will be maintained well during project implementation to realize significant improvement to their accessibility to the services and increasing their resilience to perform their home duties in dignified manner, and protect their children through applying a proper hygienic practices without receiving blames and enforcement to improper water reduction procedures.Moreover, PEF and its implementing partner will carry out regular and irregular progress and control meetings with gender-balanced communities’ committees to ensure the community participation and engagement in decision making process and increase their loyalty and ownership to the project deliverables which will be reflected in realizing sustainability and effectiveness.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PEF is committed to the core humanitarian standards including the “Do no harm” principle. PEF is accountable to ensure that any provided services and activities will take all risks seriously into consideration and will have possible mitigation measures to avoid harming targeted or untargeted populations including the surrounding environment or any unintended negative impact. PEF will ensure applying of protection and safeguarding measures through excessive training of project staff and supporting staff, signing code of conduct, activation feedback and complaints mechanism and continuous follow up. A protection officer will be allocated to follow all detected cases and refer them to the specialized agencies and organizations for managing their cases. Through training of committee and pre-implementation orientation sessions, PEF will ensure knowledge receipt of communities’ members regard PSEA, GBV, protection and safeguarding and how the process is going on. All team members will be trained to detect potential protection risks, PSAE, Child safeguarding and gender equity. Moreover, all suppliers and contractors that will be contracted should sign code of conduct, commit to avail safe and protected working/implementation environment through applying health and safety measures and mobilizing required insurances’ polices. PEF will also ensure that the local contractors provide safe access paths and passage to all people (Include disabilities) in the targeted locations during the installation/construction works of sewer networks. However, some access restrictions to the construction sites will be put in place to protect the community members and project team. Nevertheless, alternative safe passages and routes will be provided in coordination with local authorities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This action’s response is grounded on the fact that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries including people with disabilities, youth, children and elderly, both direct and indirect, must be protected. Installation of wastewater collection networks and HH connections will improve the access to sanitation to different gender groups and will address individualized sanitation needs and priorities, thus protecting their dignity. These works, combined with community mobilization activities and orientation and awareness sessions, will minimize potential health and hygiene risks associated with the existing unsafe and unsuitable cesspits. The action will also focus on the need of women to be engaged in community committees and municipality activities due to their severe lack of representation in public life. Key humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration especially the do-no-harm principle, particularly important in Gaza’s context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative gender norms. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be activated to ensure their direct engagement and ongoing consultation with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the project, thus enhancing community engagement and reducing risks or exclusion at community level. Building the capacities of targeted communities to improve their self-protection mechanisms will be ensured through conducting orientation sessions at households’ level to inform and consult with targeted communities about services that will be provided as well as on safety measures and access modalities. It is worth mentioning that preserving dignity is essential to maintain self-esteem and confidence, important to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will be positively reflected and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in the overall improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers. A diverse and gender-balanced staff, including teams of male and female and community committees’ members, will be deployed in the field during both door-to-door assessment and project orientations and community mobilization activities to mitigate cultural and gender-associated risks for targeted communities.
