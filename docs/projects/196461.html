Project Summary

The project aims to respond to 6,800 HHs (34,000individuals) from the most vulnerable conflict affected IDPs, and host communities in Idleb governorate, and prioritizing households headed by female, child, disabled, or elderly, then households with severely ill or disabled members, children below 5, elderly, pregnant or lactating women; with special attention to those recently displaced or living overcrowded.
The project contributes to providing lifesaving and life-sustaining shelter & NFI assistance through the following activities:
1- provide 3,750 HH with seasonal NFIs including heating material In the villages and camps of the following sub-districts (Dana and Harim ) in Idleb governorate. Each HH will receive heating material and prioritize its needs of these items accordingly.
2-provision 1,100 of core NFIs in the villages and camps of the following sub-district Harim) in Idleb governorate
3- Construct 700 houses with an average cost of 2571.43USD in sub-district Harim in Idleb governorate.
4-Implementation of 1,250 HH level improvements (Tent graveling) in sub-district Harim in Idleb governorate.
This project will also contribute to the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing through construction of 700 houses with an average cost of 2571.43 USD in (Harim sub-district) in Idleb governorate.
Needs assessment was conducted early this year to identify the project locations and all activities are tailored with respect to the needs. In all activities IRW considers the protection of the beneficiaries, so brochures of safety and security practice will be distributed to beneficiaries. Moreover, female staff will be recruited and trained to ensure more protection from exploitation and sexual abuse for girls and women and to deal with any GBV cases may encounter during the project.
Most importantly IRW pays high attention to people with disabilities, elderlies, and people with special needs, and for those assistance will be delivered to home.
Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights of shelter sites, shelter beneficiaries, and house owners will be considered throughout all stages of shelter repair interventions, and agreement will be signed to guarantee the rights of the beneficiaries. Coordination with SNFI cluster and other active members is essential to ensure geographical separation and avoid overlapping/duplication.

3 - Needs

In 2023, the estimated number of people requiring NFI support has increased by five per cent to 5.7 million. With funding shortages currently at a historic low, the NFI sectors’ limited response in 2022 is further expected to exacerbate needs as 2023 unfolds. Harsh winter conditions affect further the most vulnerable and access to basic NFIs is needed to ensure that health, well-being and protection concerns are met. Key NFI items such as, winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential, accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals. With the primary driver of displacement reported as a worsening economic situation and lack of access to livelihood opportunities, household ability to meet basic needs is projected to further decline thus increasing the severity and intensity of shelter and NFI needs across population groups. Evidence from 2022 already points to this assumption/expectation, as the average monthly shelter and NFI expenditure has risen to 26% and is further expected to rise in 2023 forcing the population to rely more on humanitarian assistance. The CCCM winter weather vulnerability analysis classifies IDPs sites on a winter weather vulnerability scale, 61 sites hosting 64,000 individuals are in the catastrophic vulnerability level, 293 sites hosting 354,000 individuals are in the very bad level, and 484 sites hosting 600,000 individuals are in the bad vulnerability level.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Potential conflict between the beneficiaries and nonbeneficiaries as well as between IDP and host communities. Strategy: Build on IR reputation among all stakeholders in cooperation with the community representatives choose the beneficiaries according to selection criteria that is agreed with the community. It will be clearly communicated that the non-selected ones will be included in the upcoming project with respect to the selection criterion. • Inclusion and exclusion errors leading to sexual exploitation for extremely vulnerable population especially IDPs and female: IR will share precise guidance to all members of the project team on the beneficiary selection process and criterion to avoid errors. • Mistreatment of beneficiaries by the project staff or the health facility staff - Strategy: All of the team members will be ensured to have trained on code of conduct and ethical behaviours. • Risk of GBV/safety around the latrines maintained or constructed through this project- Strategy: IR will ensure women, girls and PWSN are consulted for the design of latrines in terms of reflecting their special security concerns.• Fluctuations in exchange rates leading to differences in amounts at the time of purchase: Try to speed up the procurement of local materials so that there is no significant difference between the date of raising the need and the date of purchase as well as contracting with reliable suppliers and agree on the financial value to suit the exchange rate.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IR Syria field offices comply the PSEA policy and prior to the project start as part of the orientation of the project staff that is recruited, they will be informed of this policy. The collected feedback and complaints will be received and analysed by the complaints officer/focal point and will be addressed according to IRW CFM policy, special attention will be given to the GBV, PSEAH, CP, etc, potential incidents and complaints, such cases will be directed to the country director to take the required actions. IR Turkey will make sure that the health workers of the supported health facility have adequate knowledge of the CFM and the related subject. IR Türkiye has provided training on the CFM to the staff in the supported health facilities in the past two years IR team will make sure that all the health staff in the supported health facilities have adequate knowledge regarding the CFM and there are no gaps in this regard. In the context of Cholera, IR will try to adopt suitable approach that will allow IR team to conduct trainings\sessions to strengthen the capacity of the CFM and safeguarding for the catchment population and main stakeholder. IR will be very clear about its roles and responsibilities as well as roles of the main stakeholder and target communities during the implementation of the project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area: IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees who will help in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation and the checkpoints. No access to certain target area Islamic Relief can cancel the inaccessible target areas from the distribution plan. Islamic Relief will reallocate the goods to more secure and accessible areas. On certain occasions, delaying the distribution for a limited time until accessibility is again possible. Closure of the Turkish border, IR will coordinate with Turkish authorities and the Turkish Red Crescent to enter the goods through relief cross points and to have exceptional border passing permissions. Interference of armed groups in the project areas (In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, IR will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.) Market fluctuation changes in prices. Prior to the start of implementation, a market assessment will be conducted in order to see what is available and what is not and establish contingency plans.
