Project Summary

This project will undertake the provision of access to enhanced, unhindered and equitable WaSH services to Vulnerable IDPs and host communities in MMC, Jere, Ngala and Rann in Borno State.
Borno State in the North-East region is largely affected by insurgency and has left most vulnerable crises-affected people in need of access to timely and appropriate WaSH assistance. Access to humanitarian assistance has improved throughout the State, though periodic access constraints have persisted notably in many parts of the State. Despite this improvement, Crisis and Emergency remain widespread, and an estimated 1.75 million affected persons are targeted for assistance and support in the year 2020.
The protracted crisis in the North East of Nigeria has caused unprecedented consequences on men, women, boys and girls across sectors. The continued insurgency has disrupted livelihoods, broken-down basic infrastructures and services. In addition, sporadic attacks and military operations have forcefully displaced population from their homes to safer places in search of humanitarian assistance; of this number, women and girls are the most vulnerable.
In 2018, there were 12, 276 Cholera cases with 197 associated deaths in 35 LGAs in the North-East. A total of 1.5 million people were considered to be at risk and required cholera prevention interventions measures. In 2018, flooding, fierce winds and sand storms caused substantial damage to WASH infrastructures which affected at least 20 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Borno state and 28 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Adamawa states respectively. New arrivals from hard-to-reach areas, especially women and children, are also in urgent need of WASH support. They include at least 100,000 children under five.
This program shall therefore deliver equitable and unhindered access to WASH services to IDPs in targeted and host communities in Borno State. Also, addressing the Covid 19 related challenges particularlly.

1 - Needs

According to an OCHA humanitarian report released on the 6th of May 2020, the number of people in need of urgent assistance in North East Nigeria rose from 7.1 million in 2019 to 7.9 million in 2020 as a result of displacement emanating from the activities of insurgents According to DTM for Round 33 which is 2,118,550, and DTM round 31- 2,046,604 or 420,072 households were recorded as displaced, an increase of 7,512 persons against the last assessment conducted in December 2019 when 2,039,092 internally displaced persons (IDPs) were identified . Also, a total of 1,673,862 returnees were recorded in the DTM Round 31 assessment, an increment of 62,186 or 4 per cent from the 1,611,676 persons that were identified as returnees in the last round of assessment that was conducted in December 2019. The increase in number of IDPs indicates a continued plateauing in numbers of displaced persons in the region over the last couple of rounds. As per the Round 29 assessment that was published in November 2019, 2,035,232 IDPs were recorded. A similar trend was observed in previous rounds of assessment since August 2019. The number of displaced persons in the region is now well above the number recorded in Round 25 (2,026,602), which was conducted before escalating violence was observed in October 2018 even though accessibility remains lower. During Round 25, a higher number of Local Government Areas (LGAs or districts) and wards were accessible. Given that the numbers of IDPs is increasing slowly although accessibility remains low, it can be inferred that the actual displacement figures could be much higher. To gain insights into the profiles of IDPs, interviews were conducted with 4 per cent of the identified IDP population — 86,268 displaced persons — during this round of assessments. The information collated and analysed in this report includes the reasons for displacement, places of origin and shelter types, mobility patterns, and unfulfilled needs of the displaced populations. Additionally, site assessments were conducted in 2,372 locations (down from 2,375 in the last round of assessment, conducted in December 2019). The purpose was to better understand the gaps in services provided and the needs of the affected population. These sites included 290 (down from 293 in the last round of assessment) camps and camp-like settings and 2,082 locations (no change since the last assessment) where IDPs were residing with host communities. Site assessments included an analysis of sector-wide needs, including shelter and non-food items, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), food and nutrition, health, education, livelihood, security, communication and protection. Also from previous assessments, ngoing and protracted displacement has created continued humanitarian needs for WASH services and placed additional pressure on already fragile WASH services and facilities, especially in 27 LGAs across Borno state. With congested populations in displacement sites, notably in Jere, Rann (Kala/Balge), Ngala, and MMC LGAs in Borno State, water-borne diseases can spread rapidly. Spiraling cholera outbreaks have taken hold each year. In 2018, the cholera outbreak has affected 18 LGAs with a total of 4,250 cases, in addition to 1.5 million people who are considered at risk and require cholera prevention interventions. Flooding, fierce winds and sand storms have caused substantial damage of WASH infrastructure with at least 20 per cent of IDPs living in camps in Borno state seriously affected in 2018. New arrivals from inaccessible areas, especially women and children, are also in urgent need of WASH support. They include at least 100,000 children under five and 30,000 pregnant and lactating women across nine LGAs in Borno State. 56 percent of the IDP population is children (under 18 years of age).

2 - Activities

SHO shall conduct an assessment to MMC, Jere, Ngala and Rann (Kala-Balge) prior to implementation of the program to screen vulnerable IDPs and deliver WaSH services through the following activities: Activity 1: Provision of safe water through the construction and rehabilitation of water systems: this will be carried out in camps and settlements, host communities and areas of return which are yet to reach humanitarian standards, mostly located in the selected LGAs. SHO will seek to consolidate and expand coverage to comply with international standards, including the provision of at least 15 litres of safe water per day per person. Activity 2: Provision of safe water and sanitation in public institutions, through the construction and rehabilitation of WASH infrastructures: these interventions in camps and settlements, host communities and areas of return will aim to stabilize and reduce the decline in efficiency of existing public infrastructures, especially health and nutrition centres and schools with a special focus during cholera outbreaks and foster an integrated response with health, nutrition, education and early recovery sectors. Activity 3: Provision of sex-segregated sanitation facilities (latrines, showers and hand-washing stations) in camps and settlements: SHO will consolidate and expand coverage to reach international standard of a maximum of 50 people/latrines and 100 people/shower. Latrines and shower blocks will be physically separated and pictorially demarcated by sex, age, dis-ability and other Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs), in Hausa and Kanuri languages, with a 1:3 ratio for males to females. Activity 4: Maintenance of provided WASH facilities: SHO will collaborate with affected communities and where necessary, trained laborers/technicians and contractors, to ensure the functioning and maintenance of installed/rehabilitated water systems (boreholes, pumps, solar panels, overhead tanks, taps and chlorination), and sanitation and hygiene facilities (including latrine dislodging, fixing latrine doors/locks, and regular restocking of hand-washing stations). Activity 5: SHO will carry out regular safety audits to monitor and address safety concerns related to WASH facilities in a participatory way including through community feedback mechanisms.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SHO will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, SHO will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, persons with disabilities and persons with specific needs, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities/specific needs are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. SHO will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the community-based WASH committees. SHO shall ensure feedback from the vulnerable IDPs and host community members through single and collective reports and provision of suggestion boxes. After the project has been completed, SHO will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.Salient Humanitarian Organisation will at all times liaise and coordinate with partners on site to call to mind their mandate which requires the incorporation of all principles of protection so as to prioritize safety and dignity, avoid causing harm, enhancing meaningful access, for accountability to the project beneficiaries and enhancing participation with the intention to empower the project participants. The involvement of humanitarian workers in acts of sexual exploitation and abuse is a grave violation of our responsibility to do no harm and to protect people affected by crises. Sexual exploitation and abuse violates universally recognized international legal norms and standards. It brings harm to those whom humanitarian agencies are mandated to protect, and jeopardises the reputation of these agencies and their ability to provide protection, therefore, Salient Humanitarian Organisation will in all areas of operation activate kin attention to monitoring and reporting PSEA modalities that will help in identifying and handling PSEA cases accordingly. Although States have obligations to respect, protect and fulfill rights related to SGBV, a growing backlash caused by the activities of insurgents has weakened political, financial and psychological support for protection concerns of the displaced persons living in camps and host communities. These barriers relate to availability, believes, accessibility and cultural norms as well as the quality of the services provided. Entrenched social norms in Borno and gender inequality around women and girls, means women behaviours are controlled and they may be stigmatised for being violated. Due to their age, teenage girls’ ability to express herself over violation may not even be respected but rather, damned. All these make it difficult, and often prevent young people and other minority groups from accessing services having to do with SGBV and SEA. Salient Humanitarian Organisation will on a weekly basis create door to door awareness and carry out mass sensitizations to open the eyes of the Internally Displaced Persons to Sexual and Gender Based Violence among divergent topics affecting the PoCs, the Persons of Concern will also be taken through the intricacies of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and how to report cases regarding these concerns. At the management level, SHO already has policies (PSEA Policy, Gender Policy and Child Protection Policy) that are assented to by all staff and by implication, the policies are binding on all and the organization does everything possible to educate her staff concerning these policies and how to mainstream them while in their places of primary assignment.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Salient Humanitarian Organisation has feedback mechanisms that would help in giving back information on the status of the project. Our feedback mechanisms range from suggestion boxes, telephone lines and one on one contact made with the IDPs either for questioning or to answer interview questions prepared as a formal questionnaire. we will at certain instances reach the IDPs for Key Informant Interviews and Focused Group Discussions among others like radio programmes etc. More so, SHO has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. SHO will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, on CCCM activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to SHO’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. SHO will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. SHO’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable SHO to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner.
