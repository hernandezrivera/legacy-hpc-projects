Project Summary


According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Report, 14.9 million people in Syria require life-saving primary and secondary health assistance in 2024, a slight decrease of about 400,000 people from the previous year. The significant increase in the number of those in need is attributed to repeated outbreaks of diseases and a change in methodology to become more proactive rather than reactive, focusing on service availability and the deteriorating social and economic situation of local communities. Alongside the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent infection cases, the Syrian Ministry of Health announced a cholera outbreak in September 2022, which spread rapidly and is expected to continue with seasonal variations in case numbers. As of October 21, 2023 (epidemiological week 42), 217,512 suspected cases of acute watery diarrhea/cholera were reported across all fourteen governorates of Syria, with 106 deaths associated at a fatality rate of 0.05 percent.
5.1 million internally displaced persons, 2.3 million children under the age of five - including 532,595 expected live births, 4.1 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years), over 700,000 elderly people, and 2.4 million people with disabilities.
Exacerbated emergencies, including the February earthquake and its aftershocks, in addition to social and economic deterioration, have caused widespread psychological distress among the population and exacerbated existing conditions. Estimates suggest that about 1 in every 10 people in Syria suffers from a mild to moderate mental health condition, and 1 in every 10 children needs focused mental health care.
Emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases, vaccine-preventable diseases, and neglected tropical diseases such as influenza-like illnesses, measles, meningitis, and waterborne diseases including acute watery diarrhea/cholera and hepatitis A, and leishmaniasis, still pose a significant threat to affected communities throughout Syria, accounting for more than 16% of all health consultations reported through the health system's surveillance network.
Project Activities: Integrated primary and secondary life-saving health services will be provided to () million targeted needy individuals.
Improving access to and provision of equitable, integrated life-saving health services across all levels of care - community, primary, secondary, and tertiary. This includes emergency and trauma care, maternal and reproductive health, child health - including immunization and nutrition services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), diagnostic services, and specialized care for people with disabilities, those suffering from infectious and non-infectious diseases, and children suffering from severe acute malnutrition with medical complications, and survivors of gender-based violence in need of first-line support. Ensuring the supply chain of essential medicines and medical supplies, as well as the availability of functional devices and equipment, must be guaranteed.
Protection Risks:
Takes into account protection risks that may arise during the provision of humanitarian health assistance, including violence against healthcare, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, discrimination against vulnerable groups, and inequality in access to health services - especially for people with disabilities.
To mitigate these risks, it involves the regular updating of Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) guidelines, mainstreaming protection, implementing conflict-sensitive programs in the health sector, building partner capacity to identify and address protection risks, ensuring that health partners implement the appropriate duty of care for staff, and the safe closure of facilities. Health sector partners also consider allocating a dedicated budget to support the identification and mitigation of protection risks.
