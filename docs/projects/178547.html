Project Summary

The project will aim to ensure access of all children and women to comprehensive
quality CMAM services, disseminate and support C-IYCF practices and promote health
and nutrition seeking and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need.
throughout the project, the following facilities will be supported:
- Orient OTP center.
- 10 RRT teams.
The project will improve equitable access to high quality, life-saving, curative nutrition
services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished boys
and girls under five years and pregnant and lactation women (PLW). Provision of
management of acute malnutrition will be provided at the health facility and community
levels and in integration with infant and young child feeding services and primary health
care services.

4 - Needs

Ten years of protracted conflict in Syria has led to a staggering 4.9 million pregnant and lactating women and children in need of life-saving nutrition interventions, of which 3.7 million are in acute need, according to HNO 2021, It is estimated that an additional 0.3 million mothers and children will be in need of life-saving nutrition intervention compared to 2020. Malnutrition will continue increasing across Syria and will have devastating, long-term, and intergenerational impacts If the urgent life-saving nutrition needs have not been properly addressed.Chronic malnutrition remains one of the major public health and development concerns in Syria, where annually, more than 0.6 Million Syrian children and one out of every three displaced children lose their future development and learning potentials, according to HNO 2021, which is strongly linked with poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and maternal malnutrition, high levels of anemia (one in four children and one in every three to four pregnant and lactating mothers are estimated to be anemic).Maternal malnutrition remains a concern, with nearly 11 percent of mothers are malnourished In North-west Syria, and in some areas in Idleb this rate could go as high as 40 percent, according to HNO 2021, maternal malnutrition is worse among displaced and hard to reach populations.It is estimated that one out of each four women is not optimally feeding their infant and young children, additionally in some areas affected by conflict and among displaced population in North-west Syria, close to 60 percent of children of the age 6-24 months are not receiving diets of acceptable quantity and diversity.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be provided through 2 modalities: 1. Orient OTP center: provide lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, and treatment of acutely U5 children, and PLW’s, integrated with IYCF services, and primary health care services. In addition to receiving all the referred cases that need either nutrition or primary health care services from Orient RRT teams or any other partner in the area. 2. Orient Rapid Response Teams (RRT): 10RRT’s covering the whole of Idleb subdistrict, and part of Ariha subdistrict, Provided services include screening for malnutrition and referral, one-to-one IYCF-E counseling, IYCF-E messages, screening for external visual signs of corporal punishment, and suspected abuse and violence cases and referral, screening for danger health signs and referral to healthcare centers, in addition to WaSH messages on handwashing practices, food processing, and toilet and sanitation, in addition to information about COVID-19 signs, symptoms, means of infection, and IPC measures. Monitoring will be conducted at every stage of the program through the collection and analysis of routine data on a continuous basis throughout the life of the program. Data collection tools are appropriate and tailored to the program's needs. A feedback mechanism will be established to affirm that all beneficiaries and affected communities have the right to complain if Orient is not abiding by commitments made to them, and provides communication channels to the communities to voice their suggestions and provide their feedback about our intervention. in addition to contributes to improving the quality of our programs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Orient will report any attack on health facility, and support any efforts to highlight protection challenges facing health workers across Syria. -Gender-Based Violence GBV: Hiring of female staff to increase access to health services for women and girls, training gynecologists, midwives, and nurses on the clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals, and link the health facilities with the social services to ensuresafe referral pathways between both of them. -Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: Orient is an active member in the WoS PSEA network, all the staff have been trained on PSEA on all health facilities, all the recruitment process includes PSEA in the criteria of selection and all the staff should sign Orient code of conduct, and PSEApolicy.-COVID19 spread among health workers as well as among beneficiaries: All Orient facilities have been provided with triage tents and other infection prevention and control measures, in addition, all the staff has been trained on the IPC, Orient will insure to provide PPE equipment to all the staff.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Changing front lines, changes in the parties controlling the implementation areas, and significant population movements as they flee shifting conflict areas: Orient regularly monitors the local situation, and Context-specific Safety and Security Management Plans will be in place, and regularly reviewed and updated.- Risks due to the COVID-19 pandemic, that may result in lack of access of children and their caregivers to the project sites or even the suspension of project activities: relevant program adaptations have been developed and are currently in place, in addition to strict IPC measurement.- Interruption of nutrition supplies line if the UNSCR was not renewed: Orient is supporting all the advocacy efforts about the importance of the UNSCR renewal and works closely with the nutrition cluster and other partners to develop a contingency plan to mitigate this risk.
