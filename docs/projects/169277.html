Project Summary

This project aims to conduct contractor-led immediate stabilisation of structurally damaged residential buildings that are already evacuated, and includes those which still present an immediate threat to life at community level through risk of collapse of architectural (balconies, rooves, NON-LOAD-BEARING WALLS) or load-bearing (foundations, walls, columns) elements in Beirut Municipality. Targetting 100 buildings (or around 9% of all Level 3 damaged buildings as per the Shelter sector's appeal needs calculations), the flash appeal scope over 3 months is seen as part of a longer 18 month scope for structural repairs. The proposed work builds on UN-Habitat's experience in repairing and rehabilitating structurally unsound heritage buildings accommodating vulnerable households in Saida and Tripoli in 2019. The current financial proposal is for immediate works COSTING 20% of the total structural repair cost. THE PER BUILDING COST OVER THE 3 MONTH FLASH APPEAL PERIOD IS $18,000, NOTING THAT EACH BUILDING IN THE TARGET AREAS (MAR MIKHAEL, KARANTINA) ACCOMMODATE APPROXIMATELY 8 HOUSEHOLDS [IE 100 BUILDINGS WITH 8 HH PER BUILDING = 800HH; 4 INDIVIDUALS PER HH = 3200 INDIVIDUALS], and that these areas contain concentrations of heritage structures which are both more expensive to address and more likely to have suffered sever damage. RESOURCE mobilisation for the remaining 80% IS underway. DONOR DISCUSSIONS ON a parallel initiative to undertake minor repairs in households in lightly damaged buildings (100 units) amounting to $0.128M are at ADVANCED STAGES.
For the structurally unsound buildings, the scope includes modern and heritage buildings in reflection of the building mix of the target area of Mar Mikhael & Karantina AS two of the most affected areas. In-house architectural and engineering expertise on structural works for heritage buildings will be utilised as needed. The project is geographically aligned to a blast-related CERF-funded ($800k) project UN-Habitat has already commenced with implementing partner PCPM on conditional cash for rent in Mar Mikhael (primary area) and Karantina (secondary area, anticipated gapfilling in soft collaboration with NRC); operational complementarities to meet the cash for rent needs of households evacuated from unsound buildings will be addressed directly through the CERF project and if necessary topped up through referrals. For the minor repairs, it is anticipated that buildings at some distance from the immediate blast zone will be targetted, in coordination with the Shelter sector,
Targetting of buildings will be based on
(1) Beirut Municipality Rapid Damage Assessment (RDA) survey of all buildings (which has been facilitated by UN-Habitat);
(2) Field and phone survey of occupiers of buildings surveyed (undertaken by UN-Habitat) in RDA that are evacuated or are to be evacuated;
(3) Emerging COMPREHENSIVE AND detailed assessments of damaged buildings by Order of Architects & Engineers IN BEIRUT MUNICIPALITY;
(4) Household-level multisectoral needs analysis data from LRC.
The project will capitalise on UN-Habitat's links with the Order of Architects & Engineers as well as its staff presence currently embedded in Beirut Municipality Emergency Cell to engage in capacity-building on the shelter response to structural unsoundness, specifically at the levels of technical surveying and information management, technical works in the field and HLP awareness-raising to support the housing rights of evacuees among related issues. The HLP training will integrate gender sensitisation, reflecting the Shelter sector's prioritisation of female-headed households in particular.

1 - Needs

As noted in the OCHA SitRep # 5 (17 Aug), 40,000 buildings were damaged and 3,000 residential structures seriously damaged. According to the Port Blast Response Shelter Sector Strategy, "(an) estimated 1,144 buildings (30,660 ind.) fall within level 3 have heavy damages to the property including structural damages compromising the physical safety of the building and the household. It was reported that several buildings have either collapsed or are at risk of collapse". As at 23 Aug, an ongoing rapid damage assessment of buildings in Beirut Municipality facilitated by UN-Habitat has to date already identified 101 buildings that are at risk of collapse and for which evacuation notices have been served. The rapidly gathered and rich survey data that now exists identifying structurally unsound building needs presents an urgent obligation to immediately respond to known threats to life (at community level; emergency phase) as well as to expedite the return of evacuees (household level; early recovery response phase). At zone level, the target areas of Mar Mikhael and Karantina are classed as socio-economically 'majority poor' or 'mixed', indicating limited capacity to absorb the shock of the explosion, particularly so with regard to the relatively high-cost residential work of structural repair.

2 - Activities

1. Select a list of evacuated buildings based on (a) damage status (particularly in terms of the imminence of collapse risk of architectural and/or structural elements) and (b) socio-economic profile of community and displaced households. This is through triangulated analysis of (1) Beirut Municipality Rapid Damage Assessment (RDA) survey of all buildings (facilitated by UN-Habitat); (2) Field and phone survey of occupiers of buildings surveyed (undertaken by UN-Habitat) in RDA that are evacuated or are to be evacuated; (3) emerging detailed assessments of damaged buildings by Order of Architects & Engineers; (4) household-level multisectoral needs analysis by LRC. To reach the target of 100 actionable buildings, the selected list will number approx 120 buildings in order to allow for attrition at field validation stage. 2. Conduct a rapid technical field verification exercise to update and validate current understanding of structural status of selected buildings, to allow for the possibility that high-risk structures may have undergone further deterioration. 3. Utilising well-established agency linkages, liaise with the regulatory entities relevant to undertaking stabilisation works, including those specifically on heritage grade buildings, to determine whether any of the shortlisted buildings may need to be removed from further consideration. 4. Drawing on in-house and rapidly mobilised engineering and architectural expertise, conduct works through contractors to rapidly stabilise architectural (eg balconies, rooves) and/or structural (eg foundations, load-bearing walls, column) elements at imminent risk of collapse in selected buildings. Deployed personnel will have completed PSEA and gender-related online training as is mandatory in UN-Habitat. 5. In soft collaboration with other partners working on structurally unsound buildings (notably NRC), in consultation with relevant Lebanese entities (notably Beirut Municipality, Order of Architects and Engineers), and in coordination with the Shelter sector, produce written guidance on best practice and minimum standards in responding to structural unsoundness in residential buildings, incorporating both technical and HLP-related content, the latter drawing on UN-Habitat's engagement in a pending HLP Technical Committee being established under the Protection sector with the Shelter sector. The HLP-related dimension of this guidance will reflect and highlight the existing Shelter sector focus on challenges disproportionately affecting female-headed households in order to offer prioritisation guidance. 6. Undertake capacity-building through the hybrid remote and in-person training of Beirut Municipality staff, utilising interim outcomes of the process of guidance production, to foster holistic responses to structural unsoundness in residential building covering assessment data management and analysis, structural works technicalities, HLP considerations, with cross-cutting PSEA and gender sensitisation. Steps 1-3 will be carried out to identify, in coordination with the shelter sector, appropriate targets for the minor repair works in terms of areas characterised by light damage where the available budget is suffiicient to bring units up to habitable standards.
