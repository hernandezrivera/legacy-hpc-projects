Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen access to quality integrated GBV prevention and response services both in the contexts of protracted crisis and CIVID-19 pandemic in the conflicted affected states of northeast Nigeria. This includes provision of lifesaving multi-sectorial services that will address the medical, psychosocial, legal and protection/safety needs of GBV survivors in IDP camps, host communities and communities of return, as well strengthening community protection mechanisms to mitigate and prevent GBV. The project will focus of expanding and maintain services in safe spaces/shelters and one-stop-centre (OSC), improving referrals to medical services including clinical management of rape (CMR), strengthening case management, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) as well as legal and protection services, procurement and distribution of menstrual, dignity kits, cultural appropriate clothings, and strengthening field coordination including referral pathways and ethical data management. Other complementary activities will focus on strengthening community resilience and promoting GBV/SEA risk mitigation mechanism in humanitarian setting. Our target is to reach 50% (650,000) of the sectors targeted (1,300,000) population in the BAY States, owing from the fact that UNFPA and its implementing partners reached about 40% of all beneficiaries of GBV services in 2020. Special emphasis will be put on strengthening joint inter-agency coordination, M&E, capacity building, assessment and partnership that will translate to better outcome.

1 - Needs

In 60% of the LGAs in the northeast, survivors of GBV do not have access to GBV case management and comprehensive multi-sectoral services which is critical in assisting survivors. This includes gaps in health services, including clinical management and forensic evidence, inadequate mental health and psychosocial support services, limited legal/justice assistance and limited functionality of safe shelter facilities. Safe and confidential survivor-centred services must be made reliably available. If these conditions are not met, survivors may be deterred from reporting incidents of GBV which may result in long-lasting physical, social, medical and emotional consequences. Strengthening the capacity of responding actors to provide timely survivor-centred services in a dignified and respectful manner is critical. At least 18% of households surveyed in the MSNA reported that at least one family member had disappeared or had been abducted. According to the GBVIMS 2020 mid-year report, at least 30% of incidents reported between January and June 2020 occurred in the context of sexual slavery; that is to individuals held against their will, abducted or kidnapped and sexual violence. Several cases have been documented of women and girls who were abducted from their homes, markets, while travelling, collecting firewood or water by NSAGs. Reports also mentioned many have been raped, forced into marriage and labour, physically, sexually and emotionally abused, exposed to sexually transmitted diseases, and often impregnated by their captors. Provision of safety measures (including safe energy options) is needed to reduce women and girls’ vulnerability to conflict-related sexual violence in addition to the provision of comprehensive response and reintegration services for survivors.According to the MSNA, 22% of the households surveyed reported having 11% girls under the 18 years married off, and 11% of the households reported having girls under 16 years being pregnant or as lactating mothers. Significant gaps exist to address specialized reproductive health needs of women, and adolescent girls exist, especially in deep field locations. Adolescent girls, female child-headed households, orphaned girls living with foster carers among others are particularly at risk of GBV.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

UNFPA is a nexus agency by its organization design and mode of engagement as it has the tradition of working with the state governments and key implementing partners in both development and humanitarian contexts. The project will contribute in strengthening basic government health and social services like health infrastructure and women and girls empowerment centers (safe spaces, OSC...) in the three conflict affected states. Capacities of government frontline health workers and management teams will be built on relevant themes and standards to ensure sustained quality GBV services in the context of nexus. Peace building will be mainstreamed in all community engagement activities involving women, youths and men through established community structures.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project ensure the affected persons and the beneficiaries are involved in planning , implementing and monitoring of the programs. The survivor centered approach is an integral component of this project were the survivors drive the aid delivery process toward personalized need. The production of contents of the dignity kits will be purely managed by women themselves who are beneficiaries of the project, The packaging and distribution of the kits will managed by the women led organizations. As part of the accountability to affected population the following steps will be taken • Consultation with the affected population, especially women and girls on culturally appropriately contents • Community Engagement on menstrual hygienic education and awareness • Engaging women in key decision making process like the vulnerability assessment • Conduct Post distribution monitoring to guide the next batch of production • Use of locally source materials and engaging women and girls in production process
