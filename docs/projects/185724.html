Project Summary

The unprecedented violent conflict in the North West/South West Cameroon that broke out in 2016 has caused more than one millions people to flee their homes as IDPs to other relatively more secured municipalities within the NW/SW regions, where they now settle temporary. To the host municipalities/communities the crisis has equally created logistical and humanitarian nightmares – despite the dangers posed by this conflict-induced displacement, local governments only possess a partial understanding of the implications of the crises.
Generally people forced to flee their homes (IDPs) - particularly in situations of armed conflict as the case of NW/SW are generally subject to heightened vulnerability. IDPs suffer various forms of involuntary social exclusion – with high risk of physical attack, sexual assault and abduction, and frequently are deprived of adequate shelter, food and health services. They equally face significant higher rates of mortality than the general population.
The overwhelming majority of IDPs are women and children who are especially at risk of abuse of their basic rights. More often than refugees, the internally displaced tend to remain close to or become trapped in zones of conflict, caught in the cross-fire and at risk of being used as pawns, targets or human shields by the belligerents. In some other cases IDPs may locate in worse marginal habitats, environmentally disqualified for human wellbeing. Besides, estimates on the number of IDP are often very rough, and they tend to differ greatly in terms of the source (governments, international agencies, non-governmental organizations and civil society organizations). This is partly because IDPs most often may be less willing to register. Worse is even the fact that most scenarios of IDPs are observed to have been abandoned to the responsibility of the host government. Ironically some host governments are very unconcerned with the plight of the IDPs especially when the causes of such movements are related to persecution from the host government.
Most often, rights of IDPs are violated not because of conscious intentions but because of the lack of awareness or planning based on a rights-based approach as remarked by the UN High Commissioner for Refugees (Filippo Grandi) that;
“We are at a watershed, where success in managing forced displacement globally requires a new and far more comprehensive approach so that countries and communities aren’t left dealing with this alone.”
Councils and Civil Society of the NW/SW therefore owe a duty to save and serve the internally displaced people living within the municipality following the ongoing armed conflict in these regions. Eventual livelihood transformation and suffering alleviation is not only necessary but urgent.
The Main objective of this project therefore is to “Build the capacity of councilors, quarter heads, council staff, FBOs and CBOs with skill sets on responsiveness to the plight of vulnerable persons living within the municipality especially IDPs as well as their preparedness against any worsening scenarios and participation in early recovery.
Specifically the workshop would bring to understanding;
 An overview of the state of the displaced in the world by end of 2021
 Types of disasters that lead to humanitarian crisis
 Disaster management (the disaster cycle (phases)
 Humanitarian Actors (System): Roles, Responsibilities and Coordination
 Principles of Humanitarian Assistance
 Women, girls and children’s concerns in humanitarian response planning
 Personal and collective planning methods for effective preparedness
 Brainstorm on the contribution/responsibility of host communities (Municipalities/other) during disasters
A similar project has previously been implemented by Green Partners Association (September 2018) at Bamenda II Municipality with great impact. This project is projected to cost US $ 178,213.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The ongoing persistent armed conflict in Cameroon between the State Forces and Anglophone pro-independent fighters since November 2016 has led to a protracted forcefully displacement situation of over a million persons (including children and women), with most movements into relatively secured urban municipalities. Specifically in the NWR the municipalities of Bamenda I, Bamenda II Bamenda III, Wum, Nkambe, Misaje, Tubah, Santa, Mbengwi are among such converging centers for IDPs. Whilst IDPs are concentrated throughout these municipalities, majority are living under very vulnerable conditions; all of them residing in private accommodations as there are yet no collective centres.Unfortunately all figures of IDPs remain estimates especially as data collection processes have hardly saw need of engaging councils. And based on the fact that humanitarian response scarcely involves the participation of the local authority (council) and FBOs/CBOs, the impact of such responses on the most vulnerable remains inadequate comparatively. For instance the City of Bamenda has experienced a visible decline in the level and quality of solid waste management and municipal services has resulted from the sudden and sharp increase in demand and utilization by increasing number of IDPs. Solid waste generation has doubled in several neighborhoods, thus contributing to ground water contamination, which may result in pollution of water resources and spread of water-borne diseases in not too distant future. The immediate challenge of dealing with attitudes of IDPs who went through traumatic mass killings and destruction in their former communities is real. The tendencies of decline in health facilities, nutritional challenges, increase crime wave, weak local economy are immediate but just a few obvious challenges among many that have to be dealt with in their current municipalities of residence. It is of urgent necessity to start sourcing for avenues of intervention in order to mitigate eventualities such as epidemics (as the cholera epidemic in Ndian Division) that could even be worse in not too distant future if something serious is not done. Unfortunately, the municipalities have to deal with administrative, fiscal, and capacity constraints to provide designated services meant for IPDs.The international humanitarian community is not yet getting aware about the necessity to address the needs of IDPs and vulnerable host families with the full participation and engagement of councils. The State either is yet to do an inventory for a better clarity on the situation – in a sense; the key role of local authorities should be defined in a roadmap of priority interventions for stabilization from the conflict. All figures of IDPs remain estimates and with no effective assessment done to ascertain the actual situation of IDPs any possible attempt of humanitarian action would yield any positive results. On the bases of numerous uncertainties and challenges surrounding the humanitarian crisis and situation of the IDPs in the relatively calm municipalities, acting with the technical expertise of Green Partners Association, training workshops shall be organized for key stakeholders of those councils/civil society so as to equip/empower them to better understand, reflect and brainstorm on eventual response to the current emergency, prepare against possible worse scenarios and intervention partnerships and how available relief can be linked to rehabilitation and development activities. GPA already implemented this same project at Bamenda II Council (2018) and therefore has experience to broaden it to other municipalities.
