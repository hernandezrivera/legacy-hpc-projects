Project Summary


The project intends to expand its provision of Inclusive Non-Formal Education to conflict-affected populations across NWS.
Balad has access to targeted sites through its team on ground due to the implementation of a WASH project in same targeted camps currently, and has the necessary expertise due to implementation of several educational projects (2 projects funded by HF, 2 projects funded by UNICEF, and others), the number of beneficiaries of Balad' projects within several sectors in 2019 was more than 435,000 BNFs, with a total funding of $ 1,400,000.
Balad will provide Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for IDP children who have been displaced to Dana (severity 5) and Maaret Tamsrin (severity 4) according severity scale of HNO 2020, the targeted children are 2,734 (1,504g/1,230b-3% Children with Disability (CwD) aged between (8-16) (grades 1-9). Balad will establish 15 TECs (accessible to CwD) – 6 classrooms and an admin room in each – in 15 camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts. A preparation period of 1 - 2 months to establish the TECs, hire the staff and lightly train them. Trained staff and members of EAs in Harim will apply ASER to decide the levels of students. Two cycles of ALP to be conducted (one-month set up followed by 5 sessions /day, 5 days/week to cover 1 cycle in 5 months.). ALP is the best fit for children who have been out of school for 2 years or more, and are older than their classmates who are in formal education. One academic semester is accelerated and covers two semesters, and final goal is to transition of students to formal education, as defined by NFE Task Force of Education Cluster.
146 teachers and education personnel (45%m,55%f,60%IDPs,40%HC) will be provided with incentives and trained on ASER, TICC and ALP. All of them will get orientation about CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and let them sign Balad`s Code of Conduct.
2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each TEC.
Classrooms in TECs will be lightly decorated to make TECs safer and more welcoming
Adapted curriculum B will be printed and distributed
TECs will be provided with school supplies, teaching & learning materials and furniture
Safety and security plans will be established and drilled in TECs which will be provided with safety equipment
TECs will be provided with operational running costs
15 Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) will be established
Project will target IDP students and their parents through mobile outreach teams covering 17 communities surrounding the TECs in followıng activities:
- Conduct BTL campaigns
- Run recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in TECs
- Awareness raising activities for children and parents focusing on Education related issues, Child Labour, Family tracing and reunification, Basic CP, and Child Rights
- Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid
- Providing psychosocial support and referral mechanisms based on needs

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria has forced an estimated 2.1 million – over one-third of Syria`s children – out of school. A further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (238,869 in Dana, and 50,256 in Maaret Tamsrin) and over 120,000 teachers (779 in Dana and 952 in Maaret Tamsrin) are in need of education assistance (WoS Education Severity Needs Analysis 2018). 68% of 226 camps have no education services at all (The schools in Northern Syria Camps report 2018 issued by ACU). Furthermore, according to HNO 2020 "The total people in need that Whole of Syria aims to support is 6.8 million people (severity 3-6), 86% of these people in need have acute and immediate need of humanitarian education assistance (severity of 4-5-6)" and "the protection of children remains a great concern in Syria. These concerns are varied and inter-related and therefore must be met through protection services".- Balad conducted a baseline assessment in 17 camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts, hosting 19,172 IDPs. There are 2,817 (77 of them are with disability) school-aged children (2,511 grades 1-6, 223 grades 7-9, 83 grades 10-12). There are 92 teachers as well. 16 of 17 assessed camps have no schools, and a minority of students are attending a school which is far away. All of them are not supported by any NGO. The camps managers of 16 of them confirmed the presence of spaces to establish TECs and construct WASH facilities.During the field mapping, it was noticed that sensitization campaigns and recreation activities are not available. There is various anxiety levels among some families due to the presence of some cases resulted of the absence of education (mainly girls) and low level of awareness of parents - cases like early marriage, child labour and ignorance among some families.

5 - Implementation of activities

Non-formal education:- Training teachers and education personnel on ASER, TICC and ALP- Capacity building of PSS workers- Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid- Conducting Back to School campaigns to encourage children to back to schools, it will include awareness-raising sessions for children and parents, in addition to distributing brochures on awareness to return to schoolAnd then distributing Educational Kits to children who will enroll in schools- Providing recreational activities for boys & girls. Activities will be concentrating on enhancing different concepts among children like (Positive Competition, Cooperation by participating in Mental or Kinetics activities)- Awareness raising activities for children, parents- Providing non-formal education opportunities for OOSC- Establishing 15 TECs- Providing TECs with running costs- Providing gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities in TECs- Providing schools with school furniture (desk, chairs, blackboards,..).- Providing children with textbooks- Providing children with learning materials (bags, stationary, pens, notebooks)- Paying teachers and education personnel incentives- Providing TECs with safety and security equipment- Establishing PTAs in TECs- Referring the identified cases to the specialized services using the existing CP referral pathwayEvaluation plan: - 2 M&E field officers will be assigned to produce daily field reports to M&E officer at the HQ who is responsible for monthly reporting which include implemented activities, challenges, learned lessons, and planned activities for following month. Monitoring tools include beneficiaries’ surveys, field monitoring reports, interviews, and pictures. M&E officer is responsible for administration of complaints mechanism so users can submit their complaints and suggestions anonymously through complaint boxes located in each school, phone number, an email, or by direct communication with M&E team

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, women and children are exposed to additional protection related risks.Women and girls occupy a central role in BALAD’s strategy for 2018-2023. BALAD has a set of policies and procedures to be respected when working.Some Protection risks that beneficiaries or staff and other workers may be exposed are:1- Learning facilities could be attacked (airstrikes, shelling, fire ......)Mitigating Measures: Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid.Providing TECs with safety and security equipment.Reporting Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism.2- Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students, students participate in bullying of students.Mitigation Measures:Activating the complaints mechanism and clarifying the mechanism used for studentsTeachers sign code of conductTraining teachers and other staff on professional development on preventing and dealing with bullying, positive discipline, classroom management, and participatory approaches with children.3- Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protectionMitigation Measures:The construction of the learning centers and thier facilities should be under supervising of specialist engineers to ensure that all TECs' facilities are safe and protected and supplied with necessary equipment, and also will be gender-sensitive and disability-friendly.4- Humanitarian staff, volunteers or associated personnel are not able to make informed decisions.Mitigating Measures:All teachers and educational personnel will get orientation about CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and will sign Balad`s Code of Conduct, and also the organization has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify the organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1:Parents (women/men/IDPs/Host Community) support children's enrollment.Out of school children (Boys/Girls/IDPs/HC, including Children with Disability) show interest to back to school.Risk 1: Children (Boys/Girls/IDPs/HC, including Children with Disability) aged 8 to 16 are not interested in the program because they are affected with child labor, early marriage and other exploitation form.Mitigation 1: Outreach mobile team will conduct awareness sessions to communities about the value of education.Back to learning campaign will be launched to explain more about project to communities.Risk 2:Girls aged 11 to 16 cannot attend due to social norms of the communities.Mitigation 2:Mobile team to advocate the right of Education to communities. Assumption 2: Learning facilities have safety protocols with adequate resources and are regularly practiced.Risk 1:Attacks on learning facilities (airstrikes, shelling, fire ......)Mitigation 1:Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid.Providing TECs with safety and security equipment.Reporting Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. Assumption 3:All teachers and education personnel have a good understanding about CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and have signed the Code of Conduct.Risk 1:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students, students participate in bullying of students.Mitigation 1:Activating the complaints mechanism and clarifying the mechanism used for studentsTeachers sign code of conductTraining teachers and other staff on professional development on preventing and dealing with bullying, positive discipline, classroom management, and participatory approaches with children.
