Project Summary

BRAC under JRP 2022 will build on previous experience and lessons to continue to serve the Rohingya and host communities adjacent to the refugee camps with quality WASH services. BRAC will allot 70% and 30% of the proposed funds for refuges and host community respectively. This will include sustainable access to basic life-saving inputs and improvement of the quality of socially inclusive and durable WASH infrastructures, with special attention given to potential disease outbreaks such as AWD and Covid 19 and preparedness against disasters. BRAC will continue to prioritize water quality at household level and at water points. This will include periodical water quality testing, along with operation and maintenance of water sources, treatment, and equitable distribution of water to the target population. Regularly water networks data will be collected. Free Residual Chlorine and fecal coliform (FC) checking will be conducted in communities (1 East, 1 West, 2 East, 2 West, 3, 14, 21, 25, 26, 27 and host community). Given the seasonal scarcity of water, BRAC will also focus on sourcing surface water i.e., rainwater harvesting in both communities.
Through continuous operation and maintenance of latrines and cubicles, functionality will be ensured through increased durability, privacy and safety addressing the special needs of women and girls and other vulnerable people in the communities. Assessments, repair, upgradation, and maintenance of FSM, waste collection and SWM will be carried out to prevent pollution of the environment. The communities will be engaged in solid waste management systems which will include segregation of waster at household level, collection, and safe disposal and where possible, recycling and composting of waste. For regular waste management, the beneficiaries will be selected in consultation with the community campaign, coordination, and consultation with CiC, local government and with other stakeholders.
Hygiene promotion activities under the WASH sector (in coordination with health, nutrition, and education sectors) will continue with emphasis on preventing high-risk behaviour and thus enabling safe water use, healthy hygiene practice and solid waste containment. Based on continuous monitoring and surveillance, the response to diseases such as AWD and Covid 19 will be addressed along with needs for critical supplies in soap, water container and menstrual hygiene materials. BRAC will prioritize community engagement, BRAC has already been formed various groups & building their capacity like WASH committee, water user group, latrine user group applying the RANAS approach, engaging leaders (LC teacher, Imam), CBVs, Children Group Leader & MHM Group Leader an alternative vehicle of change to ensure proper hygiene in their respective sub-block level/areas. In host communities, the project will support the efforts of social cohesion and attempts to integrate the services between the camps and the host community program.
BRAC will adhere to GoB’s aims to ensure quality, sustainable and gender-responsive, public service infrastructure and delivery of WASH services and in particular, scale community led sanitation in the communities adjacent to the Rohingya camps. WASH sector strategies and guidelines will be followed in all stages of the implementation with respect to thorough gap analysis CHS standards and at the same time ensuring close coordination with other agencies to prevent overlap. Through inclusive and gender-responsive approaches, BRAC will ensure participation of all, especially considering the special needs of women and girls, disabled and elderly as well as opinions of the youth in the design, implementation, and use of WASH facilities. BRAC will ensure a feedback mechanism, research, and evaluation whereby accountability to affected populations is ensured complemented by in-built monitoring with disaggregated data with respect to age, gender, and disabilities.

1 - Identified Needs

JMSNA (May 2021) reports 23% of households (HHs) across the communities as not having enough water to meet domestic needs and 12% does not have enough water access. The water facilities require up-gradation and improvement and water safety facilities to ensure better quality water in both communities. Between 45% and 70% (WASH Sector water quality dashboard; IOM KAP survey Oct 2021) of the population accessed piped water networks, however 19% still use negative coping mechanisms for water needs, where the main problem is in collecting water from sources distant from HHs. Hand pumps are a major water source which require continuous O&M. Water scarcity in Teknaf prevails, requiring water trucking. 88% host communities (JMSNA July –Aug 2021) access water from tube wells but only 77% of HHs report having enough water for their domestic needs, which in Teknaf is 74% where groundwater is scarcer.JMSNA (Jul-Aug 2021) finds that 99% water networks tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) meeting the minimum sector standard although 71% were above the maximum recommended 0.5 mg/l indicating gaps in O&M. At household level, water quality surveillance found 30% to be below sector standards (23% of the 30% having zero FRC). The number of adult household members openly defecating is 27% and 14% respectively in Rohingya and host communities (HC) (JMSNA May 2021). 11% HC HHs and 27% Rohingya suggest gaps in sanitation infrastructure and around 22% report need for O&M of sanitation infrastructure. Despite latrine installation, improvement in inclusion and privacy, the overall number of latrines was the same as 2020 indicating continued shortage of space for WASH infrastructure. 63% HC HHs reportedly have basic access to sanitation. An additional 7,000 latrines were constructed in 2021, but 20% of the population still report the poor sanitation facilities (JMSNA July–Aug 2021).The gap in in number of bathing spaces has reduced, but 86% Rohingya HHs instead have private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter, mostly unhygienic with poor drainage reported in only 11% HHs (NPM Report Practices, Dec 2020-August 2021). Solid Waste Management remains problematic, where only 51% of the refugee HHs dispose waste in designated areas (WASH Sector July 2019) and 16% dispose waste in open space. On the other hand, 74.5% HHs are satisfied with household level segregation and waste collection practices.20% of total waste is collected and 80% of waste is not collected in camps (MRF mapping WASH Sector 2021). In the host community, 44% reported not having a bin and 69% throw household waste in open spaces. Based on assessments, integrated solid waste management including recyclable/compostable waste management with business chain development needs to be initiated through income generating schemes. Monthly effluent quality tests of the Fecal Sludge Treatment Plants (UNICEF/ICDDRB 2020) finds a prevailing concern in quality of the faecal sludge treatment, indicative of need for regular maintenance or up-gradation to keep the FSTPs functional and effective. 19% HC and 40% Rohingya do not practice hand washing or hygienic behavior (JMSNA May2021). Based on KABP survey, the Rohingya have gained knowledge on hygiene behavior but in terms of practice, there are gaps. The access to MHM kits for women and girls is also limited and needs to be better coordinated among the implementing agencies. Disasters are frequent in Cox’s Bazar. Approximately 23% of the HC population was affected by the floods in 2021, affecting both water and sanitation facilities, however there is not enough data for an accurate evaluation of the overall WASH infrastructure within HC. BRAC’s assessment of its project areas in the camps reports floods in 2021, affected: 500 latrines, 96 bathing cubicles, 149 tubewell, 21 communal pits and 114 shallow tubewell. While some repairs have been done in the short term, there is a need to ensure the repairs are durable.
