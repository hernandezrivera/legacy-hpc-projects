Project Summary

11 years of conﬂict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure. Needs assessments through various sources indicate that currently PIN for WASH Support increased from 12.2 M in 2021 to 14.5 M will be reached in 2023 (as an assessment of WASH sector), which means a 19%% increase for people in need (from 2021 to 2023, due to the increased pressure on essential services and infrastructure. Water supply is a major challenges in Syrian cities due to damaged, broken and underperforming water supply infrastructures because of the prolonged conflict and limited capacity of the authority, as well as because of climate change.
UN-HABITAT has been supporting municipalities and local communities to develop area-based recovery plans through a participatory process. Recovery plans identify the prioritized actions to improve the living condition of vulnerable populations in the target locations. Based on those area-based recovery plan developed in the target location, the proposed project will address the urgent needs of urban community for water for drinking, cooking and personal hygiene in the target area. As such, it will support the rehabilitation of water and sanitation infrastructure as well as solid waste management (SWM) systems to improve the living condition of vulnerable populations including returnees, IDPs and host communities.
As public WASH systems are the most economically viable and equitable way to provide services at scale UN-Habitat will also support enhanced capacity for WASH related public service delivery including securing necessary energy supply through small scale hydraulic electricity generators servicing water pumping stations. The interventions will also contribute to reducing the risk of COVID-19.
The proposed activities of the project for 2022 and 2023 will include:
• Repair and rehabilitation of water supply systems.
• Support to water quality assurance, operation, and maintenance of water supply systems.
• Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems.
• Restore functioning water supply system by introducing alternative energy source by installation of Hydraulic Power Generation Stations.
• Mainstream COVID-19 mitigation measures and response throughout all regular WASH activities
The project aims to reach target 250,000, distributed equally in 2022 and 2023 (125,000 each) as following disaggregation:
Female: 127,500, Male: 122,500, Young children (<5): 27,500, Children (5-17): 82,500, Adults (18-59): 127,500, Elderly (>59): 12,500, Persons with disabilities: 60,000
Bassline (HNO 2022): 1,752,633 acute PIN, Average PIN Ratio: 74% in addition to IDPS: 458,176 and returnees: 19,580
Following the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations will be based on the following criteria:
• Being in line with WASH Sectors and HRP 2022 strategic priorities and objectives, as well as severity of needs
• Being priority interventions and locations identiﬁed by area based recovery plan
• Complementarity with ongoing projects already through UN-HABITAT resources and donor commitments including UN-Habitat projects under HRP Shelter and ERL sectors
• High numbers of people in need, vulnerable and expected returnees in target locations

4 - Needs

Based on the priority interventions identified by area-based municipal recovery plan in different municipalities, UN-Habitat identified prioritized needs in WASH sectors in following municipalities. The concrete needs in target locations are improvements in water supply, wastewater management and solid waste management. The repairment and rehabilitation of damaged water supply system hinders the vulnerable population form access to affordable and reliable drinking water. There are also needs for access to improved wastewater management and solid waste management. The damaged wastewater management system and improper management of solid wastes affect the healthy living condition of vulnerable population.Furthermore, in light of continued COVID-19 pandemic, the COVID-19 prevention measures are remained to be required for the vulnerable population. 9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Potential exposure to (UXO/ERW) in case of support to municipal solid waste removal, water supply and sanitation. Likelihood M, Impact H- Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators; Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area; Add explosive hazard awareness material to your programming.Risk: Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. Likelihood L, Impact H.- MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities; Promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership.Risk: Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to raw sewage and gases. Likelihood H, Impact M- Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with raw sewage wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.Risk: Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of humanitarian organization, its partners or contractors. Likelihood L, Impact H.- Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts; check and register civil documentation.
