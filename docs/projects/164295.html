Project Summary

The project aims to reduce vulnerability and Strengthening resiliency for most vulnerable households by enabling them to improve their food security, secure source of income and mitigate the dependence on humanitarian assistance.
The project has these main components: dairy workshop, a mobile clinic to provide care for cows, and awareness sessions for stockbreeders.
Dairy workshop: project will train 60 women (Every month 6 beneficiaries) theoretically and practically on how to make cheese and other dairy, the Products will be distributed to IDPs in camp for free.
Inside dairy workshop, there will be a special room for children of beneficiaries with disabilities, and providing them with psychosocial support activities by a psychosocial support specialist,
There will also be a specialized babysitter to take care of beneficiaries' children under 5 years.
The required amount of milk for dairy workshop will be purchased from the cow breeders who benefit from the project, alternating between them.
Beneficiaries will be trained in one day a week to produce cow cheese and yogurt from powdered milk, which will be distributed for free to disability care center for 9 months.
After the end of each training month, beneficiaries will receive a 100 kg of milk and $ 100 to start producing the dairy products.
Mobile clinic: A committee composed of the M&E team and veterinarians will be set to select 100 BNFs according to specific criteria.
Cows of selected beneficiaries will receive health, reproductive and nutritional care by field visits and provision of necessary treatments, vitamins and supporting minerals, and provision of food supporting.
There will also be 7 awareness sessions for cattle breeders in Mare' and three nearby villages about how to deal with animals of various kinds, a kit of vitamins will be distributed to the BNFs of these sessions.
In the tenth month of the project, the workshop will continue the production with the last 6 BNFs and sell the products to commercial market in coordination with local council to collect financial revenues of this month and deliver it with the workshop assets to local council to be able to pay the expenses of milk and fuel to operate the workshop with a number of beneficiaries who have completed the training, in addition to train others who have the same selection criteria.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020 9 years into crisis, food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 m people in need of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 m estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 m people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity due insecurity, lack of ﬁnancial and physical access to food, and depletion of livelihoods. In particular, lack of ﬁnancial and physical access to food is the result of a big drop in food production in Syria, lack of income and low purchasing power, high prices, also high fodder prices, insuﬃcient coverage of veterinary services. Due to multiple displacements and reduction in livelihood opportunities people have resorted to multiple harmful strategies include spending savings, borrowing money, and depleting productive assets. Balad M&E team conducted RNA and market assessment in Mare’ assesses availability of dairy products in local market and to compare prices against purchase power. Major findings of assessment were: (estimated number of female headed households is 450). 1- 93% said that milk and dairy products are available in local market.2- 66% said consume 1 liter of milk per day per household, 19% said 2 liters, and rest reported no consumption of milk.3- for yogurt (66% consume 1 kg, 19% consume 2kg, 9% consume more than 2).4- Numbers for cheese were different with only 33% reported consumption of 1kg of cheese daily, and the rest reported no consumption at all. Average price of milk is 200 SYP/liter, yogurt is 275 SYP/kg, cheese is 1200 SYP/kg (more than 2.5 $ - the average daily income is 2$).5- 58% of surveyed said: price is high since it is not compatible with weak salaries.6- 80% agreed that establishing dairy production will decrease price and enhance availability. Local council explained that higher prices due to transportation costs as most of milk and dairy products are being procured from nearest villages

5 - Implementation of activities

• Establishment of a dairy workshop in A'zaz- Contracting with milk providers to supply cow milk (500 kg daily for 5 days a week for 10 months)- Purchasing powdered milk to be used for one time a week- Training 60 women to produce milk, cheese, and other dairy products for one month. After completion of training a certificate, equipment, 100 kg of milk, and $ 100 will be delivered to beneficiaries- Providing protection services for beneficiaries' children in coordination with CP sub- cluster and appointment of psychosocial support specialist and babysitter, and equipping a suitable rooms with all necessaries one for children with disabilities and one for children under 5 years- Free distribution of dairy products for 9 months in camps(about 400 yogurt pail will be distributed for 400 families 5 days a week for 9 months)- Providing Yogurt and cheese which are produced from powdered milk to disability care center once a week for 9 months- Selling products for one month under supervision of LC- Handing over fixed assets of project and proceeds of sales during last month to LC to continue operation of workshop after closure of project• Organizing awareness sessions for cow breeders in targeted area to correct malpractices in cows breeding and how to benefit of their available possibilities to improve health and production of cows (distributing vitamins kits for cattle breeders who attended awareness sessions)- Conducting daily field visits and providing health and reproductive care (providing medicines, vaccines according to a specific program against endemic viral diseases to prevent their occurrence) and distribution of fodder for free to selected beneficiaries (per milking cow 225 kg per month at a rate of 7.5 kg per day for 9 months)• Project will be monitored by M&E officers • psychosocial support specialist and babysitter and all other staff will get orientation about CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and let them sign Balad`s Code of Conduct

Assumptions1: Availability of fuel at an appropriate price.Risk1: Sudden raising of fuel prices.Risk mitigation1: Securing enough amount of fuel since the beginning of the project.Assumptions2: Availability of feed and medicines at an appropriate price.Risk2: increasing prices of feed and medicines.Risk mitigation2: signing a long-term contracts along the project period with the selected suppliers.Assumptions3: Availability of milk production equipment.Risk3: Difficulty in the availability of some production equipment.Risk mitigation3: Manufactured it locally.Assumption4: Registration of 60 women householders in the target area.Risk4: Lack of necessary number of beneficiaries during the specified registration period. Risk mitigation4: Extend the registration period and publish more brochures.Assumptions5: The presence of highly qualified specialists in the provision of psychosocial support servicesRisks5: beneficiaries do not accept the idea of taking their children with disabilities to the workshop.Risk mitigation5: Providing awareness services for mothers by the psychosocial support officer.Assumptions6: Ability to distribute the product dailyRisks6: The inability to commit to producing 500 kg milk per day, especially in the start of the project.Risk mitigation6: compensation for the lack of quantity in another day.Assumptions7: Response of cattle breeders to these campaignsRisks7: Cattle breeders do not respond to such campaigns.
