Project Summary

Handicap International (HI) is dedicated to mitigating accidents related to explosive ordnance (EO) through its fourfold approach: Clearance, Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE), Victim Assistance, and Advocacy.
1. In the Clearance component, Non-Technical and Technical surveys, Land Release Process application, Marking & Demarcation, and Demolition work are conducted. As the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA) -NESMAC is under capacity building , up to date clearance activities are self-prioritized focusing on community needs, this may evolve and HI work in strong coordination with NESMAC. Shallow Water Clearance Diving (SWCD) and High Angle Safety Systems (HASS) are identified as niche areas that HI developed and trianed national capacities on in order to cover for critical clearance needs in Northeast Syria (NES) as underwater clearance and hard to reach/confine spaces. Land release aims to enhance community livelihood by improving access to vital infrastructure, agricultural lands, and cattle grazing areas.
2. The EORE component encompasses direct sessions, public information activities, and Training of Trainers to empower at-risk individuals with information on adopting safe behaviors in contaminated areas. In addition, Contamination Impact Surveys (CIS) and Accident and Victim surveys aim to collect data on EO contamination levels, hazardous areas, and accidents. Safety briefings for humanitarian actors (local and international NGOs) and for public and private sectors raise their awareness about risks and the consequences of explosive hazards in order to minimize the risk of accidents among staff and aid workers.
3. In the Advocacy component, HI lobbies for the ban on Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas (EWIPA), protection of civilians, and access to remote areas. This includes meetings, workshops, and a lobby tour based on the Syria humanitarian agenda. HI engages with the Whole of Syria Humanitarian Mine Action (HMA) sector coordinator to facilitate and coordinate sector work, ensuring coherence and consistency in the humanitarian response.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

NeedsExplosive ordnance impact in Syria is severe, affecting 14.4 (PIN) out of a 23 million population. Daily activities are hindered by contamination, primarily in farming/herding areas. Incidents contribute to higher male exposure, 63% of 218 victims between April and December 2023 (boy and men) in NES. Accidents result in fatalities, injuries, and permanent disabilities. The square meter of contamination reported is increasing every year (above 9 millions m2 in 2023) and 350 single EO awaiting in NES. Victims face barriers to essential services, emphasizing the need for comprehensive intervention for safety, reduced vulnerabilities, and facilitated humanitarian assistance. Implementation of activities The intervention deploys teams in NES (6 RAQ, 2 DEZ) and NWS (2 Aleppo, 4 Idleb) for Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE). Starting with a Contamination Impact Survey, they conduct direct awareness sessions, training, and victim referrals. Inclusive to Disability, Gender, and Age (DGA), the intervention prioritizes culturally appropriate EORE communication material. In NES, clearance activities are implemented in close coordination with NESMAC and the HMA NES Forum working group Protection Risk AnalysisThe INGO prioritizes safety with low visibility, closely monitors clearance activities, and follows a conflict-sensitive approach for land/property clearance. Safety measures include secure facility closure, data protection, staff training, and PSEAH policy adherence. RE training ensures awareness, and inclusive planning with community focal points addresses accessibility concerns through information collection. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy The INGO is committed to combating sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. Robust policies and a Code of Conduct are in place, with mandatory and refresher training. Recruitment includes background checks, and PSEAH policies apply to staff, partners, and accompanying individuals. Information sessions for beneficiaries, complaint mechanisms, and gender balance in staff address cultural sensitivity. Any partner involved in abuse faces case follow-up and termination unless a drastic behavioral change is ensured. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures In NES and NWS: Access loss and border closures are managed with a regularly reviewed strategy. Positive community relations and coordination with local authorities mitigate acceptance challenges. Increased community participation is a focus. Funding risks are alleviated through donor communication and direct negotiation. Exchange rate loss is managed by using USD for money transfer. Epidemic spread is countered with remote modalities, PPE availability, messaging, and monitoring. HMA supply delays are anticipated, coordinating with NGOs and Coalition forces. Border closures impact HMA operations; advocacy efforts involve NES Forum, HMA INGOs, donors, and international actors in addressing authority interference.
