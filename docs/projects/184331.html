Project Summary

This intervention is designed to ensure that most vulnerable crisis-affected children and youth - including children living with disabilities - have access to safe, protective, inclusive and continuous learning opportunities in Gedo, Lower Juba, Galgaduud and Mudug Regions of Somalia. In achieving this, the intervention will implement a set of integrated actions aimed at increasing access and retaining these vulnerable learners in school whilst ensuring they are able to access lifesaving learning within a protective environment. The 12,400 learners (6,820 boys, 5,580 girls) will primarily be targeted from IDPs and vulnerable host communities in these regions. The intervention will be designed to ensure retention of crisis affected school children and increased access to education for vulnerable displaced children and children in host communities in the targeted locations. The project will also focus on schools as an entry point for life saving assistance to vulnerable children and will integrate provision of food security, WASH and protection services at the school level. In line with the Centrality of Protection Strategy for Somalia, the project will enhance focus on the protective role of education and integrate child protection activities in schools. Of these targeted beneficiaries, the project will target 1,240 children living with disabilities, 50% of whom are girls. The project proposes a set of integrated activities including establishment or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces with gender friendly and inclusive WASH facilities, provision of school furniture and teaching/learning materials and teacher incentives. The intervention will also prioritize provision of emergency school feeding and water. In order to strengthen capacity of education actors to deliver effective and coordinated EiE preparedness and response, the action will train teachers on inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning and psychosocial support; CECs on school management, child protection and school safety. Disability mainstreaming will also be targeted, to ensure CwDs are able to access inclusive education. In the targeted locations also, protection related issues have been identified as one of the hindrances to safe access to protective learning environments. These include the presence of armed personnel at checkpoints on the road to school, as well as at the school premises. GBV on the way to school and back is also another concern, in addition to lack of fences and gender sensitive WASH facilities, which particularly impacts girls’ access to education. To respond to these challenges, the project will establish referral system in schools and community-based Child Protection Mechanisms for quick and efficient referrals of children with protection needs. This will include training, mapping of services and focal points within community and school (community child protection committees, School Management Committees, school Child Protection focal points); Provision of community and school based PSS to the affected children and their families through CFS, school clubs, community groups activities, procurement of recreational and dignity kits.; Case management – for Unaccompanied and Separated Children through documentation, immediate care, referrals and family tracing and response to GBV survivors & capacity building and life-saving messaging on prevention of family separation, child recruitment Risk Education, and other protection concerns- for teachers, community members, CP committees. The action will provide and support safe and free education that will help mitigate the impact of conflict and disasters by giving children and families a sense of normalcy, stability, structure and hope for the future, especially post-COVID.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project targets the poor host communities, returnees with specific focus on the IDPs. The IDPs are more vulnerable to protection and education inclusion since they are mostly affected by the environment they live in. Women, girls and boys from the internally displaced communities are exposed to sexual violation and abuse with no/minimal referral system in and out of schools. Insecurity and clan conflict is now one of the main causes for displacement. Drought, floods and cyclone are also the root cause of the displacement in the targeted locations. These locations are facing a dire humanitarian situation, having endured drought and famine as well as continued fighting and unpredictable heavy rains and floods; which is now compounded with recent COVID 19 outbreak, all of which have aggravated the conditions of IDPs and poor host Communities. In Galmudug region also, breakout of armed conflict between armed opposition groups and the Somalia National Army has exacerbated the already fragile situation. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the Jubaland and Galmudug State Governments who are under their responsibility to protect them. Most of the IDPs left behind their livelihood assets and displaced to the big towns with limited assets. Thus, the IDPs have the right to receive necessary service, such as protection, education and other services in order to save their lives. Unfortunately, conflict sometimes is preventing humanitarian agencies from delivering assistance to the IDPs. In this project, ADRA/WI will address protection concerns and supporting education retention and access activities for children already in schools e.g. through provision of safe drinking water in schools, provision of emergency school feeding, hygiene promotion in schools, school supplies and construction of temporary learning spaces among other related interventions is thus vital in ensuring retention of learners. The project will also establish referral system in schools and community based child protection mechanisms for efficient protection needs.
