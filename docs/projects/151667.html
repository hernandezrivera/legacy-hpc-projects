Project Summary

The project is intended to provide treatment and prevention services to children under five in Magumeri, Bama, Dikwa and Ngala LGAs, through the establishment and running of various OTP centers where children with severe acute malnutrition will be treated with RUTF. These OTP sites will also be sites where children with moderate acute malnutrition will be supplemented with micronutrient powder. In addition, INTERSOS is establishing a stabilization center in Magumeri LGA and is taking over another in Bama LGA for the treatment of children with complicated Severe Acute Malnutrition. The project intends to mainstream gender-based violence risk mitigation strategies in all of its different parts.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in the North-East of Nigeria has remained critical since the onset of the conflict between Armed Opposition Groups and Security Forces about eight years ago. This has resulted in the displacement of more than 1 million individuals to date in the BAY states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. INTERSOS has been responding to the needs of these individuals since 2016 in various locations in Magumeri, Bama, Dikwa and Ngala and has nutrition activities specifically in Magumeri and Bama who have been noted to be food insecure, with Magumeri classified as IPC Phase 3 during the second round of the 2017 Cadre Harmonise. Despite all of INTERSOS activities, rates of malnutrition have remained precarious in all locations. In Magumeri LGA, the most recent mass MUAC screening in November 2017 identified more than 2,300 children with severe acute malnutriton in need of intervention. The malnutrition problem has been been associated with a number of causal determinants, from the lack of access to food and health interventions for the beneficiaries which is primarily due to the conflict, to the social determinants such as poverty, a lack of education and various sociocultural factors which encourage patriarchy and place men as the sole decision makers in most family settings. A SMART survey is planned in order to objectively assess the rates and specific determinants of malnutrition in the various Local Government Areas. Referral for stabilization center care from Magumeri LGA has also been a huge challenge as the predominant sociocultural factors that place men as sole decision makers result in a lot of referral requests refused by the husbands of most care-givers. In Bama, a recent SMART survey which was done by the United Nations’ Children’s Fund, the rate of malnutrition in the Bama IDP camp was noted to be about 7.2% and about 3% in the host community, both of which are markedly above the emergency thresholds. In addition to this, the stabilization center in the Bama host community currently run by Medecins Sans Frontieres continues to have an admission rate averaging 10 patients per day and the number of individuals in both the host community and the IDP camp continues to grow, in part due to the government’s Bama Initiative, which involves the repatriation of displaced individuals back to Bama, despite humanitarian and protection concerns. Dikwa and Ngala LGAs are also hotspots for migration according to the IOM’s displacement tracking matrix and INTERSOS has also continued to intervene in health and nutrition in these areas through the provision of primary healthcare and facility-based counselling sessions for Infant and Young Child Feeding as well as through Mother-Support Groups.

Activities

INTERSOS is continuing its nutrition activities in both LGAs through the provision of a global package of CMAM. This is through the maintenance of the thirteen OTP sites in Magumeri LGA which are currently being run through the in-kind support of the UNICEF and the SPHCDA, a stabilization center located in the Magumeri General Hospital supported by the HMB of the Borno SMoH and UNICEF, as well as continuing standard IYCF Practices through supporting various Mother Support Groups (MSGs) as well as ensuring MNP supplementation at the various OTP sites through the continuing support of UNICEF. In Bama, INTERSOS will continue to provide outpatient care to children with SAM in the host community as well as the camp IDPs. In addition to this, INTERSOS is pursuing an agreement with MSF to ensure the smooth handover of SC in the Bama MCH center at the end of 2018. This will be to ensure the continuum of care to children U5 living with severe acute malnutrition in Bama LGA. INTERSOS will also continue facility-based IYCF in both the proposed stabilization center as well as its clinic in the IDP camp. In Dikwa and Ngala, INTERSOS will also continue its facility-based one-on-one IYCF counselling at the health facilities supported by the organization. All OTP services as well as MNP supplementation will continue to be supported in-kind by UNICEF through its partnership cooperation agreement with UNICEF, supported by the NHF/OCHA and USAID/OFDA. INTERSOS proposed intervention will be targeting primarily children under-five with treatment services for malnutrition. The proposed action would contribute to mitigating access constraints to both health and nutrition services especially for children under-five. As this project is also linked to INTERSOS other health, WaSH and protection interventions, it will be a multi-sectoral intervention that will aim to tackle all of the gender-induced constraints mentioned above. Through this project, INTERSOS will also mainstream services for survivors of GBV. These services will act to link some survivors of GBV who fit into the criteria for admission into support structures for mothers of children with severe acute malnutrition for income generating activities and short-term skill training activities. The project will also engage in community health and hygiene promotion as well as mass MUAC screenings through volunteers who are selected from the local community and this will function to improve its community acceptance and engagement and ensure the dissemination of IYCF and GBV messaging to all individuals.INTERSOS will engage with the community where we have these projects through consultation with various community groups – through agents of change including men, through mother-support groups where we mainstream income-generating activities, and through daily consultation with the various caregivers of children living with malnutrition. In addition to this, INTERSOS will also engage in house-to-house hygiene promotion and health messaging in the various communities where we work. Through the same project, we will be conducting periodic SMART and KAP surveys which would be inter-sectoral and would act as baseline, midline and end-line surveys for the project to drive the direction of intervention.As mentioned previously, INTERSOS through this project will engage in various surveys and consultations. These surveys would be measuring impact and needs met. The surveys would also involve objectively measuring rates of malnutrition in the different project locations and this would double as an impact assessment of the effectiveness of INTERSOS’ nutrition interventions. In addition to the surveys, INTERSOS plans to conduct periodic intersectoral safety audits which would be led by the protection team and done in conjunction with the nutrition department.
