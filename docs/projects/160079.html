Project Summary

Somalia has recently made significant progress with the establishment of permanent political institutions which in future, is to lead to decreased dependency on humanitarian aid, and an increase in advancing the country’s development. Despite these positive developments, 4.8 million people are in need of assistance and 2.6 million people are in protracted internal displacement. Somalia continues to present a volatile and challenging working environment. Unprecedented drought and ongoing conflict have severely aggravated vulnerability, and humanitarian needs continue to rise. Though the humanitarian-development nexus (triple nexus) discourse may be at its infant stage, there has been a shift towards strengthening linkages between humanitarian and development activities to promote and maintain lasting solutions to Somalia’s dependence on humanitarian aid. Recognizing the challenges of the past and the new operating paradigm, there is a desire for NGOs to contribute to building the resilience of Somali communities by engaging with the Regional Durable Solutions Secretariat and facilitating meetings and workshops. The SNC is the primary NGO coordination mechanism with a membership of national and international NGOs working in all parts of Somalia (Somaliland, Puntland and South), where it represents its members before governments, UN agencies, donors and multilateral organisations at local, national, and global levels. The core activities (coordination, advocacy, representation and information sharing) of the SNC has remained constant; while at the same time developing to meet the changing needs of the beneficiaries who are working in an increasingly complex and insecure environment. It is through these core activities the SNC is able to raise ‘NGO Voice’ before multi-stakeholders. While Somali NGOs have greater access to populations in need, their capacity to deliver large scale interventions and meet donor requirements is limited. Though, there is an opportunity for better engagement with the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS), International donors and the UN, to ensure coherence between humanitarian interventions and longer-term development strategies and greater ownership and governance by Somali institutions and communities. Despite all efforts to support the FGS in supporting Somalis and tending to their humanitarian needs, there is still tension between the need of the current government structures to build confidence and demonstrate relevance, and NGOs operational requirements for independence in an insecure and unstable environment. It is therefore paramount for coordination meetings with the Federal Government of Somalia as well as the Regional Administrative representatives to ensure concerted effort towards helping the Somalis in need is addressed. Owing to high staff turnover within government offices, it is essential for this meetings to keep them abreast with NGO initiatives. The SNC anticipates developing a localisation framework on behalf of the Somalia Humanitarian Country Team under the leadership of the Humanitarian Coordinator. The localisation framework aims to advance the localisation agenda within the Somalia humanitarian context focused on four focal areas namely funding, partnership, capacity strengthening and local voices. The SNC will continue to provide a platform for local and international organisations to discuss and debate on the localisation agenda, document and share learning on principled partnerships. The SNC anticipates strengthening coordination of protection services by creating awareness through coordination meetings and workshops to ensure that evidence-based advocacy to address protection concerns. Additionally, SNC further anticipates advocating for persons with disabilities through conducting a workshop to understand their plight and develop recommendations or initiatives that are ideal in influencing legislation and empowerment of this community.

Mitigation Measures

The SNC continues to play an interlocutory role between NGOs, donors, government and other key stakeholders. The SNC anticipates strengthening coordination of protection services by creating awareness through coordination meetings and workshops to ensure that evidence-based advocacy can be better utilised to address protection concerns. The SNC further anticipates strengthening rights for persons with disabilities through a facilitating workshop on people with disability to better understand their plight and develop recommendations or initiatives that are ideal in influencing legislation and empowerment of this community in order to change attitudes and create a more inclusive environment. The SNC will be keen on ensuring sensitivity of these discussions are considered and adhered to.
