Project Summary

AHF will continue maintaining the provision of paediatric services through Sham children’s hospital in Sarmada which considered as one of the specialized referral hospitals at north east of Idleb governorate. AHF- Sham Children specialized hospital provides 24/7 main of paediatric healthcare services include: - outpatients department (OPD), which run by 3 paediatricians and 3 nurses cover OPD services on daily basis at morning and afternoon shift, providing assessment and management of outpatient paediatrics patients including inpatient follow up and nutrition screening CU5 and PLW for malnutrition and providing treatment micronutrient supplementation( iron, folic acid, multi- vitamin and Vitamin A), routine laboratory screening and EPI routine vaccine clinic - Paediatric inpatient department; run 24/7 by 3 paediatricians and 9 nurses, the ward has capacity of 60 beds for children required inpatients medical treatment, close monitoring and follow-up by specialised health professional. In addition, there is isolation ward devoted for highly contagious communicable diseases - Paediatric intensive care units; consist of two incubators, 10 ICU beds and 5 Medical ventilators the units run 24/7 by trained 6 anaesthetic technicians working under supervision of paediatricians and guidance of structured emergency treatment protocols - Neonatology department; comprising of 15 incubators, two intensive treatment devices (submarine) and two normal treatment devices the department is equipped with both the manpower and the infrastructure required to care for the new born. - Paediatric hospital equipped with emergency treatment protocols, basic equipment, laboratory services and essential medicines and consumable medical supplies - AHF will support Saramda hospital with medicines, consumable medical supplies, and logistic and running costs of the facility, including cleaning materials and stationary, waste management, maintenance, fuel/electricity, and other supplies that are essential for the facility to continue to function and provides standard quality health services.
Before the project implementation, M&E team will make a regular collecting the required data that help in making enlightened decisions. 4 - Needs

according to HNO 2019, 2.2 million children between 0.4 and 2.9 million children between 5-17 are in need all over syria. Dana sub-district in Idleb is one of the 4 most crowded sub-districts in Syria with an approximately 361,545 persons in need for healthcare assistance, with dozens of camps on the Syrian-Turkish border like Atmeh camp which includes about 73,000 IDps (21% of total IDPs in Dana) and many other camps that were set up for IDPs near Dana close to the border, and the sub-district have a number of 700,000 IDPs constituting about 85% of its population after the displacement waves in Nov., Dec. 2019 plus Jan. 2020 as a result of the recent escalation. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: average, effect: high mitigation: Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for patients –Allocating the guard to regulate the flow of patients –Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism and activating the role of the M&E team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with these problems. -Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts 2- Neglecting the most vulnerable (children, women, the elderly and the disabled): possibility: average, effect: high mitigating measures: Employing female staff to ensure that services are being provided women–Ensuring that the wheelchairs are available for elderly and disabled people - Activating the role of the monitoring and evaluation team to evaluate the satisfaction of patients and to and to ensure that the project management team is dealing with the project problems. 3- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: average, effect: high mitigating measures: A comprehensive services mapping of the area and selecting accessible and safe locations of HFs. Monitoring the risks: Post-monitoring/ feedback forms are used. Resources Required: Monitoring staff. Acceptable Level of Risks: Yes.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AHF will coordinate closely with the health cluster and the TWG, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention. the Equipment are available the place is available, the staff are available the required supplies and medication available the electricity is available by functioning electrical generators Risk 1: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas) Risk 2: Conflict related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors and shifting alliances. . Mitigation: If any unseen circumstances happened, we will take the following actions: Postponing the services till safety is back or relocation if the project's activities can't be provided in the same locations.
