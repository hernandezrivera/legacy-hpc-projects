Project Summary

This project will target a total of 13,400 persons (2,680 men, 3,752 women, 3,350 boys and 3,618 girls), which is further broken down into 6,700 IDPs, 4,020 host communities and 2,680 returnees, with a main focus on protection analysis and advocacy and reducing the sufferings of gender-based violence (GBV) survivors. For the purposes of ensuring an in-depth understanding of protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities, Nile Hope (NH) will focus on protection data collection and analysis. This is to say that, NH will ensure that protection concerns are captured and highlighted in toolkits such as assessment reports, monitoring reports and protection snapshots, therefore, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the national cluster, humanitarian partners and other relevant stakeholders.
In order to ensure that appropriate and principled actions are taken to prevent and respond to protection risks or concerns, NH will ensure that this project is centered on both individual and community level by ensuring evidence-based interventions. During the implementation of this project, NH will promote partnership with the affected communities through already established and function community structures including vulnerable groups and individuals, with the aim of identifying protection risks and coping strategies, community and individual intentions and resilience amongst the affected population. Notably, these activities will enhance communities’ and at risk individuals’ preparedness and resilience. Moreover, NH will work closely with communities’ protection watch groups/committees, facilitate focus group discussions, awareness raising and training on basic human rights and protection mainstreaming, with an aim of preventing and mitigating identified protection risks.
Needless to say, NH will provide timely, coordinated, and complimentary protection responses based on the identified protection needs. Most important to realize, is that protection will be central to all interventions which will include service mapping, safe referrals, individual protection assistance for at-risk individuals, information sharing, awareness raising on fundamental human rights and protection mainstreaming. To ensure that this objective is achieved, the project will integrate with other sectors including S/NFIs, FSL, WASH, Education, Nutrition, Health and Peace Building Programs. Furthermore, the project will primarily focus on ensure that the safety and dignity persons and communities affected by displacement are prioritized and a do-no-harm approach is mainstreamed during all aspects of the implementation. Out of the 6,600 persons that this intervention seeks, 1000 persons will be people with disabilities.
Accountability to the targeted beneficiaries will be adhered to through appropriate information dissemination alongside beneficiary feedback sought during post-intervention monitoring. In addition to this, ad-hoc assessments in the proposed locations will be conducted, with priority being given to the highly affected regions and persons with special needs. Special attention will be given to people with disability to make sure that they are not left out, we shall ensure we mainstream protection principles throughout the implementation of this project to ensure that we are not exposing the beneficiaries to harm with our interventions. We have ongoing partnership with UNFPA in Akobo and Wau Counties, however, the protection risks remain enormous in these locations and the proposed ones as well.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict and other emergencies such as famine and floods have continued to weaken the socio-economic foundation of South Sudan for years. These continued man-made and natural disasters have affected the populations to a point of no return, with women and children mostly affected, and the Government of South Sudan unable to provide basic protection services. Consequently, since the beginning of December 2013, grave human rights violations have been committed including but not limited to rape and other forms of sexual violence against women and children, abduction of children and denial of humanitarian assistance. As per UNOCHA Report, the population in need for GBV interventions had increased by 25% as of 2022 compared to 2021. This increment is arguably because of alarming reports of rape incidents from different parts of the country, especially following the flood and conflict affected hot stop areas. GBV, abuse and exploitation remain the greatest protection risks to women and girls, due to a deeply partriachial society which sustains gender inequalities and discrimination. Unfortunately, females aged 15-25 have experienced some forms of GBV, and shockingly cases are underreported by survivors due fear of stigma, re-victimization and reliance on traditional justice structures. Despite the emergence of gender equality legislation and increased women engagement in public and private spaces, men continue to lead power, and decision-making processes due to deeply entrenched partriachial norms and rigid gender rules. Notably, the widespread acceptance of violence against women by both women and men, is indicative of its socialized tolerance. Intimate partner violence regularly comprises at least half of all the GBV incidents. The most pervasive risks that women and girls face, regardless of displacement status and contributing factors, is the prevalence of violence, rape culture, and perceptions of inferiority of women and girls. A staggering 82% of respondents said that they would report an incident of GBV, however, only 15% could specifically identify to whom or where they would report. Only 20% of at-risk women and girls have access to services related to GBV prevention and response. The provision of medical care, MHPSS services, safety, legal aid, and strengthening of referral pathways remain critical interventions for women and girls. Furthermore, there is a need to continuously monitor and analyse the situation and needs of the affected population, to inform responses and adhere to the ‘do-no-harm’ and protection principles. By the same token, there is also a high need to continuously advocate for improved access to services, particularly for PSNs, the most vulnerable and displaced persons, focusing on remote locations. Community Protection Committees, will be encouraged to include men and women with disabilities to determine accessibility of protection services. Similarly, volunteers will be tasked with the duty of championing for needs and safeguards of households, in which the caretaker is a person with disability. This project will put an emphasis on working closely with affected communities/groups to identify needs and empower them to become more resilient, based on their protection risks.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NH staff will be encouraged to ask the vulnerable members of thecommunity to feel free and discuss the issues affecting them with the people they trust the most. The vulnerable members will be urged to seek assistance to write and drop their complaints anonymously in the suggestion boxes provided. This project will ensure that the vulnerable members play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the mostmarginalized and affected are represented and have influence.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NH will establish community feedback and complaints response mechanisms (CRM) such as pre-activity meetings to allow communities to voice their feedback to NH regarding the implementation of the project. . Also community leaders and volunteers will be trained on collecting and providing feedback on community complaints in timely manner. The community feedback accountability mechanisms will be managed by NH’s MEAL Officer. On a monthly basis, an accountability report will be generated for all complaints received and will seek solutions to those complaints from the relevant staff. The project will involve community members in gathering and analyzing monitoring data, documenting best practice and success stories. Monthly, quarterly and annual review meetings and reports will be prepared as a result of this community partnership and shared with the relevant stakeholders. NH will inform participants about planned assessments and involve selected participant representatives to plan for the assessment, participate in the assessments as both respondents and facilitators, and participate in review of findings, defining recommendations, including agreeing on what success will look like at the end of the intervention.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Nile Hope staff will be required to sign a code of conduct that will guide them in the project implementation and. This code of conduct will also stipulate on the guidelines and measures of how to deter and handle sexual exploitation cases and abuse. The staff will also be trained on PSEA principles and through the staff and community workers; the beneficiaries will be sensitized on their rights and the existence of the policy to protect them from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with any breaches of guidelines and stakeholders’ dissatisfaction. In situation where the feedback does not result in significant change of the project design, internal deliberations including with the respective donor(s) will take place and agreed actions implemented. Feedback that requires support beyond the project scope will be referred to appropriate entities for action.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

NH will ensure that Protection risk assessments are conducted and protection mainstreaming is included throughout the program cycle, such as aligning to the protection sector recommended response packages for a harmonized response, ensuring the understanding and monitoring of the contextual environment is well known, up to date and integrated into the distribution processes, and applying needs based targeting / selection criteria. On mitigation aspects, NH will conduct ad-hoc need assessments in the aforementioned counties to examine the protection risks facing the communities. NH considers the following factors that might bar access; • Inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions in the communities • Shift of power dynamics within the households • Increasing exposure toexploitation by employers, clients, suppliers • Inciting backlash from family or community members when women start earning money • Increasing vulnerabilities of theft and violence due to greater assets and wealth • Reinforcing inequalities by continuing to limit choices to more vulnerable groups • Causing false expectations and dependence on short term surge of cash or assets • Increasing costs and drawing on resources, such as providing small livestock without fodder or veterinary care • Exacerbating tensions between host community and affected community Nile Hope’sintegrated approach programming begins with an analysis of target beneficiaries to understand the barriers. Our analysis take into account protection issues and concerns to include internal and external shocks that are often the immediate barriers, meaning that NH program is built on a model that is focused on sustainability by designing interventions that address the multi-dimensional barriers to achieving gender equality.
