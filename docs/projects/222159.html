Project Summary

The intervention intends to support 6,600 children, out of the targeted (4000 girls, 2000 boys), 400 female and 200 male includes people with disabilities, widows and others with special protection needs affected by floods and returnees, people living in remote, insecure and hard to reach, IDPs, Returnees, host community, pastoralists, vulnerable adolescents, girl mothers, elderly caregivers, female headed households, UASC, medical concerns, UNIDOR’s purpose to address the child protection risks of UASC, school dropout early/forced marriage and child labour, family separation and neglect, prevention of recruitment, distress and other MHPSS issues, support for persons with disability, dangers and injuries, access to justice and access to multi sectoral services, UNIDOR strategies to address the issues includes provision of age- gender responsive comprehensive case management including FTR, alternative family based care, cash assistance (CVA), NFI to address complex needs, PSS-MHPSS for children/caregivers, positive parenting and life skills education, CP capacity building for staff, volunteers, community structures and stakeholders, tailored community awareness and dialogues on harmful cultural practices, and CP monitoring and coordination with actors, mainstreaming diverse and inclusive feedback mechanisms including integrated help desks to address CP risk PSEA/safeguarding. UNIDOR will collaborate with Government, community structures, UNICEF, Protection cluster, CPAOR, CPWG, CMTF, UASC, PSS/MHPSS, GBV and CAAFAG WG. Others includes WASH,Education, PSEA, shelter, Nutrition, FSL for service deliver. Mixed static and mobile programming approaches including face to face and remote modalities will be integrated to allow adopting to changing needs and context especially deep field locations to support beneficiaries. Key inter agency guidelines and UNIDOR manual will be utilized for quality programming which includes revised CPIMS standards, IACM/CP/FTR/ SOPs, IA PSS working group PSS manual, CFS standards among other guidelines. UNIDOR will also adopt gender transformative approach, rights based, child/survivor cantered approaches to programming to address root causes of child violation and GBV to shift community attitudes and negative behaviors that harm children. Gender/disability inclusion training will enhance access for persons with disability. Activities integration will be prioritized in Nutrition, Education, GBV, Food Assistance, WASH to holistically address diverse needs of beneficiaries including elderly.Safety audits, safeguarding risk assessment, community risks mapping and safe planning will strengthen centrality of protection/Protection monitoring to mitigate risks for beneficiaries and strengthen protective environment of children. Sustainability strategies includes capacity strengthening of community structures on CPIE creating pool of change agents and linking to development programs for resilience and working with Partners and stakeholders. AAP and feedback mechanisms will be integrated to address issues affecting boys, girls, men, women and enhance women and girls' access to information through integrated and child friendly desks in CFS, schools, Nutrition sites etc., use of phones, girl's symposiums, community feedback sessions.

1. Needs

Reports from protection monitoring spotlight July-August 2024, indicate that the major underlining of the current needs or vulnerabilities are protection risks which are linked to the November 2023 incident displacements, Sudan crisis of mid April 2023 to date resulted into continuing huge numbers of returnees to south Sudan through different ports and the prolonged flooding, continuing clashes between armed forces and armed groups and the ongoing inter communal violence places children at highest risk of abduction and forced recruitment, depriving them of their innocence and childhood with lingering physical, emotional and social effects in their lives. Report from (Secretary General on the children and armed conflict Jun 2024), many children are separated from their parents and guardians, undertaking arduous, risky journeys to safety often unaccompanied. They are particularly vulnerable to recruitment into armed forces and armed groups, physical violence including sexual, psychological distress from being exposed to traumatic events, and limited access to education and health care and given the shortage of services, including in hard-to-reach areas, the lack of social and psycho social resources, the lack of education and employment opportunities and the risk of re-recruitment. According to the Area-based Protection Risk Assessment conducted in August by the Protection Cluster sub-national coordination mechanisms, Child, Early, or Forced Marriage is considered a very high in five counties (5%), a high risk in 32 counties (41%), and a medium risk in 27 (37%) counties of South Sudan. This reads that a staggering 83% of the country, mostly girls affected, suffers significant exposure to the risk of early or forced marriages. Figures estimate that over 50% of girls are married before the age of 18, often forced to marry much older men. In addition, 28% of them get pregnant before even reaching adulthood. Additionally, south sudan humanitarian Needs overview 2024, Indicate over 253,000 children and caregivers lack/have inadequate community based mental health and psycho social support services, 546,700 Individuals have limited access to CP/GBV prevention, risk mitigation measures, life-saving specialized response services and effective referrals. Furthermore 420,000 South Sudanese returnees lack life-saving protection services. UNIDOR intend to provide life-saving child protection services to 6,600 children, out of the targeted (4000 girls, 2000 boys), 400 female, and 200 male including people with disabilities were adolescents while 15% of beneficiaries are persons with disability and services include comprehensive case management, family tracing and reunification, psycho social and mental health support and community engagement to raise awareness about services guided by Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) minimum standards, caregivers will be supported with immediate life-saving and specialized protection services, UNIDOR will enhance knowledge on accessing services and CPiE standards among community members, support with cash-based interventions, setting up mobile units and capacity-building of frontline service providers. UNIDOR will ensure the effectiveness of interventions, monitoring will occur through the weekly and monthly reports, state coordination meetings, maximum collaboration with stakeholders.
