4 - Needs

Increasingly complex and inter-linked protection needs continue to prevail in Syria, resulting from a variety of situations ranging from direct exposure to hostilities, displacement, conditions in sites/collective shelters and protracted displacement. Girls and boys of all ages continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Continuous displacement, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and the persistent lack of access to services and even the most basic necessities, including in newly accessible areas, are taking a huge toll on children. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern. A rapid needs assessment conducted by DCA 2019 in project locations found that a constant feeling of fear for the well-being of their family members permeates all members of society, causing family members to be very irritable most of the time. This also opens the way for harmful coping mechanism: increase of domestic violence, isolation, and constant frustration as a result of increasing stress levels. The most disturbing behavior reported by mothers in their children included unwarranted fears, nightmares, irritability, crying for no reason, and bed wetting. The assessment also showed a response gap for PSS activities specifically targeting adolescents. The MSNA 2019 found that 42% of surveyed households demonstrated cumulative toll on child and adult mental and physical well-being. The need for community based psychosocial support activities and awareness raising on child protection issues is acute.

5 - Implementation of activities

Community-based child protection for girls and boys, as well as male and female adolescents will be provided in targeted locations through: * Provision of structured and sustained child protection programmes, incl. psychosocial support, to vulnerable boys and girls. Following a needs assessment, mixed-gender teams will design a multiple week curriculum for boys and girls in Community Centres. The activities will promote individual and collective support through art, music, drama, sports, and story-telling, and will employ culturally sensitive action methods, as well as games and activities aiming to develop a feeling of safety, trust, connection, communication, relationship, collaboration, play and imagination. Community Centres provide a safe space for children and can reduce the impact of protracted conflict by reintroducing a routine into lives disrupted by war and school closures. * Provision of awareness raising sessions to male and female adolescents in outreach centers/schools in order to prevent and respond to psychosocial and child protection issues. Following a needs assessment with input and feedback from male and female adolescents, mixed-gender staff will facilitate awareness raising sessions for adolescents including topics such as how to recognize and deal with stress, how to deal with everyday problems caused by living in prolonged conflict, and negative coping strategies and how to correct them.* Provision of awareness raising sessions and parenting programmes to parents and caregivers, who are predominantly women. Project staff will conduct FGDs and design a curriculum for training on topics incl. Child Protection, good parenting techniques, skills how to deal with children who are displaying negative coping mechanisms.* Provision of specialized child protection through case management and referrals, in line with sector standards and guidelines.Regular M&E will be conducted by team leaders and officers, and 4Ws will be reported monthly.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High.Mitigation: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. Protection Risk: Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation: Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Protection Risk: Protection risk faced by girls and boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High.Mitigation: Ensure consultation with communities and neighbouring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place.Should the security and/or access situation deteriorate, the project will prioritize safety of staff and beneficiaries and will shift activities to other locations, as and when possible.
