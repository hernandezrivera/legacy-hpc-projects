Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to provide support in line with the spectrum of the Somalia food security cluster strategic objectives of:
1. Improved Access to Food and Safety Nets (IASN): SC will provide unconditional unrestricted cash assistance to improve access to immediate food needs for the most vulnerable and marginalized households (HH) with emphasis on Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), HHs with under 2-year-old (U2) and under 5-year-old (U5) children and households with people with disabilities. Cash transfer will be delivered through mobile money platform to minimize protection risks.
2. Protecting and restoring livelihoods related to food security and income sources. SC will provide livelihood inputs to the two main sources of livelihoods in Somalia i.e. crop and livestock agriculture. In the crop agriculture sector; seed inputs, tools, agriculture trainings, tillage hours, rehabilitating main agriculture related infrastructure e.g. canals will be done while in livestock agriculture, livestock treatment, provision of water and restocking where and when is possible will be done.
In 2022, SC plans to reach 232,119 people (cash -146,919, agriculture – 27,600 and livestock – 57,600 of whom 50% are children.

1. - Projects activities

1. Unconditional and unrestricted cash transfers targeting HHs 24,487 i.e. 146,919 vulnerable and marginalized people. 1.1 Community mobilization and beneficiary identification. SC will carry out awareness creation to target communities about the project, their allocation and responsibilities in collaboration with the Village Relief Committees (VRC). SC’s new cash transfer Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) sets milestones for women representation in VRCs (at 40%). A targeting criterion to ensure inclusive targeting of most vulnerable and marginalized groups will be agreed upon including targeting PLWs and U2 and U5 children as primary focus to ensure better nutrition outcomes of children and mothers and complement SC’s Health and Nutrition sectoral activities. SC will engage a third party to monitor and evaluate the process by conducting interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to verify fairness of the selection process. Baseline assessment and protection measures (safety and dignity) will be undertaken.1.2 Monthly cash transfers. Based on Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for communities in IPC 3,4 and worse, the value will be 100% whilst communities in IPC 3 will be 80%. Payment will be made on a monthly basis through mobile phone as it reduces exposure to protection risks. 2/3 of beneficiaries will be women.1.3 Post distribution monitoring (PDM). Proactive calls will be done after every transfer to solicit beneficiary feedback. Beneficiaries will have access to the project complaint and response toll free telephone number throughout the project period. For transfers more than 3 rounds, SC will also conduct PDM after 2 weeks of the transfers to assess usage of cash, purchasing power and spot check operational efficiency of the transfer. SC will commission an independent third-party monitoring. 1.4 End line evaluation. Done by SC Monitoring, Evaluation Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team will be undertaken to evaluate the extent the project has achieved its intended outcomes, impact on beneficiaries, lessons learnt and good practices. 2. Provision of agriculture and livestock seasonal inputs. The project will support poor pastoral and agro pastoral communities to protect and recover their livelihoods and increase income through providing livestock and agriculture inputs. 2.1. Beneficiary selection: Community based targeting will be pegged on cash supported HHs, those in IPC 3and 4, high Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates and villages not targeted by other organizations.Beneficiaries will be registered using KOBO, a mobile phone data collection platform.2.2. Livestock treatment. This will be done though a voucher system. Ministry of Livestock veterinary teams will be deployed to each selected sites and beneficiaries will use the vouchers to access treatment services. The treatment will be conducted during in Xagaa season (July to September). 2.3. Post-Distribution Monitoring. SC will conduct post-treatment monitoring at least one month after the treatment which will assess the treatment process, performance indicators such as satisfaction with beneficiary selection process, treatment modalities, treatment amounts, fraud etc. 3. Provision of agriculture inputs. 3.1. Beneficiary Selection: Vulnerability criteria will guide in selecting beneficiaries prioritizing HHs with low purchasing power or those who have no access to humanitarian support and are likely to cultivate less land or fail to plant at all due to lack of production inputs. Categories that will be prioritized include female-headed HHs, HHs headed by elderly people, poor returnees, and HHs headed by children or disabled people. 3.2. Provision of seeds and tools: The crop production kit will be aligned to the recommended packages outlined in the guidance note on the provision of agricultural inputs in Somalia provided by the Food Security Cluster . SC will also align the kit as per the Household Economic Analysis (HEA) minimum package

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash programming is an appropriate modality within the current context, as markets are functioning with adequate availability of food and non-food supplies. Cash is cheaper, more efficient and safer for beneficiaries to receive than in-kind commodity transfers, and gives HHs the complete choice and freedom to use the cash to buy what they need when they want. Cash is the most appropriate modality to address reduced purchasing power through improved food access, while also increasing demand in local markets, with the intended result of stimulating markets and increasing trading opportunities. It likewise reduces a number of protection risks associated with cash transfers including limiting unnecessary movement. Cash programming will enable HHs to purchase preferred staples (rice, sorghum and wheat) from a wide number of shops and markets while also strengthening local crisis-affected shops and markets, with a potential broader impact on markets than voucher programming (which typically works with only a few commodities and or vendors). The bulk of the transfers will be delivered through unconditional means; this is because it targets populations who are in IPC 3 and worse who are already experiencing consumption gaps. Unconditional cash will benefit poor and marginalized HHs, including PLW-headed HHs, child-headed HHs, and, elderly-headed HHs. To ensure market inflation is taken care of, SC will keep monitoring market prices in both the main markets and local vendors through the use of existing monitoring mechanisms including FSNAU, REACH, WFP and through market observations. In case of any possible inflation, SC will carry out market assessments to confirm how key food commodities are performing. If there is substantial increase in prices, SC will try to amend the project in consultation with donor. SC also includes a buffer into the project so that transfers are increased in response to price increases.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Cash for Work (CfW) will be implemented with some of the beneficiary HHs who will be working for conditional cash. Cash will be delivered through mobile money transfer which is a very feasible transfer mechanism for many reasons, including wide community acceptance and use; an efficient and timely set up; and safe and secure means for HHs to receive and manage cash transfers. Across Somalia in general, and in the proposed program areas specifically, mobile money is a mechanism widely accepted for payments, with a broad array of vendors operating as mobile money agents. According to findings from SC post-distribution monitoring, shops and cash-out agents are generally available within a 30-60-minute walk for most HHs, with the exception of pastoralists.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

• Awareness raising and sensitization: SC will conduct public sensitization of stakeholders including community leaders, beneficiaries and local authorities on the objective, processes, entitlements, duration, targeting criteria, targeting approach and targets of the project will be shared. • Project relief committees: After sensitization, communities will be asked to establish project committees in public and endorse the committees in public. The committee will represent all different groups and age brackets (excluding children but including youth), and any other groups including women and minority. SC will sign an MoU with the committee which will detail the role of the committee and beneficiary selection criteria. • Setting selection criteria: SC will, in consultation with the local authorities where possible, and by endorsement of the community, will establish a selection criterion which includes but not limited to: very poor HHs with limited income, few assets, and no external support, IDPs and newly displaced or evicted HHs, high number of malnourished children under 5, child headed HHs, female headed HHs, elderly-headed HHs, disabled HH members, PLW among others. This will help ensure minimize age-based inclusion and exclusion. • House to house and public verification: Community committees will do the selection. Once the list is submitted by the committee, SC, committee and local leaders will take a sample (10% -12%) from the list and verify them physically, after which a public verification will be conducted. • Setting a community response mechanism: The action will establish community response and feedback mechanism. A hotline will be provided where people can report incidences or give feedback. During verification and mobilization, SC MEAL team will provide a help desk.• SC has strong safeguarding policies including sexual harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse policies. SC has mainstreamed these policies with implementation of gender equality policies into the programs, this includes making 40% of VRCs women.• SC will not be asking beneficiaries to travel to far places in order to be registered or receive assistance. In addition, SC will support communities and aid assistance to communities where they are living or located. Cash will be delivered via mobile phones where beneficiaries will not need to travel to far places to receive assistance. • To minimize the risk of making humanitarian assistance as a pull factor for people to access, SC will employ geo targeting where support will be channeled to places where the need is greatest e.g. areas of IPC 3 or worse and to IDPs particularly. SC is aiming to cover as many beneficiaries as possible so that those left will not have to migrate. In addition, one of the criteria of admitting beneficiaries to the program will be that the person receiving assistance is from the target community.
