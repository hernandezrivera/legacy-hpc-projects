Project Summary

In accordance with the revised HRP due to the escalating violence across the country since 15 April 2023. Fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) has caused tremendous human suffering and exacerbated significant pre-existing humanitarian needs amongst the civilian population in Sudan, also affecting urban areas previously not or only marginally covered by the HRP. The revised HRP updates the response strategy in the initial 2023 HRP, specifically regarding the scale-up of life-saving multisectoral assistance and protection services in line with HRP Strategic Objectives (SO) 1 and 2, including related to implementation approaches and costs.
Taking into account that key infrastructure, such as power plants, the electricity grid, water stations, banking, health facilities and communications, have been severely damaged, making basic services unavailable with stocks and supplies rapidly reducing.
The project will address energy shortage and provide solutions to community facilities and critical infrastructure by distributing solar powered generators, a hybrid solution that has a capacity to supply power from the office level to a mini hospital or Emergency Rooms, also assisting at the concentration or assembly sites (IDP camps, community centers). The community based solar powered solutions are extremely important enablers for access to energy, access to information, access to online banking and finance, and overall access to services.
Furthermore, to account for immediate needs and to enable access to power at household level the project will focus on providing lifesaving assistance (solar powered solutions) to crisis affected populations. the household level assistance provides 3 in 1 solution (lights, solar FM radios and power stations), providing access to energy, information, preventing GBV, reducing crime rate and contributes to social cohesion.
Furthermore, the proposed action will also contribute to:

- Enabling crisis affected populations to have access to solar energy enabling wider communication, enhancing safety and protection through clean energy kits.
- Reducing the overall cases of GBV and crime by ensuring access to places with light in vulnerable host communities and additionally refugee and IDP camps.
Immediate emergency response will continue to be critical in light of the deteriorating security and economic situation and recurrent events that drive displacement and will require country-wide prepositioning of contingency stocks of non-food items and emergency solar kits.
UNDP is ensuring that IDP's, hosting communities and vulnerable population have access to the solar powered solutions which will ensure livelihood and protection in an emergency setting.
The focus remains on displaced populations (lighting for vulnerable/displaced populations at night provides protection of civilians in terms of minimizing the risks of robbery, etc.), solar mobile chargers to secure communication channels and generate nominal income even during emergency situations, and other relevant solar items.

Implementing Partners

UNDP works closely with a wide range of national civil society organizations, including women's groups, youth organizations, activists, and peacebuilding actors. UNDP supports these partners in various ways, such as providing technical assistance, capacity building, funding, advocacy, and networking opportunities.
UNDP has been able to reach out to some of these partners, most of who have expressed interest and ability to deliver services for the proposed intervention. The communication channels are restored, and partners are alerted about possibilities to be engaged in the project implementation, and also relevant communication about needs assessments is performed on daily basis.
Additionally, UNDP has previously established grassroots level community structures (community management committees – CMCs) in over 70 communities across 10 states in the country. UNDP has invested in building the capacities and skills of these CMCs through a series of training in various domains including service delivery, monitoring, follow-ups, outreach activities, gender equity, human rights, mediation and conflict management, social cohesion, etc.
These community-based structures ensure the effective mobilization of communities to action and will play a key role in the identification of beneficiaries, management and storage of product inputs and the effective distribution to beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The impact of the fighting on the civilian population, particularly on the elderly, women and girls and people with disabilities, is devastating. Key infrastructure, such as power plants, the electricity grid, water stations, banking, health facilities and communications, have been severely damaged, making basic services unavailable with stocks and supplies rapidly reducing. There are reports of increased criminality by both combatants and armed criminal gangs, including forced evictions, looting, carjackings and sexual violence. At the onset of the crisis, Shelter and Non-Food Item (NFI) needs were already acute, exacerbated by significant underfunding of the cluster. Underserved communities, forced to depend on their sparse resources, have amplified existing vulnerabilities and exposure to recurring hazards. The intensity of the new clashes and their indiscriminate nature is now causing widespread devastation to housing, household assets and public infrastructure, prompting many to leave their homes.• Countless people, fleeing for their lives, urgently need Shelter and NFI assistance, as many, including children, women, elderly, and others with specific needs, find themselves crammed into overcrowded public buildings, or forced to sleep outdoors exposed to severe weather and threats. People in need of shelter and NFI assistance are estimated to rise from 3 million to 5.7 million, while the target will see a more modest increase from 1.5 million to 1.9 million due to challenges with access, fuel, market functionality and banking services.• Immediate provision of functional shelter will be critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amidthe upcoming rainy season. Provision of essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities. Items such as bedding and cooking utensils, solar lamps for safety and security afterdark, and solar chargers for maintaining connectivity and having access to information, as well as mosquito nets to combat the upcoming malaria season, are all vitally important. The standard household solar NFI kit combined with assistance in distribution and additional “Cash for Work” approach will act as a stabilizing influence and allow displaced and vulnerable populations to sustain during the crises and resume their family and community life by providing them with safety, protection, financial means, health risks and other factors that could increase their vulnerability. Displaced women and girls are particularly vulnerable without secure environment, basic equipment and resources for survival and maintaining their dignity. Addressing gender specific needs and capacities ensures an adequate standard of living for women and girls. Camps and settlement areas need adequate lighting to improve the quality of life and provide protection. Solar lanterns and solar lightning can reduce GBV and reduce crime while improving security at night. Based on the sectors' detailed analysis of key indicators, prioritized vulnerable localities will have acute needs that are either extreme or catastrophic.

2 - Activities/Outputs

the project is focused to provide assistance at two levels: 1. Households solar powered solutions, and 2. community level solar powered generators and hybrid power bank systems. 1. Provision of standardized household NFI kit/solar items to access affordable, clean and sustainable energy focusing on 3 in 1 solution by providing solar powered device that consist of highly efficient Lantern, Powerbank and FM radio. Each of the functions is enabler for a life-saving activities and contributes in a different aspect: - Lanterns are providing sense of security and are proven important element of prevention of GBV incidents, also are used to maintain family tides by enabling lights at the gathering points and family houses.- Powerbank is extremely important in the situation in which the electricity supply is widely interrupted and unavailable, solar powered powerbank can provide enough power to charge laptops, cell phones and similar electronic devices enabling access to energy and online services (banking and similar).- FM radio is a conflict situation is widely used as an early warning system and provides important aspect to access to information, which is crucial in the times of uncertainty and conflict. The project will utilize UNDP's existing presence and local partnerships with CSOs and CMCs to identify the most needed households and vulnerable groups in accessible locations and communities that will benefit from 3in1 solar powered solutions, enabling access to energy, information, security and prevention of GBV and incidents related to the conflicts. The projects activities are envisaged to ensure that the gender component is put into the consideration with an aim to empower and protect women by distributing at least 70% of the NFI kits to vulnerable women. - Solar Lanterns with FM/AM radios and mobile chargers target population 30.000 x unit price 100 USD total 3,000,000 USD 2. Community level Solar Powered Generators and a Hybrid Powerbank systems. Distributing solar powered generators and Hybrid Powerbank systems to communities affected by conflict facing power shortage is a critical way of providing energy and enabling access to water, health, education, communication services, as well as regenerate critical livelihoods. Hosting communities are at the extreme pressure to provided needed services to the thousands of IDPs and refugees and as such must be supported with solutions to provide power to Emergency Rooms, cooling facilities for medicaments, to power water pumps and provide power for all other services and needed community assets. The project has identified several solutions: - Office Pro Solar Box: providing enough energy to power small office or gathering area - 3kW or 5kW All in one Hybrid System providing enough energy to power small hospital, Emergency rooms, schools or other service provider places. target communities 50 (45,000 people) Office pro Box 625 units x unit price 800 USD total 500,000 USD All in one Hybrid System 100 units x unit price 10,000 USD total 1,000,000 USD 3. Provision of livelihoods through the distribution and installation of equipment using the “Cash for Work” modality: Where applicable, cash for work arrangement will be pursued mainly for vulnerable youth and women with provision of working equipment and tools for distribution and installation of NFI's.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project is focused to provide an energy clean solution by introducing solar powered solutions to community and household level. Over the last several years of provisions of solar powered solutions such as: PV powered offices and hospitals, solar powered water pumps for irrigation, 3 in 1 solutions for household level which include Lantern, FM Radio and Power-banks UNDP has combined various research and assessments to 'profile adequate assistance to the Sudan environment and greatly contributed to climate change mitigation and adaptation. Furthermore, UNDP’s various assessments and studies revealed that unsustainably harvesting and ravaging local forests and other natural resources were among the coping mechanisms of the displaced and vulnerable members of communities. Actions such as firewood collection, use of burnt bricks for housing, collection of wild fruits, uncontrolled clearing and burning of farm sites, to name but a few were among the environmental hazards practiced in the proposed localities. The proposed interventions will incorporate environmental safeguards in all aspects of the project activities to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection through complimentary UNDP projects that are delivering training on Natural Resource Management (NRM) and community environmental action plan (CEAP) development and implementation.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Solar powered solutions are a useful and sustainable way of providing light, safety and education to vulnerable populations living in camps, concentration places, or given the current conflict affected areas those living in urban or remote areas. The project will: - Conduct a protection analysis of the context and the needs of the affected population, taking into account their perspectives, resources, risks and capacities. Identify the most vulnerable groups, such as women, children, elderly, disabled or minorities, and their specific protection concerns. Consult with them and involve them in the design and implementation of the distribution.- Coordinate with other humanitarian actors, such as UNHCR or IOM to avoid duplication, overlap or gaps in the distribution. Agree on common criteria and procedures for the selection of beneficiaries, to facilitate data management and information sharing.- Plan the distribution in a way that minimizes risks and maximizes benefits for the recipients. Choose a suitable location, time and modality for the distribution that ensures accessibility, security and dignity for all. Provide clear and timely information to the beneficiaries about the purpose, process and expectations of the distribution. Ensure that they understand how to use and maintain the solar powered solutions and what to do in case of problems or complaints. Provide feedback mechanisms and follow-up support as needed.- Monitor and evaluate the impact of the distribution on the protection situation of the beneficiaries and the wider community. Collect feedback from the recipients and other stakeholders on their satisfaction, challenges and suggestions for improvement. Track The principle of equity and or “leaving no one behind”, targeting the most vulnerable and most in need with all its interventions and protection. The project will additionally seek cooperation with NGOs, civil society and community-based organizations on any protection issues that will be countered during project implementation.
