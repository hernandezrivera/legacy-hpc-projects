Objectives

"SO 1: Ensure effective, sufficient and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girls. SO 2: Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviors to mitigate public health risks for the well-being of the affected population. SO 3: Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes the protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with the focus of sustainable resilience-building approaches.

Needs

"Access to Safe Water: The Sector Standard of 250 people per functional improved water source has been exceed with an average of 47 people recorded across all camps although some camps in Teknaf where groundwater is scarce still face access difficulties. . The challenge remains water quality, with up to 30% of hand pump water points showing fecal coliform contamination, this figure rises as high as 80% in household stored water. There is a need to improve water quality at source and improve household water storage and safe use. The WASH Sector has developed a Water master plan which aims to replace hand pumps with piped, solar powered water supplies which will allow for mass chlorination and reduce the need for maintenance of multiple hand pumps. UNICEF is moving forward with implementing the Water master Plan in the 8 camps under its responsibility. Access to Sanitation: The WASH Sector Standard of one latrine per 20 people is being slightly exceeded with, on average across the camps, 26 people per functional and safe latrine. With lack of space making construction of new latrines difficult, there is a need to focus on operation, maintenance and cleaning of existing latrines. The emptying and safe treatment and disposal of fecal sludge (FSM) remains a challenge with many FSM sites not functioning effectively or at full capacity. There is a need to improve the transport and treatment of fecal sludge to ensure continued, safe operation of latrine facilities. The Sector is developing a Sanitation Master Plan which aims to explore sustainable options for FSM within the camp areas and UNICEF is working with the Sector to develop sanitation solutions within the 8 camps under its area of responsibility. Solid and liquid waste management remains a challenge in camp areas and this is compounded by the informal use of indoor makeshift washing areas within households which may or may not be being used as latrines adding fecal matter to the greywater flowing through the drains. There is a need to find sustainable approaches to ensure safe, environmentally acceptable solutions to solid and liquid waste treatment and disposal. Hygiene Promotion and community engagement: Household assessments conducted throughout the year have shown that whilst some hygiene indicators have improved, (availability of soap in households and satisfaction to access to bathing facilities), others have worsened (community engagement in hygiene promotion). There is a need to understand barriers and motivating factors for behaviours that contribute to public health risks for households and individuals to address and minimize hygiene risk through appropriate community sensitization. Particular attention needs to be paid to:- Capacity building: Significant support is required for WASH sector actors to improve capacity in areas such as community engagement and participation, accountability to affected populations and gender mainstreaming and feedback mechanismMHM: ensuring that menstrual hygiene needs for girls and women are being met requires better understanding of socio-cultural practices and barriers to Menstrual Hygiene Management. Supplies: Provision of appropriate hygiene items which requires a review of preferences to support market based approaches, including cash and vouchers, allowing for greater choice and accessibility to key hygiene items. Gender: There is a need for a gender analysis to understand the roles, responsibilities, needs and access to resources for both men and women with respect to water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and practices."

Activities or outputs

Water in Settlements, Operation and maintenance of piped water supply network, Operation and maintenance – Minor and major repairs of handpumps and tube wells / Water Point Attendant trainings and equipment’s including for bucket, Emergency phase bulk water treatment- batch treatment with coagulation/flocculation and chlorination when surface water is the water source, Excreta management in Settlements, Development of a common and centralized system for fecal sludge management, Solid Waste Management, Shared services in Host Communities, Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviors to mitigate public health risks. Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes the protection, safety and dignity of targeted people, and is used equitably to men, women, boys and girls.
