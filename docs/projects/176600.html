Project Summary

As a CCCM co-lead, UNHCR project for 2021 is focused on the coordination and provision of timely, well-coordinated and integrated humanitarian interventions aimed at improving the well-being of displaced populations, strengthen their resilience and increase their participation in decision-making processes within IDP sites. The main geographic focus will be in Central Equatoria and Unity states. And the provider of ‘last resort’ of CCCM response in the remaining states.
This project will bolster community and local stakeholder engagement in the delivery of services in an equal and needs-based manner, ensuring participation of the IDP community through regular feedback mechanisms and dissemination of information including use of community-based networks in displacements sites, host communities and areas of IDP return.. There will be particular focus to enhance current gender sensitive leadership structures or to create them if not present.
The project encompasses continuous and targeted support to new IDP arrivals to current sites and in areas of return. UNHCR will ensure protection mainstreaming in all of its and their partner’s activities. Guided by UNHCR’s age, gender, and diversity policy, the needs of the most vulnerable and those with specific needs (PSN), will be taken into account during the programme cycle in all CCCM activities. The project will strengthen community-based networks to encourage participation in planning, decision-making, response and monitoring of activities within the site, especially from women. Feedback mechanisms will constantly be evaluated to ensure the participation of vulnerable groups so that their views are accounted, and service delivery can be improved according to the needs identified. UNHCR and its partners will work to diversify communication channels to better relay messages and enhance two way communications. This will serve to facilitate timely and accurate information to respond to the communities’ needs, mitigating identified risks and responding to the identified priorities. The project also includes, multi – sectoral assessments on number of displaced persons affected by natural or man-made disaster and conflict resolution services through CCCM monitoring, coordination meetings at site level, care and maintenance activities conducted by the safety committee through cash for work initiatives and enhancing feedback and complaint mechanisms.
The project will also seek to promote capacity building and development of community management structures, local authorities and national NGOs. With the gradual hand over of CCCM activities by the end of the project, should conditions allow.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is grappling with multiple displacement emergencies and a protracted IDP situation, challenged by the complexities of the implementation of the peace process and ongoing intercommunal violence. The formation of the Transitional Government of National Unity took place in February 2020, and the ceasefire has been holding since 2018 with no major armed conflict between the two main signatories. However, progress on key components of the agreement to ease conflict in the country remains slow. Despite the formation, a marked escalation in subnational violence in 2020 threatened the fragile peace and pushed vulnerable communities deeper into humanitarian need. Power vacuums at the state and local levels persist, hampering conflict-resolution in response to the inter-communal violence, with complex conflict among ethnic groups along increasingly fractious alliances and targeted security campaigns. The onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and its related restrictions on movement, also worsened an already fragile economic situation. Adding further complexity and challenges, severe flooding affected over one million people during 2019 - 2020.On top of which, the lack of access to and lack of services continue to perpetuate protection and humanitarian needs. There are currently an estimated 1.6 million IDPs, over 315,000 refugees, over 350,000 South Sudanese refugee returnees recorded since 2017 and 1 million IDP returnees since 2016. The vulnerabilities are varied and complex across the country – including widespread insecurity, exposure and risk of human rights violations, specific needs emanating from conflict and natural hazards, lawlessness, GBV incidents, looting, destruction of property, coupled with extremely limited livelihood opportunities. During regular protection monitoring and AGD assessments, IDPs throughout the country expressed their feeling of insecurity, inter- and intra-communal fighting is a common occurrence in South Sudan. In 2020, there was an increase in the reported cases of suicide and attempted suicide, mainly from POCs and Collective Sites. Mental Health and Psychosocial support services are limited. Persons with Specific Needs (PSN) face heightened protection risks due to their individual circumstances owning to discrimination, their identity, or other factors that prevent them from fully enjoying their rights or accessing services they need. The majority of IDPs surveyed in POC and collective sites showed interest to return to their area of origin/habitual residencies to rebuild their lives sustainable peace had been achieved in the areas of return. Of all the needs, availability of food, land shelter, security and better education opportunities for children were highlighted as key considerations for IDPs wishing to leave the Collective Sites and the POCs and return to their areas of origin. New security developments may allow for safe areas of return or relocation. The potential for local integration in certain collective sites, such as in Bentiu and Juba could be explored. This would include implementation of quick-impact projects and strategies to transfer services to outside the site's perimeter could boost the interest of IDPs to locally integrate, even if as a transitional solution.The UNMISS Protection of Civilians sites, which had accounted for approximately 12% of the IDP population, has begun the transition process into IDP camps, transferring the management, including camp administration, security and some service provision from the United Nation’s mission to the Government. Each of these sites presented various challenges.

2 - Project activities

This project will target a population of 70,000 individuals within the 15 prioritized counties in Central Equatoria and Unity states. As the provider of ‘last resort’, UNHCR will also remain prepared to support cluster partners should gaps in other locations emerge and provide holistic CCCM support in the remaining states. The majority of the IDP population are female (60%) and the remaining 40% male. The project is implemented in collaboration with the Protection Cluster to ensure equal access to intervention according to the age, gender, and diversity policy of UNHCR and AAP policies. UNHCR and its partners will ensure stronger community networks, leadership structures and community watch-groups trained to support each other in a coordinated manner for improving the overall security and information dissemination around the sites, to establish/develop early-warning systems and prevention of incidents. As a protracted displacement and the redesignation of POC sites to IDP camps remains in full swing, major efforts will be required in 2021 to ensure durable solutions. This includes local integration is fully explored, boosting capacity building for community-based structures and peaceful co-existence initiatives. To better understand the general intentions and plans, UNHCR will seek to continue its successful initiative of profiling IDP sites and implementing the Protection Monitoring Tool (PMT) in regular rounds for comprehensive assessments, analysis of risks and challenges based on geographical data to ensure better understanding of population profile, risks, progress in service delivery and information needs. Coordination to address gaps in basic services and information sharing needs to be improved across all remaining POC sites and IDP sites. During these assessments targeted assistance to be provided for the most vulnerable. The project will assist in the coordination of multi-sectoral services that will help stabilize the living conditions in areas of IDP returns. This will include the implementation of community-based quick impact projects and cash for work schemes with an aim to support livelihoods, and complement humanitarian assistance. The QIPs must also enhance peaceful coexistence between communities. The cash for work schemes, will be scaled up to increase cash-based assistance where feasible, and will be based on the identified needs in the targeted community. For example training, mentoring and support to youth and the establishment of cooperatives for the fabrication of shelter materials such as mud bricks, which could then be sold to humanitarian actors. Some 15 percent of the project will be allocated to cash-based interventions. p In terms of the money we have, how many per cent will go to CBI in these sites, Adequate information sharing and data management will be improved through preferred feedback and complaint mechanism identified by persons of concern.By engaging two partners, UNHCR is seeking to carry out one safety audit per site, across the four locations. The safety audit is an essential activity as it guarantees the safe itself is safe for persons of concern, such as hazards such as – site is safe and habitable for IDP to stay, flood risk area, presence of IED, area prone to natural disasters – awareness, and if it requires relocation of the IDPs, this will need to be done.

3 - Project indicators

Rights-based and the Age, Gender, and Diversity Approach principles are applied throughout the programme cycle to ensure that the voice, needs and feedback of persons of concern, particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities, women and children are taken into account, and that gaps, challenges and solutions are identified. Various community communication and communication modalities will be used to ensure accountability to affected communities in the response. This includes bolstered feedback and complaint desks, hotlines, focus discussion groups, community-based networks, and community coordination meetings.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNHCR has a zero tolerance policy with regards sexual exploitation and abuse and has clear communication and messaging strategy to inform persons of concern that will be absorbed in to the project. UNHCR has systematically integrated the issue of PSEA in its information campaigns, trainings and meetings with PoC’s and applied through the AGD lens. A PSEA focal person is usually appointed and discreet information sharing lines made available.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

UNHCR's holds planned feedback sessions through its age, gender and diversity (AGD) and Focused Group Discussion (FGD) policies which is at the forefront of every intervention to ensure that every facet of its persons of concern fully participate in decisions that affect them and enjoy their rights on an equal footing with others.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

UNHCR generally undertakes a community-based approach in all its projects relating to affected population. Through consultation and participation, communities engage meaningfully and substantively in all programmes that affect them and play a leading role in change. UNHCR recognizes that, without the engagement of persons of concern, our intervention cannot be impactful on the lives of persons of concern. UNHCR incorporates the knowledge and resources of communities to achieve a holistic intervention which ultimately strengthen their capacities. If communities affected by crises are empowered, they are in a stronger position to protect and support their families, promote social cohesion and peaceful coexistence with host communities, respond to the aspirations of young people, and rebuild their lives. Feedback and complaint mechanisms, community-based networks, coordination meetings, and regular meetings with IDP leadership and communities have been rendered crucial. Setting up via protection or complaints desks provides the needed feedback and complaint mechanisms in order to ensure a two-way channel which maintains the integrity of persons of concern likewise providing feedback for appropriate action. There will be hotlines to complaint boxes depending on location.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

An annual participatory assessments exercise is undertaken using the Age, Gender-Diversity Mainstreaming Methodology in all project locations or implementation areas. The PA outcome sets out the agenda for program planning and design. It is a feedback session engaging the communities which involves representatives from all target populations including youth groups, community leaders, women groups and persons with disabilities for feedback on implemented projects and provided UNHCR resource for advocacy and review if any of its project implementation based on the needs of the persons of concern.
