Project Summary

It is estimated that 3,571,191 million people in South Sudan will be in need of humanitarian healthcare services by the end of 2018 and most likely the whole of 2019. The main health related humanitarian needs in the country include; access to basic primary, secondary healthcare and specialised care such as reproductive health, GBV services and others; access to comprehensive obstetrical care surgical care and case referral; and access to essential medicines and medical supplies.
The situation of reproductive health and especially the availability of commodities and supplies remains one of the major challenges of increasing access to services. Antenatal visits and skilled delivery remain a major challenge with only 17% of pregnant women attending four visits and a chronic shortage of Basic emergency obstetrics and neonatal services. The unmet need for family planning remains high at 24%.
Through this project, UNFPA will directly target 180,000 of the estimated 892,798 women of reproductive age, including pregnant women affected by the crisis. Using pre-crisis birth rates, there will be a projected number of 107,136 births, among which 16,068 will require Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EMONC) services of which 5,352 will need a caesarean section, without which death is likely. Lactating women also require special support to ensure safe infant feeding practices and family planning
The main purpose of the project is to provide lifesaving emergency healthcare services to vulnerable populations by increasing and expanding coverage and access to essential quality Sexual and Reproductive Healthcare services. The main interventions to achieve the project purpose include among others:
• Health education to women, men, families and community to increase awareness on benefits of safe sex, family planning, ANC, PNC and delivery in health facility by skilled birth attendants;
• Procuring, prepositioning, and distribution of a whole range of RH kits for delivery of RH services at the community, and health facilities;
• Procuring other RH equipments for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting, and others.
• Training service providers on essential lifesaving service areas like rational use of RH kits, MISP, CMR, PAC and other lifesaving interventions
• Procuring materials for IEC materials.
• Conducting quarterly joint assessments and monitoring of services including support supervision missions to the field to ensure proper warehousing, usage of supplies and quality delivery of SRH services.
• Supporting the 24/7 referral system of complicated obstetric cases
• Establishing a functional CEmONC Centre in Mingkaman
The activities of the project will ensure that pregnant women have access to ante natal, peri natal and post natal care, including for services for complicated pregnancy and delivery, benefit from basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care. Lactating women will be assisted to benefit from health education and promotion information related to exclusive breastfeeding and supplementary feeding. Men and boys will also be targeted in community awareness sessions and as referal agents for maternal and newborn care services.

Needs

Despite the revitalisation of the peace process and the recent signing of the agreement in September 2018, it is anticipated that around 3,571,191 civilians of which 892,798 women of reproductive age, 600,000 adolescent girls and 400,000 boys will continue to be in a dire humanitarian health care need in 2019. Women and girls have been severely affected by the conflict-related sexual violence with an estimated 800 reported cases rape from January to September 2018. As a result of lack of access and limited availability of reproductive health care services across the country, pregnant mothers continued to die while giving life as the maternal mortality ratio still ranks one of the highest in the world (789 deaths per 100.000 live births). The assessment on the Minimum Initial Services package for reproductive health services conducted by UNFPA in 2018 highlighted significant breakdown of functioning health facilities. The already limited and poor health systems have almost collapsed in many parts of the country and communities still rely on humanitarian agencies for basic health services provision as the capacity of the government's health service delivery is very limited or non-existent. FGDs and key informants’ interviews highlighted high demand for specialised care for reproductive health including family planning, PAC, SGBV services and others; access to comprehensive obstetrical surgical care in many parts of the country, limited number of skilled birth attendants and the lack of ambulance services as well as access to essential medicines and medical supplies is still critical in humanitarian protection of civilians sites and in government facilities across the country. The activities of this project will contribute to addressing the immediate needs of pregnant women, who want to access ante natal, peri natal and post natal care, including post-artum family planning and services for complicated pregnancy, PAC and delivery by ensuring that they benefit from basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care. SGBV risks will be mitigated by ensuring that women, girls and boys have access to services through community awareness sessions and protection networks, especially to engage them in HIV prevention and combating gender-based violence; and Youth friendly service, including recreational spaces, for young people (both male and female) to be able to get some positive outlets for their energies and to tap into them as a positive source of role models and peer educators within their communities.

Activities

This project is intended to actualise one of the core strategies of the health cluster of ensuring availability of life saving maternal health services including commodities and supplies in all service delivery points among affected populations. Therefore, the activities below are intended to ensure that RH supplies and commodities are available and accessible in all the locations serving the affected population. These are; 1. Procure, preposition, and distribute a whole range of RH kits for delivery of RH services at the community, and health facilities (from Kit 1 to Kit 12). 2. Procure other RH equipments for EmONC (delivery beds, examination couches, lighting; fridges; and others. 3. Train service providers on rational use of RH kits, MISP, CMR, PAC and other lifesaving interventions 4. Procure materials for IEC materials. 5. Conduct regular joint support supervision missions to the field to ensure proper warehousing, usage of supplies and quality delivery of SRH services. 6. Conduct services and commodity needs assessments regularly from location to location 7. Support the 24/7 referral system of complicated obstetric cases 8. Maintain functionality of CEMONC Centres in Juba POC, and Malakal POC

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Project consultations with communities will be broad and representative, beyond community leaders and usual constituencies. Women, men, boys and girls across all social setups will be engaged in the consultation. So far UNFPA consulted women, men, boys and girls through focus group discussions and key informant interview. The team will also engage community leaders and religious leaders including special groups in the community like women group, Youth group and people living with disability so that the project will be design to meet their specific needs.

The project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. Males and females will have equal and gender sensitive access to sexual and reproductive health services; women and men will be trained as community volunteers without prejudice. Sexual and reproductive health services will be provided with special sensitivity to women. Health facility staff will receive training in GBV and clinical management of rape; health facilities will offer the basic package of GBV services. Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring scope will take into account accessibility of services by beneficiaries in terms of easy to reach the service delivery points, distance covered to reach services delivery point, transport means, cost related to services, and acceptability of service providers by beneficiaries. During project monitoring process views of beneficiaries will be sought to identified obstacles to service access.

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project will ensure the services provided does not pose any harm or dangers to the service beneficiaries and providers.
