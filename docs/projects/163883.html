Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to support the conflict-affected populations of Aleppo, Hama, Dar’a and Rural Damascus Governorates, focusing on sub-districts with a severity range of 4 or 5, by providing a comprehensive protection assistance through an integrated and community-based approach. Based upon previous interventions in Aleppo and Nashabiyeh, were around 13,800 people in need where reached with protection activities, the following proposal intends to strengthen the quality of assistance provided and to extend it to new areas of intervention, with a focus on specialized services that COOPI successfully implemented in the previous and ongoing action. Locations and target have been identified based on the ongoing areas of intervention (Aleppo, Dar’a, Izra’, Nashabiyeh) and upon assessments and consultation with local authorities and stakeholders about new areas considered potentially accessible in the coming months (Atareb) or where protection assistance is needed (Suran, Hamra, As-Salamiye). While the impact of nine consecutive years of crisis is generically critical all over the country, the proposed project will be adapted to the variety of protection needs in the different areas of intervention, targeting 25,420 beneficiaries (IDPs, Host communities and returnees) across the four Governorates. The high levels of psychological distress, child protection and GBV risks, to which the beneficiaries are exposed at different level in the targeted locations, will be addressed through FGDs and assessment for the identification of needs. Beneficiaries will be supported by project activities such as MHPSS support, CP/PSS and SGBV case management and referral, home-based assistance to elderly and disable children, speech and hearing therapy, awareness campaigns and recreational activities. The project will contribute to facilitate the access to protection services especially for beneficiaries leaving in hard-to-reach areas, where social support structures are fragmentated and there are persistent challenges in access and availability to protection assistance. Based on the identification of needs, the activities are intended to be delivered through static facilities already operational, while out-reach activities will be carried on through mobile units in less accessible and rural areas, with a particular focus in supporting community structures (women and child committees) and community-based initiatives to contribute in restoring the social cohesion and resilience and to prevent harmful coping mechanisms (child labour, exploitation, early marriage). Due to the presence of explosive hazards in several areas targeted, risk education campaigns will be conducted inside the Centers, through the mobile units and during livelihood kits distribution. COOPI will ensure synergies with other projects submitted to HRP 2020 by complementing food security and livelihood activities already ongoing to promote an integrated approach within the local communities.

4 - Needs

Aleppo Governorate has been recently marked by intense shelling and airstrikes. Among the 300,000 people displaced in December only, 80% are estimated to be women and children. As per contingency plan recently prepared for north-west Syria, it is expected the arrival of 100,000 people in rural and western rural Aleppo (Atareb), and 30,000 in Hama. As per protection severity scale, the estimated people in need (PiN) of protection assistance are 1,304,376 in Jabel Saman and Atareb Districts, 103,800 in Hamra, Suran and As-Salamiyeh Districts. The prolonged crisis has increased poverty and incremented child protection risks as child labour and early marriage, with access to basic rights and services even more complex for children with disabilities in need of home-based assistance. Especially in north-west Syria, along with economic violence and denial of resources, new GBV trends have been identified in 2019, such as sex-selected abortion, forced pregnancy and forced puberty of girls, with an increase in boys seeking care through GBV specialised services (UNFPA, Voices from Syria 2020 Draft). The protection needs in Hama have been assessed through meeting with SARC and Protection FP, especially regarding the lack of specialized services in hard-to-reach areas. Nashabiyeh and Duma (Rural Damascus) have a protection severity scale of 4, with 59,000 PiN of protection assistance. In the assessment conducted by COOPI, protection risks as child labour, GBV and CP risks, lack of assistance for disable people were detected. Other 162,500 PiN of protection assistance (Severity scale 4), live in Dar’a and Izra districts (Dar’a), facing restriction of movements, high level of destruction, lack of core services and limited humanitarian access, in an area still unstable, with frequent security incidents on sensitive targets. COOPI’s assessment detected the presence of many female-headed HHs, PwD, limited and unreliable PSS support, along with ERW heavily contaminated areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

To respond to the specific needs of vulnerable people, COOPI will provide specialized protection services, such as home-based assistance, speech/hearing therapy, case management and risk education awareness sessions, through static facilities (Aleppo, Nashabiyeh, Dara), and mobile units, in collaboration with community committees and volunteers. Based on the experience in Aleppo and Eastern Ghouta, the intervention through static facilities will be ensured providing integrated services to the community in the surroundings, complemented by mobile unites and volunteers operating in rural areas. In hard-to-reach and new areas of intervention (Hamra, Salamiyeh, Suran, Izra, Atareb), COOPI will focus on community-based intervention to reinforce self-protection through prevention and response activities. Upon need assessment and FGDs, COOPI will adapt the intervention to the specific needs of the area. The mobile units, working in several sites on a rotational basis, will raise community capacity supporting committees, as children’s and elderly club, women committees, CBIs and volunteers, to have an active role in identification of persons at risk, awareness campaigns on protection and risk education, information dissemination on referrals pathway and referral to specialized services, to cover response gaps and create resilience, with a right-based and sustainable approach. COOPI MU will provide specific services as home-based physical rehabilitation for PWD, survivors of mine, ERW, cluster munition or IED accident, CP/MHPSS/GBV case management. To ensure confidentiality and do no harm principles, in particular regarding Child abuse and GBV cases, WGSS or mobile protection points will be used for case management. Risk education will be conducted by trained staff through awareness sessions and dissemination of specific protection messages. To reach more beneficiaries, it will be integrated in Livelihood/Education/ERL activities, as successfully implemented in Nashabiyeh.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Establish complaint and feedback mechanism in each catchment area; b) Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA; c) Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services.Protection Risk 2: Lack of consideration of information about context and social dynamics, hostilities in new accessible areas.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Ensure the new communities are informed about project activities, services provided, beneficiaries targeted and scope of the intervention; b) Recruit the staff with adequate knowledge of the context and respecting; c) Engage with local authorities and community key informant to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered.Protection Risk 3: Content and materials for awareness campaigns on protection (SGBV in particular) and mine risk education are not fully adapted to the social and cultural context and create misunderstanding or conflict among community members.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Develop clear communication line to receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information provided; b) Recruit staff who is familiar to the cultural context; c) Tailor the messages content and refer to sector guidelines and SoPs.Protection Risk 4: Survivors of GBV face consequence and social stigma for having reached out for assistanceLikelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Ensure confidentiality of the information; b) Train the staff involved (from the identification to the referral and follow-up) on clear procedure on information gathering, file management and storage, by ensuring fully protection of personal data; c) Ensure the space dedicated to case management provide comfort and privacy.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Direct access to the target areas and HHs is not permitted or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. Access to new areas is granted and later withdrawn by the authorities.ASSUMPTION: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will maintain constant communication with agencies, government and in-country security monitoring networks including SARC. All staff will receive security on the field trainings in order to gain knowledge how to act if face problems on the field. RISK: Case referral might not be successful. ASSUMPTION: Other humanitarian actors might not have the capacity to assist additional beneficiaries or the referral pathway is not fully designed/implemented.MITIGATION MEASURES: In this case COOPI will advocate with the sector to fill the gap and finding exit strategies. RISK: Lack of information about MA specialized service providers. ASSUMPTION: Lack of collaboration/information about access and conditions for fruition of the services and referral pathway. MITIGATION MEASURE. COOPI will contribute in strengthen the referral pathway, through service mapping including health service providers.
