Project Summary

Al Manar is eager to contribute to the response for the South Sudanese refugees who have been affected by the ongoing fighting between the SAF and the RSF. The overall goal of the project is to provide multi-sectoral life-saving interventions covering H/N and Protection sectors. As the fighting in Sudan has increased the vulnerabilities of refugees and asylum seekers across Sudan, the need is continuously increasing, the nutrition planned intervention is to deliver CMAM services in the targeted 5 States targeting U5 children, through the provision of the full package of treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition among boys & girls U5 as well as providing Healthcare and treatment for South Sudanese Refugees. The project targets acutely malnourished children (SAM with or without complication and MAM) through treatment. The project will enhance the need for analysis of the nutrition situation through conducting regular community screening of cases, involving the communities in tracing SAM/MAM children and referring them to the H/N centers. Mother-to-mother groups were a group of mothers with malnourished children, grandparents group) to help empower Mothers to solve issues related to nutrition with a focus on infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and knowledge and coordination with relevant sectors (for multi-pectoral nutrition activities). Mainstreaming child protection through reflecting in the assessment, design, monitoring and evaluation of the project. The ultimate goal of the project is to save the lives of acutely malnourished children U5 as well as provision of medical services to South Sudanese. Also ,the project will target the same localities covering health components ensuring live saving is to exist for the SS refugees in the targeted 5 states as well as existing refugees in Khartoum state. This will be through providing medical treatment by qualified trained Health staff, and conducting Mobile clinics for the population in the catchment areas. Protection services will be provided through referral pathways to the project health, nutrition and livelihood services and other relevant service providers to provide the cases with relevant needed protection support, support the vulnerable individual with direct material support based on need, also to conduct focus group discussions will be held to raise women awareness about GBV and protection related issues, their rights and provide needed, referral, consultations and protection care. In addition ,child protection services will be provided through referral and case management services for refugees' children with disabilities.

1 - Needs

The situation in Sudan has significantly worsened with the beginning of the fighting on 15 April 2023, between the SAF and the RSF which has killed hundreds of people, forced more than 218,000 to flee across borders and displaced over 730,000 people inside the country in the first four weeks of the conflict.In addition to Sudan's growing humanitarian crisis, this fighting has led to large-scale displacement and shattered the access to food, water, cash, fuel, health care and other basic services for millions of people. Before the current wave of fighting, Sudan hosted 1.14, million refugees from South Sudan, Chad, Central African Republic, Ethiopia, the Syrian Arab Republic, Eritrea, and other nationalities. As of May 4th 2023, nearly 60,000 refugees have moved out of Khartoum, 55,000 of these have moved to refugee camps in White Nile State; and 10,000 refugees have moved to the East through Madani, including Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees moving to camps in Kassala and Gedaref (5,000 to each state). While out-of-camp refugees in Khartoum are unable to pursue secondary movement due to a lack of resources or other vulnerabilities with few options for respite available. The UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) estimates that more than one million refugees in Sudan require humanitarian assistance. The number of people in need (PiN) of humanitarian assistance in Sudan has increased from 15.8 million, estimated in November 2022, to 24.7 million in May 2023, representing a 57 per cent increase e.g. increasing the number of refugees in need by 18 per cent.Furthermore, the need continues to increase as access to humanitarian warehouses is restricted and the loss of humanitarian stocks to criminality has depleted response capacities, specifically in conflict hotspot areas. High insecurity and looting of humanitarian assets and offices have compromised humanitarian access to key locations. Moreover, critical buildings and essential infrastructure in Khartoum, have experienced devastating consequences, including the airport, presidential palace, ministries of defense, energy, education and health, and several hospitals, fewer than 20 per cent of health facilities in Khartoum remain fully functional and 60 per cent are not operating, severely limiting access to urgent medical services for millions of people. These challenges are compounded by soaring prices for food, fuel, and other essential goods, as well as recurring disease outbreaks, pre-existing malnutrition, and acute food insecurity.

Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated through the entire project management cycle. Environmental mainstreaming will be integrated in the activities to ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Key environmental concerns will be built into the project in order to reduce deforestation in Sudan. Environmental mainstreaming will be included during monitoring and evaluation of the project outcomes of the response to ensure that the effects sought was indeed delivered, and to help identify possible negative environmental effects in time in order to adjust activities accordingly. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Almanar through coordination with the local government authorities and humanitarian partners will incorporate protection principles with DO NO HARM and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity in the implementation of this project by avoiding causing harm preventing and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project activities which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Protection mainstreaming is one of the most significant factors, which is increasingly recognized as an integral component of UN programming, is the gender equality and protection of girls and women. Inequalities between the sexes typically reflect, and can increase, vulnerabilities of pregnant and lactating women and girls, may face different protection risks, have different needs and choices, and possess different skills, knowledge and coping strategies. The project activities, all throughout the execution, will aim to ensure community participation through the project cycle, capacity and vulnerability analysis, reduce the level of exploitation to women and promote protection of girls in communities and families with the support of legal and traditional protection systems. The project will use and advocate for Minimum Standards of Protection package amongst the targeted communities all through the project implementation. This project through the referral pathway between the two targeted sectors will ensure that women, girls and boys victims of S/GBV, elderly, disables and their families and guardians are fully aware of the project interventions in 4 targeted States. Almanar will ensure the served locations are safe and accessible to all without discrimination, and ensure that the beneficiaries receiving services in dignity and confidentiality.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In the all stages of the project the communities are kept engaged and involved through community dialogue meetings, project dissemination. Accountability will be ensured across the activities in this project through establishment of complaints and response mechanisms at project level aimed at ensuring that the voices of the affected populations will be heard. The established nutrition and protection community committees and community leaders networks including volunteers will facilitate community engagement at all levels of the project including planning, management, monitoring and evaluation.These committees are expected to be gender and ability sensitive with the inclusion of both males, females, elderly and people with disabilities to ensure that all voices, including those of the marginalized are heard, equality and equity are realized with regards to access to services that will be provided by the project. In addition, AMVO will ensure that all staff/volunteers are trained on PSEA policies and procedures which are in line with UN's protocols. The Complaints handling mechanisms will be established in the targeted area and fully inform the community about the complaint mechanisms, their purposes and use, and what constitutes a complaint to allow people confidentially report their concerns and with developing monthly tracker to ensure that communities complaints/feedback are comprehensively analyzed and responded to with the beneficiaries getting feedback on a monthly basis. Project staff will ensure adequate consultation with the communities to ensure that existing complaints handling mechanisms are incorporated into the complaints and response mechanisms to be established by the project. I.e.(complain box, a complaint committee or direct complaints to project staff members trained to act as “focal points,” receiving complaints sensitively and without judgment, and ensuring the complainant’s safety.
