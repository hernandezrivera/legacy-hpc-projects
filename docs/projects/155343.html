Project Summary

The objective of this project is to strengthen support for rapid response to malaria outbreaks, to strengthen monitoring, evaluation, and surveillance at the community level, to intensify behavioral change communication, and to support partnerships at the local level in the fight against malaria.
Direct beneficiaries: 14,271 people in the Departments of Grand' Anse and Sud classified "red zone" with high epidemic potential, according to the epidemiological stratification (2017). Indirect beneficiaries: 827,999 people.

Needs

This project will support efforts to reduce the morbidity burden of malaria in Haiti through the continued support to the interventions carried out by the National Malaria Control Program (PNCM) and its partners since the beginning of the implementation of the national strategic plan to eliminate malaria (PSNEM 2016 – 2022). Although many efforts have been made in the past years, malaria is still a serious public health problem in Haiti and strongly affects the country's health budget. The epidemiological stratification of malaria in 2017 shows that the departments of Grand' Anse and Sud hold the highest number of cases. The number of cases at national level has decreased slightly from 21,430 cases in 2016 to 19,135 confirmed cases in 2017. The epidemiological situation within departments is characterized by the resurgence of cases, outbreaks, deaths and severe cases. To address this preoccupying situation, the Ministry of Public Health and Population (MSPP) – supported by PAHO/WHO – carried out activities of diagnosis and treatment, investigation and sensitization.In the course of the year 2017, the malaria burden in the department of Grand' Anse decreased slightly compared to the year 2016. However, in the department of Sud 3 337 cases were reported in 2016 compared to 5 919 in 2017. In fact, by appreciating the oscillation of the number of cases in these two departments, the epidemiological situation would appear to be static.In order to respond adequately to the needs of the population in terms of health care provision related to the prevention and treatment of malaria, the Ministry of Public Health and Population (MSPP) of Haiti seeks to maintain an integrated community-based strategy through community health workers (CHWs). However, the MSPP is also adopting the regional DTR-I strategy (Diagnosis, Treatment, Response and Investigation) with the support of PAHO/WHO in order to respond in real time while taking into account the context of each transmission focus. The main objective of the MSPP is to treat all febrile patients and all confirmed cases of malaria. The package also requires investigation and adequate response to cases found during the investigation. Therefore, for the implementation of the strategy, an implementation plan must be drawn up for each transmission center and for each emergency that requires targeted and adapted interventions. The response plan also includes the deployment of mobile units for active case detection during epidemics and the installation of units for the management of severe cases in remote areas without health facilities.

Activities or outputs

• Support the rapid response to epidemics:PAHO / WHO will work closely with MSPP to update the response plan at local level. The Departmental Health Directorate of Grand’ Anse (DSGA) will coordinate the implementation of the plan at the operational level with the support of the PNCM and PAHO/WHO. At the operational level, the community health worker (CHW) will be trained in case diagnosis and treatment delivery to malaria patients. The network of CHWs, health committees and community organizations will participate in outreach activities. Other partners involved in the fight against malaria at the community level, including the Haitian Red Cross and NGO sub-recipients of the Global Fund will also be involved. They have a dense network of volunteers and community workers who will make a significant contribution to the fight against epidemics and towards awareness-raising activities after the epidemic.• Strengthening the evaluation of monitoring and surveillance at community level:Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be trained data collection and active case-finding tools. In the context of elimination, CHWs will be asked not only to diagnose suspected cases with Rapid Diagnosis Tests (RDTs) and to provide treatment if necessary, but also to detect as many cases as possible at the community level. They will be supervised by the Epidemiological Surveillance Officers (OSE) who will capture the data on the DHIS2 electronic platform. This system will allow the reporting of individual cases and the real-time transmission of collected field information, thus facilitating rapid decision-making at the central level.• Intensification of communication for behavioral change:The strategy put in place must be well understood by all stakeholders at the central, departmental and local levels. Healthcare providers and CHWs will be sensitized to the need for early diagnosis and treatment and the need to detect as many cases as possible at the community level in this context of malaria elimination. Communities will also be made aware of the malaria situation in the region, the national elimination strategy and their role in its implementation. They need to be informed about the CHWs' strategy to provide them with timely diagnostic and treatment. The use of personal protective equipment, such as insecticide-treated mosquito nets (LLINs), will be an integral part of the communication message. All means of communication will be used for the diffusion of the messages, namely the local or community radios, the networks of CHWs, the health committees and the communal committees for the elimination of malaria.• Strengthening the DTI-R Strategy:To better intervene at local level, it is necessary to undertake a micro-stratification of the foci of malaria. To do this, we assess the source of suspected cases to identify areas with high transmission or hot zones. For the response to be effective, it will be necessary to carry out micro-planning through an integrated framework.• Strengthening the partnership for the fight against malaria at the local level:As part of the implementation of the 2016 NESP 2022, the PNCM has set up communal committees for the elimination of malaria. These committees, made up of all the actors involved in development issues at the local level, are intended to help mobilize stakeholders to integrate the fight against malaria into their daily activities. They also work with local authorities to mobilize the resources and energies needed to achieve the program's goals. However, their actions are hindered by a lack of resources and training. This project will allow strengthening their capacities through training and technical and financial support to carry out activities in the field and coordinate their interventions at Community level adequately.
