Project Summary

WATAN Shelter/NFI Programme 2019, seeks to contribute in the Shelter Sector by developing and reviewing integration methodologies to be applied through its all sectors in a clear and simple operational strategy, decreasing families inability to repair their damaged/unfinished shelters, also providing daily living assistance such as seasonal clothing, heating fuel, and shelter/NFI kits and for humanitarian purposes.
Reaching the most severe communities the main outputs of this project are:
- Rehabilitation works for 1500 (houses) targeting the most vulnerable host and displaced families.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Collective Centers Rehabilitation works for 620 vulnerable Households IDPs.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Rental support for 2500 vulnerable families both returnees (host community) and IDPs.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Providing NFI and Shelter kits for 4000 vulnerable IDPs.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)
- Distributing 6000 winter clothes for 1000 Households
And 3000 heating fuel/(300 liters/family/3 months/25 day per month/4 liter per day).
for the displaced families, returnees and the vulnerable host community.
(Aleppo Rural and Idleb Governorates)

Needs

In North-West of Syria Aleppo and Idleb Governorates had been marked by several conflicts throughout 2018, also weather bad conditions in the informal settlements, unplanned camps had its effect on the vulnerable community in both Aleppo and Idleb. As the summarized result of 2019 HNO, there has been a slight improvement in the overall NFI situation, but a worsening of the shelter situation. WATAN SNFI 2019 submitting this plan seeking more durable solutions, supporting the main key message: "The shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen, in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions, the ability of people to repair or rehabilitate their own damaged shelter, and in particular the affordability of rent" (2019 HNO). Main objectives of this proposal are increasing Rehabilitation and Maintaining Activities for the existed shelters and infrastructure, and Collective Centers Maintainance for Multi-Porpuses.Also to support daily life needs, such as (winterization activities, NFI kits distribution, and Rent Assistance). Ensuring that this strategy is responsive to the changing humanitarian situation in Syria and aligned with the Sector objectives with its operational in scope, and continuing with 2019 HNO, the following statistics will be the main targeted group in this study: - Aleppo 883,834 PiN - (Severity: 4 - Severe)Idleb 23,813 PiN - (Severity: 5 - Critical)People are in need of shelter support - Aleppo 31,419 PiN - (Severity: 4 - Severe)Idleb 411,262 Pin - (Severity: 5 - Critical)People require NFI assistance WATAN is conducting extensive field surveys as a baseline for the current response, carrying out investigations in different targeted areas.https://watansyria.sharepoint.com/:b:/s/Shelter/ETa2qJU7IshHpJrPJOl_VrAB-fgojbMQ6EHm6TQcOgkewA?e=duC07f The needs survey revealed the following results: 1. There is a lack of adequate income sources to finance the basic humanitarian needs for 90% of the newly displaced people in targeted provinces.2. Dealing with the extreme cold is the greatest concern among 80% of the families interviewed. 3. 90% of the families suffered from cold-related diseases during last winter and they still live in the same place.4. All respondents confirmed that they do not have any solutions to cope with the cold which will severely affect them as they continue to live in tents, shelters or residential buildings that neither are equipped nor insulated.Moreover, given the high population density "especially in Afrin and Rural Aleppo", access to shelter, basic services, and livelihood opportunities is severely constrained, with particularly worsening overcrowding in IDP sites. The population also include a high portion of self-organized returnees who further place a strain on remaining services in communities, Therefore, the proposed intervention will provide people with acceptable heating tools/methods, which will decrease the incidence of disease among the most vulnerable ones (babies, children, people with special needs) and it will be covering and decreasing the overcrowding and the lack of shelter capacity, in accordance with a clear and flexible HLP mitigation as clarified in the .

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1B. The tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees Mitigating Measure 11. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. 3. Make effort to target a % of the vulnerable host community in every intervention that aims to support the IDPs4. Establish a feedback mechanism and conduct follow-up visits if needs be5. Involve the local stakeholders throughout the process Protection Risk/Threat 2F. Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population. Mitigating Measure 21. People are informed about their rights to receive assistance regardless of the documents they have.3. Presence and information about complaints mechanism. Protection Risk/Threat 3J. Closure of the border for commercial shipments or not the renewal of SC would have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities for both NGOs and UN agencies as it would prevent the transshipment of the NFIs across the border from Turkey into Syria. Mitigating Measure 32. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement3. Proposition items through UN shipment inside Syria Protection Risk/Threat 4K. Depletion of the market and inflation of the prices. As a consequence of the closure of the border or other internal security situations, agencies might start to resort to local procurement. If not well conducted, this could have a negative effect on market prices and items availability. Mitigating Measure 41. Conduct a strong and constant market monitoring before starting any local procurement2. Keep the prices of the market monitoring with short and periodic frequency3. Liaise with Cash WG before shifting to local procurement

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key challenges and expected solutions to work in Aleppo an Idleb will be as the following: Assumptions:1- BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open.2- HLP will be taken into consideration before delivering field works or any assistance.3- Flexible in-site implementation/reallocation, when certain areas are inaccessible.4- Good collaboration with the IDPs and Host communities.5- Access will be conducive to deliver the assistance.6- Deliver the maximum number of shelters in conflict affected areas for the most vulnerable households.7- Beneficiaries will receive fuel or rehabilitation vouchers and use it for the same target purpose. Risks:1- Borders closure can affect money transferring or any cross-border and local activities.2- Application of humanitarian accountability principles HAP where the risk of aid diversion caused by armed groups exists although thus far not reported officially.(HAP Reference: https://watansyria.sharepoint.com/:b:/s/Shelter/EcbaMVcSanFLofNMdGGXnnYB-CFnFQemUdoar7gQ8JW1xw?e=7d3KTs)3- Duplication in distribution activities and distribution locations with other organizations.4- Some sites are on land privately owned, and private owners may refuse physical works. (Housing, Land and Property issues).5- Staff mobility and access restrictions to the affected population, with active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines might prevent delivery of materials and workers.6- Lack of funding and lack of provided humanitarian aid through cross border.7.1- Change in fuel and exchange rates.7.2- Beneficiaries may sell the vouchers (through the black market). Risk Mitigation:1- Coordinate with SNFI Cluster, local authorities, and funding partners to avoid any delay or lack of approvals.2- Referencing the SHELTER REPAIR AND REHABILITATION GUIDELINES in any activity design.3- WATAN will report back to SNFI Cluster for any possible aid diversion incidents.4- WATAN will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land and discuss with owners and available site management.5.1- Deconfliction referencing with UN OCHA.5.2- Coordinate with SNFI Cluster to highlight vulnerable sites, also to identify reallocation sites in case of a security situation.6- Exalt the benefits of rehabilitation works reducing fuel costs, fire risks and also uses during summer months to help IDPs maintain a long-term of benefiting shelter units.7.1- Restriction of vouchers and limited Timeframe/Expiration.7.2- Vouchers will be cashed after ID Strict Verification.7.3- Monitoring team will follow-up the mentioned exchange.7.4- Improve implementation: work will be assigned to local workers and involve host households in implementation to deliver the requested work on time.
