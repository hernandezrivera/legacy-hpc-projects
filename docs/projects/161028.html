Project Summary

The recurrent conflict and disasters in South Sudan especially since 2013, have enormously contributed to the humanitarian crisis that has seen families separated and many children going unaccompanied into places strange to them but in search of survival. This project seeks to provide Lifesaving Child Care Services and Psychosocial Assistance for Girls and boys affected by disaster and armed conflict in Rubkhona, former Unity State and Juba former Central Equatoria state; who on a daily basis are at risk or indeed exposed to violence, abuse, child labor, neglect and all sorts of violence especially, physical violence given their vulnerability is providing personal protection. Through our already existing child protection field team, our tested community child protection networks are to aid identification and documentation of unaccompanied and separated children as well as, identify befitting foster care families that are in effect to provide a durable solution to the UASC. All activities are to embrace messaging for peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence, avoidable of Gender-based Violence and awareness of the dangers of UXOs.
The project will provide life-saving child protection prevention and response services through provision of Comprehensive case management services and referrals for children with critical protection concerns; Psychosocial Support (PSS) activities; Child protection awareness raisings or campaigns and Strengthening of community-based child protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to child protection concerns targeting 23,960 most vulnerable individuals ( 17610 children both boys and girls and 6350 adults both male and female). The activity will target 13530 persons who will be targeted in Rubkona and 9830 individuals in Juba PoC 1 & PoC 3. The target group includes unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed forces and armed groups, children victims of or at risk of GBV, domestic violence, physical and sexual abuse, GBV survivors and women and girls at risk of GBV. There is a timely need for protection services in Rubkhona.
GUNO’s current program in Rubkhona identifies of child abuse and exploitation and UASC have been identified and there is still consistent need to proceed with further identification, registration and reunification with their families and this is to be full accomplished through proposed activities such as; Unaccompanied and Separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC) ; Psychosocial Support for Children who are affected by the Conflict; Mine Risk Education; Education for special need for disability Children and Adult; Cash based Individual Protection Assistance; GBV Prevention Awareness and Case Management; UXOs/Mine Risk Awareness community campaigns; Peace Building engagements among affected children and adults and training on Child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including Case management and PSS. GUNO will work closely with other child protection partners to avoid overlapping and duplication of efforts. GUNO will also coordinate with CP partners through referrals of CP cases. This project will ensure close collaboration with the Child Protection and GBV Sub clusters and will be realized in a period of 12 months in Rubkna county and Juba PoC 1 & PoC 3.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, GUNO subscribes to the human rights perspective which recognises that all human beings, including refugees, asylum seekers, migrants, and specifically UASC are equal and have the same right to protection. The child and other human rights are at the center stage of our Child protection project and this is to always be amplified and recognized in all our project engagements, products and services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, The designing of this project followed a number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs of affected population, all age groups and sex were consulted and the activities and resources requested are adapted to the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women men of different ages. The targeted actions address the needs and priorities as well as the discrimination faced by specific groups of women/girls/boys/men.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project has aligned to the Child Act (2008) and the 2011, undertaking between the National Council for Child Welfare and MoGCSW on Protection of Separated and Unaccompanied Children.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes.  The project is aligned to national objectives to keep a register of orphans, child-headed households, disabled and other children in particular need of material and other assistance within each area of jurisdiction and monitor and assist them in order to protect them from abuse and enable them to grow with dignity and develop their potential and self-reliance captured under the Child Act 2008.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

GUNO had a number of consultative meetings with the communities involving different population groups and analyzed the needs and dynamics between women, girls, boys, and men in different age groups. The risks were identified and measures factored in to mitigate the potential risks to ensure that this project reaches the intended target groups

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

By involving communities in project management the communities will adopt and own the project. The proposed project is to assist in reducing and preventing the longer-term effects of crises and assist in recovering the community from the conflict. • Train community-based paralegals that will be carrying out litigation Beneficiary and community participation to ensure project ownership and accountability shall be achieved through community contribution in the project implementation cycle. Peacebuilding activities are to form part of the project in order to provide sustainable lasting solutions to communities in addressing some of the drivers to situations pose a protection risk to children, girls, boys, women, and persons with special needs. • Establish child protection clubs in schools. The schools through teachers will be able to adapt and practice the shared learned activities and ensuring children practice. The project recognizes that Sustainability of the protection and promotion of rights must be enjoyed and easily accessed by UASC and hence durable solutions suggested to enable maintaining the level of necessary care and protection that is afforded to these children such as the planned family reunification. 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

GUNO will conduct regular meeting for raising concern through community focal persons. This will ensure all the target groups’ concern are listen to and addressed appropriately through community focal person from the community. This project will be community owned and everybody will participate in the project cycle. GUNO's approach of operation normally focuses on a person as a whole by providing psychosocial/physical, emotional, economic and social support. There is normally accountability and feedback mechanism in place to ensure that the needs of the affected people are taken into consideration while developing and implementing a project. GUNO had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and the affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups) in the target county. The design of this project is in accordance with the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women, and men of different ages so that no activity will be carried out in isolation or fragmented manner as the proposed project aims to adopt a comprehensive approach based on the participation of all stakeholders. Once a project is approved and funding available, all necessary authorities will be informed, documents will be shared and meeting will be conducted with community leaders and representatives of all age groups and sex from the affected population to be informed about the project and during implementation information will be passed continually during monthly review/feedback meetings.
