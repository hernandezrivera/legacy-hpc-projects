Project Summary


An estimated 3 million school-aged children 6- 18 years are out‐of‐school and that 1.4 million crisis affected children (45% girls) will need education assistance in 2021. The SG Report on Children and Armed Conflict 2020 further states that in 2019, 1,442 children (53 girls) were recruited and/or used mainly by non-state armed groups and that 227 girls reported sexual violence. The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 1,184 grave violations, registering the recruitment of 546 children (30 girls), the killing and maiming of 469 children (107 girls) and 29 attacks on schools during the reporting period of January and June 2020. COVID-19 school closure had also affected 1.1M8 children previously enrolled in schools from March to August 2020 leaving many children to engage in child labor, early marriages and increased child trafficking. GBV AoR rapid assessment on effects of COVID-19 reported a 34% physical violence, 20% sexual abuse and harassment and intimate partner violence. 81% of the respondents stated that they know ten or more girls cut or undergone FGM. Also, girls were not able to access sanitary kids as parents could not afford. The project will ensure protection mechanism are in place for children including disable 10% of the target gender sensitive hygiene facilities, psychosocial support, teachers training and communities in identifying and preventing gender based violence against girls in school. The training and support of teacher and community education committee(CEC) will ensure to provide equal opportunity for men and women and will provide critical skills for duty bearers to identify how gender roles create risk for girls and challenge to their enrollment and attendance in schools, inequalities in classrooms and ensure that both boys, girls including disable children are able to access safe learning space in which they are protected from violence (both in schools and in the community).The project will employ several strategies to promote girls and boys education among the pastoral communities in Bakol, Bay and Hiran where HIDIG have active presence through social mobilization campaign, gender dis-aggregated WASH facilities in schools. The project will specifically address the issue of equity by targeting IDPs marginalized boys and girls in the community through provision of school feeding and learning materials. Through this project HIDIG intends to ensure the target schools are provided with safe drinking water hence improving school’s retention and enrollments.
The project will also focus on pedagogical component related to mobilization, establishments of 24 an/or rehabilitation temporary learning space, training and mentoring of 207 teachers and 90 community education committee, 24 children to child club and delivery of teacher’s incentives including teaching and learning materials with regular monitoring and supervision.
HIDIG project seek to involve officials from both south west and Hirshabelle regional state in the Ministry of education in participating project inception, training, monitoring and supervision in different level. HIDIG will also engage other partners working in these area and education sub cluster on information sharing and coordination to the affected boys and girls in the target regions.
Finally, the target communities remain to be main partners in this project and will be front and centre in driving the activities including establishment of temporary learning, social mobilization, Emergency school feeding, community education committee participation and child to child club.HIDIG will ensure all target children boys ,girls including disable have access to child protection information and emphasis will be psychosocial support services

Project Activities

1.1: Provide learning opportunities to 2,500 (10% disable children) in emergency affected children (1,500 boys and 1000 girls) and youth in Bakol(Rabdhure and Ceelbarde 1,000), Bay ( Baidoa 700) and Hiran(Beletweyne 800 ) region Somalia. 1.2: Rehabilitation/establishment of 24 temporary learning spaces for emergency-affected boys and girls in Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Beletweyne district in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region. HIDIG will enure the learning spaces are design to suit the needs of the disable children in the target learning centers 1.3: Conduct 24 social mobilization campaign to increase enrollment of boys and girls including disable children in Bakol, Bay and Hiran target location. 1.4: Establish 12 child to child club and trained 24 facilitators four in each of the three target district of Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Beletweyne to enable provide monthly mentoring support to vulnerable boys and girls. 1.5: Distribute learning materials to all the target 24 schools by providing efficient learning materials 2.2: Provide monthly incentive to 207 trained teachers (150 male and 57 female) in Bakol, Bay and Hiran region Somalia. 1.6: Provide 2500 (10% disable children) school feeding program to vulnerable boys (1,500) and girls (1000) to enable improve access and equitable learning environment to these emergency affected boys and girls. 1.7 Capacity building of 207 (150 male and 57 female) on basic pedagogical, lifesaving learning skills and psycho social support to enable improve the capacity of school management and learning in Bakol ,Bay and Hiran region. 1.8 Conduct training of 88 community education committee (44 Male and 36 female) on DRR, Hygiene promotion, maintenance and management of learning spaces in Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Beletweyne district

Mitigation Measures

HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing temporary learning spaces in consultation with the host communities an education authority. Where not possible in areas like Ceelbarde and Rabdhure pastoral communities will be provided with mobile school where HIDIG will procure donkey and carts to ensure schools are integrated within the already existing dugsi to enable provide needed services. Based on the education Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the IDPs and host communities.The following strict measures will be adhere to: 1. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile schools will be used to reach rural communities 2. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. 3. HIDIG will ensure learning space are constructed in far from military base to avoid the cross ﬁre between the militia group and the military thus reducing the risk of explosion and gender based violence against vulnerable girls. 4. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence5. HIDIG will capacity build its staff and the community leaders on protection, sexual exploitation abuse (PSEA) to enable increase awareness of such vice in HIDIG operation areas as well as its target beneficiaries

Please provide further explanation

The project is meant to build community resilience by strengthening local education infrastructure and thus provide timely action, maintaining momentum of the response, and expanding reach in underserved areas promoting equity. Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of equitable access to basic education services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of school attacks, poor enrollment and retention rate. To increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. HIDIG will strengthen capacity of teachers by training them on life-skill, school management as well as community education committee. Communities will be involved in project designation and will be part of the committees supporting district level education committees. Working with other actors will ensure leverage among themselves and providing maximum support to target communities strengthening their capacities in various aspects
