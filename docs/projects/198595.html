Project Summary

The project will seek to increase access to Basic health services and provision of sexual reproductive health services including family planning, Post Abortion care, comprehensive safe abortion care, antenatal care, post-natal care immunization, clinical management of rape, and prevention of Human Immunodeficiency Virus and to strengthen healthcare facilities by training healthcare service providers on clinical management of rape and behavior change communication exercises, an approach that will increase the quality and availability of life-saving Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) services that allow Multi Aid and Charity Initiative to meet the needs of the target population.
Multi Aid and Charity Initiative will support healthcare facilities with Emergency reproductive health kits, and lifesaving emergency equipment/supplies. Mobile clinics will be established and supported for those unable to visit clinics because of insecurity and distance. In addition, Mobile will be attendants will be trained in testing and referral of Human Immunodeficiency Virus and be provided with Safe Delivery Kits that will target visibly pregnant women. Throughout the project period, depending on need, Multi Aid and Charity Initiative will distribute dignity kits to women through our mobile clinics.
Multi Aid and Charity Initiative will provide community volunteers with SRH, PSEA, and community mobilization training. In turn, these community volunteers will undertake regular community mobilization and awareness sessions on these key topics throughout the project.

1 - Needs

The northeast region has the highest maternal mortality rates in Nigeria, estimated at 1,549 per 100,000 live births. Approximately 28% of IDPs (370,000), 34% of people in host communities (400,000), and 32% of returnees (170,000) do not have sufficient access to safe water for drinking, cooking, and personal hygiene. About 5 million people are in dire need of humanitarian health assistance across the BAY states. Among the key risks are severe malnutrition, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, and the outbreak of cholera and vaccine-preventable diseases like measles and cVDPV2. The public health systems in the BAY states cannot meet the needs of the populations. Health systems face a variety of challenges including dilapidated infrastructure and equipment, lack of qualified staff, gaps in the supply of medicines and medical supplies, and under-funding. The Health Sector provides services to all age and demographic groups through different standard packages, prioritizing children under five, pregnant and lactating women, and older adults with chronic health conditions. Life-saving and vital health services are delivered through a combination of minimal and comprehensive packages, including services for maternal-and-child health, sexual and reproductive health, GBV survivors, management of malnutrition with medical complications, management of non-communicable diseases, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and the strengthening of response to outbreaks. In rural communities in Northeast Nigeria, Primary Health Care Clinic (Mobile Clinic) also plays an integral role in the social, cultural, and religious fabric of the community. Primary Health Care clinics (Mobile Clinic) are widely accepted and patronized in Borno state, despite their limitations, which has also contributed to high rates of Mother-to-Child Transmission of Human Immunodeficiency viruses. It is possible to attribute the high population to the high total fertility rate (5.6%), a high proportion of women of reproductive age, and a low contraceptive prevalence rate (15%). Mother-to-Child Transmission of Human Immunodeficiency Virus is high due to the high prevalence of Human Immunodeficiency Virus among pregnant women, the culture of prolonged breastfeeding and missed feeding, and the non-use of modern health facilities during pregnancy and delivery. The need for prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) of Human Immunodeficiency Virus programs is one of the health sector's responses to the Human Immunodeficiency Virus/AIDS epidemic. A majority of pediatric Human Immunodeficiency Virus infections occur through mother-to-child transmission (MTCT), which accounts for more than 90% of all pediatric Human Immunodeficiency Virus infections. A National Demographic Health Survey also revealed that 36% of pregnant women receive no antenatal care (ANC), only 35% of deliveries occur in health facilities, and 56% of mothers receive no postnatal care.

Establishment & running of complaint and response mechanisms (CRMs) To increase accountability and community engagement and improve response to Gender-Based and human rights incidents, the project shall establish/strengthen complaint response mechanisms in the target communities. A dedicated CRM officer will manage complaints desks weekly in each community. Experience has shown that the complaints desks, which provide beneficiaries with the opportunity to provide feedback face-to-face and to receive an immediate response, are the most popular and effective. Besides, complaint boxes will be placed in each community. All complaints and responses will be collected in a dedicated tracker, including referrals made.
