Project Summary

Through this project, IMC aims to meet the humanitarian needs of GBV survivors, by providing clinical services for sexual and physical GBV, availing quality psychosocial support and working with communities to improve awareness and reduce risks and vulnerabilities to GBV.

Needs

The multi-layered complex humanitarian crisis in Somalia continues to result in a myriad of Protection risks. Gender based violence (GBV) in particular is among the most common form of harm that vulnerable communities face, particularly displaced populations. UNOCHA estimates that 2.6 million Somalis are internally displaced across the Country. More than three quarters (76%) of recorded GBV incidents in Somalia occur among vulnerable IDP communities who are exposed to GBV risks in large part due to the circumstances they live under (Protection mid-year review, Oct 2018). IDP settlements are often unplanned, crowded, with very poor living conditions, have little to no security, and face demolition due to forced evictions. 2018 has seen a massive increase in vulnerability due to the increased number of forced evictions of IDPs in Mogadishu, and to a lesser extent Mudug and Bay regions. The Protection cluster estimates that as of October 2018, 28,441 households (171, 000) individuals across the Country have been forcefully evicted, placing more women and girls at risk of Protection violations, including GBV.The burden of GBV varies by location with sexual violence more prevalent in districts with high IDP populations. In 2018, IMC facilities in Middle Shabelle have seen 54 survivors of GBV, 30% of whom were aged 17 years and below. Sexual violence and physical violence are the most common forms of violence reported, accounting altogether for 59% of reported cases. In Baidoa region, the IMC facility sees on average 10 GBV cases a week with sexual violence alone accounting for 70% of reported cases. With the largest IDP population in the Country, Mogadishu has the highest reported rate of GBV. Unlike other regions, just over one fifth (21%) of the GBV caseload are survivors facing denial of resources or opportunities. In rural Galmudug region, FGM and Physical violence are the most commonly reported forms of GBV. In almost all cases, survivors are women and girls.

Activities

1. Provide clinical and individual psychosocial support to GBV survivors2. Distribute dignity kits containing solar powered torches to survivors and vulnerable households in the community.3. Establish Women Friendly Spaces within the community to promote group based Psychosocial wellbeing among marginalised and vulnerable women and girls.4. Train community leaders on basic psychosocial first aid and safe referral of survivors.5. Increase access to services for people with disability.6. Train health facility staff on caring for child survivors of GBV.7. Form balanced and representative community support groups. These will be trained on GBV awareness, in order to embed GBV advocacy and risk mitigation within the community.8. Work with partner agencies to refer GBV survivors to cash/ livelihood services available within the locality.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project is linked to community justice systems and will train key community leaders on providing basic psychosocial first aid to survivors and on how to make safe referrals to health facilities and other services as needed. Sustainability is built into the project through the formation of community support groups who will be trained to advocate for safer communities and support the community to mitigate GBV risks.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

1. Risk: Consultations with displaced communities from marginalized communities risk being hampered by gatekeepers or local leaders. Likelihood: High – Impact: HighMitigation measures: - Request the systematic participation of representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation meetings with local leaders.- Condition the implementation of the project on equal and direct access with all communities- Establish direct contact directly with leaders of IDPs from marginalized communities, outside camps if necessary. 2. Risk: Forced EvictionsLikelihood: High – Impact: HighMitigation measures:- Train community leaders on provision of psychosocial first aid and risk mitigation activities including how to form watch groups among themselves. 3. Risk: Distance travelled to access servicesLikelihood: High – Impact: HighMitigation measures: - Avail cash to facilitate transport and referral of survivors 4. Risk: Weak referral mechanismsLikelihood: Medium – Impact: High - Co-ordinate with Health, Protection and Livelihood actors to set up safe referral mechanisms and protocols to protect the privacy of victims of GBV. 5. Risk: Cultural norms that exclude IDPs such as single women, persons with disabilities or female/child headed householdsLikelihood: High – Impact: HighMitigation measures:- Form and train community support groups that contain vulnerable community members.- Ensure GBV advocacy and outreach information considers literacy and disability in order to reach the broader community and consider literacy levels (e.g. use pictoral leaflets, radio messaging)
