Project Summary

To contribute to the overall WASH objective and ensure populations affected by displacement have reduced risk of exposure to WASH related diseases, NRC will strengthen populations’ capacity to engage in safe practices to promote better hygiene behaviours to limit health risks associated with poor WASH practices. NRC will use a range of technical approaches to provide the hardware required for access to safe water, improved hygiene and safe sanitation.
Target group
NRC will target the most vulnerable individuals and communities affected by crisis including IDPs and vulnerable host communities, in both urban, peri urban hard to reach areas including bushes and rural areas. NRC WASH interventions in the North West and South West regions aims to reach 77,000 persons (39,269 females and 37,731 males) in 12,834 Households Specifically, the following beneficiaries will be targeted:
1. Internally Displaced Persons: NRC seeks to address the needs of vulnerable persons who have been displaced as a result of the different crises in the North West and South West Regions. These include: a) persons affected by primary, secondary or by multiple displacements situation with limited access to WASH services; b) protracted displacements, host communities, and IDPs living in collective buildings with limited access to WASH services c) those who are unable to return for security reasons to their area of origin or who have not consented to reintegration in displacement areas for one reason or another.
2. Returnees (includes refugee returnees): These include IDPs returning back to their original locations of displacement.
3. Vulnerable Host Communities: These include vulnerable families living within communities hosting at least 50% IDPs as compared to the host population. Competition over limited resources over time can result to tensions which may cause further harm
4. People living in areas currently not accessible: These are groups of people who in one way or another did not move and have been blocked from access to services due to the ongoing conflict. These can only be reached should the security situation and access improve.
5. Persons living with disabilities and those with special needs: These group include the disabled, persons with special needs and the elderly. By bringing water points and sanitation facilities closer to populations, NRC considers that beneficiaries will be less exposed to protection risks and will have more time to engage in income generating activities for women and school attendance for children.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North-West and South-West crisis continues to exacerbate the water and sanitation needs of the affected people, including in the neighbouring Littoral and West regions. People are displaced with low or no access to safe drinking water and basic sanitation services. Access to safe drinking water remains the second top need expressed by the affected population in assessed villages in the North-West and South- West, while hygiene and sanitation ranked sixth. The assessment also shows that in 71 per cent of villages, pit latrines without slab are most common and are reported to be unhygienic and insufficient Also, in 31 per cent of villages, almost all households lacked handwashing facilities at home. In over half of the villages assessed, lack of privacy was a main challenge in accessing hygiene and sanitation infrastructures, affecting mostly women and girls, and increasing the risk of GBV (OCHA, MSNA August 2021). NRC conducted a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment in the North west, South West, Littoral and West Regions in November 2021 and the WASH needs identified were linked to rehabilitation of water sources, treatment and improvement of the quality of water from the water sources, latrine constructions, hygiene promotion centred around the eradication of open defecation, improvement of knowledge on key hand washing moments and the need for personal hygiene items. Access to drinking water and latrines was a great concern in assessed villages. Findings from the assessment indicated that the main sources of water varied across the locations of interest and displacements. Overall, 72% of the households assessed reported to be collecting water from unprotected water sources with over 83% reporting this to be their drinking water source. Furthermore, 24% of the assessed households spend over 30minutes on a round trip to collect water. Waiting time was highest amongst IDPs in the North West (37%) and Loum in the Littoral region (48%). The existence of open defecation amongst community members were examined and largely reported by assessed households in the South West followed by HH in the North West. Most HH latrines were shared between families and not gender segregated. The use of shared latrines was highest in Nguti (81%) and Loum (70%) subdivisions of the South West and Littoral regions respectively. Only 18% of the assessed HH confirmed that they wash their hands during 5 critical moments and expressed knowledge on at least 03 critical hand washing moments with the most key hand washing moment being after toilet used and before eating. There is therefore an urgent need for WASH interventions to improve access to sanitation facilities, improve access to safe water and promote good hygiene practices in the community and in schools to break the chain of transmission of water borne diseases.The cholera outbreak in the SW region continues to spread with a surge in the number of cases in the Buea and Limbe health districts. The number of cases increased from 1,320 in February to 3,559 cases as of 30 March 2022. The number of deaths increased from 37 to 71 during the same period (2.0 per cent fatality rate). The initial capacities of the treatment centers in Buea, Limbe, and Tiko towns were overwhelmed, and the resources dedicated to the response to the outbreak depleted. Partners have supported the response and preparations to carry out large-scale vaccination campaigns in high-risk health areas in the Buea, Limbe, and Tiko health districts and to implement case area targeted interventions in less affected health areas (OCHA Situation Report, 11 May 2022).
