Project Summary

The program objective is to reduce harmful practices against women and girls because of continuing instability through providing lifesaving, specialized, quality interventions for GBV prevention, mitigation, and response, such as case management, psychosocial support, referral pathways, empowering women and girls, capacity building, and the integration of reproductive health (family planning only). Incorporating RH into GBV programming is the expertise of the MF. MF will target ( 5000 men, 26000 women, 1000 boys and 8000 girls).
This project increases measures to mitigate the risk of GBV in the humanitarian response in areas of increased need due to large-scale. • Integrating reproductive health and family planning into GBV services, through individual counseling, awareness-raising sessions, family planning materials, and referrals to other health services, for women and adolescent girls, including Widows camps
• the needs and protection of PWDs and adolescent girls, as well as the elderly, empowering them and benefiting from their experiences, and providing activities for girls using curricula designed for them.
The project proposes to provide outreach, awareness-raising, and GBV services (the GBV toolkits) through four Safe Spaces for Women and Girls (WGSS), Jasmine 2 (located in Atmeh camp and owned by Maram Foundation), and centers, so MF will coordinate with the GBV sub-cluster as needed and the services available within the area if a new center will be established
(they are likely will be established in Idleb city, Azaz and in Al bab). Maram Foundation will provide GBV services through these Centers in Idleb and north of Aleppo
MF will establish a mobile team to reach larger catchment areas and beneficiaries who cannot visit the center. The project targets IDPs, host communities including widows’ camps, The women and girls’ safe spaces (WGSS) also have child-care spaces as deﬁned by UNFPA guidelines, ensuring a fully participatory approach of the female beneﬁciaries.
Through the centers and mobile teams, MF will provide services (GBV tool kits) and ensure coverage of specific locations as follows:
• Provide specialized GBV services to survivors, including CM (all cases will be upload to GBVIMS) and individual PSS.
• Women and girls’ empowerment: MF will provide (vocational training) and will seek to encourage the widows to start their businesses through IGA and small grants activities that will be distributed to widows in camps.
• Awareness-raising services will be offered to all groups using GBV toolkit."
• Provide GBV programming for adolescents and youth girls for life skills using the guides dedicated to this category" Safety My Health," "Psychodrama for girls," " I deal with teenagers and young mothers club
• Recreational activities
• Provision of reproductive health services (family planning) linked to family planning by midwives.
• Boys, men, people with special needs, and persons unable to reach through mobile teams will be targeted to provide a GBV awareness-raising toolkit, and young father club
• IPA: For immediate protection services, MF will provide the (IPA) as in-kind assistance for individuals and those most vulnerable including GBV survivors.
• Case Fund: Women and adolescent girls will be targeted with GBV CM, with direct support (case fund) to those under high-risk. case funds for GBV cases which would be used for referrals, direct support, transportation, and medicals
Capacity-building, will be carried out in two phases:
• Training the project team [PFA], PSS, CM, PSEA, awareness-raising toolkit)
• Training humanitarian workers in other sectors on (GBV basic, PFA, and Referral pathways.

3 - Needs

Ongoing violence, widespread poverty, loss of livelihoods, destruction and loss of housing and property, protracted and multiple cycles of displacement, substandard living conditions, family separation and the breakdown of family or community support structures have depleted the coping abilities of individuals and communities and generated high levels of psychological distress. As a result, people resort to harmful coping mechanisms such as different forms of exploitation, child/forced marriage, child labour or child recruitment. Different forms of GBV are routinely used to reinforce norms of dominant masculinity. access to services has reduced, and GBV within the home increased. Intersecting vulnerabilities mean that women and girls who face compounded forms of discrimination, such as widows and divorcees, and women and girls with disabilities face stigmatization and are exposed to even harsher and more frequent forms of GBV. Of particular concern is the situation in so-called widows’ camps, where access to essential services is limited or non-existent, and women and girls face serious rights violations.Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection crisis compounded by over ten years. Referring to SYRIA HNO 2022 the expanded analysis. For GBV AOR; the people in need 7.3M/ 92.9% Female, 7.1% Male - 41.3% Children (0-17), 54% Adults(18-59), 4% Elderly (>59) - 28% PwDs. Based on a World Vision survey: 32% of early marriages. 9% Had experienced sexual abuse.

Provide GBV programming for adolescent girls for life skills. WGSS Center offers recreational activities. Provision of reproductive health services linked to family planning by midwives. Boys, men, people with special needs, and persons unable to reach through mobile teams will be targeted to provide awareness-raising toolkits, PSS activities and specialized services, case management including case fund and IPA, counseling, and referrals. Women and girls’ empowerment by vocational training. Capacity building for (associations, organizations, health facilities, and humanitarian workers) on GBV basics, PFA, and referrals.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: the populations will be hesitant or unwilling to engage on this topic. Possible increased reporting of SEA with increased awareness about beneficiary rights and reporting mechanisms. Risk: the potential for the very difficult working environment in integrating the SOPs and best practices of PSEA due to the sensitive nature of the topic in the culture, and/or other risks include facing increased reporting of SEA without adequate capacity to respond. Maram foundation team will be conducted PSEA Awareness-raising and information-sharing efforts and be scaled up where possible, by respective PSEA focal points supported by the PSEA Networks program and by the Maram foundation with the presence inside Syria to communicate information on PSEA. At the least, The following are standards for the affected communities will be informed on: I. The definition of sexual exploitation and abuse. II. The standards of conduct for humanitarian workers. III. Humanitarian workers have a right to humanitarian assistance without being subjected to SEA IV. Where to report SEA incidents (all possible options/channels). V. What services are available, including complaint confidentiality assessments, and how to access them. VI. What to expect after making a complaint, including potential referrals, timeframes, and the roles, responsibilities, and any limitations of actors involved. VII. What steps the Maram and the concerned agency will take to ensure safety and confidentiality.
