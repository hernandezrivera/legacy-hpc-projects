Project Summary

The goal of the project is to improve food security, nutrition and resilience of vulnerable people, especially women, youth and PWDs from the FDMNs and host communities. The theory of change of the project is - if people have availability, accessibility and knowledge to utilize nutritious foods; if their vulnerability to climate change and disaster is reduced; and if they can live in a healthy environment, then, they will have improved household food consumption, will be more resilient to disasters and shocks and will be less exposed to communicable diseases and other health problem contributing to improved food security, nutrition and resilience.
In order to increase food production and thereby increase availability, the project will bring a large area of fallow land in the host communities under cultivation by promoting climate-smart saline agriculture. It will also use the limited lands available in the camps and promote homestead gardening for increasing the supply of fresh food to the FDMNs. This will be achieved through building the capacity of small-scale producers in the host community and FDMNs, introducing new or improved technologies for increased production and by providing technical support along with inputs. The project will also support establishing market linkage between the host community producers and fresh food corners in the camps. In the host communities, aggregation centers and women-led sales centers will be established, product fairs will be organized and linkage will be established between market actors and small-scale producers, especially women producers, to improve the bargaining power and ensure proper marketing of the products.
For improving food accessibility, youths will be engaged in market-driven skills training following three different models - (i) center-based model, (ii) TVET and (iii) apprenticeship model to enhance their capacity and create employment opportunities. The skills training will be complemented by entrepreneurship training, equipment supply and capital support. The project will also create linkage with potential employers, engage youths from the camp in cash for work opportunities and facilitate the establishment of group/community-based and gender-transformative enterprises. All of these activities will contribute to increased household income and the ability to access nutritious foods. For food utilization, the project will focus on behaviour change communication and nutrition promotion both in the camps and host communities. In this regard, the project will organize courtyard sessions, community campaigns along with local TV channels, community radio, mobile phone, social media, billboard and various other channels to disseminate nutrition messages and promote dietary diversity. For climate and disaster risk reduction, the project will build disaster-resilient structures in the camps including WASH facilities. It will prepare the local communities with early warning systems and other preparedness activities. It will also promote climate adaptation technologies i.e. renewable energy, rainwater harvesting, solar irrigation and seaweed production in the host community for animal feed production.
To ensure a healthy and hygienic environment, ICCO - part of Cordaid will focus on waste management in the camps. This will include community awareness-raising in waste management, establishing systems for waste management (i.e. setting up dustbins, waste collection system) and setting up waste upcycling systems where current wastes will be converted to useful materials with the help of the community people. Protection, life-skills, inclusion and safeguarding will be cross-cutting areas for the project. Activities such as life skills training; awareness-raising about protection issues (GBV, child marriage, trafficking, etc.) among women, adolescent girls and other community members; providing assistive devices and working on accessibility for PWD will be part of the project.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar is one of the country’s poorest and most vulnerable districts in Bangladesh. The influx of 727,164 Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs) strained the socio-economic condition of the host communities. The makeshift settlements for the FDMNs resulted in deforestation of over 13 square kilometers of forest land. Host community farmers lost cultivable land and access to water bodies for fishing that adversely affected their ability to earn a decent income. For the Joint Response Plan (JRP) 2022, ICCO - part of Cordaid conducted a rapid needs assessment in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila of Cox's Bazar district covering 372 respondents. This rapid assessment looked into the livelihood opportunities, climate vulnerability, women’s access to skills, capital, market, and other activities in these Upazilas. The findings revealed that day labor, farming, and fishing are the top three livelihood options. Apart from these 3, the participants also mentioned salt production, dry fish processing, fishing net production and handicrafts as alternate livelihood options. The absence of a deep irrigation system, soil salinity, and lack of knowledge on improved farming practices prevent the farmers achieve the desired volume of crop production. Due to low food production and lack of access to nutritious food, malnutrition is widely prevalent, especially among children. People living in the coastal belt regularly experience tropical cyclones, storm surges, and flash floods which damage crops resulting in reduced income. Lack of employment opportunities, limited access to government services, and inadequate skills training facilities also contribute to fewer income opportunities for the host communities. Households incurring debts have increased in the host community from 41% in 2019 to 53% in 2021. About 50% of the participants responded that women do not have access to the market due to social barriers, religious beliefs, distance, interference of the middleman and the customers’ tendency to pay less to women entrepreneurs. Lack of capital, market linkage, business knowledge are some of the challenges experienced by women while marketing their products. Additionally, women, children, and persons with disabilities have difficulty availing transport facilities which further restrict their ability to access markets. Earlier this year, ICCO conducted another study to assess the current condition of livelihood and enterprises in Cox's Bazar that echoed lack of market linkage, raw materials, access to finance and access to employment as the most pressing problem in the host community (ASM Amanullah, 2021). The situation is similar in the Rohingya camps as FDMNs are heavily dependent on humanitarian assistance. The overall vulnerability has increased compared to 2019 due to COVID-19 and 86% of households are highly vulnerable which is 16% more than that of 2019. Despite receiving the assistance, 49% of Rohingya households are not able to afford their basic needs (consuming below the minimum expenditure basket-MEB). If assistance were removed, 96% of Rohingya would not be able to cover the MEB. About 62% of Rohingya households engage in crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies to cover food and non-food needs, compromising their future productivity and coping capacity. J-MSNA 2020 report stated that an alarming 89% of adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years do not have access to any education or skills training which is needed to get a job or start a business. Older adolescents and youth (15-24), especially females (21%), face a greater risk of forced labour, sexual abuse, exploitation, trafficking, and drugs due to the lack of livelihood development opportunities. FDMNs adopted different coping strategies to address the challenges caused by the displacement. Some of the FDMNs are getting involved with income generating activities (Cash for Work Activities) under DRR project which helps them to live with dignity.
