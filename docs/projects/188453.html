Project Summary

WASH infrastructure requires significant support and substantial investments. Reliability and efficiency of water systems have sharply decreased for the first time since 2016, with nearly 2 million fewer people using water networks as the only household water supply modality, in comparison to mid-2020. This means up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet or complement their water needs (vis-à-vis 38% in mid-2020). Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. In Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor, for example, pretty much all the untreated raw sewage is discharged into the Euphrates River, which also serves as the source of drinking water to a vast majority of the population. In NWS, wastewater from huge camp clusters is openly discharged, forming sewage rivers. Similarly, in NES, no wastewater treatment exists for the IDP sites, resulting in environmental pollution and increased disease prevalence. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental and protection risks.
Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people in IDP sites, highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance. Up to 96% of interviewed residents of IDP sites received some WASH humanitarian assistance, but 44% were not fully satisfied with it, mainly due to insufficient quantity or quality of assistance. This is potentially linked with shortage and/or discontinuity of funding. Water trucking services are the only source of water for 73% of IDP site residents and only 24% households are connected to (simplified) sewer networks, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP sites infrastructure to provide more sustainable, affordable, and safer way to supply water and dispose wastewater. Despite the sector assistance, 24% of IDPs in sites couldn’t access one or multiple hygiene items, 23% faced barriers to effective handwashing. Also, 35% of households reported issues with toilet functionality or wastewater disposal. Disruptions and poor quality of services in IDP sites have also direct negative physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, notably on women and girls.
The 4 Ws indicators are consolidated with WASH sector.
The project will also cover the monitoring of safe drinking water quality for 11 camps and 93 IDPs shelters with estimated targeted population of 600,531 individuals in NES GOV.

4 - Needs

Adequate supplies of good quality water are essential to maintaining health and reducing the risk of waterborne and food borne diseases. Due to the prolonged crisis, living conditions have deteriorated, especially in overcrowded settings, such as IDP camps and IDP shelters with limited available water and difficulties in maintaining hygiene. Throughout 2021, clusters of waterborne diseases have been reported contributing to potential waterborne disease outbreaks in fragile communities. In addition to the already disrupted and fragile WASH system, the COVID-19 outbreak has highlighted the essential needs on WASH in health facilities. Due to lack of adequate WASH infection prevention and control measures in health facilities, frequent clusters of COVID-19 have been reported in healthcare facilities, with more than 1400 cases of COVID-19 reported among healthcare workers in 2021.To ensure the safety of drinking water for vulnerable populations, WHO will continue to conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water to regularly monitor chemical and biological contamination of different water sources. WHO will perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in primary healthcare and will enhance medical waste management.In addition, WHO will enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing any nosocomial infections among healthcare workers and patients through capacity-building along with provision of healthcare waste management containers, vehicles, incinerator

5 - Implementation of activities

• Conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water by regularly measuring PH, ATP, chlorine residual of water samples from various water sources (e.g. water networks, ground wells, reservoirs, water tanks, jerry cans) and culture to identify pathogens. These activities will cover all governorate with special focus in Rural Damascus, Aleppo (Rural), Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor, where the severity scope is over 3, by deploying WHO focal points to conduct testing in collaboration with Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and WASH partners.• Perform laboratory analysis of contaminated water samples based on surveillance data at public health laboratories affiliated with MoLAE.• Support MoLAE in provision of laboratory equipment and supplies and sterilizing the contaminated water• sources by distribution of chlorine tablets to households and Directorates of Health in the targeted governorates along with awareness campaigns for communities • Perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in primary healthcare centers in Homs, Hama, Aleppo, rural Damascus and NES.• Enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing nosocomial infections through procurement of 5000 healthcare waste containers to be equipped in primary healthcare centers. • Monitoring Visits to the project sites to monitor the progress and implementation status.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WHO has established mechanisms to treat reports of SEA as a priority. Based on the gravity of proven cases of SEA involving WHO staff members, and in addition to any criminal verdict that may result from legal proceeding for cases that are referred to national authorities for criminal prosecution, or other measures taken against the staff member concerned, WHO will apply disciplinary measures pursuant to Section 11 of the Staff Rules, including as applicable summary dismissal. Disciplinary sanctions will also apply to staff members who are proven to have failed to report SEA or to have encouraged or condoned SEA to happen. Malicious reporting of SEA without evidence or reasonable suspicions with the intention of harming another person’s integrity or reputation amounts to misconduct and is subject to disciplinary action. This is distinct from reports of suspected wrongdoing made in good faith based on the judgment and information available at the time of the report, which may not be confirmed by an investigation
