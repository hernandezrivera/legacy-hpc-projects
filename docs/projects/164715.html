Project Summary

The proposed intervention will directly target IDPs, host community, and returnees at a household, collective centres, informal settlements, collective centres and camps in NWS including Azaz under the following objectives and outputs:
The project protection activities are in-line with the following protection cluster strategic objectives
Objective 1: The protection of population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers (SO1/SO2/SO3):
1.1. Provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms
1.2. Advocacy with duty bearers and key stakeholder to promote a conducive protection environment
Objective 2: Strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and duty bearers at national and community level to assess, analyze, prevent and address protection risks and needs
2.1 Capacity building activities targeting humanitarian actors and national/ local authorities to improve quality
2.2 Protection monitoring and protection needs / risks identification conducted by sector members
Objective 3 : Survivors have access to quality and life-saving GBV response services and measures are in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV
3.1 Provide quality and life-saving GBV response services, including case management and psychosocial support, enhance vulnerable groups' access to these services (with a focus on women and girls with disabilities and adolescent girls) and reinforce referal pathways
3.2 Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on most at risks groups (for ex. adolescent girls and female headed households, and divorced and widowed women and girls) 3.3 Prevention and response activities targeting adolescent girls
3.4 Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response
Objective 4: Reduce the impact of explosive hazards
4.1: Conduct risk education for at risk groups
Objective 5: Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action
5.1 Community based child protection for girls and boys is available in targeted locations
5.2 Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in targeted locations
5.3 Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns in Syria

4 - Needs

HNO 2020 reported that sudden disruption in especially specialised protection services, is likely to lead to increased risks of GBV and CP especially for women and adolescent girls and children. Lack of certainty around continuation of services is adding to the stress faced by people in need especially those already experiencing restrictions on movement. IDPs are subjected to security screening procedures and deprived of freedom of movement. As a result, people recourse to harmful coping mechanisms while over-dependence on humanitarian aid persists. Additionally, the crisis has deepened poverty, fueled harmful coping mechanisms and i=][creased the risk of a suite of child protection concerns such as child labor and child marriage, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk. Child labor and child marriage is reported in all 14 governorates of Syria , depriving many of their childhood and basic rights. Access to basic rights and services are further jeopardized for children with disabilities and children that cannot access civil documents. Separation from caregivers continues to be reported. The absence of a continuum of alternative care options puts some children at increased risk of protection concerns. The lack of livelihood opportunities, and increased poverty are ultimately exposing women and girls to higher risks of all forms of violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse. Girls as young as 11-12 years old are being married by families believing it will protect them and ease the financial burden, whereas working women are often reportedly denied the opportunity to marry to continue supporting their family financially. The number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased the burden on the health sector, particularly with regards to the provision of specialized services including trauma care, physical rehabilitation, and psychosocial support in the short and long-term. While needs are high, available resources remain limited.

5 - Implementation of activities

World Vision current approach to protection services is focused on community centers,WGSS and CFSs, static facilities will be complemented by outreach and mobile teams covering and supporting emergency activities where required. Services offered consist specialized and none specialized services MHPSS, awareness raising on GBV, CP and mine risk education. Provision of GBV services to survivors including case management, individual protection assistance and referrals.To ensure safe and confidential referrals, case management teams will create linkages with other GBV and other service providers this is applied internally the referral process will be fully in line with the GBV SC referral pathway/SOPs. Awareness activities will be conducted with accordance to the GBV awareness toolkit. WV with the GBV SC will produce the 2nd module of an e-learning on inclusion.Protection activities will be focused on strengthening humanitarian actor’s capacity and provision of information to ensure impartial and timely access to protection services displacement. Protection emergency services will be provided where needed and in coordination with relevant clusters and emergency focal points.Given the volume and complexity of protection challenges in northwest Syria, the project team, in close coordination with protection technical team, will mainstream protection, GBV and CP across sectors in line with the relevant global standards. The recent comprehensive gender analysis will be integrated into any project needs analyses to ensure that relevant responses are provided and that gender-based injustices and inequalities are not exacerbated by the proposed interventions through this integration WV will promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response. WV includes disability inclusion in activities. The project will be implemented directly through WV and IPs IHSAN, SHAFAK, SR, SRD, SEMA and Bahar. Activities will be monitored by MEAL teams.
