Project Summary

The proposed project aims to improve the access of vulnerable and food-insecure households (HHs) to diversified food and to support their basic needs from further deterioration of living standards, as a result of the protracted crisis and socio-economic conditions. The project aligns with the HRP's second and third Country Strategic Objectives: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL”, and ”The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome the protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”. The project is also aligned with the Food Security Sector's (FSS) Specific Objective 2: "Households suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities are able to meet their basic needs", and to the MPCA's Specific Objective: “Vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to conflict and natural disaster supported with MPCA are able to meet their immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services, decreases the incidence of negative coping strategies”.
The project will target 500 vulnerable HHs (2,800 individuals; 736 Boys, 717 Girls, 683 Men, and 664 Women; among them 87 Elderly, and 56 Disabled) with high levels of food insecurity and those on the Ministry of Social Development's (MoSD) waiting list. Households with cancer patients needing urgent assistance as well as HHs with people with disabilities (PWDs) will be prioritized.
The project aims to reduce the immediate food gap by providing a bundle of food items through an e-voucher system (345 ILS/month) for six months, targeting 350 HHs (1,960 Individuals; 515 Boys, 502 Girls, 478 Men, 465 Women; among them, 55 Elderly, and 39 Disabled) with no or very limited access to food. In addition, the project seeks to support an additional 150 HHs (840 Individuals; 221 Boys, 215 Girls, 205 Men, and 199 Women; among them, 24 Elderly, and 17 Disabled) with unrestricted cash assistance. The target groups will be supported through MPCA (1,080 ILS/month) over the duration of four months to cover their basic needs (food, non-food, health, hygiene needs and other basic needs). The selected HHs will be those with the lowest PMTF scores and those with protection concerns. HHs selected for food vouchers will be those with severe and moderate food insecurity.
The project, implemented in partnership with WEFAQ Society for Women and Child Care, will target Rafah governorate. Al Wefaq has extensive experience in the targeted localities, efficient capacity in implementing cash assistance interventions, as well as a strong profile in protection which will help achieve project outcomes. Al Wefaq will have a leading role in reaching out to the project beneficiaries and implementing the planned activities, in close cooperation with and support of Oxfam. All project activities will be closely coordinated with relevant stakeholders, including the MoSD and the FSS cluster.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Gaza continues to face multi-layered stressors and prolonged restriction policies imposed by Israel on the highly vulnerable and fragile enclave. The 16 years of blockade, the intermittent military escalations and restrictions imposed on the Gaza strip exacerbated the humanitarian situation. According to the Palestinian Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) and quarter 2 of the 2022 Labour Force Survey, the unemployment rate in the Gaza Strip stood at 44.1%. Unemployment among females in Gaza reached 65% and up to 38% among males. According to World Bank (June 2021), the humanitarian needs of Gaza's more than 2 million population are further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic and the escalation of violence in May 2021, which negatively impacted livelihoods and access to essential services in Gaza. According to the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) in the Gaza Strip, the last hostilities in August 2022 hit tens of agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at $1m. Based on the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2022 results, 81% of households reported financial difficulties in meeting any of their household’s basic needs, and 70% of households reported difficulties meeting essential needs because they could not afford them. The MSNA key findings indicate that 55.7% of household expenditure (in cash or credit) was reportedly spent on food. The MSNA indicates the total number of people in need (PIN), in the Gaza strip in 2023 is 1,188,989 (53.4% of the total population), and also indicates that about 62% of Rafah governorate's total population are PIN, which is the highest percentage among other governorates.According to the MSNA (July 2022), the median amount of estimated monthly food expenditure by gender of the head of the household was 380 ILS when Female-headed households, and 575 ILS when Male-headed households.The SEFSec report shows that the food security status in Gaza worsened in 2020, with the share of severely food insecure HHs reaching 40.7%, increasing by 4.9% since 2018. This brings the total number of HHs experiencing severe or moderate food insecurity to 64.4%. Moreover, according to World Bank (June 2021), poverty rates were above 50% , and 77% of those in need of humanitarian assistance are in the Gaza Strip (1.19 million people). The proposed project targets Rafah governorate, where 61% of the population (165,605) is considered in need for urgent food assistance (MSNA). Around 75% of the population of Rafah have a low rCSI, 7.7% have poor food consumption scores (FCS) and 29.7% have FCS borderline classification. Oxfam and Wefaq conducted an assessment through carrying of 10 focus group discussions (FGDs) in the targeted communities, to illustrate the specific needs and the level of vulnerability. Although all the participants of the FGDs were women, the findings of these FGDs are in line with the data presented by MSNA, which highlights that 81% of HHs experience difficulty in meeting any of their basic HH needs. It also indicated that 70% of HHs experience difficulty in covering their food needs. The FGDs also aimed to identify the main concerns of the participants, which included fear of increase in prices of basic food items and a loss of in-kind assistance received by aid agencies

2 - Activities

In order to contribute to improving the access of vulnerable and food-insecure households (HHs) to diversified food and to support their basic needs from further deterioration of living standards, Oxfam and Wefaq will jointly conduct a variety of activities to provide food vouchers and multi-purpose cash assistance to the most vulnerable population in the targeted communities. Output 1: 350 poor and food-insecure households have access to diversified food items. Activity 1.1: Targeting and Selection of Households (HHs): To fairly and transparently select HHs to receive food vouchers, Oxfam will ensure coordination, nomination and cross-checking with relevant stakeholders for potential beneficiaries. Oxfam will contact the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) to nominate HHs with high food insecurity and poverty, according to the proxy means test formula (PMTF). Beneficiaries on the MoSD waiting list will be prioritized, depending on gaps identified by the MoSD at the time of the intervention. Al WefaqWEFAQ will carry out a HH verification and vulnerability assessment survey through home visits using the Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT). Based on the survey, scoring will be done for all nominated HHs, prioritizing families affected by the last escalation, families with cancer patients, families in need of urgent assistance, families with PWDs, and female-headed HHs. The list of HHs will be cross-checked with the FSS and FSS partners (i.e., IRPAL, CRS, Mercy Corps, and others working in parallel to this intervention) to avoid any duplication of assistance. Activity 1.2: Contracting shops: Oxfam has existing MoUs with more than 15 shops in the target areas. Selection of the shops will be made based on the localities of selected beneficiaries. Activity 1.3: A total of 350 selected households, at least 15% are female-headed households (FHH), (a total of 1960 individuals; 515 Boys, 502 Girls, 478 Men, 465 Women; among them 55 Elderly, and 39 Disabled) will receive food vouchers for six months. The value of the food voucher will be as per the recommendation of the food security sector and CWG to cover up to 60% of the food needs of the beneficiaries’ household (345 ILS). A monthly top-up will be provided, which will include a diversified food basket. Output 2: 150 affected and vulnerable HHs received MPCA to meet their basic needs. Activity 2.1: Targeting and Selection of Households (HHs). Selection of HHs will be made alongside the selection of beneficiaries of the food vouchers (A1.1), with the nomination from the MoSD and the use of the UVAT tool. The selected HHs will be those with the lowest PMTF scores and those with protection concerns. Activity 2.2: Provide 4 rounds of MPCA transfers to 150 households (HHs). Four cash transfers will be delivered through a local financial service provider. Each beneficiary will receive the transferred amount according to the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) for 4 months. The transfer value (1,080 ILS/month) is in line with the cash working group’s (CWG) recommendation, reflecting the monthly income of the targeted HHs. MPC will support 150 HHs, at least 15% are FHH, ((840 Individuals; 221 Boys, 215 Girls, 205 Men, 199 Women; among them, 24 Elderly, and 17 Disabled); to cover and access other urgent basic needs (food, non-food, health, hygiene needs and other basic needs) to decrease the use of negative coping strategies, such as selling assets or incurring debt. Activity 2.3: Monitor cash disbursement and delivery (Payment Verification): Oxfam will conduct four (4) payment verifications (PV) for 100% of the MPCA cash transfer beneficiaries, utilizing Oxfam PV digitalized tool, which is designed for remote data collection and management. The PVs will ensure that all targeted households have redeemed their cash in a timely, safe and dignified manner, as well as identify any gaps or challenges faced during the redemption process in real time in order to adapt and to improve the used transfer modality. Cash disbursement will be also monitored by Oxfam, directly in the field at the redemption sites, through regular field visits each cycle. Activity 2.4: Post distribution monitoring (PDM): The PDM survey will be conducted after the third payment. Oxfam aims to use the vulnerability assessment data (A2.1) as baseline information, and to monitor the cash transfers through its PDM of targeted beneficiaries, utilization of cash and, anywhere possible, changes to the income status at the HH level. Oxfam will collect disaggregated data for all project beneficiaries by sex, age and disability. Oxfam has provided training on safe programming to its partner staff working in its humanitarian programme, particularly those in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance. It will ensure that risks related to access of women to benefit from cash transfers are continuously analyzed and mitigated. Oxfam and Al Wefaq's field teams have joint experience in field monitoring of activities and in providing adequate and appropriate orientation to beneficiaries at the project start, including sharing feedback mechanisms and compliance with safeguarding policies. Oxfam will support Al Wefaq's capacity in aligning its data collection and monitoring activities towards gender-aware and gender-sensitive approaches, aiming to capture risks and benefits towards women in the project.

3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common Global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA), the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability and is aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts, providing guidance, tools and methodologies for various topics such as gender analysis and assessment of gender outcomes. Oxfam and its partners are committed to the EU Data Protection rules represented by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and will enforce Oxfam and partners’ staff members to strictly consider its rules in all data processing and beneficiaries' database, including accountability mechanisms. Oxfam is also committed to participatory MEAL in which partners, service providers, project stakeholders and beneficiaries have active and significant involvement in monitoring and evaluation actions through the continuous and regular consultation in the entire project cycle. All targeted groups, project beneficiaries and stakeholders are considered as influencers to the design, implementation and results of all monitoring actions, including needs assessments FGDs, baseline/end line surveys, project reviews, and evaluations. Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Quality Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project's logical framework, theory of change, MEAL plan and monitoring, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. The MEAL framework will promote gender and age sensitive and results-oriented MEAL practices, whilst ensuring accountability towards the affected populations, throughout project implementation. Technical Monitoring: Oxfam FSVL technical team and partners will ensure that the quality of implementation is in line with technical standards, action plan and budget, as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct direct implementation of the project's actions with day-to-day follow up by their field teams and experts. Oxfam has designed specific monitoring mechanisms for food security programs, in which the MPCA program is monitored through:1. Targeting HHs verification: It aims to ensure that all selected households fit with the selection criteria, updating their contact information through which they will receive the payment related information.2. Baseline Assessment: To identify the pre-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. In regards to the baseline methodology, the main tool for data collection is the Household Questionnaire, which is a structured questionnaire that seeks for quantitative and qualitative data to measure the following indicators:- The average livelihood Coping Strategy Index (LCSI) based on the cash working group (CWG) agreed criteria (None, Stress, Crises, emergency). Baseline: 80% of HHs, either crises or emergency; Target: 40% of the HHs crises or emergency.- The Food Consumption Score (FCS) (Poor, Borderline, Acceptable); Baseline: 80% poor and borderline scores, Target: less than 10% poor, and more than 50% Acceptable.- The baseline value of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) of the targeted households.- % of beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner; Baseline: 0, Target: 90%- % of the target population who report being able to meet the basic according to their priorities; Baseline: 0, Target: 70%.- % of children with disabilities, whose caregivers are females (classified to paid and unpaid).- # of beneficiaries receiving MPCA; Baseline: 0, Target 150 HHs * 5.6 (840 Individuals; 221 Boys, 215 Girls, 205 Men, 199 Women; among them, 24 Elderly, and 17 Disabled).3. Payment Verification: The payment verification process aims to ensure that the MPCA targeted HHs received the allocated cash assistance in a safe and dignified way.4. Post Distribution Monitoring: PDM aims to identify the progress value of the project indicators, measured under the baseline, and to provide real time recommendations to the project implementation team.5. End-line Assessment: The endline assessment aims to identify the post-intervention status of the project's outputs and indicators. The end-line methodology will use the same tools of the baseline, a Household Questionnaire, to collect post-intervention data. 6. Learning Event: The final learning event workshop will be conducted to present and disseminate the key findings of the assessment actions, including the baseline, PDM, HH verification, payment verification, and the end-line. The presentations will be “recommendation focused” on both policies and procedures of the social transfer programs and modalities in Gaza.Feedback Mechanism: Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) provides wider access to diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints at project implementation level. This system uses Information Communication Technology (ICT) for receiving feedback, either through an online link, verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels; such as the free helpline, text messages, Missed Calls, and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and Oxfam’s Global Safeguarding Team, and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. Moreover, media and communication tools will be utilized during public awareness sessions to raise the targeted population’s awareness on safe and responsive feedback tools. However, all received feedback and complaints will be reported and managed by utilizing the ICT tool.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

This action will enhance the food security situation of poor and vulnerable HHs by enhancing their access to nutritious and sufficient food. Hence, the voucher modality (unconditional but restricted cash assistance in the form of food vouchers) was selected to ensure that each HH receives the minimum needs of food items. Since consultations with surveyed families reported priorities other than food items (i.e., rental subsidies, and non-food items), the MPCA modality was also selected to support poor and vulnerable HHs to cover their basic needs in a flexible and dignified manner. Bank ATM cards and/or over the counter modality as such have been utilized for similar projects in the past by Oxfam. The final selection of the delivery mechanism will be determined at the project start, depending on any developments within the local context. Beneficiaries will be benefiting from the flexible and unrestricted cash in various ways that will enable them to allocate the cash according to their own priorities.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam's Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive online feedback through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels, such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam's MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam's Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam's confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. Field supervisors will be handling inquiries and operational complaints from beneficiaries by making their numbers available to all beneficiaries. This mechanism will help resolve issues in the field. All feedback and complaints will be handled responsibly following Oxfam Responsible Data Policy which ensures its confidentiality to protect the identity of those providing data, unless otherwise outlined and agreed to in the informed consent. Oxfam will ensure that the process of collecting feedback, and the feedback channels, are in an environment where the privacy of the participant (or group of participants in the case of focus group discussions) is upheld. Oxfam will also ensure to close the loop of the received feedback within a maximum of 2 weeks, while initial action will be taken in 48 hours.The results of the feedback collected will be used to address any potential concerns of beneficiaries/stakeholders, to inform decision-making and improve response, and to increase accountability to affected populations. Additionally, relevant lessons learned will be taken into consideration in the planning and designing of of future projects.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The action, in alignment with Oxfam programming parameters, will be premised on the grounds that the lives and dignity of all women and men beneficiaries, both direct and indirect, must be protected. The main humanitarian standards and protection principles will be given careful consideration, especially the do-no-harm principle, to ensure that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to essential protection needs. This is particularly important in the Gaza context, where social and political instability and conflict are evident in many areas with conservative protection norms. Explicit plans to reduce risks and to competently mitigate and respond to any incidents, including safeguarding training (i.e., on sexual harassment, discrimination and exploitation, and child protection) will be elaborated by Oxfam, monitored and adjusted if the context changes. These will address and take into account risks like COVID-19, risks related to safe programming, safeguarding, and potential gender specific risks. The Safeguarding focal point will carry out a refresher session for the project team on the overall protection principles, equipping them with the knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis and mitigation measures. As Oxfam is committed to following a participatory and inclusive approach, all activities have been designed in close coordination and full engagement with the target communities. The targeted communities will also be involved in the implementation and monitoring of all activities that aim to respond to vulnerability in all its forms, ensuring its reach to all marginalized people (including children, youth, older people, people with disabilities, ethnic groups and others who are marginalized due to their social status).Specifically, this project will work towards: Participation: We will systematically engage with all affected people and encourage active and meaningful participation of the most marginalized, including people with disabilities, to ensure that their views and capacities are reflected and built into all aspects of the response including assessment, design, delivery and monitoring and evaluation. Data: We will collect, fully disaggregate, and utilize data for different population groups in order to provide robust evidence to design, revise and learn from programming that is reflective of and appropriate for the people and needs identified.Oxfam and Al Wefaq have already tested the two related measures and SoPs in previous and current interventions, especially with regard to increasing women's access to food and cash by providing safe and appropriate locations, time and environment within project activities (i.e., voucher shops safe access, banks to receive cash transfers). All activities under this project will follow a quick risk assessment to identify any potential risks for women participating in the activity and will identify means to support them within the project or beyond through a referral mechanism. Oxfam has already carried out assessments through previous projects, resulting in learning and applying corrective measures during the implementation of the project. Regarding the protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Oxfam has a designated PSEA team, including integrity and safeguarding focal points, as well as confidential counsellors trained on PSEA, that is responsible for ensuring that all field staff comply with Oxfam’s SEA policy. Oxfam’s PSEA focal point is trained as a trainer of trainers by the UN PSEA Network, which is expected to have a greater role in further enhancing awareness on the PSEA and improving current mechanisms and systems in partner organizations.

Oxfam and Al Wefaq have solid previous experience and engagement with communities targeted under this project. The community engagement mechanisms will also provide clarity, orientation of the selection process, and the announcement to communities about planned activities and procedures to receive benefits; (d) Safe programming training for Al Wefaq's field and project staff to ensure the continuous identification of risks and response plans throughout the project cycle
