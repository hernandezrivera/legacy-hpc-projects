Needs

WASH and emergency assistance activities are needed to provide lifesaving assistance in the project area for IDPs, returnees and host community members. Findings from the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicate that 14.6m people are in need of water and sanitation services throughout Syria, including approximately 241,157 in Hassakeh, 165,325 in Ar-Raqqa and 961,845 in Aleppo and 710,355 in Deir ez Zor Governorate. The lack of access to clean water impacts the lives and health of all community members. In these circumstances, households are relying on trucked water which is not chlorinated and financially unsustainable. Lack of access to safe water and sanitation services continues to affect the lives and health of hundreds of thousands of households with many families, particularly in Der Ez Zor, still relying on water trucking. Trucked water is not chlorinated and financially non sustainable for vulnerable families, costing between 20 and 40 USD per month for a daily using of 60L per person in an average family of 5. Water stations and water networks have been severely damaged during the conflict and therefore not functional in large parts of Deir Ez Zor Governorate in particular but also to a lesser extent in Raqqa and elsewhere, which is currently counting for the greater share of IDPs and people in need of access to clean and safe water. A recent REACH assessment highlight that on average 72% of the population in Deir Ez Zor rely on water trucks for their daily water needs, with a peak of 92% in the East line. The limited availability of electricity and limited technical and financial capacity of local councils to operate and maintain water supply systems has resulted in a reduced productive capacity of water pumping stations and water supply networks. Many water supply systems along the Euphrates River have been damaged in the conflict, while others stopped working due to lack of either maintenance or specialized staff for operation. Leaking sewage pipes and disruptions to the removal of solid waste from urban areas such as Raqqah city also pose a significant environmental health risk and can result in vector-borne diseases. Before the conflict, most of Syria had high sanitation coverage. Urban coverage was as high as 96% of households connected to sewage lines, while rural coverage was at 80%. Water and sanitation systems were state-owned and centrally managed. However, because of the prolonged conflict and lack of maintenance, sewage systems in the target location are in need of rehabilitation and repair. Drainage of black/grey water from settlements is a major issue in the majority of towns, and the situation is exacerbated by the heavy run-off from winter rains. The combined factors of limited access to clean water and poor environmental conditions due to stagnant water from damaged sewage networks and poor solid waste collection and disposal have exposed communities to very high public and environmental health risks. The presence of solid waste, raw sewerage and rubble after the conflict in Ar-Raqqah city and Deir-ez-Zor has created a breeding ground for sand flies, mosquitoes, flys and other vectors which combined with the lack of access to clean drinking water has created a public health risk and resulted in increased reporting of acute watery diarrhea and leshmaniasis from areas or Ar-Raqqah Governorate. Implementation of activities

Concern will rehabilitate and re-commission 25 water supply systems primarily in Ar-Raqqa and Deir ez Zor governorates with limited activities in Aleppo and Al-Hasake Governorates in order to provide a minimum of 15L per person per day to returnees. These stations will be rehabilitated (were possible) to meet the designed capacity. Rehabilitation of water systems includes lifting and inspection of existing pumps, installation of new submersible pump generators and transformers where deemed necessary, replacement of cables, raiser pipes and pump control equipment flushing, yield testing and sanitization, repair and supply of new equipment, fittings and pipes, repair and replacement of damaged and rusted pipes in distribution network and installation of chlorine dosing systems and the provision of the appropriate type of chlorine and consumables (filters, engine oil), installation of water meters, training for water department technicians, tools kits and spare parts.following criteria: • Current water supply for the village of community is not enough for the current population • Water supply systems must be community owned and under the management of the local council in each village and neighborhood Concern will conduct a comprehensive technical assessment in order to identify the exact water supply systems in the 4 Governorates which will be targeted by this action. Concern’s WASH program in the target area is continuously assessing and prioritizing the most vulnerable communities when selecting which water pumping station should be rehabilitated next. The rehabilitation of 25 water pumping stations proposed in this action reflects the current locations which have been prioritized according to needs of the civilian population and existing WASH infrastructure. Concern will also complete the basic rehabilitation of rehabilitation of WASH facilities in 25 collective centers (schools). The rehabilitation of WASH facilities will ensure that IDPs have access to gender desegregated latrines including hand washing stations and that the ratio of people per latrine does not exceed 20 as per SPHERE standards. Concern will provide 3 months solid waste removal activities in at least 10 urban areas or IDPs camps after which responsibility for this service will be handed over to the local authorities. Each solid waste removal activity will be implements for up to 3 months and will utilize up to 10 incentivized volunteers plus heavy machinery in each location in order to improve the environmental hygiene of villages or neighborhoods in the targeted districts. Vector Control activities including indoor residual spraying (IRS) and outdoor thermal fogging (fumigation) to control the spread of sand flies and other vector and to control the risk from Leshmaniasis. It will target 24 communities in Deir Ez Zor, with each community on average comprising 5,000 individuals. An estimated total of 120,000 people will benefit from this activity in Deir Ez Zor Governorate. Concern’s rehabilitation of sewerage networks is designed to remove waste water including surface water from broken and blocked sewerage pipes from urban areas in close proximity to residential areas, schools, shops or other community infrastructure. This is done through small scale repairs and replacement of underground sewerage pipes to fix the leak. Concern will rehabilitate a minimum target of five different sewerage networks. Concern will also aim to distributed a minimum of 5,000 hygiene kits to to IDP families in informal IDP camps, collective centers and to IDPs living with the host community. Distributions of NFI/hygiene/shelter kits and food rations/vouchers/cash, plus emergency WASHactivities will target all new IDPs displaced within the previous month according to their needs and incoordination with other organizations. Limited assistance will also be provided toreturnees who are returning to their homes in rural areas which were previously conflict affected.

Protection Risk Analysis

Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) or Unexploded Ordnances (UXOs) are DiscoveredProbability: Unlikely/ Severity: Moderate - CatastrophicThere remain numerous IEDs and UXOs in Northern Syria that pose a significant risk to civilians and NGO staff. Concern and Bahar staff, as well as program participants, will receive mine awareness training with specific focus on high-risk activities such as rehabilitating irrigation channels, tilling soil, or conducting other activities involving digging. Concern will consult with local authorities and with mine clearance organizations before starting work. Those Concern staff who have been trained to give mine risk awareness trainings are responsible to share this training with all program staff and project participants. Concern maintains regular coordination on de-mining and mine risks. A activities will be halted in areas were mines are discovered, local authorities and Mine removal agencies will be called to deal with any suspicious devices. Activities will not resume in area until mines have been cleared. Attack Targets NGO StaffProbability: Unlikely / Severity: Severe - CatastrophicBoth Concern and Bahar are careful to maintain their neutrality and independence and take a conflict-sensitive approach in order to avoid being targeted by any party to the conflict. This is especially important in North East Syria where any NGO implementing activities may be perceived to be aligned with the Self-Administration who are currently controlling the program target areas. An attack could take form of a direct attack against an NGO office, vehicle, or individual. The cross-border security group – in which Concern is a member – provides a forum for Concern staff at different levels in the organizational structure to analyze security risks and to update the security management plan to reflect evolving risk. Security groups are used for tracking staff movements within Syria and for dissemination of information about security incidents to all staff. Should Concern receive credible evidence that its staff, volunteers, or beneficiaries will be targeted, all stakeholders will be informed and all program activities will be immediately suspended until Concern determines the risk has adequately diminished. Exploitation of beneficiaries by Concern StaffProbability: Moderate / Severity: ModerateConcern has developed online tools for sharing of information, photos and mapping of project activities and all project activities are monitored by an independent monitoring and evaluation team (M&E). Projectparticipants are informed about Concerns community response mechanism (CRM) to prevent any bias intargeting. Careful attention is given to the gender, ethnicity and the home town of new Concern staff to ensure a diverse team and to prevent bias. The behaviour of Concern staff is governed by Concern's Project Participant Protection Policy (P4) which explains the importance of Concern's independence and neutrality. Any reported instance of exploitation by Concern staff would be considered a sensitive and serious complaint and would be directed directly to the County Director to start a formal investigation.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk- There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS or FSA groups supported by Turkey, especially in border districts such as Manbij and Tabqa. In 2018, peaking at the close of the year, Turkey has taken a much more aggressive stance against areas controlled by the self-administration in North East Syria and is threatening to hold a third military campaign there to establish a buffer zone along the length of the border. There is also the risk that the fragile coalition of parties and groups that form the Syrian Democratic Council could unravel as a result of internal differences and/or external pressure, which would weaken the local authorities and likely spell increased conflict, criminality and insecurity. This situation might prevent Concern working in several areas in NES, could lead to the evacuation of international staff, scaling down to emergency assistance or closing down of some or all operations depending on the severity and scale of the conflict. - Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioriation in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. In these instances, partial or complete access to Der Ez Zor - where a significant proportion of this Action's activities are targeted, would be prevented. AssumptionsThe security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling Concern to establish a field office in Basira in the first quarter of 2019 and to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Tabqa. - Concern will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating MesuresConcern has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict - particularly in the wake of the likely US troop withdrawl in early-mid 2019. Concern's response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localised insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, Concern would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly dispaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), Concern would remotely manage staff and the project from its support office in Dohuk and using its remote-management team based in Turkey
