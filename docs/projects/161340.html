Project Summary

In 2020-2021, International Medical Corps aims to reduce the mortality and morbidity of people affected by the conflict and other vulnerable people such as IPDs and host community by improving access to quality health care services and lifesaving services to vulnerable people including IDPs and host community through fix and outreach activities by emphasis on primary health care including provision of outpatient consultation and drugs dispensary, immunization, comprehensive reproductive health service, laboratory test, response to disease outbreaks through disease surveillance intensification and timely response to confirmed epidemic-prone disease outbreaks and secondary health care including emergency surgical especially CEMON, management of chronic disease and referral system in Wau, Malakal and Juba PoC, Twic, Panyijar and canal Pigi
In Juba, IMC will continue support to IDPs within their facility in complementary other existing project support by other donor such as OFDA and UNFPA, especially on emergency obstetrical complications in Wau and Juba POC.
In addition, IMC will conduct mobile clinic with a team of Clinical officer, midwifes, nurse, CHW, doctor and nutritionists to provide outreach health services to people located to hard to reach areas including Twic, Bagarri, Malakal town and Nyal.
IMC will support the implementation of Boma health initiative to support community health activities including screening of patients, defaulter tracing for ANC, PNC, immunization education and awareness and ensure early referrals to health facilities. Printing of IEC materials and provisions of the ICCM and supplies for community awareness and management of diarrhea, malaria and ARI in the community will be conducted by IMC.
With this project IMC will improve the Immunization services coverage as most of the areas has low coverage due to unavailability vaccines and lack of cold chain facilities. Vaccinators will be identified, trained and equipped to conduct outreach immunization and referral for defaulter tracing.
IMC will hardly work to increase the indicator related to RH such as ANC, health facility delivery and FP by emphasizing sensitization for women and girls of the importance of utilization of RH services, as well as by engaging men and community leader on the availability of RH services and the advantages of the utilization of RH services.
IMC will ensure quality of services by mitigating medical commodities and equipment shortage due to weak supply chain, interruption of medical supplies and nutrition supplies is affecting the quality of preventive and curative services in all locations.
IMC will preposition essential to and train health workers on rational use of drugs and proper drugs prescriptions.
IMC will address the high stigmatization of mental illness at the community level which affects case detection and management. As actually its observed in some of selected areas such as Malakal a increment number of suicides
Disease surveillance and response in the selected areas will be strength and Rapid response teams will be established ready to respond to any outbreak especially in remote locations such as Twic and Panyijar.

1 - Needs

In South Sudan, the conflict has heightened the chronic vulnerability of the population and the MoH does not have the capacity to meet basic needs of all affected populations. Juba POC, Canal/Pigi Panyijiar, Malakal, Twic, and Wau are areas identified as highly in need of basic primary health care services. Many roads are impassible especially during rainy season, which seriously constrains health care delivery efforts. If the peace process goes well and a transition government is established, the POCs will gradually disappear, and most IDPs will be expected to return to their original place, thus the existing health facilities may not have enough capacity to serve all. Primary Healthcare: malaria, ARI and AWD continue to account for the highest proportion of morbidities among IDPs and host communities, especially affecting children under the age of 5. Primary health care services offered are minimal as well as community health education and awareness to reduce morbidity and ensure early referrals to health facilities. Immunization services are low and absent in some locations due to unavailability of vaccines and lack of cold chain facilities. Live saving assistance for the most vulnerable, including women, under five children, people with disability and people living with HIV/AIDS/TB is minimal. All these factors also contribute to the acute malnutrition situation with prevalence above the emergency threshold (GAM above 15%) in areas such as Malakal, and Wau. Secondary Healthcare: Juba and Malakal are the only locations where IMC offers secondary health care services within the PoCs and Malakal teaching hospital, providing emergency surgical interventions and CEmONC. The needs are high in terms of human resources, provision of supplies and equipment to ensure proper service provision, and to ensure strengthening of a reliable and strong referral system for remote areas to access CEMONC. Reproductive health: data collected on all health facilities/MMUs offering basic to comprehensive reproductive, maternal and neonatal health care are showing that access and utilization of essential reproductive healthcare services (including indicators for ANC; health facility deliveries assisted by skilled personnel; PNC;CMR and timely STIs prevention/treatments especially for sexual violence survivors) is low due to insecurity, lack of awareness on the importance of utilization of RH services as well as lack of skilled RH providers in all selected locations. There is a need to reinforce sensitization for women and girls of the importance of utilization of RH services, as well as for men and community leaders. Mental health: it is observed an increase in the number of new cases of MH conditions specifically epilepsy, psychosis and acute stress, especially in Malakal PoC. Lack of adequate and trained medical personnel on mental health conditions is a challenge in all locations. High stigmatization of mental illness at the community level is affecting case detection and management. Disease surveillance and response: Lack of material resources for prompt response to emergencies is a challenge. Rapid response teams are functional and operational only in Wau and Malakal. Transportation of samples for suspected epidemic prone diseases to the national reference laboratory is a challenge especially in remote locations such as Twic and Panyijar. Medical commodities and equipment: most areas experience shortage and out of stock in essential drugs due to weak supply chain, interruption of medical supplies and nutrition supplies, which is affecting the quality of preventive and curative services in all locations. There is a lack of propositioned essential supplies for prompt response to emergencies. Insecurity and lack of transportation to remote locations is also affecting the availability of supplies at filed level. In addition, there is a lack of health workers properly trained on rational use of drugs and proper drugs prescriptions

2 - Activities

The activities will be implemented at fix points (PHCC, PHCU) together with outreach activities for HTR areas through mobile clinics following the South Sudan BPHNS including: Provision of Child health services including: immunization for preventable diseases through routine and outreach immunization; provision of health services through integrated management of neonatal and childhood illnesses (IMNCI) and rapid response and referral for complicated cases; disease prevention and LLITNs distribution; vitamin A supplementation; Health awareness and education. Provision of Basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric and newborn care: through trained and qualify health workers including midwife and nurses to provide a package of antenatal care; skilled deliveries; postnatal care of mothers and newborns; family planning; PMTCT/HIV; improve referral system for CEMONC; health awareness and education will also be conducted. Gender-based violence services: including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. Procurement and distribution of essential medicines, equipment and supplies such as essential medical kits including Cholera Kit, Trauma Kit, PEP Kit, etc. as needed in target locations. MHPSS: capacity building of health workers; services provisions for both pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatment; community follow up. WASH in the facility: improve latrines, waste management, IPC, placenta pits, incinerators; provision of IPC materials including bucket, disinfectant, liquid soap, etc. Emergency preparedness and response: Building the capacity of health workers to detect, assess and respond to health needs in the event of emergencies and disease outbreaks; procure and distribute outbreak commodities; distribution of SoPs; establishment of an Emergency Response Team; develop contingency plan. Disease surveillance and outbreak response: In line with the nationwide Integrated Disease Surveillance Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert and Response (EWARS) systems. In addition, Epidemic disease prevention and control measures. Capacity building of health workers In-service and on job training on RH including Focused ANC, PNC, FP, PMTCT/HIV, CMR and PAC, Standard treatment guidelines, Immunization and MHPSS. Regular supportive supervision and monitoring of activities to improve the quality of health services provided through on job training, coaching and mentoring. Referral system; ensure availability of an ambulance to support referrals from the community to health facility and from PHCU/PHCC to the hospital especially for children and pregnant with emergency obstetrical complication. IEC production and dissemination for community awareness

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IMC will consult entire community and local authorities in all stages of the program through beneficiary community consultation and feedback assessment. IMC will double its efforts towards people-centred approach to mainstream Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), Gender and Age considerations into programming and improve programme responsiveness to evolving and varied needs of the affected population. Through this, IMC will also establish Quality Accountability of the affected population ( QAAP) committees, to ensure complaint mechanism that is confidential, accessible and responsive. IMC will facilitate QAAP committees to undertake quarterly meeting to review complaints from the community, actions taken and way forward. QAAP committees is deemed best approach for illiterate people who cannot read and write. . IMC will train all staff, Community Nutrition Volunteers and Mother support groups on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ( PSEA). IMC will ensure to have a joint monitoring and evaluation system, that reflects how well the project tracked and responded to beneficiaries' complaintsIMC ensures to have a joint monitoring and evaluation system, to sustain the approach and engaged the process to QAAP committees, County Health Directors, Community Leaders, Beneficiaries and religious leaders.
