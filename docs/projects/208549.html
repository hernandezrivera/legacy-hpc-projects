Project Summary

Street Child proposes a holistic, child-centered and survivor-centered comprehensive Mental Health and Psychosocial support to address the outlined gaps within the current response, including reintegration of CAACs and addressing urgent needs of vulnerable UASC and children who are survivors or at risk of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) in coordination with States Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development across the Project targeted locations. Services will also be made available to WAAC in the community, primarily via external referrals but also through dedicated advocacy and rights-affirming activities targeting women/girls, men/boys, community and religious leaders, community based networks, and other key stakeholders to support in strengthening community/government systems and the protective environment for reintegrated people at the community level.
The package impact will be maximized through multi-sectoral referrals and complementary approaches with other protection actors, and the use of proven internal coordination mechanisms which facilitates rapid support for children requiring MHPSS, FTR, case management and livelihood support (for instance, referral to SMWASD on alternative care and social/economic reintegration arrangements for CAAC and UASC). Safe Spaces including child-friendly spaces within this project will serve as community protection hubs, hosting structured activities to support referrals, group healing, life skills, build the spirit of individual and group advocacy, and care pathway integration, etc, while community-based structures will receive capacity strengthening to maintain integrated systems in the future.
We will also work with men and boys in target communities, in tailoring GBViE prevention and response in their communities.
The program design is intentionally aligned to the UNICEF and CP AoR 2023 strategic plan/HRP, building on pillars of 1) access to services for children at risk, 2) promotion of positive social norms and protective environments through engagement with caregivers to protect women and children from abuse, 3) strengthening domestic capacities of key local stakeholders, 4) supporting ongoing localization initiatives. In addition to CP AoR strategic priorities, the project will combine technical guidelines on reintegration, alternative care pathways and expanding scope of alternative options in line with cultural norms as well as alignment with Handover Protocol.
This project will maximize the impact of its holistic response package for the targeted children with extensive opportunity for cross-referral and complementary approaches to protection programming. Proven internal coordination mechanisms will be implemented to facilitate rapid support for children requiring referral across MHPSS, FTR, case management and livelihood support teams.
Street Child will target duty bearers at all levels, such as the MWASD and community religious and traditional leaders as well as peer organizations within the national Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR), with evidence-based advocacy and capacity strengthening sessions that reinforce positive reintegration strategies and activities. Advocacy and dialogue to communities and community leaders, coupled with providing access to critical psychosocial protective services and education/livelihood activities, will serve to strengthen the overall protective environment within communities that are capacitated on key child rights points and ensure successful and sustainable reintegration of vulnerable children

1 - Needs

Child Protection needs are likely to increase in 2024, due to persistent conflict and insecurity, population movement, displacement, camp closure and the continued influx of people from NSAG-controlled areas, and other socio-economic conditions. The well-being of children is threatened by multiple child protection risks/needs inclusive of early/child marriage, family separation, physical and sexual violence, psycho-social distress, recruitment by non-state armed groups, limited access to food, limited access to education, andinadequate parental care, particularly for children with disabilities. Also, the current economic inflation has further exposed children to risk, as care givers do not have enough resources to provide the basic needs of the children leading to neglect and harmful child labour. Girls, especially adolescent girls, will be at risk of early child marriage and sexual violence continues to significantly affect the physical, mental, emotional, and social well-being of children, and social norms affecting children’s rights.

2 - Activities

The program design is intentionally aligned to the UNICEF and CP AoR 2023 strategic plan/HRP, building on pillars of 1) access to services for children at risk, 2) promotion of positive social norms and protective environments through engagement with caregivers to protect women and children from abuse, 3) strengthening domestic capacities of key local stakeholders, 4) supporting ongoing localization initiatives. In addition to CP AoR strategic priorities, the project will combine technical guidelines on reintegration, alternative care pathways and expanding scope of alternative options in line with cultural norms as well as alignment with Handover Protocol. It will be funded by UNICEF

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will mainstream child protection and GBV prevention activities into all activities, in line with both organizations’ child-focused status. Safeguarding and protection risk assessments will be developed jointly and will be regularly reviewed and updated, in order to provide a best possible protective environment within the communities of implementation. Such assessments will, for instance, consider the varied protection concerns of women, men, boys, girls, elderly people, people with disabilities, survivors of protection breaches or GBV, former CAAGs, and other diverse or intersectional populations with additional protection needs. Education and health stakeholders will be encouraged to join CBCPC structures and meetings, in order to build linkages between community protection mechanisms and adjacent services

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Diverse community members and leaders including women, men, girls, boys, and people with disabilities will be engaged from start-up to close-out through comprehensive sensitization activities that ensure their understanding and empowerment of project activities and will be supported to propose mechanisms that reflect their own needs. For instance, communities with strong mobile network signal and access to mobile phones may prefer to primarily use toll-free lines to register complaints; other communities with poor coverage might prefer either written or pictorial IEC and safeguarding materials in different local languages including Hausa, Kanuri, and Fulfulde
