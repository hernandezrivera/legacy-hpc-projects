Project Summary

Kismaayo district has been devastated by civil conflict, floods, famine and the prolonged presence of Al Shabaab, until October 2012 which now turned to be main hub for IDPs and Returnees from Dadaab Refugee Camps in Kenya with an estimate population of 243,043 people. The IDP population in Jubaland is estimated to be 135,000 IDPs (UNHCR total IDPs per region report, September 2014). Kismaayo is believed to have the largest IDP population in the region mostly located in Dalxiiska, Central, Galbet and Fanole areas. Recent IDPs profiling conducted from 13th to 17th January 2019 indicated that there are 9863 IDPs households in Kismaayo towns in 133 settlements.50% of the IDP and vulnerable host community’s households in Afmadow,Badhaade ,Kismayo and surrounding liberated access to unsafe water from unprotected and home based shallow wells. 65% of IDP heads of households in Galbeet sites interviewed are aware that their drinking water needs to be treated while 35% do not consider water treatment as important. The barriers that deter water treatment are costs, availability and cultural believes. There is weak government regulatory framework which affects water quality and tariffs. Despite the cost getting safe water entails both longer time and additional transport costs. All the Liberated villages within a radius of 60km get safe drinking water from the private companies in Kismayo town.
Open defecation is relatively widespread in Kismayo IDPs and surrounding villages with 30% of households indicating no latrine or filled latrines (full). 40% of household heads in Galbeet sites do not know the cause of diarrhea while 15% believe in prayer to prevent diarrhea. This indicates inadequate awareness of safe hygiene practices. 0% of the IDP households in Galbeet has hand washing facility or dedicated station while 100% of the people use only water for hand washing before eating and after defecation. This entails lack of awareness on the use of soap for hand washing.All the target population including 7,600 individuals from Afmadow and Badhaade District will benefit from outreach hygiene promotion will have knowledge and means to practice good hygiene and water treatment and transmission of AWD/Cholera is disrupted and brought under control. Villages targeted in Badhaade District are Kudhaa,Burgabo,Madawo and Raskamboni while villages targeted in Afmadow district are haya,qoqani,dhobley and Deglema village.

Mitigation Measures

1.Regarding Priorities, safety & dignity and avoidance of harm:Intersom will ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe.Intersom will locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 meters from settlement.2.Intersom will promote equitable and impartial access for all.Intersom will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming.Intersom will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups (refer to Vulnerable Categories guidance note).Intersom will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered.Intersom will ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services and Install lights near communal facilities. If lighting is not possible, consider coordinating with NFI sectorfor the provision of solar lamps / torches for each household and subsequently avoid placing facilities or distribution points near military installations or threats such as attacks stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Weapon bearers should not be present inside distribution points.Intersom will introduce accompaniment arrangements to reduce threats to the safety and dignity of women and girls e.g. fetching water in groups.Regarding Ensure accountability to affected populations:Intersom will be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures and processes that affect them.Intersom will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in hygienekits, feedback box at WASH facilities, phone lines and set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff.Intersom will respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. 4.Strengthen participation and empowerment :Intersom will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to WASH needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics.Intersom management will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific WASH requirements and preferences for location and design of WASH facilities. Topics to discuss include privacy - use of lockable doors and ‘walls’ to provide sufficient/safe cover; access for persons with disabilities.Intersom Considering that most often women and children collect water, talk to them directly about safety of the location of and routes to the water collection point.Intersom will engage the community and committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.Build community capacities to maintain WASH structures and ensure sustainable provision of WASHservices eg. establish WASH committees, provide tools for minor repairs to infrastructureIntersom will coordinate with civil society specializing in working with persons with impaired mobility or disabilities to help identify such individuals and use them as a resource to improve service delivery, train staff and for the referral of cases.

Please provide further explanation

The project will ensure participation of female groups in all aspects of the project. Gender equality will be considered in the staffing of the project specially in the hygiene promotion.Intersom will also ensure disability inclusion by engaging people living with disability in consultation, monitoring and implementation of the project.Intersom will engage disability representative or leader within communities in ensuring their rights InterSOM will preserve 50% for female while recruiting CHP to scale up project participation by girls and women since they constitute largest population in IDP set ups. Equal opportunity will be given both to men and women during training and while establishing WASHCOM with 50% women representation.This project will ensure sustainable solutions are chosen to be implemented to provide safe drinking water and raise awareness on open defecation. Therefore environmental and gender friendly technology option is chosen for water lifting from shallow wells. Emphasis will be put on capacity building to enhance technical skills of hand pump operator and hygiene promotion team to ensure sustainability of hand pump facilities. The project will also work closely with existing structures i.e. Local authorities, community leaders and Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) to ensure support from governance structures to promote ownership and future sustainability. Careful selection and prioritization of target groups was made to enhance equity. Somali Bantus and Bajuni minority clans who are marginalized and socially excluded and considered outside the Somali traditional will be given priority and this project represent the first intervention to address equity service delivery in lower Juba. People living with disability will also be given higher priority. Rural CLTS will target areas inhabited by minority and marginalized communities who are mainly farmers and static in residence will be targeted. During identification for WASH supplies including hygiene kits, households with special need, those with many children under five years, female headed households, families with physically challenged household member (disabilities) will be given special consideration.
