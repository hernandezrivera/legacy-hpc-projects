Project Summary

REACH proposes a mixed-migration study to provide rapid in-depth assessments on emerging thematics in relation to mixed migration in Libya. The aim of these rapid qualitative studies will be to inform the wider humanitarian community on emerging trends, which are identified on the basis of continuous secondary data monitoring, inclunding of IOM DTM Migrant profiling.
More specifically, the result envisioned is to provide UNHCR and other migration stakeholders with evidence-based outputs to inform migration related programming through rapid in depth qualitative assessments, which topics will be decided during the project's timeframe to ensure that assessments respond to emerging information gaps. REACH will also seek to collect in-depth data protection risks faced by migrants and refugees throughout their journey and the source and level of information about the journey prior to embarking on the journey.

Needs

Refugees and migrants are among the most vulnerable population groups in Libya. As many of them are hidden and difficult to reach out to, this assessment will aim to increase evidence base and information of humanitarian actors to support refugees and migrants in Libya. While other data collection actors, such as IOM provide much needed quantiative evidence, including the number and locations of refugees and migrants in Libya, this project aims to provide more qualitative information to inform the humanitarian community's understanding of mixed migration dynamics in the country more broadly. This is crucial to support a more context-sensitive humanitarian response to refugees and migrants in Libya. Topics overall will aim to inform the whole humantarian response, but could, in some instances, be more geared towards some sectors.

Activities

Assessments through primary data field collection with key informants and refugees and migrants across Libya. Qualitative assessments with data collection across the country to capture the needs of as many refugees and migrants, in as many locations, as possible. With a total of 5 rapid assessments, in each round 120 respondents will be interviewed within the course of 1-2 weeks, ensuring that different views are captured through in depth qualitative data collection tools. Questionnaires will be analysed used qualtiative data analysis software with each assessment resulting in an accessible assessment report available publically and presented in the various fora and working groups.
