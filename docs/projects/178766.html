Project Summary

In support of the Education Sector UNICEF through this project in 2021, UNICEF will further support its No Lost Generation Agenda by empowering vulnerable adolescents and youth (10-24 years), girls and boys, to have access to age and gender sensitive, learning and skills building opportunities for improved learning, empowerment and employability.
UNICEF will build on strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most Vulnerable adolescents to strengthen skills development, and employability (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches) through friendly spaces and mobile teams.
Before, during, and after the implementation of the project UNICEF will work on evidence generation, Documentation & Innovation to support adolescent’s empowerment and development.
UNICEF will make sure the adolescents and youth have access to safe, appropriate and equitable opportunities to take part both in decision-making and in the processes and activities right across the programme cycle. Adolescents and youth will have equal access to information on rights and how to exercise them, taking into consideration the specific context and preferences of different groups of people. UNICEF will secure and appropriate means for affected populations to provide feedback and to complain about their experiences and perspectives on the programme , including on sensitive issues. These mechanisms will ensure that information is regularly collected, analyzed, integrated into decision-making and acted on, and that the results are communicated back to people in a meaningful dialogue.
UNICEF will build on strategic partnerships, integration and at scale programming focusing on the most Vulnerable adolescents to strengthen skills development, employability and meaningfully engagement (factoring COVID-19 focused approaches)
This will be through the below focus areas:
- Adolescents have access to age and gender sensitive, skills building and learning opportunities for improved learning outcomes, empowerment and employability.
- Adolescents and youth meaningful engagement and systemized participation.
- Evidence generation, Documentation & Innovation to support adolescent’s empowerment and development.
- Programmatic Integration and collaboration across programme areas:
• Skills development and employability through non formal education platforms (ILCs)- Education, CP, C4D, SP
• Meaningful engagement of adolescents in their communities (. i.e. in RCCE, Back to school campaigns, Hygiene promotion etc.)- All sectors in UNICEF
• Maximize adolescent girls and boys well-being (. i.e. MHH, Nutrition supplementation, Health related LSBE etc.) Health & Nutrition, WASH, C4D)
• Evidence generation-SP, PME
• Strategic partnerships and interagency collaboration & coordination to leverage resources and maximize scalability for Skills development and employability –Academia, Private sector, UN agencies, YP

4 - Needs

The UN population in 2019 estimates show that there are 4.6 million adolescents in Syria, representing 22.5 per cent of the total population. The unemployment rate among adolescents as per the HNO 2021 is estimated to be close to 75 per cent and is significantly higher among girls. With limited opportunities, and uncertain about what the future has in store, adolescents have grown increasingly disillusioned, frustrated and disempowered. Adolescent boys are at a substantial risk of being exploited and involved in child labour. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage and other forms of exploitation and gender-based violence, including sexual violence. Adolescents have been drastically affected by the crisis. Security and peace inside Syria are the strongest and the most heartfelt aspirations by adolescents. They have expressed fear and uncertainty of the future, with some of their key concerns being limited access to quality education and opportunities for safe, dignified livelihoods, issues related to documentation and dangerous onward movement. Many adolescents are reported to be adopting risky coping strategies, such as drug use, or illegal work.Income and multi-dimensional child poverty, aggravated years of conflict and hyperinflations, is a serious underlying factor constraining adolescents from realising their potential and rights. Poverty limits educational attainment and skills acquisition is related to poor health and nutrition and a risky poor health outcome, preventing adolescents from realizing their rights. Income poverty rate increased over the last nine years; it is higher among IDPs and varies by area of the country. Adolescent girls are much less likely to hold wage or salaried Jobs

5 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will build on its comparative advantage and experience since 2009 in of skills-development programmes for adolescents in Syria. This experience encompasses successful partnerships with line ministries and CSO. The planned interventions are based on experiences from prior programming. UNICEF’s comparative advantage in the proposed areas, lessons learned, as well as global UNICEF priorities and national priorities . UNICEF will focus on strengthening and improving access to skills-development programming that is comprehensive; reaching the most vulnerable adolescents especially those not in Education, Employment and Training (NEET). UNICEF will address the lack of relevant and contextualized non-formal education which causes poor learning outcomes; promote alternative pathways, learning beyond schools and implement 21st century skills-development programming in line with the UNICEF MENARO Life Skills and Citizenship Education (LSCE) framework. UNICEF will support development of foundational, transferrable, and technical skills (e.g. entrepreneurship) programming to prepare adolescents better for their future, including professional, technical and entrepreneurial training, for work and life.ADAP will work with education programme on: 1. promoting learning and teaching, aimed at the acquisition of skills, to prepare adolescents and youth better for future challenges and contribute to the acquisition of foundational and technical skills, 2. creating open systems, multiple pathways that provide learning opportunities for all, beyond schooling, to meet the needs of individual learners.3. ) ) strengthening local governance and accountability for results, and regulations necessary to ensure open learning systems.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Adolescent and youth empowerment may lead to greater exposure for young people causing them unintended harm and also motivate youth to unintentionally put themselves in danger while addressing sensitive issues; Due to social norms, girls’ parents may not allow them to attend as planned; Sport/cultural/civic engagement activities. To mitigate such possible risks to young people, the principle of Do No Harm must be emphasized throughout all stages of programming. Working with parents will also be critical to ensure equitable participation and rights of boys and girls. As the project will address actual needs of young people and voices of young people will be adhered to, the principle of Do Good aims to support a process whereby young girls and boys are able to share their views freely on interventions relevant to their needs.UNICEF will assess, identify and respond to protection risks and vulnerability of adolescents and youth by ensuring safe and reliable mechanisms are in place to access assistance and to raise concerns about sexual exploitation and abuse issues, and other protection issues such as genderbasedviolence and child safeguarding. UNICEF will work with and through local actors, first responders and national coordination mechanisms, and increase support to local actors in order to build their capacity before, during and after an event, crisis or development intervention
