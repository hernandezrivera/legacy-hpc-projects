Project Summary

The project aims to address the WASH needs of a targeted total of 69,500 conflict affected populations across Borno and Adamawa States. The overall project objective is to ensure that conflict affected have reduced exposure to WASH related illnesses through improved access to potable drinking water, appropriate/dignified sanitation facilities and strengthened hygiene practices. Prioritization of interventions will be based on the needs identified to and tailored to the needs for the disabled, elderly, different age groups and displacement status. The project will address the needs for the internally displaced persons (newly displaced and protracted crisis), returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities. The targeted communities will contribute to the implementation of the project through further needs assessments, contributing to the design, and participation during project monitoring and giving feedback during the project surveys and post distribution monitoring. The project will also be tailored to address the emerging and re-emerging epidemics, related to provision of adequate water, sanitation and hygiene services. This will include responding to such outbreaks such as cholera, the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and other related epidemics. More specially, the project activities will include the following;Rehabilitation/upgrading and construction water points. This includes optimization,solarisation, replacing pumps to optimize the capacity of the boreholes, improvement of the water collection points, replacement of taps and old pipes.Water treatment and water quality monitoring. This includes introduction of inline water chlorination, monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels both at the point of collection and at the household level. Samples will be collected for bacteriological analysis from households that will have zero free residual chlorine levels.Emergency water distribution/treatment via water trucking (where all other water supply options are not available). This activity shall be limited to a period of not more than 3months. Establishment and capacity building of water management committees. Water committees will be equipped with tool boxes in order to undertake minor works Construction of new latrines and bathing shelters. 1 - Needs

Entering into the 11th year of the conflict, Northeast Nigeria is still characterised by both immediate and secondary forms of displacement due to clashes and violence from non-state armed groups and the Government of Nigeria’s (GoN) counterinsurgency initiatives. The security situation has continued to deteriorate, while the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance increased from 7.7 million at the start of 2020 to 10.6 million people after the spread of Covid-19 to Northeast Nigeria. However harsh weather conditions causing flooding and fire outbreaks also represent a reason for displacement. In addition, the scale of needs dramatically increased due to COVID-19, further exacerbating existing challenges including climate change, insecurity, displacement, human right violations and access restrictions. Across the targeted areas of under this intervention in Maiduguri and Jere in Borno state and Yola North, Yola South, Girei, Mubi in Adamawa state, emergency and makeshift shelters are the shelter options for the majority of IDPs in host communities and IDP settlements. Currently in Northeast Nigeria there are 1.3 million people in need for shelter and NFI assistance as per the 2021 HRP. Shelters constructed in informal settlements have been predominantly temporary and are emergency shelters not meant for long-term use. Outside of camps, semi-permanent shelters used by displaced people are dilapidated. Nearly half a million homes and 700 public buildings have been destroyed by the conflict, along with 1,200 schools, nearly 800 health facilities, and 1,600 water supply sources (various assessment reports). According to 2021 HNO, up to 758,000 IDPs, returnees and host communities in BAY states are without adequate shelters using makeshift shelters, sharing accommodations with relatives and friends, living in partially damaged shelters, in public buildings and collective centres. The government plan for camp closure may further deteriorate situation as it may lead to the secondary and even tertiary displacement of IDP population. Similarly, n Maiduguri and Jere in Borno state and Yola North, Yola South, Girei, Mubi in Adamawa state access to WASH services continues to be a challenge with gaps in services delivery and growing needs across the sector. According to the HRP 2021, 2.8 M individials lack access to WASH services and in severe need of water, sanitation and are subject to cholera, malaria and other water brone diseases. Borno State has majorly been affected by the ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria, hosting 81% of the 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the country. Maiduguri and Jere Local Government Areas (LGAs) host an estimated 19% and 18% of the IDPs in Borno respectively. Moreover, communicable diseases are highly prevalent, with IDPs and other vulnerable groups at high risk due to overcrowded conditions and limited services. Additionally, in Maiduguri and Jere, 47% of individuals surveyed reported that they did not have access to 15 liters of water per day while an average of 30 individuals in Maiduguri and 45 individuals in Jere are reported to be sharing the same latrines. In addition, the WASH gaps conducted by the WASH sector in July 2021 indicates the need for hygiene promotion, provision of more sanitation facilities to enhance access to sanitation, provision of additional water to reach the SPHERE minimum standards of 15 liters per person per day. Additionally, the gap matrix indicates the need to provide of hygiene items across the four LGAs selected for response in Adamawa state. Provision of adequate WASH services, more specifically regular handwashing beyond the 5 critical moments is one of the key measures recommended towards prevention of COVID 19 pandemic. So far, The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and inflation has deepened vulnerabilities.

2 - Activities

Emergency response: This response focuses on any unforeseen natural disasters, displacements or epidemics and will be implemented jointly with the Rapid ResponseMechanism (RRM). The aim is to provide immediate assistance to the affected populations or basic survival before the conventional WASH response. The response will be closely coordinated through the RRM working group. These specic emergency response includes emergency water trucking, which will only be limited to a period of no more than three months, while looking for other more sustainable water supply options.Response to outbreaks including disinfections, water point treatment, distribution of kits, awareness raising and coordination, provision of communal latrines and rehabilitation/dislodgement of existing ones, distribution of essential NFI hygiene kits that will includes soaps, detergents, water collection containers, menstrual hygiene items amongst other items. WASH response to protracted crisis: The WASH response will focus on improving service coverage, building community structures and setting up more sustainable systems beyond the emergency phase. The response includes the following; Water Supply: This activity will be conducted and guided by the needs identified through rehabilitation of existing water both functional and non-functional water sources, water point treatment and drilling of new boreholes. NRC will work closely with the state, local government and federal ministries of water resources. For sustainability of the infrastructure provided, NRC will take into consideration the issue of operation and maintenance on all the water facilities provided. To address this, NRC will establish a water user committee in close consultation with the target communities within camps, urban displacements and out of camps. Before handing over the constructed water points, NRC will ensure that water quality tests are conducted, and the results meet the recommended standards for drinking water. Batch chlorination will be conducted at the water distribution points/boreholes. Water quality monitoring will be done at the point of distribution and at point of use to ensure that it is in line with the SPHERE standards.Sanitation: NRC will provide user-friendly and culturally acceptable latrines and bathrooms (shared household latrines, communal latrines or emergency latrines) that are gender-segregated to prevent and mitigate the risk of potential GBV-related incidents, including harassment against women and girls. NRC will work closely with ICLA-HLP colleagues to negotiate for land for latrine construction. All facilities constructed will be equipped with a cleaning kit and community mobilization will be done to ensure proper use and maintenance. Hygiene promotion: Implementation will target behaviour change and prevention and mitigation of epidemics. WASH will lead in the harmonization of messages to be shared, should there be need to jointly disseminate some messages along with the implementation of activities from other core competencies. To address outbreaks,and with the understanding that behaviour change is a process that might take time, the hygiene promotion teams will work directly with the target beneficiaries in denying the health risks and economic burdens associated with these practices rather than disseminating direct messages. Contextualized Information, Education andCommunication (IEC) materials addressing the key risks will be developed and adopted tore in force the messages. Hygiene Non Food Items (NFI) Distribution: Supplies to support the adoption of good hygiene practices will also be provided either through in-kind supplies or through the use of cash & vouchers, subject to proper markets assessments.The use of vouchers will be preferred over in-kind as it gives the beneficiaries a variety of choices to purchase the actual items needed and in the desired brands.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming NRC will put in place some key protectionmainstreaming elements in this project. NRC will ensure that the proposed interventionprovides safety and dignity, and prevents harm to the target beneciaries. NRCrecognises that the individual needs of women, men, girls and boys differ based on theperson’s health, age, physical and displacement status, and living conditions.Therefore consultations shall form a key component on project implementation.WASH needs are also different for women and girls compared to men and boys, andalso the general protection risks are highly dependent on gender, age, displacementstatus, ethnicity, geographical area and poverty level. NRC has developed detailedprotection guidelines and will deliver trainings to all project staff, to increase staffcapacity to identify protection issues and refer accordingly. NRC did map out theprotection risks affecting the displaced populations in some locations in Borno willcontinuously monitor the information throughout the project life. In WASH, some of thecommon mitigation measures will arise as a result of siting and appropriateness ofthe WASH facilities. NRC will ensure that r women and girls (mainly charged with theresponsibility of fetching water for household chores) are consulted on safety andsecurity concerns, and site the water points to ensure they are close enough to thecommunity to mitigate GBV risks for women and girls. For sanitation, NRC will markthe latrines for use, ensuring clear gender segregation, more applicable in camp-likesettings. To further improve on protection, in regards to utilisation of WASH facilities,NRC will install lockable doors in latrines and bathing shelters and equip thebeneciaries with solar lamps for use at night. The latrines will be located near theshelters to mitigate possible cases of sexual harassment. NRC will ensure that aneffective complaint and feedback mechanism is in place to receive opinions frombeneciaries and local authorities. A stronger NRC presence on-the-ground will ensurethe ability to identify quickly and refer (to other partners) protection concerns throughNRC’s eld staff. Finally, NRC has established a specic Standard Operating Procedure(SOP) developed for crowd control during distributions, which will reduce the risk ofattacks towards beneciaries. The SOP consists in limiting the number of peopleduring distributions (focusing on the most vulnerable rst) and coordination with localauthorities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback from the targeted beneciaries will be implemented throughout the project,addressing the needs for the vulnerable communities. In order to collect feedback,beneciaries shall be involved at different stages of the project, in which feedback willbe collected. It includes the following; - Involving the target communities in the initialneeds assessments. The beneciaries will give feedback to the project design, this willensure that the program delivery is based on the actual needs assessed andcontributions from the community. - Establishment of a community feedbackmechanism. This will be done by conducting scheduled meetings with thebeneciaries to receive their feedback on the project. Information provided by thebeneciaries will be used to improve the programme quality, and the complaints andissues will be addressed. - Organizing for Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), the projectaddresses the needs of different groups and through focus group discussions (FGDs)with men, women, elderly, and people living with disabilities, to collect their feedbackon the appropriateness of the project design. - Identication of focal point personsfrom within the affected communities, such as through the WASH committees whowill be the contact persons for the target beneciaries. Any complaints or concernsabout the project can be channelled through them. They will also ensure that the targetbeneciaries get their entitlements as per the project description. For example, for thewater trucking, community representatives will be required to verify the quantities ofwater deliveries, along with NRC staff and the water vendors.
