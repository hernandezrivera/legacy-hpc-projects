Project Summary

The aim of the project is to improve access to quality and lifesaving nutrition services including screening, treatment, referral and preventative measures of acute malnutrition among Internally Displaced Person (IDPs) and vulnerable crisis-affected communities. The intervention will also aim to reduce mortality and morbidity among children under two (U2), under five (U5) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) who face the highest risk of malnutrition, mortality, and morbidity, exacerbated by the current military conflict between Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Response Forces (RSF) that commenced on 15 April 2023. The conflict has had a significant impact on humanitarian needs. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis (October 2023-February 2024) indicates that 17.7 million people across the country (37 per cent of the analyzed population) have been driven into high levels of acute food insecurity, classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of those, about 4.9 million (10 per cent of the population
analyzed) are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), and almost 12.8 million people (27 per cent of the population analyzed) are in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). With 4.7 million in need of critical nutrition services, the project aims to contribute to the nutrition cluster target of 1.9 million.
The project is in line with the Nutrition Cluster’s key response focus on prioritizing the youngest and most vulnerable children with the highest mortality risk, providing a life-saving preventive and protective nutrition package, treating Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) cases with medical complications, treating SAM cases without medical complications among children under-five years, implementing non-food and food-based malnutrition prevention programs and generating evidence for planning, advocating for resources and decision-making. The project will provide a minimum package of nutrition services through the health facilities in Abyei PCA area, Gedaref, Tawilla, Dongola, Halfa, Port Sudan, El Geneina, El Jabalian, Kosti as well as other locations affected by the ongoing conflict. Being cognizant of the unprecedented conflict, IOM will continuously liaise with the Nutrition sector, the Federal Ministry of Health and relevant stakeholders in coming up with alternative mechanisms of serving the people in need. The multi-sector approach with Health, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) elements will maximize the impact of prevention of malnutrition, by providing basic and life-saving assistance to the vulnerable target population. The project covers basic and minimum package of nutrition including community outreach; outpatient treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) and SAM without medical complications under community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) protocol; referral of SAM and MAM cases to higher level of care at Stabilization Centers (SC) or hospitals. The project also provides counselling and health education at the community and facility level, such as optimal infant and young child feeding in emergency (IYCF/E) message dissemination, promotion of exclusive breastfeeding and continued breastfeeding, close monitoring and reporting of BMS code violations and critical early detection of malnutrition.

Implementing Partners

IOM has the technical and operational capacity to provide quality nutrition service in the support of, and in coordination with, UNICEF, WHO, SMoH and other sector partners. IOM will coordinate with local authorities, field office, UNDSS, to obtain timely information and mitigation measures to ensure the continuity of service provision if security risks arise and humanitarian access to target locations are hampered. Non-governmental organizations (NGOs), Civil Society Organizations (CSOs), State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and Federal Ministry of Health (FMoH). The intervention will be done through competent NGOs or CSOs, selected through a competitive bidding process and due diligence. In emergency health and nutrition response, IOM may provide direct support if there are no NGOs or CSOs which have physical presence and demonstrated a strong capacity in delivering the necessary responses.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will address the protection needs of IDPs, women, and other vulnerable groups such as, returnees and other affected communities through strengthening the capacity of the community to create a protective environment. The project will give particular emphasis to women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under the age of five and persons with disabilities who do not have adequate access to essential health and nutrition services, making them vulnerable to large scale epidemiological events and disease outbreaks. The planned interventions will focus on enhancing women’s capacities, strengthening their participation and direct interaction with communities as well as health providers. To facilitate improved access, the project will promote the availability of both male and female staff to support beneficiaries with specific needs. Additionally, negative health seeking behaviours will be addressed to promote gender equality and reproductive health rights. To prevent gender-based violence (GBV), the project will focus on women, girls, persons with disabilities as well as boys and men and address their specific needs and vulnerabilities. Additionally, the project will strengthen coordination between the supported health facilities and GBV Sub-Cluster. The supported nutrition facilities will refer cases to identified appropriate services and partners. GBV key messages will also be shared through community outreach services.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will keenly prioritize community feedback to facilitate improvement and adaptation of project activities. To ensure the continuous process of listening, responding and adapting programs based on the needs of the community with a conflict sensitivity lens, IOM will apply the use of communication with communities. The identified gender-inclusive community committees, including men, women, and persons with disability in the project planning, designing and monitoring stage. With identified communication channels such as community engagement forums, focus group discussions, face-to-face, and complaint desk mechanisms, the project will endeavor to support follow-up with communities to ensure at the very least that referrals are made to relevant partners and where possible concerns are addressed directly, to close the feedback loop. Community structures involved in the project will be strengthened to enhance two-way communication and empower communities to provide feedback. Community feedback will be documented in results monitoring frameworks, activity and field visit reports, surveys analyses and actively referred to throughout the project. The feedback received including best practices and lessons learnt will be incorporated during regular project review exercises, to improve the quality of the ongoing work, adapt to emerging needs and changes, or improve the planning of future programming.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The project will embrace accountability to affected populations (AAP), including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected IDPs and local communities to the implementation of the interventions. Efforts will be made to engage in particular women including PLWs, children, and youth. The project will empower these groups by encouraging direct participation in community-based health awareness activities. As part of capacity development, community health workers (CHWs) will be trained as mentors and key messengers addressing negative practices as well as protection threats such as harassment, sexual exploitation, and abuse, targeted at women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Accountability will also be promoted through the capacity development of CHWs and health providers as well as NGOs/CSOs in the health sectors as duty bearers to inform Cluster partners in health, nutrition, and protection if protection threats were reported. Applying IOM's experiences enhancing the protection of migrants through the provision of direct assistance services, IOM will provide direct assistance through psychosocial support, counselling, and medical assistance to survivors in coordination with the sector partners as needed. Through previous interventions, IOM and NPO established trust and linkage with the community in the camp. IOM will continue to coordinate with local authorities, field office, UNDSS, to obtain timely information and mitigation measures to ensure business continuity and service provision if security risks arise and humanitarian access to the camps and gathering points is hampered. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines from IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV in crises IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to handle a GBV disclosure and make safe referrals through existing referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
