Project Summary

According to the mini HRP, SEMA and through its supported health facilities (2 general hospitals, 3 COVID -19 isolation hospitals, 5 CEmONC facilities and 12 PHC (4 are mobile) will ensure access to quality, lifesaving and life-sustaining health services across all levels of the health system - community, primary, secondary and tertiary. Essential services comprise surgical and trauma services, reproductive health, child health – including immunization and nutrition services, mental health, diagnostic services, and specialized care for persons with disability, those with communicable and non-communicable diseases, and children with severe acute malnutrition with complications.
Through supporting the mentioned listed health facilities, SEMA aims to ensure provision of primary health care services according to the WHO essential Primary Health Care Package which includes six pillars; child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS and nutrition in addition to secondary health care services including specialized reproductive health care services BEmONC and CEmONC services according to the international guidelines and UNFPA protocols; the services will be provided through ER, OPD, IPD, operations department in addition to radiology and laboratory. Moreover, neonatal, vaccination, reproductive health care including care during pregnancy and childbirth and conducting normal and C-section deliveries will be provided in particular in the maternity and RH facilities.
SEMA will provide the health facilities with the staff salaries, operational costs, medical commodities including medicines, consumables and equipment. SEMA will provide recruitment support, capacity building, and ongoing medical supervision to ensure the target HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and enhance compliance with standard treatment protocols. SEMA will continue to provide training to the HFs on various health topics, either by conducting training or securing training opportunities through inter-agency opportunities. Including management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, pharmaceutical supply management, patient data management, SRH treatment protocols, and gender-sensitivity and protection.
SEMA will collect health data from the HFs monthly and share it accordingly. All SEMA - targeted HFs are part of the EWARN network and report weekly bulletin to EWARN regularly.
SEMA health facilities will be a part of the referral pathways supported and organized by WHO and health cluster partners to ensure quality of the health services and referring the needed cases to a higher level of health care.
A network of CHWs will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities and will be linked with the health facilities to enhance the community health and synchronization of the trends, knowledge requirements with the distributed messages to the communities.
Due to COVID-19 outbreak in NWS, SEMA will participate actively in the COVID-19 Task Force which was established to follow the COVID-19 outbreak in Syria and will ensure following the protocols and guidelines according to WHO eight pillars. SEMA health facilities will be provided with the required PPEs to keep the frontline staff safe and keep the health facilities running as well. Risk communication and community engagement messages will be distributed to the beneficiaries during seeking health care services. Additionally, surveillance measures will be enhanced in order to early detection of COVID-19 cases and refer to the respective health facilities. Furthermore, IPC measures and tools will be secured and followed to ensure adherence to the WHO standards and guidelines and prevent the health facility to be as a potential source of infection. Moreover, three hospitals will be supported by SEMA to provide case management to COVID-19 cases according to WHO and COVID-19 task force protocols.

4 - Needs

According to the Health Resources Availability Monitoring System HeRAMS for NWS Q4 (Oct – Dec 2020), 547 assessed health facilities 50% (272) were reported fully functioning and 6% (32) partially functioning. None of the functioning health facilities reached the WHO benchmarks of health workers (benchmark >22 HWs for 10,000 population), the gap is about 64%. None of the communities has CHWs reached the WHO benchmark (benchmark >= 10 CHWs for 10,000 people), the gap is about 81%. Moreover, number of hospital beds per 10,000 population (inpatients & maternity), by administrative unit (benchmark >=10 beds for 10,000 population) is not reached in any of the assessed districts, the gap is about 52%.According to HNO 2021, the health situation became worsen due to the COVID-19 outbreak which stretched the capacity of the health facilities more. In 2020 saw 39,773 COVID-19 cases and over 1,300 deaths reported across Syria, but as the outbreak continues, self-reported compliance with preventive measures remains below 50 per cent while laboratory capacity and access to testing are also below recommended levels. Non-COVID outbreak risk remained present, with continued spikes of leishmaniasis cases across northern Syria and increases in proportionate morbidity for acute diarrhea in the first 34 weeks of 2020 as compared to 2019. Essential health services were disrupted as agencies and health workers alike rushed to respond to COVID-19. For example, the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) experienced temporary suspension due to COVID-19 risk.Due to the limitation of space kindly refer to the comments where the rest of needs in addition to SEMA RNA.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals exclude others. Accordingly, SEMA will follow the risk analysis and mitigation measures that conducted and developed by the cluster and sub-cluster; ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed, ensure consultation with communities related to location to understand relevant community perspectives and care is taken to ensure that the specific requirements of the most people in need are taken into account.The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-family tents, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Additionally, early marriage is also increased. SEMA will Engagement Community throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders and Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture. As well as SEMA will advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided, sensitization of women, girls, and boys will be conducted and SEMA will hire localized and gender balanced staff, the staff will be trained on GBV core concepts and referral mechanism to refer any GBV survivor upon disclosure.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:The border between Turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistance and staff.The targeted location remains free of armed conflict, and no change in the dominant forces in the region.The security situation is stable and no IDP's movement will occur.Risks and Mitigation Measures:Overlapping in the services provided: SEMA will ensure coordinating adding or removing any new services with the health cluster lead and other health cluster partners in order to avoid any duplication in the provided services. Moreover, SEMA technical team will participate actively in the health cluster meetings and share the updates when needed. SEMA will ensure that the services change will be guided by a need’s assessment conducted by SEMA MEAL team or other partners under the health cluster umbrella.Lack of the health services quality: SEMA has a health technical team who will be responsible on following up the quality of the health services provided in the health facilities as well as link the staff with the updated international guidelines and protocols. Moreover, a training plan for the staff will be set in coordination with health facilities teams and the staff needs. Complaint mechanism is already set in the health facilities and the patients can raise any issue regarding the quality of the health services and will registered and directed to the relevant department to deal with.Not renewing the Cross-border resolution:Proposed mitigation: Raise the advocacy about the importance of Cross-border operations to people in NWS.Impact Level: Severe.Likelihood: HighProject management might face difficulties in recruiting high-qualified staff.• mitigation: SEMA will communicate and share vacancies with HD.
