Project Summary

Kukawa is one of the conflict-affected arears in Borno state in which out of its 10 wards, only 4 are accessible, namely; Baga, Doro, Kauwa and Kukawa with a total population of IDPs, Returnees and Host community of 21 671, IAF is targeting 4 302 of them due to the fact that, no referral mechanism for incidents was in place in 56 per cent of sites; while child labor or forced begging was reported in six per cent (up from 5%) of sites. In 65 per cent of sites (signiﬁcantly up from 59%), assistance provided was reportedly not adequate for all those entitled and in four per cent of sites it was inadequately targeted (IOM DTM assessment, 2018).
As at August, 2018 IDP camps in Nganzai are Bakassi, Gajiram, Gasarawa and Ajiri camp. The total plan (all inclusive) to be targeted was 54,865 people, IAF will target 10,038 of them in this project with huge number of children facing a huge gap of protection as many neglected children are seen begging for alms.
Capacity building mentoring, coaching & training with specific focus on Child Protection Minimum Standards for child protection actors is planned for coming months (in this program) in order to improve technical capacities for partners, adolescents, caregivers, parents and actors/animators to strengthen capacity on child protection case management. The purpose is to develop a pool of trainers and a cadre of country‐level child protection actors with enhanced understanding and commitment to case management. This is based on the Inter‐Agency Child Protection case management guidelines and accompanying tools and training materials.
It is against this background that, IAF propose to streamline effort towards ensuring effective implementation and coordination of the psychosocial support and case management to separated, unaccompanied and at risk children in Nganzai and Kukawa LGAs.

Needs

Situation Analysis: There are three critical needs which this partnership will address: 1. Provision of psychosocial support services to children through the establishment and operation of child friendly spaces and children’s clubs 2. Identification, documentation, family tracing, reunification and alternative care to unaccompanied and separated children 3. Identification of neglected girls and boys by parent/caregivers to go through psychosocial support. IAF in its effort towards ensuring effective implementation and coordination of the psychosocial support and case management to separated, unaccompanied and at risk children in Nganzai and Kukawa LGAs will engage all relevant stakeholders including community actors.The protection and well-being of 2.6 million children & adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the north-east remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex & protracted conflict. At least 39,500 children are in need of care and protection including unaccompanied and separated children are in need of integrated case management services. Additionally, 1,405 child-headed households will also require support, alternative care & access to basic services. These children are more vulnerable to abuse, violence and exploitation if not provided with appropriate and timely protection services that include family reunification and alternative care. The conflict in North-East Nigeria has exposed threats to the mental health and well-being of children. More than 2 million girls, boys and caregivers require psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. In addition, adolescent boys and girls require basic life skills support to help them cope better with the threats and challenges occasioned by the conflict. At least 6,000 girls and boys in North East Nigeria are estimated to have been recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups; approximately 50% of these children are survivors of sexual and gender-based violence. Looking at the trend of release of children from administrative custody in the past two years , nearly 3,000 boys and girls are expected to be released in 2019 and will require socio-economic reintegration assistance. Sustained follow-up and community-based approaches to reintegration for children formerly associated with armed groups will be required to mitigate risks such as stigmatization and rejection within communities. More than 4,000 children have been abducted, killed, recruited or have been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Such vulnerable girls and boys and their caregivers require socio-economic and psychosocial assistance to recover from their experiences and to reintegrate back into society. Threats to the physical security of children has been cited as a barrier to children’s access to learning. A multi-sectoral approach will be required to address the protection risks affecting children’s access to basic services. Based on the analysis of incidents reported in security assessment reports, it is estimated that 770,000 children and caregivers are at risk of being injured or killed by unexploded devices and remnants of war. Children and their families need critical knowledge on how to mitigate the risk of injury by such devices. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but it has also negatively affected the ability of parents to appropriately care for their children At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster carers and single caregivers with several children, will require psychosocial or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their care.In Borno State, 19 of the 27 LGAs were directly affected by the conflict; a number of households have returned to their LGAs. As at Dec 2017, some households had moved back to various LGAs Including Nganzai and Kukawa LGA (UNHCR returnee registration, 2016).

Activities

1) Psychosocial support services for children: With almost all children having been directly affected, or witnessed the death of loved ones, the need for psychosocial support to children remains great. The project intends to strengthen the resilience and wellbeing of children and adolescents through community level organized structured activities conducted in safe, child friendly and stimulating environments. The purpose of these activities will be threefold: 1) to mobilize communities around the protection and psychosocial wellbeing of their children; 2) to provide opportunities for children to play, acquire life skills and receive social support; and 3) to provide psychosocial support to children who present with psychological distress and behavioral difficulties/challenges. As part of this work, IAF will identify and train and mentor animators (community volunteers) who will engage children in interactive activities that impart children with positive coping skills, enhance their self-esteem, strengthen their relational skills, and help them explore and define their identities. In addition. Child Friendly Space (CFS) activities will be rooted in/informed by the socio-cultural context in the two LGAs, with local community members contributing to and overseeing activities ran. IAF will take particular care to ensure that neglected girls and boys engage on child labour, begging of alms or supporting physically challenge parent or caregivers for begging across the street due to hardship economic situation relating to conflict will be targeted to go through psychosocial support in the child friendly space meanwhile the parent or care givers will be supported with livelihood program referrals in other to allow the child to have a normal life. The neglected child will be mentored in the centre (a male and female).In selected schools, IAF will build the capacity of house masters and prefects to set-up a peer support mechanism that is responsive to the needs of individual children within the school setting. In schools where the house structure has not yet been set up, IAF will work with the schools’ management to set this up. As all children within a given school will belong to a house, house based activities will be used to build children’s sense of individual and group identity; and to build children’s sense of competence, self-esteem and achievement. Inter-club competitions will specifically be organized around child protection and psychosocial themes; and will provide children with a structure and platform on which to engage with the schools’ management and parents on issues of concern to them. 2) Alternative care for unaccompanied/ separated Child (UASC): About 90% of the population in Nganzai and Kukawa were displaced to Maiduguri, Jere and Monguno during the height of the insurgency in 2014 - 2017. As families have started returning to the LGAs, the need to have an organization on the ground to take on case management of UASC moving back with spontaneous carers is urgent, as the risk of secondary separation and abandonment remain real. 3) Case Management: IAF will thus provide case management services to UASC through a system of identification, documentation, tracing, and reunification that is broad based and organized around the network of animators/community volunteers manning the CFS and adolescent centers; as well as the network of community organizations and groups; reducing overall cost associated with case management.
