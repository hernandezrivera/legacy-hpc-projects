Project Summary

The proposed project’s objective is to enable conflict-affected populations to meet their basic needs and provide vulnerable individuals with support when they experience harm, while promoting a transition towards early recovery and resilience-strengthening in the medium term. This strategy is aligned with the signature outcomes of the IRC Strategic Action Plan. Accountability to affected populations will be ensured through participatory approaches in project such as community-level selection committees, and IRC's active community feedback and response mechanism that allows for real-time response, follow-up and adaptations according to community needs and context.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon have increased in early 2020 compared to those planned in the 2020 HRP; COVID 19, combined the deteriorating security situation in South West (SW), and North West (NW) of Cameroon led to an additional 2.3 million people in need, with 6.2 million in need of humanitarian assistance. In the NW/SW regions approximately 2.3 million persons need humanitarian assistance (+80% compared to 2019). The deteriorating security situation is resulting in population displacement, including 11,718 people in August 2020 forced to flee their homes to seek shelter in nearby bushes and villages. The NW is particularly affected with 10,200 displaced people, and access to the NW/SW regions for humanitarian actors remains challenging due to movement restrictions and ongoing fighting. In July and August 2020, two humanitarian aid workers were killed in the NW/SW by armed groups, and lockdowns and ghost towns occur on a regular basis that hinder humanitarian actors’ capacity to implement activities. The above highlights the high level of tension resulting from political and security factors, which heightens the risk of rights violations such as targeted killing, disappearances, kidnappings, family separations, violence, and abuse in the home. Between February and August 2020, 5,086 protection incidents were registered in NW/SW . Protection service gaps are prevalent in remote locations and insecurity and ongoing conflict are reducing populations’ capacities to meet their basic needs including food needs, with the two regions projected to be in phase 3 of IPC of acute food insecurity for October 2020 to January 2021. A total of 1,473,048 people were previously estimated to be food insecure in this region (HNO 2020), comprising 34% of the population in the NW and 33% in the SW. To respond to the most urgent needs of displaced and vulnerable host communities, in-line with the HRP and IRC SAP, the IRC in Cameroon has prioritized lifesaving and life-sustaining assistance through a multi-sectoral, coordinated response to address needs in WASH, emergency basic needs, and prevention and response to protection through two priority outcomes: Outcome 1) People are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm; Outcome 2) People meet basic needs and avoid negative coping strategies.The key activities related to Outcome 1 outcome will focus on a protection continuum strategy that include : (a) protection monitoring at community and individual levels to capture protection trends and immediate life-saving needs related to human rights violations including data on specific risks faced by women and girls; (b) immediate, timely and adapted responses to urgent protection needs identified through protection monitoring which can include direct support (NFI/kits or cash depending on context and needs), referrals, PFA and PSS support including GBV response services within the community women and girl safe spaces and health facilities and (c) strengthening of local capacities, systems and services to build a sustainable protective environment and decrease protection risks through community-based protection activities, awareness-raising activities, and support to community organizations. IRC will also strengthen capacities of health facility providers in clinical management of sexual violence.Under the outcome 2 IRC will provide cash-based assistance for basic needs, support for training, and grants for Income generating activities (IGAs). IRC will also work to ensure basic needs are met by ensuring that affected populations have (a) Safe and reliable access to water, (b) Safe access to sanitation infrastructure (c) access to basic equipment such as WASH and NFI kits and (d) have the knowledge and adopt good hygiene practices which will help them to be protected from WASH related disease and COVID-19. These activities will limit protection risks associated with access to water and sanitation infrastructure.
