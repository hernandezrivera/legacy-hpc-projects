Project Summary

•Needs:
climate change (drought like conditions, abnormal rainfall and temperature, floods, sand storms, etc.) has created difficulties for the farmers, especially the most vulnerable ones in terms of access to good quality/certified planting materials and other key inputs such as water, plant protection means and fertilizers and how to adapt to the climate change. This will likely push the already vulnerable food insecure smallholders’ farmers to the very extreme considering the fact that use of good quality seed is known to contribute to about 30 percent of any improvements in overall production and productivity. However it is not the only factor that affect the production and that requires to be addressed timely. At the same time, vulnerable smallholder farming households have no resources to access information on how to adopt their farming practices with climate change using local available resources.
Farmers field schools is a very efficient tool that could be used to help farmers to improve their practices and mainstream climate smart practices.
• Intervention, Implementation of activities.
Output 1: Awareness raising of farmers’ on the risks of climate change and adoption of climate-smart agriculture (CSA) approaches and practices enhanced. Activities:
1. Selecting the appropriate CSA practices that can be adopted
2. Selecting the vulnerable farmers who will be targeted
3.Contracting with an appropriate IP capable of facilitating and delivering the required training.
5.Pilot farmer field schools (FFS) the main districts of each governorate,
6. M&E of planned activities.
Output 2: TOT for agricultural extension technicians to conduct farmers’ schools and train them on climate-smart agriculture applications, Activities:
1. Selection of extension technicians (20 - 25 in each governorate)
2. Designing a training program to raise their efficiency in CSA practices
3. Contracting with a suitable IP capable of facilitating and delivering the training.
4. Conduct the training according to the agreed curriculum for agricultural extension technicians,
5. M&E the planned activities and consider gender equality issues
Note: All the activities under each outputs will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.
* Protection Risk Analysis:
1) Beneficiaries being targeted due being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation: Inclusion of different categories of affected population based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.
2) Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; and ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.
