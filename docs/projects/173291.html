Project Summary

HEKS has been implementing a two-way communications project that produces the Camp Information Line, a twice-weekly audio program providing important information to population living in Jamtoli camp-15 as well as the same production in camp-14 & 16 and Aror Hota in the host community area of Palongkhali union since June 2020.
The narrowcasting session will provide information to live saving and resourcefulness of all diversified and vulnerable community people (women, man, girls, boys, disable). Gender norms and roles have barely changed during the pandemic; decision making power is still firmly controlled by men. This can limit the ability of women and girls to make critical lifesaving choices around COVID-19. Most women who reported not being able to make their own health or purchasing decisions said they need their husbands’ approval. For addressing the findings, the project will conduct skill developed volunteers in which above 50% are female volunteer. They are conduct as listener with women member of household for empowered them through disseminate information and feedback collection. All listener men and women will be able to show their opinions collectively or individually further or ongoing problem in their area through feedback process then HEKS/EPER will prepared a plan as per communities’ demand.
This proposed project will build on our previous programming and seeks to contribute to refugee and host community resilience, resourcefulness and social cohesion through increased access to important information. HEKS/EPER will continue to produce a 30-minute weekly program to considering DRR issue (Cyclone preparedness, land Sliding, Caution of fire) & environmental issue (Waste management, Camp cleaning, Immunization) and narrowcasting to 210 locations in Camp-14, 15 & 16. Listener groups and refugee facilitators will play these programs in the three camps, and receive immediate feedback on the program and on information gaps. Fondation Hirondelle and HEKS will produce a 30-minute weekly program that provides information to the host community about services and advice that could improve their food security, livelihoods, health, rights, risk of disaster and environment.
Considering the social Cohesion issue, HEKS/EPER will be produce 15-minute program and shared that brings host and refugee communities together to discuss common concerns against fostering social cohesion and peaceful co-existence. Lastly, a 3-day training on ethical and neutral reporting around the refugee crisis will be organized to support more responsible local reporting.

1 - Identified Needs

The humanitarian response in Cox’s Bazar has achieved some good progress in terms of the affected population being able to access information. COVID-19 prevention measures were generally understood but following was challenging for all. Women prefer door-door visits, men prefer loudspeaker announcements, host communities prefer television. Rohingya men were most likely to be consulted, as well as Rohingya women, with host community men and women the least likely to have spoken with humanitarian actors before. However, transgender persons and women are less consulted overall and reported not being able to participate in decision making. Considering the communities (Rohingya & Host) needs, the narrowcasting to information dissemination will be very effective to empowered women for decision making through diversified listener group (male, female including boys and girls) and diversified place (household, shop, woman friendly space, child friendly space). Earlier assessment showed, ninety-two percent Rohingya community feel that they have enough information to make decisions for themselves and their families. Gaps remain, however, in who can access information, which are driven by vulnerability (women, girls & disability) and geographic locations (Camp and remote hilly area). A substantial number of Rohingya refugees are still not aware of complaints and feedback systems. Language is also an issue for Rohingya refugees. The challenge for ensuring access to information for the affected people in both Rohingya and host community has been compounded from March 2020, with the outbreak of Covid 19. It is reported in Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment of 2020 that 37% of households do not have enough information on service availability since Covid 19 outbreak, as well as only 54% reported that the gave feedback/complaints without any problem/difficulty. Households not speaking English/Bangla were found to be significantly more likely to report not having had to give feedback/not having faced problems. 25% of households consulted who did not speak English and/or Bangla were found never been consulted about needs, preferences and delivery of humanitarian assistance.For host community, there was consensus that households were not facing any significant challenges receiving information and/or providing feedback; however, it was mentioned that people not owning mobile phones might not receive information, people not being allowed to gather complicates information-sharing, elderly may face more problems receiving/understanding information and providing feedback.Preferred means for receiving information for Rohingya community are site management volunteers/Rohingya-speaking volunteers, NGO staff, community leaders, imams, mahjees, women leaders, block committee, mosque miking and/or banners; and for host community people Uthan boithok perceived to be very effective by key informants; otherwise information-sharing via ward members, elderly respected citizens and village police.Contingency for Covid 19 response and natural disaster: A certain percentage of budget allocation is already justified for the contingency for responding Covid 19 pandemic and natural disaster. Align with CwC WG mandate for the Rohingya Community as well as host community this project will implement 72 hours plan.Nevertheless, the project is designed to enhance access to information, access to complaint and feedback mechanism, social cohesion within the communities both in Rohingya and host. However, as it is a need based program and if the budget is not fully funded, reaching a large number of beneficiaries will be difficult and they will be deprived. Moreover, the whole process of engagement will be hampered due to the reason.
