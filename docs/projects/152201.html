Project Summary

Activities in the area of protection will pertain to capacity building on protection
mainstreaming and establishing of referral pathways to relay protection concerns with the
sectors active in North-East Nigeria with priority consideration for the centrality of
protection. The livelihoods component will target women and girls, addressing vocational
skills and 􀂡nancial management training, granting support for a cooperative for markets
approach to maximize returns and sustainability of the household economies. The
programme will utilize Women and Girls friendly safe-spaces, and community-based
prevention approaches to address the protection concerns for women and girls, boys and girl.
Support State and field level coordination initiatives and development of protocols/guidelines with partners and relevant institutions such as security actors
• Capacity building of partners and relevant federal, state and local government ministries & support policy engagements around GBV specific legislation
• Data analysis on trends/types/nature, incident management, policy and capacity to improve evidence-based advocacy, monitoring and knowledge management.
• Capacity building for sectors on GBV integration and technical support for implementation of GBV integration activities across sectors
• Joint/inter sector assessments, safety audits, monitoring and analysis & support institutionalization if GBV risk mitigation

Needs

The crisis in North-East Nigeria remains one of the most severe in the world. In the threeworst‐affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe, the insurgency has displaced 1.9million people. Over eighty per cent of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are in BornoState, the epicentre of the crisis, and over sixty percent are living among host communitieswhile other IDPs are living in formal and informal camps, collective settlement centres,and transitional centres and in host communities. The insurgency has led to a meltdownof socioeconomic and development structures, the destruction of health facilities,insecurity, dysfunctional legal and justice systems amidst increasing safety and securityconcerns. Termed a protection crisis, human rights violations including GBV occurringduring 􀂢ight within IDP camps and other settlements are widespread. Insecurity, triggeredby the insurgency and military operations against Non‐State Armed Groups, has increasedthe number of displaced persons with an estimated 130,000 new arrivals in variouslocations since the end of October 2017. These movements present major humanitarianchallenges to the already stretched resources in arrival locations. The ensuing insecuritycontinues to compromise safety, propagate a lack of access to essential services for thecommunities and hinder the capacity for humanitarians to provide much-neededlifesaving support; hence the di􀂣culty in humanitarian aid delivery. The insurgencyexacerbates challenges related to the protection of women and girls. Furthermore, as thecontext continues to evolve, the recent past has witnessed a signi􀂡cant threat of evolutionwith an upsurge in the instrumental use of women as perpetrators on the one hand andtheir position as survivors of GBV, abductions and detentions on the other. The resultantdirect and indirect consequences are kidnapping, early forced marriage, rape, sexualharassment and extra judicial killings. These actions indirectly lead to reduced access toeducation in general and bare even worse implications for girls, already subject tominimal participation in the region. Women and girls endure negative impacts onreproductive, psychosocial as well as their general health. This group is exposed to anincreased vulnerability to sexually transmitted diseases, unsafe abortions andreproductive health challenges. Human as well as economic and social rights of womenand girls are frequently violated, and there is an increased pressure on women to act asheads of households amidst high levels of poverty for these individuals in particular. Theproject aims to adopt a multi-sector approach in addressing the protection concerns thatexist in North-East Nigeria, while promoting action on the Humanitarian DevelopmentNexus (HDN). A consortium programme implemented by United Nations Population Fund(UNFPA) and International Organization for Migration (IOM) is envisaged. The multi-sectorintegrated programme will intervene in GBV prevention and response in the areas ofHealth, Psychosocial Support, Protection and Livelihoods. Prevention will utilize SocialBehaviour Change Communication based on Start Advocacy Start Action (SASA!) andEngaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP) models, while response programming willtake both medical and non-medical approaches. Medical response will be implementedthrough Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support(MHPSS) and Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) including for Adolescents and YoungWomen (AGYW). The Non-Medical response will involve Case Management,Psychological First Aid (PFA), community-based psycho-social support and referralsincluding access to justice, safety and security mechanisms.

Activities in the area of protection will pertain to capacity building on protectionmainstreaming and establishing of referral pathways to relay protection concerns with thesectors active in North-East Nigeria with priority consideration for the centrality ofprotection.
