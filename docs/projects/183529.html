Project Summary

The purpose of the project:
The space for individual Human Rights Defenders (HRDs) and civil society organizations
(CSOs) in the occupied Palestinian territories (OPT), speciﬁcally those working to end the Israeli occupation in Palestine, or to promote human rights (HRs) in the OPT, is
increasingly shrinking. The project responds to an increasingly dangerous environment for HRDs in the OPT. The vision of this project is that HRDs will be free to carry out their
peaceful and legitimate work, and to promote human rights and justice. The goal is
defending HRDs and minimizing the “chilling effect” that targeted harassment has on
their work. The project intervenes by building resilience of local HR communities to
withstand the persecution they face.
Proposed Outputs/target Include:
Direct Services for individual HRDs (men and women and, where applicable, children) and organizations at risk, aimed at mitigating these risks (legal, ﬁnancial or others) to their body, person or work. These services focus on coordination—via a 24-hrs hotline—and ﬁnancing of legal counselling and representation, in order to increase likelihood of early release from detention/arrest and avoid indictment and/lawsuits. Other services include capacity-building training and media support for HRDs (men and women) and
organizations. In addition, HRDs have access to a 24-hrs hotline +972 (0) 504479993 that is utilized for complaints including SEA nature that is accessible to all (female, male, children and people with disabilities.)

The advocacy work is based on the legal cases that HRDF represents. This aspect of the work is valuable and needed, in order to expand the space for HRDs’ activity. In this work, we give voice to the persecuted HRDs, because we believe they own their stories.

1 - Needs

This project responds to the need of protection for individual human rights defenders(HRDs) and human rights organizations, mitigating threats through direct legal support,resilience building and advocacy. In recent years, we are witnessing a growing systematic attack on HR organizations and individual HRDs (men, women and also children) who are peaceful protesters, intended to delegitimize and deter them from working, and silence criticism over Israel’s violations of human rights. Concerted campaigns and incitement by the Israeli government and non-state actors against HRDs is endangering the space for opposition of human rights violations. This was seen vividly in 2021 during the non-violent protests in support of the families of sheikh Jarrah as a wave of arrests was carried out by the Israeli forces against HRDs.This project will focus on HRDs and the different challenges they face: (A) Legal Risks: Israel violates HRDs’ rights and freedoms by issuing arbitrary military/police orders, using police/military force, and surmounting legal procedures - criminal or administrative. Men, women as well as children are affected.Palestinian HRDs in the OPT face greater risk since they are subjected to a military regime with little access to justice within the conﬁnes of the military court system. HRDs and organizations are not equipped to legally defend themselves, and the costly defensedemands resources that could otherwise be used for activities. (B) Public Targeting andincitement: Coordinated efforts to discredit organizations who oppose the occupationincluding attempts to restrict their ability to fundraise and label CSOs as ‘foreign agents’or ‘state enemies.’ Without rapid professional response to counter attacks, suchallegations may actually turn into restrictive legislation or initiatives endangering HRD'sfunding sources. (C) ‘Chilling Effect’: The intimidation resulting from the targeting leavesactivists (men, women and at times children) in a state of emotional ‘burnout’; withoutinvestment in activists’ wellbeing, this leads to a ‘chilling effect’ against HR activism. Thework of women human rights defenders (WHRDs) underlies not only the power dynamics between Human Rights Defenders and state representatives, but on top of this, women face the pressure of gender dynamics of society in general, creating a two-fold burden. Incrimination, arrests, investigations, imprisonment, legal proceedings and trials create a complex reality that is different for women to cope with. Many women human rights activists have testiﬁed to being verbally, sexually and/or physically harassed by soldiers/police during protests, arrests or investigations, as well as being subjected to humiliating treatment based on gender, sexuality and appearance. The HRDF constantly considers special needs of female HRDFs in its work by providing “tailor made” training to female HRDs and women activists groups that has extra focus on how to deal with sexual harassment by soldiers/police during protests, arrests or investigations. When female HRDs informs that they been harassed HRDF offers them support if they are interested and refers them to organizations that helps and follow up on the case till its closed.

2 - Activities

Activity cluster 1 –Risk Reduction:1.1. Legal Aid including direct representation, advice, and counsel to HRDs and organizations. Legal representation will be provided in cases of criminal prosecution (detention/arrest, interrogation, indictments) for men, women, children HRDs and organizations. Aid is rendered by contracted top attorneys. 1.2. Urgent aid will be facilitated through 24-hours phone hotline to HRDs (men and women) and organizations, to provide timely and effective legal counselling. Activity cluster 2 -Resilience Building 2.1. Workshops to inform HRDs (men/women) on their special rights and provide them with tools to better prepare activities, to minimize risks, and to defend against different threats and considering speciﬁc gender needs. The workshops are designed together with expert lawyers and activists (men and women). 2.2 PSEA awareness raising messages including information on rights and safe complaint channel (hotline) will be disseminated during the workshop. 2.3. Media crisis management for HRDs who are victims of public smear campaigns or of incitement to violence. The HRDF contracts/outsources media experts to provide consultations to HRDs (men and women) and push to include the voices and perspectives of targeted HRDs and HR organizations in mainstream media. Activity cluster 3 – Advocacy 3.1. Outreach activities to disseminate information to international community members through meetings, briefs, regular newsletter, and urgent updates. 3.2. Using mechanisms of international monitoring and action, such as ﬁling formal “urgent complaints”, issuing reports, submitting opinions to the UN state review, and so on.

3 - Indicators

The success of the proposed project will be measured through reaching the followingindicators: Activity Cluster 1: • Direct legal aid to HRDs (men and women; children ifnecessary); hotline calls answered to individual HRDs or human rights organizations peryear.• Overall good legal results for the large majority of legal aid beneﬁciaries, such as: -early release from detention/arrest (after no more than 2 arraignment hearings); - reducedand/ dropped charges from the indictment; - lighter sentences/punishments. • Activists’groups will contact the HRDF prior to organizing actions, to get legal advice or counseland/ to arrange to have standby lawyers in advance. Activity Cluster 2:• Number of trainings held for an average of 10-20 HRDs per training. • Number of HRDs who are raised their awareness with regards to PSEA • Media discussion of HRDs and HR organizations will include the voice of at risk HRDs and/ their lawyers. • Public discussion will include perspectives that counter-act the targeting of HR community. Activity Cluster 3:• Brieﬁngs held to international diplomats; 2-3 newsletters or press releases sent to over 650 recipients, in addition to providing legal updates about differentcases of HRDs. • Advocacy work and activities to mobilize the international mission representatives and international community to attend trial hearings of local HRDs, to be more informed about the situation on the ground and show more support for HRDs. The expected outcomes of the advocacy work will include involvement of the international community in these cases through diplomatic channels, integrating HRDs stories in their analysis, and impact of these stories on their policymaking towards the Israeli Occupation of the Palestinian Territories.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The feedback and complaints collected will help us to learn from beneficiaries and communities targeted in this project, and be responsive to their views, opinions, and complaints, so that we will be able to improve the quality and direct impact of our work in the field. Also, the feedback will help us to identify problems, protect the safety and dignity of those who made the complaint, in addition to sharing information through the process of receiving and responding to feedback and complaints. The feedback and complaints gathered will be used to inform decision-making counterparts as it will safely voice the complaints and grievance of beneficiaries, to receive their rights and gain protection especially human rights defenders, under the human rights and international humanitarian laws, and to hold decision-making figures to be accountable to imply human rights laws and abide to international conventions and treaties. In addition, HRDF and DCA/NCA holds regular field visits and meets with beneficiaries to receive feedback from them and the beneficiaries are able to filea complaint through DCA/NCA complaint mechanism. The feedback mechanisms/tools that HRDF and DCA/NCA uses will be accessible by all including persons with disabilities. DCA/NCA and HRDF does not differentiate based on gender or disability. HRDF had provided and will continue providing its legal aid services to all, with no restrictions on types of groups, gender, or kind of disability. HRDF major goal is always to support and protect HRDs and HR organizations and groups in the Occupied Palestinian Territories. As for accessibility, all HRDs have access to HRDF’s hotline and can raise any concern and HRDF will review their hotline data log and complaints database. For sure, these mechanisms and tools will be accessible to all HRDs including persons with disabilities.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

DCA/NCA and partners apply Do No Harm (DNH) approach that is integrated in the project at all levels. Both partners are trained and aware of the approach which is part of the human rights-based approach adopted by DCA/NCA and HRDF. This project works on bringing social change to the lives of human rights defenders throughout their different activities including awareness raising and media crisis management. Both DCA/NCA and its partner (HRDF) prioritized the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and considered the principles of do no harm. With regards to HRDF, sensitivity and confidentiality is the main pillar of HRDF work as all data is treated with high sensitivity and confidentiality. All the legal data of the cases is saved and stored with high confidentiality. The main reason for this act is to protect HRDs, in front of Israeli courts especially concerning the legal data pertaining to their cases, and all the other related details to be spread by anyone, on one hand, and on the other hand to be protected against the continuous attacks conducted by Israeli right wing organizations such as smear campaigns, and inciting false information about HRDs cases in media, in addition to the Israeli government actions and the shrinking space which HRDs and human rights organizations, activists groups, and others are facing continuously. With regards to safety measures, HRDF always insist on taking all safety measures for the protection of targeted HRDs and human rights organizations, and with a network of experienced lawyers. These measures include keeping all data secured with high confidentiality, informing networks of activists and groups working in field of day to day legal developments in targeted areas, in addition to continuous direct communication with a network of lawyers, board of directors, and staff members. With regards to DCA/NCA, DCA/NCA is a Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) certified agency (www.hqai.org) and works continuously to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance it provides to the affected population. PSEA mainstreaming will be part of this project in the following ways: • DCA/NCA implements a code of conduct that is signed by all its staff that reflects among others the values and standards regarding PSEA. DCA/NCA ensures that the cooperation agreement with implementing partners consists of an article that requires its partner to have an internal staff code of conduct in place that reflects as a minimum the values and standards of partners regarding PSEA. • DCA/NCA will ensure that the target groups and communities have an accessible, a confidential and a safe complaints handling mechanisms during the implementation of the project, where the sensitive complaints are dealt with and acted upon with confidentiality (either by using DCA’/NCA’s already functioning complaints mechanism or using the partners complaints mechanism) • DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA training to its and partners’ staff at the beginning of the project and will ensurethat a plan is put in place on how the partners will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities. • DCA/NCA staff will monitor closely the practices of partners to reinforce the PSEA and ensure that the targeted communities are aware of PSEA and the available functional complaints mechanism. • DCA/NCA’s PSEA local focal point is an active member in the oPt-HCT-PSEA Network. Accordingly, DCA/NCAwill continuously update its partners with relevant and needed PSEA information especially on available referral pathways for sensitive complaints and will continuously seek support from the available network services when needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project enables access to assistance and services and targeting of vulnerable groups. This will mainly focus on providing assistance and services to HRDs. To add, the protection mainstreaming that the project is highlighting, comprises the principles of prioritizing safety, dignity, avoid causing harm, participation, and empowerment.This project is providing equal and impartial access to aid through the concept of inclusion: thus the main focus of this project is targeting human rights defenders including vulnerable groups, activists, human rights organizations, men, women, children, and others. This included continuing providing our legal counselling over the HRDF 24 hours hotline. Additionally, network of Lawyers on behalf of HRDF remains on stand-by, and are ready to provide legal counsel, representation, and aid to different HRDs, HR organizations, andactivists’ groups. With regards to gender and age mainstreaming, HRDF has been taking into accountand will continue doing so with issues related to gender and age of the targeted HRDs.HRDF provides its services including legal aid, media crisis management, capacitybuilding trainings, in addition to advocacy and lobbying activities, to all HRDsincluding individuals, activists groups, and human rights organizations, without any discrimination to any gender, race, color, etc. For gender analysis, HRDF provides services to all HRDs, as they have male, female, and other (such as LGBTQ groups) HRDs in their targeted beneficiaries.In certain cases, for tackling gender mainstreaming, HRDF purely provides direct services to women HRDs and women human rights organizations. Furthermore, in the work HRDF takes the disaggregation of needs and assessment data and identification of gender and age groups that are most vulnerable. Giving an example, HRDF provides legal counseling and representation to Israeli and Palestinian HRDs who participate in nonviolent protests against the Israeli occupation in Israel and the oPt; HRDs fighting for LGBTQ rights; Palestinian inhabitants residing the Jordan valley and many more. To add, HRDF works with lawyers who are HRDs by themselves, and most of the volume of the work is done by female lawyers, who are prominent HRDs. With regards disability mainstreaming, HRDF doesn’t have any limitations to respond to the specific needsand vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities. Services are provided to all HRDs, including individuals, groups, human rights groups, men and women including persons with disabilities. Being aware that the needs for legal protection provided to persons with disability are higher than the ones provided to regular HRDs, as they more marginalized and they need more attention and support. Therefore, disability mainstreaming is an important part of HRDF’s legal aid work on the ground, especially focusing on legal cases dealing with persons with disabilities.
