Project Summary

The Provision of Integrated Lifesaving GBV Services for Vulnerable Communities in Aweil West and Aweil North Counties (NBeG State) - project aims to deliver comprehensive Gender-Based Violence (GBV) prevention and response services to vulnerable populations in Aweil West and Aweil North Counties. Inter-agency assessments in Aweil West and North counties (September 2024) highlight severe protection concerns, with 65% of women and girls experiencing GBV within the past year. Health facilities report a 40% increase in GBV cases during January-August 2024, dominated by domestic violence (45% of incidents). Both counties are classified as "severe" (Level 4) in Protection Cluster mapping, with only 30% of survivors accessing comprehensive services. Service mapping reveals only two functional women and girls' friendly spaces serving 20% of the population, while just 35% of health facilities have CMR-trained staff. Legal aid remains limited to quarterly mobile courts, and psychosocial support reaches only 25% of identified survivors. Targeting 15,000 beneficiaries from January to June 2025, this initiative focuses on strengthening community-based protection mechanisms while providing essential services to GBV survivors. Recent assessments indicate that 65% of women and girls in the target areas have experienced some form of GBV, while only 30% have access to response services. The situation is particularly acute for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees, who face increased vulnerability due to displacement and limited social support networks. The project will ensure; 50 awareness-raising sessions conducted with women, men, boys and girls on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting; 1,500 of women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community cares programming); 1,000 women, men, boys and girls reached with group PSS services, including recreational and social activities; 625 of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits # of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits; 200 of men, women, girls, and boys supported with cash and voucher assistance; 100 women, girls supported with GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel efficient stoves, intergradation of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors); 2 GBV referral pathways established and functional at county level and 100 women and men (community leaders or humanitarian, GBV partners and government social welfare staff) trained on GBV. The implementation strategy will emphasizes a survivor-centered approach, incorporating protection mainstreaming and conflict sensitivity throughout all activities. Regular coordination with local authorities, traditional leaders, and other humanitarian actors will ensure effective service delivery and avoid duplication of efforts. The project will establish clear referral pathways and standard operating procedures for case management, ensuring quality service provision while maintaining confidentiality and ethical considerations. Monthly coordination meetings with stakeholders will facilitate information sharing and adaptive programming based on emerging needs. Expected outcomes include increased access to quality GBV response services for 80% of identified survivors, enhanced community awareness reaching 15,000 individuals, and strengthened local capacity for sustainable GBV prevention and response. The project contributes directly to Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 3.
Hope Africa South Sudan will regularly report the implementation status of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services to the Protection Cluster and GBV AoR both at the national and state levels.

1. Needs

Inter-agency assessments in Aweil West and North counties (September 2024) highlight severe protection concerns, with 65% of women and girls experiencing GBV within the past year. Health facilities report a 40% increase in GBV cases during January-August 2024, dominated by domestic violence (45% of incidents). Both counties are classified as "severe" (Level 4) in Protection Cluster mapping, with only 30% of survivors accessing comprehensive services. Service mapping reveals only two functional women and girls' friendly spaces serving 20% of the population, while just 35% of health facilities have CMR-trained staff. Legal aid remains limited to quarterly mobile courts, and psychosocial support reaches only 25% of identified survivors. The target area's population includes 42% women and girls of reproductive age (12-49 years), with significant displacement presence: 3,500 IDP households and 2,800 returnee families. Women head 38% of displaced households, with 55% reporting increased protection risks due to economic constraints and weakened community protection systems. Economic assessments show 72% of female-headed households below SMEB threshold, leading to negative coping mechanisms including early marriage (35% increase from 2023) and survival sex. Only 25% of women access sustainable income-generating activities.Community mapping shows only 40% of communities maintain functional protection committees, while traditional leadership structures lack GBV response capacity despite 65% expressing willingness to engage in prevention. Geographic analysis reveals 70% of the population lives beyond 5km from GBV service points, with access further complicated by transportation and seasonal challenges. Social stigma and fear of retaliation deter 60% of survivors from seeking help, while 45% lack confidence in available services.Health data indicates 55% of GBV survivors experience moderate to severe psychological distress, with strong correlation between GBV incidents and school dropouts among adolescent girls (40% of cases). Mental health screening shows 68% of survivors need long-term psychosocial support, yet services only meet 25% of this demand. The service provider capacity gap remains significant, with 45% of required positions filled and 70% of providers needing additional training in case management and psychosocial support. The GBV sub-cluster identifies major gaps in service provider capacity across both counties. While inter-agency referral pathways exist, they operate at limited capacity due to resource constraints and coordination challenges. The situation demands urgent strengthening of both prevention and response mechanisms, alongside addressing underlying socio-economic vulnerabilities that heighten protection risks in target communities.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Community Awareness and Prevention Programming The project will implement 30 structured awareness-raising sessions across Aweil West and North counties, utilizing culturally sensitive approaches to address GBV risks and response mechanisms. These sessions will integrate SASA and EMAP programming to reach 1,500 community members. Sessions will be conducted in local languages, incorporating interactive techniques such as role-play, community dialogues, and storytelling. The awareness campaign will specifically target key community influencers, including traditional leaders, youth groups, and women's associations, ensuring messages reach diverse audience segments through multiple channels. Activity 2: Psychosocial Support and Recreational Activities Group psychosocial support services will be provided to 1,000 beneficiaries through established safe spaces and mobile outreach teams. Activities will include structured group therapy sessions, life skills training, and recreational activities designed to promote healing and social cohesion. Specialized PSS activities will be tailored for different age groups, with dedicated sessions for adolescent girls and women. The project will establish women and girls' friendly spaces as focal points for these activities, offering regular weekly sessions and providing safe environments for social interaction and skill development. Activity 3: Material Support and Dignity Kit Distribution The project will distribute comprehensive dignity kits to 625 women and adolescent girls, prioritizing those most vulnerable, including IDPs, returnees, and survivors of GBV. Each kit will contain essential hygiene items, culturally appropriate clothing, and protection-related materials. Distribution will be coupled with hygiene promotion sessions and information about available GBV services. An additional 200 beneficiaries will receive targeted cash and voucher assistance through a carefully designed mechanism that considers protection risks and market accessibility. Activity 4: Economic Empowerment and Risk Mitigation Implementation of GBV risk mitigation interventions will support 100 women and girls through various initiatives. This includes establishing income-generating activities, distributing fuel-efficient stoves to reduce exposure during firewood collection, and ensuring GBV considerations are mainstreamed across other humanitarian sectors. The project will form women's savings groups, provide business skills training, and support market linkages. Integration with other clusters will ensure comprehensive risk mitigation across sectors including Food Security, WASH, and Shelter/NFI. Activity 5: Referral Pathway Development and Capacity Building The project will establish and strengthen 2 GBV referral pathways at the county level, ensuring comprehensive service mapping, clear protocols, and regular updates. Each pathway will connect health services, legal aid, psychosocial support, and safe houses through standardized procedures and documentation. Training will be provided to 100 stakeholders, including community leaders, humanitarian partners, and government social welfare staff, focusing on survivor-centered approaches, case management, and referral protocols. All activities will be implemented through a phased approach, beginning with capacity building and system establishment, followed by direct service provision and community engagement. Regular coordination meetings with Protection Cluster partners will ensure complementarity of services and avoid duplication. Monthly progress reviews will track implementation against targets, allowing for adaptive management based on emerging needs and challenges. This project will ensure that the needs identified from the IRNA Assessments for Aweil West and Aweil North are solved through conducting GBV prevention and Response services with 50% women and girls involved during in implementation. HASS will coordinate with GBV AoR and 5Ws reporting
