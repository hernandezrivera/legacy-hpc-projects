Project Summary

INTERSOS proposes an integrated program to address critically poor nutrition outcomes in vulnerable populations through targeting severely and moderately acutely malnourished children under five years old and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), severely food insecure households with poor hygiene practices and behaviors. Through a triple-pronged approach, the program will directly support the priorities outlined in the Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan, and the 2019-2021 Humanitarian Response Strategy as was agreed with the Government of Nigeria by focusing squarely on improving nutrition outcomes, while addressing underlying causes of malnutrition and meeting urgent needs that, if not met, can escalate malnutrition. INTERSOS will contribute to improved nutrition outcomes of vulnerable households in target areas of Magumeri, Maiduguri and Bama through life-saving nutrition services, provision of food baskets, and restoration of essential WASH facilities in health facilities – all complemented by health, nutrition, and hygiene education aimed at behavior change. This program will directly reach out to 13,219 SAM/MAM children and 10,123 PLW across three target locations.
Nutrition activities are designed in line with Ministry of Health protocols and guidelines for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition. These activities will support life-saving needs of children under five and PLW employing a CMAM Approach. INTERSOS will support health facilities and mobile clinics to serve hard-to-reach areas in Magumeri. Support for food insecure households will include provision of supplementary food for children U5 and PLW, and food baskets. Food baskets will be provided to food insecure households with SAM cases. The dual approach of meeting immediate nutrition and food needs will help alleviate the pressures of underlying causes of malnutrition
Within Magumeri LGA, INTERSOS is proposing to continue the existing mutual collaborations with the SMoH and the nutrition sector by prioritizing and strengthening the scale-up of SC treatment of SAM with complications among U5 children. INTERSOS will continue to provide SC operational and technical support, as well as the procurement of therapeutic formulae milk, drugs, and equipment required for timely and effective management of U5 SAM with complications at the SC hosted within Magumeri general hospital. In addition, through the already established outreach OTP sites, INTERSOS will continue to cover 7 accessible wards within the H2R remote areas in Magumeri and Gajiganna a s well as adding the TSFP Component in its CMAM program for holistic approach. In particular, Kashia/Ardoram, Malanari, and Talwari-Gajiganna are currently covered with NHF funding support to INTERSOS.

In Bama, INTERSOS will partner with UNICEF to continue providing operational and technical support 1 OTP site and with WFP to support the TSFP in the GSSSC IDP camp, including the RUTF, RUSF, drugs and equipment for timely and effective management of SAM without complication and MAM cases. INTERSOS has renewed Partnership Commitment Agreement with UNICEF up to June 2021 to benefit from the UNICEF’s supply RUFT.
In Maiduguri INTERSOS will support the operations of SC in University of Maiduguri State referral and teaching hospital for management of SAM cases with complications, as well as supporting SAM without Medical complications and MAM cases in 5 sites.
INTERSOS is proposing to introduce targeted conditional cash transfers to households with SAM cases as away of preventing relapses escalated by the prevailing COVID-19 situation.
Through the adapted family MUAC, INTERSOS will reinforce timely screening and self-referrals. All activities will be undertaken in line with the nutrition sector guidelines for management of child acute malnutrition in the context of COVID-19. GBV risk mitigation, and child protection will be harnessed through continuous engagements of care givers and the community participatory IYCF.

1 - Needs

According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) with GAM levels of,10.7%. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 with SAM and 514,526 with MAM. According to the IPC Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020, the immediate causes of acute malnutrition in NE Nigeria included inadequate breastfeeding, poor food consumption patterns (quality and quantity) and high prevalence of diarrheal and acute respiratory diseases. The hostile security situation characterized by indiscriminate attacks (including on humanitarian actors) continues to hamper efforts by humanitarians to improve on nutrition parameters and indicators in the affected populations. The conflict triggered displacements have resulted in disrupted healthcare, social life, livelihoods and traditional food security safety nets, which together with overcrowding and congestion in the IDP camps exacerbate the risks associated with acute malnutrition, morbidity and mortalities in the affected populations, more-so among U5 and PLW due to sub-optimal access to health care services as well as compromised child feeding and care environment. Consequently, GAM prevalence rates have persistently remained high, above recommended SPHERE standards. Despite exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) being recognized as the most economical, socially and culturally embraced and safe public health intervention, many mothers are unable to initiate and sustain breastfeeding due to extreme external stressors. According to INTERSOS mid tern Evaluation report (September 2020) Infant and young child feeding practices are not optimal with exclusive breastfeedding rates of 24.8%. Complementary foods are often introduced early, and continued breastfeeding until two years is not common . Overall, only 24.9% of sampled children received foods from 4 or more food groups, the result is particularly worse in Bama (7.8%) but Dikwa (44.7%) and Ngala (40.9%) are significantly better than the other LGAs. This is likely so because most of the accessible communities live in hard-to-reach remote areas with limited access to government or humanitarian assistance. For instance, a recent (May 2020) SCI SQUEAC survey reported that a single coverage estimate for OTP in Magumeri LGA was only 38.0%, which was interpreted as low since it was below set SPHERE threshold for CMAM programs within rural settings (>50%). The trend is projected to persist or escalate as the security situation continues to deteriorate and exacerbate the immediate, basic and underlying causes of U5 malnutrition among the disaster affected populations. To contribute to improved nutrition status and decreased nutrition related mortality, INTERSOS, through this action will implement basic package of integrated nutrition interventions in Magumeri, Maiduguri and Bama. The interventions are aimed at enabling timely identification and management of acute malnutrition among U5 children and PWL, while mainstreaming COVID-19 and GBV prevention. The beneficiary targets for the proposed actions have been informed by April-December 2020 HRP and the needs shared by the nutrition sector.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially PLW, in the areas of intervention. The involvement of FtFSGs is considered a positive step towards engaging men in family and household care of children. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by OFDA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person.In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities. Furthermore, accountability towards beneficiaries will be guaranteed through the application of internal tools already available, such as the PSEA Policy, Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
