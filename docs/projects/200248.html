Project Summary

The Danish Refugee Council (DRC) together with partners is appealing for support to implement comprehensive child protection prevention and response services in the Bekaa, Baalbek, North, and Akkar, targeting the most vulnerable and hard-to-reach Lebanese and Palestinian Refugees in Lebanon (PRL). The compounded socioeconomic crisis in Lebanon has significantly deepened the vulnerability of aforementioned target populations, and has resulted in exacerbated protection concerns for vulnerable children as confirmed by most recent MSNA data. Through programming in 2022, DRC has identified significant increase in exposure of children to violence, exploitation and abuse, severely impacting the well-being and healthy development of children. Vulnerable households are increasingly adopting negative coping mechanisms to meet basic needs, including child labor and child marriage, with severe protection consequences for children. Proposed activities under the ERP are designed in coordination with the Child Protection sector, and aim to complement services provided under the LCRP to allow for better and increased inclusion of PRL and Lebanese into child protection services.
DRC will respond to increased needs for specialized child protection interventions by providing comprehensive case management services tailored to the needs of children and following a child-friendly approach to support children and where relevant caregivers in informed decision making about how to address child rights violations or incidents, and understand which services are available and to facilitate access to these services in a holistic and dignified manner. Protection cash will be integrated into case management services where relevant and appropriate and to support the achievement of protection outcomes, and children and caregivers will benefit from have access to individual psychosocial support as part of case management, including psychological first aid. For child survivors of sexual abuse, DRC and partners will leverage CP and GBV case management capacities to ensure a tailored response to child survivors following established GBV and CP guiding principles and approaches. Quality and outcomes of case management services will be monitored through satisfaction surveys, ensuring services can be continuously improved and any challenges addressed in a timely manner. DRC as part of child protection response services will also provide Focused Psychosocial Support services based on the “I Am Here” approach, developed to identify the most marginalized adolescent boys and girls through a door-to-door assessment methodology. Adolescents will participate in the design of participatory curricula whereby adolescents prioritize their own needs in terms of skills, knowledge and attitudes in pre-defined thematic areas related to cross-cutting themes of GBV and child protection. The FPSS curricula will also target caregivers and other male household members as decision-makers in adolescent’s life’s, requiring their engagement to empower adolescents and facilitate access to rights and services.
To complement response services, DRC will implement CP prevention activities targeting children and caregivers at community-level through community-based psychosocial support (CBPSS) activities aimed to contribute to enhancing the protection of children. The project will also establish youth committees to strengthen community-based networks with the aim to empower communities and engage them in the protection of children. Youth committees will receive capacity-building and coaching on child rights, protection, gender mainstreaming and inclusion, to be able to develop, plan and implement two youth-led initiatives within their communities aimed at mitigating child protection risks. Initiatives will be driven by priorities and needs identified by youth themselves, with the project ensuring youth have the necessary capacities and means to implement these initiatives.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Provision of child protection case management services DRC will provide holistic case management (CM) services. CM services will be provided in safe spaces or through home visits. Individual PSS, and Psychological First Aid will be provided. Referrals will be conducted to ensure access to complementary services. For child survivors, DRC will leverage both CP and GBV expertise to provide quality CM services.Provision of Emergency Cash AssistanceProtection cash will be integrated into CM services where relevant. Both ECA and Recurrent Protection Cash Assistance will be provided as part of CM. Provision of specialized/focused PSS services:Focused PSS services will be implemented to target adolescent girls and boys, their caregivers and key male household members. The “I am here” approach will be used to identify the most vulnerable adolescent boys and girls, using a door-to-door outreach methodology. Strengthening child protection community-based networks and provision of community-based PSS servicesCommunity-based PSS activities will be provided to children and their caregivers, using tailored curriculums to target different age groups of children. The sessions provide PSS and address basic principles of child rights violations, including sexual violence.Youth committees will be established and capacitated through trainings and coaching. Youth committees will then develop, plan and implement initiatives within their communities aimed at mitigating CP risks.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Gender, diversity and inclusion has been integrated into the project design in line with the IA Standing Committee GMT. DRC will disaggregate all beneficiary data by sex, age and disability. DRC will protect the identity and data of beneficiaries as per international standards and internal data protection protocols. DRC ensures that all staff are aware of the Secretary General Bulletin Special Measures for PSEA, and the internal reporting mechanisms for SEA cases, through mandatory trainings and the signature of the code of conduct. DRC will work on enhancing its awareness raising activities with communities to disseminate information on SEA key messages, available reporting channels and how the overall procedure functions, from the point of receiving the complaint, to investigation, resolution and feedback to communities on the outcomes of complaints and actions taken.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

DRC maintains a set of robust accountability and feedback mechanisms committed to participation of affected communities. DRC’s community feedback mechanism (CFM) ensures voices of beneficiaries are included, valued and heard. It systematically captures records, tracks, and follows up on the feedback it receives to improve elements of a response. Any person or community affected by DRC, or the DRC’s partners’ actions, decisions or policies can submit feedback and complaints through the CFM system, including through physical Feedback Boxes (FB), a hotline and email. Information on the availability and purpose of these channels is consistently disseminated by DRC staff through business cards. An information poster is installed next to each FB explaining its purpose. DRC staff are trained to explain the existence and importance of these channels. DRC ensures to close the feedback loops through communicating a response back to beneficiaries about the actions taken to address it.
