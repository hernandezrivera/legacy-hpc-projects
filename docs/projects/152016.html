Project Summary

Street Child will provide educational opportunity for 50,000 conflict affected children who are not able to access the formal school system, or are not supported to maintain the level expected in the formal classroom. Based on specific community education needs, Street Child will increase access by creating or strengthening safe learning environments. Street Child will offers non-formal basic literacy and numeracy and provision of psycho-social support classes for IDP, host community and returnee children in host communities for those out of school or those who missed out some year of education. Ongoing professional development will be provided by mentor teachers from local government agencies, to improve the quality of education.
Children in the formal education system will be helped to faster their learning at the right level building the capacity of teachers with targeted pedagogical skills.
In addition, Street Child’s integrated approach to EiE and CP in all areas of operation will ensure that personnel in the school are organised and trained to respond or prevent protection concerns for children with a system for safe reporting of incidents.

Needs

An estimated 1.8 million people are displaced in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe and around 40 percent (0.8 million) of that population is comprised of school aged children between three and seventeen years displaced across the three states. While the number of displaced persons and returnees increased compared to the previous year, the total number of children and education personnel in need decreased from 2.9 million for 2018 to 2.2 million for 2019 based on education sector estimates (EiEWG HNO, 2019).Recent data on the key education indices in the NE still indicate a dire situation in education with Borno recording the highest number of girls out of school in Nigeria at 71%. Education sector faces an upward task of infrastructural rehabilitation and expansion, education management stretching and the need to address emerging education needs such as skills acquisition and protection of education against attacks. There is an urgent need to engage the local communities in supporting education as a longer-term strategy for recovery and peacebuilding. Between October and November 2017, education sector partners conducted an education needs assessment across the three states. The findings indicated that poverty was identified by the respondents as the greatest barrier to access to education expressed as inability of parents to meet financial costs demanded in schools. Others are lack of infrastructures and school materials Street Child will fill gaps by building the capacity of teachers, provision of quality learning materials and equipment for new and renovated classrooms (prioritizing formal education, non-formal semi-permanent learning spaces only where highly needed) to respond to the education needs.

Activities

Street Child will provide educational opportunity for 50,000 vulnerable children (27,500 girls and 22,500 boys) who are not able to access the formal school system, or are not supported to maintain the level expected in the formal classroom. Street Child will offer a non-formal basic literacy and numeracy classes to 15,000 IDP, host community and returnee children in host communities (8,250 girls and 6,750 girls). 300 volunteers from the communities (150 women and 150 men), who will receive an allowance and training in EiE subjects, will facilitate these non-formal classes. 35.000 vulnerable children (19,250 girls and 15,750 boys) will be supported to attend schooling in formal school system at their right level, with school materials to ensure a minimum quality of basic education will take place. This approach will also support teachers with on-going supervision & mentoring by SC staff and government education personnel.SC constructs semi-permanent learning spaces (200 classrooms in year one), when highly needed due to lack of any structures or similar to conduct teaching in, that works with a double-shift when needed. Street Child prioritises the safety and security of children in school. All TLC structures are being established in consultation with the communities’ members in order to identify the safest locations, clean the area of any hazards, and engage community volunteers as unarmed guards. Every TLC cluster will have a first aid kit, and perimeter fence. To support the formal system, Street Child will trains formal school primary teachers to deliver remedial lessons and catch-up classes. in 2019 we will target 700 state school teachers (350 women and 350 men) This is especially for children that have specific knowledge or skills gaps and low level grade achievement, these remedial classes areas will be offered to bring students at their right level. We establish Community Education Committees (at least 40 in year one) to be strengthened in all locations to support the TLC, and provide sustainable community engagement on the importance of education to. Ongoing professional development will be provided by mentor teachers from local government agencies, to improve the quality of education. Street Child’s integrated approach to EiE and CP in all areas of operation will ensure that personnel in the school are organised and trained to refer children to professionals if they are at risk of gender-based violence (GBV) or other serious protection concerns with a system for safe reporting of incidents. they will also be able to identify and mitigates protection risks in schools. We will leverage and expand on this previous investment to support / extend local, vastly overcrowded schools, and provide educational opportunity in host communities where there is no existing school.
