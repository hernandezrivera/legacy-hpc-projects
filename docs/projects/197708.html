Project Summary

Providing Shelter and NFI emergency assistance for the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community families in conﬂict-affected areas of North East Syria, reaching a total of 900 conflict-affected individuals. Through the provision of shelter rehabilitation in host community of private shelters.
The NGO undertake shelter activities to support recent returnees, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa, Kobani and Manbij, living in unsafe and inadequate housing. This will be done through undertaking essential repairs of 150 partially-damaged or unfinished homes as well as ensuring a minimum level of privacy, dignity and access to WASH facilities for each household. This will benefit an estimate 900 individuals.

3 - Needs

The 2021 HNO reports that around 5.88 million people are in need of shelter assistance and 4.69 million in need of NFI support in Syria, with over 1.4 million located in NES in Hasekah, Ar Raqqa, Aleppo and Deir ez Zor governorates. There remains a growing housing crisis in some of the largest urban centers in NES, particularly in Ar Raqqa, Tabqa, Kobani and Manbij. A significant surge in returns and IDPs to Ar Raqqa city over the past few years has led to a sharp increase in rental prices and a shortage of adequate shelter. 41% of communities in Raqqa find current rental prices unaffordable (REACH; January 2021). Many new returnees are forced to occupy partially and severely damaged buildings, raising serious health and protection concerns. Given the high levels of vulnerability of many of these returnees, most are unable to afford the cost of even the most basic shelter rehabilitation works. These shelters are often unsealed and exposed to the elements making them difficult to insulate, and raising serious safety, protection and health concerns. At the same time, as the economy continues to stagnate and prices rises, the majority of basic NFIs have become unaffordable to vulnerable IDP and host families.

4 - Implementation of activities

In line with the Whole of Syria sector objectives, this project comprises 3 core activities. The activity, which falls under Sector Objective 2 (Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure) entails rehabilitation of partially damaged and unﬁnished buildings (Sector activity 2.1) in urban areas. 150 vulnerable households will be supported through shelter rehabilitation (900 individuals). In collaboration bilaterally, and through the NFI/Shelter working group, with other humanitarian actors working in shelter rehabilitation, will develop a shared beneﬁciary selection process, HLP due diligence, MoUs and other best practices. In terms of beneﬁciary selection within these districts, house-by-house assessments will be conducted and referrals accepted. Each shelter targeted will be rehabilitated to meet basic SPHERE shelter standards and will be in accordance with the Shelter sector guidance on rehabilitation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The SNFI cluster Protection Risk Analysis identifies over 50 key protection risks specific to the sector. Based on this, and the NGO's own internal protection analysis, identifies the key risks as follows: inter-community tension as a result of inclusion/exclusion, unclear selection criteria or varied assistance packages by different actors; increased tension between host and IDP communities; and risks particularly associated with distributions. To mitigate the first risk, the NGO adopts SNFI cluster guidance in its vulnerability assessments and its assistance packages to ensure harmonization with other actors. Similarly, the NGO hires local staff to carry out activities to ensure all staff have strong local knowledge, extensive informational networks and good community acceptance. This ensures conflict sensitivity as our staff have an awareness of the social, cultural and political dynamics of the area and are representative of the program participants. In terms of tension between host and IDP communities, the NGO conducts extensive consultations with both groups before commencing any activities. The NGO does not target on the basis of status, and thus supports IDP and host communities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with support from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods.
