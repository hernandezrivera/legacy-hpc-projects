Project Summary

The Govt. of Pakistan launched military operation Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan Agency (NWA) on June 15, 2014, targeting non-state armed actors’ hideouts to curb the menace of terrorism. This prompted large scale displacement to neighboring districts in KP province. Return to NWA officially started from April 2015 till Oct. 2016 . According to the FDMA’s (FATA Disaster Management Authority) Situation Report of TDP’s return as of October 10, 2018, out of the total caseload of 107,391 families, 92,618 families have returned back to North Waziristan Tribal District (NWTD) erstwhile NWA. ADF conducted a household survey between October 11-14, 2018 to identify the WASH sector needs/gaps in return areas of Tehsil Ghulam Khan-NWTD. During this assessment, the secondary data was gathered and analyzed from WASH cluster sources, district administration, FDMA, UN-Agencies, and other working humanitarian partners whereas the primary data was collected through structured household questionnaires, Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussion (FGDs), community meetings and field observations.
As a result of this assessment a substantial need in WASH sector was identified. 49% of the households are relying on damaged water sources whereas 33% are relying on open streams/unprotected springs. 68% have declared their drinking water as contaminated but still 100% are using no domestic water purification techniques thus exposing themselves to water-borne diseases primarily stomach disorder (60%) and diarrhea (40%). 49% of the community is walking up to more than 500 meter while 30% up to 500 meters to fetch water on daily basis with routine water collection duty being performed primarily by 77% women and 16% children thus resulting 42% general protection, 29% accessibility and 29% workload as the major issues pertaining to water collection. Only 13% have reported of latrine availability at home whereas 87% have no latrines at homes which is a major human need and a serious sanitation concern. 42% of have pit latrines while 33% are using conventional latrines which are structurally inconvenient for special persons ( PWDs, pregnant women & older people). 96% are practicing open defecation with 67% reporting of associated risks with open defecation. Moreover, There are no WASH facilities in schools which has thus exposed the children to WASH related vulnerabilities. Water is not available and sanitation facilities are damaged in schools.
To address the identified WASH issues in the proposed areas, ADF proposes installation of 03 Solarized Pressure Pumps (SPPs) in 02 Government Schools (01 boys and 01 girls schools) and 01 in a Govt. Health Facility, installation of 04-SPPs in villages with substantial water need, rehabilitation of 02 damaged Drinking Water Supply Infrastructures (DWSI), construction of 270 cost effective household Pour Flush Latrines (PFLs), Rehab/construction of 06 number of (3-stance) PFLs with hand washing facilities in 02 schools and 01 health facility, conduction of 300 health & hygiene (100 male, 100 female and 100 children) sessions, distribution of 300 culturally appropriate and gender sensitive
hygiene/dignity kits among vulnerable families, arrangement of 09 Operation and Maintenance (O&M) trainings, distribution of 09 O&M kits among Water Management Committees (WMCs), conduction of 18 pre & post water quality tests and distribution of Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials in Tehsil Ghulam Khan-NWTD.

Needs

Alfalah Development Foundation (ADF) conducted a household survey between October 11-14, 2018 to identify the WASH sector needs/gaps in return areas of Tehsil Ghulam Khan-NWTD. As a result of this assessment the survey team identified that there is a substantial need in WASH sector. 49% of the households are relying on damaged dug well whereas 33% are relying on other sources including open stream and unprotected spring as their main existing drinking water sources. However, 60% of the respondents have termed their primary drinking water sources as damaged and repairable while 21% as replaceable since these are open and unprotected. 68% have declared their drinking water as contaminated thus majority of the community is not satisfied with the quality of their existing drinking water but still 100% of the families are using no domestic water purification techniques thus exposing themselves to water-borne diseases despite of the fact that the existing sources are contaminated. Because of using contaminated water for drinking, 60% have reported abdominal/stomach disorder whereas 40% reported diarrhea as the common water-borne diseases in the community. 49% of the community is walking up to more than 500 meter while 30% up to 500 meters to fetch water on daily basis with routine water collection duty being performed primarily by 77% women and 16% children thus resulting 42% general protection, 29% accessibility and 29% workload as the major issues pertaining to water collection. 100% of the population is using containers for water storage with 66% of the surveyed population reporting that some of the water containers are covered with cap whereas 7% reported that none of the water containers are covered. 27% of the respondents have declared the available water as insufficient for their household consumption, while 18% have reported using water for drinking purpose only. Only 13% have reported of latrine availability at home whereas 87% of the respondents are having no latrine facilities in their homes which is a major human need and a serious sanitation concern. Out of the total 13% having latrines, 92% have only one common latrine with 33% being damaged and in repairable condition. 42% of the families have pit latrines while 33% are using conventional latrines covered with veil/curtain (used by women/girls). 99% of the respondents have reported that the available latrines are structurally inconvenient for special persons including PWDs, pregnant women and older people. Out of the 87% families having no latrine facilities at their household level, 96% are practicing open defecation outside the home with 4% accessing fields/forests for their defecation needs. 67% have reported that there is risk associated with open defecation with reported wild animals’ attack particularly at evening/night time. 61% of the surveyed population reported hand washing with water only with 76% reporting the unavailability of soap as the major reason. Moreover, There are no WASH facilities in schools which has thus exposed the children to WASH related vulnerabilities. Water is not available and sanitation facilities are damaged in schools. Consequently, children are facing problems in satiating their drinking water and sanitation needs.

Activities

To address the identified WASH issues in the proposed areas, ADF proposes installation of 03 Solarized Pressure Pumps (SPPs) in 02 Government Schools (01 boys and 01 girls schools) and 01 in Govt. Health Facility, installation of 04-SPPs in villages with substantial water need, rehabilitation of 02 damaged Drinking Water Supply Infrastructures (DWSI), construction of 270 cost effective household Pour Flush Latrines (PFLs), construction of 06 number of (3-stance) PFLs with hand washing facilities in 02 schools and in 01 health facility, conduction of 300 health & hygiene (100 male, 100 female and 100 children) sessions, distribution of 300 culturally appropriate and gender sensitive hygiene/dignity kits among vulnerable families, arrangement of 09 Operation and Maintenance (O&M) trainings, distribution of 09 O&M kits among Water Management Committees (WMCs), conduction of 18 pre & post water quality tests and distribution of Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials in Tehsil Ghulam Khan-NWTD. ADF proposes the installation of SPPs because of being more protected drinking water solution and also because of being feasible for installation in the target villages due to water table between 180-300 feet and unavailability of electricity to run the conventional submersible water pumps. This will reduce the incidences of water-borne diseases in the community and will also reduce the accessibility and workload issues that women and children are currently facing. Installation of SPPs inside/surrounding of schools and health facility will also facilitate the school children and patients with availability of potable water. Additionally, rehabilitation of government/communal DWSI will ensure sufficient, safe, acceptable and physically accessible water for personal and domestic use. Household level PFLs will be constructed through community participation wherein ADF will provide sanitation kits to the selected vulnerable beneficiary household whereas the beneficiary will construct the latrines as community contribution to increase their ownership as well as to encourage replication of this modality in future. This will not only reduce open defecation in the area but will also ensure protection and dignity of people particularly women and girls. Similarly, 3-Stance PFLs with washing facilities in schools and health facilities will be constructed by ADF through the selected vendor. This will familiarize the school children and visiting patients with use and significance of latrines in disease control and reduction. Hygiene sessions will bring adoption of good hygienic practices and sensitization for ultimate behavioral changes in the community. ADF will ensure that all the schemes and sanitation facilities are culturally appropriate, secure, user-friendly, DRR compliant and gender appropriate. All these proposed interventions will be carried out in consultation with WASH cluster, district administration and male & female WASH committees in the target villages. The installation of SPPs and rehab of DWSI will be carried out in consultation with Public Health Engineering Department (PHED), District Education Department and Agency Surgeon of NWTD. Furthermore, community mobilization and capacity building of WASH Committees and the community on the importance and adoption of hygiene practices at all levels through training programs implying different culturally sensitive approaches such as PHAST, CHAST, etc. will be arranged. This will help in controlling sanitation and water related diseases and ultimately promoting a healthy and happy environment.
