Project Summary

Syria grapples with complex humanitarian crisis marked by 11-year conflict that has led to considerable decline in Human Development Index. With 6.8 million IDPs, Syria has highest number, facing economic downturns, collapsing essential services, cholera outbreak, climatic shocks. Economic hardships, and ongoing conflict contribute to dire situation compounded by climate-related shocks.
Afrin district in NWS, grappling with aftermath of conflict and displacement, faces severe WASH challenges. Home to over 548,000 people, including 418,000 IDPs. The region has been sanctuary for IDPs from Damascus, Homs, Idleb, making it one of most densely populated areas, 83% identified as people in need according to HNO 2023.
The humanitarian context in Afrin district, further complicated by significant earthquake in February 2023, necessitates urgent and adaptive response. The earthquake resulted in over 1,000 fatalities and injuries, with substantial damage to buildings and infrastructure, amplifying need for humanitarian aid and recovery efforts.
Access to WASH services encounters severe challenges due to conflict damage, limited maintenance, and weak management. Intensified by climate change, economic downturn, 13.55 million people face 2.6% increase in need from 2022. Unsafe water sources affect 52% of population, with significant water systems often non-operational. Economic obstacles hinder vulnerable communities, leading to sanitation issues, health risks, and challenges for children, women, people with disabilities, and elderly.
The increased WASH needs, driven by public health crises, cholera outbreaks, new displacements, economic challenges, potential UN resolution non-renewals, funding gaps, water disputes, power supply issues, and sanitation crises. Including need to strengthen hygiene promotion and RCCE is crucial to addressing these multifaceted challenges.
According to Bahar’s recent assessment, Bahar proposes program aligns with WASH cluster and UNICEF strategy, focusing on restoration of WASH infrastructure and services. Utilizing its experience in implementing WASH activities, Bahar aims to enhance WASH services in communities, IDPs sites and schools, increase community engagement, facilitate recovery.
The program designed to address WASH needs and foster long-term sector recovery by utilizing Bahar's proven operational capabilities, including delivery of water and sanitation systems rehabilitation, and supporting IDPs sites with sustainable solutions, and coordination of rapid response nutrition teams. This approach ensures immediate effectiveness and leads to sustained health improvements.
Targeting vulnerable populations, particularly children, women, IDPs, the program is strategic response to the urgent WASH needs in Afrin, A’zaz and Albab districts. It aims to deliver integrated WASH services for the survival and well-being of Syria's most affected populations, ensuring a comprehensive approach.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Bahar Organization's comprehensive response plan to address the urgent needs of IDPs across 26 sites aimed at enhancing WASH services, along with measures for COVID-19 and cholera outbreaks. The approach includes the provision of safe and potable water, emergency latrines in camps, solid waste management, distribution of consumable hygiene kits, rehabilitation and expansion of water networks, repair and operation of water stations including installation of solar systems, and provide awareness sessions.To ensure improved access to safe chlorinated water, Bahar is committed to continued water trucking for approximately 16,641 IDPs these IDPs will be covered for 7 months and will consider covering them under next BHA fund, where Bahar will continue covering around 34,000 IDPs from the current case load with UNICEF under BHA fund starting from 01st Jan 2024. Bahar's WASH interventions extend to rehabilitation and expansion of water networks in Jandaris, Ma'btali, and A’zaz, aiming to benefit 125,490 individuals, including 86,428 IDPs and 39,062 host community. To ensure sustainability, the organization will enhance cost recovery system in collaboration with local councils and water units. An exit strategy is meticulously planned, involving supporting water administrators, conducting multiple sessions for water authorities and operators, and ensuring daily rehabilitation activities' coordination. Awareness sessions on water conservation and COVID-19 precautions are integral to the plan, covering Jandaris, Ma'btali, and A’zaz, fostering better cost recovery in targeted locations.Moreover, Bahar addresses critical sanitation issues by repairing and expanding sewage networks in Jandaris, Raju, Ma'btali, Afrin, and AlBab (Al-Rai). The initiative, benefitting approximately 87,032 beneficiaries, includes constructing standard sewer systems and establishing a wastewater treatment plant to address environmental challenges, particularly the discharge of untreated sewage into Midanki Lake, a primary water source for around 200,000 people in Afrin and A’zaz communities.An elaborate exit strategy for Wastewater Treatment Plant involves detailed documentation, operator training, and formal handover to local authorities, ensuring continuity of operation and maintenance.Bahar commitment extends to schools, with intervention focusing on 30 schools in Raju, Bulbul, Sharan, Ma'btali. This includes targeting students with hygiene promotion activities rehabilitate WASH facilities.Bahar's comprehensive plan reflects a dedicated effort to address the urgent needs of displaced populations, emphasizing sustainability, community engagement, and a proactive approach to public health challenges.This project will target 207,958 IDPs and host communities living in Afrin, A’zaz and AlBab districts and Idleb as direct beneficiaries and around 200,000 Indirect beneficiaries.
