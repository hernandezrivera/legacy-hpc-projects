Project Summary

The Education system in South Sudan was brought to a close with the spread of COVID-19 pandemic. Owing to a weak health structure and shrinking economy, the government decided to shut down schools so as to control the spread of the virus. The closure of schools has had a multifaceted effect on the Education sector. A UNICEF report from December 2020 reports that 2.8 million children had dropped out of school due to the closure of schools. Similarly, teachers who were relying on incentives to sustain their livelihoods could no longer do so as incentives were as well stopped.
The children who have dropped out of school become vulnerable to recruitment into armed forces as child soldiers. The Humanitarian Needs Overview report of January 2021 notes that there has been an increase in sub-national violence manifested as cattle raids and/or revenge killings. Youth, especially of school going age have been easy to recruit due to idleness. Conversely, the fighting has had a negative effect on these youth. Children and youth have been made increasingly vulnerable as there has been increased exposure to protection risks such as sexual violence, child labor, early marriage, and recruitment into armed groups.
Based on this, World Relief seeks to intervene in six counties, will the aim of mitigating these challenges and building resilience within the Education sector to be able to handle not only effects of COVID-19, but also other shocks that might be experienced in the future.
This project targets to reach out and support 20,000 learners in Early Childhood Development (ECD), and Primary and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) Centers. The intervention will ensure children out of school, internally displaced persons (IDP) and host community children, adolescents/youth aged 3-17 years have equitable access to safe and protective learning opportunities. This will be done through establishment and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, latrines and handwashing facilities in the project sites. The project will target to establish/rehabilitate four temporary learning space (TLS) per county, 24 TLSs in total. The project will also conduct back-to-learning campaigns, improve quality of education through enhanced numeracy and literacy skills, train and provide incentives to teachers, integrate with nutrition program to encourage learning. This intervention will ensure improved recovery and resilience capacity among children by linking education with child protection services as well as psychosocial support to children and adolescents/youth including survivors of gender-based violence, aged 3-17 years.

1 - Needs

In addition to the needs outlined above, there have been some efforts made to mitigate the effect of COVID-19 on the Education Sector. UNICEF and its partners have been able to provide distance learning through radios. However, this has had some challenges that includes the cost implications of radios, allowing only a limited number of children to access teaching through radios. Again, the teacher student interaction is limited which hampers learning. Environmental factors, especially flooding that was experienced in many parts of South Sudan has as well continued to have a negative impact on Education. Flooding has affected the school infrastructure destroying learning materials and the TLS’ which had been established. A UNICEF report from December 2020 notes that at least one third of schools have been destroyed by the flooding water. In Fangak County, an initial rapid needs assessment (IRNA) conducted in February 2021 notes that most of the schools assessed in that area have been destroyed by the floods. Floods have also had a negative impact on the food and nutrition situation in South Sudan. Flooding destroyed farms and food just before its harvesting in many parts of the country. An IRNA report conducted in Koch County in October 2020 as well as the IRNA report from Fangak County 2021 both note the devastating effect of flooding on the food security situation. Most parts of the country are reporting IPC level 3 and above, with both Koch and Fangak classified as IPC 4. The overall food insecurity situation in the country is likely to have a negative impact on the Education sector. Children are likely to be forced out of schools to fend for the family and therefore exposing them to more risks. In 2020, before the closure of schools, WRSS was supporting close to 20,000 learners in ECD and Primary and ALPs. WRSS was running 21 schools in Schools in Rubkona County with an enrollment of 16,658. In Koch County, Unity State, there were 18 schools with a total enrollment of 2,582. In Fangak County, Jonglei State, WRSS had 21 schools with an enrollment of 5,758. In Guit County Unity State, there were 11 schools with enrollment of 2,352. In Ibba and Mvolo counties of Western Equatoria State, there were 23 and 15 schools respectively with enrollment numbers of of 6,655 for Ibba and 2,818 for Mvolo. Most of these children are currently out of school and vulnerable for recruitment into armed forces for boys and early marriage for girls. Without this intervention, the future of these children will remain bleak.

2 - Project activities

This intervention will aim at mitigating the challenges highlighted above in a timely manner and to be able to handle not only the effects of COVID-19, but also other shocks that might be experienced in the future. It will ensure children out of school, IDP and host community children, adolescents/youth aged 3-17 years have equitable access to safe and protective learning opportunities. This will be done through establishment and rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces, latrines and handwashing facilities in the project sites. The project will target to establish/rehabilitate four temporary learning spaces (TLS) per county, 24 TLSs in total. The project will also conduct back to learning campaigns to mobilize children of school going age to return to the classrooms. Secondly, the project will improve quality of education through enhanced numeracy and literacy skills as well as life skills and build the resilience of children, youth and adolescents aged 3-17 years. This will be achieved through several ways. First the intervention will provide mentorship/coaching to teachers and Parent Teacher Association (PTA) members. Teachers will be trained on pedagogy methods, classroom psychology, psychomotor skills and management. PTAs will be trained on their roles and responsibilities as well as general school management to continue to take responsibility for the management of the schools. To retain teachers in school and enhance access to learning, the project will provide an incentive and support package to all teachers starting from ECD centers, Primary schools and teachers under the ALP centers. The project will also work closely with the nutrition program to stimulate learning at an early age by integrating ECD into nutrition centers. Thirdly, this intervention will ensure improved recovery and resilience capacity among children by linking education with child protection services as well as psychosocial support to children and adolescents/youth including survivors of gender-based violence, aged 3-17 years. To achieve this, the project will integrate child friendly spaces (CFS) into schools. These CFS’ will have school clubs that include girl guides and scouts for boys. They will also have drawing and art competitions. To mitigate effects of food insecurity on education and encourage learners to stay in school especially during the lean period, this intervention will include a school feeding program. Through this intervention, learners will be provided with fortified meals during lunch. This feeding will be managed by the PTA on a day-to-day basis. To build resilience, each school will have a kitchen garden to train both PTA and school children on vegetable training. Vegetables harvested will also supplement school meals. In summary the project will: 1. Establish/Rehabilitate 24 TLS with environmentally friendly materials. 2. Establish/Rehabilitate 24 gender segregated latrines for children and youths. 3. Establish/Rehabilitate 24 on-site kitchens to provide shelter for preparation of meals. 4. Provide school children with school meals/take home rations. 5. Establish 24 School gardens learning demonstration on vegetable production. 6. Equip 140 teachers with Education in Emergencies (EiE) skills to provide improved teaching and learning experience for affected children. 7. Train 140 teachers (M: 70 F: 70) on school nutrition programming, psychosocial support, life-skills awareness, commodity management, storage, record keeping, data collection and school supervision and monitoring. 8. Train 140 PTA members (M: 70 F: 70) and 56 cooks on emergency preparedness, roles and responsibilities. 9. Train 700 vulnerable girls on Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) and form 24 school hygiene clubs in 24 schools. 10. Form 24 school farmer clubs and train 240 members, teachers and learners on vegetable growing. 11. Form and train 24 school health clubs of 240 members on WASH, health and nutrition guidelines.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

World Relief South Sudan (WRSS) has in place a PSEA policy. All staffs are sensitized on the policy and are required to sign on it as proof of having gone through and comprehending the policy. During project implementation, all staff are expected to adhere to the PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The protection team within WRSS is charged with the responsibility to ensure continuous sensitization about PSEA. The protection officer will be provided time to explain to beneficiaries concerning PSEA as well as the role of community in ensuring PSEA. The School Management Committee (SMC) and Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be involved in all aspects of school management including location of schools to ensure tenure of land on which the school will be located. They will ensure that the location of the school site is accessible to all learners and is free from risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Teaching will be conducted and completed mid-afternoon to allow women and girls enough time to reach home before dark. WRSS will also provide a feedback mechanisms. All beneficiaries will be sensitized on the availability of this feedback mechanism for their use. Confidentiality on any information provided through the feedback mechanism will be ensured to build the confidence of the community to use this feedback mechanism. Complains registered will include Sexual Exploitation and Abuse or any other form of protection violence/concern. The reported complaints will be handled with upmost respect and confidentiality and will be handled firmly. Establishment of school help desks will enable teachers and students to receive feedback at the school level. SMCs/PTAs will be able to receive feedback from community/parents during door-to-door mobilization campaigns, concerning drop outs of school aged children. Teachers will receive feedback directly during WRSS trainings. The community level engagement will be done through conducting regular meetings with the teachers/volunteers and SMCs/PTAs. WRSS sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at community level and discuss progresses, challenges, plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. Feedback is discussed with the management team in Juba and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The most vulnerable members of community will be included in every aspect of project implementation. The project will target 50% of learners as women and girls. Women and girls are often discriminated from accessing education, yet they bear the burden of ensuring proper health and nutrition at home, and educating future generations. Providing women and girls with an opportunity to have better access to education will empower them in understanding health and nutrition concerns at home as well as ensure that hygiene and sanitation at home is improved. Learners living with disabilities will be given special consideration. For instance, in establishment of latrines, handwashing facilities and classrooms will be constricted with the needs of these leaners taken into consideration. Girls will be targeted for MHM training and distribution of MHM kits to ensure that they continue being in school throughout the learning period. Similarly, teaching will also take into consideration needs of the leaners. Most of the project leaners are illiterate while most of the teachers may not express themselves fluently in English or another foreign language. The project will employ teachers from the local community who can be able to communicate and conduct teaching in the local language. Teaching will be competency based and practical sessions which emphasize more on learning by doing than theory. Information Education and Communication (IEC) Materials will include more pictorial information for ease of understanding. Teaching materials to be provided will also be in Nuer local language for ease of translation and learning in ECD centers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meetings with the volunteers and community-based committees as well as focus group discussions. WR sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meetings at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plans and opportunities, community concerns, and risks. This feedback is discussed with the management team in the country office and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs. A variety of mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to provide inputs and express concerns. • First, local staff hired at the community level, are trained on how to receive feedback from participants, serving both as staff and as trusted community members. • Second, program management staff at the field level conduct regular Focus Group Discussions with project participants and relevant community members and leaders. To ensure the comfort of project beneficiaries, various discussion sessions are conducted with segregated groups of individuals with particular sex, age, and minority (e.g. non-dominating clan or sub-tribe) identities. • Third, teachers and PTA members are able to receive feedback from students and parents one-on-one at the TLS’ and other education outreach activities. • Fourth, beneficiaries are informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a WR staff member at a project site.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be the gist of project development. Given that WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time, WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and times. In the past, WRSS was able to conduct post distribution monitoring, Post-planting and post-planting surveys. The project has also been able to conduct Knowledge, Attitude and Practice Surveys. The baselines being used to develop this project are from those past surveys. Appropriate assessments / survey will be continued even during this project. Beneficiaries will be engaged during mobilization and sensitization. Project objectives and key activities will be discussed. Feedback provided from those sensitization meetings will be used to tailor project interventions to meet the needs of the beneficiaries. The Parents Teachers Association (PTA) will also be very instrumental in providing feedback on the project. Selection criteria and targeting of beneficiaries will be discussed with the SMC/PTA. The PTA will also be involved in monitoring project progress. During these monitoring sessions, views from the PTA as well as views of the Leaners will be used to adjust subsequent schedule and type of activities to address the needs expressed by the leaners. For instance, if certain trainings were not well understood, they could be offered again.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Women and young children that may be at risk through the implementation of project activities. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries to avoid causing harm will be prioritized. WRSS will therefore undertake several measures to ensure protection of the intended beneficiaries. These measures will include: Learning centers will only be established after consultation with the intended beneficiaries to ensure they are sited in safe locations and easily accessible to women and girls. Learning centers will be established in consultation of the local government and community to ensure that the PTAs have tenure of land for learners to continue accessing learning without interruptions. The project will construct separate latrines for males and females, clearly marked in pictorial form for illiterate users and work with community to ensure they are used by the indicated sex. For privacy, locks will be provided on the doors to latrines. The safety risk to children will be ensured by making size of drop hole small enough Recruitment of learners will promote equitable and impartial access to learning for all. This will include collection and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Teaching will be done in a respectful manner and inclusive of cultural and religious practice of the intended learners. The project will as well endeavor to make infrastructure adaptions to WASH structures to make them accessible to persons with reduced mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities and children under five years). All learners, especially women and girls will be made aware of the available complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure their views are taken into consideration and any grievances are registered and addressed. Every school will have a focal point for complaints registration. This project will also work with protection players to ensure child protection in schools.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The Education project will target to have school feeding especially during the lean period when malnutrition rates are highest. School feeding will not only help to retain learners in school but will also reduce the proportion of population in IPC 3,4 and 5 by providing meals. The project will also establish kitchen gardens and training children and PTA members on vegetable production. The vegetables produced will supplement meals in school and therefore improve on nutrition. The training on gardening will improve the agronomic skills of children and community who will then be expected to practice the same at home, thus increasing food availability at the household.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

This project aims at establishing learning shelters to ensure that learners can learn in a safe environment sheltered from adverse atmospheric conditions. The project will also establish/rehabilitate handwashing facilities and latrines to ensure improved hygiene and sanitation environment within the school. These facilities will be constructed to provide for the needs of the disabled and young children. Handwashing facilities will be constructed in such a way that they can be reached by children when they want to wash their hands. Latrines will be constructed with holes that will be small enough for safety of children. similarly, the latrines will be disaggregated for male and female use and have lockable facilities for safety of the female users. The school feeding times will be designed to ensure that disabled can be served first on the cues. The desks and sitting places will be appropriate and take into consideration of the use by the disabled learners.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This project will target to integrate ECD activities into nutrition program. As such, cognitive and psychomotor skills of the children under five years of age will be stimulated to develop from an early age. Within the ECD centers, this project will also target to provide fortified meals to reduce instances of malnutrition within the under-fives.
