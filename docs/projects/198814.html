Project Summary

Displaced households in Cabo Delgado often lack legal documentation, including civil and property documents. This can have far-reaching implications on their ability to fully enjoy freedom of movement, access basic services or secure land tenure rights. NRC will address this through a 2-prong approach. Firstly, the ICLA programme will support displacement affected households in obtaining legal identity documentation through the establishment of mobile legal clinics and access points providing information, counselling, and referral to relevant Government authorities. Secondly, through the co-chairing of the HLP Area of Responsibility (AoR) Working Group, NRC will provide leadership, technical support, and capacity building to the humanitarian community, to mainstream HLP into assessment tools used in localities where HLP issues of highest prevalence and concern are reported. NRC will carry out joint HLP field assessments with the HLP AOR co-chair, Protection Cluster, and other humanitarian actors focusing on sensitization and information sharing regarding HLP legal frameworks.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

IDPs in Cabo Delgado often lack civil documentation (birth certificates, legal identity/national ID cards, and HLP documents), exposing them to myriad protection concerns and preventing them from accessing their rights, including freedom of movement, access to state services such as education and health care and participation in the formal economy. Owing to their lack of legal identity, IDPs are faced with multiple barriers to securing employment, formal economy financial inclusion (opening a bank account, registering a mobile SIM card and use of mobile money services), and self-reliance. IDPs in relocation sites and selected host community areas will be identified and referred to national authorities responsible for issuing relevant documentation. NRC will work through mobile legal clinics, to provide legal assistance by utilizing brigades of functionaries (DNIC and MOJ officials and technicians) and conservadores (registry officials) to facilitate issuance of civil documentation. While doing so, NRC will also provide information and counselling on how to access these services so that IDPs are empowered with the knowledge about how to access these services for replacement and renewal of documentation. NRC has been centrally involved in the set-up and launch of the Cabo Delgado HLP Area of Responsibility (AOR), structured under the Protection Cluster., NRC Co-Chairing. Through this leadership role, NRC has consistently promoted the mainstreaming of HLP considerations in the northern Mozambique response. NRC will also continue to work on HLP issues through its programme activities in Mueda and newly accessible areas in 2023. NRC’s work on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) will provide support to IDPs to access information related to their occupancy status in relocation sites. NRC will support local authorities with technical support to adapt sensitization and context-appropriate land tenure documentation to strengthen it from a legal standpoint, including providing increased security of tenure for women. NRC will continue to Co-Chair the HLP AOR as technical lead, thereby raising awareness among humanitarian and government actors on HLP issues of greatest concern in the northern Mozambique response. The HLP AOR will promote the participatory and inclusive adaptation of tenure verification guidelines to the Cabo Delgado context and promote the uptake of the guidelines among actors working on issues relying on land and resources.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IEC with messaging about PSEA, anti-corruption, and informing potential beneficiaries that NRC and humanitarian services being free-of-charge will be mainstreamed into community engagement and information/counselling sessions. Although not specifically identified, there is the risk that some young male members of the IDP population may encounter secondary screening to verify their identity and affiliations. Safeguards will be put in place to inform communities about the process of identity verification involved in mobile legal clinics, along with the consequences of non-Mozambican applicants being identified while attempting to gain fraudulent access to Mozambican citizenship. The legal repercussions for false testimony during mobile brigade verification will feature in information and counseling sessions. The overall protective benefits of having access to civil documentation are well understood and are frequently reiterated during discussions with affected communities, community leaders, government officials, and humanitarian actors. Freedom of movement is a major concern for IDP populations who lack legal identity documentation. While some potential risks in programming do exist, the Protection risks of not engaging in projects that provide access to civil documentation far outweigh potential risks of harm that could arise during the process of providing access to documentation. Where resources do not allow all residents of a relocation site to benefit from access to the respective civil documentation issuance process, a vulnerability-based targeting criteria in alignment with Protection Cluster norms and approaches will be adopted to ensure that the most vulnerable can gain access to the relevant service.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

For land tenure documentation, special consideration is required for women’s HLP rights. These will be incorporated in discussions with communities and advocacy for inclusion of women’s names on land tenure documentation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Disabilities will be identified through NRC’s registration and verification process and confirmed through focus group discussions with affected communities. Measures to ensure appropriate access, including geographic positioning of mobile legal clinics, will be integrated into the “mobile” approach. Tailored information and counselling sessions for male and female beneficiaries will be produced. NRC will request mobile brigades to include both male and female functionaries.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Macro level: NRC is committed globally to reducing the environmental impact of its operations through its initiative “Greening the Orange.” to National level: Frequency of staff use of UNHAS flights is considered in programme planning to reduce the carbon footprint of NRC’s fieldwork. When and where possible, in 2023, access to coastal areas will use boats, if feasible and safe. NRC staff are encouraged to use reusable bottles for water to reduce single-use plastic consumption. The NRC office uses re-usable bottled water jerry cans in offices spaces. Programme level: Issuance of civil documentation is not expected to significantly impact the environment. Small 2.8 KV, petrol fueled generators will power electronic equipment of mobile legal clinic brigades. It is well documented that strengthening security of tenure increases responsible use of resources by land users. Promotion of tenure security documentation may encourage increased stewardship of land-based resources by tenure and resource rights holders.
