Project Summary

The 2022 South Sudan HNO shows that the ongoing protracted crises has left 8.9 million people in dire need of humanitarian assistance which is an increase from 8.3 million people reported in 2021. Despite the signature of the Revitalized peace agreement, the Country continues to face different shocks that contribute to inadequate access to basic services. Access to basic WASH services remains very low with the living standards and well-being of 6.1 million people expected to be impacted due to inadequate access to WASH. This project will provide WASH integrated programming with Nutrition and emergency basic WASH services to the most vulnerable population affected by floods and inter-communal violence (ICV) that leads to massive destruction of facilities, IPC food insecurity and disease outbreaks. This is in line with the 2022 HRP prioritization of communities in locations with the utmost WASH Severity and the Strategic Objectives that contributes to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition to ensure that vulnerable people who experience multi sectoral severity levels 4 and 5 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross sectoral essential services and meet their needs. Further, the project is also focused on providing access to basic WASH services and mitigate WASH GBV in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties.
Both temporary and (semi-)permanent solutions will be considered to ensure that most vulnerable communities are reached with timely and equitable basic WASH services. The project will deliver rapid assistance to affected communities affected by a sudden or rapid onset of disasters mainly with temporary services. This will involve distribution of WASH minimum package to promote access to safe water, access to basic and dignified gender sensitive sanitation and hygiene promotion. Emergency WASH services for 3 months (i.e. EPR/RRT) and more sustained assistance that include rehabilitation of water points and sanitation in crowded settings will be considered. Repairs and maintenance of water facilities including contamination protection through flood-proofing and disinfection and refresher trainings of WASH community structures will be done to ensure continuity and proper management of water sources. Vulnerable HHs will be provided with water containers and minimum package for water treatment products to enable safe and hygienic water access. Communities will be trained on hygiene and sanitation around water sources, maintenance of water containers and hygiene behaviour and practices to reduce disease, prevent malnutrition and improve general well-being. Hygiene promotion will focus on safe excreta management, handwashing, and safe water chain among other WASH domains. WASH NFIs will be distributed to enhance hygiene standards of vulnerable households. At Juba IDP camp sites 1 & 3, the project will provide WaSH services in line with the WaSH transition strategy that is focused on family shared facilities, on-site waste management and participatory hygiene promotion. Oxfam will directly implement the project but in close coordination with the National and Sub-National WASH Cluster coordination, EPR/ERRT task force, Protection Cluster, relevant TWGs groups, The State line Ministries of Physical Infrastructure and Health and the local authorities. This will ensure that there is complementarity among all actors devoid of duplication of services. Important key cross cutting issues will be mainstreamed during the entire project cycle including Protection, Gender, Disability Inclusion, Accountability to Affected Population and Conflict Sensitivity.

1 - Needs

Ranking 185 out of 189 countries in the 2022 Human Development Index (HDI), South Sudan's context remains complex, with socio-economic development impacted by multiple shocks and crises including conflict, public health emergencies, and climate change where above normal rainfall continues to cause widespread flooding leading to displacement of vulnerable population. The South Sudan HNO 2022 shows that the living standards and wellbeing of an estimated 6 million people will be impacted in 2022 due to inadequate access to WASH. The WASH conditions in the Nile Basin of Jonglei among other 6 states were found to be most severe. Only 39% (urban population) and 34% (rural population) have access to sufficient water to meet their household needs. Out of this population, 33% of households are forced to travel for longer than 30 minutes to access water thereby creating potential GBV risks among women and girls (12% of the population) and difficulties for persons living with disabilities. Access to sanitation facilities in South Sudan is very low with 80% of households lacking access latrines. For Jonglei, less than 10% of households have access to latrines with open defecation being the norm among most communities. Long-lasting development challenges largely explain the fact that South Sudan has historically displayed one of the lowest sanitation coverage globally. Latrine use is also considered a taboo in most communities especially among the rural and/or pastoral population who often prefer to defecate in nearby bushes. The perennial flooding always damages existing sanitation infrastructure making communities hesitant to invest in, reconstruct, or repair the facilities. On hygiene practices, only 16% of the households have access to soap for handwashing with Jonglei State having the lowest coverage as low as 4%. This is one of the main barriers that hinders improvement of personal hygiene among most communities in South Sudan. Increasing insecurity due to localized community violence and disputes over land, has been an underlying driver of WASH conditions in Jonglei limiting the ability of service providers to deliver WASH services while also restricting people’s access to existing WASH infrastructure. Protection concerns are present when accessing WASH services with a significant 18% of households feeling unsafe while collecting water (FSNMS, 2021). Of those, 12% of women and girls reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns. The issue is particularly acute in Akobo among other counties where more than 40% of households feel unsafe while collecting water. Oxfam’s Integrated Needs Assessment of August 2021 show that 34% of water sources in Akobo are dysfunctional, and that 62% of HHs collect drinking water from unprotected sources, with most HHs (52%) walking long distances to fetch water. Access to latrines remains very low leading to high rates of open defecation. In GPAA, a lack of functional water sources and water storage containers is common with 83% of the population accessing water from unsafe sources, along with high rates of open defecation. The lack of access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity has led to a high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases, with 65.1% of children being malnourished. Widespread flooding has continued to impact people and their livelihoods across the Country with 854,000 people affected since May 2021 in 33 of the 78 counties (UNOCHA report, November 2021). Flood-affected people in flood prone areas remain vulnerable as floodwaters continue to rise. Physical access is a huge challenge for humanitarians trying to reach affected people as roads become impassable and floodwaters cut off communities.

2 - Project activities

Oxfam will contribute to the WASH Cluster Sectoral Objective 1 that aims to contribute to reducing WASH related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition. In addition, the project will contribute to WASH Cluster Sectoral Objective 2.1 to provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties and Objective 2.2 WASH specific activities for GBV (or mitigating WASH GBV). The WASH interventions considered under this project include: WASH Cluster SO1: Assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facilities and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water through construction/repair of dysfunctional boreholes to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission. Targeted support on Nutrition facility package through distribution of standard WASH kit items to unit “caretaker/SAM child” seeking nutrition services at nutrition facilities. The standard kit will comprise: (1) one bucket with tap, 20l; (2) one bucket without tap, 20l or one jerrycan (10 or 20l), (3) chlorine tablets for 3 months (e.g. Aquatabs 67 mg) – depending on NTU, (4) Sedimentation/Disinfection powder for 3 months (e.g. PUR) with one piece of filter clothes and (5) soap, 250 grs per person per month for 3 months. Enhance WASH service through construction/repair/rehabilitation of WASH facilities. Establish new/repair existing but dysfunctional handwashing facilities to promote hand hygiene. Construct new/repair existing toilet facilities to ensure a ratio of 1 toilet to 50 adults and 1 toilet for 20 children within the Nutrition facilities. Provide WASH services (focus on water supply and hygiene) in communities with high GAM and WASH vulnerability. WASH Cluster SO2.1: Distribution of household water treatment and safe storage items: containers, Aqua Tabs, PUR sachets and filter cloth to enhance access to safe water at the household. Emergency Surface Water Treatment (SWAT) system operation for displaced population in concentrated sites. To include water treatment, testing and quality monitoring through community-based water quality monitors. Hygiene promotion through distribution of WASH NFIs and female dignity kit items. WASH Cluster SO2.2: Conduct Gender Safety Audit prior to all WASH interventions including other cross-cutting issues such as disability Inclusion, AAP sessions and safe programming sessions. Repair/rehabilitate and upgrade broken boreholes including contamination protection through flood proofing including elevation of platforms. Train/Refresh community Pump mechanics (with at least 50% female composition) in the Operation & Maintenance of boreholes/ hand pumps. Conduct refresher training targeting Water Management Committees (7 members per committee with at least 50% female composition) on the management of the WASH facilities and ensure that they are self-sufficient. Construct gender sensitive and disability friendly elevated emergency sanitation facilities at concentrated sites. Repair and rehabilitate stances of broken down and existing sanitation facilities to guarantee safety and privacy for users including PLWDs. Conduct regular jerrycan cleaning and environmental clean-up campaigns at concentrated sites. Hygiene promotion, behavior change awareness and sensitization of the target communities which will include use of public address at public spaces, door-to-door campaign visits, radio messaging and use of IEC visibility materials where appropriate. Training/refresh Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs), Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) and community leaders on community led emergency and sustainable WASH interventions. Establish communal handwashing facilities at public and other strategic spaces to promote good hand hygiene practices among target communities. Establish/reinforce hygiene clubs in schools to promote WASH In Schools.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Guided by the accountability framework of the country programme the project will have built-in feedback and/or complaint management system that is effective, accessible, and safe for intended beneficiaries, affected communities. Feedback and compliant collection mechanisms will be defined after discussion and agreement with people affected by the project/programme. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community. We keep records of complaints and feedback regularly using the recording data base we have and follow up on the referral and response progress. views and requests of community members will be feedback into the programme cycle to make sure that affected people are making decisions in the issues affecting their lives in relation to project deliveries. Feedback from beneficiaries that have influenced the way activities or outputs are delivered will be documented for evidence and learning. Oxfam will also ensure that the project is implemented through a participatory approach where the target population shall be actively involved during the entire cycle and are put at the center of the interventions. This will ensure the communities' proactive involvement and engagement at each stage of the project cycle. This will be achieved through collecting community feedback through community engagement, FGDs, project surveys, semi-structured interviews, community meetings and daily engagement based on the humanitarian principles, as part of activity implementation and monitoring.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will ensure that feedback from the most vulnerable community members is provided through the established CFM mechanism. This shall be carried out through holding continuous meetings with the beneficiaries through door-to-door visits, having coordination meetings with key stakeholders, community leaders and government authority to ensure that all issues raised are amicably addressed. The project will ensure timely and continuous monitoring of project implementation and collection of feedback from the beneficiaries through conducting post distribution monitoring for WASH NFIs and other items. Person to person talk sessions will be conducted through use of the local language to collect the views and issues relating to the project from the most vulnerable persons. FGD sessions and dialogue sessions will be conducted with persons living with disabilities, not so literate persons including women and girls to ensure that their views are captured and are addressed. Oxfam has existing Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) that provides for follow-up action on issues reported and are affecting the beneficiaries and thereafter provide the necessary feedback. The CRM approaches will be adopted and used under this project to ensure that all interventions are community centered and encourage community dialogue, consultation, and their participation in delivery of WASH activities. The project will ensure that girls, boys, women, and men (including older people and those with disabilities) have access to timely and appropriate feedback on any complaints. This will promote corrective actions towards addressing their specific protection and assistance needs and documentation of the same. Regular meetings with community representatives will be held to provide a platform where Oxfam will regularly inform and update community representatives on the planned, progress and achieved project activities including timelines. Participation of female and male beneficiaries and the most disadvantaged and vulnerable community groups will be ensured through various mechanisms including regular gender assessments, monthly feedback sessions via sex specific FGDs, surveys and assessments, community consultative sessions, verification exercises and others.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Oxfam will adopt conflict sensitive and ‘do no harm’ approaches with the full participation and inclusion of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation period. Should any issues arise related to WASH, Gender and Protection, the CPC/WASH Committee and other stakeholders will be actively involved. Gender and Protection will be mainstreamed in line with the Oxfam commitments to promote gender equality and women’s rights and ensure safe programming. Oxfam has a Minimum Standards for Gender in Emergencies to ensure a consistent approach in all its interventions that will be carefully followed. A rapid gender and protection analysis will be conducted to identify the most vulnerable amongst target communities, as well as the relevant protection threats, they face and gender-specific needs. To promote gender responsive, safe, dignified, and equitable access to assistance, Oxfam will consult the target communities on the appropriate locations/sites and time for distributions. For example, women and girls of different age groups will be consulted to identify and address their reproductive health specifically menstruation-related needs and to ensure support with the distribution of dignity kits. Gender responsive selection criteria will be developed to ensure women’s voices in decision-making platforms. Community Protection Committees and women’s group will be specifically consulted to identify GBV risks and ensure safe access to water points. Oxfam has an existing Complaints Response Mechanism for follow-up action on issues reported and the provision of timely feedback. This will ensure that girls, boys, women and men have access to gender sensitive, safe, timely and appropriate feedback options. Full participation of women, men, boys and girls will be ensured through consultation and community mobilization approach and safe approaches guided by GBV risk analysis will be adopted to promote women’s meaningful participation. Oxfam’s policy on safeguarding prevents, protects, and promotes the rights and dignity of beneficiaries. On recruitment, every member of Oxfam staff is required to sign mandatory policies and the Code of Conduct. This is complemented by mandatory safeguarding, gender and power training for Oxfam staff, partners, consultants, contractors, and volunteers. To ensure a safe, accessible, and dignified response which will create no (further) harm and help reduce existing vulnerabilities and risks, elements of safe programming will be kept at the core of each phase of the response ranging from assessment to evaluation. Community inclusion and feedback will help to design and deliver a safe and accessible response. The WASH and protection team will ensure that WASH infrastructure is built in an accessible and safe location for the community, to avoid any social or GBV-related risk. WASH facilities (communal latrines) will be clearly marked with the appropriate signage for gender segregation of facilities as well as being placed in the most convenient locations to be accessible to the community, including those living with disabilities. Oxfam considers strong community awareness on Sexual exploitation and abuse and jointly set culturally sensitive and socially acceptable reporting mechanisms in cases of happening. This form part of our mandate in empowering the communities that we serve in all our target locations. As we have safeguarding focal points in each project location and at Juba level we will encourage community members to report on staff behavior to these focal persons using the most preferred and safe mechanism they think and Oxfam will make sure appropriate action is taken on time after investigation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Oxfam has an existing Complaint Response Mechanisms (CRM) that provides follow-up action on any issues reported during the project period, to ensure that necessary feedback is completed. These CRM approach will be adopted and used under this project to ensure that all interventions are community centered and encourage community dialogue, consultation, and community participation in delivery of activities. The project will ensure that girls, boys, women, and men(including older people and those with disabilities) have access to timely and appropriate feedback on any complaints related to WASH/GBV affecting the community. This will promote corrective actions towards addressing their specific protection and assistance needs and accurate documentation. Multiple and complementary feedback and complaints collection mechanisms will be considered to avail safe and convenient options to the community. Appropriate mechanisms for sharing feedbacks/complaints will be selected through discussion and agreement with the target community. We keep records of complaints and feedback regularly using the recording data base we have and follow up on the referral and response progress. Oxfam has developed community engagement tools in WASH for use in ensuring that the community is engaged, consulted, and actively involved during the entire project cycle. In addition, Oxfam will adopt the guidance on best practices in sanitation 'Sani tweaks' through which it details on the steps under which WASH practitioners should adopt in ensuring that the community is consulted and engaged in the entire programming. This includes consultation during design and siting of all WASH facilities to ensure that the views of girls, boys, women, and men including persons living with disabilities are considered. This will ensure that issues of access and the appropriate design of safe WASH facilities and services are considered to reduce associated GBV risks. Oxfam will share timely, relevant, and clear information to the target community and other stakeholder through organizing inception workshops, conducting different community level meetings, Meeting with local authorities, etc. Most of the information we share are details of the project including the project activities and budget, beneficiary entitlements, reporting SHEA, etc. Oxfam also ensures that vulnerable community groups are included in information sharing and information shared is in languages, formats and media that can be easily understood by the communities we work with. Participation will be ensured through engaging of women, men, girls, and boys within target communities and their representatives in all the stages of the project to make sure that Programme decisions are based on informed consent and feedback. Oxfam also regularly conducts exit interviews at the spot of distribution of WASH NFIs and setting up compliance desk in all distribution sites to get real time information about the quality of distributed items, the appropriateness of distribution location, etc. Moreover, conducting post distribution monitoring (PDM) is a common practice in all field locations to get views and opinions of targeted people and gage the quality of distributed items, safety, and access to distribution location, etc. Periodic review and reflection session that will be organized with local community and stakeholders is also another platform used to share information and get views and opinions from the people we are serving. GBV risk analysis shall be undertaken before the onset of implementation of activities through FGDs and other consultative forums to ensure inclusiveness of women and girls in sitting and design of safe, equitable access and appropriate WASH facilities. The project will ensure that staff from both WASH and GBV partners co-chair and are actively involved in the risk analysis. Community leaders, other WASH and Protection partners and the local authorities will also be consulted and involved.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Oxfam will ensure adequate access to clean water for drinking, cooking, washing hands, other hygiene needs and domestic use. This will help mitigate the impact on communities in IPC 4 & IPC 5 situation where access to water is key for human survival. The project will ensure that broken down water facilities are restored through conducting repairs and rehabilitation works to hand pumps and boreholes to ensure that communities have access to clean water for drinking and for food production. Access to water will mitigate the risk factors of waterborne diseases such as cholera and the ongoing COVID-19 disease outbreak. Oxfam will ensure that the project only targets the most affected locations that experience high WASH vulnerability (4 and 5) as per the WASH Cluster and are in IPC levels 4 and 5. The project will be implemented in close coordination with the key stakeholders such as the WASH Cluster, line government departments, local authorities and other Clusters such as Food and Security Livelihood (FSL), Health and Nutrition and Protection to address the extreme humanitarian needs among the most affected population. The project will also ensure that vulnerable communities in IPC 4 & 5 situation have access to WASH NFI items such as water storage containers and soap to promote safe water storage and enhance personal hygiene. Provision of sanitation facilities through repair and rehabilitation of existing latrines will help improve the coverage for latrines and thereby reducing the incidences of open defecation which is a key cause of malnutrition in IPC famine situation.
