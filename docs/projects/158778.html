Project Summary

The initiative aims at increasing the access of vulnerable women, women who use drugs to the services and therefore reducing their HIV vulnerability. The project objectives are:
• Increase awareness and understanding regarding comprehensive gender-sensitive HIV services for women who use drugs (WUD) among health care managers, service providers and decision makers;
• Increase knowledge of women and girls who use drugs about safe behaviour;
• Motivate women and girls to access HIV prevention, care and support services;
• Create favourable, non-violent environment, free from stigma and discrimination for provision of services for women and girls who use drugs;
• Improve and monitor quality of services.
The project will lead to development of accessible gender responsive services and, therefore, will contribute to reduction of HIV cases among WUD in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. To achieve this goal the project will partner with local counterparts to prevent, and treat HIV; and, to promote peer involvement in addressing stigma and discrimination. It will strengthen existing peer networks, support their development, and advocate for their inclusion. It will also advocate with the government to establish these groups legally within existing civil society structures.
Beneficiaries:
- 30 doctors and 30 service providers will get direct technical support
from the project to design and implement gender sensitive HIV and other social support services in specific localities.
- About 300 vulnerable women and girls (220 in Donetsk and 90 in Luhansk), socially marginalised and poor, female partners of the drug users, women who use drugs and involved in commercial
sex to facilitate their access to integrated and comprehensive HIV prevention, care and support services.

1 - Needs

Since 2014, Ukraine’s conflict with Russia has significantly affected regions that have a high HIV burden. The Ukraine State Statistics Service estimates Ukraine’s total population as 42.4 million. Ukraine’s census data predates the Maidan Revolution and the ongoing war in the eastern Donbas region. Approximately 2.3 million people live in Russian-occupied Crimea and another 3 million in separatist/Russian-occupied portions of eastern Luhansk and Donetsk regions. Over 1.4 million people from these regions are internally displaced. Spectrum 2019 results estimate 237,000 PLHIV in Ukraine (0.5% of the total population) with the majority of cases among men. An estimated 196,000 PLHIV live in Ukrainian government-controlled areas (GCA). Case reporting data show 142,000 (72%) of PLHIV in GCAs were registered at an AIDS Center as of Jan 2019. Estimated 52,000 PLHIV remain unaware of their status or have not yet registered at an AIDS Center in GCAs. Approximately 17% of total estimated PLHIV live in occupied Crimea or uncontrolled Luhansk/Donetsk (NGCA). Ukraine’s HIV epidemic remains geographically concentrated within a belt of regions in the South and East .The armed conflict has worsened the risk of sexual, physical, and economic violence faced by women who use drugs. It has also negatively impacted WUD’s access to essential healthcare, including HIV treatment and prevention, and opioid substitution therapy (OST).Many WUD in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts were displaced by the conflict and migrated to other parts of Ukraine but have subsequently returned after being unable to find housing or employment - a challenge made worse by stigma and discrimination . Stigma remains a central barrier in receiving state-guaranteed medical and social services for WUD, including obstetric and gynaecological care. Self-stigmatisation and non-compliance with gender stereotypes and expectations about the behaviour of a “good mother” affects relationships with other family members, including children, and is leading to the social isolation of WUD. The stigma and discrimination that they experience, which often is heightened by gender-based violence and abuse, increases their risk for contracting HIV and other blood-borne viruses as well as a wide range of sexually transmitted infections. Many women are also torture survivors and are therefore in-need of specialised services - they need psychological and psychotherapeutic support on their traumatic experience of torture or other violence. National and international research, services, guidelines, training programs and surveillance concerning people who inject drugs remain overwhelmingly gender neutral or male-focused. Partly as a result, limited data exist as to women’s share of those who inject drugs, and their specific challenges and needs are rarely recognized or understood. WUD are an invisible group for most UN missions and human rights organisations working in the conflict zone. The health and human rights impact of such invisibility can be very harmful. The shortage of trained gender responsive service providers often discourages WUD from accessing the services.There is the critical need to improve understanding of comprehensive HIV services for WID among health care managers and providers at both government and civil society organisations. Implementation of HIV prevention, treatment and care service delivery expansion will be focused in 2 oblasts townships in Kurakhove and Svytlodarsk (Donetsk oblast) and Starobilsk, Sievierodonetsk (Luhansk oblast). There are high levels of drug use and HIV risk behaviour in these areas, and no existing service providers for WUD.

2 - Activities & outcomes

The project intends to achieve: - Improved accessibility to HIV prevention and health protection services for WUD and sexual partners of men who use drugs in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts: capacity building, draft SOPs/protocol on provision of the comprehensive gender sensitive package of HIV prevention, treatment and care services.- Reduced stigma and discrimination toward, and greater involvement of women who use drugs (WUD) in the HIV prevention: to support the formation and expansion of peer groups and networks in collaboration with HelpAge; strengthening existing peer networks; supporting their development and legalization.The Project aimed at providing of three sessions (x2 days) training events for HIV-related service providers, managers, health-care workers and outreach workers, as well as those planning to work directly with women who inject drugs, and complying with one day event for trainees together with representatives from Donetsk and Luhansk oblast health care administrations, drug control agencies and other relevant officials in charge of policy and programmes development in the field of HIV prevention among women who inject drugs (WID).Implementation of HIV prevention, treatment and care service delivery expansion will be focused in 2 oblasts townships in Kurakhove and Svytlodarsk (Donetsk oblast) and Starobilsk, Sievierodonetsk (Luhansk oblast). There are high levels of drug use and HIV risk behaviour in these areas, and no existing service providers for WUD. Consultancy to HelpAge will be provided to support the peer networks.
