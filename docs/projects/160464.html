Strengthening the resilience of conflict affected persons (IDPs, returnees and host community members) through the provision of sustainable livelihoods

Basic Info

Project Name

Strengthening the resilience of conflict affected persons (IDPs, returnees and host community members) through the provision of sustainable livelihoods

Start Date

01/01/2020

End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

The action aims to strengthen the resilience of conflict affected communities through the engagement of IDPs, returnees and host community members in income generating activities. The action will be implemented through a distribution of micro-enterprise, self-employment, self-sustenance and vocational training grants to IDPs and vulnerable populations such as persons with low income, families with many children, households with disabled members, single-headed households etc. The activities will also prioritize unemployed, women, young adults (youth) and persons with disabilities derived from the conflict. Proposed activities aim to target people residing close to contact line (GCA and NGCA), and in rural areas in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) with particular focus on agricultural businesses.

1 - Needs

Despite the fact that economy in Ukraine is growing and industrial production stabilize throughout the country, the economy in the Donbass region continue to deteriorate due to the instability, resulting in limited investments in the region’s industries and few employment opportunities. The unemployment rate in Eastern Ukraine has increased since the onset hostilities and the continued flow of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) further increased limited employment opportunities. In Donetsk region (GCA) the unemployment rate in the beginning of the year 2019 was 14.5%. According to the IOM’s National Monitoring System Report on Internally Displaced Persons (NMS), June 2019, the share of IDPs residing in Donetsk and Luhansk regions mentioned being employed after the displacement was 47%. Lack of vacancies in general and low pay for the proposed vacancies were mentioned among the IDPs that are actively seeking employment. In the beginning of the conflict, by order of the Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine, for the safety of staff and students, such universities as Donetsk National Technical University were transferred from Donetsk and Luhansk cities to various towns of Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA) without any job opportunities. According to the latest data published by International Labour Organization, as of April 2017 the level of unemployment rate among youth in Ukraine is 21.6%. The high level of unemployment among youth further contributes towards increasing instability in communities, as economically inactive youth remain more vulnerable to negative influence. As mentioned in the FSL Dashboard, January – March 2019, food access is heavily dependent on cash income. Food expenditure increased in Donetsk region (GCA) in 2019 and is equal to UAH 3,951 (app. US$ 163), while in Luhansk region (GCA) remains the same – UAH 2,567. According to the findings received during the post distribution monitoring (PDM) on multi-purpose cash assistance, received assistance was spend to buy food (15%). IOM’s experience implementing various livelihood programmes highlights the need to provide targeted support for women to become fully engaged in economic activity. In the previous phases of IOM’s DfID-funded livelihood programme, the number of women applying for micro-enterprise and business scale-up grants was limited. In discussion with beneficiaries, lack of confidence, flexibility of larger business ownership and poor communication and representation skills prevents women from seeking to grow their existing businesses or apply for larger grants. Significant gender imbalances were observed in all spheres of current employment of IDPs according to National Monitoring System (NMS), March 2019. Among IDPs actively looking for employment, 79% are women. The majority of IDPs who are actively looking for employment (58%) mentioned that lack of vacancies was one the most common difficulties they faced with, especially in rural areas.According to the NMS survey, June 2019, the economically inactive population amounted to 75% among the surveyed returnees to NGCA. Among the surveyed returnees to the NGCA, the main source of income remained retirement and long service pension (53%). As highlighted in the NMS report, the majority of the returnees assessed their financial situation as `enough funds for food only' (39%) or `enough funds for basic needs' (45%) and 4% have to limit their expenses even for food. Based on IOM implementation experience and observations in the field, many vulnerable households could be relieved from relying on humanitarian support if they were enabled to start or resume a micro business for family subsistence or to allow to set aside sufficient money for hygiene products, medicines and other essential goods. Since 2018, IOM is providing livelihood support in Donetsk region (NGCA) as a pilot which resulted in holding of 13 self-employment trainings and distribution of 173 in-kind grants.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have increased access to income generation opportunities to cover their basic needs. Output 1.1: Selected IDPs, returnees and host community members in areas of high concern in Luhansk and Donetsk regions GCA and NGCA set up or develop businesses initiatives. Activities: Conduct relevant assessment for livelihood activities to target locations to be supported during the project; Conduct orientation sessions to assess beneficiaries’ skills and intentions; provide basic training and mentoring on vocational trainings, self-employment, micro-enterprise development and business plan preparation; Disburse vocational trainings, self-employment, self-sustenance, micro-enterprise grants to selected beneficiaries who developed the best business plans; Coaching and mentoring of beneficiaries throughout project duration. Output 1.2. Vulnerable households residing in Donetsk and Luhansk (NGCA) are provided with self-sustenance grants to improve economic-self-reliance and food security. Activities: Select vulnerable HHs residing close to the contact line in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (NGCA) through the verification process; Distribute procured assets among the selected beneficiaries; Conduct implementation and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) through regular field trips, interviews and focus group discussions. During the implementation of the above mentioned activities contributing to the achievement of Outputs 1 to 2, IOM will ensure access of vulnerable persons to livelihood support through effective outreach and the establishment of eligibility selection criteria prioritizing HHs and individuals with no employment, especially HHs headed by people aged 40-60, HHs with members who have disabilities and people with disabilities, single parent HHs, families with two and more children and female-headed HHs etc., in Luhansk and Donetsk regions GCA and NGCA. Due to the high level of vulnerability of the people aged between 60 -70, they will be eligible for self-sustenance grants. The project will engage both men and women from IDPs, returnees and members of the host communities in project design and implementation. Beneficiaries will be offered equal livelihood opportunities that will ensure man and women not to be blocked in traditional roles through the implementation of the business plans, developed and presented by beneficiary during the defense session, in agriculture, services, production and retail areas. In line with consultations with the heads of single-headed HHs, mainly headed by women, childcare and other relative support will be offered by IOM and/or IP(s) to the beneficiaries during the training sessions. A dedicated children’s playroom to be organized by IOM and IPs will help to facilitate the participation in the self-employment trainings of participants with children. Taking into consideration the participation of people with disabilities, premises for the trainings will be selected based on their location and accessibility. To facilitate delivery of assistance to persons with disabilities, HHs with members with chronic disease, large families with three and more children, in-kind assets will be delivered directly to the beneficiary on priory agreed time. For the urgent response to the complains and suggestion on how to improve project implementation, feedback mechanism thought the internet and telephone will be established during the project implementation. To ensure that all project beneficiaries are able to provide their feedback, the specially designed questionnaire will be shared among the project beneficiaries, both in hardcopies and as an online form. In addition, IOM has Transparency Hotline and Emergency and Stabilization Informational Hotline which could be utilized in addition to other feedback mechanisms.

3 - Indicators & targets

Outcome: IDPs, returnees and members of host communities have increased access to income generation opportunities to cover their basic needs - % of targeted beneficiaries reporting an increase in monthly income (Target 70%); - % of targeted beneficiaries reporting their engagement in the project supported them to meet their basic needs (Target 70%) Output 1.: Selected IDPs, returnees and host community members in areas of high concern in Luhansk and Donetsk regions GCA set up or develop micro businesses with self-employment grants or attend vocational trainings. - 107 micro-enterprise and self-employment orientation sessions conducted throughout the project implementation period (approx. 25 individuals pes one session); - Up to 2,675 participants (of whom an estimated 50% are female) passed through micro-enterprise, self-employment or professional orientation sessions throughout the project implementation period; - 1,540 beneficiaries (of whom an estimated 50% are female) supported with micro-enterprise, self-employment grants, coaching and mentoring during the project lifespan; and - 480 beneficiaries supported with vocational trainings grants. Output 2. Vulnerable households residing in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (NGCA) are provided with self- sustenance grants to improve economic-self-reliance and food security - 750 households supported with self -sustenance grants; - 1,350 members of the households (of whom an estimated 945 adults (645 female and 300 male) and 405 children (205 female and 200 male) benefited from the provision of in-kind materials and equipment. (NOTE 1: all this based on 1,8 members in a HH and 30% of kids in a HH. NOTE 2: in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (NGCA) beneficiaries will be selected adhering to the gender balance principles to the greatest extent possible, given the specific local conditions.) IOM will conduct regular monitoring and evaluation activities in all project locations to assess progress against set project indicators, to collect sex and age disaggregated data and to allow for necessary adjustments that might arise as lessons learnt. Phone surveys, individual interviews and monitoring on-sight visits will be carried out by IOM to collect both quantitative and qualitative data to assess the level of satisfaction and how beneficiaries are impacted by project activities. Drafting of regular and ad hoc progress reports, infographics, briefs and other documents for the donors and the broader humanitarian community, as well as a final evaluation report meant to assess performance and inform future similar programming will also be carried out.
