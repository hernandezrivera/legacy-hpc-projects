Project Summary

Education Cameroon (EDUCAM) is a child/youth centred community development organisation that seeks to create environments where children, their families and communities can live and benefit from their own societies. EDUCAM works together with children and communities to promote quality education and psychosocial wellbeing of children and youths.
The dual emergencies (Covid-19 and socio-political crisis) in the North West and South West (NWSW) has greatly reversed progress in access to learning. In these regions, challenges in learning still persist which has bearings on psychosocial wellbeing of children and young people. The socio-political crisis, now entering its fifth year has led to massive population displacements. As of August 2020, the North-West and South-West crisis has displaced over 1.1 million people, 4 an increase of over almost 200,000 people in comparison to December 2019, when 930,000 were displaced (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2021).
According to the Humanitarian need overview (2021), lack of access to basic social services including child protection services remain a key risk factor. Trauma and the psychological impact of the violence is directly affecting children’s emotional and cognitive development. Learning cannot be effective both at home and in school when the psychological and social consequences of conflict such as exploitation, drug abuse, physical abuse, GBV and so on are very visible in our children receiving education.
Accessing education in emergencies services, or going to school in the NWSW, is proven to be risky as attacks against education increased and became more violent. The regions lack psychological units, necessary to provide psychiatric and psychosocial support to those traumatized by the violence.
The general objective is to provide psychosocial support to children and youths in learning spaces (schools and temporal learning spaces) in Manyu and Fako Division in the South west.
Manyu Division is one of the most affected zones in the North West and South West Region of Cameroon. Education Cameroon carried out a need assessment for 3,000 students and pupils in three (3) subdivisions, from Manyu Division in the month of October and November 2020. The results show that at least about 50% are IDPs and are in need of serious psychosocial support to improve on their learning. Schools in Fako Division are overcrowded with IDPs causing serious problems on learning.
The project targets the following:
-8000 children and youths accessing mental health and psychosocial support services in learning spaces
-30 community structures/institutions trained on identification of needs
-200 children/youths referred to relevant protection actors in order to receive legal assistance (Excluding GBV Cases)
-100 children/youths receiving life-saving GBV services
-1000 children accessing education through alternative learning platforms and accelerated programs.
-7,000 people reached with messages on the protection of education against attacks and safe schools’ activities
-200 school community members trained on risk mitigation/reduction
-1000 students and teachers receiving school kits
-200 affected teacher’s and other Education personnel receiving psychosocial training

1 - Besoins / Needs

The crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon is a risk factor for children going to school. The deteriorating situation in the concerned regions in terms of economy, social structure and psychological wellbeing for the affected population have serious consequences on children who have the opportunity to attend school. In terms of the economy, most parents have either lost their source of livelihood, or business is not as successful as before the crisis, making it difficult for them to fully support their children’s education. IDP parents are faced with serious challenge sustaining in their new environment, an issue having serious consequences on their school-going age children. Foster parents hosting IDP children of school-going age also have an additional burden to care properly for children, not theirs. Teenage mothers have issues balancing schooling and child-rearing. Within the framework of providing psychosocial support to children and youths at risks in the South West Region of Cameroon, Education Cameroon carried out a need assessment in the month of October and November 2020 for both secondary and primary school children. The results show that most children going to school in Manyu have serious needs such as school needs, psychosocial needs, nutritional needs and health needs. -50% of students/Pupils assessed are IDPs (Separated and Unaccompanied children) -80% of students/pupils lack school needs such as textbooks, exercise books, and school bags -50% of students/pupils do not eat most of the time before going to school and need food support in schools -60% of students/pupils have lost a love one in the crisis -5% of students/pupils’ report being sexually assaulted either in their home, school or in the quarter. -50% of students/pupils need some form of psychosocial support The needs in Fako division are very evident as can be seen in the overcrowded classrooms and an increase number of separated and unaccompanied children in schools.On the basis of the above, children and youths attending schools in Manyu and Fako divisions are in dire need of psychosocial support.
