Project Summary

According to humanitarian need overview (OCHA 2022), widespread insecurity in the Lake Chad basin region continued to affect the Far North in 2021. And also, shocks related to violence continue to affect the Far North region. In the Far North region, the civilian population continues to be subject to armed attacks, IEDs, kidnappings, including of children, looting and destruction of property and infrastructure.
In 2021, almost 90 per cent of security incidents in the Far North occurred in the Logone et Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga divisions, with most attacks taking place in areas close to the Nigerian
border. The civilians continue to carry the brunt of the violence: 80 per cent of victims of NSAGs attacks were civilians between January and October 2021.
Due to the inter-community conflicts among terrorist group commonly known as “Boko haram” in Far North region, population moved from their localities to new areas with or without water and sanitation facilities to seek for security, save their life and hope for wellbeing.
This project therefore seeks to improve water, sanitation and hygiene response to some affected communities in Far North region of Cameroon with a focus on communities and schools hosting IDP and returnees, assess and construct/rehabilitate boreholes and small water supply networks powered with solar energy, construct gender segregated latrines in schools and health centers and assist affect household to construct family latrines in IDP host communities and distribute minimum WASH kits in target communities in Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Danay and Logone & Chari divisions. This project will target 5,000 people on access to water, 5000 people on access to adequate basic sanitation and 2500 households reached with critical WASH supplies (including hygiene items) and 30,000 through safe WASH messages.

1 - Besoins / Needs

About 0.7 million of people need humanitarian assistance in the Far North region. This situation is a result of low investment in the water and sanitation sector. The WASH situation is exacerbated by the numerous humanitarian crises affecting the Far North region: internal and cross-border population displacements (Nigeria to Cameroon), inter-community conflicts, floods, and cholera. The access to safe drinking water is estimated at 40% (RDWE, 2022) with differences among the six divisions, and 12,8% of population practice open defecation (EDS 2018). According to MSNA assessment (OCHA, Sept 2022), 34% of population in Far North still collect drinking water directly from stream and rivers. Over 35% of assessed villages, insufficiency of improved water points and non-permanent functionality of existing water points remain the main challenges. For 5% of assessed localities, access to improved water points remain a challenge for people with disabilities. In addition, out of 31% of villages assessed, the distance to water points is too long and 1% of them report gender-based violence as one of the main difficulties faced. Concerning water quality, 66% of assessed localities have reported an issue. For sanitation even if 81% report using latrine to ease their self, 58% of the same population practice open defecation simultaneously. This is due to insufficiency of sanitation facilities (68%), lack of privacy and poor security of sanitation facilities (34%) and 4% of them report gender-based violence. Therefore, there is an urgent need not only to increase access to sanitation facilities but also improve the quality of sanitation services offered to affected population in Far North region. In order to contribute in the response to the above challenges in the Far North region, EPDA will use collaborative and community approach to address the following: • Construction/rehabilitation of 10 water points which correspond to reaching a total of 5,000 people;• Construction of 25 gender disaggregation blocks of latrines in schools, markets and households which correspond to 5,000 beneficiaries; • 2,500 households to benefit from minimum WASH kits which correspond to a total of 12,500 people• 30,000 persons to be reached with messages on social and behavioral changes through awareness raising including PSEA training.
