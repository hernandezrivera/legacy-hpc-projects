Project Summary

Street Child will strengthen access and quality of GBV prevention and response services, particularly at community level, to enhance the protection of 51,920 GBV survivors and persons at risk, particularly children.
The project will prioritise linkage and access to a package of quality MHPSS, case management, and cash/livelihood services for survivors and people at risk with a focus on child and adolescent survivors. System strengthening will primarily take place at community level through improved service mapping and linkage to improve access to services, while community structures and groups will receive awareness raising, training and capacity strengthening to replicate and adapt GBV protection mechanisms to their community/local context. Through holistic service provision the project will facilitate psychosocial recovery and reintegration.
Street Child aims to operate in the following LGAs under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan:

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria's humanitarian crisis remains, primarily, a protection crisis. In 2022, the Humanitarian Needs Overview identified 1.4 million people in need of GBV services (82% of which are women and girls). In 2023 this figure is expected to increase due to security and macroeconomic trends including continued and renewed displacement, including movement of displaced persons from Maiduguri to LGA of origin. High levels of local and global inflation have had commensurate impact on prices, particularly basic foods and gasoline/petrol, reducing families' spending power. Economic and social challenges are linked to an equivalent rise in risk of GBV incidents, particularly Intimate Partner Violence (IPV), while also reducing access to GBV related health, legal, and protection services. These acute needs are in the context of a long term humanitarian crisis and pre-existing social norms that also contribute to GBV risk. Female-headed households and adolescent girls are extremely vulnerable to sexual exploitation and abuse, including subsequent to forcible recruitment and marriage by armed groups. Women and Girls formerly associated with Armed Groups, as well as unaccompanied and separated children, experience social stigmatisation which frustrates socioeconomic reintegration and increases isolation and vulnerability. While men and boys make up a smaller percentage of reported GBV incidents, the incidence rate is likely to be higher than reported. Men and boys experience GBV in different ways and contexts to women and girls and holistic GBV prevention and response services will require unique approaches for engaging and linking men and boys in project design and delivery. Key GBV needs are mainly at the community level, including an expansion and strengthening of identification, reporting, and response mechanisms. Particularly, survivors of GBV require increased access to mental health and psychosocial support services/psychosocial first aid, as well as livelihood/cash support to improve protection outcomes through independent income. There is need to urgently intervene to increase awareness and foster dialogue between key stakeholders within the State, LGAs and Communities, between community leaders and parents, as well as community gatekeepers to mitigate, prevent, and respond to GBV in the BAY States. Communities and community structures require additional awareness-creation and engagement to understand different forms of GBV and pathways to report and refer cases for response services. Coordination between different partners requires strengthening at the LGA level, by building coordination capacity of community stakeholders and local partners.

2 - Activities

The project will focus on the protection needs of the most vulnerable children and adolescents in line with the priorities reflected within the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Humanitarian Response Plan 2023 for the GBV Sub Sector. The response will be premised upon a conflict-sensitive framework and centres a holistic package of service provision, advocacy, and capacity-building that improves overall community capacity to identify and respond to GBV risks: • Conducting regular community mapping of GBV services, particularly medical and legal services, for survivors of GBV and disseminating referral pathway awareness across communities. Referral mapping will also cover livelihood and economic empowerment opportunities and services to support survivors and people at risk of GBV – in areas where Street Child is also implementing livelihood projects it will make provision to also support GBV survivors cross-referred. GBV survivors will also be supported to access mental health care or higher-level health care, including fistula repair, through IOM, MSF and UNFPA.• Establishing women and girl’s safe spaces (WGSS) and Men and Boys Safe Spaces (MBSS) in communities to provide ongoing psychosocial support and facilitate access to comprehensive GBV case management. WGSS will host protection and empowerment activities specifically tailored to most at-risk adolescent girls and women, including life skill sessions and social network building, while MBSS will focus on participation in mitigation and response to GBV issues in the community alongside support to identify and address male GBV concerns.• Providing cash and in-kind support as Targeted Individual Assistance to women and girls at risk and GBV survivors. Assistance will be prioritized to address urgent protection needs of women and girls identified and referred for cash support by GBV case workers within established WGSS’. Cash will be utilized to support women to meet their basic needs. Dignity kits will also be distributed to vulnerable women and girls to reduce risk of abuse.• Supporting developmental and psychological needs of child sexual violence survivors through joint management between GBV and CP teams. Where necessary Street Child will collaborate with clinical providers to refer cases requiring medical attention.• Building capacities of government and community stakeholders including government ministries, NGOs, National Population Commission (NPC), National Identification Management Commission (NIMC), and community groups to strengthen GBV-focused referral pathways and support mechanisms within the LGAs of implementation.• Conducting advocacy with health facility and hospital leadership to establish confidential spaces, appropriately equipped to provide GBV response services – including physical infrastructure, clinical training, and staff awareness. Street Child will prioritise targeted, community-led approaches to awareness-raising and grassroots activism on GBV-related messaging and standards. Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations will enable the collection of actionable information and analysis of on-going risks while supporting the communities’ own capacity to utilise AAP mechanisms independently and take responsibility for mitigation and response.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The prioritisation of protection, safety, dignity, accountability and minimising unintended negative impacts ensure that the communities where Street Child works can access meaningful, dignified support. Street Child is committed to centring and mainstreaming protection throughout its activities and projects, and has pre-emptively identified potential protection risks within the project as well as taking active steps to integrate mitigation and management strategies. Protection reporting, response and prevention mechanisms are integrated into the design, and protection mainstreaming indicators are included within the monitoring and evaluation framework. At the beneficiary selection stage, Street Child will use tested vulnerability assessment tools and clear selection process to ensure that beneficiaries are selected based on need and through predetermined and clearly communicated criteria of vulnerability. In order to ensure participation and minimise inter-group tensions, targets have been set to include different communities (IDP, host community and returnee) as well as both men and women. Community leaders and members will be informed of the selection process and criteria during the inception stages, and continuous sensitisation of the selection criteria will be used to ensure that possible intra- and inter-group tensions are minimised. Sensitisation on these topics through regular community visits and feedback sessions has proven successful during past projects, where dissatisfaction about not being included in the project has been reduced by clearly explaining the aims of the project and how beneficiaries were selected. As part of our commitment to accountability and protection, Street Child commits to work towards the elimination of exploitation and abuse in the communities where we work and in our engagement with vulnerable populations. Street Child has a clear safeguarding policy in place which applies to all personnel and affiliates, including implementing partners and community members who are affiliated to our work. All Street Child staff, partner staff, and project-affiliated community members such as volunteers and committee members receive comprehensive training of the Street Child safeguarding policy. This comprehensive approach, which is also intended to be rolled out to vendors, ensures that all actors within SC projects are operating under the same rules and procedures and creates a harmonised safeguarding environment. In addition to training key community members on the Street Child safeguarding policy, wider community outreach and sensitisation is carried out via hosting of community forums and meetings, or use of visual aids including posters and murals which offer accessible and culturally relevant and appropriate key messages to empower community members on understanding and reporting concerns. Street Child is also a member of the PSEA Working Group and the focal person representing the organisation is also the safeguarding focal person and is involved in this project. Any incident report will be also shared with the WG who request regular reports.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback mechanisms will be contextualised to the community in question. Street Child has a dedicated CRM Officer with the responsibility of implementing and managing accessible and relevant feedback mechanisms, based on community information. Previous interventions have utilised toll free lines, complaint desks, anonymous boxes, and similar modalities. Safeguarding and complaint mechanisms can be contextualised through altering images and languages to suit the community, both for audio and print material. Project staff will also attend regular coordination meetings with community structures. M&E teams will conduct baseline, endline, and regular monitoring, including FGDs/KIIs with community members, to gauge satisfaction with the implementation. All feedback received will be fed into review sessions and management decisions, allowing activities to be adapted to respond where appropriate in line with Street Child's commitment to accountability and evidence-based learning.
