Project Summary

The project will expand nutrition garden activities that target Rohingya households to increase food security and self-reliance, using the space around the shelters to grow climbing vegetable/vertical gardens, targeting both households where children receive case management support and the wider community. The production of vegetables for self-consumption reduces the reliance on market engagement. By encouraging vertical gardens, the design mitigates finite land and space concerns. Participants will receive identified seeds and other support such as tomato, pepper, chili, capsicum, okra, bottle gourd, and green beans, soil and fertilizers, as well as technical training and continuous follow up to ensure maximum growth and productivity. The project also promotes livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities for the host communities in Ukhia and Teknaf, Cox's Bazar, through agricultural inputs, cash for work and skill development training for income generation. Through cash and voucher assistance, the project will provide economic support to 15,000 households from host communities, allowing them to purchase productive inputs and assets contributing to sustainable and resilient livelihoods. Empowering the parents and caregivers of the children, the project will strengthen the protective factors at all levels of the child’s environment in order to reinforce the child’s resilience and prevent and respond to threats to the wellbeing of children. The project will improve the sustainable livelihoods and self-reliance opportunities for the host communities in Ukhia and Teknaf, Cox's Bazar, through providing soft skills, vocational skills, job linkages support, entrepreneurship development, assets and cash transfer support. The project will also provide soft skills and awareness on nutrition, health hygiene, disaster reduction, problem solving, decisions making and others relevant topics to Rohingya communities.

Needs

The Rohingya refugee population is fully dependent on food assistance. Several factors such as lack of opportunity for own food production, limited financial and physical access to food, movement restrictions, protection issues and limited access to cooking fuel and sources of energy lead to this dependency. The Rohingya influx aggravated socio-economic conditions with severe effect on lives and livelihoods of the host community. On the positive side, there is an increased demand for food and income generating activities which may stimulate local economic activities, with positive income spill-overs for businesses in the host community. Also there needs to be a shift from relief dependence to self-reliance, and from meeting immediate needs to enhancing resilience. Across camps and host communities it is mostly women who make the food decisions in their families. Women and teenage girls in the refugee and host communities generally have very few opportunities to become self-sufficient. They are the ones who often bear the brunt of food insecurity and other deprivations at the household level. On top of that cultural practices make them to have less to eat as access to food is difficult. Rohingya women’s access to livelihoods and income is more restricted than in the host community making them more vulnerable towards access to foods. The conservative views lead to a lack of opportunities and capacity building around existing skills in both communities. Concerns have also been raised around safety in aid distributions for women, girls and boys. Given the above context the project will empower women and girls through activities that will give them opportunities to access and control household resources. There is a need to promote equal access to food rations and participation opportunities taking gender and age considerations into account. The project will ensure active engagement and participation of women, men, youth as direct beneficiaries and children (boys and girls) as indirect beneficiaries. Both men and women will be given equal opportunity, with more than 60% of beneficiaries targeted being women. The project will give high emphasis on EVIs such as persons with disabilities, elderly etc. Especial measures will be taken to include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) especially having children with malnutrition will be given greater priority/focus in 2020. During formation of refugee food security committee (RFSC) and refugee food security volunteer (RFSV), inputs from women are needed to consider by developing channels of communication for them in the community. Food security for host communities showed a marked deterioration, with the percentage of households with poor or borderline poor food consumption increasing sharply from 31 percent in 2017 to 80 percent in the August to September period of 2018. Women in host communities will be provided with monthly subsistence allowance and a one-off cash grant to set up income generating activities. These skill development trainings will provide them with an opportunity to be engaged in livelihood activities and improve their food security. Enterprise development training for the women participants will enable them to improve their existing businesses or encouraging them to start "Startups". Besides, the project will provide refugee women with vegetable seeds to promote micro-nutrient gardens in the land available around the family’s shelter to grow vegetables for consumption and meet subsistence food needs at the household level.

Activities or outputs

(a) Cash and Voucher assistance to the host communities, mostly to women(b) Assets transfer to the host communities, mostly to women and vulnerable women(c) Support families from host communities for entrepreneurship development/business planning and income generation efforts by emphasizing on female members of household(d) Orientation to build host communities’ awareness on existing government social protection schemes, e.g. safety nets, food for work, cash for work tailoring both genders and different age groups(e) Provide soft skills and awareness on nutrition, health & hygiene, problem solving, decisions making and others relevant topics to Rohingya and Host communities to both genders (f) Training for families from host communities on DRR preparedness(g) Self-reliance assistance for Rohingya refugees, mostly women and adolescent girls, to reduce the risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms(h) Micro-nutrition gardens to improve household food consumption(i) Skill development/Vocational training, job linkages for host communities based on market demand covering all priority sectorsWhile implementing the activities, the project will take into consideration of child safeguarding, SEA and PSEA. Sector-led referral pathways will be followed as well as the project will refer beneficiaries to Save the Children service points if they need any assistance.
