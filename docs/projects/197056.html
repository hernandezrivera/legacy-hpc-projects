Project Summary

Humanitarian needs in NWS remain immense, with an estimated 4.1 M people in need of assistance at the end of 2022. 2.8 M people in NWS are IDPs, of with 1.8 M are living in over 1.400 IDP sites, many of them still in tents and makeshift shelters.
Macro‐economic conditions continue to worsen, evident in the currency devaluation, soaring prices, widespread losses in livelihoods, and increase in poverty. According to MSNA data, by Aug 2022, the average HH expenses exceeded the average HH income by 60%, compared to 49% in 2021. The increase
in the income‐expenditure gap has resulted in widespread utilization of increasingly desperate coping mechanisms. The water crisis affects access to sufficient and safe drinking water, irrigation, food production and power generation.
The project will support 6,970 HHs, 41,819 individuals to restore income-generating assets & increase bread and agricultural production, and improved economic conditions and labor market opportunities.
Bahar intervention will improve the wheat/bread production capacity of 10 bakeries and one mill in the targeted locations, and purchasing power, where 800 MT of wheat will be purchased from 400 farmers in planting season 2022-2023 and will divide into two parts, the first: 171.2 MT will be sieved into 160 MT of pure wheat (93%) and 11.2 MT impurities (7%). The 160 MT pure wheat quantity will be allocated as wheat seeds and distributed to another 800 farmers for next season 2023-2024. The second part, 628.8 tons will be milled into 503.04 tons of flour with 80% rate extraction and 125.8 MT impurities (20%), the flour will be supplied to the rehabilitated bakeries, and the impurities will be utilized for covering the cost of the related milling and sifting process. Consequently, approximately 710,000 bread bundles with a weight of 800 gr will distribute for 29,579 IDPs individuals free of charge for 3 months duration/78 distributing days based on the daily survival needs (250 g bread/person/day). On the other hand, 600 new farmers will benefit from the distribution of wheat inputs, including irrigation and harvesting costs. Identification of farmers will be through Sex and Age disaggregated (70% femal+30% men). The final beneficiary's selection criteria will be defined in collaboration with local authorities; however selection criteria will include vulnerability, technical capacity, access to land, access to market. In addition, cultivated area dedicated to the supported crop should not be more than 1 Ha per farmer, etc. The beneficiaries will be a combination of IDPs and the host community (30 % IDPs), selected based on vulnerability criteria, as well as their interest in and commitment to engage in the proposed livelihoods activities. Bahar will prioritize vulnerable female headed HHs (widowed, divorced) according to household vulnerability criteria (family dependency ratio, income level).
Moreover, 130 CFW (100 semi-skilled, 30 un-skilled) will implement activities related to bakeries and mills rehabilitation/maintenance works, bread distribution, and fire and vole pest-fighting. CFW beneficiaries will be selected ‎regarding the following criteria: interview result (face-to-face interviews), IDPs, women, and ‎unemployed persons, disabled ‎persons with one of the following impairments: in hearing, speaking, or minor impairment in movement ‎will be encouraged to ‎apply.‎ On the other hand, Labor wages be harmonized with other agencies and the local market rate and are anticipated to be 5-8 $ per worker per day. In addition, the wages will calculated based on similar projects implemented by partners in the same locations, and the same interventions; Also, the daily wages were calculated based on previous projects implemented by BAHAR in the same locations. Work completed will be verified through timesheets signed by each worker and their supervisor, in addition to field visits by BAHAR staff to verify work hours/ attendance.

3 - Needs

Based on Bahar assessment findings for 22 bakeries in Azaz, Afrin, Mhembel and Maarat Misrin dated Sep, 2022. The overall situation was: 65% need rehabilitation intervention (Machine maintenance, building rehabilitation and new equipment) The average cost is approximately 7000 $/bakery. 80% ownerships were private, while the else are managed by the local council. Bakeries are functional. Bakeries produce regular unsubsidized bread. Full capacity in a week reaches 72 MT whereas the current productivity reaches 36% of the full capacity. Assessed bakeries expressed their approval for cost recovery and contribute to the grant. On the other hand, a wheat value chain assessment through KIIs was conducted by Bahar M7E team on Oct 2022 in Azaz,Mhembel and Maarat Misrin, to get a better understanding of the respective domestic agri-value and supply chains and the challenges which smallholders are facing along the chain. The main findings were:Wheat account for the bulk of Agri activity Namely 75% Agricultural is the main source of income80% Securing reliable seeds represents a major challengeVole & Sunn pests are endemic in wet seasons Fires during the harvesting as a challenge to farmers, ensure the existence of firefighting teamsThe average tenure is 8 dunums per farmer Irrigation support be critical within the current climate change conditionsCash vouchers are preferable for harvesting and irrigation costs, while In-kind preferable for wheat inputs.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The following elements will be taken into account in all project activities: Bahar will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm: Bahar will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of our intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Ensure that women and children are consulted in regard to the design and implementation of this project (throughout the process) - The beneficiaries will be informed about thedates, times, and locations of planned distributions well ahead of time. It will be ensured that women-headed households and unaccompanied and separated children are given ration cards in their own names and that they receive distributions of the inputs as necessary, in a way that does not cause further separation. During the distribution, Bahar will set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations,distributions, and fairs so that vulnerable individuals such as pregnant women, people with infants, unaccompanied children, and the elderly, sick, and those with disabilities are helped first. It will be ensured that there is always female staff assigned to work with communities. It will be ensured that all staff including those working in the distribution of the inputs have signed up to and been trained in a code of conduct or other policy which covers child safeguarding. Bahar Will inform child protection actorsimmediately about observe any protection concerns for children.
