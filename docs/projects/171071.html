Project Summary


Due to combined effects of desert locust infestation, below average rainfall, socio-economic impacts of covid-19 and cumulative impacts of previous shocks, the nutrition situation in Galkayo, Hobyo and Abudwak, Beletweyne and Afgooye districts are deteriorating. The FSNAU Post Gu nutrition assessment result published on 30th of September,2020 shows serious to critical levels of GAM and SAM prevalence. The average GAM rate in the 5 districts is 14.6%. Despite the recent gains made through collective humanitarian efforts, malnutrition remains a major challenge for the children and women in in these locations. The 2020 Deyr rainfall season is likely to be below average across the country which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation. CESVI’s internal reports (2020) posits that nutritional status in these locations is declining due to poor dietary diversity, inadequate feeding and care practices of young children and their mothers, poor access to basic services such as primary health care, clean water, and sanitation facilities.
Response:
This Action will be implemented in Beletweyne, Galcayo, Hobyo, Abduwaak and Afgooye Districts of Somalia targeting vulnerable populations affected by different cyclic crises. The main purpose of this intervention will be to reverse malnutrition trends prevalent in the aforementioned districts with focus being children aged below 5 years together with pregnant and lactating mothers. This will be done through provision of quality and equitable curative and preventive Nutrition services. This will be guided by Nutrition cluster objectives 1, 2 and 3.
In order to achieve the above stated objectives, this action will be implemented in the 5 districts where CESVI has a strong operational presence implementing a broad range of interventions running across different sectors namely, Health, Nutrition, WASH Food Security and Livelihoods. This will be packaged in 7 static Health facilities and 5 mobile clinics delivering both lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services. Both static Health facilities and mobile clinics will provide treatment of children aged between 6-59 months with SAM condition without medical complications. Children with SAM condition with medical complications will be referred to the SC centres based Abduwaak, Galacayo , Hobyo, Belatwyene and Afgoye Referral Hospitals run by different NGOs. Children aged between 6-59 months together with PLWs with MAM condition will be treated in the TSFP centres in both mobile and static Health centres.
In order to provide quality services, this action will train Health workers and 60 CHWs in a number of key thematic areas, notably, IMAM, IYCF and basic protection topics. The trained CHWS will be deployed to the villages to conduct mass screening of children aged below 5 years using MUAC tapes. Mother MUAC led trainings to equip mothers with skills pertinent to screening children for malnutrition be done. The trained mothers will be issues with MUAC straps to enable them screen their children to determine their nutrition status.
This action will first train Health workers and CHWS on IYCF. The trained staff will be expected to undertake IYCF counselling for both pregnant and lactating mothers including adolescent girls. There will also be provision of micronutrient supplements in both static and mobile health facilities. This includes, supplementary nutrition products for children aged 6-23 months, PLWs, Iron folate supplements for pregnant mothers attending ANC clinics, vitamin A supplements for children aged between 6-59 months.
This intervention will be implemented in collaboration with the Local Ministry of Health Authorities, other INGO & LNGOs. Data will be shared through the existing platforms- ONA and Nutrition cluster system to enhance effective and efficient coordination of actions. CESVI will establish a referral pathway for cases that require WASH, FSL and protection services

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate exposure of beneficiaries to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, this action will deploy 5 mobile clinics to the villages without static health facilities within their vicinity. These mobile clinics will be equipped with trained staff and nutrition supplies to deliver both quality curative and preventive nutrition services to the affected populations with health facilities located in the far flung areas. As part of efforts to reduce the risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.), this action will mainstream protection components within the projects ‘s grain. Both staff working in the static and mobile clinics will be trained on basic protection aspects like psychosocial counselling and referral services. The mobile clinics will also be used to transfer protection cases to various service provision points. This will be done by printing pre-referral cards for GBV/ protection cases which shall be used for referral of such cases. The project will ensure that all staff is trained on PSEA(and subsequently sign CESVI PSEA policy) and attendant consequences as a result of a violation of this policy - including dismissal from work communicated to them. This measure will ensure that any staff in contact with beneficiaries of thisproject does not engage in any form of sexual exploitation and abuse when handlingbeneficiaries. This action will put in place measures to ensure that all marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, take part in consultations or decision-making processes affecting the project. This will be done through discussions with their leaders through various open fora where their concerns and needs will be factored in during project implementation. This will also be done during project evaluation, findings from such sessions shall be used to improve project activities.
