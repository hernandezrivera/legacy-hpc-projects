Project Summary

The Darna II project provides support to a health centre for reproductive health, maternal and child health and paediatric health, built and started through the first phase of the project financed by AICS, inside the Raqqa National Civil Hospital. The clinic will manage 1 mobile unit for reproductive health, serving the rural areas around Raqqa and the city itself. The programme also includes protection mainstreamming elements including training cycles for healthcare professionals on the identification of GBV, and capacity building in health training programmes in collaboration with the Italian medical capacity-building partner NGO. UPP will work with its local partner and coordinate with the Raqqa Civil Council to ensure ownership and the sustainability of the intervention.

Needs

Health sector: In the health sector, the needs identified by the project are, in line with the Whole of Syria (WoS) plan, the improvement of health services with a focus on reproductive health and paediatrics and training of the local medical staff. As reported in the context of the return of 147,000 people to Raqqa, there are no public health facilities that provide adequate reproductive health services (i.e. natural and caesarean delivery). There is also a lack of equipment and referral services to other health facilities, apart from the ambulances service of the project local partner. Moreover, the local health personnel, after several years of civil war, isolated within the national context and closed towards the outside, needs adequate training support and technical updating in the health sector, in particular in maternal and child health. In addition, there are very few gynaecologists. Concerning the local health professionals, composed of doctors and paramedics, even when they have adequate qualifications and professional experience, they find themselves in a context of urgencies and priority clinical emergencies, with an extremely vulnerable civilian population. Clinical situations that were easy to manage in the past years, today fall more easily in clinical emergencies and new health problems emerge: violence and abuses, explosive remnants of war (ERW) induced trauma, malnutrition, discontinuity in therapy in chronic diseases, prenatal assistance and diagnosis. Furthermore, in this context, doctors and generalist nurses, if not specializing, had to assume specialized functions, highlighting the need to intervene with an appropriate capacity building programme to improve the level of technical skills of the local staff and therefore be able to ensure an adequate health response to the population. Protection sector: In the protection sector, the needs identified by the project are the insufficiency of integrated specialized services for the prevention and treatment of gender-based violence (GBV), psychosocial support and the reproductive health of women and girls in the city of Raqqa and in rural areas. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018 of the WoS, gender violence and especially domestic violence, early or temporary marriages and the fear of sexual violence, continue to pervade the lives of many Syrian women and girls. Health and counselling services specialized in the protection of women and girls are missing in Raqqa and the stigma surrounding the victims led to the creation of a negative adjustment mechanism for which the freedom of movement of women and girls is severely limited because of the fear of becoming victims of sexual violence. This further limits access to services and participation in public life. In line with the holistic approach promoted by UNFPA that combines reproductive health, mental health, participation/empowerment and the strengthening of the resilience of women and girls, the need to increase the specific skills of the local partner medical personnel on GBV and communication techniques with trauma victims through training programmes, was identified together with the local partner. Moreover, the project intends to offer a GBV case management and psychological support service, and to establish a system for the collection of complaints and feedback with particular attention to SEA, in order to incorporate the protection perspective in the management and provision of health services. In Raqqa, the cultural context, the situation of volatile safety and the limited availability of specialist care and health services for reproductive health and the treatment of GBV victims, represent an obstacle to the implementation of protection programmes for women and girls. GBV case management services are missing. Therefore, UPP and its local partner want to create a protected space within the project in order to respond to these needs.

Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will centre around: • Rehabilitation and refurbishment of building premises enable extension of services in the Raqqa Hospital building;• The improvement of health services with a focus on reproductive health and paediatrics and training of the local medical staff;• Increasing the specific skills of the local partner medical personnel on protetcion masntreaming and GBV awareness through training programmes. Establishing a system for the collection of complaints and feedback with particular attention to SEA;• Information camapaigns in the community on health prevention with particular focus on women and girls health awareness. The results of the below activities are that the use of the project health services (hospital and mobile unit) increases by 40%; at least 35/38 people receive refresh professional training courses and capacity building; at least 22/25 members of the medical and nonmedical staff receives training on protetcion mainstreaming and GBV awareness. 1.1 Running of Raqqa Hospital. 1.2 Professional training of healthcare personnel. UPP and its local partner are supporting C-sections in Raqqa National Hospital under Darna II project. To avoid duplication of activities partners are coordinating with the health sector and local health authorities. Services have been planned in consultation with other actors planning inteventions in the area, namely MSF-OCA, with which UPP is coordinating for over a year in the Raqqa response. The project will aim to strenghthen resilience for the conflict affected popualtion in Raqqa: In line with resilience criteria: 2) the project forsees consultation with women, men, boys, girls of the targeted communities through FGDs/KIIs as well as through the feedback mechanisms; Resilience criterion 3) The project support limited self-reliance/early recovery of households and communities with focus on women and girls. This is carried out mainly through information campaigns to increase access to services and health information for self-protection; criterion 4) Through its strong capacity building component the project will contribute to strenghen local capacities with focus on training of local partner staff, including emergency health personnel and community health workers; criterion 5) The project aims to improve services in terms of health with focus on reproductive health wth focus on maternal and neonatal health.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project will be managed from Iraq and in particular from the Northern area where, compared to the current situation, both at the regional and the national level, one of the major issues concerns the possible deterioration of the security conditions in the Sinjar area, with the re-emergence of terrorist phenomena related to post-Daesh and internal battles in Syria. In this case, the real risk is to be unable to send aid to the NES area. In this area, Raqqa should not be considered stable at the moment, despite the active support of the international coalition. The specific risks in the city of Raqqa are the following: groups affiliated with Daesh increase the level of tension, attacks by Damascus, infiltration of groups affiliated to Turkey. In the event of escalation of the conflict in Syria and Iraq, the security situation may not allow the proper carrying out of the activities in the field and of the monitoring, and actions could also be momentarily suspended. At the moment, day-to-day operations of the health structures and the consensus they have among the population, give hope that it is possible to operate on a daily basis. The possible withdrawal of the international coalition could determine in any case the end of the current status quo and would open new scenarios full of risks also for humanitarian workers. During some periods in the past, the Iraq-Syria borders were closed and in the previous conveys carried out by Un Ponte Per…in at least two cases the mediation of the Italian Consulate of Erbil has facilitated customs procedures. The last convoys were also carried out autonomously by UPP and managed directly and without problems, in all their steps. Today, thanks to the multiple activities and operational bases in Iraq and Syria, UPP is able to rely on a good supply system for NES and to reach and access most areas in the region.
