Project Summary

The Nutrition project aims to address urgent and life-saving nutrition needs of mothers and children in Syria, located in high severity areas across the entire country, as depicted by the 2022 HNO. In line with the nutrition sector priorities, the project aims to provide preventative and curative nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups (namely; children U5 and PLWs) amongst all affected populations, including IDPs, host communities and returnees identified with high severity food insecurity and nutrition indicators. The project will, therefore, focus on:
- Supporting an improved and equitable access to life saving nutritional services for food insecure and vulnerable children (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) at community and facilities level to stabilize and/or reduce malnutrition (acute, chronic and micronutrient deficiencies) in the target population.
− Preventing acute, chronic malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies in children 6-23 months
− Improving diet diversity amongst pregnant and lactating women within food insecure and vulnerable families.
Trough the aforementioned activities, WFP is able to play a key role in strengthening the nutrition resilience of the population in general, and its nutritionally targeted groups in particular, by shielding them from economic volatility and vulnerability, supporting to avoid further asset depletion, while improving their access to high quality nutrition services.
WFP aims to target boys and girls aged 6- 23months and PLWs, including those with disability, through its preventive programme. The Programme is implemented in coordination with the Ministry of Health and other partners of the nutrition sector in Syria including UN agencies, National and International NGOs. Considering the high needs and limited resources, the project will adopt a context and severity-based approach to prioritize sub-districts identified with high nutritional and food insecurity severity, mainly those with severity level 3,4 &5. In case of lower funding, priority will be given to locations were severity of food security and nutrition of is more than 4 and to newly accessible areas.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis, coupled with the most recent economic downturn and the deteriorating food security situation is having a detrimental effect on dietary patterns of individuals and adversely affecting the nutrition status of the most vulnerable segments of the Syrian population. Today, 5.5 million children and PLWs are estimated to need life-saving preventative and curative nutrition services, a 12% increase from the estimated 4.9 million identified in 2021. Based on 2021 estimates, a deterioration in all forms of undernutrition was witnessed due to the aggravating factors of the crisis, compounded by the impact of the Covid-19 pandemic, leaving at least 0.6 million children at a risk of losing their future development and learning potentials due to stunting, while one in three children are estimated to suffer from iron-deficiency anemia, with a subsequent increase in trends of acute malnutrition in both children and as per 2021 CMAM operational data, which was also substantiated by the vast increase in rates of acute malnutrition in PLWs in the North West as per the 2021 SMART Survey.Hence, the drivers of malnutrition remain multifactorial, and apart from displacement, the high levels of food insecurity and the reduced resilience of families will further exacerbate the situation if a multisectoral approach is not adopted to respond to the immediate and underlying causes of undernutrition.

5 - Implementation of activities

During 2022, WFP will maximize the coverage of its nutrition programmes across the entire country to provide preventative nutrition support to a targeted 598,800 beneficiaries within sub-district identified with a severity score of 3 and above as per the food and nutrition severity scale, in addition to providing treatment services to 50,000 children and PLWs identified with moderate acute malnutrition. Both preventative and curative programmes will be implemented in coordination with key UN Stakeholders, namely; UNICEF and UNFPA, to ensure beneficiaries’ nutrition, health and protection needs are at the center of a comprehensive range of services provided through the respective partnerships. The section below describes individual activities: • Targeted provision of lipid based nutrient supplements (LNS-MQ) to children 6-23 months of age, from food insecure and vulnerable families receiving WFP general food assistance inside. • Provision of vouchers redeemable at local shops for fresh foods (fruits, vegetables, dairy, meat) and hygiene items, to PLWGs. Identification and treatment of children and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition through the CMAM approach, across Syria. Noting the importance of understanding the nutrition situation of vulnerable groups, WFP will also implement, in collaboration with key stakeholders, a nutrition assessment for school aged children (6-12 years of age) to better understand the impact of the crisis on this group.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WFP has identified protection risks that might impact proper implementation of its programme and mitigation measures put in place. Protection Risk/Threat: Lack of essential basic services at distribution sites, Attacks on health staff ,Looting of supplies Likelihood and impact: High Mitigating. Measure: Ensure FLAs clearly indicate availability of shaded waiting areas, toilets, potable water in all distribution sites. ensure community engagement and sensitization about targeting. Ensure distribution sites are setup in accessible and safe locations, Protection Risk/Threat: CP staff might be exposed to coercion or threats from individuals not eligible to be registered in the programme ,Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Likelihood and impact: High Mitigating Measure: Ensure staff are provided with clear guidelines on eligibility criteria and mobilize community sensitization to avoid friction. Design the programme with a clear built-in Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policy. Provide PSEA training to CP staff and ensure referral pathways exist when cases are reported/identified.WFP’s M&E framework will utilize third party monitoring remote monitoring to complement field visits, while continuing direct monitoring. Issues related to gender equality, disability inclusion and protection will receive particular emphasis to ensure nutrition programmes are all inclusive to needs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The existing WFP Helpline has witnessed a significant expansion in 2021 and further strengthening of the mechanism will be supported in 2022 with the development of new ones, i.e. complaint boxes and partners’ helpdesks. The line is open to all the communities that are reached through the different WFP activities and an informative flyer is contained in all the WPF food baskets that are distributed. A specific mechanism for SEA related cases has also been established so that allegations of SEA are immediately referred to trained specialized staffAll the Field Offices have an active PSEA focal point who has been trained on how to safely identify and report cases. Focal Points also raise awareness among staff about SEA related issuesAll WFP partners have been assessed within the PSEA Interagency Network on capacities around PSEA. The partners with low capacities and without clear policies to prevent and respond to SEA cases, will be trained in 2022 and supported in the development of strategies and policies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

in order to minimize the potential adverse effects to the targeted communities andhumanitarian workers while delivering nutrition interventions to the most vulnerablesegments of the community, such as girls and boys under five and PLW, the sector carriedout a thorough forecasting of likely risks that may be caused while providing nutritionservices, and developed a framework with mitigation measures. Risks related to activitiescarried out by the nutrition sector are anticipated to be mainly to the health workersmoving between camps, potential looting of nutrition supplies, attacks on health workersand facilities as well as beneficiaries accessing the facilities, losing follow up ofbeneficiaries and creating tension when targeting IDPs for distributions in the hostcommunity. In order to mitigate these risks, and ensuring adherence to humanitarianprinciples, the sector continues to adopt a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing nutritionservices to vulnerable groups throughout Syria. These mitigation measures includetraining of nutrition implementing partners on the prevention of sexual exploitation andabuse, scaling up community-based approaches to ensure quality access to services. Toensure proper and effective implementation of the Nutrition activities, WFP will maintainregular coordination with MOH, in addition to nutrition actors through the nutrition sector,and various thematic group.
