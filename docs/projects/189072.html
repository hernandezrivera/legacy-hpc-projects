Project Summary

Despite South Sudan celebrating 10 years of Independence in 2021 and the subsequent partial formation of the Revitalized governments in 2020, access to health care services has never improved. Protracted communal conflicts, cattle raiding, economic hardship coupled with climatic shocks (flooding and droughts) and COVID-19 effects have continuously devastated the lives and wellbeing of people.
Humanitarian assistance is needed to support an estimated population of about 8.9 million people in need. 8% of the 1,869 health facilities provide full package of BPHNS and 53% of the facilities are moderately functional, implying inadequate access and availability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the county. (HNO 2022). Jonglei and Upper Nile states are among the 10 States in South Sudan with highest health access gaps due to compounding shocks, weak health system, health facility destruction with floods, increase in displaced and returned population time and again. The most food insecure states are Jonglei, Upper Nile among others with more than 50% of their respective populations are facing crisis (IPC Phase 4) or worse acute food insecurity. In second Phase projection period of April to July 2021 Akobo County with 11,000 (Men 2530, women 2,640, boys 2970 and girls being 2860) in Jonglei state face food insecurity (IPC analysis 2021). In Upper Nile counties an estimated 70.8% population is likely to be IPC phase 3 or worse acute food insecurity. The overall GAM rate in the country is more than the 15% WHO emergency threshold this also increasing vulnerability due to high rates of malnutrition.
Immunization services are very poor in the country and also utilization of maternal-child health services is wanting. Cold chain availability and functionality low and transmission of infections prevented by immunization like measles remain very high. Despite efforts by health partners and the ministry of health to improve health service delivery in the country only less than 50% of the population are able to access health services with SARA report in 2019 showing that 44% of the populations in South Sudan are able to access health services. Medicair will align her responses with Health Cluster strategic objectives 1) to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone disease through timely detection and coordinated response. 2) Improve equitable access and utilization of integrated life-saving healthcare services such as SGBV, MHPSS, Disability and inclusion services. 3) enhance resilience and promotion through linkages, capacity building and coordination of health services to vulnerable population of Akobo, Duk and Maiwut Counties.
Medicair will employ static and mobile teams to provide health care services in the targeted locations (1 PHCUs and 2 mobile teams in Akobo, 1 PHCU and 2 Mobile teams in Duk and 1 PHCU and 2 mobile teams in Maiwut Counties) to improve accessibility of hard to reach persons cut off by floods, conflicts who cannot access health services.
The target Population is based on severity of vulnerable population and will include 24,819 in total men 5,708 (23%), women 5,957 (24%), boys 6,701 (27%) and girls 6,453 (26%) while persons with disability being 3,723 (15%). 12,410 (50%) of the population will be women, while 12,906 (52%) will be children of the targeted population.
The activities include i) carry out disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection prevention and response, ii) OPD consultation, iii) case management of common illnesses, iv) immunization against vaccine preventable disease, v)Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC)(ANC,PNC and skilled deliveries), vi) Clinical Management of Rape, vii) Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, viii) Capacity Building, ix) infection prevention and control, x) community health service linkage, mobilization and consultative meetings

1 - Needs

Jonglei and Upper Nile states in the recent past have experienced the catastrophic myriad of challenges such as floods, intercommunal clashes, property loss, crops and animals, health facilities destroyed by floods. Over 1,800 ( men 414, women, 432, boys 468 and girls 486) and persons with disability 2700 people affected by floods in Akobo indicated by RRC in Akobo County (October 2021 South Sudan Key Context Update). Counties like Akobo, and Maiwut have experienced an increase in IPDs and returnees. A biometric registration exercise in Akobo county conducted by IOM Displacement Matrix in 2020 shows a total of 41,644( men 9162, women 11660, boys 6663 and girls 14159) individuals (8390 HHs) while movement is observed from Akobo to Ethiopia. According to IPC projections (April-June 2021),5% of the population of Akobo will likely face famine if no actions are taken to avert the situation. In Maiwut Malaria is the leading morbidity in all locations visited with 1,476 ( men 339, women 354, boys 384 and girls 339) cases, meningitis is zero (0), Diarrheal has 462( men 106, women 111, boys 120 and girls 125) people, acute Respiratory disease has 331( men 76, women 94, boys 86 and girls 75) cases, there were no case on cholera. The other main health concerns reported by health workers are Snake-bites (IRNA, August,2021). These targeted counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile state were seriously affected by the ragging floods and most communities were cut off by poor infrastructure and flooding made it difficult to reach people in need with health services (HNO 2022). In Duk malaria is the leading morbidity at 70% ( women 23%, girls, 27% , boys 26% and men 23%) of the population followed by acute respiratory infections (ARIs), urinary tract infections (UTIs), acute watery diarrhea (AWD), skin infections, eye infections, injuries, peptic ulcers disease (PUD), fungal infections and worm infestations respectively (IRNA, Jan, 2021). Safe motherhood and ANC services first and fourth visit and skilled delivery are extremely low in Akobo and Duk County where 4th visit being 25% (DHIS 2021) is a great challenge in Jonglei and Upper Nile counties. I5% alerts and suspected cases tested positive for COVID-19. Cumulatively, 3,517 alerts have been reported, 3,386 (96.3%) were verified, and 3,303 (97.5%) sampled (IDSR WK7, Feb, 2022). COVID-19 vaccination rates remain low in the counties as 1.4% by December 2021. Given the Covid-19 pandemic and third wave underway in South Sudan, preventive measure and follow of protocol is still poor since more of COVID-19 measures dependently from across the borders which hamper COVID-19 measures (HNO 2022). Lack of WASH facilities, and High GAM prevalence above emergency threshold of 15% in these targeted counties compound the already precarious health situation, and therefore integrated health service delivery will be key in reducing maternal mortality rates. This will ensure, protection needs, nutrition needs, WASH interventions in health facilities, and provision of quality health services to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates in all the target counties

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risk 8.1 Medium Likelihood, High Impact Before the conducting treatment/registration/distribution event, Medicair will work closely with the local authority, humanitarian partner to identify the appropriateness of treatment/distribution site including the distance between the location of targeted population and planned treatment/distribution site, and space of site. In addition, Medicair will help identify potential protection risk during this distribution exercise and will respond accordingly. Technical assistance – for example in the form of training and partner agreements – will also be provided. Risk 8.4 Medium Likelihood, High Impact In its project locations, Medicair established that survivors – especially those living in remote communities – find challenges in accessing protection/referral services due to insufficient coverage of the said services, social stigma, and/or not being able to afford transportation cost. Similarly, survivors have little to no awareness of the aforementioned services due to insufficient information on available services. To counter this, Medicair will engage with available service providers and communicate their functionalities to concerning communities. This will be done through direct awareness raising and empowering multiple entry points – community chiefs, local protection structures such as Women Groups, Youth Groups – to equip them for further awareness raising, post-incident support and referral. Furthermore, Medicair will disseminate information in at least one other local language to reach the majority of the population. It will also engage influential figures, men and boys to secure their support in addressing the socio-economic barriers for survivors to access post-incident care services. Furthermore, Medicair will work with referral pathway and protection actors to review and upgrade the accessibility and quality relevant services. Finally, when there is a protection case where survivors either face direct threats or require support to access post-incident services, Medicair will facilitate access and deter threats through protective accompaniment and referral. Risk 8.5 Medium Likelihood, High Impact As part of its community engagement effort, Medicair will make a conscious effort of including marginalized groups, PwD and Older persons, women, boys and girls into its consultations, decision-making processes and/or supported community protection structures. Whenever engaging with a community, Medicair will identify most at-risk persons such as PwD, Older persons, single mothers, widows etc., and by passing this information to humanitarian partners Medicair could ensure access to humanitarian assistance. Besides, Medicair will required provide support and/or protective accompaniment to certain humanitarian assistance, for example distributions. Specific and appropriate measures – such as the use of visual/hearing aid – will be used to ensure feedback is collected from at-risks persons. Once feedback is collected, Medicair will disaggregate data by age, sex, and PwD, thus ensuring age, gender and disability specific concerns are captured and can be used in project cycle. Risk 8.9 Medium Likelihood, Medium Impact To mitigate the risk of land dispute and tenure insecurity, Medicair will facilitate dialogues between host and returnee communities. At project start, Medicair will engage host community to set a peaceful ground for returnees by conducting sessions on peaceful co-existence and social cohesion. In cases of actual disputes, meetings will be facilitated as soon as tension arise or violence erupts. Regular communication channels, engagement of local authorities and community protection/peace structures.

The project is designed to offer health services to all groups of beneficiaries without discrimination. The project will also ensure community engagement is achieved to support in building dykes around health facilities and as well as household levels, this will ensure floods do not displace the community from their original homes.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

During health service delivery, Medicair will be doing growth monitoring which is key in the identification of malnutrition and manage or refer cases for treatment and thus reducing GAM rates. The project will ensure Management of medical complications among children U5 with SAM and surveillance through MUAC screening for under 5 children who seek medical care at the health facilities and refer all malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites, Convergence of health and nutrition activities across all health facilities and enabling Joint health and nutrition education/promotion initiatives. Community Health Workers will form part of the Community based Case Management and the mobile team spearhead MUAC screen at the community level, refer and later trace and track any defaulter from the nutrition sites. The project will also coordinate with FSL sector to ensure that kitchen garden demos are established within nutrition sites situated within health/nutrition as well as hand washing & sanitation facilities.
