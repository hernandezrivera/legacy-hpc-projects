Project Summary

The project aims to enhance health conditions for displaced communitie in Metuge and returning communities in conflict-affected areas of Quissanga and Palma through sustainable water, sanitation, and hygiene interventions. The primary objectives include reducing the incidence of communicable diseases by improving access to safe water, preventing open defecation through enhanced sanitation infrastructure, and fostering behavioral change for improved hygiene practices. The project focuses on community engagement, forming local committees, and conducting needs assessments to tailor interventions to specific vulnerabilities. Objectives also include the distribution of essential items, such as concrete slabs and hygiene kits, to vulnerable groups. The project's overarching goal is to facilitate the sustainable re-establishment of communities in devastated areas by addressing critical health and infrastructure challenges.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

To enhance health conditions by ensuring safe and dignified access to sustainable WASH services for displaced and returned communities in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts. Output 1: Reduction of Communicable Diseases1. Needs and Vulnerability Assessment: - Conduct HH interviews and FGDs in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts, employing trained enumerators, particularly local youth fluent in the language. - Data analysis and beneficiaries prioritization.2. Borehole Construction and Handpump Installation: - Coordinate with SDPI in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga for borehole management. - Select contractors through transparent tender processes, prioritizing technical capacity and sustanability. - Establish and train water committees in each district for water point management and maintenance.3. Water Committee Training and Equipment Provision: - Form or revitalize water committees in each district, ensuring gender balance and credibility. - Train committees over a 5-day period, covering responsibilities, financial management, hygiene, and maintenance. - Equip committees with spare pieces and tools for borehole and hand pump maintenance.4. Satisfaction Survey: - Conduct surveys at the end of each activity in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga to assess effectiveness and responsiveness to community needs. Output 2: Prevention of Open-Defecation1. Needs and Vulnerability Assessment: - Conduct HH interviews and FGDs with trained enumerators for data collection in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts. - Assess the level of open defecation, cultural habits, and local technology used in each district.2. Training in Pit Wall Protection Technology: - Train community leaders, influencers, and WASH activists in each district in latrine construction, especially in sandy areas.3. Distribution of Concrete Slabs and Technical Support: - Distribute 1800 concrete slabs in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts, with 10% tailored for people with disabilities. - Ensure beneficiaries have necessary infrastructure ready before distributing dome slabs in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts. - Work with community leaders in each district to guarantee completion of latrine construction.4. Post Distribution Monitoring: - Monitor post-distribution in each district to ensure proper use. Output 3: Improved Access to Basic Hygiene Items and Behavior Change1. Needs and Vulnerability Assessment: - Conduct HH interviews and FGDs with trained enumerators for data collection in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts. 2. Distribution of Hygiene Kits: - Distribute kits to vulnerable communities in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts, including a 20-litre bucket, plastic buckets, water purifiers, soap, and “capulanas”.3. Training of Activists in Hygiene Practices: - Train local activists in a week-long program in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts, covering WASH components. - Include community leaders and vulnerable groups in each district in the training. 4. Hygiene Promotion and Awareness Campaign: - Conduct door-to-door activities and discussion sessions with activists in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts. - Use theatre shows to convey hygiene messages in the local language in each district, addressing water treatment, handwashing, and latrine use. 5. Satisfaction Survey: - Conduct surveys at the end of each activity in Metuge, Palma, and Quissanga districts to evaluate effectiveness and achieve expected objectives. This comprehensive WASH intervention spans displacement areas like Metuge district and return areas such as Palma and Quissanga districts, aiming to address critical health challenges in all targeted communities. The project emphasizes community involvement, capacity building, and sustainability for lasting impact across diverse contexts.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection mainstreaming is an integral part of AeA’s design strategy, this alongside the participatory methods that AeA use to collect information help greatly to identify the risks posed to each demographic. Staff working in the field are aware of the do no harm, a principle which assists in making the most suitable decisions for the populations in question. We prioritise preserving safety and dignity in the design of any installation, infrastructure or the planning of any event. Ensuring that there is equitable access for all and encouraging participation in order to empower those we are helping. A multi-sector risk analysis for each component of the project will be made to ensure that all risks are considered and mitigated. In addressing specific risks associated with the project, several strategic actions have been considered: 1. Dissemination Plan at Water Boreholes: Implement a targeted plan for dissemination and talks at water boreholes during peak usage times. This approach ensures maximum outreach to borehole users and extends the discussion to other relevant training sessions. 2. Identification of Local Committees: Identify additional local committees such as the women's committee, health committee, and protection committee. Focal points within these committees should be widely known and should not have familial ties with local leaders to prevent coercion. Their appointment is best handled independently to ensure impartiality. 3. Enhanced Accessibility Measures: Install complaint boxes and prominently display the green line contact on the borehole for universal access. Additionally, widen sidewalks to a 4-meter diameter to facilitate maneuverability for wheelchair users, provide access ramps, and ensure that communal facilities are visibly located, safe, and accessible to children, the elderly, and PwD. 4. Transparent Rate-Setting Process: Establish symbolic water rates through a public session, allowing all beneficiaries to voice their opinions. This ensures transparency and inclusivity in determining the appropriate rate for water services. 5. Safety Assurance for Communal Facilities: Guarantee that communal facilities, such as latrines and handpumps, are strategically located in visible, safe, and accessible locations, with due consideration for the needs of children, the elderly, and Persons with Disabilities. This commitment ensures the inclusivity and safety of communal infrastructure for all community members. In alignment with safety policies, Ayuda en Accion is committed to ensuring that all employees, agents, and suppliers undergo training or quarterly refreshers related to protection, standards, and humanitarian principles. This proactive approach aims to prevent any potential harm while upholding the organization's commitment to safety, ethical standards, and the inclusive accessibility of communal facilities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

In order to have a significant participation and impact on women, the project starts with the involvement of women in its elaboration in the office by gathering ideas in women's forums to include aspects related to women's culture and hygiene. Gender balance of 50% in the hiring of casual employees in the field, there is also foreseen that in the water point management committees 50% of the members shall be women. Ayuda en Acción prefer to contact more women in Hygiene Promotion campaigns to change awareness about hygiene practices. A lack of informed participation by women often may result in WASH services that are not appropriate, inaccessible, and unaffordable and do not comply with women’s need. AeA ensures that, during the design phase of its projects, information is gathered on the needs of each gender and of the different vulnerable groups. This is done through specific focus groups discussion, pre-interviews coordinated by Ayuda en Acción protection and gender specialist. Based on the information gathered, activities are then adapted to real needs on the ground. Programmes that include women at all stages of planning, implementing and monitoring are more efficient, effective and sustainable than those that do not prioritise on equitable participation and decision-making.Another aspect is that in all reports there are specific fields for reporting on the involvement of vulnerable groups such as per cent of woman on activity, age segregation, number of disabled people involved and at last, monitoring of activities is also done through PDMs so that our activities fulfil de implementation core principles.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Ayuda en Accion as a humanitarian implementer presents proactive actions to prevent risk and safeguard cases at the concentration points of our WASH activities. The following is a range of actions and is not limited to will ensure that the ideas and opinions of people with disabilities are solicited throughout the project cycle. People with disabilities will be involved in the design of WASH infrastructure, such as latrines and water points, to ensure that their suggestions can lead to improvements in access. Some physical facilities may include, but are not limited to, handrails, seats or ramps. AeA will also ensure that your issues are disseminated to the community in a meaningful way to help improve understanding of your needs. We will also share the progress of activities with the WASH cluster to share or receive more up-to-date contributions on WASH inclusion, as has always been the case.In order to ensure that barriers are removed, a survey of people with disabilities in the community will be carried out; however, this will serve to determine the number of disabled people, but even in the absence of disabled people in the community, this will be considered as a minimum number so that the infrastructure is inclusive in the long term.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The implementation of this project will be preceded by a mapping of risks and determine their degree; An analysis will be made of the risks to the environment of each installation and mitigation measures will be taken to minimize any negative impact or eradicate it completely. For example, the design of boreholes will be carried out to the highest standards, ensuring that aquifers are protected from contamination from above. This will include casing these boreholes as deep as possible and building good quality sanitary seals. Information about these boreholes will be provided to the SDPI local government structures, which will be responsible for the sources in the long term.
