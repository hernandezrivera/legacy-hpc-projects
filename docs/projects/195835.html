Project Summary

In the proposed project, Save a Life International (SLI) will directly target 3,185 beneficiaries, among them being 2,000 Girls and 1,185 boys within Aweil South County. Also, 75 new volunteer teachers will be identified, trained, recruited to support ECD and Primary school learning in targeted 47 schools. Further, 94 SMCs and 56 PTAs will have their capacity built to enable them to support Education activities in schools and in their respective communities. To achieve this, SLI will establish 3 new learning spaces and rehabilitate 4 existing ones to reduce congestion and allow for new enrollments. 94 new latrines will be established while 47 latrines will be rehabilitated across the 47 schools. All new and rehabilitated latrines will be equipped with hand washing facilities and will be segregated by gender. To increase enrollment and retention of out of school boys and girls, SLI will conduct back to learning campaigns at the Boma Level in close coordination with teachers, school management committees, parent teachers associations and Ministry of Education officials. The campaigns will be sustained for a period of twelve months to ensure enrollment and retention of boys and girls who are currently out of schools. Further, SLI will provide learning, teaching and recreation materials for pupils and teachers in all the targeted schools. To reduce incidences of school drop outs, SLI will work with community leaders, PTAs, Teachers and SMCs to monitor cases of school dropout, make follow up on cases and ensure readmission and retention in schools. Noting that adolescent girls who have reached the reproductive age miss schooling due to lack of menstrual hygiene training and materials, SLI will ensure girls are provided with reusable sanitary pads and soap. For an integrated response, SLI will work closely with child protection partners to conduct school and community based child protection monitoring, awareness on child protection concerns, making of referrals for specialized services. To reduce SGBV cases in schools, SLI will coordinate with GBV partners for information sharing in schools, GBV awareness and referrals. Prior to establishing new learning spaces and latrines, SLI will conduct in-depth consultation with Camp Coordination Mechanism and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) partners, community leaders and local administrators to address any underlying land issues and ownership before proceeding to establish or rehabilitate the facilities. In the current HRP call, FSL and Nutrition partners are expected to establish farming and nutrition demonstration sites/gardens. As such, SLI will coordinate with FSL and Nutrition partners to encourage pupils and teachers to participate in FSL and Nutrition activities conducted in demonstration gardens located within schools. Aware that the project will be implemented in Covid19 context, SLI will work closely with the Ministry of Health at the State and County level to ensure adherence to Covid19 protocols. Health partners will be engaged in Covid19 messaging in all schools targeted through the project. Wash partners will be engaged in during hygiene promotion in schools and in the community. SLI will participate in Education assessments and activities supported by the Ministry of Education, cluster and partners in Aweil South to ensure education needs that arise during implementation are identified and met as long as available resources allow.

1 - Needs

Outbreak of Covid19 in 2020 led to closure of schools. A national education needs assessment (ENA) conducted across 442 schools by Education Cluster, UNICEF, MoEST, and Save the Children, showed a rise in cases related to teenage pregnancies by 75%, early/forced marriage by 70%, gender-based violence by 58% and antisocial behavior by 30% due to Covid19 closure of schools. Also, there was a decline on enrollment by 7% in 2021 compared to 2019 and 2020. Worse still, of the children who were attending schools, the head teachers reported that only 2.3% were attending school regularly. After schools were opened in Covid19 context, the main reasons head teachers cited for boys not re-enrolling were inability to pay fees (24%) and cattle rearing (13%) while for girls the reasons were pregnancy (25%) and inability to pay fees (15%). Further, 15% of head teachers reported that all children in their school had writing materials, 24% reported their teachers had a full set of textbooks for the subjects they were teaching while 2% of the 322 schools assessed reported having children with disability having enough mobility or other assistive devices. The ENA report also notes that 64% of head teachers reported that children in their school did not get a meal at school or food to take home, while over 60% reported that the majority of learners were not getting food before attending school. Prior to Covid19, Northern Bahr El Ghazal had a prevalence of 49% (female) and 53% (Males) of out of school children. A recent report by the Education Cluster on schools affected by flooding has listed 37 schools as partially destroyed by floods and 6 schools as completely destroyed by floods. This would mean that after flooding has receded, majority of these schools will require rehabilitation work to restore latrines, classrooms and hand washing facilities to functionality. For schools completely destroyed by floods, there will be need to establish new temporary learning facilities. Failure to do so will mean pupils remain out of school or they attend schooling under trees where they will be exposed to elements of weather. According to MoEST officials, they cite non-functionality of schools due to insecurity; destruction of schools during flooding, school closures; attacks on School; limited accessibility to the schools and remoteness, poor school infrastructure, limited availability of WASH facilities, negative cultural practices where communities prefer educating boys and girls, weak capacity of teachers and Education support structures, lack of teaching, learning, recreational and Menstrual Management supplies, low capacity of teachers in implementing the current curriculum, delayed or lack of salaries for teachers as key factors leading to massive dropouts. Further, officials noted few female teachers in schools which has a negative impact on girls enrolling and remaining in schools. Failure to enroll and retain children in school means that boys will continue to be at risk of recruitment into armed groups, gangs and child Labor while girls face the danger of forced or early marriage. In order for Aweil South to contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) 3 and 4, targets and indicators by 2030, a lot of effort needs to be put in place to attract and gainfully sustain pupils in schools through inclusive, safe and quality learning.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports will be shared with community representatives, education cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in Aweil South. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by SLI’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries and at distribution venues. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, SLI will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer and Protection Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at all targeted schools. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SLI employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community, pupils, teachers and parents will be gathered through multiple channels such as CFM, during meetings, and Focus Group Discussions .The information gathered will be used to inform future programming on the efficiency and effectiveness of the assistance. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where SLI is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SLI will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, SLI will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, SLI will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SLI is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). SLI is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, SLI will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with mainstreaming protection and creating awareness on PSEA, training consultants, field staffs and enumerators on PSEA and their policies pertaining to PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries, during distribution of shelter and NFIs. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection concerns. SLI gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Aweil South. Necessary PSEA referrals will be done if and when they arise by the gender officer.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

On risks of beneficiaries being exposed to violence as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, SLI will conduct a safety audit on all school sites to map existing and potential risks. Safety Audit Findings will be embedded in project work plan and safety matrix to ensure all project activities are conducted in locations and sites where no risk is posed to staff, project supplies or beneficiaries. SLI will ensure registration and meeting points are near settlement sites to reduce on distance travelled by beneficiaries. On risks of girls being exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, SLI has a robust PSEA policy with all the staff properly trained on PSEA. SLI will conduct PSEA training targeting enumerators, community mobilizers and AAPs committees. Also, SLI’S gender and protection officers will conduct sensitization targeting women and girls, community leaders and local authorities on PSEA mitigation and reporting. On risks that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA, SLI’S Child Protection policy prohibits any form of child Labour and child exploitation. Gender Officer and the Protection Officer will conduct community sensitization and awareness to ensure child protection concerns are addressed and mitigated before they occur. Cases of Child Protection will be referred to appropriate child protection actors at project locations. On risks that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The project targeting criteria is clear and targets these category of beneficiaries. Community communication and sensitization will be conducted targeting beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in order to minimize of tensions arising from sections of the community who do not receive the assistance. To mitigate HLP related disputes and conflict, SLI through local authorities, protection partners and chiefs will conduct due diligence on land tenure system to ascertain land ownership, proof of ownership and potential and underlying land and property concerns at the community level and IDPs sites. Training on HLP access issues and HLP dispute Resolutions will be ensured to mitigate HLP cases. Staff, local authorities and AAPs will assist to clarify tenure arrangements and authentication of HLP ownership documents/alternative proof of ownership prior to establishing learning spaces and latrines.
