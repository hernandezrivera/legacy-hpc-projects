Project Summary

Ongoing hostilities and violence have heavily impacted the Far-North region during the past five years, where lower magnitude displacements are still triggered due to small scale sporadic attacks by Boko Haram and other non-State armed groups (NSAG) and natural disasters. Since the beginning of 2020, the region has witnessed continuous hostilities that have uprooted more than 321,886 IDPs, 48,769 out-of-camp refugees and 123,489 returnees. Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services such as education and health as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. IOM reports on displacements in the Lake Chad Basin (LCB) have highlighted the growing importance of return movements of former IDPs since 2017, with the total returnee population representing approximately 25% of the population affected by displacements in Far-North region; however this tendency seems to have decreased in 2020.
IOM, as the only actor present in this region with the capacity, expertise and access to certain areas to conduct comprehensive assessments on displacement trends and statistics, was asked by OCHA and the Humanitarian Coordinator to carry out multi-sectoral needs assessments (MSNA) in the region. The data from MSNA is shared with more than 30 operational humanitarian actors and is used as the primary data source for the update of the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Data that is produced and disseminated through the IOM DTM methodology has become the key source of information on internal displacement across both crisis-affected regions and is used by humanitarian partners for vital planning purposes. The last partner survey conducted in late 2019 stated that 95% were either ‘very satisfied’ or ‘satisfied’ with the data supplied, with 95% also stating that they either want more DTM data or the same amount for coordinating their activities. Comprehensive assessments provide humanitarian actors with reliable data on the number and location of displaced populations (IDPs and returnees), which contribute to a better understanding of the displacement trends and most pressing needs.
In light of the above, IOM seeks to continue providing the humanitarian community with information on displacement trends and most pressing sectoral needs of conflict-affected populations in these two regions. IOM will continue its data collection activities with additional rounds of displacement tracking, Emergency Tracking Tools, Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments as well as the general informal support to the humanitarian community in Far-North with ad-hoc information requests and situational analysis. A range of information products will be distributed throughout 2021 depending on partners’ needs, from the Emergency Tracking Tool to help coordinate partners’ emergency response to sudden displacements, to larger scale Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA) that will fit into wider Humanitarian Country Team planning.
IOM also plans to continue the roll-out of its Stability Index for the Lake Chad Basin area in Cameroon for the identification of areas poised for longer term transition and recovery programming , as well as to develop a human security dashboard for Far-North in coordination with other HDPN Task Force agencies, both for the further identification and engagement of municipalities on areas of convergence for high quality nexus programming in Cameroon.
IOM will further engage with Governmental counterparts to ensure capacity transfer and appropriation on specific data collection activities conducted for several years in the Far North region.
All these activities will contribute to better availability of data to allow for better evidence based programming.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In order to support evidence-based and accountable service provision by humanitarian partners in Cameroon, IOM plans to continue collecting, processing and disseminating information on displacement trends and most pressing sectoral needs of conflict-affected populations in the Far-North region of the country, through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). The DTM methodology, tools used, as well as dissemination strategy will be adapted to the context and will be closely coordinated with key stakeholders (OCHA, cluster leads, humanitarian actors) in order to ensure that the data collected will be used by partners for a more informed response. A variety of DTM products will be made to fit humanitarian data gaps, such as displacement reports and dashboards, Emergency Tracking Tool dashboards, datasets, reports on specific thematic sectors, data from Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA) that feed directly into Humanitarian Response Plans (HRP) and Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), as well as a wider stock-taking evaluation of DTM activities led so far to address partner’s needs. Work will be made throughout the action also to ensure that IOM’s DTM operations are contributing towards further aims to transition protection cluster programming to recovery and more sustainable development, with the continued roll out of the stability index and human security index in partnership with other United Nations agencies. Outcome 1: Humanitarian actors in Far-North regions benefit from high quality information to allow them to efficiently respond to the needs of beneficiaries based on informed decisions, for an overall more efficient and coordinated humanitarian system. Output 1.1: Humanitarian partners and relevant governmental authorities are informed properly and timely about the locations, numbers and living conditions of displaced populations (IDPs, returnees and out-of-camp refugees) present in Far-North region of Cameroon through the regular production and dissemination of DTM products and services. Output 1.2: Humanitarian, protection and early recovery partners including relevant national and local governmental authorities have sufficient contextual and situational information to aid in the transition from protection programming to more sustainable recovery and development operations.
