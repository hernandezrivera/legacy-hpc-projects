Project Summary

This Shelter and Camp Coordination and Camp Management (S-CCCM) project will cover a comprehensive target of 107,462 individuals or 22,156 households across Rohingya and host communities. The geographical coverage includes Camp 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 21, 3, 4 Ext, 5, 6, 7, 17, 21 and Kutupalong RC, and the host community in Rajapalong, Palongkhali, Haldia Palong and Jalia Palong. The targeted activities are derived from the J-MSNA 2023, Shelter minimum standard assessment report, BRAC’s database, and internal need assessment conducted in 2023.
The Shelter support activity involves the reuse and recycling of used shelter materials, and new shelter construction for 800 (650 in camps and 150 in Host community) households. Regular shelter support will be provided to 2,500 households where treated bamboo processed in BRAC bamboo treatment plants will be used. Among them, 700 Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI) households will receive labour and porter support. Household-level site improvement activities extend to 3,023 populations across multiple camps, encompassing various constructions, repairs, and improvements such as bamboo drain (1,164 meters), bamboo-geotex retaining wall (1,940 meters), geo-bag pathway (800 meters), ramp (50 meters), stair (700 meters), and bamboo wooden bridge (500 meters).
BRAC will mainstream Disaster Risk Management (DRM) with multisectoral engagement in both the camp and host community. It will provide support for reforestation activities and restore and conserve natural ecosystems. Moreover, a pilot initiative will be conducted to develop a resilience matrix, which will further help in decision-making in terms of disaster preparedness, response, and mitigation of the camp. In addition, BRAC will operate 4 Mobile Firefighting Units (MFU), and 7 cyclone preparedness units, and engage 120 well-trained volunteers for multi-hazard response. BRAC also focuses on empowering community representatives for resilience and utilizing local resources to mitigate risks. To enhance preparedness, BRAC has prepositioned Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) equipment, including search and rescue gear, first aid equipment, Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP) gear, and Mobile Firefighting Unit (MFU) gear. BRAC also will provide capacity building in EPR, communication, and overall community engagement techniques. Moreover, the distribution of Tie Down Kits and emergency shelter support for cyclone preparation, tailored for 1,500 households.
The overall camp management includes stakeholder coordination in the camp, service monitoring, and reporting gaps. BRAC will lead coordination meetings and advocate for identified gaps. To involve affected people in decision-making, BRAC will maintain community engagement structures through thematic groups formed through inclusive consultations.
BRAC will ensure a swift response to community concerns through the Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in each of the targeted camps. This system, which will be working with site management and community-based protection teams, allows for quick project adjustments based on community feedback. Monthly monitoring reports enhance project effectiveness and empower the community through a dynamic feedback loop. Besides, the 4Ws, camp profile, service monitoring, volunteer management tool, and central inventory for all the previously completed and updated assessments of service monitoring in an interactive information management system, will enable humanitarian aid providers to conduct accurate and meaningful need assessments.
For oversight, a dedicated monitoring team employed by BRAC will utilize a real-time online tool to assess progress and quality regularly. The project's success will be gauged through the frequency of evaluations and adjustments made based on valuable feedback.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The demographic data from UNHCR reveals a concerning situation within refugee camps, where 51.4% of the population is female, and 52% are under 18 years old. Vulnerable groups, such as those over 60 and individuals with disabilities, face significant challenges in shelter maintenance. BRAC has been actively supporting these vulnerable populations and is committed to addressing their specific needs. The critical need identified is shelter repair due to the use of non-durable materials and exposure to natural hazards. Annual replacement requirements for materials like bamboo, tarpaulin, and ropes are urgent due to their limited lifespan and risks. The Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (JMSNA) report underscores the vulnerability of shelters, with 72% of refugees and 60% of the host community facing challenges. Shelter materials upgrade is a converging need identified by 9% of refugees and 14% of the host community.The vulnerability of shelters is further highlighted by leaks during rain, reported by 53% of host community households and 39% of refugee households. Limited humanitarian support contributes to the challenges, with 60% of refugee households not improving their shelters due to a lack of assistance. Shelter standards assessments reveal that only 15% of shelters adhere to sector guidance in tying down, and 69% display signs of insect infestation in structural bamboo.In 2023, community-led initiative assessments identified 1,352 community infrastructure repair needs, but resource limitations allowed only 15% of that to be addressed. BRAC, through coordination efforts, implemented an additional 2%, leaving 83% of the needs to be directly addressed by BRAC or through effective coordination.With the increasing frequency of natural and man-made hazards, there is an urgent need for an improved decision support system. This system would assess overall resilience scores in four aspects (physical, financial, environmental, and institutional) to highlight gaps and needs, facilitating the decision-making process for disaster preparedness and response efforts. Furthermore, large-scale afforestation efforts are essential to mitigate the risks of landslides, especially in areas with shelters on steep and deforested slopes of sand and clay. The 'Watershed Management Plan (2020-2025)' underscores the degradation of ecosystems due to population pressure, leading to waterlogging, flash floods, and landslides.In the realm of camp management and coordination, BRAC has been a central hub, receiving 22,936 complaints in 2022 and 20,968 cases recorded until October 2023. BRAC plays a vital role in advocating for refugee protection, facilitating intra-camp relocations, and addressing community needs and expectations. The Continuous Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) awareness among respondents stands at 52%, showcasing its potential as a channel for reaching actors responsible for providing necessary support, as reported in the 2023 JMSNA. BRAC aims to continue these efforts in 2024, ensuring effective communication, relocation assistance, and community engagement.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

BRAC is aligned with ISCG’s rationalization process and strategic shift and initiated merging the Shelter and Site Management sectors into S-CCCM. In addition, BRAC is dedicated to aligning its project with the Rationalization Principles, particularly emphasizing an integrated approach and ensuring predictability and continuity. Before initiating any activity, BRAC actively engages in consultations with relevant ISCG Sectors and Working Groups. Participation in coordination platforms at various levels, including camp, Upazila, and district levels, enhances understanding of diverse needs and promotes strategic alignment. The S-CCCM project at BRAC coordinates through various units, such as WASH, education, Health, and Protection, which collaborate seamlessly within the same camps. This inter-sectoral integrated approach enables the sharing of support and resources, fostering a comprehensive response. During emergencies, BRAC collaborates actively with other agencies to ensure a swift and coordinated response.Predictability and continuity are foundational aspects of BRAC's S-CCCM project, exemplified by its remarkable six years of consistent operation. Serving as the SMS focal point in seven UNHCR-supported camps, BRAC's sustained presence with the persistent funding support from the UNHCR provides stability and reliability for both refugees and partnering agencies. Considering the changes in operational modalities, BRAC will seek additional funding for addressing the S-CCCM needs, which integrate shelter support, site management, and disaster risk reduction. This consistent presence ensures that the S-CCCM needs of the population are met with reliability, aligning with the Rationalization Principles' emphasis on predictability and continuity.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Shelter-Camp Coordination and Camp Management (S-CCCM) project will address protection mainstreaming as a cross-cutting issue to serve the affected community by integrating protection concerns across all aspects of shelter construction, disaster preparedness, and response as well as camp management and coordination. S-CCCM will also promote the active participation of the Rohingya and host community in decisions affecting their life, ensuring that their protection needs and concerns are considered where special attention will be given to the protection of women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Services and support will be tailored to their specific needs. The project will also ensure that staff adhere to a strict code of conduct, establish reporting mechanisms, and conduct awareness campaigns to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse within the camps.Moreover, during the shelter construction distribution of Shelter repair materials, attention will be given to vulnerable groups, such as women-headed households, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities. S-CCCM will also collaborate with relevant sectors (protection) to create a protective environment for children and address child labor, trafficking, and other risks. The project will also help resolve disputes between camps and host communities, promoting peaceful coexistence and addressing protection concerns related to community tensions. In addition, the SCCCM project will conduct awareness campaigns and information sharing to educate the community about their rights, available services, and how to report protection concerns. By mainstreaming protection, the BRAC’s S-CCCM will ensure that the Rohingya and host community's safety, well-being, and dignity are at the forefront.

3.3 - Age & Disability

S- CCCM will ensure that camp infrastructure, including shelters and facilities, is accessible and accommodating for people with disabilities and aged people, including ramps, handrails, and accessible latrines. In line with the commitment, the project has set a target of approximately 12% for individuals with disabilities, promoting equity and accessibility.Recognizing the pivotal role of staff and volunteers, sector-specific inclusive programming training is provided to enhance their capacity. S-CCCM will also actively involve older community members and people with disabilities in decision-making processes, ensure their voices are heard, and participate in shaping camp management and services. This project will implement measures to protect older individuals and people with disabilities from exploitation, violence, and abuse, with a particular focus on their safety and dignity. Moreover, during any emergencies, the vulnerable groups will be prioritized for evacuation to safe places and provide assistance. A referral mechanism will be established to ensure rehabilitation services for every person with disabilities. This streamlined process ensures that individuals with disabilities receive the necessary support and services to enhance their overall well-being. To address the specific needs of women and adolescent girls with disabilities, additional labor and porter support are provided to repair their shelters.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will be built on the insights gained from participatory assessments and collaborative analyses of priority needs. Project designs will be rooted in the 2023 Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) and other relevant participatory assessments, ensuring alignment with the genuine needs of the affected population. Plans for involving the community in evaluation methodologies will be formulated, and results will be shared in accessible formats during consultation meetings, providing a platform for communities to express their opinions and concerns. Transparent beneficiary selection processes, guided by engagement with the affected population, will be ensured through joint technical verification involving team members from S-CCCM and the government counterpart. A coordinated strategy for disseminating information on assistance categories distribution plans, and addressing shelter-related complaints and feedback will be put into action. Coordination meetings at each camp, with the presence of community leaders, will be facilitated to foster open communication and transparency. Regular perception surveys will be conducted to monitor the affected population, guiding improvements to the CFRM. This ensures that community feedback actively contributes to project enhancement.In the event of changes or adjustments to shelter and site management-related services and assistance, communities will be promptly informed through awareness sessions and sector-approved communication channels. Achieving accountability to the affected populations in the SCCCM project will be an ongoing and dynamic process, prioritizing transparency, community engagement, and the active integration of community feedback into decision-making and implementation phases.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

As a member of the PSEA Network, BRAC will uphold the mandatory requirement for all JRP partners to join the network. BRAC has a robust Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) prevention and response policy in place, ensuring alignment with global PSEA standards. Representation in the PSEA Network by a designated PSEA Focal Point will facilitate uninterrupted coordination and implementation of PSEA activities within BRAC. The mainstreaming of PSEA will be woven into each stage of project cycle management, with 100% of staff, including volunteers, undergoing mandatory PSEA training. PSEA awareness materials, such as posters, flyers, and videos, along with key PSEA messages and Complaint Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRM), will be made visible to beneficiaries. Confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate CFRM will be readily available. In case of an SEA complaint, investigation, follow-up, and potential disciplinary action will be the responsibility of the concerned organization, following internal procedures. The PSEA Focal Point will refer complaints to the relevant GBV camp-level focal point in line with GBV referral pathways. For survivors under the age of 18, the PSEA Focal Point will engage with the Child Protection sub-sector, ensuring tailored assistance for the specific impact of SEA.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project considers incorporating Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and environmental aspects while ensuring that the climate change issues are addressed. The identified support to the project participants will be provided on the basis of the proper Community Risk Assessment (CRA) process, which involves identifying potential vulnerabilities, eg; landslide, fire, slope failure, flash floods, cyclone, etc. To address those risks properly, the following activities will be implemented, like; integration of resilient shelter design, incorporation of improved construction materials and techniques, context-specific training sessions for the camp volunteers and Cyclone Preparedness Volunteers (CPP), dissemination of early warning messages among the camp residents, develop evacuation plans, conduct mock drills of emergency response and implement activities related to fire prevention and response. These initiatives not only enhance the community's capacity to respond effectively in the event of a disaster but also foster a sense of ownership and resilience among the affected population.
