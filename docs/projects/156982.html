Project Summary

The project will provide targeted, focused Mental Health services including assessment and treatment of priority mental health conditions as per WHO mhGAP Programme with training and supervision provided by a qualified psychiatrist, trained on mhGAP ToT and experienced in mhGAP roll out and supervision in Syria. These services will be integrated into primary healthcare and closely integrated with complimentary psychosocial support services provided under a different project (mental health case management, psychosocial support, WHO Problem Management +, group support and outreach). The project will target all community members, making a special effort to target and increase access for the most isolated and vulnerable through a strong referral mechanism between primary healthcare facilities, Mobile Medical Units and Physical Rehabilitation facilities. MHPSS services will be available for all community members, both host and displaced, regardless of age, gender or impairment.

Needs

Conflict-affected individuals, including more vulnerable groups such as women, children, and older persons, continue to exhibit high levels of psychological psychosocial problems distress as a result of the ongoing conflict and heightened violence. Overall, mental health needs are increasing. 8 years into the crisis, additional support is urgently needed to scale up services to address increasing mental health needs, with an estimated 27,635 people in need of mental health services through health centers, 61,788 people needing outpatient psychiatric care and almost 12,000 requiring inpatient psychiatric care. WHO estimates that one in 30 people living in Syria suffers from severe mental health conditions (such as severe depression, psychosis, or disabling anxiety) and at least 1 in 5 suffers from mild to moderate mental health conditions. Most children and adolescents affected by the crisis will initially display signs of psychological distress, including memories of the event, nightmares, some social withdrawal, difficulty concentrating and sometimes regression to previous developmental behaviors, such as clinginess, bedwetting or thumb-sucking. Many individuals have witnessed or experienced violence, destruction, the killing and/or injuring of family members, and have lived for over 6 years with daily fear of separation from family, displacement, injury, and/or death and loss. For those who are displaced; inadequate access to basic necessities is also taking a significant toll on psychological and psychosocial wellbeing. Initial Rapid Assessments conducted by the NGOs MHPSS department in 2018 in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates after the recent influx of IDPs from East Ghouta, indicate the need for different levels of mental health and psychosocial support services. The causes of MHPSS needs identified are both acute, resulting from the recent ongoing conflict and displacement from East Ghouta and clashes in Idleb Governorate, as well as chronic as a result of the protracted conflict which has affected peoples’ lives and psychological wellbeing in many ways as people have lost loved ones, homes, their traditional roles in family and society, ongoing displacement as well as persons who have mental disorders dating from before the onset of the conflict. The majority of persons with pre-existing disorders were receiving medication (for example for epilepsy or psychosis) before the crisis, and are particularly vulnerable due to disruption of their medication, limited access to care, and the weakening of familial networks and other social support. Children with developmental disorders and delays are among the most vulnerable, and as such, far greater efforts are needed to provide both children and their parents/care-givers with adequate support, preventing both further complications to health needs and socio-economic marginalization, while also strengthening resilience at the individual and household level. People with disabilities and those living with chronic health conditions are at a higher risk of developing mental disorders, psychological distress and substance misuse due to injury, incapacity, changes to living condition, lack of access to services and isolation. As well as this Persons with Disabilities and persons living with chronic health conditions who do experience MNS conditions have limited access to MHPSS services and opportunities as they face additional barriers. As well as areas in which service provision is ongoing (Bab Alsalame, Bab Alnur in Aleppo and Atmah Camp, Karamah Camp, Idleb City, Kafr Yamhul and Khan Thuman), Maraat al Numan has also been identified as being in need of establishing MHPSS services, there are currently no mental health service providers or NGOs providing MHPSS services operating in the area which in 2018 has seen influxes of conflict affected Syrians from other parts of the country, including East Ghouta and more recently, Western Aleppo.

Implementation of activities

All activities will be implemented in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. Activities in Idleb Governorate and Khantuman, Aleppo will be implemented directly, activities in Aleppo, with the exception of Khantuman will be implemented through a local partner. Individual MHPSS ServicesAn integrated and holistic approach to MHPSS service delivery for those with MNS Disorders and/or living with acute and chronic psychological distress will be provided at Primary Healthcare Centers. This approach will include assessment and treatment of priority mental health conditions by mhGAP-trained Doctors with referral to complimentary services. MHPSS TrainingsTHE NGO will provide trainings to local organizations and other humanitarian staff, especially those working in healthcare settings, in mhGAP-based Detection and Referral. Services will be available for all who seek it regardless of age, gender or disability. In order to increase access, strong referral mechanism will be put in place between other services the NGO provides in the areas, including outreach, as well as other organizations working in the area. Outreach services will specifically target certain groups within the community which may not normally be aware or have access to services, such as the elderly, people living with chronic disease or disability, and youths. All medical doctors will be trained or have been trained on mhGAP-IG which includes a specific module on assessment and treatment of Child and Adolescent mental and behavioral disorders. All MH services will be supervised and monitored both on the ground and remotely by MHPPS Manager, depending on access and security situation. MHPSS Coordinator will provide overall leadership, oversight and technical support to the MHPSS team. Trainings on MHPSS topics including mhGAP and on the job supervision/consultation will be delivered by MHPSS Specialist (a Psychiatrist who is trained in mhGAP TOT and experienced in training and supervision on the mhGAP Programme). mhGAP doctors will provide direct services to the beneficiaries in the field.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of sexual exploitation: All MHPSS staff will receive SGBV training and will know how and when to report suspected cases of exploitation. If beneficiaries are completing a referral with another service provider they may ask their case manager, psychosocial worker, family member or other trusted person to accompany them. Doctors will be trained in GBV and CMR principles as well as internal PSEA guidelines. Risk of violence/harm toward staff: Security guards will be employed at each PHC/MHPSS Center. Risk of work related stress, burnout and vicarious trauma: Staff Wellbeing is provided by the organization (through another project but available for all staff) in the form of activities, staff welfare meetings, trainings on stress reduction, positive coping, provided by the staff wellbeing officer, as well as referral to more advanced services should they be required.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of military incursion, forcing cessation of service delivery: A contingency plan will be in place for such as event, where MHPSS beneficiaries will be provided some additional stock of medication, if deemed safe by the psychiatrist or mhGAP Doctor. Beneficiaries will also be referred to other ongoing services in this situation or complimentary services which could help them to prepare for a gap in service provision. Risk of lack of qualified specialists: Should no psychiatrist be available to provide training and supervision, a different modality using remote supervision for MDs to continue assessment and treatment of priority conditions would be enacted. This could include contracting an external consultant or another organization (such as WHO) to provide the necessary training and supervision. Risk of lack of psychotropic medications: This is a constant risk given the situation, however if medications cannot be transported to Syria, WHO are ready to provide certain priority medications. The NGO will continually monitor medication stocks and maintain the necessary amounts as well as a small contingency in case of emergencies Risk that services are not providing benefit to the beneficiary: The NGO staff will conduct regular assessments with beneficiaries in order to assess changes in functioning. Beneficiaries with negative or neutral scores will be identified and their treatment assessed, in coordination with the mhGAP Supervisor and either altered or referral be made to a different service.
