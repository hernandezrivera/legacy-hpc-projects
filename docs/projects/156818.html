Project Summary

This project responds to the emergency needs of formal and informal education sector in Hasaka, Raqqa, and partially newly freed accessible areas of Deir Ezzor – North East Syria. The purpose of the project is to enable access to quality sustainable education for IDPs, locals most in need, and returnees in areas in North East Syria for both formal and informal education through support to schools, programs at camps, local education councils, and support for teachers.
According to HNO 2019, More than one in three schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education such as shelter for displaced people. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students, causing overcrowding. Only in Hasaka there is 321,689 children and teachers in need to education urgent support.
The target geographical area in this project is Ain Issa, Raqqa, marginalized villages East of Hasaka and close or under Areesheh and Shadadah areas, and 3 villages located between Markada and Sur under Deir Ezzor. In most of targeted areas in this project most of schools are destroyed or not functioning and teachers are unable to find suitable place to provide schooling to children living in these areas. The area is inhabited by majority tribal and Bedouin families in addition to returnees, IDPs, and refugee families from Iraq. The total number of populations in need to urgent education support is 29,010 Individuals in addition to 2,970 IDPs and circa 109 refuges from Iraq.
The suffering is double in areas recently freed from ISIS in rural Hasaka as most of schools were either partially or totally destroyed by ISIS before it was defeated. Based on an official need assessment from Rad Shakrah Local District Council in Rural Hasaka there is 54 villages freed from ISIS in east Hakaka on the road to Al-Houl IDPs Camp where poverty level is between 60-100%.
Since 2014 to today no local or international humanitarian or civil society actor have responded to the needs of those communities in most of those target areas. One example is that, Local Council of Rad Shakrah expressed their urgent need for support so education can be restored to save a whole generation of children witnessed ISIS dark times so they can access education and get back to normal life.
DOZ aims to respond to that following our commitments to Un Agenda for Humanity signed at World Humanitarian Summit in 2016 and our ongoing commitment to principles especially Leave No One Behind and No Lost Generation.

Needs

Rad Shakra District in Rural East Hasaka is an area that consists of 54 villages with a total population of 29,010 Individuals in addition to ,2970 IDPs and circa 109 refugees based on an official need assessment provided by local council to DOZ organization. Only circa 500-600 teachers live in these areas and most of them are jobless due to lack of education support and function infra structures of most schools. Also, there is 340 PWD (people with disabilities) in the target areas. Those villages are inhabited by locals mostly tribal background, Bedouins, IDPs from Deir Azzor, Raqqah, and refugees from Iraq. Children in those villages have been deprived from schooling since ISIS occupied that areas in 2014 as education was prohibited by ISIS. After ISIS got defeated in 2016 those villages don’t have schools to provide education to children. Local Council of Rad Shakrah provided DOZ with a need assessment of 23 schools that need renovation, educational materials, and support for teachers so all children can be included in the education system and have places at schools. In addition to that, the targeted villages between Markada and Sur in Deir Ezzor there is circa 3000 families where among 2000 students and pupils live there and being deprived of education for years. Also, there is circa 100 teachers in those villages that need to have functioning schools and materials so they can do their jobs and activate education again in those villages. The schools in those villages require renovation process, educational materials, and support for education materials for pupils. In Raqqa, DOZ local team aims to respond to the need of education in Ain Issa area either in the camp by setting up and running educational communal school tent or support one or two villages y renovating schools or pro viding educational equipment and materials. According to HNO 2019, 2.1 million children in Syria are out of school, and a further 1.3 million are at risk of dropping out. Displaced communities remain significantly underserved, with most IDP camps having insufficient or non-existent education services. Over 140,000 teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts. The remaining are overwhelmed, resulting in an overall poor quality of education. Quality of teaching is key to students’ performance, and teachers’ capacity is fundamental to children’s experience of a quality education. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. An extreme shortage and under payment of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education.Only in Hasaka, Raqqa, and Deir Azzor there is a total of 786,544 in need to education and a total of 16,386 teachers in need to support. Based on urgent needs elaborated above, DOZ wishes to address those needs by supporting education services and programs in those geographical areas where according to HNO 2019 severity level they come under the severity level 4-6. Sources: - HNO 2019 - Need Assessment by local council of Rad Shakra district in Rural East Hasaka- Field visits by DOZ Syria local teams to assess locations- WoS Education Severity Needs Analysis, 2018- WoS Education Needs Assessment, 2018- Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), 2018

Implementation of activities

The implementation plan of this project is made of 5 overlapping processes for one year: 1- Renovating 19 schools that serve 54 villages in most marginalized villages in Hasaka. 2- Renovating 2 or 3 schools in villages between Markada and Sur in Deir Ezzor. 3- Setting up educational communal tent in Ain Issa camp or renovating one or 2 schools in villages in Ain Issa, Raqqa. 4- Map on the micro level school children from both locals, IDPs, and refugees in each village to put final numbers of education materials and schooling bags required for each school and village. 5- Coordination and Training workshops with local councils or teachers on some villages. 6- After schools are renovated, distribution of 16,044 schooling bags to children before the start of the next school term. 7- DOZ Syria might open temporary community learning center for children and adults is some villages that don’t have schools or schools requires renovation so children can catch up their skills such as reading, writing, life-skills, and recreational activities by local teachers till the school is ready to serve and be connected to education sector.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Security escalations or violence against field staff or beneficiaries in the target areas: DOZ works and coordinates all project steps in close coordination with local councils and will be informed in advance of any potential escalations. In addition to that, DOZ Syria makes a security a safety analysis on local and regional levels to make sure that mitigating measures can be planned for any such potential case. Furthermore, the DOZ local team pay regular visits to monitor target areas before, during and after each implementation step. 2. Corruption or Fraud in one or more activities especially for renovation work and materials purchase: DOZ Germany works directly with DOZ Syria and applies its strict financial procedures and policies in each single purchase to make sure that transparency and accountability is part and parcel of each single purchase process of the project. On the other hand, in addition to internal financial controls DOZ Germany hires external auditors to control project financial files and reports. 3. Lack of inclusion and or exclusion of certain individuals or groups: DOZ has zero-tolerance policy against any act of ethnical, sexual, or religious discrimination and its policies are applied on all international staff, local staff, contractors, partners, and volunteers. Furthermore, DOZ filed team in company with local councils will supervise all steps of planning and implementation of the project in the field.
