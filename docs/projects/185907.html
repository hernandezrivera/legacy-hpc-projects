Project Summary

After the violent attack against student and teachers in G.H.B.S. Ekondo-Titi, this November 24th, this project aims to implement the Education in Emergencies methodology in Ndian Division.
Specifically, this project will provide:
• Monitor and enhance access to education in the Division in collaboration with relevant authorities and private service providers and devise community-based strategies to minimize out of school children
• Safety audits and training for teachers on safety, protection, inclusive education, to mitigate the effects of violence against education
• Pilot school feeding programs in low uptake and high food insecurity areas of Ekondo-Titi, Mundemba, and Bamusso.
• Financial support to vulnerable girls, boys, and adolescents at risk of exclusion
Overall, it is expected this combined emergency response will build community resilience and increase educational uptake, helping children in Ndian Division to bridge their educative gap with the rest of the nation.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Before the crisis, Ndian Division was already isolated. The main avenue to access the Division is Mbonge Road, which is a dirt road that becomes mud during the rainy season. Mbonge Road is considered dangerous due to the multiplicity of armed groups, and many commercial and government stakeholders are only using the sea route by boat. Ndian has more than 120 rural communities, with populations ranging from 250 people to about 6,000 and about 100 fishermen communities, ranging from 100 to about 1,000 persons.This area has been chronically underdeveloped and children of Ndian have a critical disadvantage in front of children of most other areas in Cameroon. Ekondo-Titi was one of the first communities to be affected by the education crisis and by the political violence. Today, it is still heavily affected. On the 24th of November armed men shot against children and teachers in G.H.B.S. Ekondo-Titi, killing 4 children and 2 teachers. The area is now also affected by food insecurity, and Reach Out has identified more than 200 children of Ekondo-Titi affected by Global Acute Malnutrition since the year 2020. The extending crisis has critically affected children in Ndian, if a child was 7 in late 2016, just beginning to master reading and writing, by now he is 9 and has forgotten everything about it already. This means if nothing is done, he would not really learn to read and write effectively until he is 10, 11 or 12. A child that is 7 today might have never been to school. This gap will make it impossible for them to reach University when he is 17 or 18, and compete with the rest of his peers from non-conflictive areas.
