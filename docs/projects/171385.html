Project Summary

The proposed project targets 15 districts in 6 regions of Somalia namely Nugaal region (Garowe District), Sool region (Hudun and Boame districts), Bakool region (Hudur, Elberde, Rabdure, and Wajid) Lower Shabelle (Afgoye district), Hiran region (Beletweyn, Bulo burto and Jalalaqsi districts) and Banadir (Kahda, Hodan, Abdiaziz and Waaberi). The project aims to cover the health needs of drought/conflict affected women, men, boys and girls in the target locations by providing curative and preventive basic lifesaving health services among the communities by Action Against Hunger. COVID-19 preventive measures will be adhered during implementation. Protective gear will be provided to health care workers during the service delivery to minimize disease transmission as well as capacity building on Infection Prevention & Control, and COVID-19 case management. The project is planned to reach a total of 167,190 (81,923 M & 85,267 F) for a period of 12 months. The project will provide services through one hospital in Afgoye district, one referral health centre in Elbarde district, and in fourteen health centres, in addition to fourteen primary health units, in line with EPHS package. Additionally, the health facility committee and community-level health service provision will be strengthened in the project target location with a full representation of key community stakeholders including people living with disability. The project will be linked with other interventions (Nutrition, WASH & FSL) in the same location in order to provide a holistic package to the target beneficiaries.

Project Activities

Action Against Hunger will support 30 health facilities (one hospital, one referral health center, 14 HCs, and 14 PHUs) in 15 districts to provide quality Health services including OPD, immunization, and inpatient. In line with IMCI and STGs, ACF will support the management of common childhood illnesses, the provision of prompt and effective treatment, and the promotion of preventive measures against malaria, acute respiratory infections, and diarrheal diseases. Moreover, ACF will through this action provide maternal health services through the provision of Ante-natal care services including at least 2 doses of tetanus toxoid vaccination (TT2+), intermittent preventive treatment for malaria in pregnancy, use of treated bed nets (ITN), iron and folate supplements, treatment of common diseases, deworming, treatment of anaemia, screening for pre-eclampsia with urine protein dipstick and BP and further ANC monitoring, treatment of STIs, pregnancy monitoring, early detection of high risk pregnancies and close follow up of high risk pregnancies, standardized management of pre-eclampsia, skilled birth attendants & provision of birth spacing methods. The supported facilities will carry out basic and comprehensive emergency maternal obstetric and neonatal care services. Other services provided will include prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV, CEmONC for any at risk pregnancies and any woman with complications including cesarean section and blood transfusion, postnatal care. Additionally, facilities will be equipped with a microscope, centrifuge, etc, as well as operation theatre equipment like OT bed, oxygen machines, examination lamp, suction machine, and ICU monitors, etc. Moreover, the action will support the establishment of a health facility committee in the project target locations, as well as capacity building on IMCI, STGs, COVID-19 IPC measures, and RCCE in line with WHO and MOH guidelines. Furthermore, the ACF team will ensure participation in regional and national health cluster meetings, working groups, and coordination mechanisms. The notifiable disease will be routinely reported in the supported facilities through EWARN. IPC measures will be strengthened where handwashing points will be maintained and set up at all sites. Staff or CHW will be available at the entrance of the HF to ensure that everyone coming in washes their hands. Key messages on prevention of COVID-19 will be shared during routine health education sessions both at the health facility and community. Additionally, protective gears will be provided to the health care workers and CHWs to minimize the risk of COVID-19 infection.The project is aimed at providing access to basic health services among U5 boys and girls, men, women, and PLWDs with the ultimate goal of reducing mortality. All under-five boys and girls receiving health services will benefit from routine screening, immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, medical consultation, treatment through appropriate medications; and when necessary, the care of wounds and other skin diseases. Referrals will be done for chronic and complicated cases requiring specialized medical treatment; a standby ambulance will facilitate the transport of patients in need of specialized/emergency care from the HCs to the nearest referral hospitals e.g. Medina, Benadir and Keysane in Banadir and Garowe hospital in Nugal, etc. Community health workers will sensitize beneficiaries on good health seeking behavior. The CHWs will further be supported to identify boys, girls, men and women in need of health services and refer them to supported facilities. WASH & IPC facilities will be supported in the project target locations to ensure adequate measures IPC measures are in place in supported facilities.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc: ACF will provide medical care within 72 hours for the SGBV cases reported. Through linkage with other health partners and GBV focal points, ACF will refer survivors who need additional support such psychosocial care to the GBV partners. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities, Health facility committees and Village structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them participate in project implementation decisions.

Please provide further explanation

ACF designs its health interventions in line with essential package of health services (EPHS) to ensure that local capacity and government structures such as Ministry of Health are engaged and incorporated in the programme design. ACF will engage community health workers to conduct community sensitization and tracing for various cases at the community for timely referral. Health workers will also be trained to ensure that they have adequate skills needed to ensure quality of care and sustainability of health workforce even after the project end. Through community engagement people affected by conflicts and natural disasters are allowed to identify their potential and needs though community driven actions plans. Subsequently ACF will partner with government and communities in project target locations in the delivery of services and offer offer free comprehensive services.
