Project Summary

Since the beginning of conflict, 1.84 million people remain displaced including around 200,000 individuals in PoCs with the majority, 54% being women and girls. Due to the impacts of conflict, it is estimated that over 6 million will be Crisis/Emergency Levels of food insecurity by March 2019. The 2019 HNO estimates 5,712,000 individuals will not have access to sufficient water and sanitation infrastructure with much of it destroyed as a result of conflict and the government lacking the means for rehabilitation. Unity has been one of the worst affected areas by conflict with limited WASH infrastructures: only 39% of the population in Rubkhona and 34% in Guit have access to boreholes with many of these boreholes in disrepair. Combined with already poor hygiene, health and nutrition practices, this results in high levels of illness and thus malnutrition. Rubkona and Guit Counties in Unity and are likely to be have Critical levels of acute malnutrition (GAM 15-29.9%) by March 2019. In Unity, despite the revitalised peace agreement in August 2018, localised insecurity means that IDPs are likely to remain inside the PoC requiring continuing provision of WASH services integrated with nutrition interventions to reduce morbidity and malnutrition. NBeG continues to have some of the highest rates of morbidity of 157/1,000 in children under-5 driven by poor hygiene practices along with poor nutrition behaviours and food insecurity, which in turn results in the area having one of the highest risks of malnutrition.
The overall aim of the proposed WASH programme is to reduce vulnerability of conflict affected and vulnerable populations, particularly women and children by reducing mortality and morbidity through the operation, repair and maintenance of water supply and sanitation facilities and improving hygiene behaviours. Concern WASH intervention will contribute to the following Cluster HRP objectives to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees and to integrate WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention minimum package of interventions. Concern will continue to be one of the WASH service providers in Bentiu PoC, implementing WASH interventions in Sector 5, and outside the PoC including the BBR mission in Bentiu and Rubkona towns and Guit County. Routine operation, maintenance, repair and rehabilitation of water systems to ensure 15l/per person/per day of safe water are provided in Bentiu PoC and BBR locations. In BBR, Concern will collaborate with the SMoWPI to ensure their skills are improved for maintaining water and sanitation infrastructure. In Bentiu PoC, Concern will construct simple and lockable sex-separated latrines in Bentiu PoC and continue with sanitation services such as rubbish collections. In BBR locations, Concern will construct sanitation infrastructure through an adapted-CTLS approach where communities are in involved in constructing latrines creating greater ownership, reflection of beneficiaries’ needs and acceptance of sanitation practices in preventing water-borne diseases. Hygiene promotion will focus on reducing the risks of diarrhoeal diseases and minimise other public health related risks. In Bentiu PoC, with high levels of youth unemployment, Concern will support female and male youths to develop skills in maintaining water and sanitation infrastructure along with basic literacy, numeracy and business skills as well as protection and equality components. Hygiene promotion in Bentiu PoC will involve house-to-house visits and an approach focusing on mothers to guide and assist children to properly use latrines and proper handwashing; and social events with kids clubs to promote handwashing practices. For BBR interventions, hygiene promotion will focus on piloting a volunteer approach with community volunteers, including those from Nutrition programmes such as MtMSGs and Male Change Agents will be trained on hygiene promotion and used as a community entry point. Hygiene promotion will cover topics including hand washing with soap at critical times; safe water chain; and proper latrine use and maintenance along with linking poor hygiene with malnutrition through loss of water and nutrients due to diarrhoeal incidences.
In NBeG, WASH will be closely linked with health and nutrition interventions. Safe water access remains a challenge with breakdowns of hand pumps and shortage of technicians for repairs risking reducing safe collection of water. Through village Water User Committees, Concern will support the training of hand pump mechanics as well as rehabilitation of water points at health facilities. Sanitation facilities and proper medical waste disposal facilities will be constructed at health facilities as well as continuation of ODF campaigns in communities from previous programmes. Hygiene promoters’ support will be expanded where they are linked to health facilities to integrate hygiene practices to reduce morbidity.
In Yei, there are considerable gaps in meeting WASH needs and Concern aims to partner with a national NGO with strong local knowledge. Activities will be integrated with shelter to ensure provision of WASH services to the IDP population as well as host communities in Yei town. Will Concern will support community volunteers such as Water Management Committees for managing water points, CLTS for latrine construction and HHPs.
Concern will continue to be the WASH State Cluster co-coordinator in Unity coordinating the overall WASH response as well as engaging with the NBeG and Central Equatoria State Clusters and national WASH Cluster.

Needs

Since the beginning of conflict in December 2013, around 1.96 million people in South Sudan are internally displaced with over 203,000 IDPs in PoCs and around 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries (OCHA South Sudan Humanitarian Dashboard 30 September 2018). According to the 2019 HNO estimated 5,712,000 individuals will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019, an increase by 7% from 2018. Conflict has destroyed WASH infrastructure and the government has minimal capacity for rehabilitation. Despite the revitalised peace agreement in August 2018, localised insecurity in Unity means it is likely that PoC population figures of around 112,000 individuals will remain unchanged in 2019 requiring continuation of life-saving humanitarian services. This places considerable strain on already stretched resources of WASH partners. Particularly in Sector 5, which has 29% of the PoC population, provision of services is constrained exposing the population to greater public health risks with water provision below Sphere Standards between 13.7l/per person/per day (CCCM Bentiu PoC Site Profile, February 2018). Congestion also contributes to low latrine coverage of 1:25 with reported cases of observed open defecation. In BBR, although Concern has met Sphere Standards of water with provision above 15l/per person per day, but less than 50% are accessing water through improved sources due lack of access to water containers (Concern KAP survey, July 2018). Latrine coverage remains poor in BBR locations with 72% in Rubkona and 84% in Guit not having access to latrines. Of those that do have access but do not use latrines, (18.9% in Rubkona, 50% in Guit), 50% in Rubkona counties said they do not use them due to lack of privacy and 50% in Guit due to lack of safety (Concern KAP survey, July 2018). While there is generally high knowledge of handwashing practices with 70% knowing at least 3 critical times for handwashing, this does not correlate with high incidents of diarrhoea. Concern’s KAP survey in July 2018 found that few people knew how to demonstrate handwashing properly. NBeG has one of the highest risks of malnutrition driven by high morbidity rates of 157/1,000 in children under-5. GAM and SAM rates in NBeG were at critical levels, in Aweil West, GAM was at 14.6% and SAM at 2.7%, while in Aweil North, GAM was at 18.5% and SAM at 4.8% (Concern SMART survey 2017). Acute malnutrition in both Aweil West and Aweil North is currently classified as Serious (GAM 10-14.9%) (IPC, September 2019). The high rates of morbidity are attributed to poor sanitation and hygiene practices. While significant improvements have been made with 97% and 99% of households in Aweil West and Aweil North respectively having access to safe water, uptake of hygiene and sanitation practices remains very low at community level. Only 33.8% of households in Aweil West and 15.1% in Aweil North have adequate toilet facilities within 50m of the home and only 40.3% and 24.9% in Aweil West and Aweil North respectively reported that their household has a handwashing facility. In Yei, 25,000 individuals remain in need since 2016 following the spread of the conflict with many development-experienced NGOs with limited emergency response capacity in insecure context, particularly delivery of water and sanitation services. 25% of boreholes are reported non-functional in Yei (REACH, WASH Cluster, April 2018). Yei has been declared a hotspot for EVD making adequate WASH response an even more pressing need.

Activities

Activity 1: Clean water supply to reduce morbidity and malnutrition: Lack of clean water results in transmission of waterborne diseases and subsequently malnutrition. Concern will continue to ensure access through operating 2 boreholes in Bentiu PoC Sector 5. As a pilot, Concern will support training of 45 youth in Bentiu PoC, Sector 5 on installation and maintenance of water infrastructures including water supply systems. Concern in collaboration with the SMoWPI will operate and maintain 1 SWAT in Rubkhona town and rehabilitated Bentiu water treatment plant and support maintenance of 29 water points in Bentiu and Rubkona towns and 25 water points in Guit. In Aweil West and Aweil North, 28 hand pump mechanics will be supported with kits and training to conduct repairs and maintenance and Water Management Committees will support water access at Concern’s 49 nutrition sites. In Yei, following a more specific WASH needs assessment, Concern will prioritise water points in locations where communities are receiving other life-saving services, including nutrition, health, SNFI, to ensure comprehensive services and clean living conditions. Concern will aim to ensure water provision priorities where Concern or another partner has Nutrition programmes to contribute to reducing malnutrition. Water points will be located close to communities to reduce travel times and thus, associated protection risks. Further details on minimising GBV and other protection risks are provided in the section on Protection. Activity 2: Improved environmental health and sanitation in communal areas with consideration to reducing associated health, malnutrition, GBV and protection risks: Concern will construction of new latrines and desludging of existing latrines conditions in Bentiu PoC, Sector 5 with all latrines being sex-segregated and lockable. In Rubkona, Guit and Yei the CLTS approach will support communities in latrine construction to consider requirements such as meeting female and male needs with sex-segregated latrines in safe locations with privacy. In Aweil West and Aweil North focus will be on improving sanitation in health facilities. Concern will rehabilitate latrines at CHD health facilities and mobile clinics. Medical waste remains a challenge in Aweil West and Aweil North, with much ending up as surface disposal. Concern will construct sharp pits and train health facility staff on waste management. In Bentiu PoC, Concern will engage with communities and camp leadership for solid waste management in Sector 5 supporting rubbish collections for collection by UNICEF to dispose outside the PoC and complementing with community based clean-up campaigns. In collaboration with mayors’ offices in Bentiu and Rubkona towns and the SMoWPI, plans will be initiated to form business owners’ groups for collection of increased rubbish from recovering markets including bins, disposal pits and waste collection. Activity 3: Improved hygiene practices of conflict affected and vulnerable populations integrated with improving nutrition and health behaviours: Concern will focus hygiene promotion that will be linked with its Nutrition programme in all locations. Hygiene promoters under this project and volunteers from Concern’s Nutrition programme including CNVs and MtMSGs will be supported to train others in improved hygiene behaviours, house-to-house visits and mass campaigns as well as on how hygiene impacts on MIYCN. Topics on menstrual hygiene will be introduced. To encourage early adoption of hygiene behaviours, schools in Rubkona and Guit will be selected to support children through the peer-to-peer approach and work with girls over 12 on menstrual hygiene. With open defecation prevalent among children, Concern will support establishment of kids clubs with social activities and Participatory Education Theatre to promote hygiene behaviours in their communities. Concern will repair handwashing facilities and promote the use of tippy-taps in households.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

At the start of the project, Concern will share information with communities on the project including targeting criteria (targets will be communities as a whole with WASH needs and integrated where shelter and nutrition interventions are located wherever possible). Details on WASH assistance that will be provided will be based on consultations with communities such as where water points will be located and operating hours (that minimise protection risks); location of latrines and design of latrines that reflect social norms; and hygiene promotion activities such as roles of hygiene promoters and entitlements to in-kind assistance such as WASH NFIs. Information will also be provided on the CRM mechanism. The project will provide information using visual tools considering high levels of illiteracy and will use local volunteers such as hygiene promoters, Water Management Committees and those from other programmes such as MtMSGs. Pictorial information on hygiene practices will also be displayed at health facilities and nutrition sites to considering illness as a main cause of malnutrition.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern will target both men, boys, women and girls for its WASH project. Messages will also be specific to women and men considering they have different roles in the household. As nutrition will be integrated with WASH interventions, messages on hygiene practices will be part of MIYCN education with MtMSGs and men’s groups. For hygiene sessions with women and girls, topics on menstrual hygiene will be included. Messages will also be customised focusing on mothers and caregivers on guiding and assisting children to use latrines and practicing proper handwashing. For children, messages will be adapted to social events such as such as football matches between kids clubs will be organised to promote hand washing practices with the champion team being crowned as “Sopo Champion” as well as approaches used in schools such as CHAST.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern considers protection including protection against SEA throughout its programmes. Research on GBV in South Sudan conducted in 2017 found that 65% of women and girls had experience physical or sexual violence in their lifetime. Concern has a Programme Participant Protection Policy, known as the P4, and Code of Conduct along with a Child Safeguarding Policy and Anti-Trafficking in Persons Policy which all staff and those involved in programmes must sign. Concern staff members receive yearly training on the code of conduct and it associated policies. Included in these policies in mandatory reporting of beneficiary sexual exploitation and abuse. The project considers protection risks when planning WASH activities. Water points will be located in consultation with communities to ensure they are in safe locations close to households and water flows are at a rate that minimise queueing times. Water points will only be operational in daylight hours so at risk groups such as women will not travel at night hours were risks of GBV increase. Communities will be consulted on design of latrines and location to ensure safety, privacy and dignity of women and men. Latrines and bathing facilities will be sex-segregated with locks to ensure safety of women from GBV. Approaches such as involving female and male block leaders in the PoC and CTLS outside the PoCs will improve design of latrines and bathing facilities that are specific to women’s and men’s needs. The CRM will be used to identify any protection cases as at WASH facilities and referrals made to Protection partners. Concern will coordinate with the Protection Cluster during planning of WASH interventions.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Concern during its KAP survey consulted communities both with individual key informant interviews of household heads and separate female/male FGDs to gain an understanding of community knowledge and practice of WASH practices, and included nutrition practices considering the links with poor hygiene, illness and malnutrition. Concern regularly engages with communities through FGDs to discuss programme activities, approach and receive feedbacks. Concern conducted an evaluation of its recently completed multisectorial emergency programme, which included a WASH component. Beneficiaries were provided with an opportunity to provide feedback on satisfaction levels of the WASH activities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Within Bentiu PoC, the CRM is managed by IOM. In BBR, Aweil West and Aweil North, Concern has a CRM where beneficiaries are informed on how they can lodge complaints or provide feedback including to a Concern staff, writing or telephone and complaints are followed up by a CRM committee and appropriate actions recommended. In Yei, Concern and/or potential partners will establish a CRM.Community-Concern meetings are also a forum to receive complaints and feedback. In 2018, all WASH partners and CMC in Bentiu PoC have agreed to review the lining materials of the latrine pits for more sustainable options. WASH partners in Bentiu PoC, including Concern, will be piloting different options in 2019 as a result of this feedback.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities will be informed on the importance of M&E as an opportunity for them to provide feedback on the project. This includes satisfaction levels on water and sanitation services, particularly in relation to women and men’s needs such as if privacy and dignity is respected at sanitary facilities. Concern collects much of its monthly monitoring data on hygiene activities from hygiene promoters where they can provide information directly from communities. At the end of the project, Concern will conduct interviews and FGDs with community members to analyse if the project had impacts on reducing illness and malnutrition.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Despite the re-vitalised peace agreement in August 2018, localised insecurity persists in Unity and Yei along with tensions due to overcrowding in Bentiu PoC. This can be a barrier for women and children to access safe water and sanitation facilities impacting on WASH practices putting them at risk of illness and malnutrition. Concern implements robust security management plan and protocols to monitor the situation in project locations and to ensure safety of staff members and beneficiaries. Concern mobile response is adapted to highly insecure context as it allows to follow population movements and shift the assistance to safer locations where necessary. Another barrier is socio-economic status where poorer households lack the means to purchase containers to collect water from water points, Concern’s KAP survey in July 2018 identified lack of water containers as a barrier to using water points for over 50% of households in Rubkona and Guit, which can lead to contamination of clean water supply chains.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Concern will consult with beneficiaries (male and female, including youth), female and male block representatives in Bentiu PoC and community leaders in BBR locations, Yei, Aweil West and Aweil North. This will include siting water points and sanitation facilities both in designs that reflect female and male needs as well as disabled and elderly needs and to ensure safety, privacy and dignity. This can include locating water points in central and safe locations, sex-segregated latrines and latrines with locks. Water points will be open only during daylight hours to minimise risks of GBV and protection incidents. Construction of sanitation facilities will be done with iron sheets instead of plastic sheeting to provide privacy when in use and in walking distance to reduce women’s exposure to physical or sexual assault. Privacy will enhance greater dignity for both men, boys, women and girls.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will collect monthly gender disaggregated data on WASH interventions to identify how female and male community members access water and sanitation facilities and participation in hygiene activities. KAP surveys will involve interviews and FGDs with women and men to understand their knowledge and adoption of WASH practices. SMART surveys in Concern’s Nutrition Programme will also collect information from caregivers on how access to WASH facilities and hygiene practices impact on their and their children’s nutrition.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Concern’s monitoring tools will collect information on issues such as safety and security can impact on use of water and sanitation facilities. Regular consultations with groups such as community committees, Water Management Committees and hygiene promoters will also identify any safety and security issues. The KAP survey which measures impacts of the programme on WASH practices includes questions on whether safety concerns impact on using water points or latrines. Beneficiaries will also be sensitised to use the CRM as a way of providing feedback on safety concerns.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Concern during monitoring will collect information on how activities such as latrine and bathing shelter construction impacts on dignity of beneficiaries. The CRM will also be used to collect beneficiary complaints or feedback.
