Project Summary

The planned action is in line with the strategic and cluster objectives SO2, SO3 and cluster objective 1 CO1 and CO2 respectively. Recent needs assessments and Focus Group Discussions with all local municipalities in the Access Restricted Area (ARA) informed PUI about the urgent needs for shelter interventions due to recurrent natural hazards notably flooding, the severe deterioration in the socioeconomic situation, and the Gaza protracted crisis in general.
Therefore, PUI will prioritize its intervention to protect households who are at risks of floods through urgent winterization assistance and urgent climate protection support taking into consideration the specific needs of the affected population (i.e. women, girls, boys, people with disabilities etc.). At emerging needs level, PUI will focus the intervention to target households who were evicted from their homes or at risk of eviction through temporary shelter assistance with focus on most vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, widows, divorced etc. PUI will also target households with limited economic means who live in inadequate shelter which do not have the minimum required shelter standards through the rehabilitation of their houses.
Protection threats combined with the absence of adequate shelters are particular hard on the most vulnerable groups such as women, elderly, children and PwDs, which the project will take into consideration by including different groups in all stages of the project. Especially due to an often sharp gendered division in housework, women’s participation in all phases of the programming will be essential as well as the focus on special needs of particular groups (e.g. the household’s different needs of space for privacy and socializing to ensure children’s possibilities of finding space for studying for instance).
PUI planned to provide targeted beneficiaries with Housing, Land & Property (HLP) awareness sessions in collaboration with PCHR assistance. The proposed action is part of a multi-year program. Over two years, the program will be implemented with the same activities, but including new beneficiaries and possibly new implementation areas. PUI coordinated the planned action with the Shelter Cluster and key actors working in the area of intervention.

Needs

Based on the needs assessment conducted by PUI in 14 municipalities located in the Access Restricted Areas (ARAs) in September 2018, flooding of houses covered by asbestos and metal sheet was one of main challenges faced by the population living in these communities during the winter season. In addition, spread of insects and rodents was cited by the respondents as one of the problems faced during summer season, which may affect the health of the family members, especially children adding another layer of economic pressure to the parents.In September 2018, PUI conducted Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) with a number of households who were evicted or at high risk of eviction from rented houses due to the deteriorated socioeconomic situation in Gaza Strip. 49 evicted households from 5 localities (Abbassan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Al Fukhari, Wadi as Salqa, and Al Shouka) participated in the discussions. The main common challenges were reported as follows: unsuitable living conditions in the temporary shelter (i.e. no privacy, overcrowding, low level of hygiene); households felt as an extra burden for the host families; and increased level of domestic violence and tensions inside the families, leading to divorce in some cases. According to the families, the most urgent needs at this stage are quick cash assistance to pay off the accumulated debts/rental fees to the landlord; provision of cash assistance; temporary job opportunities; food parcels, and NFI. In addition, PUI carried out meetings with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) to explore the most relevant response to support this emerging vulnerable group and to obtain official statistics. A list of 160 households evicted or at risk of eviction was shared by MoPWH and showed that more than 2,000 households were at high risk of eviction according to the Ministry’s estimations. Moreover, PUI carried out a needs assessment of 650 substandard housing units in Wadi as Salqa, Abbassan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Al Fukhari, Al Shouka, Bani Suhaila, Auraiba, and Gizan Abu Rashwan during April and September 2018. The main findings emphasized the need for urgent winterization repair interventions where 93% of the assessed shelters were affected by floods during the last winter storms. In addition, 98% of the housing units are very vulnerable to floods and other extreme winter condition as the worn-out roofs, windows, and/or doors allow the leakage of rain water into houses according to the technical assessment. Moreover, 58% of the households indicated that their houses are hot in summer and very cold in winter. 61% of the houses do not have solid lockable doors into toilet/shower. 69% of the houses do not have at least one room/bedroom with solid lockable door. 57% of the housing units are overcrowded (i.e. the covered space per person is below the minimum standard (5.5 m2) determined by the shelter cluster). 80% of the houses do not have toilet with flushing system, nor marks of leakage, with openable window or a mechanical ventilation system.The first priority intervention for most of the households is to be protected against exposure to extreme winter conditions in both winter and summer. Main problems faced by women are the lack of privacy and the small size of kitchens. For children, problems of overcrowding and light affect their preparations for studies. Finally, all visited households lacked essential NFIs and are in need of immediate winterization repairs and upgrading of their houses. During 2018, PUI upgraded 115 substandard housing units in the Southern Governorates through different funding sources. PUI collaborated with NRC and PCHR on providing targeted beneficiaries with awareness sessions on HLP. The pre and post tests showed a concrete need for better understanding of land tenure issues and legislative and customary rights.

Activities

PUI will give priority to urgent needs such as plastic sheets, NFIs, winter clothes, winterization repairs, etc. for households affected by harsh weather and those under protection threats, particularly for evicted individuals or at risk of eviction. The priority will be given to women, female-headed households, and families with main breadwinner sick or with disability. PUI will take into account women, girls, boys, and men’s specific needs in the rehabilitation process, take into consideration the specific needs of People with Disabilities (PwD) and consult with all targeted beneficiaries about their preferences. PUI will coordinate its intervention with all key actors: ACTED, CRS, MAAN development center, NRC, and others. PUI will carry out the following activities to preserve the dignity and safety of the targeted beneficiaries and contribute to the protection of affected population:Activity 1: Provision of temporary shelter assistance to 50 evicted or at risk of eviction due to deteriorated socioeconomic situation households (280 individuals). ($50,000)Activity 2: Provision of urgent winterization assistance to 150 households (840 individuals) (i.e. plastic sheets, mattresses, mats, etc.). ($ 30,000)Activity 3: Provision of urgent climate protection support (repair works for roofs, windows and external doors) for 150 vulnerable households (840 individuals), who are the same households targeted through the activity 2 at risk of being affected by floods and extreme winter conditions. ($150,000)Activity 4: Provision of assistance to 50 acutely vulnerable households to rehabilitate standard housing units for (households will be selected among those who will benefit from the climate protection interventions) during 2019. The same number will be targeted in 2020. This activity will be implemented through the owner-driven (self-help) approach. Special care will be given to groups with specific needs in regards to their use of the shelters both for privacy, socializing and work. ($125,000)Activity 5: awareness session on HLP PUI will target all its beneficiaries (300 individuals including women) through awareness sessions. In case of needs for legal assistance, PUI will refer the cases to other partners.PUI's implementing main partner will be responsible for the selection and follow up of civil engineers, participation in the beneficiary selection process, report on progress of activities, follow up feedback mechanism, update PUI’s on the challenges faced and lessons learnt during the implementation of activities and effectively contribute in M&E processes. PUI ensures participatory approach at design phase, during implementation and the evaluation of its action. This contributes to ensuring that the action preserves the safety and dignity of the target beneficiaries. The action will be inclusive, as much as possible, in terms of age, gender, and diversity, and activities will take into consideration the specific needs of particular groups, whenever possible. The action is designed to assist the most acutely vulnerable households in the area of intervention through providing them urgent shelter assistance, winterization assistance, winterization repairs and with adequate shelters that meet shelter standards defined by the shelter cluster and ensuring access to a dignified life. This will definitely help them better cope with the psychological, social, and economic hardship and meet beneficiaries protection concerns such as harmful coping mechanism, harsh weather conditions, privacy and overcrowding. PUI’s feedback and complaint system will also contribute to the protection of beneficiaries, providing them a venue to freely express their concerns about the project.

Indicators

PUI M&E team will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels between the organisations (PUI and local partners). PUI will ensure regular updates on FTS and 4Ws systems. PUI will establish selection criteria based on the different vulnerabilities. Selection criteria will also include the ownership of the houses with official documents. In case PUI faces specific cases with ownership disputes, it will seek collaboration with other key legal actors such as NRC for legal aid and the Protection Cluster for protection threats.Introductory meetings with affected population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on procedures, types of project activities, wages, beneficiary selection process and criteria and any other relevant information. Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. PUI will consult with targeted beneficiaries during the implementation of the action to adapt the upgrade works to the needs of the beneficiaries. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit. A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up, in collaboration with the local partner and CBOs, to make sure that the opinions of affected population are heard and taken into consideration. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Individual written response letters are sent to all of those who submit complaint and feedback forms. Moreover, the phone number of PUI’s office is available to all affected communities and their representatives so that they can communicate any complaints, objections, and feedback. Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results. Indicators: Number of individuals who received Temporary Shelter support (Target value: 280). Number of households provided with climate protection assistance (emergency shelter or household NFIs in-kind or in cash) (Target value: 150). Number of households assisted to rehabilitate substandard housing unit (Target value: 50).
