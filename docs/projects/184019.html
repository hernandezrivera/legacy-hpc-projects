Project Summary

As enshrined in the Juba Peace Agreement (JPA), the transitional government will apply all possible efforts to ensure Provision of Durable Solutions for displaced people to their areas of origin or choice. Large numbers returnees are intending to re-establish their lives starting with building houses that was destroyed by the long conflict and years of negligence while IDPs intending to integrate locally are also planning to upgrade their temporary shelters into more durable shelters that can endure natural elements such as floods, windstorms etc. and provide minimum needs for longer stay. Under this scenario, there will be a crucial need to assist returnees and IDPs with durable shelter options to contribute to making their return or integration a success and reduce their dependency on humanitarian aid while they gradually make it into self- sufficiency. During the rest of 2021 and early 2022, surveys, market assessment, and needs assessments will be conducted to provide better analysis of the shelter requirements of crisis affected individuals and households with equal participation of both male and female participants to ensure a greater representation of women in both planning and implementation. IOM and UNDP will prioritize rehabilitation of shelters and provision of durable shelters for the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. There are approximately 3 million IDPs and 1.2 million returnees in Sudan in need of assistance. It’s of a paramount importance to assist this neglected portion of community to restore their household shelter to be able to resume productivity and reclaim their livelihoods. Two consecutive floods during 2020 and 2021 left large number of the flood affected population still living in temporary shelters or scrambled with relatives in no conducive environment that can't allow for social distancing putting them in real danger of catching diseases including COVID-19.
UNDP and IOM will expand the provision of shelter assistance and skills building training packages including training on durable and local construction techniques and environmental awareness messaging to mitigate environmental hazard. The provision of durable shelters with resources bought from local markets to support host communities will be encouraged
These durable shelters will be built on durable foundations using blocks, the walls will be build using locally made SSB with metal structures for the window-frames and door frames to enhance longevity and provide resistance against external elements and natural disasters. This activity will also support capacity development at the local level in construction techniques adopting a Training of Trainers (ToT) approach to enable the target beneficiaries trained in construction techniques to assist others in constructing shelters, the selected beneficiaries could possibly generate a household income. Based on previous experiences constructing model houses, the cash for work modality will be utilized to assist communities in constructing their own housing while injecting cash into the local economy and provide livelihoods. Cultural and regional dimensions will be considered, and measures will be put in-place when involving women in the construction processes to mitigate Gender Based Violence (GBV) and other protection concerns.

1 - Needs

According to Sudan National Resilience and Recovery Framework, the national housing deficit is estimated at 4.3 million units, with urban poor rely on their own resources to construct their shelters, resulting in a proliferation of poor housing, often illegal, based on varying degrees of self-help by the inhabitants. This situation is critically true for prolonged displacement settlements. Aiming at providing solutions for longstanding temporary settlements and the associated socio-economic, human security and land-use planning issues; Durable Shelters came as an entry point for a full range of durable solutions for displaced population for their return, relocation, and local integration. There are approximately 2.87 million IDP individuals and 0.94 million returnees in Sudan in need of assistance according to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022. With half a million newly displaced in 2021 (due to conflict only) and growing insecurities the number of displacements has increased sharply by 55 per cent. According to Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Mobility Tracking Round Two Report, in terms of population presence, the state with the highest number of IDPs is North Darfur – having recorded an estimated 933,320 individuals (31 per cent of the total IDP count) – followed by South Darfur with 797,081 IDPs (26 per cent). Comparatively, Blue Nile is estimated to have the lowest population presence with 81,693 IDPs (3 per cent), followed by West Kordofan with 91,343 IDPs (3 per cent). North Darfur has the greatest number of IDP locations – consisting of 270 sites (31 per cent of the total), followed by South Kordofan (29 per cent) and West Kordofan (19 per cent). East Darfur has the fewest number of IDP locations and represents just one per cent of the total location count, followed by Blue Nile (2 per cent) and West Darfur (5 per cent). It is evident that IDPs are most highly concentrated in the Darfur states, with Central Darfur hosting on average 13,215 IDPs per location, East Darfur hosting 12,462 IDPs per location and South Darfur hosting 11,071 IDPs per location. In contrast, South and West Kordofan have a far sparser distribution – averaging 1,108 IDPs and 560 IDPs respectively per location. This dynamic is likely to the protracted nature of displacement ongoing since 2003, as well as the high presence of densely populated camps in Darfur in comparison to more informal gathering-sites in South and West Kordofan. Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) 2021 analysis suggests that the most common shelters where IDPs and host communities live across Sudan is Qutiyah (reported by 35 per cent of households), followed by permanent/finished shelters (reported by 29 per cent), Kurnuk (21 per cent), unfinished/non-enclosed shelters (13 per cent) and tent (1 per cent). Additionally, 27 per cent of assessed households (including both IDPs and host communities) reported shelter as a current priority need. Despite all the massive needs, the government continues to struggle with the formalization of land tenure and titles. Through this intervention all durable shelters construction will support undisputed access to housing land in close coordination with Housing, land and Properties (HLP) sub sector to ensure principles of “Do No harm” and enable better peaceful co-existence among communities’ components.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In an effort to support the transition to recovery and support the Nexus approach from dependency to self-reliance, and as per the needs identified and suggested interventions , UNDP/IOM will provide crisis affected communities with support to construct or repair Durable Shelter and cash for work to provide income generating and livelihood activities. Based on finding from the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA), shelter needs - particularly roofing and shelters walls - are a major gap. Improving construction technique and methodologies will be prioritized. Activities:Activity 1: Conduct markets / needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable returnees and flood-affected within each locality. During the assessment and project design process, consultation with the community including gender sensitive groupings, will be conducted to facilitate input from underrepresented groups where they may otherwise be reluctant to speak by having focus group discussions. That will include conducting shelter survey to confirm/identify shelter designs which are in line with the sector standards / modules that will be both culturally acceptable and climatically appropriate (with durable structural design to withstand natural hazards such as floods) for each locality with the intention of constructing approximately 6,000 shelters targeting approximately 30,000 beneficiaries. Following assessments, GBV risk mitigation measures will be integrated in the overall shelter design, location, and trainings.Activity 2: Community sessions on durable shelter construction and community work with vulnerable households and community leaders to ensure no or minimal conflicts over shelter land allocations in coordination with local authorities and land arbitration/review committees. Ensuring that committees are composed of at least 50% females’ participants from returnee and host communities. The land arbitration/review committees will conduct risk assessments to ensure that durable shelters are built on conflict-free plots the overall security in the area is ensured with ministry of planning to ensure proper settlement/site planning. The targeted locations for the durable shelters will be selected in coordination with other sectors based on availability of services like water, sanitation, health, schools, etc. A corner stone will be ensuring gender equality and rights of minority groups during engagement in shelter construction and distribution.Activity 3: Training of community members with focus on youth and women on basic construction and rehabilitation techniques as a skill set to assist in income generating and digging and other hand works targeting protracted IDP and returnee communities, community-based organizations (CBOs) and local partners to ensure structures are capable of withstanding natural hazards such as heavy rains, strong winds, dust storms (haboobs) etc. The safe construction training includes compressed stabilized soil blocks (SSB) technique, SSB machine usage, roof fixing through the production of fortified grass mats (or other locally identified options), proper drainage system around structures, as well as ensuring proper spaces between shelters. Activity 4: Distribution of durable shelter using stabilized blocks to protracted vulnerable households in the return Villages and to the most vulnerable households in floods affected communities. The target household will also receive trainings and incentives through cash for work to construct their own durable shelters.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will promote the use of the environment friendly building techniques like Stabilized Blocks at the expense of the currently used Burnet Red Bricks that has a high negative environmental impact. The project will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will follow applicable international requirements. the project will procure and purchase locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials such as SSB and grass mats for the construction of durable shelters, when materials are available, to ensure that environmental impact is limited. The shelter construction will include proper drainage systems around structures to reduce flood related risks and ensure safe distance between shelter structures to reduce fire related risks and damage to the surrounding environment.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

he project will ensure protection principles are adhered to including ensure access to impartial assistance, Protection from violence, and access to remedies and recovery, ensure safety and dignity for the beneficiaries are employed during the planning, and implementation phases. It will also ensure leaving behind community and locality level structures that can monitor and ensure protection of vulnerable people and protection from abuse while linking with locality and state protection and Rule of Law level mechanisms to ensure rights based approach is strictly followed. One important component of protection is making sure that there is a conflict management system in-place to handle smaller flares or conflict triggers related to shelter, land allocation and prevent that from escalation into ethnic or community level conflicts that produces protection concerns. Do-No-Harm principles will always be put forward during all stages of project planning and implementation All protection concerns are to be reviewed for all participatory institutions and beneficiaries, ensuring that rights of all beneficiaries are respected and promoted, A comprehensive review of any potential discriminatory approaches, including gender based discrimination will not be tolerated. UNDP is integrating the PSEA principles agreed by the PSEA network. IOM will ensure to prioritize (1) safety and security issues ensuring that shelters are built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues (ensure the land is free of claim) are discussed between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed. (2) Ensure that all community members including boys, girls, men and women and persons with special needs are assessed and those who are found to be most vulnerable are prioritized to receive durable shelters, as well as equitable access to construction trainings and skills development. (3) Through the inclusion of women in the trainings and shelter design, structures will be gender sensitive to accommodate additional gender-based vulnerabilities and protection risks. Locks will be fitted on the doors to increase security.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

All UNDP/IOM staff are carefully trained in codes of conduct and PSEA, prior to any engagement with affected communities. Trainings will be refreshed on a frequent basis. The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase - registration of returnees, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. UNDP/IOM will consult with local authorities and community members including vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities, older persons and women and girls on the design and locations for transitional shelters. People with disabilities and special needs will receive additional support for construction of their shelters and have priority. UNDP/IOM will ensure that there is a community-based feedback mechanism to take into account community complaints and suggestions and ensure that community members participate in the training and have ownership throughout the process. In addition, IOM has developed a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) format that will track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will include interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on quality and appropriateness of support provided. The system has also been developed to assess the short-term effects of transitional shelters in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum during the ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (household interviews, Focus Group Discussion and observation), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and handover procedures. Methodologies used will include a desk review before the field exercises are conducted.
