Project Summary

Decades of prolonged conﬂict and insecurity, drought and massive displacement in Galmuudug state of Federal Somalia in Mudug region have placed women and girls in extreme vulnerability and at increased risk of gender-based violence (GBV) ,during the 2019 dyer rains Mudug region was one of the most affected, with very high levels of displacement and food insecurity. While the humanitarian response has stabilized ﬂoods and famine affected populations, perpetual external shocks limit the population’s access to basic services causing ﬂuctuating levels of vulnerability. As a result women and children are forced to adopt risky and sometimes harmful coping strategies, including collection and sale of ﬁrewood and payment of debt through forced marriage. Pressure for survival intensiﬁes the potential for intimate partner violence. Furthermore, the modalities of aid assistance have sometimes exacerbated vulnerability and risk of exploitation. WISE is proposing the following interventions to lifesaving GBV and protection services to at risk population in Galmudug state of Somalia. The proposed interventions are designed to increase women and adolescent girls access to life saving GBV case management psychosocial support services and protection to reduce risks of GBV , integrate protection monitoring and mainstreaming into the GBV intervention to improve the overall context analysis and integration of protection principles in the intervention that will be scaled up to respond the identiﬁed needs. WISE will utilise existing internal response capacity to staff these teams to provide timely, effective and eﬃcient GBV and protection response during the project. This will provide much needed humanitarian services in Galmuudug state to areas without GBV and protection services

Project Activities

Provision of life saving material assistance (dignity kits, and transport cost) to GBV survivors based on individual assessments by GBV case workers. Foster families will be on standby in case an emergency safe shelter is needed to secure the protection of women and girls GBV survivors. • Conduct 16 three-day GBV mainstreaming trainings (20 persons per workshop). Target participants are humanitarian actors from WASH, Education, Health, Shelter, Food security clusters and Return programme as well as informal and formal authorities (Police, religious leaders, clan leaders, village leaders and elders) in coordination with the Protection Cluster and the GBV WGs of the 3 priority areas location. • Provide legal aid assistance for 500 women and girls in Mudug and Galgaduud regions, provide Transport facilitation cost/allowance cost for GBV survivors to link services • Establish safe spaces to provide life saving, age appropriate GBV case management and PSS services to women and girls to 1,500 women and girls • Establish volunteer reporters and GBV focal points as well as trained GBV case workers in Mudug and Galgaduud regions (25 IDPs camps, 15 host community volunteers) as frontline provided mobiles and airtime • Conduct community outreach targeting 5,000 community members to raise awareness on services and facilitate to GBV response services • Information campaigns on international human rights days such as 16 days of Activism campaigns in 16 selected locations targeting 3000 individuals • Provide psychosocial support to GBV survivors using the appropriate case management tools, make appropriate referrals and document using GBV Information Management Systems (GBVIMS) • Composing and presenting road side drama on issues affecting women and girls for positive sensitization in 15 major public gathering events targeting 3000 persons • SMS awareness raising messages through Hormuud and Golis Telecom subscribers in South Mudug and Galgaduud, approximately 10,000.

Project Indicators

The prolonged conﬂict and insecurity-affected displacements worsens existing displacement due to famine, ﬂoods due dyer rains and violence which has displaced over 6,000 people by July 2018.Sexual violence and other forms of GBV against women and girls are becoming more widespread in Galmuudug state due to continued conﬂict and population movement. Since early 2017 through WISE’s existing programming, and in line with protection cluster ﬁndings, WISE has noted increasing and targeted attacks against women and girls with growing levels of sexual violence perpetrated. These are linked to the escalating insecurity, militarization of political leadership and mobilization of armed groups. Girls are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. Based on the programming experiences in Galckacyo, Dhasamareb and Adaado the majority of girls are out of school, experience growing levels of sexual violence abductions, sexual harassment, early and forced marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse and have restrictions placed on their movement by their families. 111 GBV cases were reported by women and children over the last 10 months (GBVIMS, December 2017-August 2018); 39% were rape and 48% were physical/domestic violence. Over the last year WISE has been building capacity in case management and provision of support to rape survivors However, due to post-rape kits pipeline gaps no rape survivors were provided with post rape treatment, there are currently no safe shelters or material support for survivors to access services. It is necessary to improve the quality of care by; reception of GBV cases, referral services and material assistance and safe shelter support. Communities, authorities and humanitarian actors lack awareness of GBV risks and consequences and have limited practical involvement for GBV prevention and response.

Please provide further explanation

The volatile security situation and limited humanitarian access to some locations is a serious risk. WISE will develop preparedness and contingency plans to deal withﬂuctuating security and replicate successful approaches other locations in Galmuudug. WISE will develop contingency plans to ensure minimum services remainin place, including recruiting and training staff from the locality that can maintain services in the event of limited access or evacuation of international staff withsupport provided remotely. As part of WISE’s contingency plans, key triggers will be identiﬁed for a variety of likely scenarios in all locations. Security ManagementPlans are in place in all locations and are regularly reviewed and updated based on emerging threats and changing security. Unity State remains highly unpredictable, WISE is well positioned to provide services in Adaado as oﬃces were recently set up and the area is relatively safe
