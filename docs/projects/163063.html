Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in the English speaking Cameroon has fast deteriorated since 2018, with 1.3 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. Insecurity has forced more than 430,000 people to flee their homes. More than 380,000 people need shelter, and some 418,000 people in need of food and non-food assistance. An estimated 3,000 children are suffering from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition requiring urgent treatment. As earlier mentioned, subsistent farming which has been the means of survival in the past has been greatly affected as the displacement and violence increases hindering farming and even food harvesting. Despite the acknowledged importance of addressing these needs, little is presently being done.
The project aim at enhancing sustainable livelihood among vulnerable IDPs and Host communities in urban and peri-urban areas in targeted divisions from the NW, SW, and West regions of Cameroon through capacity building and support with start-up materials on market gardening, poultry farming and small business/trade . Many IDP households and families in the areas are increasing being led by women as most men have been killed and many others recruited into the Non State Arm groups. This has made the situation more difficult for the women to provide basic need of food and shelter to their displace and/or affected families. For this reason the primary target for this project is 2500 women led household leaders in these 5 targeted divisions (500 beneficiaries from each division)
Upon completion, the project is expected to increase family income, and also restores livelihoods of 2500 vulnerable women led households’ in the targeted divisions through capacity building and support with start-up materials for market gardening, poultry farming and small trading. The project will also provide psychosocial support to those who have been traumatized by the crisis.

Besoins / Needs

The Anglophone crisis that started since 2016 in the two English speaking regions of Cameroon escalated into an armed conflict and insecurity in 2017 and has rendered many people displaced from their localities. The increasing tension and hostilities has promoted insecurity and significant internal displacements. While humanitarian needs, joblessness, widowhood, crime and scarcity of food are on the increase, most farmers have fled from the villages into bushes, forest and a large number made it to nearby cities especially Bamenda. Consequently many have become vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. Many IDP households and families in Bamenda are increasing being led by women as most men have been killed and many others recruited into the Non State Arm groups. This has made the situation more difficult for the women to provide basic need of food and shelter to their families.According to OCHA on 1st of January 2019 about 3000 children are suffering from severe acute malnutrition. Malnutrition is a critical risk in the NW region and good nutrition and access to adequate food remains a challenge to child survival especially in the context of the current sociopolitical crises. Reports of assessment by World Food Program (WFP) indicate that 900,000 people in the Northwest region are food insecure. In this population 13% of IDPs and 10 % of non IDPs are severely food insecure. 36.6% of IDPs and 29.9% of NIDPs are moderately food insecure while 38.2% of IDPs and 29.5% NIDPs are marginally food insecure. The continuous aggravating situation and sustained prevalence of critical Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) levels (2.8%) in the Northwest region suggests that general food assistance is needed to treat children with moderate acute malnutrition and prevent malnutrition. This could be as a result of the fact that subsistence farming which was basically the means of survival has been greatly affected due to farmers not having access to farms, unable to harvest their food and even continue farming or transport food to market. A report by the Famine Early Warning System Network as reported by the US agency for International development asserts that, insecurity, displacement, a decline in crop production and subsequently strained livelihoods will likely cause vulnerable households in the Northwest regions to experience crisis levels of food insecurity through May 2019. There is therefore a need for general livelihood assistance in the Northwest, and Southwest region. the project activities include:-carrying out a need assessment to identify 2500 vulnerable beneficiaries from the five targeted divisions from three regions of Cameroon, and constitution of a beneficiaries list-organisation of capacity building training workshop on market gardening, poultry farming, and small trading management and marketing for 4 days for 2500 beneficiaries-purchase and support of 2500 trained beneficiaries with start-up materials for market gardening, poultry farming and small trading.-Coaching of beneficiaries to ensure effective activities implementation and success in their livelihood activities-Monitoring and evaluationit is expected that by the end of the project the livelihood of 2500 women head of households will be restored and they live a self-sustainable live with their family members.
