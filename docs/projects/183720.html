Global Communities in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association and Bayader for Environment and Development Association will provide assistance to the unserved and the underserved Housholds the North, Middle, and South of Gaza Strip. 1 - Needs

Mounting challenges in water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in the Gaza Strip pose dire risks to the human health, environmental health and livelihoods of nearly two million residents. The Gaza Strip is WASH fragile, and an estimated one in 10 households is not connected to the piped water network. For homes in Gaza with a water connection, the Coastal Aquifer is the main source of piped water for domestic use. Over abstraction, caused in large part by increased demand due to rapid population growth, has depleted the aquifer at four times its natural recharge rate, leading to seawater intrusion and increasing the salinity of the piped water supply. Approximately 95 percent of Gaza’s piped water is unfit for human consumption due to salinity alone, and more than one-third of households reported that their piped water quality deteriorated between 2013 and 2017. Gaps in household connections and low water quality mean nearly all households in Gaza purchase treated water from private vendors; it can be up to 50 times more expensive than piped water and can take up to one-third of household income, well over the UN recommended maximum of 3 percent. Approximately one in four households in Gaza is not connected to the wastewater network. Access varies drastically by location, and the majority of unconnected households are outside of Gaza’s five largest cities. Households without connections rely on Gaza’s 40,000 cesspits and septic tanks, which are often near homes. Where connections do exist, aging infrastructure is in dire need of maintenance, repair and renovations. As a result, untreated sewage leaks into and pollutes the groundwater, contaminating the fragile aquifer, and aging treatment plants struggle to handle the volume of sewage they receive. Chronic power shortages further limit the regular operation of treatment plants, leading to the improper disposal of poorly treated wastewater that contaminates the coastline and infiltrates the aquifer. Ongoing exposure to the chemical and biological contaminants in poorly treated or untreated sewage poses serious health risks to Gaza’s residents. Nearly one-quarter of known cases of illness in Gaza are water-related, and water-related diseases are the most common cause of morbidity in children. Improper and insufficient stormwater drainage systems compound Gaza’s WASH challenges. From September to April each year, the 560,000 residents in 60 communities located in Gaza’s low-lying areas are at high risk of flooding due to increased winter rainfall and large storm events, with the worst flooding occurring in rural areas. More than 93,000 people are under the risk of flooding. Floods displace thousands of people and destroy homes and assets; they also cause cesspits to overflow, contaminating streets and homes with raw sewage. Import restrictions continue to delay the implementation of critical projects designed to reduce the risk of flooding during large storm events and mitigate the impact of floods when they occur. Bayader and Beit Lahia were selected because of their track records of successfully implementing similar programs within targeted neighborhoods. We assessed their capacity and together structured their scopes of work to leverage and build on their current capacities. Global Communities will conduct a competitive process for contracts to assess applicants’ abilities to achieve results cost-effectively and efficiently. Payment disbursements will be incremental and tied to deliverables. Bayader currently has 41 programmatic staff, the majority of whom have over 5 years of experience with the organization’s diverse programming spanning emergency response and early recovery, community development and civic participation, child protection and education, and economic development. Recent work relevant to this project includes identifying target neighborhoods for project activities to connect households in Khan Younis governorate to water and wastewater networks, mobilizing participants for awareness sessions on hygiene, and preparing BOQs and tender documents for WASH and sewage rehabilitation projects. Beit Lahia was established in 1994 to support the sustainable development of North Gaza Governorate. They increase community participation in their work, which spans WASH, infrastructure, agriculture, environment, education, culture, and emergency relief. Beit Lahia has partnered with international NGOs and works with 33 committees and 240 volunteers across North Gaza. Recent WASH work includes supporting vulnerable communities in ARAs, installing main and sub-main pipes for water and drainage networks, WASH-related rehabilitation in schools, rehabilitating kitchens and bathrooms to improve water and sanitation facilities for vulnerable households, and installing water tanks and water pumps. Their activities are frequently accompanied by WASH educational workshops and training for householdmembers and school students.

2 - Activities

To address these critical WASH needs, Global Communities in partnership with Beit Lahia Development Association and Bayader for Environment and Development Association in coordination with the WASH Cluster, PWA, CMWU, MoSD and MoPHW will support 1,300 HHs vulnerable households to rehabilitate the WASH facilities that are damaged. The selected interventions and locations are complementary activities that will align with Global Communities Gaza Household WASH project funded by USAID to assist vulnerable families in the Gaza strip. We selected the proposed locations using a two-stage approach to understand vulnerability and identify needs. Global Communities first interviewed CMWU engineers from the five governorates to determine highly vulnerable neighborhoods based on: 1) numbers of households without water connections; 2) numbers of households without sewer connections; 3) location within flood-prone areas; 4) location within ARAs; 5) prevalence of WASH-related morbidities such as diarrhea and skin diseases; 6) environmental health vulnerability, characterized mainly by sanitary conditions; and 7) livelihood vulnerability, characterized by low economic means. Our criteria yielded 61 neighborhoods in the 16 municipalities. With the support of the WASH Cluster coordinators, we have cross-checked our identified neighborhoods with the neighborhoods presented in the WASH Vulnerability Map -2021. Based on the cross-check, we have selected the locations presented in Global Communities HRP 2022. Global Communities' assessments have been reviewed with the WASH Cluster coordinators to ensure the selected governorate and localities including targeted interventions under the HRP complement our capacities and activities. Hence, Global Communities under the HRP will work on rehabilitating four localities in North Gaza and one locality in Gaza. Global Communities will aim to support 1,300 HHs by providing cash support "cash over the counter" to rehabilitate their damaged WASH Facilities. Based on Global Communities' technical assessments, the BoQs for targetted HHs will not exceed $1,000. Targeted Locations: Al-Shiyma', Al-Salatin, Al-Amal, Wadi Gaza including Juhor Ad Dik, Um Al-Nasser and Al-Shokeh In addition to HH WASH rehabilitation, Global Communities and its partners will work on constructing or/ and rehabilitating stormwater network and rainwater damage systems supporting more than 7,000 vulnerable HHs in one locality in Gaza, two localities in North Gaza, and one locality in Khan Younis. The intervention will include but is not be limited to; installing new storm drains installed in the flood-prone areas to direct the surface runoff into the drainage line, stormwater drains, and gullies to collect and transport rainwater to the nearest box culvert disposal point, etc. Targeted locations: Wadi Gaza including Juhor Ad Dik, Um-Al-Nasser, Bani Suhaila inclduing Al-Zanah, Al-Shaimaa and Al-Hatabia. Global Communities will continue its engagement with the CMWU and the PWA to ensure the interventions are aligned with their strategic plans and will sign MoUs with both parties identifying their roles and responsibilities during project implementation. In addition, Global Communities will engage with local CBOs, NGOs, universities, and private sector entities in program implementation where feasible and relevant. Local partners Bayader and Beit Lahia will ensure that stakeholders from local CBOs and NGOs – particularly those led by or representing women or PWDs – are represented in the project implementation.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Prioritizing safety and dignity for men, women, boys, and girls, and avoiding harm by preventing and/or minimizing unintended effects of the program to both physical and psycho-social risks is a critical foundation for all programming that is guided by a protection perspective. In order to operationalize this, the four basic Protection Principles will inform this project, specifically: a) Do No Harm; b) Meaningful Access; c) Accountability to Affected Populations; and d) Participation & Empowerment. As stated above, and consistent with Global Communities' standard approach to humanitarian programming, the project is designed using the Do No Harm framework, promoting the dignity, agency, and safety of beneficiaries at all times. Program activities are designed to be culturally appropriate and tailored to the specific context while preventing competition over resources. We will take great care to broadly disseminate information about the opportunities that the project presents. The program will guarantee access through the consistent engagement of community members to identify those who are most in need. Conclusively, we will work with Beit Lahia Development Association and Bayader on their approach to communication, i.e. how to present the program transparently in a way that mitigates conflict, anticipating perceptions of unfairness or other issues that could create conflict, and managing expectations. The project will ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring theavailability of a safe water supply and creating a clean living environment in thetargeted locations. The project services will be allocated based on the number ofbeneficiaries. Target beneficiaries will be informed of the project objectives and itsimpact on the allocated quantities of water to be supplied to their respective areas Inaddition, areas supplied by the selected wells are known to be highly vulnerable andgroups like people with disabilities, the elderly and women with young children willhighly benefit due to minimizing the burden of buying and attaining un-piped water.Global Communities has a specific security manual that includes safety and securityguidance for working in Palestine and details the processes, procedures, and riskmitigation measures staff are required to take to ensure their and beneficiaries’ safetyand security. All staff expected to work in or travel to the targeted locations will receivesecurity training from Global Community Operations staff. We have establishedcoordination and security protocols working closely with Global CommunitiesDC based international security team and other organizations, such as UNRWA, on anongoing basis to understand changes to the security and operational context in West-Bank and advise staff accordingly, including in real-time as needed. 11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Global Communities understands the interlinkage in human rights and that social inclusion stretches beyond focusing on the direct needs of marginalized groups. Promoting the rights to access safe drinking water and sanitation, directly impact the rights of education, health, women‘s right, child rights, and the rights of People with Disabilities (PwDs). The unserved and underserved communities in Palestine fall under three main social inclusion barriers; Attitudinal: stigmatization and misinformation including the different roles between men and women, which negatively impacts the decision-making process. Environmental: issues in accessing infrastructures and facilities and lack of communications. Institutional/ organizational: lack of policies, strategies, skills, and information to support social inclusion. While designing the technical intervention, GC was committed to using a participatory approach and non-discrimination principles to empower the marginalized by addressing WASH barriers. To ensure successful intervention, cultural sensitivity matters were taken into consideration and were incorporated in the monitoring and evolution plan so that unforeseen obstacles can be dealt with in an effective and efficient manner. Participation of stakeholders at the appropriate time during the program cycle is imminent; it enhances ownership, power relationships, accessibility, accountability, and sustainability. GC ensured that identified WASH stakeholders in general and beneficiaries in specific are fully engaged in the program design. Further, GC is committed to disseminating information to all stakeholders throughout the program to ensure informed decisions are adopted. Root causes of GBV are directly related to conflict with Israel and internal division. Generally speaking, the available services and capacity of service providers remain limited in all domains, including water as a vital and significant sector to supply potable water with quality that ensures safe access to it. A small percentage of women and children GBV survivors sought systematic support in Gaza. This disturbing ratio is attributed to survivors’ limited knowledge of available services, limited access to confidential and compassionate services including water. Additionally, humanitarian activities and services focused mainly on managing stress and life skills. Few addressed GBV case management, access and right of basic services including shelter/ food/WASH services, and social, environmental, and economic enablement. The project will announce the startup of the activities /kick off through a community meeting with all targeted localities by providing a full explanation about the project's activities. Conduct a participatoryassessment that considers the various stages of project implementation and ensures that communities are well engaged and are participating at all stages through clear stakeholder analysis, enlisting the necessary support for project implementation, identifying other needs, and linking them to project activities. The project team will carry out a series of community engagement activities that include but is not limited to: •Providing citizens, especially women and boys with balanced information that help them understand the project intervention vis a vis problem, alternatives, and solutions •Collecting feedback from stakeholders / local community about the project and its objectives and analyzing the problem/problems, developments, and achievement in different project implementation stages with the close support of the local partner. • Work with the community directly and on an ongoing basis, ensuring that their concerns are understood, taken into account, and addressed •Partnership with stakeholders / local community in the aspects of the decision, including the development of alternatives/options, priorities, and preferred solutions Ensuring that decisions and implementation are based on the local contribution & presented feedback.
