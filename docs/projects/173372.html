Project Summary

Under the 2021 JRP, BRAC will conduct daily monitoring on protection concerns in Rohingya camps and host communities in line with Accountability to Affected Populations and generate periodical situation reports. Person with Specific Needs, vulnerable women, girls, boys and men will be identified and provided with required referral, logistics and individual case management. Trafficking victims will be referred for case management and legal support from BRAC and other organisations as applicable with consent from the individual. Non CP and GBV project beneficiaries reporting protection concerns in accessing basic services through the beneficiary complaint mechanism will be followed up to redress the problems and risks.
In both refugee and host communities, BRAC will prioritise community engagement, enabling communities to lead the provision of protection services via Information Service Centres and community groups (women, girls, men and boys) inclusive of age group and disabilities. Community centres will facilitate various activities for collective dialogue, social and health awareness (e.g. COVID-19 infections, air and water borne diseases), campaigns, life skills training and recreational activities. BRAC’s will integrate greenbelt/reforestation as collective community action in environmental rehabilitation. 103,722 individuals from more than 20,000 HHs in the camps will be supported through community groups’ and community outreach members/volunteers will respond their protection and assistance needs, and arrange social dialogue to improve social cohesion between Rohingya and host communities. Similarly community-based activities in the host community, will be led by eight community groups, each group with 25-30 members. 30,720 individuals (50% female) from almost 12,000 HHs will be reached.
BRAC’s strict safeguarding policy will be applied across all its projects, partnerships and communities through continuous training and monitoring. BRAC will engage all relevant stakeholders, participate and organize evidence-based advocacy and capacity building sessions in PSEA and AGD for service providers in WASH, Health, CwC and relevant local government and partner organisation stakeholders on protection mainstreaming.

1 - Identified Needs

The necessity of regular monitoring of the protection environment and community based protection evolves from the extent of protection risks and concerns resulting violation of refugees' rights and obstructing meaningful access to their rights and the available services. The Joint Multi Sector Need Assessment (October 2020) reports concerns in robbery, kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment, rent payments, threats by local people/armed groups, mahjee demanding money/using violence against those reporting problems. These are in not only the camps but also spill over to the surrounding host communities. Problems became acute when COVID-19 hit across the country in March 2020; 13% of households reported security issues since the COVID-19 outbreak. About 42% refugee women said it is more unsafe for women and girls inside the house since the COVID-19 crisis. Furthermore, 51% refugees and 53% host community members have said it has become more unsafe for men and boys outside the house since the COVID-19 crisis (Rapid Gender Analysis May 2020). BRAC’s protection monitoring reports and the Intersectional Analysis report (UNHCR, CARE & ActionAid 2020) finds that that Rohingya women, female adolescents and youth continue to be vulnerable to child marriage, sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation, IPV, survival sex, and trafficking. Men, male adolescents and youth, on the other hand, are at higher risk of drug abuse, drug trade, trafficking, sexual harassment and abuse, gambling, and petty crimes. Adolescents and youth who are unaccompanied and separated are at risk of exploitation and abuse. Lack of youth engagement in social cohesion and resilience building by participating in cultural activities contributes to increasing child protection risks in the camps. The Intersectional Analysis report (UNHCR, CARE & ActionAid 2020) reports that in 33 out of 34 refugee camps in 2019, nearly half of the households reported the fear of kidnapping of young girls and boys. JRP 2020 also reports child marriage takes place in 5% to 10% of the Rohingya households as a negative coping strategy either for the perceived need to protect a girl through marriage or reduce the economic burden on households.Safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for all, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. Protection has been chosen as priority need by 38% of the refugees and 34% of the host community women and girls. Contrary to popular notion, more refugee men (45%) has chosen protection as priority need. The Rapid Gender Analysis has prioritized increasing community-based protection to ensure safe access to different services for women, girls and transgender persons. It has also prioritized strengthening collaboration with the camp and legal authorities, and local police to improve security, including the safety of women and girls in the camps and ensure access to reporting for host and refugee communities. In the JMSNA (October 2020), community engagement was highlighted as a gap with efforts needing to be scaled up, including through the rapid establishment of community centres, recreational and protection spaces as critical infrastructures for the community. Protection Monitoring is essential to identify the persons at heightened protection risk and including effective protection assessments of the situation of populations of concern. This will lead to informing and designing effective responses by efficient utilization of humanitarian actors, and help establish a common inter-agency understanding. Noting the importance of maintaining the link between monitoring and devising strategies, regular situation analysis of protection environment will provide an evidence-based knowledge for medium and long terms planning.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Daily monitoring of protection environment in camps through various monitoring processes and tools, including the beneficiary feedback mechanism with respect Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and producing protection situation reports and sectoral reports on a monthly basis2. Identification and assessment of Person with Specific Needs (PSN) and vulnerable women, girls, boys and men3. Identified victims and vulnerable individuals supported with required referral for emergency protection issues by engaging relevant stakeholders by providing required referral and necessary logistic support and Individual Case Management Support (Non CP and GBV cases)4. Community group (women, girls, men and boys) formation and operation ensuring gender equality in host community. Engage them in different social awareness activities, campaigns, life skills training & recreational activities in the BRAC's community centers.5. Awareness session and social dialogue on various protection issue to promote community based protection mechanism and improve social cohesion between refugees and affected host communities.6. Community-centred emergency preparedness i.e. COVID-19, Diarrohea etc to raise awareness among the community people, and response to mitigate weather-related risks and natural disasters especially during moonson and cyclone period.7. Collaboration with Food Security Sector (FSS) to integrate greenbelt/reforestation through tree plantation as part of collective community activities to strengthen the resilience of affected communities.8. Organizing protection mainstreaming session on protection principles and AGD approach with non-protection actors in the camps through negotiation and evidence based advocacy9. Train community volunteers of BRAC PM, CBP and other agencies (protection/non-protection) on the safe identification, verification and management of protection concerns
