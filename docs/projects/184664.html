Project Summary

Acute malnutrition is a major public health problem in Somalia and it affects disproportionately children under the age of five, as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). According to FSNAU, the humanitarian situation in Somalia is still critical as per September 2021. Nearly up to 5.6 million are facing food insecurity while closely 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2021. Moreover, current estimates indicate that 1 in 10 children under the age of five are acutely malnourished with the median National GAM of 11.1%, translating to 1.2 million children; of which 295,515 are affected by life threatening severely malnutrition while 739,829 are moderate acute malnutrition. In addition to children under five years of age, a further 184,432 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance.
Middle Shabelle region, the GAM rate is 15.1% with the rural and urban people are approaching serious and critical GAM levels. In September 2021FEWS NET technical release, it is indicated that 70110 people are acute malnourished and 13220 severely malnourished. Beside, 11,430 rural and 6430 IDP are in in Emergency (IPC4).Lower Shabelle, the GAM rate is (10-14.9%) with some riverine population having GAM rate of (15-29.9%).In addition, 122,590 are acute malnourished and 24,080 people are severely malnourished. Besides, 18,110 rural and 130 IDP people are in an Emergency level (IPC4) and 22,370 urban, 75,210 rural, 12,220 IDP people are at a Crisis stage (IPC3). Poor and erratic rainfall distribution, flooding and conflict. in Lower Shabelle resulted food insecurity and malnutrition.
The efforts will be focused on maximizing benefits and avoiding harms for the most deprived and vulnerable groups including children and women within the targeted regions. The project will be aimed focus on the ''Do No Harm'' principle to protect the beneficiaries from exploitation at any given level. The project will implement activities aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of all individuals in accordance with international law. There will be continuous sensitization both internally and externally on child protection to ensure that the rights of the children within the target communities are not violated at any given point. The proposed action will be implemented to promote meaningful access safety and dignity of the target populations to the project services.
People with disabilities often face exclusion, increased vulnerability, and barriers in accessing their fundamental human rights. The project will ensure inclusion of people with disabilities, preventing stereo typing, discrimination and enhance their access to the project intervention irrespective of the disabilities. HIJRA will make a deliberate effort to ensure the project sites are closer to the community to enhance access to the services by the disabled and vulnerable population thus reducing walking distances and exposure to harm.
HIJRA Will scale basic nutrition services in priority to IDPs and their host communities and other socially marginalized groups in lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle region (men and women and youth) will be involved and will be fully participating in all project cycle and decision-making processes. HIJRA is planning to reach and treat acute malnutrition, a total of 39131 children 6months to 59 months boys and girls, 12643 pregnant and lactating women as well as promoting IYCF practices to caregivers through static OTP and mobile OTP in target areas.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Community screening for malnutrition and referral for Children. The proposed intervention will be to undertake community screening for malnutrition and strengthen referral systems for 23631 children (113471boys and 12294 girls) of 6-59 months old through community nutrition diagnosis and early detection of acute malnutrition in, Lower Shabelle and Middle Shabelle Region. Admitting of severe acute malnutrition and referral of 8000 (3840 boys and 4160girls) to the nearest supplementary feeding program for moderately malnourished children after providing basic nutrition counseling on infant and young child feeding. Activity 2: Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition in children 6-59months. The project will provide treatment of severe acute malnutrition for children who are 6-59months old. Specifically, admit and treat 8000 children (3840 boys and 4160 girls) with severe acute malnutrition without medical complications in an outpatient therapeutic programs. Through scaling up of 8 fixed nutrition centers and six mobile teams in Lower Shabelle and middle Shabelle Region. Activity 3: Provide multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women In accordance with the project objective, we intend provide multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women in the targeted regions. This will be achieved by providing micronutrient supplementation, Vitamin A and Iron folic supplementation to 6745 eligible pregnant and lactating women and 15641(7500boys and 8134 girls) children from 6-59 months in the target districts and 80% of the PLWs in the project areas will be targeted to prevent and treat anemia which is the leading cause of morbidity infant and young child feeding promotion Activity 4: Provide Infant and young child feeding promotion and Counselling We intend to Conduct IYCF Counseling (both individual and group counseling) for mothers/ caregivers visiting the nutrition centers. 4045 mothers and 200 fathers will be targeted during the project period. The project intends to feed Infant and young children and also provide counseling by Organizing 20 sessions on IYCF-E at the community level targeting 800 mothers and 100 fathers. The IYCF officer will prepare the relevant IYCF topics and appropriate IEC materials. Activity 5: Empowering mothers through educating MUAC usage at community level The project intends to provide training to 1000 mothers and 100 traditional birth attendants of using MUAC for nutrition diagnosis at the household level, through supervising of nutrition officer and educating of CHNWs/ CNV. Activity 6: Capacity building of Nutrition Staff .The project intends to Conduct 5 days of integrated management of acute malnutrition to Nutrition staff in the mentioned Districts in the Lower Shabelle, and Middle Shabelle Regions. Activity 7: Conduct one SMART survey and IYCF surveys assessments

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure the location and access routes to OTP centers and services are safe. These will be enhanced through identifying central locations and avoiding areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence. The project will ensure that the health services are respectful and inclusive of cultural and religious practices by Having a proportionate number of female nutrition staff. If female doctors/nurses are not available, consider a female doctor rotation between locations. In this case, women must be adequately informed of which days a female doctor will be available and employ nutrition staff with skills and experiences working with children. In addition, ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private.
