Project Summary

This IOM Protection project aims to contribute towards the achievement of Strategic Objectives 1 and 3 of the Protection Cluster. SO2: Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5 and S.O.3 requests to enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and livelihoods in prioritized areas.
The project seeks to provide protection assistance to the most vulnerable focusing on IDPs, host communities and returnees in Warrap, Unity, Western Bahr El Ghazal, and Central Equatoria states. The project focus will be on continuing the current support to community-based protection, provision of access to justice, protection assistance to victims of trafficking, and housing, land, and property assistance. The components to be implemented at each project site will be based on assessments and community consultations in order to address specific needs. Aside from supporting the Protection Cluster Objectives, the project is also a part of IOM’s Protection Strategy. The counties to be targeted include in Warrap State: Twic, Tonj North, Tonj South; in Western Bahr el Ghazal State: Wau, Jur River; and in Unity State: Leer. These locations have been prioritized by the cluster and have also been confirmed by needs assessments.
Objective 2: CO 1: IOM will ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas.
IOM Protection will specifically undertake to support vulnerable girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who have experienced violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect, including gender-based violence and are in hard-to- reach geographical areas, through the provision of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services by the following:
(a) Provision of protection assistance including targeted assistance to victims of trafficking, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable groups through of case management services, access to justice for survivors of gender-based violence and other vulnerable persons as determined by joint needs assessments with other partners. This will be achieved through static as well as through mobile interventions integrated with GBV as well as with other sectors such as WASH and S-NFI.
(b) Awareness raising on protection, rights and entitlements, disability inclusion, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse including sensitization of community structures on protection risks and mitigation and working with community protection committees on referrals.
In regard to SO 3 IOM will facilitate conflict- and gender-sensitive access to safe housing, land, and property for women, men, girls, and boys, with a sufficient security of tenure. IOM will do this through:
(i) Provision of legal awareness raising including on Housing, Land, and Property rights and responsibilities;
(ii) Provision of legal counselling;
(iii) Legal capacity building to dispute resolution mechanisms at the community level; and
(iv) Use of cash-based assistance to ensure individuals will not have rights denied due to lack of funds.
The project is intended to assist the beneficiaries to claim their rights and to build the capacity of the authorities to protect and provide access to rights of the affected populations.
Overall the project will target 28,925 individual beneficiaries, of these 15,665 will benefit from the HLP component of the project and 13,260 from the GP component of the project. The overall budget for this project is USD 867,750 - which averages 30USD/beneficiary.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been affected by many years of conflict exacerbated by natural disasters such as flooding and famine. While there appears to be progress towards a transition to peacebuilding and a cessation of violence, there are still pockets of sub-national violence in parts of the country necessitating the provision of protection assistance that will cater for emergency and reduction of protection risks, provision of targeted protection assistance to survivors of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect as well as strengthening structures and systems that foster protective environments. Continued protection assistance to those in displacement is crucial in South Sudan to address the cycle of violence and disasters being witnessed. One of the consequences of the conflict has been the erosion of justice mechanisms to deal with injustice faced by citizens. These conditions have led to the loss of respect towards the justice system and bred a spirit of impunity amongst community leaders and members. Vulnerable members of the community - not aware of their rights and available remedies - have been on the receiving end of this spirit of impunity. This situation alone has heightened the risks which vulnerable members of the community face. In particular, vulnerable members of the community are now susceptible to grave human rights violations such as forced recruitment, gender-based violence (GBV), child abduction, robberies, extortion, revenge killings, and forced evictions, among others. Evidence demonstrates that humanitarian crises, such as protracted conflict, can exacerbate existing trends of trafficking in persons, and lead to new forms of trafficking (1). During crisis, the weakening (or collapse) of state and community structures, coupled with a loss of protective factors (such as livelihoods), can lead to negative coping strategies. This affects upon vulnerability to trafficking and enables traffickers to thrive in an environment of widespread human, material, social and economic losses. Given the above needs, this project seeks to address this situation through engagement with multiple actors on reduction of protection risks, provision of protection assistance including to victims of trafficking and access to justice. The project will further ensure that it continues to monitor the protection environment for possible human rights risks and engage the authorities and humanitarian partners alike to advocate the mitigation of the identified risks. 2 - Project activities

The project will apply conflict- and gender- sensitive approaches ensuring assistance provided will not exacerbate protection risks or expose women and girls to GBV. Through age and diversity guidelines on community engagement, the team trained on inclusive engagement with community will target persons with disabilities, older persons, and other groups that may not have links to community representation. SO2 1. Provision of protection assistance to vulnerable cases with protection concerns including victims of trafficking IOM will undertake static and mobile protection assistance. For the mobile protection assistance, the teams will be joining the GBV team as well as other sectors in multisectoral response and IOM will support cases identified with protection concerns for the achievement of protection outcomes. For the victims of trafficking, IOM will provide comprehensive case management services to conflict-affected victims of trafficking who have been exploited due to their vulnerable situation especially women and girls and boys who have been trafficked for labour and sexual exploitation. This assistance will be undertaken through a combination of direct implementation as well as through funding implementing national partners. 2. Awareness raising on protection – rights and entitlements, disability inclusion, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse including sensitization of community structures on protection risks and mitigation and working with community protection committees on referrals. This activity will be conducted both by the protection team in mobile responses or the community protection committees in areas of static response. 3. HLP and Legal Aid services Legal Awareness Raising: This activity aims to raise awareness among conflict-affected populations of their human rights, rights to land and adequate housing, options available concerning HLP and how to pursue these rights and remedies. This awareness will assist them in making informed decisions regarding their situation. This will be conducted through focus group discussions with community members, radio talk shows and information/educational materials such as posters, banners and brochures/leaflets. Legal Counselling: the primary focus of the activity is to disseminate accurate information to affected persons. The information and counsel will be case-specific and confidential. The action will prioritize survivors of gender-based violence as well as women with housing, land and property challenges. Assistance to access legal remedies and documentation: this activity involves provision of financial resources to affected persons to pursue legal resolution of disputes, with the goal of ensuring that no one is excluded from seeking justice through the statutory legal system due to a lack of resources. IOM will support the provision of assistance to obtain documentation that will enable individuals to access their rights. In areas without functioning documentation, the focus will be on encouraging the community to obtain preliminary tokens and documentation through manual registers and where feasible showing this documentation to structures set up under the Ministry of Physical Planning and Infrastructure.4. HLP training and Legal Aid Capacity Building Training and capacity building: this activity, as previously mentioned, seeks to capacitate both statutory and traditional dispute resolution services at the sub-national and community level. It seeks to sensitize land actors and authorities of the human rights of conflict-affected populations, as well as the rights of women to access property, and relevant provisions of the Constitution, Land Act etc. which govern their rulings and decision making. Advocacy: in this activity, IOM will engage stakeholders and humanitarian partners to respond to the identified Protection risks. This will involve policymakers and national land authorities to consider how legal systems.Details on targets/activities see below 3

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

This project considers the financial hurdles facing individuals seeking legal representation in statutory court. By basing average court costs and fees on assessments conducted by South Sudan Law Society (SSLS), IOM will support the legal fees and court costs associated with resolution of housing, land and property disputes, particularly for women and vulnerable individuals.This Market Functionality assessment will seek to better comprehend the experiences of the legal aid actors in the location i.e. boma, payam and county level. This will help to build a picture of the legal services available in that location i.e. typology, price differentiation within the locality. In addition, this market functionality assessment will seek to explore the constraints that the legal aid actors face in dispensing the legal aid services.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash assistance will be used in the project to pay for the legal fees of the cases of the most vulnerable with little or no resources to pursue their legal cases, it will also be used in some cases of protection assistance for example where necessary to support persons with disabilities’ coping mechanisms. This will be conditional cash assistance specific for access to justice and this will only be done where there is an assessment that indicates access to these services will not negatively impact the conditions in the area of service or put the beneficiaries at risk.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA information will be disseminated to the beneficiaries through awareness campaigns to sensitize them about the prevention and response mechanisms to PSEA. The information will also be distributed through radio talk shows, banners, posters and leaflets.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IOM has developed materials in pictographs which IOM staff use to stimulate dialogues on protection principles and feedback mechanisms. IOM has also developed capacities of organizations for persons with disabilities to be able to lead discussions on protection principles and feedback mechanisms and they will also be useful to reach out to other persons with disabilities in the communities through discussions. In areas of operation the project will identify persons with disabilities and their specific needs ensuring to provide sign language, audio recordings and opt for face-to-face meetings to facilitate inclusion. The different mechanisms for receiving feedback including through associations/organizations for persons with disabilities will strengthen receipt of feedback. Women and girls will be engaged at times appropriate for them based on their various roles and responsibilities including presenting opportunities for engagement in areas common to women. IOM Protection will also work closely with GBV colleagues managing women and girls’ friendly spaces to obtain feedback on IOM general protection activities and barriers they have faced to ensure that these are addressed immediately. IOM will identify associations/organizations of persons with disabilities to establish barriers faced by various persons with disabilities in obtaining assistance provided and participating in project implementation, monitoring and evaluation. IOM protection will eliminate barriers and set in place facilitators to enable participation and ensure inclusion in assistance. IOM staff already trained by Humanity Inclusion on the Washington Group Short Set of Questions will disaggregate beneficiary by age, gender and disability.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will engage community members, community leaders and representatives at all stages of the project life cycle. IOM Protection has been utilizing the Washington Group of Questions while working with persons with disabilities in order to disaggregate according to their needs and also provide for their needs accordingly. The project will ensure that it understands barriers that exist for women, persons with disabilities and older persons participation in activities and will specifically look at providing facilitators to ensure participation of these groups in design, implementation and monitoring of projects. IOM’s strategy requires constant engagement with associations/networks of persons with disabilities in project activities and they will be included in the consultations on projects to establish whether different needs of men, women, girls and boys with disabilities have been factored in. Informational and communication materials will be developed in a variety of ways to cater for literacy levels as well as different needs of communication as would be required by persons with disabilities. There will be visual and audio recordings as well as sign language provision in face-to-face meetings for those in need of these services. These engagements will also give community members an opportunity to provide feedback about the project. In addition, focus group discussions, community protection committee members, pre- and post- activity assessments, and CFM mechanisms will solicit the feedback and opinions of a diversity swath of community members. Multi-layered feedback mechanisms will be established to give beneficiaries multiple avenues to voice their concerns and complaints. These mechanisms will be comprised of a toll free number, a complaints desk in areas of operation and fellow humanitarian partners on ground whom beneficiaries can reach and register their complaints. IOM Protection will utilize the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Emergencies using the same to check whether interventions meet the standards required.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable by directly engaging them through periodic focus groups discussions and key informant interviews with them and recording their views and suggestions. These views will be used to adjust project implementation and feedback provided to the community on adaptations adopted. Where possible, project implementation will be aided by community committees who will review plans and share feedback on planned adjustments.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To mitigate against the risk of women and girls being excluded from consultations and decision-making processes, IOM Protection will engage directly with women at women’s friendly spaces, water-points, markets and other locations where women are bound to congregate and feel free to talk. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons will not participation in consultations or decision-making processes will be addressed by a thorough Protection and Conflict Context Analysis that will inform on the dynamics in the communities, groups existing, establish the existence of marginalized groups and existing associations through engagement with various actors in the community including education and health. The team will work closely with organizations for persons with disabilities to expand reach to persons with disabilities. Networks of persons with disabilities often have several older persons and they will be useful to point to other older persons including those without disabilities. In this way, risk of exclusion of this vulnerable and pertinent groups will be minimalized. To reduce the risk of interference by external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries, IOM Protection will work with the Transition and Recovery Unit on peacebuilding activities in these locations. In addition, IOM Protection will inform the local authorities, community representatives and the wider community of its mandate, mission and the need for impartiality. IOM’s protection team will work to ensure that assistance is strictly provided on a needs-basis. The risk of beneficiaries failing to access/use complaint and feedback mechanisms will be mitigated by constantly sharing information on the existence of this mechanisms including through the leadership, community representatives and partners.
