Project Summary

Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) is proposing this project with much emphasis on malnutrition prevention by applying a social behavior change communication approach, MoH and nutrition cluster recommended MICYN guideline targeting caregivers of children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women in Jonglei, Northern Barhrel ghazel, Unity, Eastern Equatoria States and Western Equatoria states of South Sudan.
In this project, CRSF is proposing the following activities inline with nutrition cluster objectives and 2025 HRP strategic objectives; Conducting group and One on one Counselling sessions prioritized for mother support groups, Formation of Mother to Mother Support groups (MtMSGs) and Father to Father Groups (FtFGs) which will actively be engaged in discussion of IYFC best practices and overcoming contextual barriers that affect proper and appropriate IYCF practices in their localities. These groups will hold weekly sessions to evoke robust interactions and group counselling sessions at Boma level. CRSF will also conduct mobile and outreach visits (house to house) to encourage pregnant mothers and caregivers/mothers of children under 0-6 months on early initiation of breasting within one hour of birth, exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months, health seeking, trace defaulters and collect information on reasons for absenteeism and defaulting and carry out household health and nutrition education, and hygiene promotion messages.
Further, CRSF will carry out early risk assessment through IYCF KAP surveys, and increase dissemination of information breasting conditions and how to overcome them. CRSF will also conduct counselling sessions of individual mothers of children 6-23 month who experience challenges introducing proper complementary feeding for their children. CRSF will coordinate with CHDs to conduct behavior change communication trainings for CHD health and nutrition personnel who shall be key in promoting and encouraging pregnant mothers who come for ANCs to carry out early initiation of breastfeeding within first hour of birth, skin to skin contact for baby and mother and ensure they are enabled with knowledge to provide advice and emotional support to new mothers.
Mobilize MSGs led by lead mothers to carry out cooking demonstrations. CRSF will ensure to apply a Positive Deviance Hearth model so as to identify caretakers of children 6-59months who will demonstrate proper preparation of complementary food for children using the locally available nutritious foods. To ensure integration, CRSF will ensure all preventive nutrition interventions into other sectors; FSL, WASH, Protection, health, and education through a robust coordination the implementing partners. CRSF will coordinate with nutrition partners applying CMAM to provide hand washing education and Equipments, ensure distribution of WASH kits to caregivers of children admitted in OTP/TSFP programs. Coordination with FSL partner will ensure vulnerable children and their caregivers to BSFP and GFD interventions, Food for Asset and promotion of kitchen gardens and vegetable gardening.
CRSF will ensure coordination with MoH, UNICEF, WFP and the nutrition cluster to provide IEC materials like banners, posters, billboards with clear malnutrition preventive key messages and these will be placed strategically for instance at health facilities, roundabouts, distribution points and markets. Further, CRSF will coordinate with MoH to support government led initiatives on malnutrition prevention like SUN CSA drives, VAS, deworming, World Breastfeeding week. CRSF will strengthen community outreach component to identify children with acute malnutrition through active case finding on time and referrals to OTP/TSFP centers in the project areas. CRSF will train lead mothers and MtMSGs/FtFSGs on family MUAC and referral for timely identification of cases.

1. Needs

The problem of persistent acute malnutrition in South Sudan continue to affect mostly under five children and pregnant and lactating women and is driven by multiple factors; Recurrent conflicts that disrupt agricultural activities and limit access to humanitarian aid; localized displacements resulting into IDPs, and influx of South Sudanese returnees fleeing the conflict in Sudan/Khartoum; poor economic conditions, and recent hyperinflation; disease burdens; inadequate protections measures against gender-based violence; limited agricultural development; and limited access to livelihood inputs like crop and vegetable seeds among others.According to the nutrition cluster report of 2024, acute malnutrition rates in 49 counties of south sudan exceed the emergency threshold of 15% (Nut cluster, 2024). The latest IPC report estimates 2.1M children aged 6-59 months and 1,110,000 pregnant or lactating women to be acutely malnourished and require urgent treatment from July 2024 to July 2025 (SS IPC Nov 2024). Pibor, Aweil center, Rubkona, and Bor South counties are currently experiencing the effect of flooding causing localized displacements with significant IDPs and the influx of South Sudanese returnees fleeing the Khartoum/Sudan crisis. The IPC further indicates that food insecurity is at its worst-case scenario with for instance about 1,112, 000 people (53%) in Jonglei state currently in crisis stage (IPC Phase 3+) until the end of November 2024 (SS IPC Nov 2024). The 2025 IPC for Acute Malnutrition analysis (IPC- AMN) indicates that Pibor, Rubkona, Aweil Centre, Bor South counties are currently projected to be in Critical stage of IPC AMN Phase 4. Diseases for instance diarrheal diseases, optimal sub IYCF and complementary practices where only 4.6% of children 6-23 months receive the required food quality at the required frequency, acute food insecurity, GBV against women and girls, while acute floods and sub national level conflicts continue to result in population displacement of vulnerable people placeing many children under the age of five and PLWs in a critical IPC AMN phase 4 especially among south Sudanese returnees, IDPs and host populations. Given the burden resulting from a host of factors as indicated in the IPC analysis report, it is envisaged that, acute malnutrition among children and PLWs is likely to worsen in most counties in IPC-AMN phase 4 including counties targeted by CRSF during the peak of the dry season from April to June 2025 (IPC Dec 2024 to Mar 2025). Poor WASH practices, flooding, malaria and other diseases, Low minimum Dietary Diversity, severe food insecurity, Low access to health and preventive nutrition interventions will likely worsen the nutrition situation in South Sudan. Tackling acute malnutrition in south sudan could effectively require a multilevel approach considering the multiple factors including vastness of counties through a multisectoral and integrated approach. There is need to front an integrated malnutrition preventive activity that could tackle a ray of factors. Child Relief and Support (CRSF) is operational and has been active responding to prevent acute malnutrition through implementation of integrated MIYCN package into its large component programs in Lekuangule, Pibor, Gumruk, and Verteth with support from UNDP. CRSF has secured another funding fron from Roddenberry foundation (+1 Global Fund) to address malnutrition through dietary diversity and livelihood interventions for caretakers of under five children in Pibor, Aweil Centre and Rubkona counties. Currently, CRSF is steering Scaling Up Nutrition (SUN) Civil society alliance to work jointly in addressing malnutrition in all its forms in South Sudan. With this expertise and experience, CRSF seeks collaboration with nutrition cluster to continue exploring donors and ways of addressing malnutrition in all its forms among children under five and PLWs targeting South Sudanese returnees, IDPs, and host population.

The protection officer will also train all the CNVs and MIYCN counsellors on GBV basics, PSEA, o Activity 4.4: Conduct establishment kitchen gardens with locally available highly nutritious veggies and conduct cooking demos at nutrition sites in liaison with CMD.o Activity 4.5: Conduct linkages of children under 5 years admitted with severe acute malnutrition receiving WASH kits o Activity 4.6: Conduct training of CNVs, and BHWs on MIYCN guidelines.
