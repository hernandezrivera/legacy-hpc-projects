Needs

S01 - Ensure effective, sufficient and continuous provision of life saving water and sanitation services for targeted men, women, boys and girls; According to ISCG sitrep October 2018, 79% of populations in settlements benefit from safe water to agreed standards and meeting demend for domestic purposes of 15 l/p/d while 94% benefit from WASH hygiene kits/top-up. 74% of women, men and children in settlements benefit from functional latrines meeting agreed standards. Fecal sludge management poses a challenge as transfer station lime ponds remain important options while mid-term options are being explored.Safe water handling and storage at household levels remain poor as >70% of sampled household recorded contamination of water. Solid waste management remains also a challenge as high production of waste is poorly managed due to lack of appropriate space and technologies. Feasibility studies are conducted to provide sustainable solutions. Action Against Hunger plans to pilot biogas production through decomposition of organic waste. Many existing WASH facilities in the camp record frequent operation and maintenances challenges and malfunctioning due to regular lack of proper use, ownership and well established and responsive community WASH working groups. Destruction of WASH facilities during monsoon occurrences is regularly experienced posing challenges to re-constructions and major repairs. Due to camp high populations, high volumes of fecal sludge is daily produced and lack of sufficient and appropriate facilities for sludge management poses an environmental pollution risk and thus leads to piloting and adoption of on-site sludge management options such as biogas, biofil and other innovative technologies. S02 - Ensure that all targeted women, men, girls and boys have the means and are encouraged to adopt individual and collective measures increasing health seeking behaviours to mitigate public health risks 27% of households still report facing problems accessing basic hygiene items such as soap (REACH, 2018); While ACFFGD shows that 77% of people reported handwashing with soap as a practice, our findings corroborate REACH’s in that much fewer numbers could name 3 key times for handwashing. 27% of women face issues in accessing products to manage their menstrual periods. Combined with the issues of accessing facilities described below, this poses a significant issue for women and adolescent girls in the camp. S03 - Ensure that all WASH assistance promotes protection, safety and dignity of targeted men, women, boys and girls with focus on sustainable resilience-building approaches. Many of the WASH communal facilities in the camp lack lighting systems which make them unsafe to use during night time. According to ACF obsevatory report, 89% of communal latrines and bathing spaces are not used in the evening and at night due to lack of lighting. Lack of proper gender segregation of latrines and bathing facilites in the camp promotes risks of protection to most women and girls from using the facilities. LAck of proper compalint reporting and feedback mechanisms in the camp prevents many users from speaking out their experiences and challenges associated with protection issues in the camp.
