Project Summary

As observed during the Samaritan's Purse Assessment, the overall health of the target community is in jeopardy. Healthcare services are completely suspended in HK and hugely disrupted in TK,with poor accessibility due to chronic resource allocation deficits and the ongoing financial and political crises throughout the country. With government budget cuts and medication shortages, the ability to provide adequate consultations and coverage to the TK population has been strained. There are no qualified clinicians capable of conducting safe medical consultationsin HK, even atthe most basic levels, due to a lack of access to MoH training schools.For these reasons, SP is proposing to facilitate vital HEW trainings, using the MoH curriculum within the target communities, as further detailed below. 1 - Needs

The health needs present in HK and TK continue to raise alarm due to a lack of qualified personnel and adequate essential medicines and supplies caused by government austerity measures. An internal needs assessment conducted by SP in October 2020 found that a lack of medicine, equipment and staff, along with inadequate infrastructure, are significant challenges currently present within the localities. It is estimated that the average number of patients per day at the existing health clinic in TK is approximately 120 (nearly 35% being children), with the need expected to grow due to a higher town population. In HK, with a catchment area of approximately 300,000 people, the free-of-charge curative health services traditionally provided through a designated MoH facility are completely nonfunctional,with the closest referral for complex health situations being a costly fiveand a half-hour drive away. In order to paythis cost, some families have begun selling valuables and dipping into their finances needed to purchase food. 2 - Activities/Outputs

The comprehensive Health component of this multi-sectoral activity aims to increase local capacity to identify and treat illness, while improving preventative health behaviors through community engagement interventions. A robust curriculum with multifaceted trainings will be conducted along with additional interventions. Interventions within this sector will allow SP to:● Train and identify new HEWs to increase clinical staff availability in rural Beja communities and, therefore, increase the access to health services through a satellite training program with the Kassala State MoH, traditionally not offered on site to the HK target communities.● Conduct a refresher training for trained CHWs (there are CHWs currently existing in TK and none in HK, for that reason, trainings conducted in TK are geared towards participants known as CHWs rather than the new HEWs being trained in HK.) in the TK target communities through the assistance of the Kassala State MoH.● Facilitate refresher trainings on EmONC and on-the-job training for midwives.● Support HEW candidates through incentives to complete training (including room and board).● Build the capacity of Leader Mothers in Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (MNCH) topics.● Enable Leader Mothers to train Neighbor Women on MNCH.● Conduct group discussions for community leaders, influencers and other members of the community on MNCH. In focusing on the overall life expectancy and wellbeing of vulnerable communities in Kassala State and keeping objectives aimed at following USAID-BHA’s mission and vision, SP has designed outputs that align with the sub-sector “Basic Primary Health Care” to aid in achieving significant outcomes that will feed into achieving the overall purpose, mission and vision of the activity. Since the availability of health care in both HK and TK is severely lacking, SP is proposing a number of interventions that aim to improve health care through training healthcare personnel.Training opportunities will establish a local cadre of HEWs in HK and TK trained to treat common acute illnesses such as vomiting, pneumonia, malaria and diarrhea, which are prevalent throughout the communities. Samaritan’s Purse will facilitate trainings for HEWs using a course designed by the MoH to certify them to apply for health system opportunities. The MoH training for health workers is a 12-month comprehensive course that is traditionally offered in health training schools. Historically, however, this course has been poorly accessed by rural Beja, who make up the majority of the target population. Given the unique restrictive cultural context among residents in HK, SP will support a satellite community-based course in partnership with the MoH to train 25 HEWs in HK. In TK, a refresher-training course will be conducted for 50 CHWs who have already gone through the MoH training but could benefit from a training review. Samaritan’s Purse assumes that by building capacity into health workers and community volunteers such as midwives and HEWs through trainings on topics covering EmONC and MNCH and establishing Care Groups to cover health topics with mothers in the community, it will effectively tie into the overall multi-sectoral activity goal to save lives and improve the wellbeing of vulnerable communities in Kassala State, Sudan.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Within the Health Sector, women, girls, men and boys all face unique challenges and obstacles in regards to their protection needs. In designing this activity, SP has been diligent in assessing the potential needs that may arise from a protection perspective and has done due diligence to mitigate the potential risks. For a summary of the protection needs present in Sudan and considered throughout the design of this activity, please refer to the final section of Annex D1. Needs Assessment and Analysis Report. Samaritan’s Purse Health interventions proposed in this activity will incorporate activity risk mitigation measures including: 1) Samaritan’s Purse will aim for a balanced gender representation during recruitment of HEWs for training; 2) Health care workers trained will be exposed to information that will increase knowledge and skills relevant to working with populations with unique needs (e.g. women, adolescents, children, persons with disabilities, and older people); 3) Health Facilities that SP will assist will be accessible to women, children, the elderly, and those with disabilities to ensure that the most vulnerable community members of HK and TK are participating in the interventions and are receiving equal benefits of initiatives; and 4) Training materials under the Care Group model will be user friendly for those with disabilities and low literacy by including hands-on and interactive learning styles. A barrier analysis will be conducted before the training to learn about the specific learning barriers in the target communities regarding health and nutrition topics.When designing the proposed activities as a whole and throughout each sector, SP met with and received feedback from members of the target communities in FGDs involving community leaders, and heard from women, girls, men and boys, as well as the elderly. As described in Annexes K1. Code of Conduct and K2. Implementation Guidelines for Code of Conduct, SP takes the abuse and exploitation of staff and activity participants very seriously. Feedback mechanisms for reporting abuse will be in place across all SP activity implementation sites, including healthcare facilities. For specific information regarding these mechanisms, please see the attached annexes. Samaritan’s Purse will ensure confidentiality and protect the private information of all activity participants, preventing the unnecessary sharing of information that could lead to a participant’s defamation or increase his/her vulnerability. Gender responsive programming will be incorporated at all stages of the activity. Attention will be paid to ensure that both men and women participate and are equally represented in all aspects of intervention. FGDs with men and women will be organized to ensure a more efficient understanding of the needs and concerns of the men and women activity participants of the project. For example, the men in the targeted communities are the decision-makers and providers for their families.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Incorporated into the culture of SP is the zero tolerance policy towards SEA. A point person at the national level will work alongside the PMs and the team to ensure that safeguarding principles are embedded into all aspects of programming. All staff and representatives are provided with training on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), an understanding of the SP Code of Conduct, as well as the reporting mechanisms in place to report a concern of PSEA. In all SP field offices and intervention sites, awareness materials will be displayed to convey PSEA messages. All materials will be adapted to the local language and context. Project staff will inform and sensitize the local community/target population on awareness of PSEA and the requirement that SP staff adhere to a code of conduct, as well as ensure that the target population have knowledge of how to report an SEA concern or situation in a confidential manner.
