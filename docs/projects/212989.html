Project Summary

The planned intervention contributes to the reduction of morbidity and mortality and improves the overall access to quality health services for conflict-affected Syrians and IDPs. It will focus mainly on the provision and improvement of primary health care services, secondary health care, referral system, physical rehabilitation, capacity building of healthcare professionals.
The core of QC response for 2024 in Northern Syria include supporting 12 PHC centers distributed all over Northern Syria including the following PHC centers:
• 2 PHC centers in Idleb: Sijer PHC (G0700088 ! Sejar PHC) and Mastumeh PHC(G0700058 ! Mastumeh PHC)
• 8 PHC centers in Northern Aleppo: Basutah PHC(B0203057 ! Basuta PHC), Deir Siwan PHC(B0203074 ! Deir Siwan PHC), Bab Alsalama PHC(B0204012 ! Bab Al Salameh Camp PHC), Sandara PHC(B0204075 ! Sandara PHC), Qabbasin PHC(B0202044 ! QC Qabasin PHC), Bza’a BEmMONC(B0202016 ! Bazagha PHC), Alraie PHC(B0202043 ! QC Ar-Ra'ee PHC), and Zoghara PHC(B0208024 ! QC Zoghra PHC)
• 2 PHC centers in RATA area: Suluk BMONC center (K1102026 ! QC Suluk PHC) and Hamam Turkman PHC (K1102027 ! Hammam At-Turkman PHC)
The key services included in QC-supported health facilities are:
• General clinic
• Midwifery clinic
• Dental clinic
• Nursing clinic
• Laboratory
• Pharmacy for free-of-charge drugs
• Addinal BEmMONC services will be provided in both Suluk and Bza’a centers
As an integral part of our project, a dedicated team of Community Health Workers (CHWs) will be deployed, with each team comprising five members linked to supported health facilities. These CHWs will play a crucial role in conducting nutrition screening for young children and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), both within the health facilities and through outreach activities. Moreover, the CHW teams will offer counseling services and raise awareness within the community, utilizing a structured approach based on the six modules designed for CHWs.
Additionally, QC health response include supporting specialized pediatric healthcare services (B0202054 ! Ar-Ra'ee Hospital) including outpatient and hearing screening, inpatient ward (60 beds), incubators (18 incubators), NICU (6 beds), and pediatric ICU (6 beds).
To ensure smooth referring between the targeted health facilities, QC will run 18 ambulances (owned by QC) in the same catchment areas of the supported health facilities. All of those ambulances are fully equipped with advanced medical equipment and capable of transferring critical cases to seek their medial care in key health facilities.
Other health interventions planned to be implemented by QC include supporting an artificial limbs and rehabilitation center that will provide the following services:
• Ordinary wheelchair for a disabled person
• Electric wheelchair for a disabled person
• Hearing aids
• Smart upper prosthesis
• Smart lower limb prosthesis
• Corneal transplantation of the eye
• Rehabilitation session
Moreover, QC is planning to play a key role during this year in providing advanced capacity building activities as continuation to what have been implemented by QC in collaboration with the WHO. The planned topics include:
• Refresher training for senior anesthesia professionals
• Refresher training for senior pediatric healthcare professionals
• Refresher training for senior paramedics
• Refresher training in trauma surgery for surgeons
Most of the above components are planned to be applied through indirect implementation by QC implementing partners.
This fund of will be utilized for supporting staff salaries, procurement of drugs and consumables, and providing other running expenses for one year.
The proposed intervention will also complement the interventions of other humanitarian health actors delivering assistance in the same areas through constant communication with health directorates and other coordination networks.
QC has already approached its key donors to advocate and secure the needed fund.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Aligned with the HNO 2024, QC's project addresses critical health needs in conflict-affected Syria. By establishing and supporting PHC centers, ambulances, and specialized healthcare services, we aim to mitigate morbidity, mortality, and the strain on already limited healthcare infrastructure. Protection Risk Analysis (PRA):The Protection Risk Analysis (PRA) is a systematic approach used to identify, assess, and mitigate protection risks faced by conflict-affected populations, particularly vulnerable groups such as women, children, and persons with disabilities. In the context of the proposed intervention in Northern Syria, a PRA will be conducted to identify potential risks related to access to healthcare services, protection of healthcare workers, and safeguarding the rights and dignity of beneficiaries. The analysis will consider factors such as security threats, gender-based violence, child protection concerns, and access barriers to healthcare facilities. Through consultation with community members, local authorities, and other stakeholders, the PRA will inform the design and implementation of the intervention to ensure the safety and well-being of beneficiaries. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Strategy:The Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) strategy outlines measures to prevent, detect, and respond to incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse within humanitarian operations. In the proposed intervention, QC will develop and implement a comprehensive PSEA strategy to uphold ethical standards, protect beneficiaries from harm, and hold staff and partners accountable for their conduct. This strategy will include robust recruitment and vetting processes, training on code of conduct and reporting mechanisms for staff and partners, community awareness-raising activities, and confidential reporting channels for survivors. By prioritizing PSEA, QC aims to create a safe and respectful environment for beneficiaries to access healthcare services without fear of exploitation or abuse. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:1. Security Risks: QC addresses armed conflict and crime threats with security protocols and collaboration.2. Access Constraints: Mobile health teams and advocacy overcome barriers to healthcare.3. Resource Limitations: Funding diversification and procurement planning ensure medical supplies and staff availability.4. Protection Risks: Gender-sensitive programming and survivor support mitigate risks like gender-based violence.5. Assumptions: Contingency planning and stakeholder engagement adjust strategies to evolving dynamics.
