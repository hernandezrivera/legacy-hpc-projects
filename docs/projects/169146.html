Project Summary

The Beirut explosion caused more than 160 deaths and 6000 injured, of which around 1,000 are children.
UNICEF estimates that up to 100,000 boys and girls might be part of a family with either a destroyed or heavily damaged house. Access to basic services in the affected areas has been interrupted with the destruction (complete or partial) of several hospitals, health care facilities, schools and other educational facilities, MoSA social development centers, community centers, and offices of UNICEF partners where children and families access services.
Beyond physical wounds, mental health issues such as anxiety, sleeplessness, or night terrors are likely to be particularly experienced by children. Rapid assessments conducted during the past two weeks show that children and adults are experiencing psychological distress. Among children the signs observed include: Unusual crying, sadness, nightmares, fear from loud noises, inability to enter rooms in their homes where they were when the explosion happened, etc. While for adults signs of excessive worrying and fear, having nightmares, insomnia, disorientation, recalling incidents and trauma from the Lebanese civil war have been observed.
Research also shows that children can still suffer negative psychological effects of experiencing large scale explosion even 16 months after.
According to assessments, affected populations include Lebanese, Syrians, Palestinians (PRL, PRS), Doms Communities (Stateless Population Groups), Migrant/Domestic workers, while affected populations come from different socio-economic backgrounds. Women and girls are mostly affected.
The multiple crises that the country is facing including a deteriorating economic situation, prolonged confinement due to COVID-19, and more recently the explosion that happened in Beirut, are having an important impact on the wellbeing of families and as such, could be important drivers for an increase in incidents of violence against children and women.
Based on the above, UNICEF will work in collaboration with line ministries, UN agencies and partners to address the immediate psychosocial needs of 26,000 affected girls, boys, women and their families, using a multi-layered approach through the provision of an integrated package of services, including community-based and more focused psychosocial support activities, case management and access to specialized services, such as mental health psychosocial support services.
Moreover, UNICEF will provide emergency cash transfer to approx. 60-80,000 individuals, primarily children, living in the poorest and most blast affected areas of Beirut. The programme will be linked to broader services that vulnerable households will need, particularly child protection. Extensive community engagement, outreach and communication on the programme, efficient payment channel, and clear grievance and accountability mechanisms will be crucial.
UNICEF will also include timely and consistent AAP mechanisms (Accountability to affected population) to ensure that the programme responds to people needs.

1 - Needs

As of 17 August, at least 180 people have been killed and 6,000 injured, of which over 1,000 are children. A dozen people at least continue to be missing following the Beirut port explosion. Up to 300,000 people have lost their homes, with up to 100,000 children directly affected. The explosion caused extensive damage to essential public services. At least three hospitals have been severely damaged. Community government structures that provide essential services to families have also suffered structural damage, including 10 Social Development Centers and community centers operated by UNICEF child protection partners. This crisis is unfolding in the context of a collapsed economy, political instability and a surge in COVID-19 cases. Under the current circumstances, there is already a rapid spread of COVID-19. The longer-term consequences of the explosion particularly on children are yet to be fully grasped, and the fall out will be felt for months and possibly even years to come. Rapid assessments conducted during the past two weeks show that children and adults are experiencing psychological distress. Among children the signs observed include: Unusual crying, sadness, nightmares, fear from loud noises, inability to enter rooms in their homes where they were when the explosion happened, etc. While for adults signs of excessive worrying and fear, having nightmares, insomnia, disorientation, recalling incidents and trauma from the Lebanese civil war have been observed. 2 - Activities

Within the coordination structures in place, UNICEF in collaboration with line ministries and in partnership with national and international NGOs will provide an integrated package of services to address the different vulnerabilities of affected girls, boys, women and families. This will be done using a multilayered approach in line with international guidance and frameworks. UNICEF will also include timely and consistent AAP mechanisms (Accountability to affected population) to ensure that the programme responds to people needs. More specifically the below activities and actions will be implemented: -Activity 1: Effective leadership in the coordination of child protection (CP) sector and sub-groups (MHPSS, Case Management) is maintained while ensuring linkages with critical inter-sectoral issues. -Continue to coordinate the CP Working Group and play an active role in the SGBV task force and with other relevant actors, including municipality. Providing guidance about roles and priorities for first 72 hours and beyond. (secured funding) -Activity 2: Monitoring and reporting on the situation of children, women and families are undertaken and systematically trigger response (including advocacy).-CP issues to be included in ongoing/planned assessment in affected areas, including in Palestinian camps and gatherings. (secured funding)-Assessment of damage to partner community centers and offices and MoSA social development centers and support in rehabilitation of 14 locations. -Activity 3: Girls, boys and women who are at increased risk of CP concerns and gender based violence and their caregivers are identified and provided with a comprehensive package of prevention and response services:-Provision of psychological first aid (PFA) and referral to services through partners to affected children and women and girls and their caregivers; (secured funding) -Mass communication, information to parents/families on children's normal reactions to unusual events' and what parents can do to support. (25,000); (secured funding) -Community-based PSS included remote PSS (through mobile and static services, establishment of child-friendly spaces, equip with recreational and ECD kits…); (secured funding) -Distribution of 7,000 PSS kits for individual children; (funding required for 6,500 kits) -Provide focused PSS support and mental health for 1,000 women and girls (Establishment of PSS /referrals tents for women and girls in affected areas and equip with kits (PSS, hygiene) and information leaflets); (Partially secured) -Provision of case management and specialized services in affected areas to 550 girls and boys victims of CP violations; (secured funding) -Provide focused PSS for 6,000 children and caregivers around Lebanon (through mobile and static services); (Partially secured) -Provide mental health PSS to 2,500 children and caregivers (through individual and group sessions and trauma therapy) - Capacity building of volunteers, frontliners and specialized teams on PFA, management of distress, emotional support for caregivers, Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE), Inter-personal Communication (IPC), etc. ; (Partially secured) -Support UNICEF partners in taking actions in support of well-being and staff care for frontline workers and CP and GBV specialized staff -Ensure necessary follow-up on children who have been separated as a result of the blast for family reunification and specialized support. -Activity 4: Rapidly implementing all components of Emergency Cash Transfer for Child Wellbeing UNICEF will provide emergency cash transfer to 60-80,000 individuals, primarily children, living in the poorest and most blast affected areas of Beirut. The programme will be linked to broader services that vulnerable households will need, particularly CP. Extensive community engagement, outreach and communication on the programme, efficient payment channel, and clear grievance and accountability mechanisms will be crucial.
