Project Summary

Through the 2023 humanitarian response plan, The Rescue Mission intends to contribute to WASH cluster Sectorial objectives 1) Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition to ensure vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4,5 and 3 have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral essential services to meet their needs, TERM will support Nutrition facilities with WASH activities mainly focusing on providing access to safe water, adequate sanitation and distribution of hygiene kits to SAM children and caretakers of WASH Severity Classification 4,5 and 3 counties of Twic East, Bor South, Terekeka, Juba and Kajo Keji respectively.
Under WASH Custer Sectorial Objective 2 ,(Providing access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties and WASH specific activities for GBV or mitigating WASH GBV) that will contribute to exposer of vulnerable people to fewer protection threats and incidents, with those who are exposed to have access to safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action, TERM will provide access to WASH services in an equitable timely dignified manner through construction/ rehabilitation of water points to mitigate and reduce high levels of malnutrition, water related diseases like Acute Respiratory infections(ARI), Acute Watery diarrhea(AWD), cholera etc and Mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence (GBV) issues among the affected population in targeted location of Twic East Bor South, Terekeka, Juba and Kajo- Keji counties.
The proposed intervention will reach a target population of 48,645 vulnerable Individuals (Host 17,215, IDP’s 13,500, Returnees 12820, People with disabilities 5110).(Men 9,115, Women 15819, Boys 11,261, Girls 12450) . The activities will include, Construction of new water points; rehabilitation of water points, Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities at Health/Nutrition facilities, Schools and at the community levels; installation of hand washing facilities to promote hand washing; hygiene promotion education through door to door visits, participatory hygiene sessions in public places for effective behavioral change; distribution of hygiene kit to SAM children WASH NFIs to the most vulnerable.
Menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits will be provided to adolescent girls and women of reproductive age group accompanied with menstrual hygiene management sessions. TERM will ensure ownership and sustainability of the response through involvement of the targeted community throughout the life cycle of the intervention. All the WASH temporary and sustainable services will address the vital needs, through accountability to affected populations (AAP) , working with all the sex and age groups, people with disabilities (PWD), pregnant and lactating women (PLW) incorporating gender dynamics and specific needs of different groups into needs assessment and response recommendations. WASH structures and infrastructure in the flood prone areas, Contributing to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition focusing on Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children/caretakers, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion.
TERM will provide women, men, girls, and boys affected by multiple shocks and hazards in South Sudan with critical WASH/NFIs. The project shall also assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facilities level and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water, hygiene promotion activities and complementary sanitation services where possible
TERM will ensure all water points, sanitation facilities and water containers provided are both child and women friendly and as well addresses needs of PLWD

1 - Needs

South Sudan communities continues to face multiple shocks as the number of People in need keeps rising, according to OCHA ICCG/IMWG Joint Session 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview( HNO),People in Need ( PiN) and Severity Endorsement, the severity classification in 2022 is higher than 2021An estimated 6,159,886million people’s living standard and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in the Nile Basin states of Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria. Water supply and sanitation (WASH) is severely constrained, according to UNICEF only 36 per cent of households report having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing protection concerns and just 17 per cent have access to improved sanitation. While an estimated 1.4 million children under 5 years of age are acutely malnourished, including 313,391 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM). The lack of adequate, sustainable water, sanitation, and hygiene services, which is triggered by multiple factors including conflict, economic meltdown, flooding etc in many of the areas, has left communities—particularly women and children—at a heightened risk of water-related diseases. In addition, women and children have faced risks of sexual violence when they engage in survival activities such as fetching water. Parts of Jonglei state such as Twic East, Bor South counties have severely been affected by flooding and associated effect, this also includes recurring inter communal conflicts which resulted into multiple displacements. In Central Equatoria counties such as Juba, Kajo Keji and Terekeka are hosting IDPs and returnees as a result of conflict and flooding, this has exuberated the WASH needs, and caused strained on existing facilities, further worsened by the already destroyed infrastructure during the conflict. Despite the fact that major waterways were replenished by the long rainy season, only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas (FSNMS, 2021). State like Jonglei where 33 per cent of households travelling for longer than 30 minutes to access any type of water, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities (FSNMS, 2021. 49 per cent of households are reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source, this along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods, when latrine pits seep into waterways. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing particularly in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases. Water-borne disease remains the third largest cause of morbidity in South Sudan. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continues to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Sanitation facilities across South Sudan are uncommon. Overall, 80 per cent of households do not access a latrine (FSNMS, 2021). With more returnees arriving from IDP and refugee camps, majority of the population continue to share existing facilities. This might result in increased WASH-related disease morbidity among children, risk of disease outbreak, and limited sustainability of Nutrition and Health outcomes and higher prevalence of GBV at WASH facilities. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food in security has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition.

2 - Project activities

In order to reduced morbidity and mortality of vulnerable people who experience multi-sectoral severity levels of 4, 5 and 3, TERM will implement the following activities (1).The Rescue Mission (TERM) will ensure to conduct safety Audit prior to all its WASH activities programming. (2). Conduct community/Nutrition partners consultation meeting, (disaggregated by sex/age) consult with women and girls for the allocation of the sites for for construction/ rehabilitation of water points and toilets to mitigate gender based violence risks before designing/operationalizing the program/project and proposes the following activities to mitigate WASH related Humanitarian Crisis in the targeted locations, identify Nutrition facilities with no/limited WASH facilities. (3). Provision timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees, People with disability through Technical assessment of water points, water quality testing/monitoring and Construction 4 new and rehabilitation of 40 existing broken hand pumps and 1 new motorized water yards, flushing of selected boreholes to remove turbidity and treat borehole to improve water quality for 35000 individuals (Men 6000, Women 10,500, Boys 8750, girls 9750 in the affected communities. (4) Training of members as water management committee 7 members as water management committee, 7 per each borehole (Men 3, Women 4), (5) training of community pump mechanics to futher continue repair and maintenance of water points. (6) Nutrition workers and CHW/CNV will be trained on hygiene messages and support the operation and maintenance of provided WASH facilities at Nutrition sites. (7) SAM children and their care takers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women shall be targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services and NFIs distribution to reduce disease transmission at household level, total of 5500 individuals shall be targeted. (8) Protection mainstreaming and GBV mitigation measures will be mainstreamed throughout WASH programming. (9) Provision of sanitation cleaning materials to Nutrition centers with constructed/rehabilitated latrines. (10)Installation of 60 Hand washing facilities of 100 liters capacity to constructed/rehabilitated latrines to promote hand washing at Nutrition centers, health facilities, Schools, and public places. (11) Provision of latrine digging tools to the affected community and training of the community on how to construct the HH latrine using available local materials as an exit strategy. Undertake Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities. (12) Training of 40 Hygiene promoters (20men, 20women) and Conduct participatory hygiene promotion at community level with emphasis on disease prevention (WASH related disease and COVID) in the targeted areas of response by disseminating key hygiene messages in line with cultural practices of the community i.e. hand washing with soap or ash at critical times, water protection (safe water chain), domestic hygiene (food hygiene), environmental hygiene (safe excreta disposal and solid waste disposal) etc. The response will promote door to door visits and participatory hygiene sessions in public places to improve the knowledge on prevention and mitigation of WASH-related diseases to a target population of 48,645 individuals (Host 17,215, IDP’s 13,500, Returnees 12820, People with disabilities 5110).(Men 9,115, Women 15819, Boys 11,261, Girls 12450).(13)Distribution of MHM kits to 3,000 adolescent girls and 2,000 women of reproductive age group, . (14)Distribution of WASH NFI’s water treatment/purification products (PUR/Aqua tabs) buckets with lit with tap, bucket with lit without tap, soap, filter clothe promote household water treatment.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

To avoid exclusion and marginalization, all gender age groups will be given a room to participate in all consultations and decision-making platforms throughout the project beginning from assessment, baseline, project design, implementation and closer so as to be able to include diverse opinions and views of the beneficiaries. Mechanisms for collection of feedback and complaints should include, through suggestion box, telephone (where there is access) physical collection by protection staff and Monitoring and Evaluation staffTERM will consult separately with girls, boys, women and men (including older people and those with disabilities) to ensure that activities are designed to provide equitable access and reduce incidences of violence and exploitation. At the design level, the project will ensure that girls, boys, women and men (including older people and PWDs) have access to appropriate and safe services with risk analysis done prior to rehabilitation or establishment of WASH infrastructures, aid distribution events and public gathering etc . At the implementation level, the project will ensure that girls, boys, women and men (including older people and PWDs) have access to feedback and complaint mechanisms so that corrective actions can address their specific protection and assistance needs. TERM will ensure frequent collection of feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries through appropriate feedback collection mechanisms, such feedback shall be used to improve the design and the services being provided. The project staff will always carryout consultation with various groups among the beneficiaries to ensure no one is left out. During the project life cycle, a periodic monitoring, collection of feedback and complaints shall be established, this will help TERM to determine areas of improvement/adjustment. It also gives clear view of whether beneficiaries are comfortable with the provided assistance and whether they are satisfied with the quality and safety of carried activities. Outcomes from feedback and complaints shall be documented, lessons drawn and recommendations will be used to improve planning, design and implementation modality of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

TERM project team will conduct meetings with all the community groups, and will ensure the diversity in all those meetings to ensure Data for assessment, baseline and monitoring will be collected through institutions and HH survey, focus group discussions with representatives of various groups disaggregated by gender and age, people with disabilities, interviews with key informants, authorities and other implementing partners present in the project location.. Activities plans will be presented to the authorities, plans are discussed with the community. People are engaged as community-based hygiene promoters, water user committees' members, translators, enumerators. Regular meetings with the community representatives during intervention implementation will be organised to present plans of the activities and receive feedback on the implementation process, as well as suggestions for improvement. At all phases of the intervention, To ensure effective records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, TERM Project team will establish accessible complaint response feedback mechanisms such as the use of complaint forms, Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and will be used to adjust project activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TERM has a comprehensive PSEA policy that all staff are trained on and special consideration shall be made to have it applied to all the persons involved in the program implantation through training and emphasis on community awareness shall also be done on the information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The project team will conduct PSEA training for different groups of the community including Chiefs, Local authorities, IDPs, Returnees and host communities males and females from different age groups and people with disabilities shall be engage, The PSEA information is part of information campaign conducted at the beginning of the project implementation and TERM will ensure that project staffs are oriented about protection mainstreaming on the inclusion and ethical conduct, including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse focusing on the vulnerability of women and girls.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

TERM will treat all feedback it receives and uses it to inform the design of projects and activities where possible, the project team engage the community in decision making during the project life cycle right during planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, FGDs, KIIs and HH interviews will be conducted in order to clearly define out beneficiaries’ needs, consultation of beneficiaries of all age group (people living with disabilities women and girls) on type of assistant required.
