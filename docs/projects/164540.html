Project Summary

Shafak Organization proposes to mitigate/respond to the protection risks faced by the conflict-affected people in Daret Azza, and Azaz sub-districts in Aleppo governorate, as well as in Salqin, Dana and Idleb sub-districts in Idleb governorate through a community center and community outreach teams. The purpose of this project is to empower local communities and conflict-affected individuals in North West Syria, and support their role in building society and in determining their future, through the provision of protection services and emergency protection services to 57940 displaced people and host community at a community center and through mobile outreach teams. The provided protection services will comprise both mitigation and response services as well as specialized and non-specialized protection services. The project technical staff composed of 38 (Female and Male), will be providing the protection activities in Salqin and Idleb community centers and through eight mobile teams in Daret Azza, Dana and Azaz districts.
The proposed project will offer gender and age disaggregated general protection services, PSS/recreational activities, case management, Referral, awareness raising, vocational training, legal assistance and legal awareness raising sessions of
HLP and CSD, protection monitoring, and to community networks support. In case of emergencies, the project activities will be shifted to respond to the emergency protection needs of new displaced people delivering the protection emergency package like: PFA, referrals and case fund support to respond to the urgent needs of IDPs.
Training on HLP and CSD will be delivered to partners’ legal staff and community actors in Syria and Turkey by specialized legal staff in accordance with the protection cluster and HLP WTG guidance and SOPs. 400 legal staff and humanitarian actors will be trained on CSD and HLP.
These protection services will be offered to vulnerable IDPs in Salqin, and Idleb sub-districts via community centers, as well as Daret Azza, Azaz, Dana, sub-districts will be targeted through protection outreach activities. The proposed intervention will be implemented with particular emphasis on quality services to People with Disabilities (PwDs) and elderly women and men.
Shafak will consult with the community members at the beginning of the project to introduce the project activities and have them involved in the selection of the community centers locations, as well as Shafak will establish community committees from the same communities and train them on protection topics such as; CPMS, Safe identification and referral and communication skills to enable them identify and refer people in need to the case management team. Moreover, the community committees will play a significant role in raising protection issues that are faced by the community and mobilizing them to participate in project activities.
The activities will emphasize equity and non-discrimination, reinforcing the basic social and human rights of participants while taking into consideration the cultural, historical and social specificities of targeted populations.

4 - Needs

Civilians living in northwest Syria have been enduring a drastically worsening humanitarian situation as hostilities continue to intensify since November 2019. From 1 December 2019 to 27 January 2020, nearly 390,000 people – around 80% of whom are women and children – were displaced in northwest Syria.Protection risks have increased with the ongoing displacement, lack of shelter options and sub-standard living conditions in areas of displacement, specifically related to gender-based violence (GBV), child protection (CP), and exposure to explosive hazards. Overcrowding in IDP sites and makeshift shelters and a lack of WASH facilities places women and girls at particular risk of GBV. (Situation report No. 7) According to Draft HNO 2020: • Violence, protracted displacement, and extensive destruction of infrastructure and services has steadily eroded coping mechanisms of individuals and communities and disrupted community protection mechanisms. • Older people, in particular women, continue to have specific protection needs, including in accessing services, in securing personal documentation, and in reuniting with family members; they are prone to verbal violence and are often marginalized within by family members.• Despite the known vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities and older people, their needs are not always adequately taken into account in programming, resulting in barriers in accessing much needed assistance • Civil documentation, HLP issues and freedom of movement remain major and countrywide protection issues. Civil documentation was reported by respondents as occurring in all assessed communities and very commonly in a third of these communities. HLP and freedom of movement were reported as occurring in 60% and 58% of assessed communities respectively . The occurrence of these issues derives directly from the effects of hostilities and forced displacement.

Project activities will be delivered through the static community centers and through the outreach teams and will include:•Establish referral pathway in the area to support CM services. (service map to be updated on a monthly basis) by the outreach workers•Provide CM services for 600 cases, cases will be identified by outreach teams, and referrals from the community committees.•Provide Case fund support for 400 vulnerable able cases,100$ each. This service will complement the CM services and will be delivered by the Case workers. •Deliver structured-PSS activities (within the center and in the community by the PSS workers) for 3400 individuals. •Deliver non-structured PSS and PFA activities for 21,500 (4000 M, 5000 W, 6500 B, and 6000 G) through the centers and the mobile team by the PSS and outreach workers.•Provide referral services for 1200 cases, these cases will be identified through the project activates and referred to the available services based on the updated service map.•Conduct awareness raising session on Protection-related topics (GP, CP, GBV, RE) for 18,000 (2000 M, 4000 W, 6000 B, and 6000 G) through the centers and the mobile team. and 12000 for legal.•Provide legal counselling for 450 (225 M, 180 W, 11 M and 34 G), these cases will be identified by the legal mobile teams while delivering the legal awareness sessions.•Provide CSD and HLP training for 390 legal staff and humanitarian staff (195 men and 195 women). Legal counsellors will be responsible for delivering these trainings.•Implement protection monitoring assessment in cooperation with the Protection Monitoring Task Force (PMTF) in 30 communities. •Establishment/Support 16 Community Committees and train then on protection related topics.MEAL plan: MEAL activities will be conducted to measure the impact of the delivered activities through sessions feedback and pre/post-tests for PSS activities, in addition to applying the complaints mechanism including (complaints boxes,brochure)

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption (1): Local authorities do not interfere with the projectRisk (1): Local authorities decide to ban the implementation of the project.Mitigation (1): ongoing coordination and sensitization with the local councils/actors. Assumption (2): Security situation will not deteriorate.Risk (2): Shift in power balance, increased airstrikes and proximity to ground battles severely compromising access and implementation.Mitigation (2): Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. Set the response plan and designing the activities in a flexible way where Shafak can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation to achieve the objective, including the possible temporary suspension of activities. Assumption (3): Targeted population is willing to actively engage in activities.Risk (3): unwillingness or inability to participate in decision-making mechanisms due to cultural norms.Mitigation (3): building the trust of the population through awareness raising initiatives, leadership trainings and develop a sense of ownership to the project. Assumption (4): No tension occurs between communities due to the implementation of the project activities. Risk (4): Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served. Mitigating (4): 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed.2. Ensure consultation with communities and neighbouring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. 4. Support national actors and community groups to ensure sustainability of protection intervention.5. Established committees to enhance community based protection mechanisms.6. Ensure information sharing with community members on services available.
