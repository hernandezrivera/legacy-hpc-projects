4 - Needs

Telecommunications infrastructure throughout Syria has suffered significant damage due to nine years of the ongoing crisis. While Damascus maintains good network coverage, locations including Aleppo, Der-Ezzor, Hama, Homs, Qamishli, Tartous and Latakia have limited coverage. Lack of electricity is a challenge for the whole country and regular power outages impact the entire communication network in Syria.Security conditions have improved during the past year in parts of the country, particularly in the south of Syria, while a serious deterioration is reported in the North West and North East areas of the country. Although reliable connectivity remains a major challenge throughout Syria, telecommunications infrastructure and services are steadily improving. This is crucial for the humanitarian community to perform their work in operational areas of the field.Based on the assessment figures in the draft Humanitarians Needs Overviews (HNO), there may be a need for scale-up of Emergency Telecommunications Cluster (ETC) services in 2020, as UN agencies start to prepare for a possible scenario of large increases in their assistance to affected populations including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnees. The plan includes the setup of several additional UN hubs pending government approval, which will all require shared connectivity and security telecommunications services.There is a need for the continued provision of shared connectivity and security telecommunications services in the surrounding countries where humanitarian activities supporting operations inside Syria are ongoing.Contingency human and financial resources are needed to run ad hoc projects based on ongoing assessments.

5 - Implementation of activities

The overarching goal of the ETC is to ensure that humanitarian actors have access to vital telecommunications services that allow them to perform their work in common UN operational areas. Effective communications systems are also critical to support and enhance the safety and security of the humanitarian community. In the context of the Syrian crisis where the humanitarian response spans the neighboring countries, strong coordination is essential. Coordination provided by the ETC helps to remove duplication by encouraging humanitarian partners to share technical infrastructure in common UN operational areas, where feasible. In areas where there is limited access to telecommunications infrastructure, this coordination is particularly important in ensuring the availability of services to UN agencies and INGOs/NGOs including the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC). Recognizing the integral role of national humanitarian actors in the response in Syria, the ETC will provide support by assisting national staff with communication technology needs through guidance, information sharing and capacity building. In consultation with the response community, the following areas have been identified as the focus of the ETC response strategy in 2020:- Implement common Security Operation Centres (SOC) in new operational hubs;- Continue providing ETC connectivity/voice services in Aleppo, Der-Ezzor, Hama, Homs and Qamishli;- Deliver ETC security telecommunications technical training to local staff from UN agencies and partner NGOs;- Deliver ETC soft and technical IT capacity building trainings to local staff from UN agencies and partner NGOs;- Continue to provide support services to other agencies and SARC, namely a radio helpdesk and technical assistance, radio installations, radio training, radio programming and radio troubleshooting;- Maintain and expand the VHF/UHF radio network in common operational areas;- Support a humanitarian response scale up in new common operational

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In volatile environments, the provision of reliable inter-agency communication services is key for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In case of underfunding, the ETC will be unable to fulfil its mandate to provide all operational areas with vital communications services, which will seriously hamper humanitarian operations in Syria and will affect staff security in the field. With the shortfalls in 2018, the ETC couldn’t proceed with the replacement of faulty security communication equipment in Tartous neither could the infrastructure upgraded in Damascus to address security communication services coverage in the city Capacity building is a critical element in Syria as some areas where it is hard to send skilled international staff and entirely relay on national staff. Shortfall of fund restricted organizing training in 2018 for national staff.
