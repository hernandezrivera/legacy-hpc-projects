Project Summary

Kuwaiti patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to assist vulnerable population affected by conflict, displaced and malnutrition by supporting OTPs, TSFPs and SCs in North and South Darfur and Kassala States. The project proposed to support 93,781 direct beneficiaries including (men 7,251, women 37,593 boys 25,550 and girls 23,387) comprising of IDPs, returnees and host experiencing with displaced and malnutrition problem. Malnutrition remains a critical problem affecting the North and South Darfur and Kassala States. KPHF will provide health services through SCs and nutrition services through OTPs and TSFPs. Nutrition services to cover SAM and MAM cases for U5 and PLW which include screening at admission, medical treatment of severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children with and without medical complication, dietary management and individual child follow up, among others. It will also cover the administrative (support) cost of case management of moderate acute malnourished (MAM) girls and boys of 6-59months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Furthermore, nutrition education and counseling will be provided for mothers/caregivers in Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) practices and hygiene promotion as well as MSGs will built to facilitate of nutrition education and contribution in MUAC screening activities. Selected trainings will be provided on SC case management, CMAM, NiE, identification of medical complications at OTPs and TSFPs level, community screening and follow up. In addition to, women club, women cornea and community awareness session campaigns will be conducted under nutrition program.

Needs

The nutrition need continue to persist in North, South Darfur and Kassala States on the background of low coverage and access of nutrition services and inadequate response capacities. Malnutrition remain major cause of morbidity and mortality among children under five and PLW in above mentioned areas, particularly among vulnerable population. The nutrition services in Kassala state fragile and unable to provide essential life-saving services and respond to malnutrition situation. Poor socioeconomic status &food insecurity contribute to malnutrition rates in these localities, together with micronutrient deficiencies &lack of Vitamin A, iron, zinc &iodine. The Simple Spatial Surveying Method (S3M) 2013 shows high rates of malnutrition, with a GAM rate of 15.8%, SAM rate of 54%, & Oedema prevalence of 1.3% which are all well above WHO emergency thresholds. Distribution of GAM prevalence based on MUAC follows a similar pattern to WHZ, and areas with high GAM by MUAC include North & South Darfur and Kassala. Data from MUAC mass screening conducted by MoH in North &South Darfur and Kassala all have revealed that the malnutrition rates remained between 15.9%-36.0% for GAM while SAM rates were in the range of 2.5% &10.1%, above emergency thresholds in both localities (Tawella & Dareslam in ND,Nyala North ,Tulus and Kubum in SD and Hamshkoreb in Kassala, also The number of children with SAM depends on the population number, highly populated states with a lower prevalence still carry a very high burden of children with SAM. Most of the children with SAM are found in these above mentioned areas and other States these States carry 51% of the total SAM burden in Sudan. A household survey conducted by KPHF in above mentioned localities revealed a very poor knowledge on Infant &Young Child Feeding practices, in addition to poor sanitation practices & a very limited coverage of community nutrition preventive package. The report indicated that in North and South Darfur and Kassala, 92% of mothers begin breastfeeding within the first hour after delivery, but only 38% of mothers subsequently breastfeed their children exclusively, 24% of mothers prepare a separate meal for their children as a supplementary food & only 47% of children take meals three times a day. Moreover, lactating mothers who become pregnant report that they immediately stop breastfeeding, even if their baby is less than 12 months of age, due to a deep-rooted belief that a pregnant mother's breast milk is harmful & causes diarrhea. These above elaborated dismal nutrition situation requires urgent intervention to fill this gap and address the malnutrition status among children U5 and PLW.

Activities

• Rehabilitation of OTPs, TSFPs sand SCs sites through using local environmental friendly materials and stabilized soil picks. • Conduct Community mobilization and sensitization for targeted communities on MUAC screening, active case finding and referral of acutely cases to OTPs, TSFPs and SCs.• Conduct different community awareness sessions campaigns on IYCF practices, maternal nutrition and other related nutrition topics.• Conduct regular counseling and BCC messages on infant and young child feeding (IYCF).• Conduct regular MUAC screening campaigns in low coverage areas. • Conduct training workshop on CMAM protocol, NiE, SBCC and database management for project staff.• Conduct weekly focused nutrition education group sessions for male and female caregivers at OTPs, SFCs, SCs, & PHC clinics. Sessions in the clinic will include topics such as: IYCF practices, diversification of the diet with local foods, micronutrient supplementation, nutrition for a sick child, etc. In addition to establishment of MSGs.• Disseminate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials with key standardized nutrition messages.• Equipping and supplying all supported centers with medical supplies and non-medical supplies (staff, U5 drugs, RUTF, RUSF, F75, medical equipment’s, furniture, washing soap and disinfectant materials in the targeted areas.• In collaboration with SMoH KPHF will contribute in commemorate National breastfeeding week.• Provide supplementary food rations to children aged Under 5 years, pregnant and lactating mothers in SFC for treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition.• Provide therapeutic food rations to children aged under 5 years in Stabilization Center for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (with medical complications).• Provide therapeutic food rations to girls and boys aged under 5 years in OTP for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (without medical complications).• Provide treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in TSFPs.
