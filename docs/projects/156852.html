Needs

The 2018 HNO reports over 14.6 million Syrians have water, sanitation and hygiene needs, including 7.6 million people with acute WASH needs. With public water systems operating at less than their pre-conflict capacity and unable to ensure the same water quality, lack of access to and limited availability of safe water, both in terms of quantity and quality, has continued to affect Syrians disproportionately. Up to 35% of the population relies on unsafe water sources to meet their daily water supply needs.The 2018 HNO reports that PiN in Daraa are 599564, PiN in Aleppo are 2250806 and PiN in Rural Damascus are 2348448. Poor hygiene conditions lead to disease outbreaks, especially in areas of high concentration of IDPs and camps. Individuals and communities remain challenged in their efforts to cope with the crisis, often having to resort to harmful coping mechanisms to survive. The conflict has had a particular impact on children. Two thirds of schools have been damaged or destroyed The HNO further highlights that WASH infrastructures in school need to be addressed. As a middle-income country, critical hygiene practices in Syria were satisfactory prior to the conflict, but lessons learned have shown that in contexts of displacement, ongoing reinforcement of key information is required. Similarly, provision of hygiene supplies positively complements good hygiene practices. Health conditions in the targeted areas are further exacerbated by bad hygiene practices; according to HNO 2018 WASH services and hygiene supplies are available but are relatively expensive. Considering the range and varying cleanliness of water sources being used (public tap 26%, surface water 16%, piped outside house 14%, piped inside house 14%, borehole 9%, bottled water 18% [6]), a hygiene intervention is required, especially considering expected population flows into the area. IDPs and returnees are not able to purchase and sustain basic hygiene practices. Displaced populations travel long distances with limited access to basic services including water and key WASH NFIs. IDPs have fled their homes due to escalating hostilities in search of safer communities and are in dire need of access to safe drinking water and basic WASH NFIs. Increased incidences of Waterborne and excreta-related disease and transmission mechanisms in the area of intervention (diarrhoea, typhoid, amoebic dysentery, hepatitis A, scabies, skin and eye infection etc.) have also been reported with the increasing population of IDPs and returnees having increasing unmet needs. According to the 2018 HNO six priority groups were identified as the most vulnerable and require specific consideration when planning and prioritizing the response, Children are especially vulnerable, particularly those who are unaccompanied and separated or living with older/disabled caregivers or out of school. Children engaging in child labour or recruited for military purposes will require specific consideration, People with chronic illness, disabilities and injuries have specific needs which cut across sectors including WASH and may face difficulties in accessing WASH services, Women and girls may face particular risks due to their gender and situation, including harmful survival mechanisms such as early marriage, trafficking and sexual abuse and exploitation. Unaccompanied girls, pregnant and lactating women and female-headed households all have specific needs which require consideration for accessing services and facilities, participation, empowerment and safely accessible feedback systems EPDC current expertise, capacity and operational presence: - In Aleppo EPDC provided 2,000 HH with access to sufficient and safe water for domestic use and 250 HH with hygiene promotion activities- In RD EPDC rehabilitated 100 HH sanitation systems, rehabilitated 150 M river canal and hygiene awareness session for 400 beneficiaries.

Implementation of activities

The project will complement EPDC health interventions in Aleppo, Rural Damascus and Dar'a with provision of WASH supplies and services PHC centers and HH level, as well as hygiene promotion and distribution of hygiene kits and aquatabs to the most vulnerable HHs in the selected areas. These activities aim to reduce the incidence of poor hygiene practices and conditions that pose a threat to the health of vulnerable children, men and women. Result 1: Repair and rehabilitation of water systems and Support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems Activity 1.1: Rehabilitation of water system The project will aim at rehabilitating water system in one of the governorates, supporting an estimated number of 25,000 people in the area. EPDC will ensure that the water system become functional and well maintained through the provision of generators, pumps, electromechanical parts, chlorination and water testing, depending on the initial assessment. Routine maintenance, repairs, and the provision of spare parts will also be a critical component to ensure that the water systems remain functional. Result 2: Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion Activity 2.1: Distribution of hygiene kits to vulnerable populationMost vulnerable HH in the area of intervention and project intervention will be provided with hygiene kits and aquatabs on monthly basis (estimated 1,500HH/area). These activities aim to reduce the incidence of poor hygiene practices and conditions that pose a threat to the health of vulnerable children, men and women, in parallel with hygiene promotion and other sector's activities. Kits composition will be based on cluster content requirement, and vulnerability selection criteria will be set up by EPDC M&E department to ensure the most in need are reached. Activity 2.2: Hygiene promotion in 10 public schools, 5 non-formal education centers and PHC centersHygiene promotion activities will be provided to 12000 children attending the Education and Child Protection activities in centers in Dar'a and Rural Damascus, in line with international models for hygiene promotion. Hygiene promoters will conduct hygiene promotion sessions to raise awareness on water borne diseases and enhance prevention mechanisms, focusing on the most vulnerable groups, such as children under-five years, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly and households with critically ill family members. Key messages covered include proper hand washing, personal hygiene, proper disposal of solid waste, and safe water storage, among others. Subjects the sessions cover will be adjusted in order to include particularly important information as needed, such as if a waterborne disease has recently broken out.The project will also provide hygiene promotion sessions targeting adults pregnant and lactating women and most vulnerable heads of HHs in Aleppo, Rural Damascus and Dar'a through the 4 established PHC centers. Activity 2.3: Improved access to emergency WASH facilitiesThe project will include rehabilitation of 4 PHC center (Aleppo, Dar'a and Rural Damascus) as well as 800 facilities at HH level (estimated 200HH/location). The project will include a gender sensitive approach. WASH Rehabilitation of 5 non-formal centers and 10 public schools will include separate and accessible latrines and washrooms, separated by and suitable for each gender. The project activities are comprehensively inclusive for the children and their caregivers in the areas of operations. For WASH facilities’ rehabilitation at the HH level, selection will be based on a vulnerability criteria created in outlying a point system based on HH size, current support of family/caretaker, sex of head of HH, type of water source, hand wash accessibility and latrine access, and current hygiene of WASH facility with an ultimate priority to a PWD (physical disability)

Protection Risk Analysis

WASH is a valuable intervention thus; tensions can run high when interventions are made. Vulnerable individuals and groups may be unable to obtain their entitlement, or may have it taken from them by force. EPDC will be assessing in advance and adopt mitigation measures, in terms of selection criteria and to minimize the risks, these should include adequate supervision of selection and service delivery, the involvement of local authorities might be required. Measures to prevent, monitor and respond to gender-based violence or sexual exploitation associated with service delivery will be considered. Moreover, Sexual activity cannot be requested in exchange for humanitarian assistance and EPDC must NOT be party to any such forms of exchange. EPDC follows SPHERE standards for safe and confidential complaints and response mechanisms would also be in place to respond to any reported incidents of intimidation, violence and sexual exploitation (that have been committed by community members and/or staff). Gender balance of assessment teams will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the distribution areas. In addition, it may be advisable to rotate distribution and assessment teams so that no one team constantly visits the same community. This will reduce the potential for exploitation by staff, as well as providing a greater opportunity for protection issues to be picked up by a broader range of staff with different expertise. Emergency sanitation: Ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned. Support to water supply systems: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time;

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:● Conflict lines shift due to conflict activities, affecting the safety/mobility of program staff and areas of proposed intervention● Loss of support from the local governing authorities or councils● Beneficiaries do not want to participate in the project● Duplication or gaps in activities due to miscommunication amongst humanitarian agencies and stakeholders● High fluctuations in prices of items needed for the project as the waring context escalates● Limited availability of medicines ● Delays with distributions caused by checkpoints/delays in obtaining necessary approvals● Lack of full time medical staff and specialization Assumptions:● The local security situation remains stable within this current time and beneficiaries will not face large scale displacements from the target areas.● The local governing authorities support the project in a continuous manner● Coordination and communication lines with other agencies, other services and with local authorities stay in place● Acceptance and engagement of targeted communities. It is assumed that through investment of resources, sound project design and use of appropriate techniques, the benefits of the action will be accepted by the community. Mitigation:• EPDC will implement a robust contingency plan to fluidly relocate selected vulnerable project locations in the event of a shift in security context which could compromise the selected areas.• EPDC will continue to utilize their strong relationship and reputation with the Ministry of Health and Directorate of Health to identify other opportunities although the risk is deemed very low.• EPDC will meet local government officials on a regular basis and update them on the progress of activity implementation. They will also involve them if there are any modifications from the initial plan.• EPDC will disseminate information about projects within the community and will also organize community events to build acceptance and provide services, in coordination with local authorities.• EPDC will remain an active member of the coordination system at the national level led by UN OCHA. At the local level, EPDC will continue to coordinate with humanitarian actors already implementing projects in the areas of operation.• EPDC continues to strengthen its on-site Field Monitoring capacity to ensure a standardized but flexible approach for verifying programmatic and operational aspects of each hub-level program
