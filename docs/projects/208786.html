Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in North-east Nigeria is now in its eleventh year with no end in sight. Incessant attacks from non-state armed groups (NSAG), coupled with counter-insurgency operations by the military has led to a never-ending cycle of violence and displacements and creating new emergencies in what is already a prolonged emergency situation. Despite a significant scale-up of humanitarian response since 2016, the crisis continues, and significant gaps exist.
Novel Alliance for Development Aid (NADA) prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm, we will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention process as we equally ensure meaningful access by arranging for people’s access to assistance and services. The organization will make sure that the capacity for self-protection is provided through capacity building and assistance to claim rights, including but not exclusive to health, food, shelter, WASH and education
The HRS puts the number of people in need of protection and assistance in the three most affected states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) at 7.1 million - including 1.8 million IDPs – and to 3 million the number of people at risk of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) in the three states. The project will consider the following; door-to-door sensitization on GBV, SGBV and SEAH prevention and reporting within IDP camps and host communities of target locations, community GBV volunteers will be trained on SGBV/GBV core concepts, navigating social norms affecting GBV, GBV referral pathways and prevention of sexual abuse amongst women and girls of reproductive age have increased access to high quality standardized dignity kits and GBV/SGBV services, women and girls will also have improved access to case management and other GBV services.

1 - Needs

According to the Global Protection Cluster, the conflict between the Government of Nigeria (GoN) and NonState Armed Groups (NSAGs) in North-East Nigeria enters its 12th year, the people in the states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (referred to as the BAY states) continue to face widespread insecurity and ongoing military operations, damaged infrastructure, tattered local economies, loss of livelihoods and gross violations of their human rights by all parties to the conflict. The COVID-19 pandemic and ever deteriorating global food security have exacerbated already precarious conditions, leaving a projected 8.4 million people across the BAY states in need of humanitarian assistance and protection in 2022. Among them, 4.2 million people live in Borno state alone with around 1.6 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) out of the 2.2 million persons displaced across the three North-Eastern BAY states. 1 Insecurity and resultant restrictions imposed by the Borno State Government (BSG) on movements make humanitarian operations both dangerous and difficult. Humanitarian actors cannot reach an estimated 1.1 million people in need who reside in inaccessible areas, while humanitarian operations mostly focus on the state capital, Maiduguri, and garrison towns in Local Government Areas (LGAs). As displaced, returning and host communities are all experiencing critical needs across all sectors, the inaccessible areas in Northern Borno, where food security levels are likely to have reached famine levels, are of highest concern, the camp closures in Maiduguri Metropolitan Centre (MMC) and Jere LGAs with enforced relocations and returns to various areas across Borno, food insecurity and restrictions related to humanitarian food distributions, as well as the curtailment of the freedom of movement. Is even making the displacement situation complex

2 - Activities

The mode of operation of NADA will allow monitoring within camps, in rural areas or in urban settings. While always based on the same international standards, we will also focus on certain aspects of any given situation. For example, within camps, our monitoring will examine distribution systems, the physical locations and layout of camps, health facilities, the situation of vulnerable groups, physical protection, including sexual and gender-based violence and abuse, and issues related to psychological assistance and social welfare. NADA will also cover the various phases of the displacement cycle and include persons already displaced, persons at risk of displacement, returnees, and affected host populations. Different monitoring approaches may be required to monitor these different types of populations. No one activity or tool for monitoring is appropriate for all situations; yet, the activity or tool used to gather specific pieces of information will be clearly recorded in each situation. That will make it easier to verify the data collected and to confirm that the tool used successfully captured the protection concerns specific to the situation. Novel Alliance for Development Aid (NADA) will be sure of the fact that staff members are independent and neutral, and conduct with respect for the do-no-harm concept. Monitors must respect the principle of confidentiality, protect their sources, be sensitive to the feelings of victims and witnesses, and not make promises they cannot fulfil. The field staff members will respect the proper functioning of national and local authorities and seek ways to support local capacity building.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NADA shall consider differentiated needs women, men, girls, and boys may have and give attention to persons with special needs, such as elderly, disabled or chronically ill people. Disaggregated data is key. Protection considerations, notably the principles of do no harm and non-discrimination will be adhered to during assessments, programme design, while implementing as well as when monitoring and evaluating the project. Thus, NADA will prioritize, Safety & security, including land tenure security, and dignity; meaningful access, including for women, children, persons with disabilities or other persons with special needs. NADA shall work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches shall align with the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk in compliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle. Also, NADA shall be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero sexual exploitation and abuse. All staff, volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project shall be trained on PSEA. They shall be familiarized with FSACI's policy on PSEA, and they must express their unwavering commitment by signing the NADA’s PSEA code of conduct. Also, NADA shall also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately, they occur. NADA shall also ensure that the identity of the reporters is kept very confidential. When convenient, NADA shall encourage that reports are made on anonymous grounds.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

This project is designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and based on lessons learnt from our previous implementation in the target LGAs. NADA shall use a people-centered feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach will be used to form a LGA level project committee that will include all community groups' representative and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process. NADA project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the already existed community-based project structures to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. NADA will also use a feedback mechanism through focus group discussions to gather perceptions of the project beneficiaries and community members on the services rendered which will form part of the discussion in the project implementation review meeting with the community-based project committees at the end of the project.
