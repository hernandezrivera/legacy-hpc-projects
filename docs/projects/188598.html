Project Summary

The project goal is to response to the core needs of the vulnerable displaced families and host communities through provision of water tanks which will lead to have access to clean water and raise the people hygienic conditions, and will provide long-term sustainable resilience to those most in need for WASH assistance. The targeted beneficiaries are those who have been forced from their homes in Ghweran neighborhood of Hassakeh city (3,500 HH of 21,000 Inds, out of which around 1,500 HH of 9,000 inds are still living in collective shelters in Hassakeh city (Security square) who are unable to return back to their homes for many reasons mainly their home either partially or totally destroyed or/and due fear of persecution and unable or unwilling to avail themselves of that neighborhood. As well, the targeted beneficiaries are those who have been forced from their homes in Ras Al Ain and its countryside, where around 18,000 HHs comprises around 126,000 people have fled to Hassakeh city and to Tal Tamer town and its countryside, in which they become under further pressure, who have been already exhausted due the Syrian crisis which enters its eleventh year, where the displaced families and the host communities continue to bear the burden of ongoing conflict which marked by unapparelled suffering due to the shortage of humanitarian support which they are in need to urgent humanitarian assistance and services. This intervention aims to provide effective humanitarian assistance to meet the immediate survival needs of WASH materials of the most extremely vulnerable people including women and children especially children less five years old to water-borne infections and other diseases related to personal hygiene. The unavailability of, or lack of access to a continuous safe water source put everyone at risk of disease transmission. the adopted criteria stipulates the targeted families will be poor female headed families, poor widows, poor divorced women with her children, people with disabilities and families without incomes who have been originated from Ghweran and Ras Al Ain areas as well to support the host communities who have been accommodating displaced families.
The targeted IDPs and locations has been selected from the database available at the association in close coordination with DoW, DoSA, and SARC.
In this regard, since mid-2013, Allouk water plant in Ras Al Ain city, around 80 km away in the northern countryside of Hassakeh, is considered the only source for provision of drinking water for the population including the IDPs in Hassakeh city and Tel Tamer town and surrounding settlements.
Allouk water station has completely stopped for the twenty times in a row over the last two years depriving more than one million of people including the IDPs from getting their needs of potable water, where the population as a last resort, despite the available ground water has been unsafe, started digging surface well (from 10-70 m depth) to get their needs of water which led to negatively impacts on the health in particular the sick and children including children L5, women and other vulnerable groups.
This proposal has been designed mainly in order to provide only water tanks to increase the capacity of household storage and complete WASH projects to making it better which serving as a complement to trucking potable water, hygiene kits, submersible repair projects that provided and implemented by other local humanitarian actors in the intervention areas. The reason behind this proposal has been as the most displaced families have no water containers or they have old and dirty small ones, the proposal will focus on WASH sector with longer-term solution with proper effective exit strategy, as it is the main demands of the crisis affected people as well the host communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented in 2022 and 2023 by an ad hoc distribution committee composed by Association qualified field staff, and representatives of qualified beneficiaries including women, local community and local municipality at the areas of interventions, DoSAL and SARC and UN agency partner. The distribution operations will adhere to the workplan and timelines as of the PCA of 2022 is expected to start at the beginning of next July and ended in March 2023 in case the required formalities have been completed. Regarding exit strategy, Since humanitarian projects are intended to be temporary interventions responding to the needs of populations affected by crises, project closure is an inescapable component of humanitarian aid. To do so, BICA, start of planning, communicating clearly, setting hard deadlines for exit time, and going unconditionally.BICA is working to achieve Sustainability on the extent to which an evidence-based intervention can deliver its intended benefits over s period after external support is terminated. BICA works to achieve sustainability to meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of beneficiaries to meet theirs. through three main pillars: economic, environmental, and social. where, the modality of a distribution is determined by the number of peaks it contains. BICA prefers to distribute and hand over the water tanks directly to eligible females, to avoid males from might sell them to traders.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Inequality in distribution: Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV.Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating Measure: Al Bir Wa El Ehsan Association (BICA) will promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff.Risk of theft of NFIs: Likelihood: High. Mitigating Measure: BICA provides feedback and accountability mechanisms to different groups of users; work closely with all partners on the ground; promotes distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).Gender insensitive contents varieties; of kits with inconsistent items.: Likelihood: High. BICA ensures harmonies and standardizes gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicates quantity and instruction for use; hygiene promotion. Distribution of expired unhealthy items: Likelihood: Medium. BICA will establish and monitor expiry date of supplies prepositioned in warehouse, ensure relevant storage provision and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. Strengthen end user monitoring.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

BICA is committed to mainstreaming the protection considerations at all stages of the project cycle. The project is deigned to make the concerns and experiences of women, girls, boys and men an integral dimension of it. BICA field team will select community volunteers from both genders and rely on their feedback and inputs throughout the implementation period. Men and women will also be separately consulted with regards to the day-to-day logistical considerations, such as regarding the most appropriate locations for gender-segregated distributions points. As a result, women and men have an equal the right and opportunity to participate in decision-making. The risks of PSEA will be identified through publicly discuss and address ongoing violence where men and women will be given a chance to express their different concerns, experiences, needs and capabilities through gender-age including persons with disabilities and survivors of GBV as identified. The assessment phase will highlight different gender- age specific gaps, such as unequal access assistance and services for men and women, in addition to likely protection risks for women, boys and girls. And as prevention measures that: 1. Employees sign code of conduct related to sexual exploitation. 2. Training of employees on procedures related to sexual and material exploitation. 3. Posters that include the complaints numbers. 4. Assigning a specialized team to follow up and verify complaints in order to respond immediately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security risk: the security is unpredictable although the areas of intervention is enjoying calm security situation, Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: BICA closely monitors the changes in the overall security situation in targeted areas and adjust accordingly its operations, adapting its protocols, systems and contingency plans, to minimize safety and security risks for its personnel and beneficiaries, and ensure safe transportation and storage of goods. BICA engages community leaders, authorities in order to determine the most suitable and safe locations for distribution, and to ensure that all target beneficiaries are informed including people with disabilities about distribution dates and locations. BICA ensures that distribution points are accessible to the most vulnerable including women and persons with disabilities. Fraud risk: Aid diversion, fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of supplier, triangulation of information, standard monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments tables updated and followed. Financial risk: Substantive devaluation of USD against the Syrian pound. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: The eventuality of significant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be closely monitored and, if materialized, timely communicated with OCHA office in Qamishli and options discussed.
