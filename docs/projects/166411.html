Project Summary

By 29th April 2020, from the onset of the infectious Corona virus disease (COVID-19), 3,018,952 confirmed cases and 207 973 deaths were reported globally, with the WHO Africa Region reporting 23,254 confirmed cases, and 903 deaths . In Nigeria, as of 30th April 2020, 1,932 confirmed cases were reported, with 58 deaths. Of these cases in Nigeria, 66 confirmed cases and 6 deaths were reported in Borno state, 2 confirmed cases in Adamawa state and 1 confirmed case in Yobe state . UNICEF’s water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) humanitarian response in northeast Nigeria, which plays two roles: as service provider; and as the provider of last resort (POLR), is going to be adapted to ensure critical services are continued, while incorporating new interventions, as well as modifying on-going activities to contribute to COVID-19 transmission risk reduction.
UNICEF’s interventions will have specific target IDP camps in Maiduguri Metropolitan City and 10 LGAs (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Jere, Kara Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Mobbar, Monguno, and Ngala), targeted under the on-going HRP. In addition to these locations, UNICEF’s WASH support will also target the whole HRP PIN, by supporting the WASH sector coordination, as well as making available critical supplies as POLR to address sector gaps across the 3 BAY states. UNICEF will continues to adhere to the SPHERE standards in the humanitarian WASH services it provides, and scale up the sustenance of the services as needed.

1 - Needs

WASH Sector’s COVID-19 response target is 771,428 people, of which UNICEF is targeting to reach an estimated 540,000 people (70% of sector target) with WASH emergency supplies, such as COVID-19 adapted Hygiene kits and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) supplies to Health Care Facilities (HCFs) and isolation centres. Out of this projected target, UNICEF will also reach 50% of the sector target (385,714 people) with safe water supplies and hygiene promotion services in the BAY states. Key WASH Services Gap AnalysisAccording to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 an estimated 3,577,800 people need humanitarian intervention related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in Northeast Nigeria. The WASH sector targeted 2,394,020 individuals to provide water, sanitation and hygiene services in Northeast Nigeria in 2020 and UNICEF planned to respond to 1,126,800 people with a variable number of beneficiaries for different activities, according to assessed needs and vulnerabilities. UNICEF HRP project for 2020 is to address approximately 50 percent of the sector targets. The needs of the beneficiaries are variable by age group and by gender. For instance, children’s potties are part of the hygiene kits to address the needs of young children who are not able to use the standard latrines. Further to these needs, the novel COVID-19 is going to worsen the targeted communities’ vulnerabilities. COVID-19 will disrupt social and community systems since physical distancing is encouraged. Hygiene promotion and community engagement remain key components of the outbreak response to prevent the spread of disease. It is important to understand and address community perceptions and beliefs, as some of them can support and others can hinder a response. Some social norms may need to be modified to prevent disease transmission. For example, work with the community to find alternative forms of greeting to replace handshaking. Focus on the immediate public health risk and build trust and accountability with the communities. Prioritize response based on epidemiological findings, assessment of risk factors, transmission routes. For a community that mostly receives less than 15 litres of water per person per day, the increased demand for water and soap to clean hands will pose a huge burden. Due to increased demand to use soap and water, negative coping mechanisms may flourish, which can present a false sense of protection against COVID-19.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement four key activities through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts, Small Scale Funding Agreements (SSFA) or Project Cooperation Agreements (PCA) with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State Govts. This project consolidates UNICEF capacity as a sector partner implementing in allocated locations/camps in MMC, Jere, Dikwa, Mafa, Gwoza/Pulka, Rann, Konduga, where UNICEF will reach SPHERE standards; and in isolation centers in Adamawa and Yobe States. Additional resources will be needed for UNICEF to play the second role of provider of last resort (POLR) where there is no other capacity from the sector. 2.1 WASH NFI Supplies (hygiene kits, soap, sprayers, chlorine, disinfectant, hand washing facilities)UNICEF will procure critical WASH supplies for COVID-19 response, to be distributed to 54 Health Care Facilities/isolation centres, to support transmission prevention initiatives. UNICEF will directly reach 330,000 people (55,000 households) with household hand hygiene kit, comprised of a rigid jeri-can with handle to construct tippy tap and soap for hand washing. A total of 540,000 people (70% of WASH sector target population) will be reached indirectly through WASH Infection Prevention and Control supplies in health facilities and supplies that will go to the WASH Sector common pipeline, to be accessed and used by all partners to fill gaps across the 3 BAY states. 2.2 Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and Personal Protective EquipmentUNICEF will make available specific supplies such as latrine cleaning kits, disinfection chemicals, mainly HTH, items for installation of hand washing facilities (buckets, 50 – 100l plastic drums with tap and lid and rigid jeri-cans with handles for tippy taps), protective clothing, sprayers, and soap for hand washing purposes to affected and at-risk communities, Health Care Facilities (HCFs) and isolation centres established during the response. UNICEF will also have an assigned staff member to participate in the EOC IPC pillar for the COVID-19 response. 2.3 Infrastructure improvement (water points and latrines)UNICEF will conduct emergency rehabilitation/upgrading and/or construction of new safe water supplies and sanitation facilities in affected and at risk HCFs, Nutrition rehabilitation centres and communities, to improve access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities for the affected population. Latrine construction, upgrading or rehabilitation will include the installation of handwashing facilities and the promotion of tippy taps use within communities. UNICEF will also provide support for capacity strengthening of LGA level structures for O&M of water supply and sanitation systems. Availability of safe drinking water supplies will also be complimented by emergency water trucking to cover critical gaps in affected Isolation/HCFs and communities through Long Term Agreement (LTA) contractors. On-going latrine desludging services will be maintained, and UNICEF will also have the capacity for emergency latrine desludging in affected and at-risk Isolation/HCFs and communities through LTA contractors. 2.4 Risk communication and community engagement (Hygiene Promotion, IEC materials)UNICEF will support the Training of Trainers (ToT) for implementing Partners’ Focal persons on COVID-19 prevention and control communication, in collaboration with C4D. The training will be cascaded to the training of Community Based Hygiene Promotion Volunteers, Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), Traditional and Religious Leaders on COVID messaging. In collaboration with C4D and the EOC Risk Communication Pillar, UNICEF will support the production of IEC materials on COVID 19, for use by partners in risk communication activities. Mass awareness campaigns using community radios and mobile Broadcasts will also be conducted.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNICEF over the years has worked closely with community leaders and community-based volunteers and network groups in the design, implementation and monitoring of services including taking feedback from these groups for improvement in service delivery. During this funding window, similar consultations and experiences from all were considered for the designing of this project. The project will build a strong community ownership with implementation and monitoring being done jointly with the community hygiene volunteers and the Camp Coordination and Camp Management on one hand and the traditional leaders recognized by the beneficiaries on the other hand who will serve as community liaison on the project providing community feedback through their traditional community meetings. The project will also collaborate with U-report to harvest information and provide feedback to the user through SMS
