Project Summary

The provision of health care inside Syria remains hindered by the widespread damage and destruction of health facilities, insufficient human resources and access constraints in some areas. While the general cost of living, including of health services, has dramatically increased since 2011, the ability of public and private hospitals and clinics to respond to the needs of the population has decreased. As a result, the vast majority of Palestine refugees in Syria have become increasingly dependent on UNRWA for the delivery of healthcare services.
In 2020, UNRWA will continue to deliver quality primary health services through 17 health clinics, six health points operating in areas of displacement of Palestine refugees and two mobile clinics which are deployed according to needs. UNRWA health facilities provide a range of primary medical services, including out-patient consultations, immunization services, growth monitoring, provision of medicine, gynecology consultations, laboratory services, non-communicable disease care, maternal and child health care, dental and MHPSS services.
Furthermore, the Agency provides support to Palestine refugees seeking secondary and tertiary health care through a system of referrals to public, private and NGO hospitals. UNRWA subsidizes 75-95 per cent of the total cost of treatment for referred patients, depending on the socio-economic status of the patient. UNRWA will continue to ensure that patients have adequate access to essential medical supplies and drugs,and will strive to maintain contingency stocks of medical supplies and drugs to address any emerging situation.
Finally, the Agency will focus its efforts on the resumption of health care services in newly accessible areas and in areas of spontaneous return. Those areas will include the South (Dera’a camp, Muzeirib, Jillin) and the South Damascus area where accessible (Yarmouk/Yalda) as well as main gatherings, depending on access and based on needs. Should funding become available, UNRWA will also reconstruct its health facilities that have been completely destroyed by the hostilities in selected locations to ensure that Palestine refugees have access to services. The Agency will continue to deploy its mobile clinics, to sustain the provision of medical and dental services to refugees in areas where facilities are not fully operational. UNRWA will also equip rehabilitated health centres with confidential spaces for patients facing protection concerns, including cases of gender-based violence (GBV) and violence against children.
UNRWA has an internal Results-Based Monitoring system, with indicators tracking the progress of activities and impact of interventions for all programmes, including the Health Programme. Data is entered on a monthly basis by focal points, and Quarterly Monitoring Review meetings are conducted to analyze the data, measure achievements, and address shortcomings and challenges. In addition, mid-year results review and annual results review meetings are held with the involvement of implementing programmes and management at Field and HQ levels to ensure lessons are extracted and fed back into the design of programmes and strategies. Regular field visits are carried out by programme senior staff to monitor day-to-day operations and delivery of service. Third-party-monitoring exercises and external evaluations are also conducted periodically in coordination with donors and the Evaluation Division of HQ.

4 - Needs

While the conﬂict has subsided in parts of the country during 2019, hostilities continue in other areas, resulting in further loss of life, injury and displacement. Palestine refugees (PR) are amongst the worst affected by the conflict. Of the 438,000 PR currently estimated to be in Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conﬂict. Approximately 40% continue to be Internally displaced, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and EinElTal camps largely destroyed. While the general cost of living, including of health services, has dramatically increased since 2011, the ability of public and private hospitals and clinics to respond to the needs of the population has decreased. The majority of PR have become increasingly dependent on UNRWA for the delivery of healthcare services. An UNRWA vulnerability assessment (end of 2017) found that 96.3% of PR do not have any kind of medical insurance other than UNRWA services. Access to humanitarian life-saving reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health services are particularly critical for vulnerable groups, as pregnant women, newborns, and those under GBV risk. The conﬂict damaged many Agency facilities, with 29% of health facilities that become unusable necessitating reconstruction and rehabilitation works. Dera’a, EinElTal and Yarmouk camps were severely affected by conﬂict with UNRWA health facilities remaining substantially damaged. In Dera’a camp, UNRWA has witnessed the return of approximately 800 families and in 2020 health needs are expected to increase and resuming basic services in the camp is expected to be a priority. Rehabilitation of UNRWA clinic in EinElTal camp where families have started returning, despite the lack of infrastructure service, is also necessary to ensure the provision of health services in the camp. In line with the overall humanitarian needs in Syria, mental health and psychosocial support needs are acute among PR due to prolonged displacement and exposure to violence.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will delivery primary health services to up to 438,000 PR in Syria through 17 Health Clinics offering the full range of Agency services, 6 Health Points operating in areas of displacement of PR and two mobile health clinics deployed according to needs. Throughout 2020, UNRWA will provide primary medical services, including immunization services, maternal and child health through its installations and medical staff in Syria. UNRWA will also offer secondary and tertiary health care through a system of referrals to public hospitals. UNRWA will subsidize 75-95% of the total cost of treatment for referred patients, depending on their socioeconomic status. UNRWA is a direct implementer and service provider, and deploys its own staff, including medical doctors, nurses, lab technicians, pharmacists among others and delivers drugs and lab tests through its clinics. During 2020, UNRWA will prioritize the rehabilitation of UNRWA Health Centers in Dera’a and Ein el Tal camp to ensure the resumption of health services in the camps and ensure PR returning to the camps receive primary health care support. Should access and authorization be granted, UNRWA will rehabilitate health clinics in Yarmouk camp. The rehabilitation works will be implemented in coordination between the Health, Engineering and Procurement departments. Despite UNRWA is the main provider of services to Palestine refugees in Syria, the Agency continues to coordinate its operations with the various stakeholders operating in the country. Within the Health sector, UNRWA closely coordinates with WHO as the leading Agency and receives technical and logistical support. UNRWA also regularly coordinates with MOH, especially in relation to vaccines, family planning methods and immunization campaigns. This project will be implemented by the Field Health Programme and with support from the programme support and emergency teams.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Exposure to Explosive remnants of war as Palestine refugees access UNRWA Health centres. Likelihood:Medium Impact:High Mitigating measures: 1.Training and awareness-sessions to UNRWA staff and community members is conducted on a regular basis. 2.Mobile health clinics and health points have been established, in close proximity to the Palestine refugees communities, minimizing refugees’ movement to access the health services. Immunisation campaigns limit unnecessary movement. Protection risk:Safety, dignity and privacy are eroded for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups when attending health clinics due to overcrowding. Likelihood:Medium Impact Medium Mitigating measures: 1.Ensure numbering system and prioritization of most vulnerable (older persons, PWDs); 2.Ensure private spaces are made available where possible 3.Train staff on GBV. Protection risk:The risk of SEA of beneﬁciaries persists. Mitigation measures:1. Enforce UNRWA PSEA policy and raise awareness about its zero-tolerance 2.Ensure community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance 3.Train staff on code of conduct, and PSEA 4.Regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. UNRWA closely monitors and encourages feedback during provision of services through complaints and appeals mechanism, which enables refugees to submit claims and feedback. The custodian of this process is the investigations intake committee that receives complaints through various channels (e-mail, a telephone hotline, social media networks and/or in-person). The committee reviews the complaints and issues actionable recommendations. UNRWA Health department receives beneficiaries feedback/complaints through boxes available in the UNRWA Health facilities, regularly collected during supervisory visits and investigated accordingly. Lessons learnt and corrective measures are subsequently developed and implemented.
