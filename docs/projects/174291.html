Project Summary

The proposed intervention seeks to increase access to legal and civil documentation (LCD) and Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights by providing information, counselling and legal assistance and capacity building in the selected LGAs in northeast Nigeria. Legal assistance to obtain LCD (includes ID slips/cards and birth certificates) will be provided through the provision of logistical support to National Information Management Commission (NIMC) and National Population Commission (NPC). LCD Legal assistance on HLP will include eviction monitoring, response and mitigation activities; negotiation for land access, cash for rent and recovery of title documents among others. The HLP Sub-Working Group will coordinate and strengthen HLP response for the target communities by organising meetings and engagement events. Similarly, logistical support will be provided to dispute resolution centres/structures and the land ministry to facilitate their work. The project will also enhance capacity of local authorities and community leaders on legal identity rights, HLP and collaborative dispute resolution so they can uphold basic human rights of IDPs, returnees and host communities. These set of actions respond to increasing LCD and HLP needs. Pre-existing obstacles to birth registration and acquisition of national identity documents have been exacerbated by the ongoing crisis compelling populations to flee leaving behind or losing personal documents. Furthermore, the absence of civil administration in the LGAs pose a challenge in terms of accessibility especially for the physically disabled. Meanwhile gradual return and spread of COVID-19 in all the BAY States have resulted into return-related conflicts, including ownership disputes and secondary occupation of land among other issues. Yet, the dispute resolution structures are not well-equipped to address arrange of disputes that arise. With the spread of COVID-19 and related measures that has exerted economic pressure on IDPs, more cases of evictions have been reported, and it is likely that the trend will continue into 2021, with the most vulnerable households at risk of eviction and other related HLP right violations.

1 - Needs

An estimated 100 million people lack civil and identity documentation, and about 2 million individuals are from northeast Nigeria alone . Legal and civil documents enable individuals to access and exercise their rights to basic services and freedom of movement. Pre-existing obstacles to birth registration and acquisition of national identity documents have been exacerbated by the ongoing crisis compelling populations to flee leaving behind or losing personal documents. Furthermore, the absence of civil administration in the LGAs pose a challenge in terms of accessibility especially for the physically disabled. In addition to the usual challenges of accessing NIMC services, conflict-affected communities have to access nearby centres to enrol for ID cards on their own, an endeavour that is only logistically and financially constraining but also risky due to restriction of movement. The NPC that is mandated to issue birth certificates lacks the logistical capacity to register births in remote areas. As such the majority of the local communities in remote areas end up giving birth outside of the hospital and the failure to track births and issue birth certificates is prominent. Worse still, ordinary people are generally unaware of the importance of LCD and some are unable to meet the logistical constraint to pursue these documents. Lack of awareness, access to information, and illiteracy are contributing factors to the lack of access to civil and identity documentation. A MSNA conducted by the NRC in 2020 reveals that out of the 67% of IDPs who reported not to be in possession of ID cards, 43% reported lacking the knowledge of the processes and requirements for obtaining ID and birth certificates . The challenges of access to HLP rights for the different population groups revolve around lack of access to farmland, evictions, living in poor housing and crowded conditions, and conflicts over land, among others. Some return locations lack the basic housing facilities and farmland, and the affected communities are unable to claim HLP rights, especially in garrison towns in Borno State. Evictions are severe, with female-headed households having difficulty in accessing and paying for rental accommodation, especially more in Borno than Adamawa and Yobe States. Eviction exposes women to significant risks, including sexual exploitation and abuse. The situation has deteriorated since the spread of COVID-19 and has led to increase in the number of eviction cases from an average of 6-10 households per month to over 100 cases per month. More of the eviction cases have been reported in MM, Jere, Mobbar, Gwoza LGA of Borno State; and Mubi and Yola in the Adamawa States. While eviction cases continue to grow, the cases identified can only be viewed as a proportion of the cases, as many remain undocumented and unresolved. The challenges are generally compounded by lack of awareness, illiteracy and restrictive religious and cultural practices that affect different members of the community. The traditional dispute resolution structures in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe are existent but require support for capacity in order to adjudicate justice in an efficient, fair and transparent manner.

2 - Activities

1. Provision of information on LCD and HLP rights and obligations.2. Provision of counselling tailored to the context and challenges of conflict-affected individuals/households in relation to HLP and LCD.3. Provision of legal assistance on LCD and HLP. These includes birth certificates, ID card and related documentation for LCD. HLP legal assistance includes: recovery of lost HLP documents; cash for rent; eviction monitoring and reporting; negotiating for land access and long-term tenure for IDPs. 4. Organising meetings and engagement events with key stakeholders to promote HLP rights.5. Provision of trainings and logistical support to key stakeholders to enhance capacity of duty-bearers to uphold LCD and HLP rights of conflict-affected communities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC will adhere to the core guiding protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NRC has a dedicated protection team that works together along with the programme teams to map out potential protection risks and advise the project staff on how to mitigate them. NRC will also ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process, to ensure a better representation. Hence, woman participation but also youth, elderly of persons with disabilities’ engagement will be highly encouraged and supported. NRC will thereafter work in close coordination with these community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate. services. NRC will also mainstream protection and GBV in the site improvement and maintenance activities, by ensuring that specific groups of the population have an appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructures and services will be improved to integrate accessibility and inclusion facilities. NRC has developed detailed protection guidelines and will deliver training to project staff, to increase its staff capacity to recognise and deal with sensitive protection issues. NRC will ensure that the Community-Based Workers are trained on basic protection principles, as part of the induction process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of accountability and understands AAP as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes complaints and suggestion boxes at project sites. NRC will ensure that these sites and CFRM are accessible for people with disabilities. NRC’s CFRM mechanism will also include complaint and feedback hotlines, email, and face-to-face sessions between staff and beneficiaries. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. This CFRM is delivered by a dedicated team, which is responsible for ensuring multiple channels and tools for complaints and feedback are available for the affected population. Complaints related to NRC services will be managed by the M&E team, as well as technical program staff. CFRM staff reporting directly to senior management provides additional accountability toward targeted communities and ensure feedback and complaints are discussed on a senior level within NRC. NRC encourages face-to-face meetings with senior managers for all site visits, as well as ensuring a practical ratio of female field programming staff. Beneficiaries will therefore have the opportunity to express their grievances, feedback and report challenges with accessing the assistance directly. NRC’s M&E department will carry out post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the progress of activities towards achieving the project objectives.
