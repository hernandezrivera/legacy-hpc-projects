Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Relief International (RI) intends to reach 169,810 individuals and provide integrated emergency nutrition services to conflict- and natural disaster-affected IDPs and host communities. The project will cover four localities in North Darfur (El Fasher, El Fasher Rural, Mellit, and Al Malha), three localities in Blue Nile (Al Rosaries, Damazine, and Gissan), one locality in Al Jazirah (Wad Madani), and one locality in Khartoum. RI plans to reach the following number of beneficiaries with emergency nutrition response: North Darfur - 114,242 individuals (99,215 under-fives and 15,027 PLW), Blue Nile - 38,018 individuals (25,803 under-fives and 12,215 PLW), Jazirah - 3,250 individuals (2,250 under-fives and 1,000 PLW), and Khartoum - 14,300 individuals (9,900 under-fives and 4,400 PLW). RI will ensure that the proposed nutrition intervention is integrated into other services such as health and WASH. Protection and SGBV will be mainstreamed across RI's nutrition response. Under the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan, RI will align its emergency nutrition interventions with the nutrition cluster strategic objectives (SO): 1) Scale up access to quality integrated lifesaving nutrition preventive and protection minimum package services to contribute to preventing malnutrition, reducing morbidity, and mortality among children under two in crisis-affected and vulnerable populations; 2) Improve access to quality lifesaving nutrition services to treat severe and moderate acute malnutrition among under-fives, pregnant and lactating women, focusing on the youngest, poorest, and those with an increased mortality risk. RI will ensure that the proposed emergency nutrition response is integrated into the 34 RI-supported health facilities. The proposed nutrition response will be integrated and prioritize the treatment of severe inpatient, outpatient, and moderate acute malnutrition. To reach the estimated targets and planned response scale-up, the nutrition sector will deploy an integrated response approach and the response will use two modalities: 1) Delivery of nutrition services through existing health facilities, and 2) Provision of emergency nutrition through mobile outreach teams and satellite nutrition sites, especially in IDPs and hard-to-reach areas. The emergency nutrition action will be provided in line with the FMOH CMAM and IYCF guidelines. It is worth mentioning that RI is currently implementing a WASH program in ND and BN states; therefore, it will ensure that all nutrition sites have access to clean and safe water and adequate sanitation. Also, RI will ensure that health education and hygiene promotion are integrated into the proposed emergency nutrition intervention. RI's emergency nutrition response will be implemented at community levels using Community nutrition volunteers. When necessary, RI will scale up its intervention and reach newly displaced populations through the Emergency Response Team. RI will use needs assessments, report analysis, routine monitoring, and end line assessments to measure the impact of this project on vulnerable populations, including women and girls, and the severely and moderately malnourished under-five children. RI will improve access to nutrition services by delivering services through 28 existing health facilities (in ND and BN) and 6 new health facilities (4 in Al Jazirah and 2 in Khartoum). For beneficiaries in hard-to-reach locations, RI will conduct mobile outreaches and ensure that they have access to lifesaving preventive and curative interventions. Lastly, RI will build the capacity of 100 nutrition staff and at least 200 community nutrition volunteers through on-the-job and formal trainings and will strengthen the referral of SAM cases with medical complications. Additionally, RI will provide food for one caregiver while they are with their sick children in the stabilization centers.

1 - Needs

According to the revised 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview, the nutrition crisis in Sudan is dire, with the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) reporting the highest rate of child malnutrition globally. Approximately 3 million children under the age of 5 are affected by malnutrition, including over 610,000 suffering from severe acute malnutrition. The suspension of aid has disrupted treatment programs for 50,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition, putting their lives at risk. Additionally, a factory that produced vital supplies for the treatment of malnourished children in Sudan has been burnt down, resulting in the destruction of supplies to treat 14,500 children. This factory was responsible for producing 60% of the ready-to-use therapeutic food used by UNICEF to treat children with severe acute malnutrition, as well as ready-to-use supplementary food provided by the World Food Program (WFP) for children with moderate acute malnutrition. The drivers of the deteriorating malnutrition situation in the two states include food insecurity, a deterioration in the provision of health and WASH services, economic decline, and sub-optimal caring and feeding practices for children under five years.From May 3 to May 8, Save the Children conducted a needs assessment in Khartoum, Gezira, North and West Darfur states, and the Red Sea state. The report indicated that ongoing clashes were creating an unprecedented humanitarian crisis in Sudan, particularly in Khartoum, West, North, and South Darfur, and North Kordofan. Health facilities in Khartoum and greater Darfur were collapsing, limiting access to care, with 15,000 malnourished children having their treatment interrupted. Pre-crisis, there were already a high number of food-insecure children and adults, with most of the country rated as IPC 3. The states projected to have the highest proportion of people in need of urgent action are West Darfur (42%), North Darfur (36%), and Central Darfur (35%), with the most affected groups being internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees, those stranded in conflict areas, and refugees. In this action, RI will respond by treating severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases, integrating Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) who will be involved in active case finding, defaulter and absentee follow-up, and home visits. The CNVs will also provide nutrition education to the beneficiaries and caregivers during one-on-one sessions in their homes and group meetings in the community. RI will also train all its staff to ensure they adhere to the minimum guidelines in service provision, including the use of approved guidelines for the treatment of both SAM and MAM.

2 - Activities/Outputs

With the requested $4,550,000 for the HRP nutrition emergency intervention, Relief International (RI) will implement a comprehensive response to address malnutrition in North Darfur, Blue Nile, Al Jazirah, and Khartoum states. The proposed emergency nutrition response will be integrated into the 34 RI-supported health facilities, prioritizing the treatment of severe inpatient, outpatient, and moderate acute malnutrition cases. To reach the targeted beneficiaries and scale up the response, RI will utilize two response modalities:1. Delivering nutrition services through existing health facilities.2. Providing emergency nutrition through mobile outreach teams and satellite nutrition sites, particularly in IDP and hard-to-reach areas.RI aims to scale up the management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) cases among children under five years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), expanding coverage to reach the most vulnerable populations. The referral system for SAM cases with complications will be strengthened, ensuring timely transportation to and from stabilization centers (SCs). Additionally, food will be provided for one caregiver staying with the child in the SC.RI will support the scaling up of minimum life-saving, preventive, and protective interventions to prevent malnutrition in children under five years. This includes counseling on maternal and young child nutrition (MYCN), early detection of malnutrition using family mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) measurements, micronutrient supplementation through multiple micronutrient powders (MNPs) for home fortification, vitamin A supplementation, and deworming. Dietary diversification will be promoted, with a focus on locally available and culturally acceptable complementary foods prepared at the household level. IYCF counseling sessions will engage caregivers and influential individuals such as fathers and grandmothers.Monitoring of IYCF behavior change and practice adaptation will be conducted through routine interactions between mothers and IYCF counselors during health facility visits. Emergency blanket supplementary feeding programs (BSFP) will be provided to prevent MAM and SAM among children aged 6-59 months and PLW. Growth monitoring for children under two years will assess growth adequacy and identify early signs of faltering.RI will provide CMAM and IYCF trainings to frontline staff in line with the FMOH CMAM and IYCF guidelines.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender Based Violence (GBV), a lack of AAP, and disability have detrimental effect on the nutritional status of under-five children and PLW most often contributing to the deterioration of the nutritional status as well as impacting their mental and psycho-social well-being. Moreover, persons with disabilities are at heightened risk of malnutrition and face barriers to accessing nutrition interventions. RI aims at promoting equitable access to nutrition services which ensures that the projects reach the most deprived members of the targeted communities. Nearly all the 2023 nutrition indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers, female caregivers and PLWs during service provision. Privacy and confidentiality of beneficiaries will be respected and shall be ensured at all times of service delivery. For example, IYCF counselling and SAM cases consultation sessions with be done in a separate room to ensure privacy. RI will deploy nutrition workers of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of both women and men in a comfortable, private and confidential way. The proposed project will aim at a 50:50 gender representation for community nutrition volunteers (CNV) who will be conducting active case finding to identify malnourished cases, absentee and defaulter follow up and home visits. RI will take a socio-inclusion approach and shall ensure that beneficiaries with special needs such as people with disabilities (PwD), persons with mental health challenges, those that are in hard-to-reach locations and those with special needs are able to access the needed nutrition services by working on both supply and demand sides. Service providers will be sensitized on rights and needs of PwDs/ those with special needs; and will be trained on inclusive nutrition services provision looking at the dimensions of gender, age, and abilities. RI will also promote the recruitment of inclusive teams, in particular for engaging with communities, and deliver awareness sessions on equality of rights, needs of PwD and promotion of equal access to nutrition services. Finally, RI will ensure to enforce zero tolerance for discrimination and lack of respect towards PwD among staff. RI will engage with community representatives to get their support to reach out to the most marginalized individuals and to spread awareness messages in the community. Working with influential community allies to engage men, boys, women and girls is expected to foster community acceptance for the participation and access to nutrition services of deprived individuals and groups, including PwD and women and girls. Timing and location for at-risk groups will be considered to facilitate access to assistance. There will be GBV awareness posters in the HFs and RI will use nutrition seconded staff to work with populations with unique needs e.g. persons with disabilities, women and children.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported nutrition services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy and are required to sign the policy as of way of showing commitment. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices. RI will continue to strengthen capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedback and will use the feedback to improve its project designing and nutrition service delivery, and will ensure nutrition project recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle. As part of the existing accountability system, RI documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
