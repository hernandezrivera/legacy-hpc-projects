AKF proposes a community-based approach to early childhood programming to ensure children, especially the most marginalized in Syria, have a good start in life. The purpose of the project is to scale up community-based ECD as a complementary programmatic vehicle to traditional KG models – while at the same time strengthening the institutional measures (within the UN system and government) required to coordinate and support community-based ECD. The specific objective of the project is to increase equitable access to quality education opportunities, with a special focus on quality and inclusive early childhood development for children 0-6. To achieve this, AKF will increase the capacity of communities, caregivers, and families through the establishment of community-driven ECD models to be reviewed by ALaaty, and implementation of a national system strengthening programme. Through this project, 2000 girls and boys (aged 0 to 8) from Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Tartous, Lattakia, AlHasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor, Quneitra, Dar'a, and As-Sweida, are expected to benefit from equitable access to quality early learning and essential protection services that address their developmental needs.
The project also aims to increase opportunities for 960 adolescents between the ages of 10 to 18 years (out of school or at risk of dropping out), with a particular focus on girls, PwDs and IDPs, to pursue education in a safe environment in Salamieh City. The project will work with local teachers and community groups to establish safe learning spaces to deliver non-formal education (NFE) for out of school children or those who are at risk of dropping out; develop and deliver education gender-sensitive curriculum, designed for target students, that integrates child protection, psychosocial support, special education and self-learning; and conduct awareness-raising campaigns to parents and communities about the role of NFE in providing a pathway for children to continue their education. AKF believes in the importance of work on authorities' levels and continuing professional training for teachers. Thus, the proposed intervention in Formal education for 2022 will be continued in previously proposed activities. During the last two years, AKF trained more than 170 supervisors and heads of sectors in (Hama, Idlib, Ar-Raqqa, Damascus, Rural Damascus and Tartous) and built their capacity to be professional trainers. Then the trained supervisors transferred the training on their turn to more than 2000 teachers and primary and secondary school's principals. To ensure sustainability, AKF will follow up with them through Community of practice CoP sessions and continue the professional development training for 500 male and female supervisors and teachers on three online courses (Creating an Inclusive Learning Environment, Developmental Dialogues, Facilitating an Inclusive Learning Environment Workshop)which focus on the equity, teachers' sensitivity, Classroom, Agreement, Differentiation, Individual collaborative learning, Effective facilitation, Learning objectives & sequencing, besides 5 face to face training days about blended learning and basics of IT skills. This project is an extension of a previous experience initiated by AKF in 2017 and in partnership with UNICEF established in 2020 to implement the project, and the Aga Khan Foundation is still following up regularly on these centers technically, and logistically in times of need

4 - Needs

Syria is experiencing the lack and low quality of educational opportunities and weakened the education system. Children and youth are left unprotected, and have no access to education. One of three schools is out of service(UNICEF, March 2021), either it is damaged, destroyed or sheltering IDPs, while functioning schools are poor and crowded. Economic pressure on crisis-affected families led them to adopt negative coping mechanisms increasing out of school children and youth. Child labour and child marriage frequently occurring in 22% and 18% of assessed communities(HNO 2021). COVID-19 preventive measures including closing schools and shortening the semesters left school-aged children with a gap in their knowledge compared to the original Ministry of Education plan. Also, the lockdown increased economic pressure that let children leaving schools to work to support their families. In Salamieh, as AKF’s assessments(2020), the rate of out-of-school-children is 14.6%(6 to 8 years). Dropouts are driven by low HHs economy and children disability preventing them from accessing services. 72,800 children(ages 6>18) enrolled in 121 active schools. Overcrowding, poor educational support, and teachers’ low salary are contributing to poor education efficiency. HHs with economic means seek extra educational support which is not accessible for many due to cost barriers. Non-compulsory education in preparatory and secondary levels and a weak follow-up system on drop out children.

5 - Implementation of activities

It aims to improve the access to basic services, electricity, water, education, health, and enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth through:1-Scaling up community-based ECD platforms for marginalized children in all governorates except Idleb through the following activities: establishment of 10 new Community Based Child Centre(CBCC) to provide non-formal education.2- Enhancing a community-based NFE platforms in Salamieh as follows:i) Develop a curriculum for out of school children or at risk of dropping out which is gender-sensitive and based on UNICEF’s standard operating procedure for Non-Formal Education(NFE);ii) Establish Community Consultative Group(CCG) (50% F)who will select 2(1F) Lead Teachers(LTs) and establish10 NFE centres where each one will have a centre management committee(CMCs) with 5-7 members(50% F) who will be trained on soft skills, GBV, and gender equality. CCG and CMC will select 100 non-formal teachers (50% F);iii)Train and mentor 181 non-formal teachers at CBCCs to deliver curriculum. Parents will have awareness on(PSS) for children. Promotional materials will be made for distribution among communities. Outcome monitoring activities will include baseline and end-line assessments of the target group, beneficiaries feedback and FGDs with project stakeholders.3-Train 50 of young mothers with skills like reading and writing, financial literacy, digital(IT), and PSS.4-300 children(age6-8) benefitting from remedial classes.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

nsufficient water and sanitation facilities; inability to maintain Covid-19 safety measures including positive behavioral changes. Mitigation: Learning facilities will be improved to ensure sufficient water and sanitation facilities will be provided and maintained. WASH facilities will be sex and age-segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. Students will be trained on proper hygiene practices. All procedures will be coordinated with health authorities. Learning facilities will have Covid-19 safety protocols including identification, prevention measures, awareness-raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health centre Safety and Security concerns about male and female youth exposure to physical or verbal harassment on their way to the centres. Mitigation: AKF will ensure a safe and secure environment by selecting centre locations in safe and easy-to-access areas, and providing transportation. CBCC facilitators selection committee might face pressure or might be accused of bias in selecting the facilitators. Mitigation: AKF will start with mobilizing communities to consider both qualification and social representation as selection criteria for committees. AKF will expand reach to communities as possible, to increase the probability of qualified and agreed upon facilitators to lead the CBCC, these committees will be responsible for parents’ engagement in Centres management through follow up meetings and sharing feedback.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: There is a risk that the project activity may lead to safeguarding issues for women involved in the project. Risk 2: The project activities time and locations might be unsafe for the vulnerable groups especially for women and children. In line with AKF safeguarding policy, manual and standards, the project PSEA strategy that will ensure the following mitigation measures are in place: • Train all project staff on safeguarding policies. • Ensure all involved partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct • Develop gender-responsive referral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms. • All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies. • Conduct an assessment of safety aspects in spaces used for activities implementation as well as the surrounding environment. The project will adopt measures and mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and hold superiors and subordinates to account alike. AKF follows specific process and procedures to integrate safeguarding in the Project Cycle which are: • Determine expected safeguarding risk using (Risk Register tool) • Determine safeguarding measures to reduce the risks through (mitigation plan and indicators). • Evaluating safeguarding at the project Cycle before, during and after implementing the project, using Project Cycle Check list.
