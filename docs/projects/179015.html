Project Summary

The goal of the proposed project is to improve access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and women and girls at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV for women and adolescent girls with a focus on the most vulnerable individuals (e.g. children, elderly and single-headed households and PWDs) through supporting a women and girls safe space in Kafr Takharim in Idlib, and jendares in ifreen, Syria, the proposed project is based on continuing to supporting a safe environment for W&G, where they feel physical and psychological safe (absence of trauma, excessive pressure, violence, and fear) and is free to express themselves without fear of discrimination or any kind of harm.
and the activities will include IDPs and people from the host communities.
Amal seeks also to strengthen the capacity of its team through the sporadic training that will be provided to them by taking advantage of the training in the protection sector provided by GBV Sub-Cluster in OCHA, Amal field team will also be provided with the needed technical support and follow up from Amal HQ team.
To achieve this, Amal is proposing in this project proposal to provide activities through its center dedicated to W&G as the following:
Amal will continue in providing protection activities and responding to GBV cases, Amal will also develop the sessions and consultations that will be provided to them through using and implementing the updated 2021 GBV Guidelines issued by the GBV sub-cluster of Protection Cluster in OCHA.
Amal will continue to provide PSS services on the individual and collective levels through divided sessions in the center,
Amal will continue to provide life-saving services, the most important of which is the case management of survivors of GBV to reach the recovery stage, worth mentioning that the number of these activities implemented in the previous project was higher compared to the numbers planned due to the needs.
Amal will continue providing the referral services for GBV, Amal will also update constantly services map and identify referral paths and coordinate with the active organizations in the field to refer the survived cases to be benefited from other services.
Amal will continue to implement the response plan through the provision of PFA for W&G,
Amal will integrate the PWDs in this service provided in the center, and add some dedicated services to them to ease integrating them with the other BNFs, target groups from 12 to 30 years old for PWDs (mobility - auditory)
Based on the W&G-oriented needs assessment, Amal will add the following activities:
provide a Sexual and Reproductive Health Minimum Initial Service Package for women through using the curriculums of the Reproductive Health Working Group.
provide (Vocational Training - Livelihoods - life skills)
provide recreational activities as this type of activates has a great impact on the well-being and psychological side of women.
Providing tool kits to increase the awareness of women and girls’ beneficiaries on GBV
Furthermore, the outreach team will provide preventive and responsive services for women and girls outside the center activities.
Amal will use the GBV SC M&E Toolkit to monitor its activities and report on the project in line with the inter-agency guidance. worth mentioning that the M&E officer is specially trained in the use and dissemination of the GBV SC M&E Toolkit.
Amal will continue to support a committee of beneficiaries had selected by direct election. This committee will contribute to leading the center and selecting appropriate activities according to the wishes and ages of the beneficiaries. The committee will also have a role to lead the center at the end of the project period and has the right to communicate with the interested parties working in the area to sustain the project activities even by transferring the project to another organization or by extending the project activities.

4 - Needs

GBV remains a major protection risk, such as domestic violence, forced/early marriage, and the fear of sexual violence and harassment...etc.The lack of understanding of the need for GBV services in some communities remains high, indicating the need to strengthen GBV awareness, referral pathways, as well as outreach and prevention activities.According to Voices 2021, 62% of communities confirmed that cases of early marriage affected adolescent girls between the ages of 12 and 17, and 62% of communities surveyed agreed that adolescent girls between the ages of 15-17 drop out of school in order to work.According to HNO 2021, Isolation and quarantine during the Corona pandemic caused increased risks of violence against women and girls. GBV service providers have reported an upsurge in intimate partner violence and other forms of violence (physical, emotional, marital rape, denial of resources, sexual exploitation, harass and abuse via technology, early/forced marriages, based on GBV Trend analysisthere is the problem of early marriage, families forcing their little girls to take hormone medications believing this would fasten puberty and enable them to get married and conceive afterward, the reason for this is lack of awareness and poverty.Amal has made a Rapid Assessment of the current situation in the target area.the evaluation indicated that the most vulnerable were women with disabilities and widows and divorcees and women who had been subjected to physical violence and emotional abuse and had no access to services.This underscores the importance of safe spaces for women and girls to help them engage in the activities and facilitate access to safe services.

5 - Implementation of activities

Amal is an active member of the GBV SC, So, Amal will adherence to the inter-agency guidance and resources, such as the Awareness Toolkit, the GBV SOPs, GBV SC M&E Toolkit through the implementation of her activities Amal will provide the following activities Providing protection services and response activities to GBV. Providing PSS services at the individual and collective levels. Providing the most important and life-saving services to manage the cases survived of GBV until their recovery. Providing referral services for the cases survived GBV to benefit from other services. Providing PSS education services through implementing recreational and educational activities. Providing support to parents to increase their psychological awareness and education. Amal will provide a Sexual and Reproductive Health Minimum Initial Service Package for women through using curriculums of Reproductive Health Working Group. Amal will provide (Vocational Training & Livelihood (in the following fields:•Computer literacy (essential principles to using Computers). • Hairdressing. - Life skills (Literacy, communication skills, negotiation skills, time management, entrepreneurship and leadership skills, facilitation skills, public speaking skills, first aid, communication skills and writing a CV, English language, Manual embroidery).- Providing tool kits to increase the awareness of women and girls’ beneficiaries on gender-based violence (GBV). - Providing particularly in open days for women and girls and in public events (International Women's Day, International Children's Day, International Day of Disabled Persons, International Mother's Day ...etc.).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk 1: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also leads to fewer disclosures and a lack of trust in services. Likelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor-centered services.Protection risk 2: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging 2. The backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational, or empowerment. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Community engagement from the onset of a project, from the assessment phase, and throughout all project cycles. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders 2. Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activitiesProtection risk 3: Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigating Measure: Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as a safe entry point Protection risk 4: survivors drop out of the case plan because they have high expectations that cannot be met.probability: medium, Impact: highMitigation measures: the case management team will have a survivor-centered approach and will remain realistic when discussing the available options with the survivor.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers, and low knowledge on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. Impact: high protection riskMitigation:Amal has policies, procedures, and other measures in place that relate to preventing sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment. So, Amal will work on the following: 1- train the staff on code of conduct, PSEA, CSG and follow them periodically2- Amal ensures to have in a place an accessible mechanism for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse in line with Amal safeguarding policy.3- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp)4 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.Amal is a new member of PSEA network and will work to communicate with the PSEA network and benefit from the training provided and circulate it in its projects

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risk: Legacy and new explosive devices that might exist. Impact: Medium, Mitigation measures: The safety and security team will work on communicating with local councils and organizations operating in the targeted area to know the area’s most dangerous for the presence of mines, and the organization will also conduct awareness sessions for the team and the beneficiaries to report any suspicion of the existence of these objects and do not touch or approach them2. Risk: Change in lines of control, Impact: High, Mitigation Measures: unfortunately, we can’t mitigate this risk but we will work on decreasing the consequences by having an evacuation plan that we train our team on and also a closure plan that we will use including a policy to destroy all confidential documents3. Humanitarian actors suspend their funding to those locations: Impact: high, Mitigation Measure: Train the committee of community members to lead and being able to map the resources available in the community to run the basic group activity and donate any equipment available to this committee to support them doing this role.4. COVID-19: positive cases are reported to be widely spread among the population of Northwest Syria, Probability: high, Impact: mediumMitigation measure: using hand sanitizers, and the beneficiaries, avoid sharing personal items, wear gloves and masks, Promote the hygiene of the CFS and sessions locations by ensuring and appropriate sterilization and cleaning daily, Emphasis social distance during sessions inside the CFS and during the outreach activities among children and parents, provide awareness-raising orientation about COVID-19 risk
