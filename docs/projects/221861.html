Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Upper Nile State, particularly in Longechuk, Maiwut, Renk, Malakal, and Fashoda counties, remains critical, with significant challenges in child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response. The region continues to grapple with the aftermath of prolonged conflict, displacement, and climate-related shocks, exacerbated by recent influx of refugees and returnees from neighboring countries. In Longechuk County, humanitarian needs are particularly acute. According to OCHA's Humanitarian Needs Overview, approximately 80% of the county's population (around 43,000 people) require significant humanitarian assistance. The need for GBV services in Longechuk is classified as "extreme," highlighting the severity of the protection crisis. The county serves as a crucial transit area for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees from Ethiopia, particularly through Mathiang and Udier routes. Maiwut County has been severely impacted by the ongoing crisis, with a steady flow of refugees and South Sudanese returnees crossing the Sudanese and Ethiopian borders to escape violence. Women and girls in the county struggle to access basic hygiene products, including menstrual hygiene care. To address these critical needs, humanitarian organizations are implementing targeted interventions, providing dignity kits containing essential hygiene items. Additionally, Women and Girls Friendly Spaces have been established, offering classes on hygiene, health, and information on GBV services and referrals.
In Renk County, the humanitarian situation has been particularly challenging due to its position as a key entry point for returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan. The influx has strained local resources and protection services. Emergency WASH interventions have been crucial in addressing the immediate needs of vulnerable populations. Malakal, as one of the larger urban centers in Upper Nile State, faces complex protection challenges. The county hosts a significant number of IDPs and has been a focal point for humanitarian interventions. Child protection services in Malakal are critical, with efforts focused on preventing and responding to various forms of child abuse, including recruitment into armed groups.
Fashoda County faces similar challenges to its neighbors, with protection needs exacerbated by displacement, conflict, and limited access to basic services. Child protection remains a critical concern across these counties. The ongoing conflict and displacement have left many children vulnerable to various forms of abuse, including recruitment into armed groups, sexual exploitation, and separation from their families. The project targets these five counties in Upper Nile State, identified as high-priority locations due to their complex humanitarian situations. The Child Protection response aims to strengthen protection mechanisms through establishing community-based networks, providing psychosocial support, reunifying separated children, conducting awareness campaigns, and offering life skills training. Gender-Based Violence prevention and response focuses on establishing women and girls' friendly spaces, providing case management and psychosocial support, conducting community awareness sessions, offering livelihood support, and training local health workers on GBV response pathways. Community Engagement and Capacity Building emphasizes training local community members, strengthening existing protection mechanisms, engaging with local authorities and traditional leaders, and supporting the development of community action plans for protection. Expected outcomes include increased access to protection services, strengthened community-based mechanisms, improved awareness of protection issues, enhanced coordination of services, and increased economic resilience for women and girls through livelihood support programs. This project aligns with Protection Cluster Objectives of 1 and 3

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in Upper Nile State, particularly concerning child protection and gender-based violence (GBV), remains critical in 2024. The ongoing conflicts, displacement, and economic challenges have significantly impacted the vulnerability of children and women in the region. As of June 2024, 4,900,000 children in South Sudan are in need of humanitarian assistance. In Upper Nile State, the influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan has exacerbated the child protection crisis. Between January and June 2024, 1,872 children (864 girls and 1,008 boys, including 82 children with disabilities) were newly registered for comprehensive case management services due to separation from their families. 91,231 children (41,702 girls and 49,529 boys, 374 being children with disabilities) accessed mental health and psychosocial support activities through child-friendly spaces, schools, and communities. Intercommunal conflicts in Upper Nile State have led to school closures and displacement of families, affecting children's access to education. In the first half of 2024, 47 girls and 66 boys were successfully reunified with their families and guardians in South Sudan. The ongoing influx of refugees and returnees has increased the risk of child exploitation, particularly in transit areas like Renk County. Between January and June 2024, 166,731 individuals (44,440 girls, 24,155 boys, 72,063 women, and 26,073 men) were reached with GBV risk mitigation, prevention, and response services across South Sudan. Upper Nile State, particularly areas like Malakal, faces a high prevalence of GBV due to displacement and conflict. Women and girls in target counties struggle to access basic hygiene products, including menstrual hygiene care, increasing their vulnerability to GBV. The situation is particularly dire in overcrowded transit sites, such as those in Renk County. UNICEF and partners have established women and girls' friendly spaces in Upper Nile State to offer safe environments and support services. Case management and psychosocial support for GBV survivors are being provided, though the demand often outstrips available resources. Access to remote areas in target counties remains a significant challenge for humanitarian organizations providing GBV services. The ongoing influx of returnees and refugees has strained existing GBV prevention and response capacities in the state. Renk County serves as a key entry point for returnees and refugees fleeing conflict in Sudan. Faces significant challenges in providing adequate protection services due to overcrowding in transit sites. Emergency WASH interventions have been crucial in addressing immediate needs of vulnerable populations. Malakal hosts a significant number of internally displaced persons (IDPs), increasing protection challenges. Child protection services focus on preventing recruitment into armed groups and responding to various forms of child abuse. GBV prevention and response services are a priority due to heightened risks in displacement settings. Longechuk, Maiwut and Fashoda face similar challenges with displacement, limited access to basic services, and protection risks. Humanitarian organizations are working to provide integrated protection services, often as part of larger regional initiatives covering multiple counties in Upper Nile State. As of June 2024, UNICEF's Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) appeal for South Sudan had only received 13% of the needed US$252.5 million to reach the most vulnerable women and children. New partnerships have been established to enhance child protection and GBV response. Efforts are ongoing to strengthen the capacity of local social workers and community-based protection mechanisms. The situation in target counties remains dynamic, with ongoing challenges in addressing child protection and GBV needs. The combination of conflict, displacement, and economic hardship continues to put children and women at significant risk

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Establishment of Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS): Set up 15 new Child-Friendly Spaces in Renk, Malakal, and Longechuk counties benefiting 15,000 children (7,500 girls and 7,500 boys) through these spaces. Sub-activities include: 1.1 Provide safe environments for children to play, learn, and receive psychosocial support; 1.2 Conduct daily structured activities including educational sessions, recreational activities, and life skills training; 1.3 Offer basic literacy and numeracy classes for out-of-school children, 1.4 implement child protection awareness sessions for caregivers and community members. Activity 2: Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) Program: Expand FTR services to cover all five counties (Longechuk, Maiwut, Renk, Malakal, and Fashoda) aiming to register and provide case management for 1,000 unaccompanied and separated children. Sub-activities: 2.1 Establish a centralized database for tracking separated children across the state; 2.3 Conduct regular family tracing activities in collaboration with local authorities and community leaders; 2.4 Provide interim care arrangements for children awaiting reunification; 2.5 Offer post-reunification support and follow-up visits to ensure successful reintegration. Activity 3: Community-Based Child Protection Networks: Establish/strengthen 25 community-based child protection networks across the five counties by training 200 community volunteers (100 women and 100 men) on child protection principles and practices. Sub-activities include; 3.1 Conduct training sessions on child rights, protection risks, and referral pathways; 3.2 Support regular meetings of protection networks to discuss and address local child protection issues; 3.3 Facilitate community-led awareness campaigns on child protection; 3.4 Establish linkages between community networks and formal child protection services. Activity 4: GBV Prevention and Response Services: Establish 5 new Women and Girls' Friendly Spaces (WGFS), with at least one in each target county; aiming to reach 1,000 women and girls with GBV prevention and response services. Sub-activities: 4.1 Provide case management and psychosocial support for GBV survivors; 4.2 Conduct regular awareness sessions on GBV prevention, women's rights, and available support services; 4.3 Offer skills training and income-generating activities to enhance women's economic resilience; 4.4 Distribute dignity kits to 200 women and girls, with a focus on IDPs and returnees in Renk and Malakal. Activity 5: Capacity building for Local Service Providers: Conduct training programs in all five counties; through training 100 local social workers, health providers, and community leaders on child protection and GBV response. Sub-activities: 5.1 Organize workshops on case management, psychosocial first aid, and referral mechanisms; 5.2 Provide specialized training on working with child survivors of violence and abuse; 5.3 Conduct regular refresher courses and on-the-job mentoring for service providers; 5.4 Establish a peer support network for service providers to share experiences and best practices. Activity 5: School-Based Child Protection Interventions: Implement programs in 10 schools across target counties, with a focus on areas with high returnee and IDP populations reaching 2,000 school-aged children (1,000 girls and 1,000 boys) with protection services. Activity 6: Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Services: Establish MHPSS services in all five counties, with mobile teams to reach remote areas; providing specialized MHPSS support to 1,000 children and 1,000 women affected by conflict and displacement. Activity 7: Community Engagement and Awareness Campaigns: Conduct large-scale awareness campaigns in all five counties, with a focus on high-risk areas identified through needs assessments reaching 15,000 community members with child protection and GBV prevention messages.
