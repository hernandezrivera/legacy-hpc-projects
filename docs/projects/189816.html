Project Summary

Effects of the Tigray conflict that has been going on for more than 1 year expected to continue impacting access to equitable health care to more that 2 m persons displaced. IOM will aim to provide essential life-saving health services to IDPs and affected host communities returnees. Response will leverage pre-existing facilities in areas of high need, such as Tigray-Mekelle, Shire, Sheraro, Adwa, Western part, Amhara, Oromia-East and West Hararge, East and West Wollega, Somali-Qoloji, and SNNPR-Konso.

1. - Projects activities

 Deployment of Mobile Health and Nutrition teams (MHNTs) to provide essential Primary Health Care services. The MHNT package will be comprehensive to include Nutrition screening and management through collaborations, treatment of common morbidities and chronic illnesses including referrals of cases that need further care. In areas of returns and where health facilities exist but non-functional, IOM will support provision of essential health services in static facilities until the government is able to fully take up service provision. Integration of Gender Based Violence, Mental health, and psychosocial support with health care workers teams to provide integrated approach consultations at existing health facilities and mobile teams.  COVID-19 response will be integrated to PHC to include facility-based screening, awareness creation, COVID-19 vaccination, referral of suspected cases to management centres. Provision of integrated of safe motherhood and other reproductive health services such as antenatal care (ANC), post-natal care, family planning (FP), post-abortion care (PAC) with MHPSS mainstreaming activities  Outbreak preparedness and control: support routine and mass vaccination campaigns, Training of government workers on RCCE, case management, provision of operational and logistics costs during outbreaks. These activities will be integrated with Mental health and psychosocial support and ensuring GBV mainstreaming. It will also include COVID-19 Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE).  Health surveillance and MHPSS teams will also be deployed to border points of Entry (POEs) to provide health screening and psychosocial support for migrants and vulnerable population in collaboration with the regional health bureaus. Health post rehabilitation; will include physical rehabilitations or constructions as well as procurement of equipment and supplies to operationalize vandalized facilities.  Capacity building; on job through by attachment to MHNTs and through formal trainings to improve quality of service delivery as well as prepare MOH teams to respond to outbreaks. Human resources support to existing health facilities: deployment of IOM MHNTs, payment of MOH staff salaries or incentives in locations that require surge support.  Procurement of essential supplies for existing health posts and MHNT activities. Provision of MHPSS integrated into PHC activities and or as stand-alone activities, in collective sites, such as individual, group counseling, referral to multisectoral assistance for the affected population, through Improving and making access to MHPSS services more equitable to all population groups, including women, men, children, youth, older people, male and female GBV survivors, people with disabilities, etc, counseling of persons with protection needs will happen in a setting that guarantees privacy, security, confidentiality and safety, and yet is not stigmatizing. Provision of outreach services: affected population in the community who cannot access facilities and services will be reached out with the MHPSS/GBV basics services, through a door to door visits, and with the awareness and psychoeducation sessions ( tailored to the local languages) focus on the promotion of mental health, mitigate COVID-19 stressors and Gender-based violence such as the psychosocial effect of early/forced marriage, FGM prevention in the community. Scale-up MHPSS community-based activities, including protection aspects; socio-cultural and recreational activities will be organized in the community which aimed to reduce stigma and discrimination of marginalized groups in communities (e.g., persons with disabilities including those with intellectual, developmental, cognitive disabilities, and GBV survivors) these activities will ensure a safe entry point for the women and girls including survivors to get information and available services and re-build the social network of the affected population.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

In line with its commitments to AAP, IOM maintains a participatory approach by involving direct beneficiaries, host communities and other stakeholders throughout the full project cycle, enabling them to influence and shape the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of activities and decision-making processes. It also continuously consults extensively with the local community including women, men, boys and girls, youth, religious and community leaders, minority groups, people with disabilities and local administration to better understand their needs. To further strengthen accountability, IOM established diverse and contextually appropriate mechanisms and systems that enable communities to provide feedback on its programmes and submit complaints. The complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) include kebele committees, different community and government representatives, site management committees, community meetings, help desk, focus group discussions and exit interviews. These CFMs regularly collect feedback from beneficiaries and community members, enabling IOM to respond to the gaps and needs of affected population and modify its projects in a timely manner. Additionally, IOM has a designated AAP focal person works together with programme and field staff to facilitate this process. Finally, as part of monitoring and evaluation, overall AAP data will be analyzed periodically to generate lessons learned which will be used to adjust ongoing project implementation and inform future design of projects.
