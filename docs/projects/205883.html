Project Summary

The overarching objective of this project is to deepen CAFOD on going response and prevention intervention in Fashoda county. This project will target 5,000 beneficiaries (2776 women, 422 men, 1430 girls and 372 boys) respectively in Fashoda County of Upper Nile State. The multitude of needs presented by different beneficiaries will be addressed through a tailored approach to GBV prevention and response and mitigation. This project is designed to improve response to survivors of GBV to enhance the ability of survivors to recover from shock and make progress towards changing the social norms that perpetrators of GBV with the determination of mitigating the effects of GBV and preventing future cases. It will provide GBV response services and address critical gaps in information and knowledge among women and girls on GBV response, which limit access to services. The project will focus on provision of psychosocial support for women and girls through cases management in women and girls’ friendly spaces. This will be in line with minimum standards on case management and GBV. Access for services for survivors will be promoted through community wide campaigns on availability of services. Women will also be provided with some cash support to safely access services with dignity to support their healing process and resilience. GBV risk mitigation will also be conducted through joint safety audits with different actors to identity risky areas for women and drive mitigation methods. Therefore, the project will address GBV issues affecting both Women and girls in the Communities. In complimentary with ongoing CTP project in Fashoda county, this project will enhance women and girls’ participation in peacebuilding for peaceful co-existence through capacity building of women groups and leaders on conflict resolution and staff will be trained on conflict sensitivity and Do No Harm. This is meant to create opportunities within the local community to eliminate local drivers of conflicts and Gender based violence. This project will build on the current CTP programme in Lul and Dethwok Payams of Fashoda County and will address the unmet needs. Caritas Diocese of Malakal, CAFOD local implementing partner has well established office in Malakal and has good working relationship with authorities in the region, have experienced staff and being a church-based charity organisation, is widely accepted by all communities despite the complex nature of security situation in Upper Nile State. CAFOD has over 10 years of working with Caritas Diocese of Malakal in Upper Nile, and last year implemented a project in Panyikang County but suspended operations because of insecurity. Currently, Caritas Diocese of Malakal is implementing an $865,684.09 static multisector programme (food security, WASH, protection and peacebuilding) funded by Secours Catholique (Caritas France) CAFOD’s back donor for this project and funding from CAFOD internal fund to respond to needs of the returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host community in Fashoda County. Caritas Diocese of Malakal has robust financial management system and internal controls and CAFOD providing overall technical support and guidance throughout the project cycle to ensure quality programme delivery and accountability.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face unprecedented and persistent humanitarian crises of physical violence, rape, and other forms of GBV towards women and girls are pervasive. Societal norms, values, practices, and gender related power imbalances are key drivers of the various forms of GBV women and girls face, even not related to conflict. The Humanitarian response overview estimated 2.8 million people will face and majorly women and girls will risk face GBV in 2023. This represents a 9 per cent increase compared to 2022 estimates. Recurring conflict, flooding, and drought continue to contribute to the increase in the risk of GBV. In August 2022, conflict erupted in Tonga and Panyikang, and spread to Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Fashoda, causing major displacement, cutting off populations from essential services, and resulting in severe protection incidents and violations against women and children. Following the conflict in the upper Nile state, women and girls continue to be exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities to access their basic needs, which are further exacerbated by displacement due to conflicts, flooding, and drought that limit livelihood opportunities. Increase in sexual violence against, and harassment of women and girls along with the risks of abduction, killings, revenge killings and threats from armed forces coupled with insufficient humanitarian assistance and lack of livelihoods forced women and girls to make longer and more risky journeys in flooding affected areas in search of livelihoods activities, exposing them to a higher risk of GBV, including rape. Similarly, the conflict in Leer resulted in an increase in conflict related sexual violence. The outbreak of conflict in Sudan on 15 April 2023 has further negatively impacted the situation in South Sudan, as the price of fuel, foods and services have increased further. As of 27 October, 341,967 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children. The HNO 2023 presents increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services and highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized GBV response services. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, health response to GBV survivors, psychosocial support, safety and security, legal service, livelihood, economic support, and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practices, and the Community Cares programme, integration of GBV actions into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are high priority interventions for the GBV Areas of Responsibility (GBV AoR). The current CAFOD project Fashoda is only reaching 1000 households (6,000 individuals) and huge gap is still unmet which this project will address.

2. Project activities

The project will assist affected communities to positively cope in meeting their aspiration through establishment of women and girls safe spaces; Provision of support to existing women and girls safe spaces activities, provision of structured group and individual psychosocial support, recreational activities; Conduct safety audits with women and adolescent girls to identify GBV risk in the community; Support advocacy meetings with humanitarian actors to ensure needs of women and girls are identified and prioritised by other sectors such as WASH, NFI, shelter and food security and livelihood; Provide women and girls’ with cash assistance for risk mitigation support materials in times of acute crisis and also cover basic needs as part of case management; Provide age appropriate case management and psychosocial support services and referral services in holistic and coordinated approach for GBV survivors; Conduct case management review meetings and follow up bilateral meetings with service providers, establish and disseminate functional, safe referral pathways for GBV survivors updated regularly based on changing contexts; Ensure GBV survivors women, girls, men and boys accesses and receive appropriate comprehensive care they need; Provide training to Psychosocial support facilitators, case workers to enable them to provide quality case management and psychosocial support services to GBV survivors and people who need; Train local women’s groups on provision of psychological first aid and referrals; Train local service providers and frontline workers, community outreach teams of staff/volunteers on GBV, referral pathway Psychological first aid and GBV guiding principles; Conduct sensitization and training sessions on gender and GBV related topics targeting community stakeholders and parties to the conflict on trauma healing, conflict resolution and peacebuilding, establish functional feedback and complaint mechanism for provision of effective and appropriate services provision conduct awareness raising using EMAP, SASA, and Community Cares programming to transform harmful social norms and contribute to gender inequality. The current CAFOD project in Fashoda is implemented in Lul and Dethwok Payams reaching 6,000 individuals (1,000 households) which will run up to March 2024. The activities under this project will address the unmet needs and complement the ongoing project in the area. Communities will broadly be consulted including diverse/PLWD. Protection monitoring will be conducted to identify existing vulnerabilities, data will disaggregated by gender, age diversity, project information will be disseminated multiple channels Project information on project objective, entitlement, key activities duration, expected staff behaviour, complaint handling and feedback mechanisms will be disseminated. complaints feedback and reporting mechanisms will be established to timely responses and address Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.Community engagement, participation of the community is central to this project implementation hence feedback from the target groups will improve the quality of response especially on preferred assistance. The diverse population will be attention to ensure their active participation is attained. Identifying and addressing existing barriers to equal participation will be focused to avoid clash with women’s domestic responsibilities, ensure activity times and places are safe, accessible and in convenient locations for women girls and persons with disabilities, and men sensitised to understand the benefits of women’s participation. KOBO toolbox will be used for data collection, Feedback mechanism established will tracked project delivery and if project beneficiaries feel well informed on project, capacity building to partner organization will be provided on planning and M&E support for compliance to donor rules and regulations. Coping capacity and satisfaction survey will be conducted to establish service provision.
