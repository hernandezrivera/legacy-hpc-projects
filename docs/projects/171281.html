Project Summary

As evidenced in the 2021 HNO, the needs for sexual and reproductive health “SRH” remains high considering the protracted crises and the lock down due to COVID-19 outbreak, associated with redirection of resources (human, medical and logistics) from the provision of SRH services have affected around 80,000 additional vulnerable women. This project is aligned to HNO 2021 Health Cluster Strategic Objectives 1, Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities across oPt, and is directly linked to Strategic Objective 2, meeting health needs and providing basic health services. The project is also well aligned with Health Cluster Strategic Objective 2, Strengthen the healthcare system's capacity to respond to emergencies and build community resilience to cope with the impact of current and future crises, and is directly linked to Strategic Objective 3, building the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome the protracted crisis. The project contributes to preventing excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality, which have early indications of increasing over the last three years. The project will increase access to healthcare and nutrition services and ensuring that clean and safe delivery, and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available at all times. The project will complement previous and ongoing UNFPA projects; CERF and HPF 2020, first standard allocation and other projects; by expanding coverage of health system capacity to endure continuation of essential SRH and maternal care services at primary and secondary health care levels and community.
The project will: -
- Ensure continuation of SRH services, enhance access to quality SRH care and strengthen SRH management and counselling, including the operationalization of relevant COVID-19 guidelines and protocols.
- Build the capacity of health providers on life saving SRH issues including emergency obstetric care, high-risk pregnancy, pre-conception care and family planning etc.
- Improve SRH services coordination among various stakeholders/providers, including mainstreaming disability, protection and GBV in SRH service provision and community outreach.
- Combat social, cultural and economic factors/stigma, restricting free access to and choice of services by women.
- Actively engaging communities, supporting coping mechanism, sensitization, awareness raising and outreach.
- Support linking and informing communities to available service, including protection from GBV and psychosocial support, with special focus to marginalized communities and vulnerable groups including PwD, young girls, the poor and elderly etc.
- Strengthen the preparedness of the health facilities, fill gaps and replenish life-saving SRH equipment, drugs, commodities and supplies including PPE.
While this project is meant to address immediate response to urgent and critical needs of Gazan’s women’s, and young girls, especially those in marginalized areas, it will also support men and other vulnerable adolescents and strengthen the preparedness and resilience of both the community and the health system for absorbing shocks, saving lives and recovering from crises.
UNFPA coordinates with active stakeholders including UN sister agencies (WHO and UNICEF), MOH and major NGOS to ensure complementarity. UNFPA will with MOH, Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS) and other NGOs as needed to implement this project. UNFPA has been approached by Union of Health Works Committees (UHWC), who is applying for a project to support reproductive health services targeting women in their centers’ catchment areas in Gaza, to ensure coordination. UHWC was encouraged to apply, and to continue coordination and sharing experience and information on localities and target groups upon project implementation to avoid duplication and ensure synergism with UNFPA and other projects

1 - Needs

The situation in Palestine is well recognized as a protracted protection crisis with humanitarian consequences, driven, among others, by a lack of respect for international law by the occupying power. Palestinian women, men, girls, and boys in Gaza face a range of serious protection and humanitarian threats including threats to life, liberty and security. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to be a key protection and health concern in Gaza, further impeding women’s and girls’ access to resources including life-saving SRH services and information. Following 14 years of tightened blockade on Gaza, internal political division, Gaza faces increased poverty and deterioration of living conditions (OCHA 2020), with a health system on the verge of collapse including limited access to essential sexual and reproductive health services. These persisting challenges are limiting access to basic SRH care, including maternal health services, for around 80,000 pregnant and lactating women. High rate of anemia has been reported as 38% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in the most deprived communities in Gaza are undernourished (HNO 2021). The health system is on the edge with limited resources to sustain basic, but critical, health services (i.e. maternal health, emergency obstetric services, family planning and antenatal care etc.) with lack of life sustaining SRH equipment, drugs, and disposables, as they are under-prioritized and inadequately funded, (According to MOH September 2020 drugs report, 55% of essential MCH drugs are at zero stock level), increasing the risk of death and disability and increased GBV for women and girls.As first highlighted in May 2019 in the Health Situation Report issued by the Health Cluster, SRH continues to be of increasing concern with instable maternal mortality ratios over 2017-2019 (5.6 to 19.1 per 100,000) for Palestine, and a fear of increasing number of women dying due complications of pregnancy and childbirth. There remains a high rate of caesarian section (29%) in MOH hospitals in Gaza, compared to the WHO recommendation of 10-15% (MOH 2019 Annual Health Report, June 2020). These challenges were intensified with the COVID-19 pandemic; as of April 2020, access to SRH services has been dramatically affected, due to movement restrictions, fear of women to attend clinics, burden on health providers who are engaged with COVID-19 response and scaling down of many services. This has affected an estimated 210,000 pregnant and lactating women in West Bank and Gaza, who’s access to regular health care was impeded (Health Cluster Situation Report; Impact of COVID-19 on SRH including maternal health in Palestine, April 2020). Late August, following COVID-19 community spread, in Gaza, and subsequent strict measures, including a curfew, the lockdown of primary health care facilities, and shortages of health personnel in hospitals and clinics, especially after diversion of health staff to COVID-19 quarantine centers management, and designated COVID -19 facilities etc.; the provision and access to SRH services was further exacerbated. Even during emergencies, SRH including maternal health and family planning, GBV/protection services are essential and must be sustained, as they are central to health, rights and well-being of women and girls. However, this becomes a challenge in light of diversion of attention and critical resources away from these provision, as shown by research that unmet need for family planning is generally greater among more fragile countries (UNFPA/2020: Impact of Public Health Emergencies on SRH and Reproductive Rights in the Arab Region: The COVID - 19 Case). In general, pregnant women are more vulnerable to experiencing serious respiratory infections and must be treated with utmost priority (Health Cluster Situation Report; Impact of COVID-19 on SRH including maternal health in Palestine, April 2020). A recent systematic review/meta-analysis by the BMJ concluded that pregnant women with COVID-19 have a higher potential for admission to ICU and invasive ventilation compared to non-pregnant women. The analysis also showed that Preterm birth rates are high in pregnant women with covid-19 than in pregnant women without the disease. (https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/370/bmj.m3320.full.pdf)By implementing appropriate response and sustaining essential services and support activities that address basic SRH and protection needs, resilience and coping mechanisms can be strengthened as well as the well–being of people improved, especially for women and girls.

2 - Activities

UNFPA aims at saving women’s lives through strengthening system and community access and preparedness to life-saving SRH services and information on; safe childbirth, Emergency obstetric care, preconception care, early detection of breast cancer, antenatal care with focus on high-risk pregnancies, and enhancing knowledge of and utilization of essential RH services, during acute crises. This project will increase access of vulnerable women’s, men and girls to SRH services, support networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspectives via educating families, men, boys and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and SRH needs. This project will build on and complement previous and ongoing UNFPA interventions, both under humanitarian interventions, CERF and pooled fund, as well as joint development project with WHO and UNCEF funded by AFD. UNFPA and partners will target major maternities and PHC facilities, through provision and pre-positioning of essential SRH equipment, instruments and life-saving pharmaceuticals and supplies including family planning commodities, training of health providers on quality SRH services, including adapted service provision and counselling and launching community awareness campaigns reaching tens of thousands of people in vulnerable areas of Gaza strip.Through this project, UNFPA will enhance the Gaza health system to respond and address urgent needs and gaps in SRH services and strengthen the system and community preparedness capacity preventing system collapse through sustaining and scaling life-saving SRH services, providing critically needed supplies, community engagement and capacity building of staff and enhancing SRH coordination and reporting among various actors in Gaza. Practically, and in order to enrich the intervention and expand its coverage, UNFPA will work on the following domains; • Improve emergency preparedness and response of SRH needs in the health system and the community in the Gaza Strip. • Strengthen access to life-saving SRH services, through supporting major maternities and PHC facilities, with critically needed equipment/instruments, drugs and supplies including family planning commodities, Iron and folic acid to support the response, and capacity building of staff including on adaptive service provision skills (telemedicine, etc.) • Support preconception care, antenatal care and family planning services & information, mainstreaming disability, GBV and psychosocial support, prevention of sexual exploitation activities “PSEA” training of staff. • Enhance the proper referral of SRH services among various providers and levels of care. As chair of GBV Sub-cluster, ensure appropriate linkages and referral of GBV detection and management in the health facilities and the community. • Support networks of community activists and implement community mobilization campaigns including health education/awareness component, that will target the issues from community and women empowerment perspective via educating families and girls on basic gender and human rights issues, including women/girls with disability and their personal hygiene and RH needs, risks “health and social” of early marriage and promotion of healthy outcomes and reproduction, supporting positive participation on relief and preparedness to emergencies. • Support SRH humanitarian platform of coordination and networking by active SRH actors and active linking to Health Cluster reporting mechanisms. Activities: • Provision of life-saving needs; drugs & disposables, PPEs, equipment, instruments and medical furniture to 6 MOH central maternities (including EGH, designated COVID-19 maternity) and 40 PHC clinics (27 MOH and 13 NGOs). *Final list of supplies will be validated with MOH, NGOs partners (at the central level and with end-users health providers at facilities) and coordinated with other actors to avoid duplication.• Training of 200 health care providers/practitioners on EmOC, safe births, preconception care, infection prevention and other RH issues. The training will be based on standard developed protocols by MOH including COVID-19 protocols. • Support SRH coordination platform of major actors in Gaza to ensure complementarity, share experience and sustain regular reporting and monitoring of gaps. increase access to SRH services and information in Gaza. • Improve community emergency preparedness in isolated communities through enhancing awareness and /or linkages with appropriate service delivery points and available linkages to GBV services and referral. • Conduct community-based interventions; supporting community networks, outreach sessions, health education and media campaigns advocating for universal health coverage and right to health and access to services, and linking to protection, GBV, and MHPSS services and PSEA awareness.

3 - Indicators

Monitoring and Evaluation:UNFPA shall integrate project components/activities into its annual work plans with implementing partners (IPs) and will develop a detailed monitoring and evaluation plan, in line with UNFPA’s M&E system and framework, which includes appropriate tools that will be used to track project performance indicators, beneficiaries’ feedback and achievements of results. The monitoring framework shall track progress against achieving key deliverables and milestones both on partners and UNFPA directly implemented activities. UNFPA jointly with IPs will conduct focus group discussions with beneficiaries and regular field monitoring visits to the implementation sites, to assess and track progress towards planned results, and address challenges that may affect implementation. UNFPA and IPs will be ensuring/advocating vulnerable beneficiaries i.e. marginalized people, GBV survivors and women & girls with disabilities are reached and mechanisms to reach them and facilitate their access to services are considered by providers. Also, field monitoring visits may be planned and done jointly with the donors upon their request. The following indicators/targets shall be monitored by UNFPA and partners: - • Number of Gaza health facilities benefited from equipment and supplies provided (prepositioned and for immediate response): 40 • Number of SRH healthcare workers protected with the provision of PPE: 300 • Number of women in reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women receiving quality maternal and nutrition services: 35,000 • Number of people directly benefitting from awareness sessions and health education; reached through RCCE, community campaign’ and media: 50,000 • Number of Health providers benefited from training on several RH issues, including adaptive service provision and PSEA: 250 • Active UNFPA chaired SRH coordination platform, linked to MOH-lead SRH working group and Health Cluster, with clear TORs and action plan.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project components are based on national humanitarian plans, in accordance withIHL, and are guided by international guidelines on sexual and reproductive health(SRH) in emergencies, such as the SPHERE Standards, as well as the Minimal InitialService Package (MISP) for reproductive health in emergencies agreed upon by theInter-Agency Working Group on Reproductive Health in Crises. UNFPA takes theprinciples of “Do No Harm” seriously within its programmes including with itsimplementing partners to ensure safety and dignity of all served beneficiariesincluding women, girls and disabled people in lie with the international IASC standardsand principles including GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response toGender-based Violence in Emergencies. In addition, the project planned interventionsincluding health services will be provided in a manner that keeps all service recipientsaway from any potential to face any hazards or threats, physically and emotionally

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project is targeting all Palestinians in need for SRH healthcare, without anydiscrimination based on gender, refugee status, disability or ethnic background, in allGaza Strip with a special focus on vulnerable and marginalized communities. Provisionof supplies will be through the central stores of project implementing partners and willbe monitored by UNFPA according to internal M&E procedures. Areas for communityoutreach will be carefully chosen in coordination with implementing partners’ affiliatedactive CBO’s and key informants in the area to ensure free access of women, girls anddisabled people to the sessions and available services supported by the project.Participation of both women and men in training and other capacity-building activitieswithin the framework of the project will be strictly applied and monitored by UNFPAthrough field visits and requesting disaggregated data reporting.
