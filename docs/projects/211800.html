Project Summary

According to the 2024 GBV HNO, Voices from Syria 2024, and the GBVIMs 2023 trends reports, GBV continues to be a dominant feature in Syria, affecting the lives of millions of women and girls.
The project aims to ensure the provision of quality GBV response services to GBV survivors and women and girls at risk and that measures are put in place to prevent and mitigate the risks of GBV.
UNFPA works with local IPs to support the delivery of GBV services and will build on its achievements, promising practices and lessons learned to reach the following objectives:
Service Delivery: Increase the availability of and access to specialized GBV services, through different service delivery points (Women and Girls Safe Space and mobile teams). UNFPA will continue providing essential lifesaving GBV services and will provide CVA, specifically in the framework of GBV case management and to ensure that women and girls, including persons with disabilities, can access the care they need in some of the most vulnerable areas in Syria. Furthermore, UNFPA seeks to continue procuring and supporting GBV AoR partners to distribute dignity kits as this has proved a strategic entry point for provision of GBV response services while meeting the hygiene and protection needs of women and girls through the provision of context specific items.
Capacity Building: Improve capacity of service providers and implementing partners to provide high-quality, inclusive, and comprehensive GBV services. UNFPA Syria and Turkey XB will continue to build the capacity of UNFPA partners and, through the GBV AoR, of other GBV actors, including women led-organizations.
GBV Prevention and Social Norms: Challenge negative social norms that prevent the empowerment of women and girls and develop accountable models of engagement of men and boys. Through the Turkey XB hub, UNFPA will continue implementing the full scope of the GBV SC prevention program based on the awareness raising toolkit. UNFPA’s IPs will also carry out the full scope of activities under the “Adolescent Mothers Against All Odds” (AMAL) initiative, which has been designed to meet the immediate needs of adolescent girls who are pregnant or first-time mothers, while simultaneously addressing community awareness and engagement. UNFPA Syria’s four-year social norms pilot project in Aleppo will continue. At the same time, UNFPA will continue GBV information dissemination efforts at the community level, about GBV risks,consequences and available services.
Coordination: UNFPA effectively leads the Whole of Syria GBV coordination structure, supporting GBV coordination in Syria, NW Syria and NE Syria hubs. UNFPA Syria will continue to coordinate the GBV sub-sector, while UNFPA Turkey XB will continue to lead the GBV SC including the GBVIMs technical working group
Protection Risk Analysis
Providing GBV services is critical to ensure survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. If services are not survivor centered, this will have a negative impact on help-seeking behavior with dramatic consequences on survivor's health. Communities may be resistant to engaging around topics of GBV and ultimately prevent women and girls from attending. To mitigate these risks, UNFPA carries out thorough risk analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce risks of GBV. All services will aim to be confidential and survivor centered by building the capacity of partners. UNFPA continuously conducts assessments on barriers to access services including safety audits to identify GBV risk. Women and girls are at the centre of the decision making at every phase of the project implementation.
PSEA
UNFPA assessed and score partners capacity based on a set of eight core standards of PSEA organizational policies and procedures. UNFPA assessed all IPs and they now have full capacity to safeguard, identify report, investigate cases.
