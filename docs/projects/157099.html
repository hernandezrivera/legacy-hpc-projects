Project Summary

This multi-sector intervention will
- address emergency WASH needs (water supply, WASH NFIs, latrines construction and hygiene promotion)
- Provision of livelihood assets, foster self-reliance and contribute to the overall reduction of food insecurity among South Sudanese Refugees currently living in 11 villages within Al Lait locality: Abu Jarra, Shag Allayoun, Fattaha, Dalil Babkier, Joddat, Abo Sofyan, Haskanita, Habib Darma, Dalil Dokhry, Hilat Ali and Al Lait town.

Needs

Within Al Lait locality a total of 18,480 refugees have been registered since the middle of 2016 and are currently located in the 11 villages of: Abu Jarra, Shag Allayoun, Fattaha, Dalil Babkier, Joddat, Abo Sofyan, Haskanita, Habib Darma, Dalil Dokhry, Fajak, Hilat Ali and Al Lait town. Low access of safe water, unsafe sanitation practices, lack of awareness on hand washing and open defecation are common among SSR, IDPs and hosts in Al Lait. The incidence of water borne diseases is high with diarrhea reported as a common disease. This situation has the potential to exacerbate health risks and outbreaks during the rainy season for all 18,480 South Sudanese Refugees currently living in 11 villages of Al Lait locality and Al Lait Town. The purchasing power of the poor households is being deteriorated by the steadily increasing inflation. Prices of food and non-food items are leaving poor and vulnerable households unable to afford the most basic commodities.During the rainy season, South Sudanese men have traditionally traveled to Al Lait to work on local farms. After completion of the harvest they would usually returned to South Sudan. However, since March 2016, thousands of South Sudanese families have arrived in this locality seeking asylum.Self-subsistence farming is the main livelihood strategy for the vast majority of the households living in Al Lait locality. Cereals including sesame and millet are cultivated together with groundnuts, cow pea, vegetables and watermelon. Al Lait has a fertile soil suitable for cultivation, especially for groundnuts. Challenges faced by farmers include access to seeds, lack of knowledge on appropriate agricultural practices and post production losses due to storage and processing. In 2018, COOPI conducted numerous community based meetings and discussions on FSL involving local authorities, stakeholders and potential beneficiaries.

Activities

To ensure an adequate response to the WASH needs of refugees the following actions should be undertaken:- Drilling and equipping at least two new Water Yards. This should be done by prioritizing the villages lacking completely a reliable water source.- Support the local water department to execute the chlorination of at least 20 water sources located in the whole Al Lait locality during the rainy season.- Provision of additional soap for 2 months covering the rainy season targeting 18,480 SSRs, providing 1pcs of laundry soap (200gm) and 1pcs of bathing soap (250gm) per individual per month.- CLTS triggering and post triggering follow-up in 10 villages, support the construction of 400 sanitation facilities at community level (HHs latrines).The following priorities have been identified in the FLS sector:- Provision of good quality agricultural inputs (including seeds, hand tools and equipments)- Provision of a reliable agricultural extension service; and- Provision of donkey carts
