Project Summary

This intervention aims to contribute to PC objective #1 (Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations) and PC objective #2 (Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence) through undertaking the following activities:
(1) Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence, including grave violations against children and against ARA communities, in the Gaza Strip;
(2) Evidence generation and advocacy on the impact of violations on children including grave violations;
(3) Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers;
(4) Legal aid to secure freedom of movement for patients in need of medical care outside Gaza; and
(5) Legal representation for individual victims of IHL/IHRL violations.
The project team will:
Monitor and document over 1,000 cases of IHL/IHRL violations;
Issue over 25 press releases addressing protection concerns and IHL/IHRL violations;
Issue 1 report on children and armed conflicts;
Issue 1 report on IHL/IHRL violations;
Issue 12 monthly statistical updates on violation against children;
Isuue12 monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL against civilian population;
Answer over 50 request of sharing information from humanitarian community;
Conduct 10 briefing to members of the international community in Gaza;
Provide legal representation to 270 patients delayed/denied travel permit to access hospitals outside Gaza provided with legal intervention to secure permits (115 women, 115 men, and 40 children); and
Implement legal awareness sessions targeting 220 farmers, fishers, rural women and staff members of CBOs working in the ARA received legal awareness sessions.
Through high-quality monitoring and documentation, legal counselling and interventions, and raising awareness of at-risk communities, the project strive to strengthen resilience of the most vulnerable and involve more people in challenging the serious patterns of human rights violations. Further, monitoring and documentation data as well as outcomes of legal interventions will inform advocacy messaging vis-à-vis international community with the aim of prompting high-level statements (by HC/UN/EU member states) voicing protection concerns brought to focus under this project.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in the oPt shows no end in sight. In the Gaza Strip, where the crisis is mainly driven by 13-year blockade and closure, the PA salary cuts imposed on public servants in Gaza for the third consecutive year on account of the PA’s financial crisis has added to the struggle of the population. The outbreak of COVID-19 in 2020 further compounded and exposed the economic vulnerabilities in the oPt, particularly in the Gaza Strip, where the employment (PCBS repots) stands at around 45 percent—three folds of that reported in the West Bank—and where food insecurity affected at least 68.5 percent of the population prior to the outbreak (WFP). Even prior to detection of COVID-19 cases in the wider community in Gaza, the outbreak in the West Bank and Israel brought additional challenges to Gaza’ most vulnerable residents, i.e. referral patients. In mid-March 2020, the Israel Coordination and Liaison Administration—in charge of vetting permit applications submitted via the Palestinian General Authority of Civil Affairs—limited exit permits to urgent medical referrals, oncology and cardiology (WHO). The mandatory quarantine imposed by de facto authorities on all residents returning to Gaza may have played a role as well in deterring many patients from applying to exit. In the following two months the number of referrals patients exiting Gaza via Erez averaged about 100-120 patients per month compared to over 1,600 patients exiting via Erez each month (Jan-Feb 2020) before the outbreak. The challenges facing referrals patients were further compounded after 19 May 2020 when Israel, the occupying power, failed to promptly devise an accessible alternative mechanism to the civilian population as the existing mechanism came to a halt after suspension of coordination between Israel and the PA. Halt of coordination came in response to the newly-formed Israeli unity government’s intent to bring the annexation plan to discussion in the Cabinet and/or for vote before the Israeli parliament. Since late-May 2020, referral patients in Gaza had to rely on Israeli human rights organizations or their referral hospitals to submit permit applications on their behalf, while many other applicants were unaware to whom to resort for assistant. It is unclear how many of Gaza’s referral patients missed their medical appointments in Israel and the West Bank during that period. By late August 2020, the UN reached an arrangement with both Israel and the PA whereby the WHO country office is to play an intermediary role to transfer patients’ exit permit applications between the two sides. At the time of writing, no reports have been published indicating the number of submitted exit permit applications under the new mechanism. That being said, with the new mechanism in full operation and the continued easing of COVID-19 restrictive measures, the number of applications is expected to gradually rise to hundreds of applications per month. Past experience prior to the COVID-19 outbreak indicate that at least 30% of applications (20% male applicants and 15% female applicants, with 8% being children) submitted by referral patients are not granted approval, therefore requiring legal interventions. In 2020 (January-September), and despite the outbreak, Israeli Security Forces (ISF) opened fire and/or heavy artillery in over 900 incidents against Palestinians in the ARA (both land and sea). ISF attacks in the ARA and other areas of the Gaza Strip led to the killing of 6 Palestinians (including 2 children), injury of 53 (including 15 children and 1 woman), arrest and detention of 45 (including 5 children and 1 woman) , destruction of 12 fishing boats and equipment, and seizure of 4 fishing boats. In 2021, IHL/IHRL violations are likely to increase as the gradual easing of COVID-19 restrictive measures may lead to increased presence of civilians in/near the ARA where direct attacks are more likely to occur, based on analysis of trends of violations. Access to effective legal remedies remains a challenge for Palestinian victims of ISF attacks in the oPt. During the course of the Great March of Return demonstrations, Al Mezan submitted over 80 criminal complaints to the Israeli Military Advocate General (MAG), requesting criminal investigation into killing and injury of civilians (including killing of 19 children, and injury of 24 children and 3 women Injured). At the time of writing, over 70 cases reaming pending (including 16 pertaining to killing of children and 25 pertaining to maiming of children and women), with only 7 actively under investigation. Al Mezan lawyers will continue to follow-up on pending cases with the MAG office in 2021 and follow-up with the Israeli Attorney General office on a number of appeals against MAG’s decision to close certain cases without/after investigation without pressing any charges against perpetrators. In the face of this situation, Al Mezan continues to prioritize improving the protection against human rights and IHL violations in Gaza through high-quality monitoring and documentation, legal counselling and interventions, and raising awareness of at-risk communities to strength resilience and involve more people in challenging the serious patterns of human rights violations. Further, monitoring and documentation data as well as outcomes of legal interventions will inform advocacy messaging vis-à-vis international community to highlight the lack of genuine accountability.

2 - Activities

Activities under PC objectives #1: (1) Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence, including grave violations against children and against ARA communities, in the Gaza Strip. Fieldworkers will fully document (i.e. collected relevant data using questionnaire forms, as well as testimonies and supporting evidence) on all cases of fatalities, cases involving maiming of children and women, attacks against hospitals and schools, arrests and detentions, and damage to civilian properties. Victims will be referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to receive legal counseling and intervention. PwDs in need of legal services will be visited at home by the lawyer to provide legal counseling and sign documents relevant to legal intervention. (2) Documentation data will feed into the database for analysis of violation trends and will be utilized to draft press releases, reports, factsheets, monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations and violations against children. The produced materials will be disseminated to over 700 professionals, media organizations, and NGOs through existing mailing list, and will also be shared via Al Mezan’s website and social media platforms. Data will also be used to answers requests for sharing information received on weekly basis from humanitarian actors working in oPt. (3) Locally, representatives of Al Mezan will meet in Gaza with members of the international community to brief them on violation trends and protection concerns. Activities under PC objective #2: (4) Provision of legal aid victims of serious IHL/IHRL violations: first, though civil notifications which are sent to the Israeli Ministry of Defense within 60 days of the incident to secure the victim’s right to seek reparation through Israeli justice system, and second, by sending complaints to the Military Advocate General (MAG) requesting a criminal investigation into the incident in question or to request return of seized boats and fishing equipment. (5) Lawyers will also continue to follow-up with the MAG and provide any requested material concerning 75 pending complaints submitted on behalf of casualties of the Great March of Return requesting criminal investigation. (6) Legal representation before Israeli administrative bodies for patients delayed/denied exit permits to access treatment outside Gaza. In case of child patients, intervention will also aim to secure exit permits for the child’s companion without whom the child cannot travel to their designated hospital. (7) Awareness raising sessions for ARA communities, particularly farmers and fishers, on protection of civilians in armed conflicts, legal mechanisms to seek protection and redress, and available legal aid and how to access it. COVID-19 risk-mitigation measures:Visible notice posted at the entrances of Al Mezan’s branches instructing all visiting beneficiaries to wear face masks and avoid physical contact with staff. In addition, hand sanitizers have been placed around the office and at the reception for visiting beneficiaries to use. Similarly, staff are instructed to abide by the same rules. When feasible, beneficiaries are encouraged to send supporting evidence needed for legal interventions via email or WhatApp instead of handing-in hard copies at our offices. Participants in awareness sessions will adhere to safe distances when attending and, when possible, will be held in open-air spaces. Sanitizers and face mask will be made available to all participants. HRP and FTS reprting:Al Mezan will report on relevant indicators to HRP periodic reporting and will also disclose financial contribution towards the proposed intervention on the FTS.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Given the fact that affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation, their involvement starts at an early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Further, victims of violations are referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to discuss their legal options. Legal services are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle. The recipients of legal services are informed of possible ramifications of the legal actions, and it will be made clear that the final decisions to initiate any legal action is theirs to take. Al Mezan's code of conduct, read and signed by all staff members, sets clear guidelines for service providers on dealing with beneficiaries, particularly children who in case of being the recipients of any service must be accompanied by their legal guardian at all times.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Monitoring of IHL/IHRL violations will encompass all affected communities in the Gaza Strip (children, women, men) while documentation work will cover all cases of killings and priorities documenting cases of injuries for these groups in the following order: children; women; PwDs; medical personnel; and journalists (during documentation of injuries, cases with severe to moderate injuries will take precedence over cases with below-moderate injuries). During monitoring and documentation action, victims who are identified by fieldworkers as potential beneficiaries of legal intervention are refereed to lawyers. In circumstances were victim's condition/injures/PwDs hinder his/her ability to reach Al Mezan's offices, lawyers will arrange a home visit to provide legal aid, take affidavits, and sign power of attorney.
