Project Summary

The action assists humanitarian aid organizations in fulfilling their mandates through the provision of essential access and security coordination, information services and the availability of expertise. As a result, NGOs and humanitarian partners will better understand, design, and manage their operational capacity and to improve humanitarian access while reducing the risks that will confront them.
The monitoring mission provides scheduled and on-demand services. INSO reporting targets audiences are operating or planning to operate in areas of high risk and local engagement.
The mission delivers a monthly analytical report which includes thematic topics and analysis, data and trend information and mapping, and flash alerts and security reports. Monthly meetings will take place at NGO hubs. Members can access to our independent safety advisors for data, research and expert technical advice, and assessments. Coordination and representation on common and prevalent issues within the beneficiary and partner group, and with authorities, such as civil-military representatives, is a structural component of the mission.
Monitoring services:
• 24/7 Threat Warning System (Flash alerts) and security reports on conflict and access incidents affecting the humanitarian community;
• Monthly analytical reports;
• Monthly meetings;
• Independent Security Advisory services.
Our standardized collection and verification of data feed the INSO Ukraine database of 45199 conflict and access incidents (affecting humanitarian action) verified and recorded between August 1, 2015, and October 2019 (as of 15 October 2019). INSO Ukraine data informs INSO’s global information products, such as INSO Key Data Analysis and Safety and Access Review, reaching 871 organizations worldwide (approximately 10,000 email recipients).
INSO services are free of charge. INSO does not and will not charge NGOs to become members or to access any INSO services.
Since beneficiaries are organizations, not individuals, issues of gender balance will not arise. In addition, most of our beneficiaries are working with highly vulnerable people.
The NGO community in 2015 directly requested the action, and the modified action agreed in 2018 by members and donors.
We expect that NGO safety will remain vulnerable in areas affected by conflict and specifically for frontline NGOs. INSO Ukraine is a scalable response and, where resourced sufficiently, can respond if needs increase. INSO Annual planning 2020 will determine the continuation of the action and similar cost. In addition to our current donors USAID/OFDA and German MFA, INSO has begun discussions with GAC Canada, amongst others, for additional partnership in 2020.
The mission focuses on Donetska and Luhanska regions of Ukraine and will maintain its coordination office in Kyiv to engage partners at the capital level. INSO deploys field monitors at locations in government and non-government-controlled areas of Donetska and Luhanska Oblasts.

1 - Needs

The action addresses the critical need for impartial, reliable and timely information and advice on safety, risk management and access in a continuously evolving conflict-affected environment. Information sharing and coordination are vital for evaluating security risks and making decisions on acceptable risk and mitigation. INSO is the default mechanism for NGO safety coordination in Ukraine. It is the only NGO-driven platform in Ukraine allowing its members to receive and share on regular basis security- and access-related information in near to real-time. Informed by INSO, NGOs have taken great care to manage their exposure to conflict-related risk and inform their operational decisions. Therefore, given much uncertainty, the complex operational environment and a multitude of risks that humanitarian NGOs face in Ukraine, there is a clear need for continued coordination and sharing of information and analysis and greater NGO collaboration on issues related to safe humanitarian access. INSO Ukraine plays a critical role in assisting NGOs in making informed access decisions, while simultaneously building their capacity to be aware of, plan for and mitigate potential safety/security risks.Considering the greater exposure local NGOs face, INSO is expanding membership to include more national NGOs. Many national NGOs who operate in local languages and institutionally do not benefit from physical and documentary resources that are mainly available in English will benefit bilaterally and from tailored ‘On Request’ services and monthly meetings, aligned to the Russian/Ukrainian speaking audience. Efforts to de-escalate have led to brief periods of relative calm but have failed in deconfliction in a manner that matters to civilians. Most recently, the sides re-committed to a ceasefire on 21 July 2019 ahead of the wheat harvest in Ukraine and the de-facto DNR and LNR. On 11 September 2019, the Normandy Four Political Advisers agreed on the text of the 'Steinmeier formula' for implementation in Ukrainian legislation. The 'Steinmeier formula' should be incorporated in a new law on special provisions of local self-government in certain districts of Donetsk and Luhansk regions through holding local elections. The approval of such a peace plan has received a mixed reaction in Ukraine, as many Ukrainians think that agreeing to such conditions means to surrender to the opposing party to the conflict. In September - October, thousands of people took part in the rallies 'No capitulation' against what they say are unacceptable concessions by the Ukrainian government over the Donbas region.Based on the analysis of trends and dynamics of the conflict, INSO foresees further security developments along the LoC and highlights potential challenges for humanitarian actors due to the implementation of peace initiatives. Besides raising awareness and reducing risk, INSO Ukraine's reporting supported humanitarian principles by providing a neutral and independent conflict analysis in that pre- and post-election period.Beyond directly contributing to NGO safety and access, INSO services helped to reduce the costs of security risk management for individual organizations and redirect funds to the delivery of humanitarian goods and services. According to the May 2019 beneficiary survey, when asked how respondents use our products, most said for 'Contextual knowledge' (81%), followed up by for 'Confirmation of Incidents' (71%), 'Area Assessments' (61%), 'Input for Senior Management' (52%), 'Informing Contraction/Expansion of Programming/Footprint/Movement' (42%), 'For Independent Advice' (35%), 'For Independent Capacity' (32%), 'Development of Operating Strategy' (26%), 'Advocacy (10%) and In-extremis/Crisis Management Support' (6%).In sum, this project aims to sustain and advance the continued provision of INSO expertise, information, coordination, and analyses enabling humanitarian actors to operate safely and with increasing effectiveness.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Objective #1: Credible, reliable and high-quality safety information, analysis and advice is provided to NGOs in a timely and appropriate manner (i) 24/7 flash alerting system (via SMS and Email) – will be provided prior to or on the immediate occurrence of an incident carrying an immediate potential threat to NGOs and requiring immediate operational response (e.g. movement restriction). (ii) Security reports – brief analytical incident specific reports distributed via email upon the occurrence or in anticipation of a significant incident, including incidents directly affecting the humanitarian community, which do not require an immediate operational response. (iii) Monthly analysis reports – will provide in-depth analysis, including key data, mapping and trends for the reporting period. (iv) Independent security advisory service - INSO staff will be available to provide individual agencies with a tailored piece of confidential security advice, assessment or referral as needed (i.e. general overview/orientation for newly arrived staff; road or area assessments; project planning advice; crisis response; critical incident management; point of contact referrals etc). Objective #2: NGOs are supported in their own safety & emergency management capacity. (i) Monthly Meetings – regular meetings in the field (Slovyansk, Sievierodonetsk and Mariupol) focusing on tactical level events occurring in the operational area covered and providing a forum for NGOs to share information and reflect on risks in their operational environment.
