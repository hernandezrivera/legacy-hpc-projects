Project Summary

The ongoing project will continue to support the ongoing WASH sector activities in Borno state by providing a comprehensive WASH approach in implementing complete WASH packages and accountability to affected people in the hotspot locations of Gwoza LGA in Pulka specifically in (Camp C and sector 5 in the host community), Konduga LGA, Jere LGA, MMC, and Mafa LGA.
MI will provide WASH life-saving services through improving access to safe and sufficient drinking water supply, safe/adequate and dignified sanitation facilities, hygiene supplies, and information to mitigate risks related to public health while mainstreaming of protection, gender equality for vulnerable IDPs, returning refugees, and host communities that meets the differing needs for men, women, boys, girls, and persons with disabilities.
Activities will also include cholera preparedness and response, construction of water and sanitation infrastructure to newly-arrived IDPs, operation and maintenance of existing WASH infrastructure including water supply systems, latrines, and bathing facilities, solid waste management and distribution of hygiene kits, which are in line with the WASH sector strategy.
Malteser International will continue to provide adequate measures to curtail the spread of the Coronavirus through the distribution of hygiene kits, provision of specialized leg-pedalled handwashing stands, Sensitization/Mass Messaging of populace across all demography in camps and host communities. The targeted beneficiaries for this response will include IDPs, host communities, returnees, and most vulnerable segments from the communities such as female-headed households, elderly persons, and persons with disabilities who will be identified and prioritized in collaboration with authorities and community leaders.

1 - Needs

Malteser International identifies humanitarian needs and responds accordingly by relying on the primary source of information through conducting its own needs assessment and secondary source of information from the Humanitarian Needs Overview conducted by UN-OCHA as well as relevant information from WASH cluster humanitarian gaps mapping and multi sectoral need assessment. According to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, the major humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s north-eastern states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY states) will persist and continue to affect millions of people. Overall, 13.1 million people are in need throughout the country, the BAY states are hosting the vast majority – with around 8.7 million people in need (66%). It is therefore the region that is most severely affected by the insurgencies. While 1.9 million people are internally displaced, some 250,000 have also found refuge in neighbouring countries such as Cameroon, Chad and Niger. In Borno State alone, where 81% of IDPs are hosted, roughly half of them have settled within the host communities, while the other half stay at IDP camps. The Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 targets 6.4 million people in need, of which 57% are host communities, 26% internally displaced people and 17% returnees., WASH needs (mainly access to clean, safe water for drinking, hygiene, and sanitation) have continued to increase in scale and severity across north-east Nigeria. The COVID-19 outbreak has made WASH needs more complex. An estimated 2.8 million people across north-east Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services. The high need for WASH services amongst IDPs and returnees has increased compared to last year, mainly due to continuous displacement and the volume of expected returns. Malteser International actively participated in the data collection for the multi-sectoral needs assessment (MSNA) of 2021. In close cooperation with REACH and the inter-sector working group, Malteser International conducted needs assessments in its project implementation areas and submitted the data for compilation. In addition, Malteser International has been carrying out regular service monitoring as well as participatory methods, such as focus group discussions, taking into account the differing needs of men, women, girls, boys, people with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups.

2 - Activities

Water supply activities: 1- Construction/Upgrading/Rehabilitation of water supply systems; including drilling of boreholes, installation of hybrid motorized pumps, installation of water tanks and solar panels, construction of tap-stands and laundry aprons. 2- Operation and Maintenance of existing water supply systems through the provision of Spare parts/tool Kits and capacity building training for communities to improve access to safe water supply 3- Conduct periodic/monthly Water Quality Testing through the provision of Water Quality Testing Kit (H2S strips and Pool Testers) to test Free Residual Chlorine and Coliforms bacteria to ensure water quality. Sanitation activities: 1- Construct/Rehabilitate gender-segregated latrines/bathing facilities to eliminate open defecation and reduce public health risks. 2- Support Operation and Maintenance (O&M) of gender-segregated latrines through the provision of latrines cleaning kits to WASH Committees to improve proper hygiene practices. 3- Support Drainage Control and Solid Waste Management through the provision of Sanitation tool kits to WASH committees. 4- Support Desludging of latrines in IDP camps across Borno state. Hygiene Promotion: 1- Conduct new/refresher hygiene promotion training and sessions to new/existing Hygiene Promoters, and provide Information Education and Communication (IEC) to aid Hygiene Promoters to facilitate the dissemination of hygiene messages. 2- Distribution of Emergency Hygiene Kits which include soap, jerry-cans, sanitary pads for the new arrival. 3- Distribution of Hygiene Replenishment Kits (sanitary pads, soap) to ensure improved personal hygiene. 4- Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to monitor effectiveness, sustainability, quality, quantity, and use of hygiene items distributed in hygiene and replenishment kits. Cholera Preparedness and Response: 1. Deployment of Cholera response team and Provision of Cholera outbreak kits. 2. Conduct General hygiene awareness to contain the cholera outbreak. Corona Virus Prevention Activities: Malteser International will proactively engage in a number of activities and practices that is essential for protecting human health and preventing human to human transmission of the Coronavirus during the outbreak. Activities and practices such as.1. General Sensitization/Key Messaging: A team of Hygiene Promoters will be tasked with the responsibility of creating awareness and passing key messages in IDP camps etc. on ways to prevent the spread of Corona virus, basic underlining symptoms, and what to do if one is infected. Messaging is to be conducted at the household level while maintaining appropriate distancing and avoiding gathering people for mass messaging. 2. Distribution of Hygiene Kits: In line with sensitization and awareness campaigns, Malteser International will distribute hygiene kits consisting of soap, sanitizer etc to beneficiaries at IDP camps and host communities for the purpose of improving personal hygiene thereby reducing the risk of human-to-human transmission of the Coronavirus. Additionally, in order to minimize the risk of exposure of staff and beneficiaries during distribution Malteser International has revised its hygiene kits distribution SOPs by actively managing the flow of traffic while maintaining the required social distancing at waiting areas, providing a hand washing station at the entrance and exit of distribution sites. 3. Malteser International has prioritized the distribution of handwashing stations and soap, alongside HP messaging posters at certain locations such as; latrines, water collection points, distribution site, entrance to camps etc

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Malteser International is currently implementing protection services at Pulka, Gwoza LGA, and Mashamari, Konduga LGA providing psychosocial support, GBV case management, and skill acquisition/empowerment to GBV survivors. Aligning with its protection program Malteser International will ensure streamlining of protection to reduce risks to vulnerable populations through its WASH technical designs and activity implementation. Malteser Nigeria will strive to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV and protection issues through creating a safe, dignified and friendly environment for women and children making facilities safely accessible to individuals with special needs. Malteser International will adhere to the guidelines set by the IASC for Integrating Gender-Based Violence mainstreaming in humanitarian action and the Humanity and Inclusion guidelines in the design of facilities and services for people with disabilities. To ensure safety and dignified access for WASH facilities to women, children, and people with disabilities, Malteser International will undertake the following program approaches: • Construction/rehabilitation of latrines which are gender-segregated for women and men and constructed at suitable locations and distances which will be discussed and agreed upon with community members. • Setting up separate women and men’s committees in Focus Group Discussion (FGD) to provide spaces for the participation of and accountability to the community about the design, implementation, information sharing, and opportunity for feedback.• Positioning of latrines at visible locations far from bush or dark areas to discourage GBV perpetrators to commit acts of violence, while at the same time, ensuring dignified access to women, adolescent girls and other users through installation of privacy screens/partitions to ensure privacy. • A gender-sensitive approach to MEAL will be adopted to ensure that WASH program is having intended impacts to meet the needs of women, men, boys and girls while addressing sexual exploitation and abuse.• Installation of strong locks from inside doors of latrines to ensures privacy and protection while latrines are used by girls, boys and women. • Installation of waste bin inside the latrine designed for women and girls to dispose sanitary items appropriately, disposal of the content from the waste bin to the communal disposal sites will be a part of the regular cleaning and maintenance schedule which will be supervised by female hygiene and sanitation volunteers. • Water systems located at safe and close distance will be rehabilitated to ensure safety of women and children and reduce waiting time for water collection.• In each block of 5 latrines rehabilitated, 1 latrine will be designated to people with disabilities. This latrine will be provided with a door which is wide enough to ensure access to those with limited mobility and their caregivers where suitable; handles and other utensils to support mobility will also be installed to facilitate the use of the latrine. To ensure the project enables equal and impartial access to WASH services with consideration of people with special needs, MI will undertake the following activities: • Hygiene promotion volunteers will be dispatched to homes of the people with limited mobility and elderly people to present the content from the community level hygiene and sanitation activities as well as obtain their perspectives for the design of the services and facilities to be more accessible to their special needs. • Hygiene kits and other items such as chlorine tablets will be delivered to individuals with disability/ limited mobility to their homes, rather than to general distribution sites for general population • Hygiene promoters will use IECs for special presentations of hygiene practices to individuals with impaired hearing, if any, in order to ensure equal/ equitable access to messaging.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In ensuring integrity and accountability in our work as per the Core Humanitarian Standard, feedback from beneficiaries will be received via the following channels;•Community Volunteers will be the primary channel through which MI receives feedback from camp about its Services. Community volunteers are functional in all camps where MI is rendering WASH Services.•Complaint Desk: The M&E will station a complaint desk at distribution site to record requests, complaints and feedback from beneficiaries during NFI distributions. M&E officer will resolve simple complaints at the distribution spot and refer a complex one to the team after distribution.•Feedback boxes (suggestion boxes): will also be used to gather feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The boxes will be located inside the camp. Complaints/suggestion will be retrieved at the end of the month by dedicated MI staff in the presence of the Camp Leaders.•Protection caseworkers will use WGSS to facilitate educational and recreational activities and collect feedback from survivors after rendering services to ascertain the survivor's satisfaction with the support provided and welcome suggestion if any.•Additional informal channels for capturing feedback, requests and complaints from camp and host communities include periodic community meetings and focus group discussions conducted by staff from the (WASH, WGSS caseworkers and M&E department), regular post-distribution monitoring visits provide additional opportunities for IDPs to share feedback. Awareness and Information will be communicated to beneficiaries through community meetings, FGDs or face-to-face interactions with volunteers and staff.The Feedback Response Mechanism operates on the principle of confidentiality, meaning that complaints made through the mechanism are not shared beyond people who need to know about them to address them
