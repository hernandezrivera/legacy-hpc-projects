Project Summary

As the Protection prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict and other hazards like COVID-19, floods, a highly militarized environment, cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different tribes exacerbate the risk of Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood. VOP will continue to work with populations affected by displacement and other stakeholders, to enhance the protective environment of individuals and communities, and identify, prevent and address protection risks. The project objective is aligned with the Protection Cluster and HRP objectives by providing immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas, and enhancing their resilience and preparedness. The project targets 15,100 GP, 12,039 GBV, 17,000 CP, a total of 44,139 people (22,139 internally displaced persons, 8,017 host communities, 7,500 refugees and 6,500 returnees). VOP PC aim to focus on four components which includes: Analysis, intervention, response and advocacy. VOP will work with vulnerable groups and individuals like the elderly and people with disabilities, to identify protection risks, coping strategies, intentions and resilience among the affected population, to ensure preparedness and resilience of communities and at-risk individuals are enhanced. VOP will facilitate focus group discussions, conduct information dissemination, awareness raising, training on human rights to increase populations prevent and mitigate risks. VOP will conduct peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence including; building capacity of local women groups, individuals, and leaders on participation in meetings, mediation and peaceful resolution of conflicts, dispute resolutions. VOP GBV aims to Ensure the provision of critical protection services through an integrated/mainstreamed approach to address the priority needs of targeted vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and inclusion of people with disability and people living with HIV/AIDs in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. VOP will provide psychosocial support services, women and girls friendly spaces, CBP preparedness, Cash base intervention and protection risk mitigation. Individual/ comprehensive case management and recreational activities. VOP will continue with empowerment of women through income generating activities that support their livelihood. A selected group will be train on business skills on starting their own business in developing a business plan. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, VOP will specifically focus on engaging men and boys through EMAP strategy in its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles and ensuring gender gaps are covered through gender responsiveness. VOP as a GBV lead in GPAA assign by GBV Sub Cluster will strengthening coordination with the GBV Working group, as well as coordinating strengthening of the GBV referral pathways. VOP CP aims to provide child protection service to vulnerable, conflict affected children through a holistic approach consisting of various activities which strengthen each other. The activity intends to address lifesaving needs of children through structured and comprehensive case management, coupled with PSS provision (including PFA) and FTR services for girls and boys with protection needs (UASC, CAAFAG and, orphans and children with other specific needs identified including children with disability. VOP will provide PSS/ identification activities, malnutrition screenings and referrals for children, a CFS with daily activities in each location has proven to be successful. VOP adopts an integrated, multi-sector approach, linking FSL, S/NFIs, (WASH), education, health and Camp Coordination, HLP and Camp Management sectors. VOP will ensure Community engagement and have an exit strategy plan to sustain the implementation of the project.

1 - Needs

In Jonglei (Pibor) like other former states affected by the decades of conflicts in South Sudan, Rape, child labour, and sexual violence have been perpetrated on women, girls and children during crisis yet opportunities to access basic life-saving services have been limited. The need for multi-sectoral humanitarian response to address the multiple needs of the population and scaling up of existing services to meet the growing demand of most vulnerable and conflict affected population therefore becomes eminent. Pibor counties have experienced multiple displacements and insecurity due to tribal internal conflicts and flooding. Key drivers of conflict specifically in the above-mentioned counties were identified as food insecurity, flooding, cattle raiding/theft for commercial purposes and rising bride prices. The heavy movements of IDPs during the last years and the lack of resources have increased the number of vulnerable women, children and, raising the importance of UASC making FTR services. Among the most urgent child protection risks identified are the high number of unaccompanied, separated children, orphans, and women headed families and children-headed households. Also prevalent are high levels of psychosocial distress of the community in general, among both children and adults, given by the high level of insecurity and the spread of various forms of violence. This is exacerbated by the lack of structures able to respond to the current insecurity in GPAA) The Ongoing flood, age groups and tribal violence in Pibor escalated in August 2022, with an estimated 142,057 people displaced. While more than 80,000 people were displaced by sub-national violence in Pibor in August 2022 and the violence led to the disruption of humanitarian services which is one of the reasons why Pibor is in catastrophic need. Lack of progress on governance and peace building, combined with reduced regional economic activity and increased pressure on public finances, is likely to result in continued localized and sub-national violence with armed groups fighting for control over territory and resources. Although cessation of hostilities is holding in most places, fighting between parties of the revitalized peace agreement continues in parts of Jonglei and Pibor in particular. Forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices – is a persistent problem in Pibor which is ranked in a catastrophic need as per 2022 Gender based violence (GBV) is grossly underreported due to fear of retaliation and social stigma. Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different age group, clans/ tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in Pibor. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. South Sudan 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), as well as assessments conducted by VOP/ inter Agency, findings show that 50% of girls in South Sudan get married before the age of 18 years which has been worsened by closed school’s due flood. Historically, Nuer, Dinka and Murle pastoralists all participated in cyclical of cattle raiding and child abduction. Women/ girls are being raped and abducted, or separated from their families during the fight. protection risks continue to affect the South Sudanese population especially in Pibor County. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2022. A total of 8.9 million women, men, girls and boys are expected to be in need in 2023 including refugees across all of South Sudan’s 71 counties. VOP will provide a holistic prevention, risk mitigation/ response interventions, the project will directly support 44,139 (20,000 Women, 14,100 girls, 4,539 men and 5,500 boys) in Pibor.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Though installation of several complaint response mechanisms in confidential but accessible places, though use of community child protection volunteers who will use monthly door to door methodology to collect complaints and give feedback and through use of toll-free lines which vulnerable people will use to report complaints and receive feedback and through Focus group discussions while ensuring age, gender and diversity inclusion. Women and girls friendly spaces will also be used for feedback mechanisms. Use of Pictorial Information education and communications (IEC) will also be used

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through participatory community engagement meetings, needs assessments, inception meetings, through consultative meetings, and also awareness and social mobilization campaigns by use of community radio, use of complaint boxes, tool free lines, and community-based protection committee members reaching and providing services through door-to-door methodology to extreme vulnerable individuals. Children are consulted through participatory, child friendly techniques in safe and protective environments. Pictorial referral pathways will also be disseminated to the beneficiaries/ people of concern.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

VOP staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Child protection and safeguarding code of conduct is also signed and trainings are also provided for staff and community structures that work with children. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. VOP staff will be training/ empowering the community on PSEA through community radio, radio talk show and through social distancing community meeting and awareness raising through door-to-door methodology. The project will establish and disseminate pictorial information on awareness raising on S/NFI mechanisms (prevention, risk mitigation, response and referral pathway) to the community. During case management the case workers have mechanisms to check survivor feedback on services received from VOP has a series of practical and user-friendly tools and checklists that all staff are required to use both at management and at the field/community level to ensure sustainability, local ownership, do-no-harm and effective implementation of program.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project conducts regular gender and age-appropriate focus group Discussions in order to encourage feedback from the communities. Within VOP supported communities including schools , suggestion boxes exist to allow feedback from beneficiaries and also feedback of complaint boxes are fixed in easily accessible but confidential places like at churches, trading centers among, schools among others and the feedback will trigger conducting of needs assessment/ gender analysis, service mapping and community stake holders mapping and ensure that the needs of the people of concern is addressed immediately, referred for integration of mainstreamed to other sectors and also the feedback will be used for future proposal developments for funding to ensure the project has addressed the needs of the beneficiaries and assistance is provided.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

VOP has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the VOP. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the VOP to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. VOP’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as sexual exploitation and abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the VOP will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold VOP accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
