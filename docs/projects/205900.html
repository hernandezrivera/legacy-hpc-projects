Project Summary

Rubkona County, in the Unity State of South Sudan, faces significant challenges in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene service provision. It has experienced the worst flooding in 60 years affecting critical infrastructure, including increased population of displaced people, deteriorating the living conditions, occasional outbreaks of waterborne diseases and increased food insecurity and malnutrition. Sanitation and hygiene practices are especially a concern with just 10% of the population having access to improved sanitation (1). Poor governance of community infrastructure has also been an issue, with WASH coverage still very low at 41%, basic sanitation at 11 % and 63% practicing open defecation (2). There has therefore been cases of outbreaks of cholera and hepatitis E from 2018 till now (3). Despite this being sporadic, risk of transmission to the populace is substantial due to increased population movements, IDPs, persistent flooding and low access to WASH services. Protection issues are prevalent in IDP camps with latrines and bathing facilities, constructed of plastic sheets, lacking gender disaggregation and internal locks, being unsafe for women and girls, especially at night (4).
The resulting population displacement of over 200000 IDPs and the Sudan conflict exacerbate the situation with increased returnees in Rotriak, additionally straining the WASH situation (5). Community members have had their livelihoods and ability to cope compromised with access to resources becoming more difficult due to the livelihoods disturbances. Women and girls especially have difficulties in accessing resources with some being forced to use pieces of old clothing due to a shortage of menstrual hygiene material. With the shortage of clean water and soap, these cloths are washed in flood water, which can lead to infections and other health issues.
Without a response, the waterborne diseases will likely reappear, with children under 5 years and women potentially seriously affected. The program aims to construct of gender-sensitive and inclusive public sanitation facilities, conduct hygiene promotion, distribution of WASH kits, and rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure at health facilities. The project will focus on community-inclusive approaches to ensure participation of all affected groups. The community’s inclusion along with partnership with WASH partners, government and community leadership including women, girls, and people with disabilities in the project’s planning and implementation will improve project sustainability.
The focus will be on improved hygiene practices in the community via targeted hygiene promotion and coordination of community groups. WASH committees will be established to work with partners involved in WASH programming for information sharing.
The project will target 16889 of the most vulnerable individuals (60% women) and oher vulnerable persons affected by climate shocks. Target beneficiaries will be children, women, poor subsistence farmers, IDPs, returnees and refugees who lack or have limited access to WASH services, combined with limited capacity to mitigate against diseases. Other beneficiaries include VPMs, Water User Committees and the wider communities, Community Based Organizations (CBO’s), Faith-Based Organizations (FBO’s), and the private sector. Health facilities will also be targeted and improvement of WASH facilities on these will benefit all the mentioned target groups.

2. Project activities

The project will rehabilitate 8 boreholes, 5 in the community and 3 at health and nutrition centres. This will be done to assist in the provision of clean water to the community and at health facilities and these boreholes will be rehabilitated using climate-resilient designs to withstand future flood events.A total of 5177 kits will be distributed in the community to address WASH and MHM needs in the community. Of these, the project will distribute 3977 WASH hygiene kits to vulnerable households with severe acute malnutrition and with these consisting of, among other identified items, 20 litre water containers/2kg Soap/Aquatap tablets. Women and girls will receive resources to manage their menstruation safely and hygienically with 1200 MHM kits, distributed, targeting the most vulnerable women and girls. During distribution of the kits, information related to handwashing, GBV and where to access services will be communicated along with the proper management for these kits.The project will construct 25 public community latrines blocks/stances in the community and 18 latrines blocks/stances at 7 Health facilities and establish incinerators at 3 Health facilities and desludging of health latrines. The latrines will be constructed considering access, protection and MHM considerations for women and girls and these will be engaged in the siting the latrines. Cubicles will be disaggregated by gender, with gender-marked doors and inside locks to ensure privacy and safety of women and girls including access provision for people with disabilities.The project will conduct public health education campaigns to ensure behavior change messaging via RCCE activities. Social, and cultural norms that affect the adoption of WASH practices, and gender-based taboos around menstruation or open defecation will be addressed through engagement of men and boys.
