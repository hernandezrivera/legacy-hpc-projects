Project Summary

WHO is leading the health sector to support the MoHFW with the mandate and capacity to set priorities, minimum standards, and support quality health service delivery, and subsequently is aligned with the overall Health Sector Strategies delineated in the Joint Response Plan 2022. The activities set under this project have been selected based on the Health Sector needs highlighted by preliminary findings of Joint Multisectoral Needs Assessment (J-MSNA)- October 2021, Health Facility Gap Analysis - October 2021 and Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment May 2021.

WHO will continue supporting the Government of Bangladesh and partners towards equity and quality of services, advocate on behalf of the Health Sector on inadequately addressed issues; and to respond outbreaks of communicable diseases including COVID-19 through a well-established response system and strengthening outbreak/pandemic response and routine immunization system in line with national and global structures, while continuing to deliver an effective health response to the Rohingya crisis through increased investment in public/local institutions and capacity development of local government, in addition to that of partners. This is expected to ensure sustainability of interventions and benefit host community as well as Rohingya while contributing to building resilient systems for future unforeseen events and emergencies.
WHO will focus on monitoring and supporting revitalization of utilization of essential services with quality service delivery as part of efforts to sustain what is achieved in the two and half year before the COVID-19, while maintaining capacity to continue support response to emerging needs that result from emergencies due to natural disasters, man-made and technological disasters and other disease outbreaks in this protracted humanitarian crisis over and above ensuring continuity of emergency services during the monsoon and cyclone seasons and disaster risk reduction, resilience and contributing to addressing climate change
WHO intends to continue providing regular and timely information on public health risks and requirements and filling gaps through effective coordination of partners, and effective response ensuring access to life saving interventions mediated by rationalized health service delivery system.
The underlying principles of WHO’s work remain and will be reinforced to liaise with national government on the governance and leadership through Civil Surgeon’s Office and MOHFW Coordination Centre, support in developing and adapting standards, guidelines and protocols, extending of national programs to the refugee settings and; avail health workforce, vaccines and essential technology, health information, service delivery based on minimum essential services package, for neonates, children, boys and girls and women and men in both the Rohingyas and host community.
The planned interventions will be guided by periodical service availability assessments, and external independent reviews and assessments will be used as an opportunity for the Rohingya and host community populations to review the work of WHO

1 - Identified Needs

• The need to monitor access to healthcare services and treatment at primary healthcare center and clinic by taking initiatives to eliminate barriers related to healthcare • The need to monitor the status of recipients of health care treatment by ensuring essential health service delivery resulting from the COVID-19, considering a slight reversal of the negative impacts of the COVID-19 outbreak on health-seeking behavior, and increasing capacity to develop as a more resilient health systems within the camps and adjacent host community.• The need for coordinated health response with the ministry of health and health partners providing health services to the affected population in Rohingya camps and host community remains priority. This has ensured minimal duplication of efforts, fill critical gaps on availability, safe access and quality of health services for men, women, boys, girls and at-risk groups including the rehabilitation services for those with disabilities • The refugee population remain vulnerable to weather-related emergencies which also impact disease trends disproportionately for men, women, boys and girls. There is need for continued coordination of emergency response and preparedness in relation to monsoon and cyclone to minimize health impact on the affected Rohingya population and host community• Need to sustain improvements in immunization coverage and quality of services to achieve coverage of >95% and dropout rate of less than 5%. • Need to ensure institutionalize infection prevention and control and adherence to quality, respectful implementation of the minimum standards for services in health facilities that address the needs• Need to deliver comprehensive emergency obstetric care with services much closer to the Rohingya refugees, newborn care, family planning provision (particularly increasing uptake of long-acting methods) and be responsive to adolescent health needs•Need to operationalize prehospital and critical patient care management systems for girls and boys, women and men• Need for continued strengthening of health facilities to deliver GBV care based following several capacity building initiates that have been undertaken to bridge identified gaps. Dedicated supportive supervision, in addition• Need for continued advocacy effort of extending national health programs (such as EPI, TB/HIV/AIDS) to Rohingya men, women, boys and girls • Address shortages in Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) management, laboratory diagnostic capacity and build on community level behavioral adjustments for health promotion and lifestyles• Address mental health and psychosocial support training gaps, advocacy of child mental health services and address access to information on substance abuse to inform response interventions through collaborative arrangements • Need for health care waste management solution including safe disposal of expired medicines and enhanced water quality surveillance in collaboration with DPHE, UNICEF and WASH actors.• Rational use of medicines to reduce risk for antimicrobial resistance.• Need to address risks for communicable diseases, strengthen and sustain system for prevention, early detection and response to outbreak prone diseases and impacts of climate change

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Work with government and health sector partners to monitor quality of (through camp health focal points), and extend utilization of essential health services and provision of health needs considering humanitarian development nexus, engage household decision-makers in health education, health promotion, awareness and establishment of a robust prehospital emergency care services to strengthen referral pathways and health seeking behaviors; 2. Roll-out of WHO PEN package, mhGAP module, approaches for substance abuse, child/adolescent mental health, and other interventions for non-communicable diseases; 3. Support GBV services with technical collaboration and other initiatives, and supplement SRHR gaps and needs for comprehensive SRMNCAH interventions through capacity building, skills lab, and gap filling commodities and equipment; 4. Support the identification and set up strategies to address emerging needs including COVID-19, other infectious diseases, and related chronic conditions of the beneficiaries, and Protect girls and women, boys and men against Vaccine Preventable Diseases, deployment of vaccines for outbreak response and strengthening routine immunization in line with Immunization Agenda 2030, and vaccination campaigns; 5. Support health logistics by promoting proper stockpiling and storage practices, for emergency preparedness and response (EPR), and Monitor availability of safe essential medicines to support quality assurance of it in liaison with DGDA; 6. Support IEDCR Field Laboratory and other field laboratories, including UHCs, through coordination, human resources, necessary supplies, information management and technical expertise; 7. Develop and disseminate locally adapted strategies, plans ( including training calendar), guidelines, treatment protocols for quality delivery of essential services package; 8. Support safe blood transfusion through training, establishment of blood storage units and systems strengthening, and Explore innovations in digital health to address information gaps and utilization of services; 9. Monitor reporting in EWARS, DHIS-2, 4Ws and adherence to SADD; disseminate information management products, explore simplifying of, and conduct training on it; and support partners and government to conduct health related need assessments and research; 10. Ensure robust system for prevention, early detection, and response to outbreak prone diseases, through EWARS, community-based surveillance, train, and deploy strategies for surveillance like Go Data and contact tracing, communicating data on epidemiology and risk factors for different gender and age groups, providing health intelligence; and support clinical case management; 11. Institutionalize a robust Infection Prevention and Control systems including antimicrobial resistance stewardship in healthcare facilities through required initiatives; 12. Coordinate with WASH sector and undertake water quality surveillance to enable evidence-based decisions, in collaboration with DPHE, and Continue WHO WASHFIT and strengthen healthcare waste management including through procurements; 13. Lead and coordinate health sector and health response to emergencies and disasters in close collaboration with government and partners through technical support, capacity building for EPR through Mobile Medical Teams and provision of supplies, and also support the functionality of the CS HEOC in various means; 14. Support prevention and management of communicable diseases (e.g., HIV, Hepatitis, TB, Malaria, COVID-19, other emerging diseases and neglected tropical diseases among others) in line with national protocols and WHO standards; 15. Update risk communication strategy for public health interventions tailored to community specificities with innovative feedback mechanisms; and engage communities (harness CHWs) in health promotion interventions, including GBV in Health, environment conservation against climate change and disaster risk reduction
