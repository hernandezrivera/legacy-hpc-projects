1. - Projects activities

Conduct risk assessment (safety audit) to identify GBV risk and concern of IDP women, girls and children. Renovate/establish women and girls’ safe space for the provision of timely and confidential access to psychosocial support service for women and girls and survivors of GBV Distribute in-kind dignity kits for women and girls of reproductive age Provide case management referral service and psychosocial support services to GBV survivors Establish and strengthen the GBV referral pathway in the target woredas. Incidental support for GBV survivors (transport fee, for legal services and other needs that survivors might have) Conduct awareness-raising sessions in the women and girl safe space to aware women of available GBV service, 72hr reports of cases, IPV, and PSEA. Conduct GBV basic concept, case management and referral pathway training for BoWCYA, health, legal and other GBV service providers conduct GBV mainstreaming and IPV training to BoWCYA and health and other service providers. organize project launching and close-out workshop with government structures, community leaders, and other GBV service providers. Conduct monthly GBV coordination meetings at Woreda level Conduct on-the-job training with BoWCYA and health on ethical/confidential GBV case management service. Conduct Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training for GBV stakeholders and IMC staff. Conduct house-to-house visits to reach the most vulnerable segments of community members including women with disabilities. Develop IEC/BCC materials to aware the IDP community about GBV related topics such as PSEA, IPV and to encourage positive practices Conduct awareness sessions through community dialogue to aware the community on mitigation of GBV risks and available GBV services

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involved those concerned local and national actors (including government sectors office at all levels, community representatives, and crisis-affected communities) to gather information related to needs, gaps, and priorities of problems faced by the affected population during the project designing. During project implementation, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been also involving those actors since Grant Opening Meetings (GOM) and provided information (on the objectives, target groups, budget, and beneficiary selection criteria) to the crisis-affected community throughout the project implementation. In addition, the International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involving Zonal and Woreda Government sector offices (including Planning and Economic Development Commission (PEDCO), Women, Children and Youth Affair (WCYA), Health Bureau (HB), Water, Mineral and Energy Bureau (WMEB), and Disaster Risk Management Commission (ODRMC)) in joint project monitoring and supervision to monitor the status of the project implementation, achievement, and its relevance. Community representatives from crisis-affected communities also participated in the joint project monitoring and supervision and provided their feedback regarding the quality of the project as well as IMC Ethiopian staff behaviors. Furthermore, International Medical Corps has been organizing a final project evaluation to examine the effectiveness, targeting, relevance, and sustainability of the project through targeted, feasible, and useful performance evaluation. This final project evaluation has been carried out by regional government bodies, which signed a project agreement with International Medical Corps Ethiopia. These Signatories include the Regionals Plan and Economic Development Commission (PEC), Regional Women, Children and Youth Affair (RWYCA), Regional Health Bureau (RHB), Regional Disaster Risk Management Commission (RDRMC). Thus, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involving concerned local and national actors throughout the project cycle management.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

International Medical Corps Ethiopia conducted community consultation with various groups of crisis-affected communities (including women, men, girls, boys, and people with disability) crisis-affected communities regarding how the beneficiaries view complaints mechanisms; how complaints are currently dealt with; how they would like to submit complaints to the organization; what might potentially prevent them from complaining, and how they wish to receive feedback about complaints. As a result, International Medical Corps Ethiopia identified appropriate feedback channels, and set up safe, accessible, and preferred CBFRM that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages in new proposed intervention areas. The feedback and complaints channels to be accessible for the most vulnerable group of the affected population, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been set up multiple channels including a helpdesk, dedicated telephone, community volunteers, face-to-face meetings, community events, and complaints boxes, which increased the coverage of CBFRM in each service delivery site Feedback and complaints have been collected on a weekly basis from various established CBFRM channels, and all complaints are reviewed by the Standing and Review Committee (SRC), with details and course of action are determined, and resolution of all feedback and complaints completed within 30 days from receipt. The decision and course of action taken for each complaint have been communicated to the complainant to close the communication loop. In addition, IMC Ethiopia has been incorporated the feedback and complaints received from crisis-affected communities and taken corrective action throughout the implementation of the project. The CBFRM data are monitored and evaluated on monthly basis, with realistic and agreed action plans developed to ensure beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback are incorporated and correction has been made throughout the implementation period of the project.
