Project Summary

Improve the equal access to education and the quality of learning for vulnerable conflict‑affected children through providing the affected children with non-formal education programs, learning materials and school bags, life skills and citizenship education programs, in addition to conducting Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns, rehabilitating classrooms and gender-sensitive WASH facilities, and providing professional development training to teaching staff.

Needs

The education system in Syria is overburdened and overstretched to the extent that schools and learning environments are unsafe, overcrowded and under resourced, leaving about 6 million people in deep assistance for Education More specifically: The key impediments to children accessing education include safety, security, long roads to and from schools and alternative learning spaces; lack of teaching and learning materials; poor quality teaching; and damaged education infrastructure. Since the crisis began, thousands of classrooms have been destroyed or damaged, and school buildings are used as collective shelters for displaced families. hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools are now closed and no longer accessible or used as collective shelter. In schools that remain operational, poor water, sanitation and hygiene conditions negatively affect education, child health and girls’ dignity, which in turn, directly affects enrolment and retention rates. Due to the massive population movement an estimated 2.1 million school age children are out of school , and an estimated 1.3 million children are at risk of dropping out. Attendance is intermittent and sporadic and many attend classes at inappropriate grade levels.. Consequently, many children are unable to complete a full academic cycle. Those who cannot make the sometimes-perilous journey to public exam offices are deprived of an official certification. The formal education system has lost 140,000 education personnel, including teachers.. However, the negative effect of the crisis was different on age and gender groups; children, adolescents and youth were the most affected, as exposure to/ or witnessing violence negatively affected their psychological health, interrupted their educational process & prevented them from developing their mental health, which altogether will prevent them from building their capacities and consequently will reduces their chances in accessing the labour market and prevent them from being active citizens in the future. As conducting deep needs assessment, remain restricted for INGOs PUI field staff , through field visits and meeting with key informants in targeted locations identified, that Education was often interrupted/limited due to security, displacement and control shift. School damage, lack of education staff, and the pressing economic situation of children’s families is leading to increased risk of children drop out, additionally Many girls are deprived of education and are married early, or due to the increased financial burdens the Syrian families’, preference is only given to their male siblings to continue their education. As the context remains fluid and fragile, and political solutions are yet to come to fruition, humanitarian actors need to adapt their interventions to reduce the impact, which seven years of conflict have had on the population – focusing on linking humanitarian emergency response to the recovery in accessible areas Though emergency humanitarian response is still a requirement to cover the immediate, life saving needs of the population, the scope of humanitarian intervention needs to be adjusted. A focus on recovery is required, to mitigate the impact this protracted crisis has had on the population, and to reduce the risk of further deterioration in the coming years.

Protection Risk Analysis

Main protection risks can be summarized in the deterioration of the security situation in areas of intervention, the possibility of tension between IDPs and host communities if provision of educational programs and learning materials is perceived as unequal, and the possibility of children dropping out of school to support their families through economic activities and at times through early marriage. PUI aims to reduce the likelihood of these risks occurring through: Assessing the areas of intervention prior to the implementation, and ensuring all stakeholders are aware of PUI's planned activities; Ensuring equal access to educational activities for IDPs, refugees, and host communities; Ensuring safe, healthy learning environments for targeted populations. PUI will establish children clubs within the educational facilities to provide children with life skills and citizenship education programs. In addition to raising the awareness of children, these clubs target life skills that assist in reducing possible negative coping mechanism on children in later periods. The children and educators are the ones whom identify the key topics to be tackled by the clubs. Then, the children organize awareness days to spread awareness about these issues to a wider audience, including local community members and local authorities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following are seen as key assumptions: - Security situation remains stable, and humanitarian access consistent; accordingly PUI ensure through the intervention that all security procedures are in place and followed . - No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound; - No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas;- Financial transactions are still possible to Syria . Below are the main risks to the action and the measures PUI will take in response: - Local population does not accepts PUI´s intervention; to mitigate this PUI will ensure the full engagement of the community leaders and local stakeholders in project design and well introduce the benefits to the targeted community that will occur . - Lagging Return movements; it is not fully under PUI control, however PUI follows a multi sector integrated approach in programming to support the community resilience in a multi dimensions which might encourage more people to return to their original houses, if the lack of access to basic services is their main hinder to return . - Delay in receiving authorizations and clearances to quickly intervene. As mitigation to this PUI is fully coordinating with the relevant line ministries and updating MoLAE with them to facilitate the process of getting needed approvals
