Project Summary

Communal violence, including cyclical revenge killing, tribal conflict and cattle-related violence have continued across the country, fueled by negative coping mechanisms resulting from multi-generational trauma and militarized community response structures. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan report (2021), protection needs are anticipated to increase in 2021 despite the relatively encouraging political developments. The proposed action seeks to ensure provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical. In addition, the action also seeks to enable durable solutions for IDPs in informal settlements, spread in conflict-affected target areas due to insecurity and climate challenges in hard to reach and priority areas. The overarching objective of this initiative will be achieved by enhancing the overall protective environment, create conditions for reintegration and durable solutions to protection needs and basic services linked to other IRC multisector projects including Economic Recovery and Development (ERD), GBV/WPE, Health and Nutrition and other service providers reducing the risk thereof, and availing specialized support assistance to beneficiaries to help them address other inter-connected protection concerns. IRC will strive to strengthen and expand access to protection services in two-way information services and trauma-informed psychosocial activities as a means to reduce the effects of protection risks through a community based protection approach, strategic and result-based protection monitoring (static and mobile), client-centered two-way information dissemination, provision of life saving services including identification, case management, targeted individual assistance and referrals of persons at risk, distribution of core relief items (cash or NFI) to mitigate protection risks (including HLP related concerns) linkages to specialized services in responding to identified protection cases that contribute to beneficiaries’ overall resilience. Prevention will include awareness-raising in communities and advocacy with the government on protection issues, protection monitoring, capacity building of stakeholders, protection mainstreaming, and information services through information desks. Additionally, through peaceful co-existence initiatives at the community level, IRC will foster peace and trust between IDPs, refugee, spontaneous returnees, and host communities to create conditions for reintegration and durable solutions to protection needs. By so doing, IRC will place emphasis on early warning and early response.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 South Sudan HRP, about five million women, girls, men, and boys will face protection risks and violations in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers in South Sudan lack protection services. Protection concerns for vulnerable people including IDPs, host communities and returnees are continuously expected to increase in 2021 despite the relatively improved political developments. Ongoing inter and intra communal conflicts and violence, multiple displacements, revenge killings, cattle raiding, seasonal floods, dwindling economic opportunities, unequal access to resources and COVID-19 continue to drive humanitarian needs and protection concerns in South Sudan. Exposure to land mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) and denial of basic housing, land and property rights, amongst others, are protection concerns contributing to human rights violations. The re-designation of former POCs to IDP camps and the ensuing distrust between the IDPs in the formal camps and the new security set-up, is likely to be a push factor for people to return to their original places even though the conditions to achieve durable solutions remain elusive in those areas. Movement of people from both formal and informal IDPs settings to any destination in South Sudan, is just a matter of shifting protection concerns and risks from one point to another. Therefore, durable solutions will remain hard to achieve without proper intersectoral needs analysis and implementation of integrated humanitarian assistance, protection, and development programs within the various contexts. Realization of durable solutions for South Sudanese displaced people will be of importance in 2021 for the South Sudan Protection Cluster. The expected return of refugees and IDPs to their original places will drive HLP concerns and protection risks. Localized flooding, armed robberies and communal violence persisting, leading to fatalities, multiple forced displacement, sexual and gender-based violence, and other human rights abuses. According to a recent multi-sector needs assessment commissioned by IRC, for Mayendit and Leeh (January 2021), community members indicated that they had lost all their belongings during the floods and that in terms of needs, access to non-food items was their priority. Also 58% of the respondents indicated that they had faced multiple displacements since 2011 and that they felt insecure in their locations. Assessment participants stated that able-bodied community members had managed to relocate themselves to higher ground areas. The COVID-19 pandemic has further caused devastating socio-economic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. Tough economic situations in South Sudan impacts negatively on people’s livelihoods compounding to more protection concerns. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 have also contributed to a rise in human rights abuses and protection concerns. A multi-sector needs assessment conducted by the IRC in December 2020/21 covering Koch, Mayendit, Leah and Rubkona Counties indicated insecurity and violence have disproportionately affected vulnerable people including children, older persons, survivors of gender-based violence and conflict-related sexual violence, and people living with disabilities. Therefore, IRC will scale up its current general protection and social cohesion programming in Juba IDP camps, Mahad and Don Bosco IDP camps, Mayendit, Koch and Pariang (host community) through an integrated protection approach for IDPs, returnees and the host community.

2 - Project activities

1)Results-based and strategic protection monitoring. The IRC will utilize multiple modalities for protection monitoring to triangulate and validate information. That includes applying protection monitoring approach that achieves both results-based protection monitoring outcomes, which directly mitigate and/or respond to the immediate protection risks facing the populations of concern and strategic outcomes that strengthen the broader humanitarian response. 2) Service mapping and referrals. IRC will collaborate with protection partners and actors at all levels to conduct, expand or update service mapping and referral pathway to support better access to service providers in the areas of programming. In addition, IRC will have coordination meetings with these service providers to support referrals for services needed in their areas of programming. 3) Comprehensive protection case management, IRC will provide comprehensive protection case management services to individuals that face heightened protection risks and cannot benefit from specialized child or GBV case management services. IRC specialized protection workers will offer individualized interventions to identified clients experiencing high or medium risk of further harm while those considered to be at a low risk will be supported through safe referrals to other services. 4) Targeted individual assistance (TIA) which can be in form of cash or in-kind will be provided once to enable most vulnerable individuals to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity and reduce their vulnerability and exposure to risks and promote meaningful access and participation to services. It aims to build on rather than replace existing support mechanisms. The IRC will support in addressing immediate needs of beneficiaries that falls outside the scope of child protection and women protection and empowerment program by accepting referrals from other sectors to enable extremely vulnerable individuals in situation where no other alternative response, assistance or support exists to meet their most urgent protection needs with dignity. 5) Two-way communication and awareness approach, IRC will set-up two-way communication systems for the targeted population that will ensure client responsiveness through using both proactive and reactive feedback channels which will include use of both mobile approach protection information desks. IRC will conduct information needs assessments in targeted areas with a view to understand what information the community needs, what are the greatest sources in the language and format they can understand. Awareness sessions will include issues on HLP concerns for returnees. 6) Community-based protection approach, IRC recognizes the communities as active agents of their own protection as well as stakeholders in protection approaches. IRC will gather comprehensive information (including demographic data, stakeholder mapping, power and/or social network analysis, gender analysis, protection risk analysis and assessment of existing community groups) about each community to inform setting up or strengthening existing community based structures. Community groups are trained and supported to analyze protection risks in their community to develop a strategy including an action plan to address identified protection risks. 7) Peaceful coexistence activities, IRC will promote peaceful coexistence of social groups including returnees, IDPs and the host community through the CBP process and context-specific activities including social dialogue and trauma informed group activities. 8) Protection mainstreaming, IRC will promote integration of protection mainstreaming principles through training and development and implementation of sector specific actions. 9) IRC will also build capacity of protection and non protection actors . 10) HLP awareness raising, the action will promote community on HLP rights through awareness sessions.

3 - Project indicators

At the project design, indicators to measure the project success will be identified together with the targets. Data resulting from the project implementation will be routinely collected, analyzed and compared to the targets set during the project design. GP program will continue utilizing available monitoring tools including Protection Information Management System to record and store protection case management and protection monitoring data; two-way protection information data collection tools; Protection cluster HLP data collection tools (including online tools for recording eviction cases); Information Needs Assessment tools to understand what information the community actually needs, what are the greatest sources and; Two Way communication data collection to understand and track the types of questions we are being asked and what are our responses are; and HLP WG Eviction Data Collection using the kobo tools. Governance and Rights Coordinator will provide technical support to the field teams while Field Protection Team leads (including Protection Managers and Senior Roving Protection Mainstreaming Officer) will do regular follow-up to ensure the tools are consistently used and data is recorded in the right database on using these tools. Analysis of data will also be done on regular bases to inform the program on progress towards the milestones, adjust implementation and reporting to donors, beneficiaries and other stakeholders. In addition to that, indicator tracking table will be developed to ensure indicator’s progress is updated in a monthly basis. Similar to that, the monitoring and evaluation work plan will be developed to keep the project team well informed on the time-frame for conducting the activities contributing to indicator success. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRC will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. All project staff will be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. IRC will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focal point (s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms. IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the local language, and distributed to the communities

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Utilizing the protection desks, mobile protection teams, set client-centered feedback mechanisms, monthly focus group discussions and listening sessions will be held to collect feedback from the communities and vulnerable community members. The project takes into persons with disability (PWD) as among the most vulnerable in target communities. In ensuring that the project addresses their concerns, IRC will work with disability-focused organizations to identify and target them with specific protection needs for service provision including referrals.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRC has established multi-sector client feedback and response mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project locations through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the IRC. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any compliant report and feedback. IRC will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men and boys to hold IRC accountable. The project will integrate robust client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, older people and people with disabilities. These will include regular FGDs with men and women and listening sessions, including sessions specifically with women, youth and returnees, suggestion boxes at project sites, including within women’s safe spaces and protection information centers and though two-way communication system at protection desks and information centers. The IRC will also conduct regular consultations with community leaders to get feedback from the community about project activities including and whether the feedback mechanism itself is safe, easy and accessible. Formal feedback mechanism will include the use of both proactive and reactive channels including complaint boxes, walk ins, FGDs and phone calls.The IRC will ensure the feedback is confidential and will provide responses to the feedback that is received and use this feedback to modify its approach wherever appropriate. Moreover, monthly coordination and project review meetings will be held with stakeholders to discuss the project objectives, challenges, including feedback received from the communities as described above, and the way forward. In this way, the services and community activities remain responsive to the needs and concerns raised by the communities in the targeted areas.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will integrate robust client responsive feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all groups, including women, girls, older people and people with disabilities. These will include regular focused group discussions (FGDs) with men and women, and listening sessions, including sessions specifically with women, youth and returnees, suggestion boxes at project sites, including within women’s safe spaces and protection information centers and through two-way communication system at protection desks and information centers. The IRC will also conduct regular consultations with community leaders to get feedback from the community about project activities including whether the feedback mechanism itself is safe, easy and accessible. Formal feedback mechanism will include the use of both proactive and reactive channels including complaint boxes, walk ins, FGDs and phone calls. The IRC will ensure the feedback is confidential and will provide responses to the feedback that is received and use this feedback to modify its approach wherever appropriate. Moreover, monthly coordination and project review meetings will be held with stakeholders to discuss the project objectives, challenges including feedback received from the communities as described above and the way forward. In this way, the services and community activities remain responsive to the needs and concerns raised by the communities in the targeted areas.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Services will be provided to all communities in the target locations, to ensure conflict sensitivity, adopting a community-based approach. Sessions providing explanations on the approach and objectives of the project will be provided and community participation will be encouragedIRC will strengthen implementation through community based focal points and volunteers to continue service provision during these periods. IRC will also ramp up remote service provision where possible and embed adaptive management processes. The project will promote responsive and client-centered community-protection and peaceful coexistence mechanisms and consult community leadership that will be for safer and dignified projection interventions. All project activities will mutually reinforce humanitarian assistance, protective environment to assist build robust nexus peace in the target areas. IRC has put in place COVID-19 mitigation measures to prevent and/ or limit COVID-19 transmission, particularly in IRC work places and target communities. IRC will ensure staff are properly and regularly trained on COVID-19 IPC safety protocols to ensure they are provided with adequate PPE and community awareness raised on important COVID-related messages. IRC will work with other partners and UNHCR to improve case management, identification, contact tracing and isolation of all suspected cases. All activities will be carried out while following local regulations and project-specific COVID-19 contingency measures including hand hygiene, social distancing and face masks. Gatherings for activities will be limited in size to allow for distance and will include key COVID-19 prevention messages. IRC has strong mechanisms and policies in place to safeguard clients, staff and partners from any form of exploitation and abuse, including the following: Beneficiary protection from exploitation and abuse policy, child safeguarding policy, combating trafficking in persons policy, global reporting guidelines, anti-retaliation and reporter protection policy, harassment-free workplace policy, workplace violence policy. IRC distributes IEC materials (in local languages) for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report against SEA. The SEA prevention ambassadors will be present in all IRC health and WGSS facilities. IRC staff/volunteers are trained on PSEA and will be are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training on child safeguarding.
