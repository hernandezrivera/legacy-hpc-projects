Project Summary

The COVID-19 outbreak has since spread to about 196 countries and territories on every continent across the globe. While there are ongoing efforts to curtail the spread of infection, which is almost entirely driven by human-to-human transmission, it has accounted for over 71,237,768 confirmed cases with over 1,597,991 deaths (WHO, 2020).
In response to the growing humanitarian crisis and COVID19 Pandemic, this project will deliver on goals to support the 2030 development agenda, specifically Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3 and 6.
We are proposing to mitigate COVID-19 in the North East and sensitise vulnerable populations who are most at-risk of being infected and possibly die from contracting the disease. This project aim to sensitise, educate, and deliver essential COVID-19 kits, as well as sanitation kits, to 1,500 vulnerable households. In addition to this, the project will deliver life-saving personal protective equipment (PPE) and capacity building support to health workers so that they can continue to provide the essential health services that serve the needs of communities in which they operate, including providing essential maternal care, as well as sexual and reproductive health support. For non-health workers such as community leaders, religious leaders, women's leaders and youth leaders, the project intends to develop and disseminate sensitisation and communication materials to educate community members on personal hygiene and social distancing measures to diminish the likelihood of contracting the virus.
The project, which will be implemented in 3 LGA Borno State, and expected to last for a period of six (6) months, has a total proposed budget of US$ 300,000.00. The target of the project is to deliver personal protective equipment (PPE) for at least 200 health workers, including community health workers, at least five (5) hospitals and healthcare centres in Borno State. It is projected that this project will directly reach 7,000 beneficiaries and indirectly reach over 15,000 beneficiaries from the supported communities.

1 - Needs

With the current 2nd wave of Covid-19 cases in Nigeria and the world at large, Northeast Nigeria which is highly endemic to diseases such as diarrhoea, malaria, measles, Lassa fever, meningitis and Hepatitis E. This is compounded by chronic under-development and poverty, which is in no small part caused by years of conflict and displacement. The lack of adequate potable water and sanitation infrastructure, coupled with extreme flooding, has seen cholera and acute water diarrhoea (AWD) cases rise every rainy season, while the protracted conflict has kept internally displaced people (IDPs) in overcrowded camps and overstretched the little resources and infrastructure available in many communities hosting IDPs. Repeated displacement, trauma, poor living conditions, exposure to violence, and loss has left much of the population in the Northeast vulnerable. Women, children, persons with disabilities and the elderly are particularly vulnerable. Pre-existing gender inequalities, including the lack of access to land and basic services, as well as widespread sexual violence and engagement in negative coping mechanisms demonstrate the serious impacts that the crises have on the most vulnerable. It is expected that these vulnerabilities would be exacerbated by global Coronavirus (COVID-19) pandemic.

2 - Activities

- Purchase and distribution of 1,500 Covid-19 kits. - Purchase and distribution of 1,500 sanitation kits - Purchase and distribution of 1,000 PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) for health centres and hospitals. - Establishment of sanitation groups in the community to promote sanitation practices and encourage community participation in maintaining clean and sanitary public spaces. - 8 capacity building workshops for 100 female health workers on safe use and disposal of kits and social distancing measures. - Facilitate awareness sessions for women and girls that access supported health facilities on best hygiene practices and social distancing measures. - Develop and disseminate communications materials on best hygiene practices and social distancing measures for community members and beneficiaries of COVID-19 and Sanitation kits.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism will be established and operationalised through stakeholder engagement meetings, anchored by project staff and community leaders, to provide and receive necessary feedback about the impact of the project, including aspects of the project that are going well, challenges, delivery methodology, context-appropriate solutions to problems, and opportunities to drive community capacity and ownership of the activities. Through the engagement of an external project evaluation expert, the aim is to ensure that objective feedback is received from support communities and beneficiaries about the quality, target and impact of the intervention on addressing the goals of the project. The feedback received at intervals during the implementation of the project will, along with the stakeholders’ meetings, influence any changes to the implementation of the project to ensure that it meets the needs of target beneficiaries.
