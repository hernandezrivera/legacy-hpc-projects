Project Summary

1: EORE component of the action will target 12,000 direct beneficiaries (2000 Men, 2000 Women, 4000 Girls and 4000 Boys) including persons with disabilities. And the team will be using Door to Door methodology in reaching the targeted communities of the affected area considering the accessibility, level of the contaminations and security of the area of intervention. The team will train 200 Community focal point persons (100 Females and 100 Males), 200 PREs (Peer Risk Educators) to keep the messages within different age groups including the persons with disabilities (PWD).
2. The hazardous target during the 2021 project period will be 50 and that would be used throughout the project proposal. Beside the HA to be surveyed or Cleared, the action will be that DCA will handover at least 600,000 sqm of land released from ERWs for a minimum of 12 HA surveyed /Cleared of 50,000 sqm each. And actual number of HAs will depend on upon the UNMAS tasking and prioritization.
In summary,
The overall goal of the proposed project is to increase the civilian security by protecting the lives and property through the removal of explosive Remnants of War and sustainable Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) as the main objective for the project. The team will also be focusing on the returnees currently coming home from the Refugees camp (Uganda) within Magwi County especially in payams like Nimule, Pageri, Magwi, Mugali, Pajok through Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, and NTS/Clearnce to make the land safe for the returnees to settle and safely cultivate land for farming. The Multi-Tasking team will conduct Non-Technical Survey/Clearance while Explosive Risk Education Team will focus on the sensitization of the local community about the dangers of ERWs and what to do in case they come across the explosive devices, they EORE team will further train some community members as focal points (CFPs), and Children both at school and community level as Peer Risk Educators to educate their peer on the dangers of ERWs and landmines.
DCA proposed this activity to scale up its current Mine Action intervention in terms of Explosive Risk Education and Clearance/NTS in Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria State.

1 - Needs

The decades of civil War between SPLA/M and the SAF from North Sudan within the areas of South Sudanese land had left most of the fertile lands contaminated with Landmines and Explosive Remnants of War creating obstacle for human settlement and livelihood activities like farming and other economic activities. In addition, the decades of long civil war, the recent conflicts which started in 2013 between the Government of South Sudan and the SPLA-IO has added more threats to the local communities as most of the local communities within Magwi County fled their home areas due to fear for their lives and their areas occupied by the military personnel seen in Pageri payam along Juba-Nimule Road. With the withdrawal of the vast majority of these soldiers to other parts of the country, there are a numbers of the local communities voluntarily returning home, they would be the focus for this project where they need safe land and environment/protection as put in the project objects. This action can be achieved through direct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and Clearance/NTS.DCA planned to reach all the Payams under Magwi considering the severity of the level of contamination of the areas affected by the Explosive remnants of War and Landmines. All the age groups are targeted with equal gender considerations throughout the project period.

2 - Project activities

In line with the overall goal to “Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas – linked to intersectoral SO1 and SO2”, DCA is well -positioned to carry clearance and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE).The action shall include two main components, namely land handover to the affected communities through land release and clearance of suspected hazardous areas and EORE awareness sessions and trainings based on the inputs from the contamination severity Maps provided by UNMAS, beneficiary vulnerability feedback from the past needs assessments during the field surveys in highly suspected areas of contamination. During the project period 2021, DCA plan to reach to at least 30,000 beneficiaries with handover of 600,000 sqm of land released from ERWs threat. The crucial part would be community liaison and Explosive Risk education which will be tackled by the Explosive Ordnance Risk Education Team (EORE) as an entry point tool for the successful implementation of the project activities as per the proposal. The team will meet and liaise with different stakeholders before, during and after the implementation targeting all the age groups and the gender inclusive. Beside the Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, the EORE team will train 200 CFPs (100 Men and 100 Women) and 200 PREs (100 Boys and 100 Girls) both at school and community level to pass safely messages to other community members while identifying and reporting all the spotted hazardous areas to DCA, Mine Action Partner or UNMAS. That would justify that participation and involvement of the beneficiaries as per the overall objectives. The CFPs and PREs will continue to disseminate EORE messages after the project ends. DCA, given its long standing static presence in Magwi County, will continue to follow up and continually serves as a point of contact for communities, through trained CFPs, for any spot tasks, ensuring removal of identified explosives. DCA has ongoing relationships with many CFPs throughout Magwi county and this long-term commitment has proven very valuable. However, to measure the impact of the intervention of the action, DCA will conduct follow up visit to the trained CFPs and PREs and there will be Baseline and Edline survey conducted to assess and get feedback of the direct Risk Education sessions conducted from different age groups and gender.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The release of cleared land allows the targeted communities to use it for productive purposes. This means that local food production is increased. At the same time, released land provides space for safe and sustainable returns from neighbouring countries, predominantly Uganda. Returnees will require access to land and food to settle. As this will initially increase the burden on the host community, it is important to provide opportunities for food production, preventing an increase in the population facing IPC 3, 4 and 5.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

While the project does not directly contribute to equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services, release of safe land is expected to contribute to humanitarian access as well as to freedom of movement. This should improve access to services for vulnerable populations.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project contributes to improved food security through the release of safe land for productive use, and thereby indirectly has an expected positive effect on GAM rates. In addition, opening safe access to humanitarian services will further benefit the targeted communities.
