Project Summary

The crisis in Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency. According to the Global Peace Index (GPI, 2023), Syria remains the third least peaceful country in the world with 16.7 million people, including 5.5 million displaced and 2 million in last resort sites (HNO, 2024). The earthquake in February 2023 resulted in widespread damage, further impacting already vulnerable populations. It also worsened infrastructure challenges including limited access to basic services, damaged water treatment plants, pumping stations, elevated tanks, and electricity shortages affecting critical services.
The Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC)'s Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) intervention in Syria aims to deliver humanitarian supplies and services to the most vulnerable population by ensuring continuous water supply and restoration, sanitation, solid waste management and sewage systems support. The project contributes to HRP Objectives 1 and 3 and to sector objectives 1, 3 and 4.
Targeting: The project will target vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, host communities, and returnees, with a focus on prioritizing the most vulnerable households. Prioritization will be based on vulnerability indexes shared by OCHA and the WASH sector, with detailed needs assessments conducted.
NRC will undertake direct implementation, with geographical targeting based on needs severity, inter-agency coordination, and access considerations. The WASH Project components will include:
Emergency Response: Provision of temporary WASH services (including water trucking), household assets (Hygiene NFIs), support to Education in Emergencies i.e WASH services for temporary learning spaces) and Hygiene promotion activities. NRC has prepositioned hygiene kits to ensure a quick response in case of emergency. This stock is part of the internal emergency response plan NRC has, which aims to ensure the required surge capacity in case of an onset emergency.
Medium-term Support: Provision of WASH infrastructure and services in camps and communal shelters. Distribution of hygiene items in camps and settlements. Long-term Improvements: Rehabilitation of community WASH infrastructure (boreholes, solar energy systems, water and sewage networks, solid waste management). Rehabilitation of WASH facilities in institutions (schools, medical centers). Rehabilitation of household WASH facilities and provision of water storage units. Community Engagement: Consultation through FGDs, HHQs, KIIs, with a focus on accessibility for all and functional complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs).
Sustainability Approach: Investment in sustainable, long-term WASH service provision, reducing reliance on short-term emergency solutions.
The main focus of activities implemented by NRC will be around sustainable water and sanitation system rehabilitations, prioritizing coordination with local authorities, installing solar systems to address energy challenges and reduce running costs and Hygiene Kit Provision.

NRC implements its Project Cycle Management (PCM) approach throughout its projects to track and address any challenges on time, ensuring the ultimate outcome of the activities. NRC includes robust lessons learnt sessions in its programming to ensure that insights into effectiveness, efficiency, internal process and programmatic integration, impact, and sustainability are identified. NRC also has in place an elaborate Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Safeguarding policy with a clear complaints channel to ensure any instances of abuse are reported and handled conclusively. NRC conducts regular protection monitoring to ensure that all protections risks are identified at early stage and mitigation plans are actioned accordingly.
