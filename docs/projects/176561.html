Project Summary

This is a 12-month project seeking to provide access to quality and lifesaving education for internally displaced persons (IDPs), Returnees, and host community children, youth and adults affected by the Covid-19 pandemic, conflict and flooding in Melut and Maban counties of Upper Nile State. The project will be implemented by Community Empowerment Network (CEN) which has a static presence and experience in implementing UNICEF-funded EiE interventions in Melut county of Upper Nile state.
Inter Communal Conflicts in the target areas, COVID – 19 pandemic and perennial flooding continue to be driving factors that lead to massive displacements, loss of lives, destroyed livelihoods hence food insecurity of up to IPC 3 and above. The education facilities in many parts of Melut and Maban counties are dilapidated or destroyed. Flooding from heavy rains has destroyed public infrastructures like schools and health facilities, shelters, crops and contaminated water sources, leading to increased vulnerability, protection risk, and increased risk of water-borne and other diseases. Flooding has further rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas including schools.
This intervention seeks to improve the learning condition and address protection concerns of the affected IDPs, spontaneous Returnees from the neighboring countries such as Ethiopia and Sudan as a result of the signing of the revitalized peace agreement in 2018 that led to the formation of the new government of national unity that has given hope for peace to the communities in Upper Nile. This will be an integrated Education intervention that will create synergies with the health, GBV and WASH partners locally and at the national level.
This intervention intends to cover 6 primary schools with ECD streams in Melut County and 6 primary schools with ECD streams in Maban County of Upper Nile state. A total of 8 TLS will be established, 4 in each county, with 4 primary schools in each county benefitting with 1 TLS each. A total of 4 dilapidated TLS will be rehabilitated, 2 in each county, to provide a quality emergency learning environment to the affected IDPs, Returnees and host community children and out-of-school youth and consenting adults.
CEN will target 5066 (27,380 Boys 22,620 Girls 36 Teachers and 24 SMC members and 6 Education officials) direct beneficiaries comprising of newly displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities children and people with disabilities who have limited or no access to education services through the establishment and or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces (TLS) and classrooms, Establishment and or rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines with water points and hygiene promotion, Procure and supply Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to beneficiaries, Provision of quality education recreational materials to the conflict and crisis affected children and youth, Train teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children, Train and update teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, Train teachers and education personnel on referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health and nutrition, Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) on COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures and Organize Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns
CEN has professional and qualified staff in Education, WASH, GBV, Peacebuilding and Health who will enhance the efficacy of the project implementation. With a static presence in Melut County, CEN is best placed to offer an integrated approach to this Education intervention.

1 - Needs

1 - NeedsThe counties targeted fall under IPC 3 and above phase classification projections for the earlier part of 2020. Chronic WASH in schools needs and lack of children presence in schools continue to surface in parts of Upper Nile State mainly affecting teachers and children, majority of whom are multiply displaced. OCHA estimates close to 800,000 persons in the counties targeted are in need of humanitarian support due to adverse effects of conflict, displacement, food insecurity, economic hardships and lack ofinfrastructure. Torrential rains in the months of August – October 2020 have resulted in massive flooding thereby affecting access to basic needs and education. Remoteness and access issues into locations of Melut and Maban Counties of Upper Nile state have further worsened the already dire situation with the majority of the schools assessed lacking essential WASH infrastructure, children and teachers reliant on unsafe water due to inadequate safe water sources/points and this makes them more vulnerable to Covid 19 and also disqualifying the schools for the school feeding programme. Melut and Maban Counties experience perennial flooding that destroys farmlands, existing infrastructure including roads, schools and health facilities increasing the vulnerability of the population. Inter-ethnic conflicts with sporadic eruptions of violence has led to the destruction of homes and massive displacement of populations. Communities have lost their livelihoods further worsening the food security situation. The displaced communities are living in deplorable conditions exposed to a myriad of risks such as water-borne diseases, malaria and protection risks especially for women, girls and people with disabilities. Existing learning spaces are inadequate and mostly either destroyed by flooding or burnt down as a result of conflict or dilapidated due to lack of proper maintenance. The management and governance of schools is a big challenge to the population that is mostly illiterate. Access to sanitation facilities is constrained with most of the schools lacking basic Wash facilities for both children and the teachers. Further, the majority of the schools are served by volunteer teachers who have little or no training in Education in Emergency. Pyschosocial support is needed for conflict-affected children attending school. Awareness on Covid is also critical.South Sudan’s education system is characterized as a low investment, low capacity, but high demand system. The state building and peace-building efforts of the national plans put high demand on the education system to expand fast, reduce inequity, and provide appropriate teaching. But public expenditure is one of the world's lowest for education. UNESCO October report states that some 2.4 million children are out of school in 2020. The majority of them are girls. Children in rural areas and IDP, refugee, and returnee children are most vulnerable. About 60 per cent of IDPs and returnees live in settlements where no more than half of the children are attending primary education, mainly in Upper Nile State. The situation further worsened countrywide with the temporary closure of schools due to COVID-19, disrupting education and limiting children’s access to essential services like school feeding programs, information on disease prevention and access to water and sanitation. Support to the learning environment, teaching and learning, psychosocial support programming, infrastructure improvement, school WaSH activities, teacher training, and provision of incentives exerts more resources than the previous response to meet the needs and protect children from the COVID-19 pandemic in schools. As the Covid 19 pandemic persists and lockdown resumed, closure of schools further exacerbates the burden, many schools will struggle to reopen, and given that most volunteer teachers seldomly have access to regular pay, access to education for the vulnerable children is a mirage.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project provides beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through AAP committee in place and Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) in place. Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) is incorporated in all training manuals and also form part of staff induction including staff code of conduct. All project volunteers are selected from affected population and are provided with training and information of PSEA and referral pathway.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CEN has designed this project to ensure a constructive and robust feedback from the most vulnerable members through an inclusive AAP committee and community feedback and response mechanism in place with well-trained staff to handle the exercise.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CEN believes on the philosophy “nothing for us without us”, We therefore emphasize transparency in project implementation by directly involving the community and targeted beneficiaries in every stage of the project to ensure clear understanding of project, objectives and stakeholders. CEN has incorporated the Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP) into all relevant statements, policies and operational guidelines including incorporating them in staff inductions. CEN ensures facilitation of the provision of feedback from affected people on the services. Suggestion boxes are in fixed at all CEN field offices to maximize on inputs from communities. Information will be available to local communities in local languages; Teams are recruited with attention to a balance of women and men, cultural diversity and age. Staff, volunteers and consultants, both national and international, are provided with adequate and timely inductions, briefings, and clear reporting lines that promote positive organizational behaviours and enable staff to understand their responsibilities, work objectives, organizational values, accountability commitments, key policies and local context. CEN works with partners and other stakeholders to ensure the needs of the most vulnerable are addressed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project is designed to use the beneficiaries’ feedback to reach the most vulnerable persons in the community.
