Project Summary

The triple threat of floods, desert locust infestation and the recent COVID-19 pandemic has aggravated Somalia’s complex, protracted humanitarian crisis. Due to the combined effects of these three scourges and the long-standing previous shocks, an estimated 2.1 million people across Somalia are anticipated to face acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021 and need immediate assistance according to FSNAU Post Gu 2020 nutrition assessment . At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu). Urgent treatment and nutrition support are required for approximately 849 900 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition through August 2021, including 143 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished.
Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration of the nutrition situation. Medair project locations are among the areas of concern and need urgent nutrition and health interventions. The Shabelle riverine recorded a critical nutrition condition with a GAM rate of 16.0% . With the impacts of the locust desert infestation, the nutrition status of the IDP population in Banadir region shows a critical prevalence of malnutrition with a GAM rate of 17%. The MUAC screening of U5 children at Medair supported health facilities shows a significant high malnutrition rate with proxy GAM rate of 20%.
The proposed project interventions will target children between 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women with preventive, promotive and curative nutrition services both at the health facility and community levels reaching 21,645 direct beneficiaries thus contributing to the cluster target.
The goal of the project is to improve the nutritional status of infants (0-6 months), young children (6-24 months) and children under 5 years as well as to improve the nutritional status of women before, during and after delivery in order to ensure optimal nutrition during early development.
Medair’s proposes nutrition-specific interventions which will be integrated with health and WASH to ensure a multi-sectoral response in order to provide synergy and achieve sustainable impact on the above needs. They will be implemented at community and static facility levels.
The proposed action will entail the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition including severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), promotion of infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF-E) in emergency settings, micronutrient deﬁciency prevention and control; these activities will be implemented in the supported facilities and their catchment villages.
Medair will work closely with stabilization centers (SC) available within the operation areas for referral of children diagnosed with acute malnutrition with medical complications and after the client’s discharge from the SC, Medair supported facilities will receive and admit these children into the program to complete the therapy. Medair has established a referral system in the target communities which works very well with a vehicle in place and community health volunteers (CHVs) aware of the telephone number to call when the need arises.
During the current COVID-19 pandemic, Medair will embrace the simplified admission criteria (MUAC and Oedema only) for both SAM and MAM management as well as simplified ready-to- use therapeutic food (RUTF) dosage to reduce physical contact and thus the risk of spread of the COVID-19. Adherence to the infection prevention and control measures already established at the distribution points to prevent/ minimize the risk of the virus spread among beneficiaries will be sustained.

Referral for children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with medical complications for management at stabilization centers6. Training of nutrition staff on CMAM protocol 7. Provision of preventive nutrition services to children aged 6-23 months and PLW with supplementary foods8. Support the prevention and management of micronutrient deficiencies through micronutrient supplementation, intake of fortified foods, social and behavior change communication (BCC) to improve dietary intake in the communities 9. Promotion of IYCF practices; early initiation of breastfeeding (within 1 hour after delivery), exclusive breastfeeding until the infant is six months old, continued breastfeeding until the child is two years old, and complementary feeding using locally available nutrition dense foods.10. Refresher training on IYCF for nutrition staff and CHVs. 11. Sourcing of medication and therapeutic and supplementary food (via WFP supply pipeline with gap filling by Medair).12. Support Supervision of Care Group Volunteers and CHVs to carry out active case finding, referral, and follow-up of defaulters or poor responders amongst children admitted to the CMAM program. 13. Participate in coordination forums with other key actors in nutrition programming at all levels; national, regional, and on the ground (district and community) level.

Mitigation Measures

1. Medair will provide refresher training to all staff involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Medair has nominated a safeguarding officer to ensure continued sensitization, awareness and follow up on sexual exploitation and abuse of the beneficiaries. Care Group volunteers and health/nutrition workers will strive to identify passive cases in the community and report using the existing reporting mechanism and also continue dissemination of behavior change communication key messages on sexual violence and exploitation. Trauma care, counseling, and first aid will be provided to the survivors (victims) and staff trained on clear referral pathways in coordination with the gender-based violence actors in the target communities. Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups to sexual violence during conflicts and ensure they are aware of their rights to access treatment and support due to sexual violence. 2. Medair has an efficient and effective feedback mechanism in place for suggestions and complaints that is accessible, confidential, and well understood by beneficiaries. The system has been established with a view to obtaining feedback to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Beneficiaries are encouraged to report any incidents through the established reporting mechanisms: either through their community leaders, assigned household volunteers, or by calling the established toll-free hotline #3377. Timely responses are provided to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. A dedicated feedback and complaint focal point has been appointed and will synthesize all feed-back and engage the community in order to solve any problems and bridge any gaps. Ongoing awareness and sensitization sessions are held at the health facilities through the monitoring and evaluation officer/nutrition facility workers and in the community through the Care Group volunteers and community health workers where beneficiaries are informed on the mechanism in place and how it works. All Medair and partners’ staff/nutrition staff, and community volunteers will be trained on Medair’s Code of Conduct and required to confirm their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. Exit interviews will be conducted amongst the targeted beneficiaries to understand their needs and concerns in relation to the quality of services they received and to address these whenever possible3. Medair will ensure that members of minorities, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), have systematic participation and representation in consultation meetings with local leaders during assessments to understand their needs. Consultations will involve both host communities and the IDPs. Nutrition facilities will be open six days a week for 8 hours and will ensure access for both the IDPs and the host communities. Contacts will be established with the local leaders from marginalized communities. A hot-line number will be provided where the community can provide feedback and share concerns. Community leaders will be informed of the importance of all groups being represented in the village health committees. Consultation with the minority groups (women, boys, girls, and people with disabilities) will be carried out to identify their specific needs. Community leaders are encouraged to advocate for the minority groups in their locations and awareness-raising and sensitization will be carried out amongst health staff to prevent discrimination.
