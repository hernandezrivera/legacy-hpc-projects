Project Summary

The malnutrition situation continues to deteriorate in NWS, the main drivers include poverty, low socioeconomic status, unsafe living conditions and diseases, suboptimal maternal, infant, and young child feeding (MIYCF) practices, and high rates of malnutrition in Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and children. The economic downturn and depreciation of the Syrian Pound and the February 2023 earthquakes in NWS had an intensifying effect on the staggering results. In response to the identified need, NGO X will implement the following package of activities which will benefit 125,892 individuals.

Deliver community-based one-on-one counselling following Cluster-recommended practices for Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) and maternal nutrition. Utilize a Social Behavioural Change (SBC) approach through diverse methods, including information materials, group sessions, household visits, and training community influencers to establish peer-to-peer support groups.
Distribute small-quantity Lipid-Based Nutrient Supplements (LNS-SQ) to children aged 6-59 months and PLW. Conduct six rounds of blanket LNS-SQ distribution for the specified age groups, coupled with MIYCN and Family MUAC Approach (FMA) activities to address micronutrient deficiencies and malnutrition.
Identify and refer Acute Malnutrition cases in PLW and children aged 6-59 months, incorporating the FMA at all service delivery levels. Provide caregivers with MUAC tapes and information on the nearest functioning nutrition services for self-referral when necessary. Screening and referral by nutrition staff will be conducted during household visits.
Administer Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) to households with children under 2 years to facilitate access to nutritious foods.
Refer individuals to health services, encompassing immunization, perinatal care services, and addressing health risks associated with malnutrition.
Ensure complementarity with Food Security CVA, focusing on households within the 1,000-day window of opportunity, recognized as the most vulnerable with the highest malnutrition rates. Provide counselling on MIYCF practices to support healthier decisions for nutrition outcomes.

The program will focus on caregivers of children 6-59 months of reproductive age, and PLW. Adolescent and youth (10-24 years) are not targeted directly but included if they fall within the previously mentioned categories.
The activities in the nutrition cluster log frame does not capture NGO X’s activities therefore the targets and budget are higher than that reported within the log frame.
All projects will follow SPHERE standards, cluster SOPs, and internal SOPs and regulations. A set of indicators are in place to track progress according to standards and to report on 4W indicators to the cluster on monthly basis. Monitoring is conducted by the M&E team and by TPM, at point of activity/distribution and 3-4 weeks after to assess the quality, usefulness and retention of assistance and information shared. TPM will share reports upon completion of the project. Evaluations includes a base and end line assessment.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures
Assumption: Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations remains permissive, security conditions remain sufficiently stable.
Risk: Military offensives (death/injury, NGO damage, access reduction). Mitigation: Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures are in place.
Risk: Spread of cholera in NWS. Mitigation: Referring any suspect cases to focal cholera response teams & sharing frequent messaging on cholera to ensure prevention, detection, and timely referrals.
Risk: Limited USD availability (small denominations, poor quality). Mitigation: Distribute mostly $50/$100 notes, multiple rounds or rounded up amounts possible, substitute unavailable small USD with TRY (per CWG guidance), and notify FSP 4+ days before distribution.

Protection Risk Analysis Possible risk in lack of privacy and physical risk such as harassment/safety issues for women traveling to site of activity. Mitigation: Gender balanced team as NGO X will hire a higher proportion of female staff given the target population is PLW and children under 5; Increase home visits to beneficiaries; Locations for nutrition activities are coordinated with CM/LC to ensure safety, privacy, and proximity. Regular monitoring will include questions about safety and privacy. Possible risk of tensions between project beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries and NGO staff due to variations in voucher value due to eligibility criteria. Mitigation: E-voucher modality avoids in person distributions; Inclusive eligibility criteria cover whole AoOs; Information sharing on selection criteria and program objectives. Monitoring of tensions between HH. The likely risk of limited access to NGO X’s reporting mechanisms for women, minimizing their feedback and opinions on issues faced with assistances. Mitigations: Diversify CFM and ensure accessibility, especially for those who can’t read/access mobile phones; create community groups, including women on preferable reporting and communication channels; Conduct regular awareness sessions to increase CFM awareness. PSEAH (Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment) strategy Risk of SEAH and violence by staff or local stakeholders affecting female and child nutrition beneficiaries. Mitigation: Ensure all involved actors are informed about and sign NGO X’s Safeguarding Policies and Code of Conduct; Consult beneficiaries (including women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities) to design activities and support affected populations to understand their rights; Provide PSEAH trainings for staff; Provide GBV trainings for staff and equip them with the mechanism to identify and refer cases to GBV support with confidentiality; Inform beneficiaries of CFM channels and share information with staff and targeted communities about NGO X’s procedures to report, investigate and take disciplinary action in cases of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment; Communicate targeting criteria and provide clear information to affected populations explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for assistance; Ensure a high ratio of female staff in the nutrition team and a comprehensive recruitment process with reference checking; Ensure parents/caregivers are present during nutrition activities that involve children and when staff from NGO X are interacting with children.
