Project Summary

The overall aim of the proposed project is to protect drought and conflict affected 9,989 (4,895 M, 5,094 F) IDPs and host communities HHs in Galmudug State, South West State and Banaadir State through the provision of ESK/NFI Kits, and Return Cash Package for those voluntarily returning to their original homes using Flexible Vouchers and Cash.
Through the above stated interventions, the project hopes to achieve three main objectives, namely:-
1) Contribute to the protection of newly displaced persons, IDPs and the host communities.
2) Improve the living conditions and contribute to local reintegration of the protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs) returning back to their places of origin.
3) Improve the quality of shelter assistance and ensuring accountability.
Throughout its project cycle, including implementation, CPD team plans to mainstream protection and ensure that gender, age and minority groups/clans consideration are factored into the programming. Measures to ensure that key pillars of Accountability to Affected Populations are met will also be in place.

Needs

Despite the recent improvements in rainfalls that led to the reduction of the threat of food insecurity in Somalia, the country is still in a precarious situation and the gains made so far could be lost if the humanitarian assistance is not sustained. UNCOHA estimates that more than 5.4 million people (IPC 2, 3 and 4) – around 43 per cent of the population – still need humanitarian assistance in Somalia. Out of the 5.4 people in need of humanitarian assistance, 2.6 million are internally displaced persons who face the highest vulnerability. According to the JMCNA 2018, about 2.3 million of the total displaced persons in the country urgently need shelter humanitarian assistance. Evidence from the JMCNA 2018 data that was collected in 58 districts in Somalia show that 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees; 35% reported damage to their shelters in the last three months; 70% reported that the shelters they live in have no internal separation; 16% reported thefts from their shelters. CPD's Shelter Needs Assessment in Banadir and Galmudug States in June 2018, reveal that over 96% of the IDPs live in dilapidated makeshift tents with inadequate access to basic services. Their poor shelter conditions expose them, especially women and girls, to extreme protection risks- particularly those from the minority and marginalized clans. No formal camp leadership exist in the many of the assessed camps, and IDPs rely on the gate keeper or the “camp leader” for information and support. There exist no fire breaks and proper mechanisms for disposing wastes. There is need to re organize the IDPs in to some structure that shall mange its own affairs of planning settlements, defining fire breaks, regulating the environment, clearing and defining access roads, managing solid waste is therefore long overdue in the IDP camps.

Activities

1) To meet the objective of contributing to the protection of newly displaced persons, IDPs and the host communities, CPD proposes the following activities to meet the needs of the target population:- Selection and registration of 9,989 IDPs and affected host community members (4,895 M and 5,094 F) to receive ESK/NFI Kits- Selection of suppliers to supply the ESK/NFI kits- Distribution of flexible ESK/NFI vouchers- Facilitation for the of 9,989 IDPs and affected host community members (4,895 M and 5,094 F) to redeem their flexible ESK/NFI vouchers at the selected suppliers 2) To meet the objective of improving the living conditions and contribute to local reintegration of the protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs) returning back to their place of origin, CPD proposes the following activities to meet the needs of the target population:- Selection and registration of 2,300 IDPs HHs who are voluntarily returning to their original homes (1,127 M, 1,173 F) - Selection of Cash Transfer Agencies - Distribution of Cash to the 2,300 IDPs HHs (1,127 M, 1,173 F) as a Return Package to meet their basic needs. 3) To meet the objective of improving the quality of shelter assistance and ensuring accountability, CPD proposes the following activities to meet the needs of the target beneficiaries: -- Conducting market assessments before the Cash and Voucher distribution.- Cash and Voucher distribution- Setting up of Complaints and Feedback mechanisms.- Carryout a Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) following the distribution of the Vouchers and Cash- Conduct Settlement (re)planning trainings for the Camp Coordination and Camp Management Committees (CCCMCs)- Distribute settlement planning tools to the CCCMCs- Conduct settlement planning activities- Conduct Infrastructure mapping exercises

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Among the main risks facing the targeted population is their exclusion in terms of targeting when it comes to humanitarian assistance. The displaced families are often excluded in the targeting and distribution of humanitarian assistance despite them being the most bearers of the brunt of the humanitarian crisis. The other risk they face is exclusion from the decision making process on issues like their eviction and site planning. The project plans to incorporate the camp leadership into the local authorities' decision making organs so that the camp members are consulted whenever decisions that affect their lives are made.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The exclusion risks on gender and age include lack of consultation with the different gender and age groups whenever designing/planing/implementation certain project activities. For instance, modes of voucher distribution, who to be registered as the household head, location of voucher distribution, what items should make up the ESK/NFI kits, the appropriate time to do the distribution and composition of camp leadership. All these factors will be considered by the project from a protection lens in order to avoid any chance of excluding beneficiaries on the basis of their gender and age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

There is always the risk of the target beneficiaries to be caught up in armed conflicts and violence as the general security of the country is precarious. Children from these vulnerable groups like IDP families are also at risk of being victims of child soldiering and ideological indoctrination. The mitigation measures that the project will employ is first the choice of voucher distribution locations. The project will ensure that distribution will be done in the most safest locations and easily accessible to all. Even the distribution time will be one that is in line with the safety of the beneficiaries. The project team in close collaboration with the camp/beneficiary leadership will continuously gather information and monitor any threats of children soldiering and indoctrination in the project areas so that it can be shared with the relevant government authority.
