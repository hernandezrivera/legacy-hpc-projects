Project Summary

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy and the HNRP 2024 ACTED will continue providing integrated CCCM services to displacement affected population in Juba and Bor former PoC/ IDP sites and with mobile and roving support in hard-to-reach areas in response to emergency shocks. Due to natural hazards, localized and state conflicts, displacement waves remain high in South Sudan, with 2 million people currently being Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in need of CCCM assistance plus another 2.3 million returnees according to the South Sudan DTM of April 2023. The trend has been increasing due to above-normal rainfalls for the fourth consecutive year in 2022 generating floodings, particularly affecting Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states, leading to the multiplication of displacement sites, in addition to which the displacements caused by the Sudan crisis observed the arrival of 341,967 persons recorded as between April and October 2023, among whom 86% are South Sudanese returnees . As of September 2023, over 908,800 people remain in 142 formal and informal displacement sites, predominantly in Unity, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria. Self-settled IDP sites, often overlooked and underserved, pose an even greater risk of exclusion from essential services. In 2023, 58% of those sites (hosting 327,215 people) did not receive CCCM support. Additionally, over 400,000 projected returnees from Sudan in 2024 (HNRP 2024) will need CCCM assistance in the transit and reception centers near the Sudan border. Following a strategy for sustainability implemented since 2021 in agreement with the CCCM Cluster, ACTED handed over camp management responsibilities to the community representatives and local authorities, especially Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in Bor and Juba former PoC/ IDP sites in 2022. In 2023, ACTED contributed to strengthening social cohesion between communities in Juba to foster peaceful coexistence and will provide ad-hoc support to community representatives if needed to ensure a smooth transition. In 2024, complementing the post-handover monitoring of the Juba and Bor former PoC sites, ACTED will continue supporting self-reliance and the implementation of durable solutions for IDPs and host community populations. Likewise, since mid-April 2023, the ongoing conflict in Sudan generated a massive displacement of populations from Sudan into neighbouring countries including South Sudan. As of October 2023, it has been reported that 97% of new arrivals fleeing the Sudan conflict are entering through the Renk/Joda. Thus, ACTED will continue the CCCM response in Renk Transit Centre to provide emergency assistance to the displaced populations. In parallel, ACTED provides emergency mobile and roving CCCM assistance in response to displacement shocks across the country which will further adapt according to the context to area based strategies. Through a well-established coordination system with REACH, the Inter Cluster Coordination Group, the Needs Analysis Working Group and the clusters, ACTED has access to timely information on displacements, allowing for a rapid multi-sectoral intervention whose core is CCCM services. ACTED will continue implementing CCCM activities in line with the following cluster objectives: CO1: Facilitate safe and dignified access to protection and multi-sectoral assistance at site or area level. CO2: Ensure and facilitate access to two-way communication pathways for displaced population living in sites. CO3: Strengthen community self-organization and community cohesion. CO4: Support integrated approaches to develop exit strategies, resilience, and durable solutions for displaced population in sites.

1. Needs

According to the South Sudan CCCM Cluster HNRP 2024 over 908,800 people remain in 142 formal and informal displacement sites, predominantly in Unity, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria states. In 2023, 58 percent of displacement sites (hosting 327,215 people) did not receive Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) support, resulting in limited access to coordinated services, community support, information, or site maintenance. Additionally, over 400,000 projected returnees from Sudan in 2024 will need CCCM assistance in the transit and reception centers near the Sudan border. Based on monthly site verifications, the CCCM Cluster anticipates that around 20% of these returnees will settle in existing IDP sites throughout the country. After a sudden shock, IDPs often gather in informal displacement sites in extremely precarious conditions, most of the time without access to basic services and information, and with limited decision-making power on issues that affect their daily lives and their future. These gathering places are often characterized by an increased exposure to security risks and protection concerns, particularly for the most vulnerable groups. Poor living conditions, lack of financial resources and limited access to basic services can lead to outbreaks of disease, increased prevalence of GBV, domestic violence, and tensions between population groups within the informal camps. Likewise, overcrowding could lead to challenges in maintaining safe levels of hygiene and sanitation. Continued political uncertainty, coupled with the increased risks of climate change has also largely resulted in the inability of the international community to return these IDP’s to more suitable locations and an unwillingness from the camps inhabitants to leave. In Bor and Juba former PoC camps, ACTED further reinforced the local authorities’ resources and abilities and completed a full handover of the Camp management responsibilities to the Community Members Committees and Camp administration role to RRC and community representatives in Juba IDPs Camp 1 and 3 and Bor camp in 2022 and 2023. In 2024, ACTED will monitor and provide ad hoc capacity building support to the CMCs to ensure that the quality of service is not decreasing. Additionally, ACTED will follow up on the relocation process of the newly allocated plots in partnership with the CCCM Cluster. In 2023, ACTED mobile response unit also provided emergency CCCM assistance in hard-to-reach areas across Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states in order to support the coordination of services provision in the area of intervention as well as reinforcing the displaced population’s capacity to manage the sites. In parallel, since May 2023, ACTED is the site management agency of Renk transit centre and Joda reception centre, in response to the displacements generated by the Sudan crisis. With 327,965 individuals arrived from Sudan since April 2023, on August 9th 2023, the Renk County authorities granted access to 13,000,000 square meters of land to extend the current Transit Centre. A headcount conducted by ACTED team on September 8th estimates that the population at the Transit Centre consists of 2,142 households, encompassing approximately 12,852 to 13,923 individuals. In 2024, ACTED will provide further CCCM support to the affected population in Renk Transit Centre and Joda Reception Centre targeting the total caseload of population in the sites.

2. Project activities

In line with the CCCM cluster strategy for 2024, ACTED will continue to implement its CCCM interventions through static, roving and mobile responses and area-based approach as appropriate. In 2024, ACTED will aim to strengthen peaceful cohesion between Juba and Bor camps and in the surrounding areas. ACTED plans to provide ad-hoc support to the new camp managers (RRC) to facilitate smooth transition. Furthermore, ACTED will pursue CCCM activities in Renk to address emergency needs of the returnees. In parallel, ACTED’s CCCM mobile teams will address the needs of displacement affected populations in camp, camp like settings and informal settlements with a focus on quality and information-driven interventions that help people adapt to shocks and prepare to transition towards self-reliance. ACTED will utilize mobile responses in response to critical needs for the provision of life-saving support in emergency scenarios, specifically in hard-to-reach areas, where service provision and partners presence is limited. In particular, the response includes but is not limited to: Provision of site management services including site/area level coordination, service monitoring, community engagement; Coordination of the operational entities, provision and access to services and protection in accordance with international standards aimed at guaranteeing the dignity of people and equitable access to basic services and fundamental rights; Implementation of a CCCM referral mechanism per site/area; Conducting joint safety audits at site level and new displacement sites; Developing site level multisectoral DRR informed contingency plans, site infrastructure care and maintenance; Support to Renk Transit Center including site planning, ground works (e.g. clearance, levelling, etc.) and construction of basic site infrastructure and communal facilities; Establish and support representative and inclusive community committees and leadership structures; Provide capacity building to community committee members, site leadership and relevant authorities, to enable their participation in, and eventual take-over of the management of the site; Promote meaningful participation of local and national organizations, civil society, women-led and IDP/returnee-led organizations in decision-making processes. ACTED will conduct cash-based interventions through Cash for Work (CfW) modality when possible. CfW can be used for a variety of activities including site maintenance, community centre construction, dyke construction in line with the identified needs per location. Moreover, considering the nature of the CCCM, ACTED is seeking to bridge its current emergency response towards the restoration of self-reliance and management of displaced affected communities with a greater responsibility to the Government bodies. While continuing to use its current mechanism to respond to the immediate needs as currently implemented, ACTED would like to expand the scope of its emergency mechanism toward longer-term, early recovery response to support the communities through their path to enhanced self-reliance. In this framework, ACTED would like to deploy more robust package after the first response with a deeper involvement of governance structures through for example the formation of community based structures. In coordination with the Cluster, mobile and roving interventions will be activated as a short-term emergency response approach to new displacements in hard-to-reach areas.
