Project Summary

This project aims to provide nutrition services to 60,720 individuals by 10 RRTs, to cover the unserved areas of Jabal Samaan, Harim, and Afrin districts, 18480 PLWs, and 42.240 Children under 5, each team will consist of 4 CHWs, and each district will have one team leader and 1 data entry, the total staff is 46, 40 of them are female, the RRT will conduct the following activities:
1.1. Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergency (IYCF-E) program
• Screen PLWs and primary caregivers of children below 2 years for lactation and feeding difficulties.
• Provide emergency skilled IYCF counseling to caretakers of children aged 0-24 months including PLWs
• Nutrition education to malnourished PLWs and caretakers of severely and moderately malnourished children.
• Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices.
• Monitor and report distribution and use of breast milk substitutes.
• Support health/nutrition/WASH education and promotion.
• Provide micro-nutrient supplements to CUS and PLWS to accelerate anaemia reduction.
2.1. Community based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) program:
• Screening children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are severely and moderately malnourished.
• Treatment of children with SAM without medical complications using sector agreed standards.
• Referral the MAM cases to WFP health centers to receive the required treatment and supplies.
• Treatment of children having SAM with medical complications in Stabilization Centers (SCs).
• Follow up the malnutrition cases to ensure they are recovering as per acceptable standards.
• Train project staff on CMAM and IYCF according to UNICEF and sector standards.
2.2. Integration with Health
• Screen, referral CU5 with dangers signs and medical problems in PHCs.
• Screen, referral PLWs with medical problems in ANCs/PNCs.
• Referral of medical cases to nearby health facilities.
2.3. Integration with WASH.
• Improve the social awareness among the target population in the served locations through a comprehensive COVID-19 awareness plan where the staff will attend training about COVID-19 and means of protection. The field team will use a developed SOP to avoid any harm for people during the field visits and fully commit to the health measures.
• Reinforcement of the hygiene promotion at the community level by educating the target population regarding face masks, hand washing, and social distance.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, there are 5.5 million mothers and children are considered people in need (PIN) for nutrition services in Syria. According to the Child Nutrition (WOS, 2020) the percentage wasting in children 6-59 months was 12% and the prevalence of stunting in children 6-59 months increased from 23% in 2012 to 30% in 2020.In general the nutrition status deteriorated in all Syria and NWS is not an exception, in HNO 2022, WOS mentioned that there were increased rates across malnutrition parameters since 2020; stunting (increased by 4%), SAM cases (increased by 29%), MAM cases (increased by 44.7%) and PLW with MAM (increased by 144%). According to HNO 2022, in NWS in recent years and especially since the pandemic a rising trend was seen in acute malnutrition, chronic malnutrition, micronutrient deficiencies and IYCF practices. There was a significant rise in MAM cases and inadequate treatment services were available; this is another sector problem since untreated MAM cases can easily deteriorate to SAM and so add to the SAM caseload, especially with the WFP food basket reduction so individual energy requirements in IDPs are not being met on a daily basis.In HNO 2022 data, the targeted three locations, Jabal samaan, Harim, and Afrin districts have a GAM rate of 4.7%, while the stunting rate is 25%, in addition to an increased rate of anemia 46% for children under five and 54% for PLWs. additional barrier is disability which increased across Syria to 21% in children' U5

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented by supporting 10 RRTs, in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas "Jabel Samaan, Afrin and Harim districts"The teams will provide basic services such as screening of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs, and referring cases a nearby OTP or SC. Moreover, the RRTs will disseminate IYCF-E messages among mothers of children U5 through individual counseling and group sessions, facilitating IYCF-E campaigns and integrating them into the Child Health Days, registering all screened cases, and reporting to the nutritionists and data entry team members. In addition, the RRTs will distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women, monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes, and distribute nutrition supplements. In addition the RRT team will identify and refer the cases exposed to abuse or violence via the visual signs on the children under five and PLW.All the RRT will be trained to take COVID19 precautions during the work and the project ensure PPE support Field team will weekly report the data to technical programs team, who will also submit a monthly 4ws report to the cluster, while the meal team will conduct bi-weekly site visits, checking the services provided, conduct satisfaction surveys, and conduct FGDs. MEAL will also receive complaints through WhatsApp number, suggestion box, email and through our meal focal person visits.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Base on IHR PSEA policy and before starting project interventions, Ihsan RD will conduct an SEA risk assessment to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while providing the support to BNFs, 2-BNFs do not report SEA cases for IHR staff, which results in the IHR being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them. Mitigation: After an in-depth assessment for these risks, IHR through collaboration with PSEA focal point will be designed the mitigation plan for all these risks as follows Mitigation measures: 1- IHR will ensure that all its staff had signed a code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs.2- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware of: what SEA is, what their rights are in regard to SEA, what they can do if SEA occur 3- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g. complaints box/telephone hotline to deal with these cases).
