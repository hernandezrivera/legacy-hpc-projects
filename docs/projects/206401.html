Project Summary

The project is aimed at clearing explosive debris of war to pave way for development activities such as Rehabilitation and Construction of School Hospital and Road to be undertaken In Renk County. The OSIL team is Liaising with stake holders including the local communities to identify potentially risk areas through pre-survey activities, mapping and marking threats and deploying of One(1) Technical Teams along the Area carrying out NTS,BAC ,EOD Spot task,and Manual verification, removal and destruction of Explosive remnants of war(ERWs) in accordance with standardised guide lines.
OSIL will work closely with the UNMAS, NMAA, the SSPDF engineering division and local communities to generate the required synergy for smooth un interrupted operation for 12 months starting early January.

1. Needs

Following over 50 years of civil war and a six-year Comprehensive Peace Agreement (CPA) implementation period, the Republic of South Sudan became the world’s newest country on 9th July 2011. Throughout the decades of brutal conflict, landmines and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) were used by all parties to defend strategic positions and to disrupt the movement and operations of enemies. Their pervasive use has meant that now, years after the peace agreement was signed, multiple types of contamination exist across the country, including Small Arms and Light Weapons (SALW), were used by both parity and they continue to impact communities. Each year in South Sudan, people are maimed and killed in accidents, communities are prevented from receiving humanitarian aid assistance and development and reconstruction is stalled due to the threat of death and injury. On April 15, 2023, fighting broke out as a result of political power struggle and the resultant fighting between different elements of Sudan armed forces, which forced nearly one million people to flee their homes within the last 8 months in fear of being kill, arbitrary arrested, torture, rape and many other forms of violence implicated on them. Efforts to restore peace failed exposing these returnees and refugees into high risk because their new settlements are highly contaminated with explosive remnant of war (ERWs) including land mines. The PMS report indicated that accidents from contamination are on the rise: according to the most recent Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) figure out that; In every 7 people killed (4 men and 3 children) and 21 injured (11 men, 1 woman and 9 children) as a result of contamination across South Sudan. OSIL intend to deploy one (1) team into the IDPS camps, schools and entry points from Sudan to carry out Clearance and EORE activities in Renk county and this will provide access to safe, productive land denies the vulnerable population opportunities for livelihoods, agriculture, infrastructure and the assistance of development organizations. Furthermore, access to dangerous items by protagonists, particularly small arms during the conflict also further threatens the safety of vulnerable people, risks increasing crime and intra-tribal tensions, and poses a serious threat to wider peace and stability across the region. As contamination poses an immediate risk of death or injury, and with populations within target areas absorbing a large- influx of returnees and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), there is a significant further strain on limited resources which risks fueling further localized conflict. The humanitarian crisis has been further exacerbated by market and health facility destruction, widespread food shortages across communities, and violent attacks against UN bases which are sheltering thousands of IDPs. The current situation is equally concerning for humanitarian aid workers as it restricts access and puts personnel and beneficiaries at risk. UN reports that “More than 7 million people - about two thirds of the population - are in dire need of some form of humanitarian or development assistance and protection in 2019 While the situation is no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the country remains in the grip of a serious humanitarian crisis. Hunger, malnutrition and the safety of civilians continue to escalate”. but this can only take place if those organizations have safe access to areas required for their interventions, including establishment or expansion of IDP camps to cope with the thousands of displaced people, and access to safe routes for delivery of food aid and medical equipment but the fact reminds that the area is conduct minted with ERW/Mine which cannot permit the access to IDP Comp for Humanitarian workers. With the imminent onset of the rainy season, crops should be getting planted but lack of access to land means the current pervasive food insecurity is set to deteriorate.
