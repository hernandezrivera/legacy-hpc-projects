Project Summary

This project comes in line with HRP 2021 strategic objective 2 focusing on supporting Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. According to HNO 2021, there is an increasing need for the provision of the necessary NFIs and disinfection materials, taking in account that maintaining an adequate stock level of NFIs is considered an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. Hence, maintaining an adequate stock level of different NFIs in SIF stores is an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in the Gaza Strip and based on HNO 2021. The instability in Gaza may lead to a new devastating escalation and the displacement of large numbers of the population, estimated at 600,000 , and this coincides with the increase in cases affected by the pandemic of COVID-19.
Gaza Strip experiences chronic humanitarian crises. Sometimes, there are some acute issues on top of these chronic circumstances. COVID-19 is an alarming indicator and it could be a serious threat that can be spread dramatically any time.
In order to get ready for any intervention, SIF has already stockpiled Non-food items in safe and secure warehouses in Gaza strip to be ready for distribution as well as have a contingency fund of $50,000 to respond to people needs in the case of crisis outbreak.
This project aims at enhancing SIF capacity to provide an immediate response to families affected by any crisis through distribution of NFIs already stockpiled in SIF two warehouses. This project will target basically IDPs because of natural or human-made disasters. Through the stockpile available because of this project, SIF will target 3,000 families. 1,000 of them supposed to be in DES who will receive PK2 (Bedding sets) and PK4 (Washing sets). Another 1,000 families supposed to experience major damages; Flood;
IDPs in host families, rental accommodation or self-built shelters will receive PK2 (Bedding sets), PK3 (Kitchen sets), PK4 (Washing sets) and/or PK5 (Cooking sets and lightening). Another new 1,000 families will be targeted who experienced partial damages or emergency winterization. Those 1,000 families will receive only PK1 (sealing off kits). Taking in consideration that SIF will intervene in large scale emergencies as well as small scale emergencies with a minimum of 50 families affected according to SIF contingency plan.
These items are designed based on standard packages of the shelter cluster and specified for IDPs at host families during emergencies as well as IDPs in DES in coordination with bodies supervise these DESs. SIF team will carefully consider and tailor each response to the verified needs on the ground, paying particular attention to the most vulnerable and women, and with an overarching focus on protecting the safety, security and dignity of populations targeted with assistance. SIF will act as gap filler for the Cluster members and support all other partners as needed. In all stages of the NFI response, SIF will ensure gender and age awareness including in the disaggregation of data and the targeting of female headed households, incorporating gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations. Further, SIF will ensure that kit contents procured through this project are gender sensitive including through the procurement of items that women indicate better enable them to perform daily tasks.
Stockpiles will be maintained in accordance with an updated Contingency Plan and preparedness measures including improved capacity to respond to emergent needs of the households to host IDPs in urban displacement settings in case of conflict escalations or natural disasters. SIF will implement this project in partnership with Community Based Organization (CBOs) who are able to identify displaced people in urban settings.

1 - Needs

The proposed intervention reposed updated HNO 2021, shelter cluster contingency plan as well as the Inter-Agency contingency plan as a basis of the need assessment. Based on the results of the risk analysis and the likely impact of different scenarios, SIF has decided to focus on contingency planning for the scenarios as described in the Inter-Agency Inter-Cluster Contingency Plan (IACCP) including external escalation of violence, internal unrest and/or earthquake or other natural disasters. Need is assessed through IRNAs or sector specific assessments in which focus group discussions, key informant interviews, individual household interviews and direct observations are used to assess the extent and types of need. Due to the nature of its NFI interventions, SIF cannot know in advance the exact locations or populations it will serve in Gaza Strip; However, in consultation with OCHA, and different Clusters, and based on previous experience, SIF has confidence that there will be significant populations in need in 2021. SIF has based its target numbers on its own capacity to reach populations in the timeframe allotted by the project. Activation of an emergency scenario depends on the number of affected population. This number with help of cluster and other stakeholders should be identified within 48 hours from the onset of an emergency. As it is an initial assessment at this stage, SIF will depend on the information collected from shelter cluster, media, government, ICRC and OCHA. Information will be triangulated and best estimate should be considered.Mass displacement is becoming an increasing concern, with thousands of people at risk of internal displacement under any incursions or any unexpected emergencies such as flooding, harsh weather or internal conflict. According to HNO 2021, there is an increasing need for the provision of the necessary NFIs and disinfection materials, taking in account that maintaining an adequate stock level of NFIs is considered an absolute necessity in light of the escalating risks in the Gaza Strip. The continuous deterioration in the economic situation, combined with the restrictions on the entry of construction materials, hinders new construction as well as the maintenance of existing housing stock. This has resulted in a severe shortage in housing units needed for natural population growth, about 120,000 units, as estimated by the Ministry of Public Works and Housing in 2017. This has led to an increase in substandard living conditions at household level, and unhygienic, overcrowded and unprotected living space in existing shelters. The demand for new housing units and the need for rehabilitation and maintenance of existing shelter are increasing, with a shortage of resources to cover these needs. The partial withholding payment of salaries and allowances to PA employees in Gaza, and resulting deterioration in coping capacities, is leading to legal disputes between landlords and tenants, placing thousands of families living in rented accommodation at risk of eviction. Some 9,356 families are currently registered in MOSD social protection program, out of the 21,818 households renting, as reported in the PCBS 2017 census. In June 2018, the Shelter Cluster published the findings of the inter-agency shelter survey on substandard housing conditions in Gaza, which revealed that 70 per cent of households suffered damage or destruction from the 2014 conflict, and 51 per cent from previous conflicts or floods and winter storms. These conditions will require minimum NFIs and shelter interventions for the protection of the vulnerable families exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns. Since December 2008, Gaza has witnessed three large-scale and many severe intermittent short-term escalations of hostilities, which resulted in the internal displacement of thousands of people who were sheltered at time of conflict at designated emergency shelters (DES) and host communities or relatives. The level of preparedness to respond to the needs of IDPs effectively and in a timely manner is very limited. 52 UNRWA and 18 PA schools were upgraded to minimum standards to serve as DES in case of expected significant IDP numbers. However, there is a lack of stockpiling or arrangements in place to respond with necessary shelter Non-food items to affected people. The inter- agency contingency plan anticipates up to 600,000 IDPs will be in need of shelter support in case of a large scale emergency, both at DES or host families. It is also expected that a further deterioration in the economic situation may put further pressure on poor families to be evicted from their houses. For 2021, SIF will adopt a multidimensional prioritization approach based on adequate assessment and analysis for protection, recent socio-economic conditions, shelter vulnerability, services availability, displacement and the targeted geographical areas for the proposed interventions. Also prioritizing the vulnerable households in areas prone to flooding and weather extremes who have concerns from shelter inadequacy to withstand severe weather conditions in addition to IDPs who suffer from multiple displacement, and families evicted from their homes due to the inability to pay the rental costs. Widowed, separated, or abandoned female heads of households as well as families having PWD (persons with disability) members will be prioritized. SIF has a strong and intensive experience in such interventions. Since 2014 conflict Gaza strip, SIF has built extensive experience in emergency preparedness and response during large-scale emergencies in the Gaza Strip. SIF was one of the first humanitarian organizations that immediately distributed 53,000 hot meals serving 212,000 displaced people in UNRWA schools, and churches.Also, SIF distributed NFIs including 1150 hygiene kits, 1150 kitchen sets, and blankets to internal displaced people at host families in addition to sealing off kits as a response for 2014 crisis.

2 - Activities

SIF will use prepositioned items to cover urgent gaps during large emergency with activation of the IACP and declaration of emergency and with flexibility to respond to small scale emergencies to cover urgent gaps such as resulted from small floods or escalations without activation of the IACP and declaration of emergency. In the latter case, SIF will mobilize preposition items to support 50 HHs as a minim affected numbers according to SIF contingency plan. The following activities will be carried out by SIF prior and during the emergency:• SIF will undertake procurement of the NFIs materials in consultancy with partners (BLD & BEDA) and in coordination with Shelter and NFI cluster. All procurement will follow SIF procurement manual supported by SIF logistics department.• SIF will rent two warehouses in different safe and secured locations.• SIF will work on capacity building of partner NGOs (BLD & BEDA) by training and tools for emergency response, self-recovery, or protection measures to be able to support IDPs and affected people during and post emergencies.During the emergency where the distribution will be conducted , SIF will carry out series of activities related to PSEA through the following: • SIF will conduct refresh brush up training to Project team, Partner’s team covering an introduction to PSEA including introduction to SIF’s Abusive Behavior Procedures, Child Safeguarding Policy, and Code of Conduct. The training will focus on how to report SEA cases as it stipulated in SIF’s Abusive Behavior Procedures with special focus on referral of complaints of SEA, in accordance with IASC guidance. The training will be conducted by SIF’s HR and Protection focal point.• SIF’s human resource unit will ensure that all project-related contracts includes a clause on PSEA as part of the staff members’ contract, service providers ‘contract, partnership with other local partners to be signed as an annex to the contract.• SIF will carry out PSEA-related awareness activities with beneficiaries through PSEA related questions to examine the level of awareness of rights and information on access to SEA related complaints are integrated into surveys and distribution of printable materials include PSEA information and complaint channel information (SIF Abusive Behavioral Procedures) as well as dissemination of STOP@secures-islamique.org that can be used to reach SIF HQ directly if there was no proper response for any abuse.• SIF’s protection unit’s team (Protection manager/focal point and Counsellor as well as case manager) will carefully monitor the protection concern (GBV, abuse, exploitation, and child labor) by exampling reporting procedures against any SEA incident.• If any SEA case arises during the project implementation, SIF protection focal point will identify the case and refer it internally to the counsellor / case manager at SIF protection unit or through relevant external referral services. The following activities will be carried out at the time of crisis either as a large scale conflict or filling urgent gaps during small scale emergencies (small floods or escalations without activation of the IACP and declaration of emergency): • BLD & BEDA will identify potential IDPs through conducting assessments. SIF will do verification and validation of the lists.• BLD & BEDA will carry out the distribution of NFIs in presence of SIF team. • SIF will conduct rapid monitoring and post distribution monitoring through surveys and other monitoring tools.• SIF will conduct coordination with all stakeholders particularly the cluster ensuring no duplication.• SIF will ensure regular and consistent reporting to SIF-HQ, Donor, and cluster, as required. SIF has a good experience to intervene in any emergency needs and it needs to ensure sufficient NFIs stocks through physical stockpiling of emergency NFIs at SIF warehouse to respond to the needs of at least 5,000 IDHHs (25,000IDPs). All proposed NFIs supposed to be delivered in kind Prioritizes HHs with disabled, elderly and sick people as well as women-headed families.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

SIF will continue to ensure that protection is mainstreamed into all of its activities. Further, SIF will plan to further strengthen its links with protection actors in 2021. SIF will also continue to coordinate closely with protection actors to ensure protection representation on missions involving the ¬NFI mobile teams and will work with them to have a strong conflict analysis of the situation and areas prior to setting up distributions. As per usual practice, SIF ensures that Security Risk Assessments are conducted and a security analysis is done prior to dispatching teams to the deep field. Additionally, SIF will conduct twice daily security checks with teams while they are in the field, and ensures that evacuation procedures and guidelines are in place and up ¬to¬ date should an extraction be necessary. Also, Legal advice, overview of trends and context analysis will be sought to ensure the “do no harm” principle is upheld and to increase protection of provided assistance. Several considerations will be taken in considerations as follows: Ensure that the proposed locations for NFIs distribution are considered in terms of the threat of physical attacks, threats to safety. Establish distribution points at a safe distance from shelter sites and temporary settlements. Ensure that they are accessible to persons with disabilities and older persons; Provide NFIs materials and distance between dwellings that offer greater privacy and dignity, especially in cultures where men’s and women’s are markedly separate or the privacy of the family is very important as well as where COVID-19 spread is a serious threat; Take into account local material, existing capacities and the environment. Whenever possible, locally acceptable and available materials and labor will be used to benefit the local economy, while not depleting local resources.
