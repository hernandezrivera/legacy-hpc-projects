Needs

The project would try to address the elevated levels of acute malnutrition and resultant unnecessary morbidity and mortality, the poor health and nutrition preventive practices linked to IYCF and public health practices, as well as the poor uptake of available health and nutrition services. Cox’s Bazar has some of the lowest economic and social indicators in Bangladesh and now hosts almost one million Rohingya refugees that live in in the overcrowded makeshift camps in poor public health conditions. Due to contributing factors such as inadequate shelter and sanitation, insufficient vaccination coverage, disease outbreaks and limited food diversity, children, PLW, and older persons have high levels of acute and chronic malnutrition and those not currently malnourished are at high risk of developing acute malnutrition. The Global Acute Malnutrition rates (GAM) rate in the camp has decreased from 19.3% in November 2017 to 11% in November 2019. Although overall GAM are slowly improving over time with the humanitarian interventions to date, GAM levels remain at serious levels and large numbers of children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) remain in need of life-saving treatment as well as preventive services with the provision of emergency nutrition services. Accessibility to services remains problematic for the most vulnerable groups. Cultural beliefs and practices prevent women in their last trimester of pregnancy, as well as lactating women, from regularly accessing health and nutrition services. Data from nutrition sites continue to show concerning levels of nutritionally vulnerable PLW and infants with wide discrepancies between numbers targeted and actually reached despite improved geographic coverage. A SQUEAC coverage survey (ACF Aug 2018) showed low CMAM coverage. The May 2019 IYCF monitoring assessment recorded exclusive breastfeeding rates for children less than 6 months around 63.5%. Continued low coverage and poor IYCF practices emphasize a need to strengthen public health promotion including improving IYCF and health/nutrition seeking practices, micronutrient supplementation and community mobilization. There are many evidenced based humanitarian interventions that World Concern/World Concern/Medair has used in this and other refugee responses that have been proven to improve morbidity and mortality which could be expanded and integrated better as opportunities arise and as facilitated by the revised Nutrition Sector approach such as: CMAM, IMCI, BSFP, IYCF-E, micronutrient supplementation, peer to peer support groups, community nutrition volunteers and mobilization, home visits for the most vulnerable and C-MAMI.

Activities or outputs

• The project will deliver comprehensive curative and preventive services to target communities residing in four camps. • Integrated OTP, TSFP, IYCF, CMAMI, BSFP and nutrition promotion will be provided to all eligible target groups.• Periodic screening of children under five and PLW will be done for early detection and management of malnutrition cases both at facility and community levels. • Admit 1106 children to OTP and 4502 to TSFP for treatment of SAM and MAM cases respectively. 424 PLW will also be treated as per the national protocol for acute malnutrition. • Capacity building of project staff and camp level partners will be conducted on different thematic areas including CMAM, IYCF, PSS and PFA, Gender mainstreaming, etc. • Referral mechanisms strengthened among different service providers and coordination unit The project is designed to address nutritional needs of children (boys and girls) aged 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women through integrated nutrition services provided at household, community and facility level for targeted population groups in the 4 catchment areas. These groups are selected based on their vulnerability in terms of age and inability to access nutrition required for healthy development of young and unborn children. Home visits to PLW during the 8th/9th month of pregnancy and during the first month after delivery allows for provision of services as well as the ability to have a family member collect rations once the visit is complete Regular awareness sessions for men and women on infant and young children feeding practices and feedback mechanisms developed to help monitor access and quality of nutrition assistance by PLW and children.The project actively promotes gender issues and equality. All staff will sign a code of conduct which includes a protection policy including Sexual Exploitation and abuse and will go through compliance training as per the core values of the organization. During recruitment of staff, World Concern/Medair will include male and female individuals, and will proactively seek female applicants for roles involving interaction with PLWs. World Concern/Medair will offer initial and ongoing training on human rights and gender to equip staff with the awareness and requisite skills to improve the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. The organization uses ISCG gender profile assessments as a base to shape its intervention and all project targets are desegregated by age and sex. Moreover, since the project direct beneficiaries are mainly children and women, it is believed that sustainability of behaviour changes will be maintained and owned by primary care givers at household level to exercise optimal practices after the end of the project period. Peer to peer support groups such as mother to mother and adolescent groups will create a supportive structure at sub block level for mother to share positive practices among themselves. Despite the ongoing challenge of recruiting female volunteers, the project will work hard with local community members to identify and train adequate number of female outreach workers. In all steps of the project, women, girls, men and boys will be targeted with key messages aimed at ensuring community participation and support for vulnerable groups. During monitoring, any gender disparity gaps, and/ or threats to the safety of women and children will be addressed either by taking remedial measures or making referrals to protection actors within the project area. Mechanisms for collecting feedback and ensuring accountability to beneficiaries will be followed including regular focus group discussions, community feedback sessions, help desks and exit interviews.
