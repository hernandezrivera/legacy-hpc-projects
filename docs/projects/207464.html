Project Summary

Oxfam’s proposed intervention has been designed to support 19,152 [f-10023 (elderly: 260, adult:9763 within the total persons with disablities:1092), m-9129 (adult: 8898, elderly: 260, with in the total persons with disabilities: 1008)] in 5 Rohingya Refugee camps and Hnila union under Teknaf. Also, 2888 persons with disability are included as target beneficiaries. In alignment with the Protection Framework and needs/gaps identified under the Protection Sector JRP 2024, Oxfam will implement a holistic Community Based Protection (CBP) response to support the strengthening of the protection environment through facilitating specialized services as complementary to protection sector’s CBP. The intervention consolidates lessons learned and gains over the past 5 years of protection response, promoting localization for community-based solutions, sustaining CBP services and resources through transferring capacity to local actors and CBOs and transition of CBP approach from agency led to community led, drive for protection integration, and addressing gaps in specialized services for the Rohingya and host communities. Community’s empowerment will be supported throughout the CBP intervention. The project will engage with community to promote carrying out self-referrals, facilitate dialogue with camp authority and Rohingya individuals, arrange training on basic protection and different protection thematic. Community consultations will be carried out to identify people-specific needs and develop awareness sessions, IEC materials accordingly. The intervention will address the urgent needs as a complementary service to support GBV survivor and existing PSS services through supporting survivors to meet their essential needs and to recover from their experiences of violence and social negligence. The Emergency assistance for Protection, and community grants are two program components under the CBP that Oxfam has piloted successfully in Responses in other countries and in the Rohingya Response, the implementation began in limited scale. This project will implement structured capacity development activities to enhance the skills of existing volunteers, protection committees and women's groups. This will be carried out through phase-by-phase transition of CBP program designing with the aim to shift from agency led to community led protection intervention. The second new area of emphasis under CBP is to support small scale community actions. Oxfam target to empower community members to implement more complex solution to reduce protection risks through community grants. Awareness raising on emergency preparedness and response, information session on disaster risk assessment and warning system, Production of IEC materials on Protection and DRR preparedness messages, etc are targeted for 2024. Activities will continue focusing on capacity building on disability inclusion for partners, CBV & community network and staffs, protection analysis, facilitation of referrals, and groups representing themselves during negotiation with service providers regarding protection concerns of the community. Protection mainstreaming with DRR activities have been addressed in last year and will continue in the coming years. Oxfam will retain emphasis in promoting peaceful co-existence through facilitating community-driven social cohesion programs. Another area which Oxfam protection is investing in is integration with its GBV prevention program for supporting women group members in growing leadership. Community structures will receive training on Gender in Emergency which will strengthen community’s understanding of gender equality and other basic gender concepts. The project also will promote dialogue with service providers and local authorities in defining solutions. Evidence-based protection monitoring will feed into policy advocacy initiatives that are led by NGOs and protection working group. 4 quarterly protection monitoring reports will be prepared.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Tensions among refugees are high regarding too strict regulations by the law enforcing agencies, often that lead to allegations of coercion. Violence and insecurity have severely impacted refugee family members, as they fear attack, harassment, and conscription into gangs. Many criminal and violent (especially GBV) activities happen at night when it is dark and unseen. Protection services are still limited and many community members, especially among host communities, have little awareness of how support, like legal services, can be accessed. In some instances, access to protection by survivors of violence is hindered by lack of cash to travel to or pay for professional services if they are not free. Furthermore, some survivors do not have time to travel to services because of immediate needs like looking after their families; therefore, deprioritizing their individual needs. In this instance, survivors' recovery from the impact of abuse is hindered because of distress and pressure to feed their families, preventing access to services, like psychosocial sessions, which may provide them the space and time to heal. There is a need to upgrade the CBP approach and equip organized community groups and Volunteers with information and skills to help their neighbours access protection. Protection integration with gender, WASH and livelihood will also play a substantial role in harvesting wider impact under community led solutions. Communicating early warning of environmental/climactic hazards such as cyclones is a gap in certain areas. High levels of violence against children including SGBV, child marriage and child labor persist. 2022 saw continuous registration and assistance to refugees but protection needs for SGBV still outweigh the availability of services and specialized PSS remain limited. The current impacts need to be understood and consulted to design GBV prevention and youth interventions that engage communities. Particularly, there are no actors addressing the PSS of youth aged 18-24 who are key targets for drugs and trafficking. There is rarely any opportunity for communities to discuss their concerns with service providers and protection actors except during needs and protection assessments. “Women are largely not included in the fire response or recovery decision-making processes and adolescent boys and girls are not consistently consulted – both at community level and in the humanitarian response structures” (ref: Feb 2022: RGA, Camp 16, Balukhali, Cox’s Bazar) Enhancing their capacity to analyze protection issues, and providing them the space to discuss their analysis, and promoting dialogue with service providers & local authorities in defining solutions is important to strengthen other measures, including policy advocacy initiatives that are led by NGOs & protection working group.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This is a comprehensive protection project. The entire intervention is built upon a comprehensive Community Based Protection approach with integration with other cross-cutting areas such as gender and safeguarding. There is specific focus on strengthening community groups for identifying and addressing protection risks and concerns, arranging dialogues with camp authority and service providers to find solutions in minimizing risks of refugees, facilitating community action plans for self-protection and self-referral mechanism, carrying out protection monitoring within camps and host locations, supporting and capacitating a network of Rohingya Volunteers to implement community based protection, and such. The project has emphasis on both protection integration and mainstreaming.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Oxfam has demonstrated a robust commitment to addressing gender, age, and disability considerations throughout its operations. Grounded in its policies and practices, Oxfam adheres to the 16 Gender Inclusion and Equity (GIE) minimum standards, ensuring that all programs and operations align with principles of Age, Disability, and Gender Justice. The organization integrates feminist principles into Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) processes, emphasizing meaningful participation, dignity, and empowerment for women and girls. Specifically, Oxfam's protection team is actively engaged in strengthening disability inclusion within standalone initiatives and providing support to other sectors within the organization. This involves coordination with the Disability Inclusion lead agency and collaboration with disability partners, exemplified by reaching out to organizations for inclusive staff training in disability inclusion, particularly within sectors like WASH. By working closely with Humanity and Inclusion in previous programs, Oxfam has demonstrated a commitment to improving protection monitoring and inclusion for Persons with Disabilities and elderly people. Oxfam is holding disability inclusion focal position AHP4 programme. In essence, Oxfam's comprehensive approach ensures that gender, age, and disability considerations are integrated across its operations, reflecting a commitment to inclusivity and social justice.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Through the Protection volunteers and community-based protection structures, protection risks and trends will be identified under protection monitoring. The purpose is to increase understanding of protection trends and the engagement of stakeholders. This project will collect community concerns through the process and channel to relevant authorities for action. Some threats will be reported immediately to the Protection Sector, some to camp administration while others are reported to advocacy and policy team. The project will also implement tools like accountability mechanism and Community Perception Tracking (CPT) which are led by other programs in Oxfam but have been mainstreamed across all programs. CPT contributes to information in a more systematic manner, and translates informal data into more purposeful evidence that will inform current and future response activities. Oxfam MEAL leads the feedback hub as part of Complaint mechanism and prepares accountability report in which protection services and community feedback are also incorporated.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

For this project Oxfam along with its partners will ensure the accountability towards PSEA and will take necessary actions right on time. Exclusive training on safeguarding and child rights policy for staffs, non-staff contacts, community volunteers are designed under the project. At community level, all community group members will be oriented on Oxfam’s safeguarding policy and the key concepts related to sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation as well as the appropriate reporting mechanism. Confidentially policy is also in built within this exclusive orientation. Oxfam also proposes to carry out a PSEA risk analysis for the project activities and set mitigation measures. Oxfam is an active member of the PSEA Network and under this project also will contribute to joint planning with other programs to address SEA related risks.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. The project has capacity building on Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and protection mainstreaming into DRR activities and the disaster management cycle. At community levels, there will be awareness sessions on identifying and addressing protection risks related to potential hazards. Also, there is provision for IEC material development through community consultations for facilitating the awareness sessions and overall awareness rising through message insemination.
