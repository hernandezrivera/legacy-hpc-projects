Project Summary

Promoting Rights to Housing, Land, and Property through Mitigation of Integrated Gender-Based Violence among Women and Girls in Gujba, Tarmuwa, Bade and Geidam LGAs, Yobe State. The summary of the intervention - The intervention includes a range of integrated activities aimed at promoting socio economic empowerment, environmental sustainability, and social resilience among women and girls in Gujba, Tarmuwa, Bade and Geidam LGAs. The training programs will equip participants with skills on income generation activities like modern agriculture- rearing small ruminant animals, Production of organic soil conditioner for backyard gardening, Production of briquettes from waste materials and establishing village savings and loan associations (VSLA), to enabling them generate income and improve household food security. Other skills like tailoring, G/nut oil production, Glanding and production of local scene, soap and cream will be available to train women and support them. Advocacy campaigns will address cultural barriers to women’s and girls’ access to housing, land, and property (HLP) rights while engaging community and government stakeholders to support policy reforms and mitigate gender-based violence (GBV). Additionally, community-based dialogue and mediation committees will be established to foster peaceful coexistence and resolve disputes, particularly those related to HLP rights and GBV. Public awareness will be amplified through radio programs and jingles, promoting resilience and rights-based advocacy. Furthermore, 500 women and girls, including GBV survivors, will receive start-up kits to establish sustainable livelihoods, reinforcing their economic independence and resilience. There will be procurement and distribution of shelter kits to profiled registered women, girls and survivor of GBV.
Purpose:
This intervention aims to enhance the socio-economic empowerment, safety, and resilience of women and girls in Gujba, Tarmuwa, Nguru, and Geidam LGAs, Yobe State. It focuses on addressing systemic gender inequalities, promoting access to housing, land, and property (HLP) rights, and mitigating the risks of all types of gaender-based violence (GBV). By equipping women and girls with livelihood skills and fostering community dialogue, the project seeks to empower them economically and socially while building peaceful and resilient communities.
The Target intervention will directly benefit women and girls in the project LGAs, including those living with disabilities, survivors of GBV, returnees, and individuals from host and displaced communities. The project also engages key stakeholders, such as government agencies, community leaders, and civil society groups, to promote sustainable change.

1 - Needs

According to HRP 2023, out of the 8.3 million people in need and 6 million people targeted, Yobe constituted 2.9 million in need and 1.6 million people targeted to be reached with protection. The assessment, Rapid Overview of Areas of Return (ROAR) carried out in Yunusari LGA in Yobe state, by REACH in June 2022 to understand the need, current displacement patterns and returns in the location; reveals multiple displacements including re-displacement after attempts to return. Consideration of the presence of income sources and reclaiming belonging and assets like farmland or livestock left behind in areas of origin are the main drivers of returns.ROAR assessment reveals that between 30% to 65% of residents experienced looting or destruction of their houses and properties upon displacement. Approximately 40% of individuals lost their HLP documents, including legal and civil records, exacerbating the challenges they face in asserting their rights. Although some have taken steps to address this by applying for affidavits of loss, many still lack essential legal documentation.Compounding these issues is the fact that formal mechanisms such as courts and land registries are not fully operational. The departure of staff due to safety has left these institutions understaffed and unable to effectively address HLP matters. Consequently, residents are confronted with lengthy delays in resolving legal issues, further exacerbating their vulnerability and hindering their access to justice.

Shelter Kits Distribution: Addresses the urgent housing needs of IDPs by providing mapped and profiled beneficiaries in camps and host communities.6. Vocational Training: Focuses on equipping women and girls with income-generation skills for sustainable livelihoods.TargetsThe project targets 70% women and girls (40% women, 30% girls), recognizing their vulnerability to GBV, economic exclusion, and HLP rights denial. By including 20% men and 10% boys, it fosters community-wide participation and shared responsibility for change. Engagement of stakeholders, such as community leaders and government agencies, is integral for scalability and sustainability.Implementation ModalitiesThe implementation strategy emphasizes community involvement, capacity-building, and stakeholder collaboration:1. Training and Skill Development: Practical, hands-on sessions equip participants with immediately applicable and sustainable skills.2. Advocacy and Sensitization: Campaigns address cultural barriers and foster an enabling environment for gender equality and inclusion.3. Dialogue and Mediation Committees: Established for localized, sustainable conflict resolution and community empowerment.4. Public Awareness through Radio: Cost-effective and far-reaching channels disseminate information and advocate for change, especially in rural areas.TechnologiesThe technologies deployed are simple, affordable, and adaptable to local contexts:• Reusable Menstrual Pad Production: Ensures accessibility and widespread adoption while addressing menstrual health.• Organic Soil Conditioner Development: Promotes sustainable practices for addressing soil degradation and enhancing food security.• VSLA Tools: Proven methodologies for financial inclusion and resilience-building in resource-constrained settings.• Backyard Gardening: Combines environmental sustainability with household food security.Key OutcomesThe activities are designed to achieve both immediate and long-term benefits for women, girls, and their communities:1. Economic Empowerment: Training and income-generating activities equip women and girls with resources and confidence to improve livelihoods.2. GBV Mitigation: Advocacy and sensitization campaigns foster gender-sensitive attitudes and reduce GBV risks.3. Environmental Sustainability: Sustainable agriculture and organic practices address environmental challenges while ensuring food security.4. Community Inclusivity: Engaging men, boys, and community leaders shifts cultural norms and ensures widespread acceptance of change.5. Rights-Based Advocacy: Equipping women and girls with the knowledge to claim their HLP rights ensures long-term empowerment and equity.This intervention employs a holistic, inclusive approach to addressing the vulnerabilities of women and girls in Yobe State. By integrating economic empowerment with rights-based advocacy, it creates sustainable, community-wide impacts, fostering resilience, gender equality, and environmental sustainability.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The project is a multi-sectoral initiative that integrates humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding efforts to empower women and girls, mitigate gender-based violence (GBV), and promote access to housing, land, and property (HLP) rights. It includes capacity-building activities such as training women and girls in reusable menstrual pad production, small ruminant rearing, Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLA), organic soil conditioner production, and backyard gardening to enhance livelihoods and foster economic independence. Advocacy and sensitization campaigns will address cultural barriers to HLP rights and promote GBV risk mitigation through community awareness, radio programs, and stakeholder engagement. Economic empowerment will be advanced through the provision of start-up kits to women and girls, including GBV survivors, and by establishing VSLAs to improve financial literacy and access to credit. Conflict resolution and peacebuilding efforts will include forming community-based dialogue and mediation committees to address disputes and foster social cohesion, with active involvement from community and religious leaders. Policy engagement with government agencies and capacity-building for institutions like SEMA, NEMA, and the Ministries of Women Affairs, Environment, and Urban Planning will ensure sustainable implementation and policy reforms. Additionally, monthly radio programs and community mobilization activities will engage 40,000 individuals to raise awareness on HLP rights, GBV, and resilience-building, fostering a peaceful, inclusive, and equitable environment where women and girls can thrive.

7.1.d - In which LGA are you planning to undertake Nexus activities?

The Nexus activities for this project are taking place in Gujba, Tarmuwa, Nguru, and Geidam Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Yobe State. These areas were specifically selected due to their high levels of vulnerability to gender-based violence (GBV), limited access to housing, land, and property (HLP) rights for women and girls, and ongoing challenges related to conflict, displacement, and socio-economic instability. The intervention integrates humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding (HDP) components to address immediate needs while fostering long-term resilience and peaceful coexistence. It targets diverse groups, including women and girls (with an emphasis on GBV survivors), individuals with disabilities, returnees, and host community members, ensuring a holistic and inclusive approach to building safer and more equitable communities. The intervention addresses the socio-economic, cultural, and environmental challenges in the LGAs with a focus on empowering women and girls while fostering community-wide resilience. The integrated approach aims to mitigate gender-based violence (GBV), promote economic inclusion, and enhance access to housing, land, and property (HLP) rights.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SGDI and NADA will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations and avoids causing harm. SGDI and NADA have already mapped out protection risks across the different locations and will continue to do so during the project period. The protection risks already identified include restriction of movement of IDPs in camps, sexual harassment and violence, exploitation, discrimination of returning refugees in Yobe, abduction of women and girls when fetching firewood, perceptions of young women by their communities and others, harassment as a result of the perception of former affiliation with AOG in the family and gender based. SGDI and NADA will ensure that an effective complaints and feedback mechanism is in place to receive opinions from beneficiaries and local authorities. A stronger presence in these hard-to-reach areas will ensure SGDI and NADA's ability to identify quickly, document, and refer (to other partners) protection concerns by SGDI and NADA field staff and national and global advocacy channels. To ensure a better representation, the implementing partners will also ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process.

This project is designed in consultation with the community stakeholders and based on lessons learned from our previous implementation in the target LGAs.SGDI, NADA shall use a people-centered feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on its project to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach will be used to form an LGA-level project committee that will include all community groups' representatives and stakeholders to improve the participation of the affected community in the project implementation process.The NADA project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the already existing community-based project structures to discuss the project deliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. NADA will also use a feedback mechanism through focus group discussions to gather perceptions of the project.
