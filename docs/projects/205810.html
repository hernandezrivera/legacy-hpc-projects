Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Central/ Eastern Equatorial, Jonglei (GPAA), Lakes, Upper Nile and Western Equatorial states in the highlighted counties. This aim to upscale utilization of CP-services including GBV and linkage to multisectoral services to benefit child, adolescents and caregivers. Plan has presence in the proposed locations. are ranked severity 4 while the rest are under level 3. 81,000 (27,000 girls, 23,000 boys; 16,500 women, 14,500men). Out of the 50,000 children targeted, 10,000 (6,000 girls, 4,000 boys) are adolescents while 15% of beneficiaries are persons living with disability. Benefi ciaries profi le includes those affected by fl oods and hunger, living in remote, insecure and hard to reach, IDPs, Returnees, host community, pastoralists, vulnerable adolescents, girl mothers, elderly caregivers, female headed households, UASC, medical concerns etc.
Key protection risks to be addressed are UASC, school dropout, early/forced marriage and GBV, child labor, family separation and neglect, prevention of recruitments, distress and other MHPSS issues, support for persons living with disability, dangers and injuries, access to justice and access to multisectoral services.
Plan strategies to address the issues includes provision of age- gender responsive comprehensive case management including FTR, alternative family based care, cash assistance (CVA), NFI and dignity kits to address complex needs, PSS-MHPSS for children/caregivers, positive parenting and life skills education, CP/GBV capacity building for staff, volunteers, community structures and stakeholders, tailored community awareness and dialogues on harmful cultural practices, GBV prevention and CP monitoring and coordination with actors, mainstreaming diverse feedback mechanisms including integrated helpdesks to address CP risk PSEA/safeguarding. Plan will collaborate with Government, community structures, UNICEF, Protection cluster, CPAOR, CPWG, CMTF, UASC, PSS/MHPSS, GBV and CAAFAG WG. Others includes WASH, Education, PSEATF, shelter, Nutrition, FSL for service deliver.
Mixed static and mobile programming approaches including face to face and remote modalities will be integrated to allow adopting to changing needs and context especially deep fi eld locations to support beneficiaries. Key interagency guidelines and Plan manual will be utilized for quality programming which includes revised CPMS standards, IACM/CP/FTR/ SOPs, IA PSS working group PSS manual, CFS standards among other guidelines.
Plan will also adopt gender transformative approach, rights based, child/survivor centered approaches to programming to address root causes of child violation and GBV to shift community attitudes and negative behaviors that harm children. Gender/disability inclusion trainings will enhance access for persons with disability. Activities integration will be prioritized in Nutrition, Education, GBV, Food Assistance, WASH to holistically address diverse needs of beneficiaries including elderly.
AAP and feedback mechanisms will be integrated to address issues affecting boys, girls, men, women and enhance women and girls' access to information through integrated and child friendly desks in CFS, schools, Nutrition sites etc., use of phones, girl's symposiums, community feedback sessions.
Safety audits, safeguarding risk assessment, community risks mapping and safe planning will strengthen centrality of protection/Protection monitoring to mitigate risks for beneficiaries and strengthen protective environment of children. Sustainability strategies includes capacity strengthening of community structures on CPIE creating pool of change agents and linking to development programs for resilience and working with NNGOs IPCS, GREDO, CHADO and MOGCSW (Government) for planning handover and transitioning this project.

1. Needs

South Sudan face deteriorating humanitarian conditions, and situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, Sudan Crisis-returns, access constraints, flooding and localized drought impacting livelihoods. Protection concerns are high, HRP 2023 indicate 9.4 M people are in needs, including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million children and 337,000 refugees, 15% persons with disability; with 5.53 M-PIN and 3.15M-children in need of CP services, an increase from 2022). Government with support of humanitarian agencies facilitated 420,000 returnees hence 90,000 remained displaced within country with limited reintegration. A deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing with negative coping expected in 2023. UNICEF 2021 reports estimates over 1million children need MHPSS. HNO/HRP 2022 indicate, over 50% of girls are married before 18 years, 2.8 million children are out of school in 2021, 34 % female own a phone compared to 56% male hence need for strategies to enhancing access to information.60% of roads are affected during rainy season limiting humanitarian services especially for children, adolescents, persons living with disability. Government has developed robust legal framework to protect women and children but there is weak reinforcement. Customary courts cover early marriage, divorce, childcare and property rights and hear90% of cases. However, chiefs are predominantly older men, deeply ingrained patriarchal views favoring men. Capacity building of key stakeholders and community structures including the chiefs is necessary to address issues affecting women and girls. Food insecurity and malnutrition have also exacerbated protection risks resulting to negative coping mechanisms (GBV), child labor, child marriage, and human trafficking in 2021, (SSD 2022, HNO/HRP report February 2022). The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 196 grave violations, recruitment/use of 129 children (five girls) in 2021.GBVIMS 2021 report indicate 6,237 GBV incidents reported between January and September 2021; 28% were Child survivors below 18 hence increase from 21% in 2020 to28% in 2021. Plan has presence in proposed locations with active and caseloads of 4758 (2345 boys, 2413 girls), UAM 212 (103 boys, 109 girls), Separated 512 (301 boys, 211 girls) and 4028 OVC (1935 boys, 2090 girls) in need Child Protection services: Active load per location, 861(398 boys, 463 girls) Torit, 885 (493 boys, 392 girls) Pibor, 656 (274 boys, 382 girls) Greater Yei including Morobo, Lainya,Kajokeji, 370 (194 boys, 176 girls) Rumbek and 54 (24 boys, 30 girls) Malakal. The Proposed project will be implemented in Yei, Kajokeji, Lainya, Morobo, Magwi, Torit, Kapoeta South, North, Greater Pibor, Rumbek Center, Rumbek North, Cueibet, Malakal, Greater Pibor &Kapoeta locations. All 11 proposed locations ranked pr Severity 4, apart from Wulu, Torit and Magwi which are under level 3. Protection risks to be addressed are child labor, neglect, distress, CAAFAG, early marriage, neglect, school drop, abduction among other. Interventions proposed are comprehensive CM, foster care, MHPSS-PSS, capacity building of staff volunteers and stakeholders, dialogues/awareness, feedback etc. These interventions are with HRP-2022/2023Protection cluster/CPAOR 2023 Protection objectives PC1, PC2, & PC3 indicators CP 2024 1-6).

2. Project activities

Comprehensive case management services including FTR, alternative care and referrals will be provided to 2,000 (1200 girls ,800 boys) vulnerable children/adolescents including CWD. Interagency CM forms CPIMS V.2+ system will be utilized for CM. Cash assistant, NFI and dignity kits including referrals will be provided (UASC, girl mothers, SGBV, CAAFAG children with disability etc.) as per assessed needs and risks at family level. 2.Capacity building for case workers/volunteers including PWD will be conducted to 100 (60 men and 40women) including remote/face to face supervision and coaching on CM ISP and CPIMS+. Monthly case conferences and case meetings will help to resolve complex cases and provide technical support. 3. Plan will also update and disseminate 5 referral pathways in Greater Pibor, Greater Yei, Kapoeta, Torit and Rumbek to enhance 2-way referrals and linkages to multi-sectoral services. 4. 30,000 (17,000girls, 12,000 boys) girls and boys including children with disabilities including elderly caregivers supported through PSS-MHPSS and other related services. IA PSS WG and Plan guidelines and manuals for schools and CFS will be utilized to ensure quality service delivery. Activities will bed elivered through static sites and outreaches and mobile services. 5. 18 CFS and designated community safe sites-schools/ churches renovated, maintained and equipped to support MHPSS access for children with disability & materials to support PSS activities. 6. Procurement and distribution of play materials such as modelling clay,Manilla papers, coloring pencils, skipping ropes and footballs etc.) to support 40,000children (22,000 G,18,000B) including CWD. 7. 2,000 (1,200 women,800men) women and men will receive psychosocial support & and benefit from parenting sessions for enhanced care and support for children and adolescents. 8. 81,000 (27,000 girls, 23,000boys; 16,500 women, 14,500men); 15% persons with disability and elderly) women, men, girls and boys including children with disabilities benefitting from community dialogues, structured awareness messaging on CP/GBV and other issues affecting children/adolescents. 9. 500 staff, volunteers and stakeholders (200 male, 300 female) will be trained on CPiE including introduction to CP, GBV, safeguarding/PSEA, CPMS, CPSOPs PSS, etc.) 10. CBCPNs will also be facilitated and supported to develop and implement community action plans on CP/GBV and enhance visibility in communities. 12.Training 140 stakeholders/non-CP staff including NNGO caseworkers and MOGCSW and CWPG (70 men and 70 women) on CPMS mainstreaming of CP across sectors, disability and gender inclusion. 13. To enhance structured support to foster caregivers, 250 (150women and 100 men) foster parents will be trained and oriented on Foster parents TOR, Code of conduct, ISP, safeguarding, referral pathway, alternative care guidelines among others to enhance care, support and protection of UASC. the trainings will be delivered in collaboration with MOGCSW, UASC WG and state level CPWG. 13. 10 Safety audits, risk assessments, protection monitoring will be conducted to regularly in CFS, Schools and communities to identify risks, adapt mitigation measures and advocacy. 14. 10 Child friendly feedback mechanisms established, equipped and utilized to enhance beneficiary engagement in the project, sharing lifesaving information, addressing complaints and identifying safeguarding and PSEA concerns. Community forums including specific sessions for women, girls, persons with disability will be held to create safe spaces and platforms to address key issues.
