Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to promote, coordinate and provide accessible, confidential, survivor-centered services to address GBV in line with the Inter-Agency Standards for GBV in emergency programming with special considerations and programming to DRR and COVID-19. UNFPA will continue supporting 35 Women Friendly Spaces and selected health facilities (13) that offer integrated GBV - SRHR services which include GBV case management, mental health, and psychosocial support services, clinical management of rape, family planning services and counseling, treatment of sexually transmitted infections, and emergency referrals to GBV survivors including vulnerable women and girls. By providing multi-sectorial services, UNFPA enhances the health sector response to GBV with a one-stop-shop model ensuring a rights-based approach to GBV. The integration will also ensure SRH staff are trained on GBV principles including confidentiality and consent. The focus will also be placed on prevention and mitigation strategies, mechanisms, and initiatives to support community-based interventions in line with IASC principles and strengthening guidelines. The project will ensure community engagement and awareness aimed at mobilizing communities. This includes youth and men by utilizing tested SASA! Together methodology to address negative social norms and to prevent and respond to GBV in a safe and timely manner. SASA! Together methodology involves everyone and engages a critical mass of people across all levels of society in order to create social norm change and social cohesion between refugee and host communities. As women and girls continue to face protection risks, advocacy and communication for change will be implemented to guarantee their dignity and safety. Through a prioritization towards the safeguarding of beneficiaries, UNFPA will continue to collaborate with the PSEA network to ensure a robust implementation of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse reporting and response mechanisms.
Under the leadership of UNFPA, the GBV sub-sector will enhance engagement with other sectors and government for integration of GBV prevention, mitigation, and response programming for quality services of GBV survivors using Inter-Agency Minimum Standards. The sub-sector will expand comprehensive GBV prevention and response programs for women, and girls, focusing on case management and multi-sectoral referral systems. Capacities will be enhanced on the IASC GBV Guidelines to sector leads, government stakeholders, and implementing partners fostering effective coordination in planning, implementing, and monitoring essential GBV risk mitigation actions in all humanitarian response and services. This will be supported with regular community-based GBV risk monitoring and joint safety audits. The JRP 2022, will deliberately target disability inclusion in GBViE response.

1 - Identified Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) is a global phenomenon that exists beyond cultural, geographical, religious, social, and economic contexts. It is a human rights violation that undermines the health, safety, and dignity of its survivors. In Cox’s Bazar, (GBV) continues to be a threat to women and girls in the Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities. With many refugees having experienced serious risk of intimate partner violence, they are also adjusting to factors that contribute to the daily risk of GBV: increased overcrowding, stress due to the uncertainty of the protracted situation, limited privacy, and lack of lighting across the sites, all of which exacerbate existing safety and security risks. Furthermore, negative practices have arisen such as child marriage, trafficking, and isolation of women and girls inside households. The reported types of GBV incidents include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. According to the June 2021 GBVIMS incidence report, 84% of all violence incidents were related to Intimate Partner Violence.Due to the COVID-19 related measures leading to a suspension of services and restrictions of movement both within the refugee and host communities, women and girls experienced an increase of barriers in reporting violence. Considering that GBV is generally under-reported, and this is only being exacerbated during the COVID-19 context, the recorded cases represent the tip of the iceberg to what is really happening. Further emphasis and concerns are extended to intersectional vulnerable groups such as women and girls with disabilities, the elderly community, religious minorities, and the LGTBQ community as they face particular barriers to accessing services and discrimination. This highlights the continued urgent need of ensuring GBV survivors have access to quality comprehensive GBV services and protection mechanisms are in place. The 2021 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment reports an overreliance on ‘Majihis’ as their preferred point of referral for cases of assault or abuse. Due to the suspension in services, this may have also increased the practice resulting in gaps in accessing medical and specialized services for survivors. From April to September, 65% of households reported unavailability of service/staff due to COVID-19 as a reason for not having access to protection services. When women and girls did seek services, a reported 21% of the Rohingya community felt unsafe accessing services including protection services due to perceptions on safety and security. As such, upscaling awareness-raising on GBV services and safe referral points and intensifying behavioral change programs to address socio-cultural barriers remains a priority. Spaces like Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) are vital to fill this gap as well. In the 2021 UNFPA Vulnerability Assessment Report, a key informant explained “An adolescent girl opted to access [maternal/family planning] healthcare services within the WFS since there is privacy, unlike the healthcare centers within the adjacent host communities that are always congested with no privacy. She felt safe in the WFS.” Women and girls from surrounding host communities have unaddressed basic needs and are also exposed to serious protection risks due to a combination of factors, including poverty, limited access to livelihood opportunities, limited availability of public services which were only exasperated by the Covid-19 pandemic and consequential lockdowns restricting movement and halting services. The Rohingya influx has inevitably had a significant impact on competition for limited existing resources, access to land, and the environment in host communities as well as inter-community relations. In Cox’s Bazar, GBV continues to be a threat to women and girls in the Rohingya refugee camps and surrounding host communities.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Provision of GBV lifesaving services in 35 UNFPA supported WFS in camps & host communities including case management services, MHPSS interventions & emergency referrals.2. Provision of GBV-SRH integration in 35 UNFPA supported WFS in camps & host communities including family planning counseling, CMR, STI treatment.3. Provision of comprehensive case management services, PSS, & emergency referrals for GBV survivors in 13 UNFPA supported health facilities & awareness-raising by community volunteers on GBV, SRH, and family planning issues with the targeted community.4. Provision of community engagement and awareness aiming at mobilizing communities, including youth & men, to prevent-respond to GBV in a safe manner, to address power imbalance & the negative social norms using tested social mobilization methodologies i.e. SASA! Together.5. Engage women & girls in skill development-self-reliance activities-GBV awareness sessions in 10 UNFPA supported WLCC in partnership with WFP & FAO to promote resilience-empowerment of the targeted community to enable an environment of equal participation in both social & economic life.6. Promote the inclusion-participation of communities of concern, particularly women, PWDs, gender diverse population and minorities in UNFPA’s programming to strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to prevent-mitigate protection risks by fostering human rights awareness & cohesion among the communities.7. Ensure capacity building of partner organization on GBV related topics including GBV basic principles, GBV case management, PSEA, MHPSS, PFA, suicide prevention, GBVIMS+.8. Provide quality technical assistance supervision to ensure GBV services adhere to Do no harm principle, survivor-centered approach & IASC GBV Case Management guidelines.9. Ensure a robust PSEA response strategy is implemented across different activities & strengthen the PSEA capacity of the IPs through promoting reporting mechanism, training, awareness-raising with UNFPA & IP personnel, & program participants.10. Continue to strengthen GBVSS coordination (52 partners) to ensure multi-sector response & survivors have access to appropriate GBV-SRH and MHPSS integrated services, including legal services. This includes strengthening coordination to ensure timely-appropriate referrals, & the quality service provision through regular monitoring by field coordinators to assess compliance, identify gaps-challenges & strengthen coordination with existing actors. Do no harm & survivor-centered approaches will be prioritized through safe-dignified referral systems, mapping of available quality-appropriate services & the promotion of interagency GBV standard operating procedures.11. Ensure multi-level GBV risk mitigation by engaging the WSG, CWG, and Self-help Group members to conduct GBV service mapping, identify GBV risks, mitigation measures, and implement the identified measures through community-led initiatives.12. Strengthen advocacy-communication initiatives to ensure that GBV is understood as lifesaving & not deprioritized during emergencies. This will secure the leadership during the 16 days of Activism coordinated and led by the GBVSS (led by UNFPA) with high-quality deliverables or high-level discussions with key partners, including with the Govt. & the publication & dissemination of GBVSS policy briefs to support negotiations. 13. Strengthen referral mechanisms & GBV focal points in the camps to ensure survivors access to timely and quality available GBV-SRH/MHPSS or legal appropriate services.14. Ensure capacity building for all GBV focal points in the PERU and other sectors on GBV Mainstreaming, GBV Minimum standards, & the min. training package for case management or other GBV service providers including volunteers involved in services or referral pathways (i.e. CFM personnel). 15.Ensure the procurement, pre-positioning-distribution of dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls at risk of GBV during emergencies.
