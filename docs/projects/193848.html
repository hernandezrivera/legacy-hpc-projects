Project Summary

This proposed project will increase the preparedness and response capacity of the Middle Area, Khan Yunis and Rafah governorates in case of future escalation or natural hazards, through prepositioning and replenishing of Emergency NFI kits for Internally Displaced People (IDPs), host families, vulnerable households (HHs) affected/exposed to the risk of harsh weather and by increasing the response capacity of 300 HHs (historically served as host families in times of emergency). The project will provide tailored NFI assistance to 900 vulnerable HHs with 200 also receiving emergency shelter repair assistance. 1,200 HHs (6,600 individuals) will directly benefit from the project under activities 4 and 6. This project will reinforce the Community-based Emergency Response Networks (CERNs), through trainings and by adding 3 new Community-Based Organisations (CBOs) to improve communication channels with local communities and ensure the CERNs’ sustainability (1 CBO per governorate). This project will be implemented in partnership with Unlimited Friends Association (UFA) and Fares Al-Arab For Development (FAFD). The project leverages on ACTED’s existing network of 4 warehouses in the 3 governorates, pre-positioned with NFIs, under previous HF-funded projects to cover the needs of 17,400 individuals, and which were successfully used following the August 2022 and May 2021 escalations and winter storms/harsh weather in January 2022.
In addition, this project will benefit from the CERNs and the CBPCs established under previous projects, which have a focus on supporting emergency activities.
This project is informed by assessments ACTED conducted in 2022, consultations with key stakeholders, recommendations from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and the Ministry of Public Work and Housing (MoPWH) and the Shelter & NFI in Gaza. This intervention is aligned with the 2023 HNO priorities and supports the achievement of the 2023 HRP for the shelter cluster, most notably by contributing to the Specific Objectives 2 and 3.This involves NFIs stockpiling, improving urban displacement setting to host IDPs, and community capacity building to ensure adequate functionality at time of emergency. The intervention will alleviate unmet needs exposing HHs to harsh weather and provide an emergency assistance (including NFI distribution) to IDPs and Host Communities during escalations of hostilities or natural hazards.
ACTED, FAFD and UFA will implement a multi-pronged approach, complementary to the HF-funded projects ACTED currently implements, to increase access of vulnerable HHs to safe and secure shelters through the following activities:
A1: Strengthening 2 CERNs through trainings and by adding 3 CBOs
A2: Conducting required maintenance and improvement works of ACTED's 4 warehouses
A3: Renewing the existing Framework Agreements (FWA) for NFIs supplying and sign new FWA with a contractor for the repair/maintenance works
A4: Providing tailored urgent assistance to affected 900 HHs at risk of manmade or natural hazards (IDPs, Host communities, HHs exposed to harsh weather, damaged HHs during the hostilities)
A5: Replenishing stockpiles with NFIs to prepare for future emergencies.
A6: Increasing 300 host families’ response capacities in the targeted communities
The project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with a specific focus on the most vulnerable groups (women, girls, boys, PwD and older people). As PwD are sometimes deliberately prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct HH-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. PwDs and their caregivers will be consulted throughout the project to better understand their safety and dignity concerns. Gender principles will also be mainstreamed and CBPCs will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases such as GBV. AAP will also be core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

The Gaza Strip’s economy is at breaking point. Subject to recurrent economic shocks due to the protracted conflict, the August-2022 escalation, the May-2021 escalation, the limited ability of international actors to provide assistance, and the blockade on land, air and sea since 2007, economic activity has greatly diminished, leading to impoverishment and de-development of the population. Gaza is one of the most densely-populated areas in the world. Its 2.11 million Palestinians are cramped in 365 km2 (Gaza’s total land area), where population density is 5,800/km2 (OCHA). It suffers from a chronic shortage in housing that is expected to exceed 120,000 units, with an annual average of 14,000 additional housing units needed to meet natural growth. The housing crisis is critical: 591,000 persons in the Gaza strip are in fact in need of humanitarian aid in the shelter sector. Based on the 2022 Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA), 17.2% of the surveyed housing units in Gaza reported living in critical shelter (damaged/unfinished buildings, tent, container). 59.8% reported damage to their shelter (large cracks/ opening in the walls and roof, broken/cracked windows, broken exterior doors). In August-2022 escalation, 1,500 housing units were damaged or destroyed, of which 97 housing units had moderate to severe damages and 26 housing units were destroyed, leaving 600 people displaced. This is added to the remaining caseload of the May-2021 escalation, during which 62,000 housing units had been damaged. 7,000 people were also still displaced in September 2022 following this May-2021 escalation. According to the 2022 MSNA, 37.3% of households that had their homes damaged as a result of hostilities still had no capacity to repair their homes, even months after the escalation. The 2022 MSNA emphasized the need to target HHs with a member with disability and female-headed HHs, as 16% of HHs assessed who have at least 1 member with a disability reported difficulties to meeting their basic needs and had damaged, defects, or issues to their shelter. In addition, 72% of female-headed HHs are experiencing stress, severe or extreme need of assistance, including 452 households in extreme need of assistance. As such, this project will prioritise PwD and female-headed HHs in its BNF selection.To better inform its emergency preparedness response in the shelter sector, ACTED conducted in June 2021 a survey with 302 host families under its ongoing HF-funded emergency preparedness project. This assessment provided ACTED with the following findings: 94% of respondents did not receive assistance during the hosting period; 96% confirmed that there was shortage in NFIs during the hosting period; 79% had no idea about any referral systems; and 26% of host families reported that the living conditions available did not provide children, women, and PwDs with safety and privacy. The main shelter concerns highlighted were the difficulties to protect their shelter from weather conditions, and inadequate doors, windows and ventilation. The results of this survey had been confirmed by the established CERNs and ACTED had added participants to the waiting list of potential BNFs to support them during emergencies that may erupt. During the harsh winter conditions of January/February 2022, ACTED responded to the shelter needs of 191 HHs through the distribution of winterizations kits. The kits included tarpaulin sheets to seal off roofs, nylon sheets to seal off windows, mattresses, blankets, and pillows Furthermore, FAFD conducted in May 2022 a needs assessment in West of Khan Younis with 31 vulnerable HHs (210 individuals including 11% who were PwDs). 98% of the families surveyed lived in very fragile living conditions and could not face harsh weather conditions and climate fluctuations during the summer and winter seasons. The vulnerable HHs expressed their needs for NFI kits that could be distributed to support them during harsh weather conditions, especially during winter. In addition, since the beginning of 2022, ACTED has received numerous requests for NFIs assistance prior to the start of winter in order to seal off defects and damages that could cause flooding to vulnerable HHs. These requests could be met through this proposed stockpiling project to mitigate the risks of vulnerable HHs’ exposure to harsh weather conditions. Furthermore, ACTED conducted three assessments between March and October 2022 in the Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorates of the Gaza strip. The assessments targeted 425 HHs living in substandard shelters and shelters in need of winterization. All housing units visited were not complying with at least one of the minimum shelter standards. 53% were living in asbestos/corrugated metal shelters, and 33 % did not have access to at least one room insulated against heat and cold. Vulnerable groups (children, PwDs and older people) which made up more than 53% of the visited individuals, were affected by the impact of the recent hostilities and harsh winter conditions. In general, the lack of adequate shelter protection increases their vulnerability, and health risks in particular for children and older people HH’s members.Finally, according to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, for an effective, efficient and timely response to unexpected and frequent escalations as well as unforeseen emergencies, it is necessary to increase the partners’ capacities to respond to emerging needs. This notably involves NFIs stockpiling, improving urban displacement setting to host IDPs, community capacity building, and maintaining the existing Designated Emergency Shelters (DES) to ensure adequate functionality at time of emergency. The proposed action thus aims at addressing part of the Shelter priorities for the HRP 2023.

2 - Activities

A1: Strengthening the Community Emergency-Response Network (CERN)The CERN in the Middle Area, Khan Younis and Rafah governorates will be strengthened though refresher trainings on the main protection-related topics for their members and signing MoUs with 3 CBOs in the targeted governorates (one per governorate). This CERN involve 3 Community-Based Preparedness Committees (CBPCs), networks of volunteers, and 3 CBOs (1 in Rafah, 1 in Khan Younis and 1 in the Middle Area governorates). Emergency responses will be triggered based on ACTED’s coordination with the Shelter cluster. Upon trigger, the CERN will be responsible to ensure the timely and quality implementation of the emergency response, including coordination with stakeholders, conducting needs assessments and organizing, delivering and monitoring NFI distributions. A2: Conduct required maintenance/improvement works of ACTED's 4 warehousesACTED will ensure its 4 warehouses are well functioning and secured by conducting needed maintenance of security and safety materials, including fire extinguishers and first aid kits.A3: Renew the existing Framework Agreements (FWA) for NFIs supplying and sign new FWA with a contractor for the repair/maintenance works ACTED will launch a procurement process to renew its existing FWA with qualified suppliers to provide NFIs during any emergency. Current NFIs FWA signed under previous HF projects, will end in June 2023 and ACTED will renew it until December 2023. This will guarantee ACTED’s capacity to proceed to transparent and accountable procurement process at the early onset of emergencies; thus ensuring the capacity to provide rapid emergency response. ACTED will also launch a competitive tender in order to select a local contractor for the urgent repair works to ensure timely response that includes winterization/summarization interventions to support HHs not able to do works by themselves. This will ensure the timeliness and the quality of the response and support provided through the project. A4: Providing tailored urgent assistance to affected HHs/ HHs at risk of manmade or natural hazards (IDPs, Host communities, HHs exposed to harsh weather) ACTED, UFA, and FAFD will distribute to 900 HHs affected or at risk of natural or manmade hazards, tailored NFIs, for an equivalent of 580 ILS for each HH. It is anticipated that most distributions will take place at the nearest warehouse. However, door-to-door distributions will be used for people who are not able to go to the warehouse, for instance, PwD, older people, and female-headed HH caring for children. ACTED’s CRM card along with a PSEA leaflet will be distributed to each HH. In addition, the project team will identify the urgent repairs works for 200 HH, out of the 900, whose shelter was affected by the August 2022 or May 2021 escalations or at risk of manmade or natural hazards. Assistance for these HHs will be provided through NFIs purchased under former and ongoing projects, currently stockpiled in ACTED’s warehouses. ACTED in Khan Younis, UFA in the Middle Area, and FAFD in Rafah are expected to distribute them items of protection from rain like adhesive tape, rope and nylon, and to ensure living conditions of shelters. These HHs will have the possibility to benefit from light repair and maintenance provided by a contractor hired under activity 3, to ensure the quality and timeliness of the response and support. These HHs will receive the equivalent of an additional 3,000 ILS (as in-kind and service support) to conduct these light-repair works. ACTED’s AMEU will conduct both Post Rehabilitation Monitoring to follow up on the light repairs, and Post-Distribution Monitoring to collect BNF feedback (both male and female BNFs) on the quality, sufficiency, utilisation and effectiveness of the assistance provided. A5: Replenish stockpiles with NFIs to prepare for future emergenciesACTED will purchase NFIs to constitute/replenish stockpiles based on the signed FWA. Emphasis will be placed on having stockpiles of bedding kits and sealing off kits based on discussions with the Shelter cluster. Through this NFI purchase, ACTED will be able to cover the needs of 900 HHs (11,400 individuals) in time of emergency. Based on the 4 warehouses contracts signed under other projects, ACTED will have the capacity to store NFIs stockpiled in its warehouses located in the targeted governorates, and ensuring the capacity to provide rapid emergency response. The warehouses will remain equipped with security and safety materials, including fire extinguishers, first aid kits and garbage disposal facilities whenever needed. For each movement, ACTED program and/or logistics staff, will ensure transparency and the correct filling of related documents. A6: Increasing host families’ capacities in the targeted communitiesThe CERN supported by their volunteer networks, will identify at least 300 HHs who served as host families during last escalations, and have a maintained capacity to host IDPs in case of future emergency (at least 100 HHs per governorate). The CERN will carry out 10 information sessions with the identified 300 HHs, to build their capacity ahead of future emergencies. ACTED and the two CERN will conduct a survey with the 300 HHs to assess their hosting capacity and identify gaps, and needs that could prevent them from hosting IDPs or hinder the safety and dignity of IDPs and/or host families. The CERN will conduct trainings with the host communities to respond to their identified needs and concerns. Across all activities BNFs will be made aware of ACTED’s complaint response mechanism (CRM) and PSEA awareness materials will be distributed. ACTED will ensure that a standard close on PSEA is included in all agreements in accordance with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee principles and guidelines. Any outstanding needs detected throughout the project, especially protection-related ones (PSEA, MHPSS, GBV) will be referred to sectoral partners.

3 - Indicators

Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenges. Specifically, ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) is independent from the Program Department and will be in charge of reinforcing the projects’ accountability throughout the project cycle management by regularly assessing project implementation. AMEU is an essential part of ACTED’s project management structure, providing a mechanism to crosscheck and give recommendations on the work of the project team and partners. AMEU leads assessment of baseline situations of beneficiaries through data collection, monitors ongoing implementation through regular field visits and consultations with beneficiaries, and evaluates the outcome of the activities. This methodology ensures the AMEU uses an outcome monitoring approach to analyse the effect of the action on target groups. ACTED will measure the following indicators: # of people/HHs exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported with shelter assistance (900 HHs)# of households with improved capacity to host IDPs (300HHs); # of households have received emergency NFIs assistance (900 HHs); # of individuals that shelter agencies are able to support immediately at the onset of an emergency (11,400 individuals); # of standard/customized Shelter NFI kits prepositioned at agency’s warehouses (900 kits); % of female members are participating actively in the community-based protection committees (40%); # of Community- based protection Committees (CBPCs) reinforced through trainings to scale-up their ability to provide services to their communities (3); % of CBPC members who demonstrate improvement in their knowledge after attending the trainings (80%); % of households who report satisfaction with the intervention (80%); % of females, old people, people with disabilities who report improved safety and dignity after the intervention; Procedures are in place to receive written agreement from partners or suppliers entering into cooperative arrangements with the ACTED that they are aware of and will abide by the standards of the PSEA policy (80%); % of complaints have been managed in a timely manner(15 working days based on ACTED CRM SOPs (90%). ACTED’s project management team will use the Project Management Framework (PMF) tool to plan and monitor implementation to assess progress and highlight blocking points. The action will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to provide feedback and suggestions directly to ACTED staff through a phone line, post, email, and complaint boxes in relevant locations, as well as directly to partner’s staff members. The CRM should ensure that beneficiaries can easily and without fear of repercussion communicate cases of abuse of power by financial institutions, community leaders and/or other key actors involved in the cash-transfer program and/or aspects of program and/or transfer modality that inadvertently have negative implications. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Particular efforts will be dedicated to ensure that BNF are aware of the functioning and use of this tool since the last MSNA (July 2022) revealed that only 18,4% of HHs had access/knowledge of complaint mechanisms in Gaza. Financial monitoring will be undertaken by all project partners and then consolidated by ACTED, with external audits of the action taking place on an annual basis. ACTED staff will request from UFA and FAFD monthly reporting on activities on top of contractual obligations to ensure all indicators are on track and register the beneficiaries’ satisfaction and any difficulties encountered. This monthly reporting will minimise the risk of delays in the activities’ implementation, that any change in the implementation of the project is notified and finally that all indicators are on track and if needed corrective measures are taken to reach the target.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co- applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that ACTED teams learn from the feedback provided through this process.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, WhatsApp, email address, complaints boxes and directly through ACTED staff during field visits. This plurality of means aims to ensure CRM accessibility to all, including vulnerable groups with specific needs. During the assessment phase, field officers will provide a CRM card to each BNF, including all communication channels mentioned above, and explanations on the CRM, to ensure that all BNF feel comfortable and in measure to reach it if needed. Female staff, part of the team to which BNF will be able to refer to, will notably ensure female BNF feel safe to complain. AME staff will dedicate specific time to vulnerable groups during field visits to re-explain the CRM and emphasise that BNF can directly complain to them during visits - if one person would not have the capacity to use/access a phone/email address or the complaint box (notably children, PwD, and old people). As PwD are sometimes deliberately prevented from expressing their needs, the project team will conduct household-level visits to assess their specific needs and explain the purpose of the project. PwDs and their caregivers will be consulted throughout the project to better understand their safety and dignity concerns. ACTED’s commitment to AAP is fully integrated into its recruitment process, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and partnership agreements. All ACTED staff working for the ACTED oPt mission are trained on the Code of Conduct, PSEA and Child Protection Policies. In March 2022, all ACTED staff participated in a refresher session on PSEA organized by the Protection Cluster, to strengthen their capacities to recognize exploitation and abuse situations, as well as adopt a good behavior to report. This session also provided an overview of the Interagency Community Complaint Mechanism and its SOPs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly regarding activity design with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. In particular, ACTED, UFA, and FAFD will undertake regular visits to the communities and beneficiary HHs to ensure that protection concerns are incorporated throughout the intervention, thus enhancing safety, security and privacy for women, children and other vulnerable groups. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do-No-Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’s Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by all staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; awareness amongst beneficiaries of what is unacceptable behavior and how to report it; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. The action fully acknowledges that different groups and individuals have specific needs and assistance will therefore be adapted accordingly. To this end, ACTED will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and family and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise to authenticate the fairness of the selection process and all applicants will be notified of the final decision and provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM. ACTED will put particular emphasis on informing beneficiaries on the functioning and use of the CRM in the Gaza strip, since the last MSNA conducted in June 2022, revealed that only 18.4% of HHs which benefitted from humanitarian aid, reported awareness of how to access a complaint or reporting mechanism. Similarly, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are considered, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, older people and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities. Finally, the project will deliberately target the most vulnerable groups for inclusion into the program. For instance, women will be highly encouraged to participate in the CBPCs to ensure that membership remains at 40% female at a minimum. Similar efforts will be made to ensure that vulnerabilities do not act as a barrier to accessing aid. For instance, CBPCs will conduct HH visits to assist in the completion and collection of applications for the vulnerable (including older people, PwDs) that might be unable to reach the registration site.
