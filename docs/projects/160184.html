Project Summary

Latest September 2019 IPC FSL and Malnutrition Analysis for January-April 2019 projections indicate persistence of unprecedented food insecurity and high malnutrition levels in the country with an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Early 2020 (Jan-April 2020) projections estimate at least 61.1%, 58% and 64.9% of the populations in Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity respectively will be in crisis, Emergency and Catastrophe, as households deplete food stocks and access to livestock products and wild foods reduce seasonally.
Livestock productivity, estimated at only 20% of the full potential due to high morbidity and mortality due to inadequate veterinary services and livestock diseases which threaten to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists. These, coupled with crop and vegetable pests and diseases including FAW, diminishing consumer purchasing power and inflation, reduced market functionality, insufficient food availability, high disease prevalence, limited dietary diversity among others have further contributed to extreme Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels, which recorded highest levels in Unity (16.6) and Jonglei (19.4) (IPC, 2019).
Throughout the crisis, livestock assets, crops, vegetables and fisheries resources have provided a critical safety net to the conflict affected communities, including the displaced populations in hard to reach areas which the proposed project will focus on. In locations with IPC 4 and 5, VSF-Suisse will in addition deploy limited unconditional cash grants/vouchers to selected worst off households, to enable them access essential foods from the market.
VSF Suisse 24 years of experience in the three states, presence on the ground and proven capacity will deliver high quality programming by leveraging on strong grass root networks, local knowledge, understanding of seasonal and location specific contexts. VSF Suisse in addition currently serves as the Co-Chair of the FSL Cluster Livestock Technical Working Group.
The project which will target 120,000 beneficiaries (20000 men, 20000 women, 40000 girls and 40000 boys) will be aimed at ensuring cost efficiency through complementarities by linkages with FAO pipeline for supply of crops, vegetables and fishing kits and veterinary vaccines in order to promote preparedness, household resilience and self-reliance through protection of livelihood opportunities. The project will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

Latest September 2019 IPC FSL and Malnutrition Analysis for January-April 2019 projections indicate persistence of unprecedented food insecurity and high malnutrition levels in the country with an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Recent cycles of dry spells early 2019 and current flooding in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Jonglei low lying areas have led to inundation of crop fields and villages and significantly damaged crops in addition to a spike in livestock disease outbreaks (VSF Livestock Disease Outbreak Reports, 2019) and livelihoods, with most of the low-lying areas affected by flooding and water logging. These coupled with prevalent cyclical waves of cattle related fighting with recent clashes reported in Mayom, Panyijiar, Guit, Pibor, Koch; and endemic violent cycles between host communities and Misseriya/Reizegat migrant Arabs in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, have led to prolonged congregation of livestock in swampy/ marsh areas and disrupted migratory patterns, further leading to livestock disease outbreaks with significant high mortality rates and subsequent erosion of the livestock sector contribution (VSF Suisse Baseline Survey, June 2019; VSF Suisse Livestock Disease Outbreak Reports, 2019; REACH Overview Reports, 2019). 2 - Activities

• Prepositioning, transport and distribution of crops, fishing kits and vegetable kits among 60,000 beneficiaries (10,000 HHs) – comprised of 10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 girls and 20000 boys)• Conduct agronomic trainings and post-harvest preservation techniques training for 30,000 household heads in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei• Training/ Refresher training for 300 Community Based Animal Health Workers/ Stock Persons (20 % female, 70 % male)• Conduct refresher training of 40 Stock Persons and Animal Health Auxilliaries on cold chain management and monitoring to support safe storage of veterinary vaccines/ integrity.• Install and maintain 20 solar vaccine fridges for veterinary vaccine handling in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei.• Carry out livestock disease investigation outbreaks in collaboration with line ministries, FAO among other key stakeholders in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei • Carry out livestock vaccination and campaigns targeting 800,000 heads of livestock against endemic livestock diseases in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei• Carry out livestock treatment and deworming campaigns through targeting 400,000 heads of livestock against endemic livestock diseases in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei.• Support 5000 households with seed fairs using locally sourced seed inputs in Northern Bahr el Ghazal.• Provide unconditional cash transfers/ Voucher system for seed procurement targeting 2000 households.• Support value chain addition e.g. through milk and fish preservation and marketing among 2000 households.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will ensure consultations including beneficiaries’ rights recognition disclosure of various support packages/ activities during mobilization and sensitization in presence of beneficiary trustees and local leadership. The project will in addition ensure that their rights are protected in addition to enabling them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at activity/ distribution sites and appropriate contacts. VSF Suisse will also engage Cluster leads, communities, local leaders FAO in ensuring that the most vulnerable populations are targeted and reached. An active Complaints and Feedback desks shall be established at various distribution/ training with results analyzed to inform a better response.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will prioritize displacement-affected communities by ensuring unimpeded and timely access to prosed services; however, host communities including returnees will as well be targeted. These will include internally displaced persons with whom the project will ensure protection of their rights and dignity in addition to ensuring congregation of IDPs among other target groups does not expose them further to risks/ protection concerns using Do No Harm approaches.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As with other projects implemented by VSF Suisse, the organization will work with the relevant stakeholder groups, line ministries of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries at both national and state levels, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), beneficiaries and local leadership structures, acknowledging their immense contribution in project implementation. These will be involved across all stages of implementation including during monitoring/ evaluation phases. Inputs distribution, trainings and animal health campaigns will be conducted hand in hand with these stakeholders in addition to them taking lead in these initiatives, that will maintain implementation momentum, with a smooth project closure, hand over and sustainability.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Proposed interventions are aligned to the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan Objectives, South Sudan National Agricultural and Livestock Extension Policy (NALEP) guidelines and Consolidated Agriculture Master Plan. These will respond by prioritizing vulnerable populations who will be supported with crop, vegetable, fishing inputs and livestock animal health campaigns including cash/vouchers where feasible eg., market functionality.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Unity and Jonglei States are quite unstable with key elements of protection concerns including rampant cases of Gender Based Violence, abductions among other grave human rights violations; unlike Northern Bahr el Ghazal which is relatively stable. Hence therefore, to mitigate protection concerns across all targeted areas, VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host, IDP and returnee communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities, returnees and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In view of the current fragile situation in the country, sustainability efforts will focus on strengthening existing community structures and beneficiary participation including linkages and close collaboration with line ministries of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries. In addition, animal health campaigns will focus on strengthening Community Animal Health Workers, Cattle Camp and Local Authority institutions and promotion of cost recovery model approach in provision of livestock treatment interventions.
