Project Summary

Zimbabwe, amongst other Southern African countries, has been forecasted to expect several hazards between 2019 and 2020, the prime being drought leading to food insecurity, flooding increasing exposure to diarrheal diseases such as Cholera and economic hardships with an impact on access basic services. All mentioned hazards have an expected impact on child protection particularly family separation leading to unaccompanied and separated children, an increase in children on the move and exposure to child protection violations as children seek employment, attempt to cross borders to reunify with families in addition to risk to trafficking whilst on the move.
This project then seeks to target children at risk of family separation, unaccompanied and separated children and children on the move through the following interventions:-
i. Strengthening foster and kinship care (960 foster parents)
ii. Capacitating CCWs to respond to unaccompanied and separated children in emergency. (1440 CCWs)
iii. Awareness on child protection implications of family separation and children on the move (16 districts)
iv. Tracing, reunification and support packages (1,600 children)
v. Case conferencing and coordination
vi. Community feedback and PSEA
vii. Rapid assessments (baseline, mid-term and end line)
viii. Refresher training for project staff – Psychological First Aid, protocols on site arrival, coordination

1 - Activities

(i) Strengthening foster and kinship care - Aligning to the 6-tier system, the project will work with extended families training them on positive parenting and their role in the prevention of family separation. Further, working with the Department of Social Welfare (DSW), the project will support updating and maintenance of foster care registers at district level, in addition to strengthen the preparedness of foster parents to respond to unaccompanied and separated children in emergency. (ii) Capacitating Child Care Workers (CCWs) to respond to unaccompanied and separated children in emergency - A gap noted in previous implementation was the low level of emergency preparedness amongst CCWs. Therefore, the project will train this cadre on emergency preparedness in the handling of child protection cases for unaccompanied and separated children, children on the move, child labour and sexual exploitation. (iii) Awareness on child protection implications of family separation and children on the move - Part of emergency preparedness and response when it comes to child protection involves raising community awareness on implications of emergency situations on child protection, thereby, equipping them with knowledge on how to identify cases of family separation, children on the move and how to address challenges and push factors. Through this project, CPS will conduct awareness campaigns using platforms such as child friendly spaces, camps, schools and community halls fostering community preparedness and grassroots response systems to emergency. (iv) Tracing, reunification and support packages - DSW will be supported to conduct family tracing and reunification for children, and where a suitable home cannot be found amongst extended relatives or foster care. Due to the impromptu nature of emergency situations, the project will provide start up support for children reunified and in alternative care arrangements in the form of a package that consists of NFIs including a dignity kit, FIs, education support and support for civil registration. (v) Case conferencing and coordination - Due to the complex nature of cases the project will incorporate case conferencing platforms to discuss complex cases that require the participation of several partners and government departments, amongst other actors. (vi) Community feedback and PSEA - Foster care and CCW trainings will include messages on forms of PSEA and reporting channels. Suggestion boxes will be set up at local DSW operating offices, schools and campsite, to be opened weekly to address cases reported. Suggestion boxes will also serve as a feedback mechanism for community members on satisfaction with service delivery. Further, CPS will utilize platforms such as child friendly spaces, camps, schools and community halls to attain feedback on the same. (vii) Rapid assessments - Rapid assessments will be conducted at baseline, midterm and end line to determine the need in communities, measure change and determine short term outputs and outcomes of the project. These will be done in conjunction with DSW, partner organisations, representatives of affected communities including children and UNICEF, where applicable. These assessments will be followed by consultations on priority interventions to address risks of family separation and strategies likely to yield positive results. (viii) Refresher training for project staff (Psychological First Aid, protocols on site arrival, coordination) - To ensure delivery of quality support, project staff will receive a refresher training on Psychological First Aid from PSS Specialists, including site protocol on arrival and coordination to ensure swift service delivery on the onset of the emergency.

2 - Indicators

All indicators will be disaggregated by district, ward, age, sex and disability. Indicators will measure the number of children assisted, foster parents and caregivers trained, individuals reached with awareness activities and community cadres trained. (i) # of Unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs) affected supported with family tracing, family reunification and/or appropriate alternative care (ii) # of children and adults reached with awareness messages on CPiE including Violence against children, GBV and PSEA (iii) # of specialized GBV and CP service providers and community cadres trained in GBV and CP in emergencies response (iv) # of parents/caregivers of children affected by humanitarian situation reached with parenting support initiatives (disaggregated by primary caregivers and foster parents)
