Project Summary

Internal displacement outside of camps remains one of the challenges in global displacement and has received increased attention from the humanitarian community over the past years. As displacement trend increased in complexity, becoming more urban and protracted nature, majority of displaced population also have access to wider range of settlement options. Unfortunately, this have not always translated to increase in access to assistance and protection.
In North East Nigeria, it is estimated that 60% of the IDPs live in the host community, with some residing in spontaneous makeshift structures in hazardous environments, rented apartments within the urban or peri-urban areas, particularly in informal settlements where state capacity to deliver services and infrastructure is weak, while others live with host families. It is acknowledged that there has been a lack of capacity and limited engagement from the humanitarian community to adequately address the out-of-camp affected populations. Apart from suffering the effects of the insurgency, the displaced population and the host community are both exposed to serious risks including natural disaster, communicable diseases, food insecurity and marginalization.
The objective of this appeal is to improve access to information, protection and coordinated services while ensuring inclusive representation in host communities in MMC and Jere LGAs of Borno State using the Out-of-camp displacement approach. The project will adopt a consultative and participatory approach as consultation between the government at all levels, the CCCM/Shelter and NFI sector, as well as local authorities and community structures will be prioritized. The project will also take care to mainstream protection cutting across age, gender and diversities including people living with disabilities. The outcome of this project is to establish improved coordination of humanitarian assistance, Information provision and protection for the displaced people living in the informal settings and those scattered amongst the host communities through Mobile facilitation in project locations.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in the Nigerian North East has caused untold hardship to affected populations and led to the collapse of traditional support systems across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) leaving over 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance according to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Assessment. Of this population, 80% are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. In Borno alone, over 1.3 million people have been displaced out of which over 266,000 are in Maiduguri, the State capital and a huge others in Jere LGA. More than 80% of these displaced persons living in the informal settings and host communities have no access to coordinated multisectoral services and protection needs. The need to deploy cross-sectoral skills through the Out-of-Camp displacement approach to monitor services and map out gaps for increase advocacy and response in informal settings and host communities have become very necessary. The weakened social, economic and administrative infrastructure in affected communities need to be strengthened and reactivated in project locations through collaborations with government at different levels, local authorities, traditional leaders, service providers and affected population.

2 - Activities

The following major activities will be undertaken by the project: 1. Conduct rapid Need Assessment to identify key CCCM gaps in project locations.2. Undertake stakeholders' consultation and familiarization visits to share project objectives and outcomes for buy-in and participation.3. Constitute and deploy Mobile team to coordinate multisectoral assistance in informal sites and host communities, monitor service provision against standards and report gaps.4. Set up and maintain information management structure/facilities in project communities to enhance evidence-based decision-making in humanitarian response. this structure will support collection of information on host communities and the local authorities, monitoring of existing services and data analysis to determine the protection and assistance gaps for advocacy and referrals. Efficient and acceptable complaints desks and feedback boxes including hotlines will be provided for effective management of perceptions of all stakeholders about service provision in project locations.5. The project will conduct trainings for affected population, IDPs and host community leaders on CCCM related topics like community participation, roles and responsibilities, complaints and field backs, coordination among others.6. The project will carry out risk communication using IEC materials like posters and hand bills, banners and audio materials including placing mobile speakers in informal sites and host communities for sensitization on risks, prevention and referrals.7. Sanitation/Hygiene promotion-IDPs and community members will be mobilized to carry out routine sanitation and hygiene promotion in project areas, particularly waste management systems will be strengthen. 8. The project will carry out advocacies to government at all levels and service providers on behalf of affected populations based on complaints received.
