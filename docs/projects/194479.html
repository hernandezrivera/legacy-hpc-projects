Project Summary

In response to the growing humanitarian crisis in Lakes State. WAV Gender Based Violence project plan will directly target 10000 vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties with a total of 5000 females, 1000 males,1000 boys and 3000 girls 5% of the target population will include people with disability, female headed households, pregnant and lactating women and the elderly .The project aims at providing quality GBV services to GBV survivors and reduce suffering of women, girls, men and boys by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs and promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks. WAV proposes to implement comprehensive, lifesaving SGBV prevention and response activities by providing quality and timely, age appropriate, case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors.
The prioritized objectives to achieve the overall goal will include:
Cluster Objective 1:Reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability-challenged persons through the provision of multi-sectoral specialized GBV response services
Cluster Objective 2: Vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys including ability-challenged persons have improved access to the provision of GBV prevention and risk mitigation interventions. Reduce the suffering of GBV survivors including women, men, girls, boys and ability-challenged persons through the provision of multi-sectoral specialized GBV response services
Cluster Objective 3: Prevent and mitigate GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience
This projects is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV and enhance the ability of
survivors to recover from GBV incidences. The main activities to be carried out to realize these objectives and achieve the overall goal will include :
1. Provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery
2. Provision of appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support
3. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support
4.Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment
5.Conduct dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation
6. Conduct community awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle

1 - Needs

Lakes State has experienced a great deal of conflict and flooding in recent years that has caused degenerated and rampant instability. The current projected population of Lakes State is estimated at 1,310,142 with Cueibet and Rumbek Centre Counties having an estimated population of between 360000. In August 25-28th 2022 an IRNA was conducted by the Rumbek ICCG in collaboration with the Lakes State RRC Office for verification assessment in the affected locations with a total of 7 clusters represented with a focus on the 3 payams(Pagor,Citcok and Duony) of Cueibet and 2 payams(Matangai and Amongpiny) of Rumbek Centre counties of which 12460 (men 3492,women 6230,boys 1246 and Girls 1492)people had been displaced in Cueibet and 9885 (men 2977,women 4943,boys 989 and girls 977) in Rumbek Centre this was both direct and indirect beneficiaries. Furthermore Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties have been subjected to numerous displacements with IDPs being integrated into the host community .The displacements are caused by floods ,conflicts of inter and intra-communal violence ,revenge killing, farmers pastoralists conflicts, land disputes, clan fighting and many more making women, children, elderly and PSNs victims of circumstances. The immediate impacts of flooding included damage to property, several houses and latrines have collapsed in the villages posing danger of open defecation in bushes which increases risk of GBV to women and girls, many people are displaced from their homes and have moved to higher grounds and are sleeping outside exposing them to GBV risks . Damage of bore holes has caused congestion at the few functioning bore holes which are quite far exposing women and girls to sexual gender based violence . The 2 counties of Rumbek Centre has WAV as the GBV service provider however Cueibet lacks GBV service providers and trained health service providers there is also unavailability of PEP kit in the health facilities in all the locations visited however due to lack of awareness on GBV services women and girls were reluctant to open up on GBV issues due to fear of criticism from community members and families for discussing such issues . Sexual violence and other forms of GBV against women and girls are becoming more wide spread in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties due to movement of the populations because of flooding and conflicts. Through WAV GBV programming and peace building and governance projects in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet WAV has noticed increasing and targeted attack with existing and growing levels of sexual violence perpetrated. In Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties adolescent girls and women are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information and experience growing levels of sexual violence, abductions ,sexual harassment ,early and forced marriages, sexual exploitation and abuse and also other forms of sexual violence being perpetrated in the Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties . In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier in accessing the available services by survivors. Across the two counties the GBV situation has further been exacerbated by the insecurity situation, flooding and COVID-pandemic. Through this plan at least 70% targeted beneficiaries will be supported through PSS and life skills. Responding to the new crisis requires a targeted emergency response outside the current WAV program sites with a total of 10000 beneficiaries targeted for this intervention with 5% being people with disability .WAV will utilize existing internal capacity to respond quickly and effectively to the emerging crisis and will provide emergency GBV services in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Prior to this application participatory assessment through focused group discussion (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) was conducted in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties with 30 participants involved drawn from different ages ,gender and diversities to ensure there is commitment in placing accountability to people affected by disaster and conflict at the core of its emergency policy and practice, from preparedness and the onset of an emergency throughout the phases of the programme cycle. The current GBV concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups (women headed HHS ,people living with disability, elderly women, pregnant and lactating women) patterns and and GBV risk factors have been identified through beneficiary feedback mechanism. WAV will continue to ensure that this project utilizes a people-centered approach, sensitive to the dignity of all human beings, the varying needs of different segments within the community, and the importance of ensuring that women, men, girls and boys can equally access and benefit from GBV services provided. During the selection of the beneficiaries there will be community consultative meetings to identify the vulnerable beneficiaries to be considered for support with GBV services. WAV will follow participatory assessments to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. The selection criteria will prioritize vulnerable people, including people with disability ,IDPs, host communities and female headed HHs. In addition, consultations with women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs, and confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WAV feedback mechanism will be used for this project to guide in service provision to the beneficiaries .A complaint mechanism desk will be set up for information collection at the women and girls friendly space where different opinions will be collected and solutions generated . Communities will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication will be encouraged to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Addressing sexual exploitation and abuse in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet Counties requires robust measures to promote and protect women’s and girl’s rights, response mechanisms when those rights are breached, the services and programs to meet the needs of women and girls who have suffered violence are in place. WAV will ensure the component on PSEA is part of the Meetings, awareness raising sessions and training activities to ensure the information is disseminated to the community also at the women and girls friendly spaces they will be IEC materials on PSEA translated in local language so that women and girls can be able to read and understand.WAV also has a PSEA policy and the staffs have been trained this will be utilized to manage the conduct of WAV staff when interacting with beneficiaries in the field.WAV has a reporting mechanisms where these cases are reported to the human resources for action who in turns shares the information with the executive director for disciplinary action.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project life cycle will utilize WAV beneficiary feedback mechanism where the project will commence with the inception where participatory assessment will be conducted in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet Counties to assess the current GBV concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups patterns and the risk factors for violence and abuse while feedback on the process will be collected from the beneficiaries. WAV will strengthen the existing community-based protection structures and women groups in Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties to support activity implementation through trainings to enhance long term sustainability and to ensure that feedback from the beneficiaries is collected to inform programming and to ensure the affected population benefits. WAV will utilize its feedback and complaint mechanisms through strengthening the existing GBV referral pathway mechanisms survivor centered approaches, GBV basic concepts and the key principles in dealing with the GBV survivor will be engraved within the case management interventions. Regular consultations will be provided within the women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) with women and girls meeting quarterly to discuss key interventions implemented within the period, achievements, challenges, recommendations ,gaps and opportunities within the given intervention will be discussed to ensure responses are informed by their voices, recommendations and experiences will be captured in existing beneficiary feedback tools on key programming interventions to ensure quality service provision is adapted for this project. WAV will continue to work closely with other partners to harmonize this tools and strengthen responses to ensure integrated programming. The key stakeholders will inform programming and contribute significantly to the responses and adhere to “DO NO HARM”.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The 10 risks will be mitigated through developing projects that address SGBV the activities at the women and girls friendly space will be skill building to enable women to be able to earn a living and at the same time support psycho social support interventions .Community awareness sessions will be promoted to ensure that the beneficiaries are sensitized on the various services provided at the women and girls spaces and also other key partners providing GBV services on the referral pathway. The project will involve the marginalized population such as people living with disability ,the elderly ,youth and women in participation and representation .The information on GBV survivors shall be kept confidential and only accessible to only a few people responsible for keeping the data such as the case worker. The project will also ensure that security protocols are in place when conducting activities bringing beneficiaries together. The project will ensure there is proper awareness creation with beneficiaries aware of their project entitlements and project staff trained on how to recognize PSEA and how to handle it using confidential means. The project will utilize technology to ensure that beneficiary data is protected and project staff will have limits of authorization with passwords provided. Risk of interference from external stakeholders: The project will utilize a highly transparent approach of beneficiary selection that will involve the local authorities, traditional structures and community structures to ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are selected asbeneficiaries

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The 2 Lakes state counties of Cueibet and Rumbek Centre have been prone to floods between the months of August, October and November this project will ensure we engage other stakeholders in the private sectors and government to come up with long term interventions such as construction of dykes and also strengthen the early warning systems to ensure we reduce the affected population by the floods to 25%.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project will contribute to outcome 2 where GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response will be rolled out in the 3 payams of Cueibet and 2 payams of Rumbek Centre where the case workers will be trained on comprehensive case management to ensure they provide 1st Aid PSS at the women and girls friendly spaces. Through the existing community protection committees and the case workers WAV will ensure this 2 structures conduct awareness raising on available GBV services and dissemination of referral pathway to ensure there is effective referrals.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The current project has prioritized Rumbek Centre and Cueibet counties which are currently in severity classification 4 during the implementation of this project. WAV will ensure the number of people in IPC 4 reduces by ensuring there is establishment of women and girls friendly spaces where there will be recreation and skill building activities with a component of individual 1st Aid PSS. There will also be awareness raising sessions that will be conducted door to door and through radio talk shows to ensure information on GBV is disseminated to the rest of the community members who can access this services at the women and girls friendly space this will encourage more GBV survivors to come out for assistance. The referral pathway will be developed and this will encourage more actors to be mapped and identify areas with gaps in service provision.
