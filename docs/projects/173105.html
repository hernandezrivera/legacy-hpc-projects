1 - Needs

Through this project Mercy Corps intends to build resilience of affected populations (refugees, IDPs, and host communities) by providing basic services through provision of agricultural inputs and training to farmers, WASH/Nutrition interventions, and multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA).

2 - Activities/Outputs

-Food security and livelihoods: Provision of agricultural seeds and tools; Mercy Corps will provide improved agricultural seeds and farming tools to target farmers and, through a Memorandum of Understanding with the Ministry of Agriculture, will provide agricultural extension trainings.Establishment of VSLA groups: Mercy corps will mobilize women in to Village savings and loan associations (VSLA) and provide training on basic business skill and also provide start up grants to enable women to establish micro business in their communities -Nutrition; Mercy Crops intends to implement both CMAM (community management of acute malnutrition) and IYCF-E (infant young child feeding in emergency) programs. With CMAM, Mercy Corps will establish OTPs (out-patient therapeutic programs) for treatment of children under 5 with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and will target MAM (moderate acute malnutrition) through the provision of BSFP (blanket supplementary feeding program) for both children and PLW (pregnant and lactating women). With IYCF-E, Mercy Corps will work with community women to form mother-to-mother support groups in order to promote exclusive and supplementary breastfeeding. Mercy Corps will also assist the husbands club to enhance men's awareness and participation in IYCF-E activities.-WASH; Mercy Corps will assess and rehabilitate water facilities in target communities and promote community sanitation among community members through CLTS (community-led total sanitation). Mercy Corps will also establish and train hygiene promoters in order to carry out hygiene promotion campaigns in target communities.-Multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA); Mercy Corps will provide MPCA to most vulnerable populations in target communities which will cover the costs of basic household needs and food. To determine the amount per household, Mercy Corps will use the minimum food basket recommended by the cash working group and will carry out market assessment to determine the cost for basic household items.-Health: The project will aim to support the SMoH to increase the availability of,and access to, the life-saving primary health care services the public health sector’s healthcare centers. Mercy Corps, in collaboration with the SMoH, will support public sector’s healthcare providers in delivering a standard minimum package of primary health services, including outdoor management of common communicable and non-communicable diseases, maternal and child health, reproductive health including the management of STIs, clinical management of gender-based violence, and immunization. All activities will be carried-out in line with COVID-19 specific guidance provided by SMoH and WHO. We will coordinate with cluster leads, such as SMoH, WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA, WFP) for sustainable provision of essential medicines and supplies to ensure their timely availability and avoid inadvertent stock-outs. Interventions will be supported by awareness-raising activities and promotion of healthy behaviors within the target communities. Mercy Corps will support the local referral system by providing PHCCs with transportation options and will help to strengthen the existing surveillance system (DHIS2) and disease outbreak control efforts by providing communication equipment (mobile phones with air time and tablets) and supplies where required. These efforts will build the capacity of health facilities to assist populations affected by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks.

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mercy Corps’ work incorporates gender into all programs, as well as developing an analytical and “Do No Harm” perspective to ensure the safety and protection of women and girls in our work. In addition to Mercy Corps’ Gender Minimum Standards, the team will take into consideration USAID’s gender guidance and 2012 USAID Gender Equality and Female Empowerment Policy in the proposed program. Our protection approach takes into account and includes designing activities to actively minimize risks and consider conflict dynamics. Flooding also presents an opportunity to better understand and help protect women from the additional burdens brought on by the effect of flooding and build their capacities to be more resilient to future shocks. As such, Mercy Corps will take a gender-intentional approach ensuring we don’t miss women, girls and intersecting identities that are most at risk of being excluded from global, national and local assistance efforts.In terms of provision of multipurpose cash, Mercy Corps will ensure that thorough security assessments will be carried out to understand the security risks and put in place mitigation measures during program design. In terms of implementation WASH activities, Mercy Corps will ensure that women are consulted in establishing locations for WASH facilities. Specifically for toilets, we will ensure that gender segregation is included in the design and that solar lighting will be available at all water points to mitigate gender-based related violence at water points during the night. With MPCA, we will deliberately target female head of households to mitigate the tendency of adapting bad coping mechanisms.

7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.
7.1.a Please specify other means to collect/share feedback or information if there is any

CARMs are an essential part of Mercy Corps’ commitment to excellence and integrity. Mercy Corps is committed to preventing all forms of exploitation and abuse. Accountability is at the core of this program design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARM (Community Accountability Responsiveness Mechanism) will be established with community members to ensure the feedback and reporting mechanisms are tailored and context appropriate. The mechanisms will be developed following community consultations and will be adapted to the needs of different groups within a community insofar as possible and give consideration to how different groups, such as men and women, pastoralists and farmers, etc., access CARM. Examples of reporting and feedback mechanisms include hotline phone numbers, suggestion boxes or household satisfaction questionnaires. Specific resolutions will be dependent on the nature and severity of the complaint or feedback.Mercy Corps has a global policy against sexual exploitation and abuse, and its standards apply to all staff and partners. Mercy Corps has a policy for zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff and partners are expected to adhere to. Staff and partners regularly participate in awareness sessions on their responsibilities towards ensuring safe and dignified programs for participants. Program participants are also made aware of how they can report any incidents.
