Project Summary

ACTED will provide comprehensive WASH assistance to highly vulnerable populations residing in camp (Salamiyah 1 and 2 camp) and out of camp (Ba’aj, Sinjar, Telafar, Mosul) in Ninewa Governorate, in Iraq. The proposed interventions will target a total of 7930 IDPs As Salamiyah 1 and 20035 IDPs in As Salamiyah 2 camp, aiming at facilitating safe access to emergency water and sanitation services and hygiene practices. ACTED plans to provide emergency water supply through water trucking and/or the establishment, rehabilitation and maintenance of water networks, as well as sanitation support through maintenance and upgrading of sanitation facilities and services, including waste management and desludging.
Hygiene promotion activities will address a range of essential topics, including but not limited to: hand-washing, safe use of water, safe defecation practices, water conversation, waste management, and basic health to promote a healthy environment and prevention of disease outbreaks.
ACTED furthermore plans to target an estimated 40,000 IDP’s, returnees and host community members in Ba’aj, Sinjar, Telafar and Mosul district with WASH interventions aiming at improving access to WASH services and hygiene practices, in addition to rehabilitating and maintaining community WASH infrastructures.
In order to support a timely response in case of evolving WASH needs, ACTED further plans to establish contingency sanitation mobile capacities, focusing on providing as hoc sanitation support to WASH partners in IDP camps in Ninewa and Dohuk governorate
Additionally, with the establishment of a Sludge Treatment Plant in Dohuk governorate ACTED plans to mitigate the negative impacts of poor human waste disposal practices in the governorate.

Needs

An estimated 2.3 million people across Iraq remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to safe and appropriate WASH services. Of these around 500,000 people residing in camps continue to require some level of specialized WASH support. Sanitation coverage in IDP camps is still below minimum standards, where more than 20 people still share one latrine and about 230,000 IDPs are receiving less than 35 litres water per day. The poor water and sanitation situation in the camps is exacerbated by lack of proper wastewater treatment facilities. (IHF 2nd Standard Allocation Strategy, 2018)Salamiyah camp 1 and 2 in Ninewa Governorate were built in response to mass displacement from Mosul and were opened in May and June 2017. Considering the large percentage of camp residents who have stated their intention not to return to their area of origin, ongoing interventions are required to continue the provision of adequate services to meet the daily water, sanitation and hygiene needs of the camp residents. Although the camps are now well-established, WASH needs remain as such: continuous provision of key hygiene items for residents of the camps, continuous maintenance and upgrading of WASH infrastructure (including latrines, shower facilities, tap stands, drainage); the maintenance and follow up of water point in camps including the daily water quality; the collection of solid waste; and the desludging of the latrines. Salamiyah 1 receives water through a pipeline from the Salamiyah water treatment plant (WTP), while Salamiyah 2 continues to rely on daily water trucking from the Salamiyah WTP. WASH needs are particularly severe in Ninewa governorate. The ‘Iraq – 2018 HNO: Severity Mapping’ of the WASH Cluster highlighted that Mosul, Sinjar and Telafar all have a low level of access to WASH services. In an assessment conducted by ACTED in Mosul, Sinjar and Ba’aj districts in February 2018, water was highlighted amongst the top priority needs by 30% of respondents.Damages and theft in equipment during the conflict to WASH infrastructure were largely suffered throughout Mosul, Telafar and Sinjar districts. Additionally, observed needs by ACTED’s program team in the context of current WASH programming in Ninewa governorate include low level of education with regards to hygiene, especially in rural areas. To respond to the prevalent WASH needs of conflict affected population groups in Iraq, and in line with the WASH cluster’s 2019 HRP strategy, ACTED will focus on improving and sustaining quality of WASH services for targeted population groups. For IDPs remaining in existing camps, standardized WASH support will include upgrade, operation and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities, and enhancing sound hygiene practices. ACTED will continue to restore basic water and sanitation services and support good hygiene practices to mitigate public health risks in areas of return. In order to be able to respond to arising WASH needs quickly, ACTED plans to provide a contingency sanitation mobile capacity that includes mobile WASH staff to be quickly available for any intervention in Dohuk and Ninewa Governorate

Activities

As part of its 2019 WASH strategy, ACTED will contribute to the following objectives of the WASH cluster: ACTED is the current WASH actor in Salamiyah 1 and plans to continue its activities in camp and expand into Salamiyah 2. This strategy will be cost effective and ensure a standardized level of service provision across the 2 camps. The following activities are planned under the cluster’s objective 1: Activity 1: Rehabilitating, expanding, managing and sustaining water supply systems and services including various aspects of water quality monitoring in Salamiyah camp 1 & 2 (S1,S2)For S1, this includes the maintenance of the pipeline that connects Salamiyah 1 camp to Salamiyah Water Treatment Plant (WTP), and ensuring the provision of treated and disinfected water for residents of the camp: communal tanks and tap stands) and communal centres. Daily monitoring will be performed at the main supply pipe, communal tank, taps and HH level, for all the parameters.For S2 in addition to the above, activities will include the maintenance/follow up of all water points, incl. monitoring of the water trucking done by UNICEF, as S2 is currently still relying on trucking until the extension of a pipeline from the WTP is completed. Activity 2: Upgrading, expanding, managing and sustaining sanitation systems and services in Salamiyah camps 1 &2 ACTED will focus on: Latrine desludging, maintenance of emergency WASH facilities, community information sessions and tent-to-tent campaigns on hygiene and health, solid waste management through daily labours, Ad-Hoc maintenance for the sanitation facilities Activity 3: Distributing hygiene top up relief items to IDPs in Salamiyah camps 1 & 2Following initial kits distributions in 2017, ACTED plans to provide top up Hygiene items, containing detergent, sanitary pads, shampoo, soap and toothpaste (in line with the relevant cluster guidelines) every 2 months. ACTED plans to liaise with the WASH cluster to identify the continuous need and frequency of this activity over the next year. Families with small children will also receive baby kits. ACTED will to promote good health and hygiene practices through key hygiene messages during distributions. Based on best practices in Salamiyah 1, ACTED plans to expand this activity into Salamiyah 2. Activity 4: Strengthen technical capacities of WASH actors, formed WASH committees and that of local government WASH line authorities in Salamiyah camp 1 & 2 In 2017, ACTED established 1 WASH committee in Salamiyah 1 camp, consisting of community members who discussed, provided feedback and coordinated with the community and camp actors on WASH gaps and needs. In close collaboration with camp management, ACTED will continue to support this committee to guide in-camp WASH activities. Based on best practices in Salamiyah 1, ACTED plans to expand this activity into Salamiyah 2. Under the consideration of evolving needs, ACTED plans to provide contingency capacities for Dohuk and Ninewa Governorate. ACTED plans to establish mobile WASH teams from its bases to support in-camp interventions incl. urgent desludging, and ad-hoc maintenance of WASH facilities. In addition, teams would also be able to support capacity building of WASH actors in camp. Under objective 2, ACTED is planning out of camp WASH assistance in the form of: Activity 1: Rehabilitation of community WASH infrastructures in Ba’aj, Sinjar, Telafar and Mosul districts of Ninewa Governorate ACTED will target at least 12 large scale (between $50,000 - $150,000) and 15 small scale community WASH facilities (<$15,000) with rehabilitation work to restore damaged WASH network infrastructure in Ninewa Governorate. Activity 2: Provide hygiene promotion to vulnerable individuals in targeted districts in Ninewa governorateAwareness raising sessions on hand-washing, safe use of water, safe defecation practices, water conversation, waste management, and basic health and disease prevention will be delivered at community and HH level through trained ACTED community hygiene promoters. Under objective 4 ACTED is planning out of camp assistance in the form of: Activity 1: Rehabilitation of Salamiyah WTP The Salamiyah WTP currently provides water to both the Salamiyah 1&2 camps, and to the surrounding communities. In 2018 ACTED provided support in upgrading pumps and now plans to rehabilitate the sedimentation tank. ACTED also plans to provide spare parts to support the DoW to sustain the quality of the treatment. ACTED intends to complement the activity with training.

Contamination with Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and/or Unexploded Ordnance (UXO). Risk 4. Target population hostile towards INGOs. Risk 5: Shipment of humanitarian assistance hindered by military forces or government authorities. Risk 6: IS resorts to use of chemical weapons.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation 1: ACTED will maintain close coordination and communication with relevant security forces and community leaders (Mukhtars) in the project areas and will continuously monitor security situation. Mitigation 2: ACTED already has access permissions for some of the project areas and maintain coordination with relevant governmental offices (BRHA, Asayish) ensuring that necessary permission are obtained. Shall the access permissions be withdrawn or refused, partners might consider distribution of assistance through relevant traditional community structures, taking into account feasibility. Mitigation 3: ACTED will ensure that all staff are trained on Mine Risk Education and will closely follow on reports of ERW/UXO incidents to get better understanding which areas are mostly contaminated. Same knowledge will be obtained from host communities who usually have good understanding which areas are safe and which are not. Mitigation 4: ACTED will ensure that their operations are culturally sensitive and will involve traditional leaders to ensure that role of INGO is well understood and accepted by the community. Mitigation 5: ACTED will pro-actively follow necessary procedures and processes and maintain close coordination with relevant stakeholders - Asayish, Clusters, military actors - to ensure that necessary permissions are obtained on time. Mitigation 6: Staff will be trained on Chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear (CBRNE) defense, provision of protective equipment to field staff will be considered.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Ensuring accountability to affected populations is at the core of ACTED’s programming. ACTED’s approach is aligned with the IASC’s Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations: (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. ACTED's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict. In order to uphold transparency and avoid increasing social tensions in communities where distributions will occur, the criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be communicated in advance, publicly, and ACTED will liaise with local authorities to minimize security concerns. Adopting widely accepted vulnerability criteria used by other international humanitarian actors will also reduce the potential for conflict arising from the use of different methodologies for beneficiaries’ selection. As part of its on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED has established a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) covering each project for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive a response in a timely manner. The CRM also contributes to identifying potential negative effects of activities in order to address them in a timely manner and improve ongoing and future projects. In addition to the referral of non-sensitive complaints (sensitivity levels 1 to 3) to the respective project management team, anonymised complaints reports are shared internally on a monthly basis to provide an aggregate view of the types of complaints received and inform future planning. A range of communication modalities will be made available (as relevant) to targeted communities to ensure the mechanism is easily accessible, taking into account the specificities of the local context and culture, as well as project resources and beneficiary vulnerabilities and preferences. While the most commonly used modalities are complaints desks, a dedicated phone line and direct engagement with ACTED staff in the field (including a dedicated Accountability Officer), other potential modalities include complaints boxes, a dedicated email address and a WhatsApp number. Multiple communication means ensure that vulnerable people – especially those with limited mobility such as the elderly and people with disabilities – are able to provide feedback, while guaranteeing total confidentiality of information shared.ACTED ensures that project design is informed by previous experiences, including previous evaluations, and through qualitative data collected from targeted communities, prior to the project.
