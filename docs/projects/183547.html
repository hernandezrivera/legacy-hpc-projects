Project Summary

This project will be implemented in full partnership with the Ministry of Health (MOH). The project aims at providing integrated Sexual and Reproductive health and rights services (SRHR) to all those in need including the most vulnerable groups specifically; women and adolescents’ girls in area C of the Jordan Valley, through scaling up and strengthening the MOH emergency and safe motherhood centers in these areas. This will be achieved through the following main activities:
1. Build the capacity of MOH emergency staff at emergency centers and also at nearby secondary care services to be able to deal with emergency early pregnancies obstetric conditions, and imminent birth, and provide the basic life support techniques in a highly qualified manner.
2. Provide integrated package of SRHR and GBV services within two targeted centers in Area C of the Jordan valley including services for women, young girls, , and adolescents including people with disabilities.
3. Enhance Community outreach on essential SRH and COVID19 information, ie. danger signs in pregnancy, family planning, etc. COVID19 will be integrated into the information and community outreach in regards to how it impacts SRH. Also, information on when and how to reach services will be provided.
4. Support the two centers (Aljiftlik & Ein Albeda) with needed equipment and supplies to be able to provide emergency obstetric care per quality standards.

1 - Needs

Pregnant and lactating women, especially those living in remote and vulnerable areas such as H2 and Area C, face numerous hardships related to accessing essential and life-saving healthcare. These locations have limited options for primary health care, and where it does exist, emergency sexual and reproductive health care services are frequently not integrated. UNFPA therefore plans to support MOH to strengthen its emergency response in relation to SRHR. Specifically, the project will strengthen SRH emergency response capacity at two clinics in the Jordan Valley and Area C. it will also provide essential skilling to emergency staff at MOH to ensure they are able to deal with high risk pregnancy, emergency obstetric care, and life-threatening complications such as hemorrhaging. The project will procure equipment for such emergencies, and will ensure that women in local communities – and their husbands – have access to essential information about danger signs in pregnancy and when and where to seek services. There are an estimated 66,000 vulnerable women and girls in Area C in need of services, according to the 2022 HNO. Their specific vulnerabilities and service needs relate to high risk pregnancy, unmet need for family planning, and (for adolescent girls especially) psycho-social support needs and mental health interventions, as well as detection and referral of GBV.According to the latest MOH annual report 2020, the maternal mortality ratio has increased to 28.5 per 100,000 live births in comparison to 19.2 in 2019, In the West Bank, 9 of the reported maternal mortality cases were due to COVID-19 Infection. Additionally, and based on a preliminary analytical report on maternal deaths (2020), four women arrived dead to the hospital; deaths occurred at home and 20 deaths took place inside the hospital. This indicates an urgent need to ensure that pregnant women are able to access services much sooner – the “three-day delay” model suggests that one of the key reasons for maternal mortality is the failure – for reasons of ignorance, lack of access, lack of transportation, or other – to access services soon enough. Added to this is the concern that emergency obstetric care at hospitals is not always of adequate standard, and staff at emergency sections require additional training and orientation on life saving techniques and support.In general, the health sector in Palestine suffers from a general scarcity of resources in all terms, long protracted political complicated situation along with recurrent emergencies, added to the COVID-19 outbreak has also exacerbated the situation. For those most vulnerable population in Palestine mainly residents of area C, H2 and East Jerusalem, the sustainability and provision of services is a big challenge. Households in Area C who have experienced a medical emergency faced multi-layered challenges in accessing urgent treatment. While cost of treatment is cited as a predominant difficulty, in hamlet/encampments and communities completely located in Area C, high numbers additionally cite obstacles of distance, lack of accessible transportation, and the restriction of movement due to check-points, and the recurrent soldiers and settlers' attacks are also prominent barriers. (Household survey BZU, 2019). The sustainability and provision of SRHR services during emergencies is considered a major challenge since even during emergencies, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services are essential and must continue to be provided. The provision of SRH services, including maternal health care and gender-based violence (GBV) related services, are central to health, rights and well-being of women and girls. According to MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates in need of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services. UNFPA, through its intensive work with the Palestinian Ministry of Health, aims through this project to enhance the capacity of the emergency program in the Ministry of Health to enable it provide sexual and reproductive health services to women in need in remote areas, including emergency services for pregnancy, childbirth and newborn care and GBV needs. In addition to providing services for elderly women, adolescent girls and people with disability. Through this project, the focus will be on two emergency centers and the nearby hospitals such as, Tubas and Jericho where emergency staff will receive capacity building sessions on the “emergency obstetric care". Additionally, the two centers will be supported with the necessary devices and equipment to provide safe birth and maternity services and provide services at the highest level of quality. The targeted centers through this project are Aljftilik and Ein Albida located in Area C in the Jordan valley. Aljiftlik centre covers five main villages located in area C, with an estimated population of over than 15000 persons, and this center is around 30 minutes far from the city of Jericho, therefore, in cases of emergency deliveries, the center and its staff should be equipped to deal with these kind of emergency cases. Moreover, there is no services at both centers to identify the high risk pregnancies and provide the proper services. The General Directorate of Emergency at MOH conducted a mapping of all centers through field visits assessments, observations and interviews with the centers’ staff. At the two targeted centers through this proposal, a private space will be rehabilitated to be used by women and girls’ beneficiaries attending the two emergency centers. However, the two centers possess 2 ambulances which can transfer the patients to a nearby hospital if needed.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Upskilling for MOH emergency staff at emergency and safe motherhood centers on Minimum Initial Service Package for SRH (MISP), EmOb care and basic and advanced life support. secondary care emergency staff will also be targeted in these training and capacity building workshop. Activity 2: Support the two centers in area C Jordan valley with supplies including PPEs, devices and equipment (e.g: delivery beds, CTG, portable ultrasounds, delivery kits…) to enable the centers to provide a full package of SRHR services.Activity 3: Rehabilitation of a space within the two centers for safe and private consultations for all women, including pregnant and lactating, adolescent girls who might need information on menstrual hygiene or any other topic, also GBV services and psycho social support through these spaces could be provided based on individual case needs.Activity 4: Enhancement of the outreach community awareness program in those locations in close coordination with other organizations and active social networks in the area, to reach women, young people with key messages mainly on symptoms of high risk pregnancy, family planning message etc.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Due to the ongoing pandemic, it is crucial that the project integrates COVID-19 protection and prevention measures, such as PSEA, risk mitigation measures and adapts interventions. UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and to not cause any further negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services focusing on privacy, acceptance and person’s choices. Partners and other actors of the project will be trained and mentored to deliver non-judgmental, disability-sensitive and high-quality integrated PHC/SRH services ensuring confidentiality, privacy and respecting person’s choices about the care of those seeking help, including persons with disabilities. Safeguards are to be put in place in ensuring confidentiality to not be compromised when mobility is restricted. At the same time, the specialized personnel need to be supported in delivering services and upholding their right and dignity through being treated equitably and fairly. It is especially important to train on COVID-19 response guidelines both to protect the patient and the service provider.In relation to health facilities, safety and protection will be reached by identifying and securing surrounding areas minimizing threats and vulnerabilities, especially following and implementing guidelines on COVID-19 IPC. Reasonable accessible infrastructure adaptations will be made when needed e.g. ramps, railings, as well as information and communication means like braille sign language interpretation to partners’ health facilities in order to make services more inclusive for all.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project design takes into consideration needs based on gender, age and vulnerabilities as well as the disabilities since the need for SRH and accessibility to services are different. Primarily, the project is ensuring that selected communities receive SRH services to improve their quality of life. This project is designed to provide equal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach and accessibility by coordination with other operating actors in combination with continuously conducting needs assessments. It is of importance to highlight and promote women, girls, and young people’s rights to health care and how/where to obtain it by comprehensive awareness and education sessions e.g. utilizing existing referral systems.The project is based on the needs of women, girls, young people, but take into consideration possible differences in urgent needs, faced risks and faced traumatic experiences, which are gender, age and disability -specific, vulnerabilities and connected to location of residency. It will primarily target identified vulnerable women, girls, young people for crucial SRH including considering the arrangement of peoples’ access and availability to services including adapting services and information (developing a specific alternative communication strategy in order to ensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion). That is achieved by offering different alternatives in order to be able to widen the reach and met needs, through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) and home visits when applicable and enhance services and interventions through other channels such as phone and virtual platforms. This will allow barriers to be tackled that may restrict freedom of movement and/or people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all persons with disabilities who are in need for SRH services are considered, and those who cannot access services due to for instance restrictions related to COVID-19, no access to internet or technological device, socio-cultural norms and restrictions by tackling stigma and misconceptions at all levels. Mobile clinics and virtual platforms will especially have the role to enable services and information and community-based support interventions to reach people in more vulnerable communities.
