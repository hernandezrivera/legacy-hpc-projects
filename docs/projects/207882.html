Project Summary

"GFA: WFP will provide life-saving food assistance to the entire Rohingya refugee population through General Food Assistance. Beneficiaries will receive entitlements that can be redeemed from WFP-contracted local retailers across the camps. E-vouchers provide more flexibility, dignity and greater dietary diversity for the refugees while also creating market linkages with the host community and the wider Bangladeshi economy. To ensure an effective and efficient emergency intervention, WFP will preposition enough in-kind food to rapidly respond to the needs of approximately 30,000 households should they be displaced by monsoon, cyclone or conflict. In addition to providing large-scale food assistance, WFP makes sure to address the needs of the most vulnerable households through promoting income generating activities in the camps and host communities. WFP will provide complementary food vouchers to 250,000 extremely vulnerable individuals through Fresh Food Corners.
DRR: WFP will support Host Community – (Ukhiya & Teknaf) to ensure the Food security and improving the socio-economic service by enhancing to manage the natural resources as a part of climate change impacts (to mitigate the risk reduction). Through this project WFP will b cover 05 Union from Ukhiya and 06 Union from Teknaf ; Where targeted around total 45815 beneficiaries under age range from 18-59 years(targeting 25% women) ,Female headed household members , person with disability (5%).
To strengthening household socio-economic services the project modality will be Cash for work activities under natural resource management and disaster risk reduction such as roadside tree plantation, care & maintenance; watershed management (channel re-excavation; drainage system improvement etc.).
SR: Agriculture training and agri-inputs distribution to 30,000 unregistered Rohingya households, vegetables seed producers and vegetable traders in Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila.
Livelihood: Through multi-year livelihoods model, the project aims to improve vulnerable women’s food security and nutritional status in the Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar.
The programme provides a combined package of monthly subsistence allowance (MSA) with complementary skills development, delivered through Self-Helf Groups (SHGs). The programme works with women as an entry point to transform communities, through empowering them socially and economically and helping them become entrepreneurs. The programme also supports social cohesion and community integration for households who have been impacted directly or indirectly by the Rohingya influx, COVID-19 pandemic, and recent global food crisis.
•A monthly subsistence allowance of BDT 1,094 (approximately USD 12.50)
•Technical training to improve production techniques, bring down the cost of production, and increase profit margins
•Additional financial literacy training and negotiation training, social and behavioral change communication sessions, and modules on gender-based-violence (GBV), human rights, disaster risk reduction, hygiene, and health
•Activities related to market linkages, including aggregation centres for on-farm production and ESFN shop for off-farm production."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Five years after the influx, Rohingya refugees and surrounding host communities are more vulnerable than ever with limited livelihood opportunities and dependency on external assistance. The 2021 REVA assessment demonstrates that 95 percent of refugees were largely or entirely dependent on external assistance and while 52 percent of host community populations were vulnerable (REVA 5). 207,689 Rohingya households live in Teknaf and Ukhiya, in 2021 and 2023 WFP covered 60,000 Rohingya households and in 2024 WFP plans to cover 30,000 households under home gardening. The promotion of self-reliance and livelihood opportunities for Rohingya refugees and vulnerable host communities is therefore essential in addressing the economic repercussions of the pandemic. General Food Assistance (GFA) e-voucher outlets and distribution sites in camps. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"Overall, WFP is reviewing all costs to improve rationalization of its operations including consolidating outlets where possible, reviewing food costs and supply chain optimization.Blanket Food Assistance will cover the entire population of the Rohingya refugees with a single pipeline. Extremely vulnerable populations (approximately 250,000) will receive complementary food vouchers through Fresh Food Corners located in the same e-voucher outlets. Where possible, the outlets and the SCOPE/Blockchain system will be used by other agencies for soap and hygiene kit distribution.Under the livelihood programme, graduates will focus on business growth by: 1) linking to local supply chains for agricultural produce; and 2) connecting producers to markets for non-agricultural products. To ensure the businesses’ sustainability and profitability, market linkage initiatives are promoted. Farm production is channeled mainly through aggregation centres, while off-farm production is supported with an EFSN beneficiaries’ shop in Cox’s Bazar. WFP will work with FAO and other FSS partners to consolidate and reduce the number of aggregation centers. The aggregation centres link the Bangladeshi livelihood participants to local markets and WFP-contracted retailers of e-voucher outlets and Fresh Food Corners; playing a key role in enhancing social cohesion between both communities and increasing the localization of the WFP’s food supply chain. "

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WFP incorporates protection principles in designing and developing programme interventions in Cox’s Bazar to ensure that all activities target the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. Through robust contextual analysis WFP targeting crisis affected people including women, men, boys, girls, elderly people, person with disability as well as people with special need. WFP carried out various assessments and analysis, i.e., stakeholder analysis, gender analysis, vulnerability, protection and assessment of social and economic factor that create inequalities among Rohingya community and host communities. Selection for interventions and beneficiaries is informed by vulnerability monitoring and classifications, which are found in, REVA and FSOM, providing frequent updates on existing vulnerabilities and groups. These updates and assessments on vulnerable groups help programmes adjust their target groups while maintaining documentation on the impact of programmes on vulnerable populations. WFP has encountered protection referral and provide support through inter agency protection cluster and UNHCR to provide faster service to the affected population. WFP has followed PSEA guideline and inter agency referral pathway to report sexual exploitation and abuse, GBV and other forms of violence. Onsite protection monitoring, protection referral and follow-ups further complement protection assessments. However, continuous efforts are made to ensure that protection risk and concern is not overlooked throughout the programme implementation phases. Sectoral recommendations regarding protection are additionally adopted by WFP to enable a safe, secure, and dignified WFP service irrespective of sex, age, and diversity considerations.

3.3 - Age & Disability

WFP is committed to integrating protection strategies and actions into its programmes to reduce exposure to risk and to support a more protective environment where children can grow and learn.WFP has clear guideline and never engages child labor in its activities and strictly monitors the participation process in the implementation process. As a mitigation measure to deal with child safeguarding issues will provide related orientation sessions for its staff and cooperating partners along with sharing simplified awareness raising materials. Child protection focal points remains present during the distribution of entitlements to prevent possible safeguard-related issues. WFP provides additional top-up cash support to child headed households to meet their basic food and nutrition. Assistive and flexible nutrition services are provided to both parents with disabilities and children with disabilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP re-committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations (AAP) through adoption of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (C-AAP). WFP exercise active processes and actions that meaningfully place affected populations at the center of humanitarian response with the aim of providing assistance that meets people’s needs, elevates existing capacities and dignity, and helps strengthen long-term resilience and independence from humanitarian assistance. WFP’s priorities and approach to AAP is outlined in the Protection and Accountability Policy (2020), elaborated in the Community Engagement for Accountability Strategy (2021 – 2026) and mutually reinforces the Gender Policy (2022) and the Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020). WFP has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations. Protection assessments and the CFRM process protection issues in the community by collecting community feedback and responses in an organized manner. WFP will do capacity development training for partner to enhace their understanding on protection and AAP.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"WFP maninstream Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) through its programme, policy implementation and cooprating partner. WFP gave high priority to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by adopting the Secretary General Bulletin, incorporating PSEA core principles into agreements with retailers, cooperating partners, and vendors. This involved establishing safe and confidential reporting mechanisms, conducting awareness campaigns, and ensuring staff and partners adhered to these standards. WFP implemented a detailed Annual PSEA action plan and allocated a budget for PSEA capacity development, assessed cooperating partners' capacity using assessment tools, provided capacity development for staff and cooperating partners, emphasized ethical behaviour, incorporated protection principles into standard behavioural practices, and captured best practices for wider use in program operations to prevent abuse and exploitation. To ensure the voices of affected individuals were heard, WFP established confidential and accessible Community-based Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) for reporting concerns or incidents of abuse, reinforcing their commitment to taking appropriate actions in response. "

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project has DRR elements and environmental issues, and climate change mitigation measures. There are reforestation activities including land stabilization in the whole camp to mitigate the impact of the heat wave, landslides, siltation, and desertification. It will create a positive impact on forests and wildlife, ecosystems, and natural components (air, soil, and water) of the environment. Drainage cleaning activity will help to manage the healthy watershed.
