Project Summary

The major goal is to protect vulnerable people from disease outbreaks by quickly detecting and responding to Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreaks. This will largely focus on provinces that have previously been hit by climatic disasters such as cyclones and tropical storms, as well as an increase in the number of Acute Watery Diarrhoea and Cholera Outbreaks.
This will be accomplished through working jointly with SPS/DPS to effectively improve the current weak coordination and leadership for AWD and cholera response, guide in developmental and use of comprehensive multi-sectoral response plans at the provincial level .The project will focus on enhancement of community health surveillance to ensure timely detection and response to outbreaks.
This project also aims to promote the use of rapid test kits for suspected cholera cases, which are currently underutilized. In addition, work with the departments of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), health logistics, and Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) to put together rapid response teams from several sectors. This goal will be met by executing a capacity-building for health team aimed primarily at improving disease surveillance and laboratory pillars. Forums will be held to enable for the exchange of information on the most effective methods for enhancing disease surveillance and response, including use of laboratory protocols.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The objective of this project will be accomplished through collaboration with community health workers to engage with communities comprised of individuals with disabilities, girls, boys, women, men, adolescents, the elderly, child and female-headed households, and other vulnerable groups in order to promptly detect and respond to AWD. It is scheduled for implementation in both Zambezia, Niassa and Sofala provinces.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The priority activities will be conducted to cover the two provinces of Niassa, Zambezia and Sofala and will include collaboration with WASH, health logistics, and RCCE to assist in the operational deployment of integrated multisector fast response teams. These teams will be responsible for validating, investigating, and responding to reported cases of AWD cholera; Provide training to surveillance and response personnel at both the province and district levels, on the identification and reporting methods for cases, community-based case detection, sample collection and transportation, and emergency water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) operations; To orient community health workers to identify and report alarms within communities.; Guide the SPSDPS team in ensuring the systematic gathering, organization, examination, and distribution of case data on a daily basis, using regular epidemiological bulletins, situation reports, and other informational materials.; Guide the SPSDPS team in distributing the anonymized daily line-listing data from the field-level.; Evaluate, revise, distribute, and share protocols, case definitions, and other technical resources for the surveillance of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and cholera; Perform a needs assessment to evaluate the laboratory's capability, specifically focusing on identifying deficiencies in human resources and training requirements; Supply laboratory reagents and supplies for diagnosing the microorganisms responsible for Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and cholera; Allocate funding for the expenses associated with gathering and moving samples to central laboratories, following the established operating procedures; Provide capacity-building training to field staff on the utilization of RDTs and methods for sample collection; share best experiences related to the timely identification, prevention, and management of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreaks.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The protection hazards for all women and girls, boys, and men will be identified through each group's focused group discussion. All integrated/join mission assessments conducted by partners will include the AAP approach. WHO, through the health cluster, will collaborate closely with the protection cluster to better understand the security issues raised during their monthly field visits. The findings will be used to ensure that everyone participates in project implementation. All personnel deployed to all places will be educated on WHO's non-discriminatory policy and the humanitarian core principles.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project will be implemented in accordance with the UN fundamental principles of equity, openness, justice, and accountability, and will consider everyone's participation. Field officers who will carry out this project will be trained and sensitized on AAP and Gender-based Violence (GBV), and they will be guided to guarantee that no one is discriminated against while obtaining training or necessary health services throughout all project areas. To guarantee that all ages' requirements are met, the initiative will solicit input from all age groups through focus group discussions. The CCM findings will be used as a yardstick in the ongoing monitoring of all age groups' attitudes and perceptions. As a result, any gaps discovered will be filled. This project would prioritize the inclusion of women, adolescent girls, and children in the communities, in line with the shared commitments to being accountable to affected individuals (AAP) and protecting them from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). These projects will support the execution of the IASC GEEWG policy and its accountability structure, adhering to the instructions outlined in the IASC Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action to incorporate gender equality programming into humanitarian efforts. This will involve guaranteeing efficient, all-encompassing, modified, and open communication to facilitate well-informed decisions by impacted communities; employing feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and inform modifications in the response; and offering substantial opportunities for meaningful community engagement in humanitarian efforts. The existing platforms for community feedback mechanisms (CCM) will be utilized to solicit feedback on the implementation of the activity. The Linha Verde hotline, managed by WFP on behalf of the humanitarian community, will serve as an inter-agency platform for lodging complaints and providing feedback, even on sensitive matters. This initiative will request assistance from the Mozambique PSEA Network, which acts as the central organization responsible for coordinating and supervising efforts to integrate PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) practices into the work of humanitarian professionals. The network also offers technical support to the clusters, agencies, and partners involved in the response.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Responders will be taught on how to evaluate people with disabilities, categorize them based on age and gender, and record their information in the proper registries. Community-based APEs will focus on assessing people with disabilities for enrollment in targeted health interventions, including during the risk communication forums for AWD. People with disabilities will take part in the planned assessments

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The potential environmental consequences of the initiative encompass the production of injectable waste during the process of conducting outreach consultations and administering vaccinations for diseases such as measles, polio, and cholera. These activities are accompanied by well-defined mitigation measures or policies regarding waste management, which involve the utilization of safety boxes and incineration processes. This project's activities will be carried out in accordance with environmental regulations intended to protect against hazardous waste disposal. It will also follow established protocols mandated by government agencies as well as global health standards.
