Project Summary

A combination of conflict, insecurity, mass displacement, recurrent droughts, serious flooding and extreme poverty, coupled with poor basic health service coverage has seriously impacted to food security and livelihoods and greatly increased the population’s vulnerability to diseases and malnutrition. The worst affected are marginalized IDPs and vulnerable rural communities (men, women, boys and girls) as well as persons living with disabilities and the elderly in Afgoye and Wanlaweyne as well as Kahda and Daynile in Lower Shabelle and Banadir Region, Somalia. The situation is further exacerbated by limited capacity of trained health workers, limited and scattered population, long distances to health facilities, outdated socio-cultural practices which are not conducive with good health as well as limited interventions undertaken by a number of humanitarian agencies in the area owing to insecurity and corresponding access challenges.
According to UNFPA 2016 report, the maternal mortality in Somalia in is estimated 732/100,000 live births whereas Malnutrition has increased with GAM prevalence above 15% (FSNAU and FEWS NET Post Gu technical report of September 2021). This plight calls for need to strengthening surveillance and control systems, improving access to lifesaving health services such immunization, maternal health, undertaking public health measures, and increasing awareness and communication and education among communities are substantial elements in reducing mortality and morbidity of these diseases in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality and due disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict and improve physical and mental well-being of the conflict of the conflict affected displaced and non - displaced communities ( women, men boys and girls including those with disabilities) and ensure protection of by providing timely response are referral service to the victims of the GBV.
The Project design will enable for consultation with all community cross-section including the persons with disability to ensure their needs are covered in addition to integration with other relevant cluster partners particularly nutrition, WASH, education, protection and education. Further the project will ensure increased integration of mental health/psychosocial support Services (MHPSS )HCW and community trainings as well as training of HCW on trauma, MCM case management as well as increasing awareness through social mobilization, education and advocacy on disease control and Health and Nutrition.
The project targets a total of 25968 persons( 2036 mothers, 11712 WCBA, 5092 children under 5 and 1018 children under 1 years of age in Afgoye and Wanlaweyne as well as Kahda and Daynile in Lower Shabelle and Banadir Region, Somalia
