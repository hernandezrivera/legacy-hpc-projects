Project Summary

Concern will ensure that the Ministry of Health (MoH's) Basic Package of Health Services (BPHS) is provided to communities in North Bahr al Ghazal (Aweil North and Aweil West) and Unity State (Guit and Leer) that are not covered by other partners (Health Pooled Fund (HPF), UNICEF / WB). Concern will support the BHI in 10 Payams with 150 BHWs, 7 static health facilities, and 7 mobile sites. The mobile teams (including Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and EPI worker) will focus on providing services in hard-to-reach areas, which are generally remote areas that are more than 10km from fixed health facilities. In North Bahr al Ghazal, Concern will support 5 static health facilities and will continue to provide monthly incentives to SMoH staff, medical supplies, and capacity training to those seconded SMoH staff in Aweil North. In Unity, Concern will continue to support Chotyiel PHCC by paying incentives to MOH staff, provision of medical supplies, and capacity building of staff. Concern will also continue to support the BHI activities in 5 Payams (15 Bomas) in Leer county
The project will help to ensure that people in these communities have access to essential health services, including malaria, ARTIs, and diarrhoea treatment. It will also help to coordinate with other partners to ensure that Concern is targeting those locations where there are critical gaps in provision of basic services.

1. Needs

Amidst the high level of public health risks and malnutrition crisis, the health system in South Sudan is fragile and is still unable to cope and respond to emergencies and provide basic essential life-saving health services. As per WHO, the government funding for health is low at less than 2% of the national budget. The 2023-Humanitarinan Needs Overview (HNO) indicates that only 11% of the health facilities provide the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services (BPHNS), only 49% of the population can access health facilities in one hour’s time and 66% of the population has difficulty accessing health services, with additional barriers faced by women and girls, older people and people living with disabilities. Limited functionality of health facilities due to lack of budget allocation, limited local capacity, human resource deficit and lack of medical supplies has made it difficult to meet increasing health needs. According to WHO, the maternal mortality rate of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births and the under-five and infant mortality rates of between 99 and 64 deaths per 1,000 live births are among the highest in the world. Despite relative stability, Aweil North is among the worst affected due to welfare deprivation, high level of poverty and long history of chronic food and nutrition insecurity due to impact of climate shocks. The persistently high level of food insecurity is leading to high levels of child malnutrition and stunting. According to SMART Survey, SAM is 4.1% and stunting 22% in Aweil North. Women and girls are more vulnerable to malnutrition and poor health conditions due to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, early marriages, teenage pregnancy, burden of household activities and limited health seeking behaviors. The situation is further compounded by poor WASH conditions in remote communities. Aweil North is classified in Phase 4 (Critical) in the WASH Severity Classification (WSC), and use of latrine is reported to be low at 10%, which further exacerbates the public health risks. Access to health care services is extremely low and the recent reduction in Health Pool Fund (HPF) funding, has resulted in major gaps in health services provision and closure of many health facilities across remote communities in Aweil North. Unity has been greatly affected by Sudan war and flooding, Concern will continue to support the PHCC in Chotyiel and due to massive influx of returnees in Rubkona ( Rotriak), Concern intends to resume the support of Rotriak PHCC. Based on Concern’s assessment in the area, Guit and Rubkona counties have poor access to healthcare services is linked to multiple factors including conflicts, perennial flooding, and displacement. Conflict has led to continued breakdown of health facilities and resulted in interruptions to essential services, which remains one of the main drivers of morbidity and mortality in the county. Both counties are experiencing rising mortality and morbidity rates due to limited access to PHC services and weak capacity to deliver essential clinical and emergency lifesaving health services. While burden of diseases such as malaria, diarrheal diseases, and acute respiratory infections continues to grow, many health facilities lack essential medicines and capacity to deliver required services. Malaria is leading cause of death in children, with malaria cases currently averaging 6000 per month at health facilities. In addition, there is potential for outbreaks including cholera, malaria, and measles, which is exacerbated by limited capacity to respond to disease outbreaks. Moreover, the targeted counties are categorized as IPC phase 4 (emergency conditions), which is driven by multiple factors including limited access to basic services, insecurity, flooding as well as annual cereal deficit. As a result, malnutrition situation within targeted counties is of significant concern, making the integration of nutrition services alongside health services to be vital.

2. Project activities

The project will implement following activities in Aweil North, Guit and Rubkona to contribute to Health cluster objectives; CO1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations: In NBeG (Aweil West and Aweil North Counties) Concern will support 5 mobile clinics in remote locations and 5 statics health facilities as well as Boma Health initiatives with 150 Boma Health workers (BHWs). In Unity, Concern will continue to support Chotyiel PHCC. These health facilities will provide BPHNS as per South Sudan MoH guidelines. In all 12 static health facilities, Concern will second SMOH staff and pay their monthly incentives as per South Sudan MoH standardized rate. In all mobile sites of Concern and supported static Health facilities. Concern will provide surveillance reports and response to any suspected outbreak immediately. Concern will train and support EPI vaccinators in their supported health facilities to conduct outreaches in hard to reach areas so as many children are vaccinated as per immunization schedules, the vaccinators shall use vaccine carriers to collect vaccine from CHD cold chain in Aweil North, Guit and Rubkona. Concern will continue to support CHD in immunization campaign at all times. Clinical staff shall be trained on Clinical management of rape and they will be referring cases to the partners dealing with CMR and shall keep confidentiality across all three locations. CO2: Crises-affected women, men, girls, boys, people with disabilities and the elderly have safe access to quality gender-responsive and inclusive life-saving services. Concern will provide health services through 12 static health facilities (Aweil North: 05 and Unity: 07) and 7 mobile sites (Aweil West: 05, Guit: 02) in remote areas not covered by currently HPF/ MDTF partners and UNICEF. Both the static health facilities and Mobile clinic site will provide health services as per MoH’s BPHNS to communities not covered by HPF/ UNICEF. Concern will provide health services like outpatient treatment of common illnesses including malaria, ARTIs and diarrhoea in coordination with SMoH/ CHDs in Aweil North, Guit and Rubkona. Reproductive health will ensure attendance by pregnant mothers in remote communities at ANC, skilled birth attendant deliveries and attendance at PNC across all three locations. Community health activities will focus on improving behaviors that reduce risk of common diseases and will be linked with nutrition and WASH interventions. Concern’s health teams will respond to scale-up health responses that will integrate with its nutrition response in 12 health facilities in three counties. Although Concern does not implement specific services for survivors of SGBV and mental illness, Concern has a referral system in place and CRM to identify and refer such cases to other Health and Protection partners. CO3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms Concern will work towards health system strengthening including capacity building and collaboration with CHD in Aweil North, Guit and Rubkona. Concern will continue with regular community consultative meetings and ensure equitable distribution of limited resources like health facilities and mobile clinic sites. Complaint response mechanism will be in placed in order to provide timely feedback. Frequent and regular joint supportive supervision visits with CHD will be conducted on quarterly basis. On job, training will be provided to health workers during joint supportive supervision and action plan will be shared with health facility in charges for follow up. Concern will continue to hold quarterly stakeholders review meeting in order to review achievements, challenges and plans.
