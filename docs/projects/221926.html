Project Summary

South Sudan has continued to suffer from the effects of violence since 2013. The country has seen enormous shocks such as floods, displacements, loss of lives and property, economic collapse with deteriorating markets, increased insecurity, crime, and all forms of vices directed to gender. In 2024, it is estimated that over 2 million children are in need of education especially in rural hard-to-reach locations who are more marginalized than their urban counterparts. Children 3 – 17 years old are more vulnerable to the shocks that affect them and make it more difficult for the future of the country where some of the most important developmental milestones are deterred due to lack of education.
The education profile of the country sees several obstacles in its development such as lack of adequate number of qualified teachers, lack of payment of teachers, high attrition rates among teaching human resources, poor structures for education, lack of adequate equipment for education, lack of adequate learning and teaching aids/materials, High cost of delivering services which are worsened violence and displacement of people.
The Counties of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Pochalla, and Juba have been vulnerable to several shocks such as flooding and its destructive effects, violence, insecurity caused by cattle related insecurity, civil war, the influx of IDPs and refugees, and economic difficulties that have made developmental progress a difficulty.
In the interest of these, Aid Link Organization proposes to provide enhanced access to quality, safe and conducive education to vulnerable children of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Pochalla, and Juba counties through the implementation of activities that address the main objectives of the education cluster.
It is of paramount importance to ensure vulnerable children have access to education and are supported adequately even during emergencies and displacements.
Aid Link Organization (ALO) therefore seeks to address these with a budget of $462,570 for 12 months in 2025. These funds shall be used to reach 18,050 vulnerable children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) in targeted schools of Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Pochalla, and Juba Counties with quality, safe and conducive education services. Aid Link Organization has existing presence in the 4 counties (Akobo, Canal/Pigi, Pochalla, and Juba) where it is implementing life-saving humanitarian activities including Education.

1. Needs

South Sudan has continued to experience both manmade and natural disasters that have affected the general population in many ways. From 2013’s civil war that ravaged the country with many people losing lives, property, livelihoods, and depletion of resilience capacity. There were mass displacements caused by violence in most parts of the country, Floods affecting livelihoods and loss of property. South Sudan has been listed as a medium-intensity conflict zone at 160th position in the GPI. Many locations have traditional tribal and pastoralist disputes that cause displacements, loss of lives and livelihoods across the country. The effects of the conflicts have deepened the extreme poverty and hampered the private sector making it more difficult for citizens to survive due to very high market prices, lack of pasture for animals in pastoral communities, destruction of structures, and severe economic melt-down. In 2024, over 2 million live in IDP camps, this has been further worsened by the influx of refugees and returnees from Sudan since April 2023. Most of the returnees have been moved to their respective states without any major support for their livelihoods and a life-starting point. Due to the climate sensitive livelihoods of the population of South Sudan, the people suffer from risk of reduced access to water, sanitation, food insecurity, malnutrition, diseases due to climatic changes such as floods. There is limited access to basic social services such as health, education, sanitationOver 2 million children between 6 – 17 years and 1 million children between 3 – 5 years encounter significant obstacles in accessing education in South Sudan. Such obstacles include early marriage, GBV, long distance to the schools, insecurity, abductions, hunger, financial constraint, and parental denials to attend school. The country has scarcity of qualified teachers, worse school infrastructures, lack of learning and teaching materials, unfriendly environment for Children with Disabilities, and inadequate WASH facilities in schools especially in hard-to-reach areas. Jonglei state is greatly affected by floods, in addition to the insecurities caused by violence (inter-tribal, clan, revenge) that have led to mass displacements, destruction of infrastructure including schools, loss of property and lives. As one of the larges states that has semi-arid climate conditions, the state is affected by both draught and floods every year. In Central Equatoria, the state that host the capital of South Sudan (Juba) is not any better than other states. The County of Juba is a pastoral community with most of the rural areas being in hard-to-reach locations due to bad roads. Most schools outside Juba operate under trees, a distant away from homes, with difficult paths and insecurity along the paths to school. In Canal/Pigi many schools have been flooded, destroyed by floods, and are inaccessible due to swamps and insecurity. In Akobo, floods have affected access to schools and hampered security situations for children. In Pochalla, insecurity due to revenge killings, abduction of children, GBV especially for girls, and parental denial of education access for their children due to marriage and provision of security for their cattle. In Juba, the rural areas have access constraints and poor school structures which is made worse by poverty and lack of teachers in the rural Juba. Based on these difficulties, there is need to support the delivery of quality education to the vulnerable children in hard-to-reach areas of Jonglei and Central Equatoria States as proposed in this project.

2. Project activities

In reference to the needs identified for the 4 counties (Akobo, Pigi, Pochalla, and Juba), Aid Link Organization proposes to provide quality education services to the vulnerable children in hard-to-reach locations through the implementation of activities that align to the HNRP strategic objectives and Education Cluster objectives as detailed herein below. CLEDU/CO1Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need.Activities:1. Enroll 18,050 children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) in targeted schools in affected areas.2. Establish/rehabilitate 9 temporary learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum.3. Build/rehabilitate 5 gender sensitive WASH facilities in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard. CLEDU/CO2Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle.Activities; 1. Training of 171 teachers on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills.2. Distribution of learning materials to 16,768 Children and Youth (6-17 years old).3. Distribute ECD kits to 1733 Children (3-5 years old). CLEDU/CO3Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies responseIndicators: 1. Train 13 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities trained on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
