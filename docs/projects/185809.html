Project Summary

WFP School Feeding Programme aims to improve children’s learning by addressing the root causes of hunger and micronutrient deficiencies that can have consequences on their cognitive growth. In the camps, WFP will provide a packet of 50 g of High Energy Biscuits (HEB) to children enrolled in learning centres every school day. In the host community, WFP will provide HEBs to pre-primary and primary school children in Government schools, approved “madrassas” (Islamic schools) and NGO-run schools every school day. These locally-produced HEBs are fortified with 14 vitamins and minerals, and provide approximately half of the daily micronutrient requirements for a child between 5 and 11 years. WFP also implements literacy, health, WASH and nutrition activities and will conduct awareness-raising activities with parents, School Management Committees, teachers and children on health, hygiene, nutrition and other social issues.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox's Bazar, one of the most vulnerable and poverty prone areas of Bangladesh, has the lowest enrolment and completion rate in schools. People of this areas have very limited access to regular social and economic benefits. These everyday challenges, especially income deprivation, puts pressure on families to take their children out of school for financial reasons (WFP REVA III, April 2020). Furthermore, a significant number of Government primary schools do not have proper functioning WASH facilities and learning materials hindering the learning experiences of children. Children make up over half the Rohingya refugee camp population (UNHCR, September 2020). Significant improvements were made to the educational infrastructure in 2019 with more than 350,000 Rohingya children attending learning centres across 34 camps until March 2020. In late 2019, the continued provision of HEBs to children in schools in the host community and learning centers in the camps was reported to be a key pull-factor for school attendance and was shown to have a positive impact on children’s attendance and health outcomes (WFP REVA III, April 2020). With the outbreak of COVID-19, all schools and learning centres were closed in March 2020 forcing WFP to provide HEBs through door-to-door distributions in host communities and at General Food Assistance (GFA) e-voucher outlets and distribution sites in camps. While WFP ensured the continuation of nutritional intake, the impact on children’s learning is likely to be significant. As 14 percent of Rohingya refugee households with children previously in education, particularly those with high dependency, reported that they were not planning to send children back to learning centres, (ISCG, MSNA 2020) the need for continued encouragement to attend schools once reopened is needed more than ever. The WFP School Feeding Programme aims to: (i) meet nutritional gap among primary school children in the camps and host community; (ii) increase enrolment and attendance rate of students; (iii) improve knowledge about sanitation and hygiene practices among students in the host community; and (iv) strengthen the capacity and skills of host community teachers.

2 - Activities or outputs

- Distribution of fortified biscuits to all students every school day, otherwise through door to door distribution in the host community and through GFA outlets in camps considering COVID-19.- Distribution of cooked meal to students in Teknaf engaging community people while following health and safety measures.- Conduct awareness sessions to improve health, hygiene and nutrition and social issues for children, parents, teachers and SMCs in the host community.- Provide trainings to selected teachers and necessary learning resources to improve literacy and study environment in the host community schools.- Support the repairing and reconstructions of gender segregated WASH blocks in the host community schools. - Support the repairing of Hand Washing Stations in response to COVID-19 in the host community schools. - Dessiminate COVID-19 awareness messages to children, parents, teachers and SMCs in the host community schools.
