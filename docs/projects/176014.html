Project Summary

This response plan will be provided through emergency mobile lifesaving health care services that aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases, improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations in hard to reach areas while increasing access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders by ensuring that its activities are integrated with WASH, Nutrition, FSL and Protection including MHPSS and provision for the disabled and displaced into essential primary health care services to vulnerable populations of Morobo and Yirol West Counties of CES and Lakes State. Ebola Prevention messaging will also be disseminated across areas of operation owing to their proximity to Ebola hotspot areas and countries (DR Congo).
The response will entirely focus on both IDPs, and host communities owing to the high mortality rate of under 5 children (90.7/1000 - South Mortality statistics - 2016), maternal mortality rate (789/100,000 - South Sudan Mortality Statistics - 2016) people with disabilities and mental disorders will be included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies coupled with infrastructural impediments.
During this response, an estimate of 6,667 vulnerable individuals (IDPs and Host communities) accounting for 29% of the total target in the 2 counties (Morobo and Yirol West) are expected to be reached with emergency life saving mobile care services, in line with the health cluster target 2020. Precisely, 5,030 (2,465 men and 2,565 women) of Yirol West while 802 men and 835 women (1,637) of Morobo counties will form the target for this response. The response will attract four hundred thousands United States Dollars (400,000 USD) costed at 59.9 USD per beneficiary to run for the 12 months.
In this response, SSGID will ensure that provision of integrated life saving mobile emergency health care services and quality emergency curative, prevention and referral services through mobile clinical package is enhanced through training of frontline service providers on discharge of emergency mobile package and strengthening of referral system for rape survivors to have access to CMR and psychological first aid. SSGID will also ensure that epidemic-prone disease are detected on time, responded to and reported immediately.

1 - Needs

While humanitarian access continues to be more open and predictable in 2019 since the signing of the peace agreement, partners’ ability to provide services to vulnerable women and children in most parts of the country continues to be impeded by intercommunity clashes attributed to cattle raid and revenge limiting access to health services to an estimated 10,000 displaced and other vulnerable people in Otogo, Mugwo, Wudabi and Lujulo payams. (UNICEF South Sudan Humanitarian Situation August 2019) Heavy rains and poor road infrastructure have rendered other regions in accessible and contributed immensely to increased public health hazards including communicable diseases. Health services in South Sudan remain extremely constrained and, where functional, have focused on ad hoc services intermittently accessed by communities with women and children the worst affected. UNICEF health briefing report, notes that country ranks in the maternal mortality rates in the world (1150 deaths per 100,000 live births) Similarly, its mortality rate for children aged under five years is among the highest in the world at 99 per 1000 live births, which translates to 1 in 10 Children dying before his or her fifth birthday –16 times higher than the average ratio of 1 in 199 in high-income countries. Over 65 percent occur soon after birth or the first year of life. Most of these deaths, however, are preventable (UNICEF-South-Sudan-Health-Briefing-Note-Oct-2019.pdf) Notably, family healthcare practices and health seeking behaviors are poor, as reflected by late consultations, high levels of home deliveries and poor hygiene practices whereas social mobilization activities with comprehensive messaging are low and cannot sustain community demand for health services and appropriate care practices. The report denotes that a total of 3,386 suspected measles cases were reported, 159 confirmed IgM positive leading to 22 deaths, resulting in a case fatality rate of 0.6 per cent in eighteen counties. Similarly, women alongside children continue to suffer the greatest proportion of effects with high levels of acute food insecurity leading to malnutrition (SS IPC, August 2019). The IPC (2020) projections estimates 4.54 million people (39% of the population) likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) the response location are not spared from this trend. Poor sanitation, shortages of safe drinking water, low immunization rates, breakdown in health infrastructure and mass movements continues to present within the response locations as noted by SSGID’s assessment conducted in Morobo and Yirol west in September 2019, the report further highlights the shock and disappointment of returnees at the sight of broken bridges, broken boreholes, and primary health care centres destroyed and looted in Morobo. Long distances of 2-4 hours walk in in a bid of health services were recorded in Lojule and Payume payams of Morobo and Aluak luak and Mapordit payams of Yirol West Counties. In addition returnees in Morobo are faced with shelter challenges as their homestead were burnt. The report also asserts that proximity of Morobo to Congo poses great health risks and exposure to Ebola hence need for integrated mobile health response within the sited locations. SSGID currently implements SSHF - 2nd Round Standard Allocation - Health project with two mobile health facilities in Yirol west and Morobo counties which places it better to understand the local context. SSGID is a member of the consortium implementing the Scaling Up Nutrition (SUN) movement in the four States: CES, EES, Jonglei and Unity States. SSGID seeks your approval to enable it get funds to increase access to integrated emergency life saving health care services to IDPs/host communities of Morobo and Yirol west counties for this HRP 2021. SSGID will consult community and provide information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Youth groups and other stakeholders.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project is ready to prevent and address Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, with a PSEA communication strategy and appropriate complaints mechanism. SSGID will deploy three gender protection officers who will be responsible for gender mainstreaming during implementation. The gender officers have received training on PSEA and are well versed with GBV sub-cluster standard operation procedures while dealing with GBV cases. The GBV officers will be responsible for conducting GBV awareness and communication during project activities. They will also conduct training to project implementation committee and locally hired enumerators on PSEA mitigation, prevention, and reporting. SSGID is a member of GBV referral and will be able to refer any cases of PSEA to relevant referral pathway in order for survivors to access services. All new SSGID staffs, locally hired enumerators and project implementation committee members will receive training on organisation’s PSEA policy. They will then be required to sign the policy prior to be included as part of implementation team. SSGID PSEA policy clearly stipulates that the organisation has zero tolerance to PSEA. The policy is clear on steps to be taken if any staff or individuals working for and with SSGID is found to have contravened the policy.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SSGID will implement the project the project implementation committee. In each county, a PIC comprising of 14 members (7 females) will be formed and oriented on implementation modality, project monitoring and evaluation. The PIC will have four positions exclusively reserved for women, girls and individuals living in disability. This will ensure increased participation of females and disabled persons not only in decision making but also direct implementation of the project. SSGID will position feedback and complaint desks and suggestion boxes in locations that are safe and accessible to women, girls and persons living with disability. A male and female enumerator will be seconded in the project to receive complaints as well as provide feedback. Having both genders will encourage women and girls to make complaints since they will be dealing with individuals of the same gender. To take care of the needs of those who cannot read and write, a public announcement will be made on the feedback and complaint mechanism including on how beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can access the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SSGID is accountable to the women, men, boys and girls and places this responsibility at the core of its humanitarian policy. Before implementation, SSGID will conduct a needs assessment through focus holding group discussion with stakeholders at community level. In line with SSGID’s commitment to social accountability, which includes a complaints and feedback mechanism, In this health program, SSGID will form four (2) project committee, one in each County. These will be tasked to engage the beneficiaries about their comprehensive understanding of the project and how they perceive it in terms of meeting their expectations. Further, SSGID will hold quarterly stakeholders meetings in the Payams where mobile health services are being offered to enable community members communicate issues and concerns associated with the nutrition care services they are being provided, this will give them platform to provide feedback and monitor implementation of the nutrition response. The community will be routinely consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation through the community mobilization project officer. SSGID’s health project prioritizes highly vulnerable groups, including children and women, to improve their access to services. SSGID will ensure gender equality is at the core of its health program by collecting sex and age disaggregated data. At the community level, SSGID works to minimize the vulnerability of women to gender-based violence (GBV) by minimizing the need to travel to receive services through mobile teams. In addition to the quarterly stakeholders meetings and project committee, SSGID will form focus groups in the Payams where health services are being offered. These will hold discussions on quarterly basis to evaluate the progress of project in terms of reaching the intended beneficiaries. Issues and complaints raised in such fora will be taken seriously by the project management to address them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SSGID interventions are right based and informed by need to ensure dignity, privacy and comfort of people in need when accessing humanitarian assistance. As such SSGID will embed accessible, effective, and safe feedback and complaint mechanism throughout the implementation process. During registration and selection of beneficiaries, SSGID will avail feedback and complaint desk in every beneficiaries’ selection and distribution sites. SSGID will also provide lockable suggestion boxes where complaints can be submitted. Desks and suggestion desks will be manned by a trained enumerator who will ensure all complaints received are submitted to SSGID protection and Safeguarding officer, who in turn will feed the complaints to a complaint and feedback file for redress. Also, SSGID will provide and display toll free phone numbers which beneficiaries can use to submit their complains and receive feedback. All received complaints must be addressed within two weeks with feedback provided in safe and protective way to ensure security and privacy of complainants.
