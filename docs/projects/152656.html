Project Summary

Western Equatoria (WE) region experienced an increased number of Internally Displaced Populations (IDPs) across the region due to violence. 2019 HPC estimated 5078 IDPs living within the 83493 host community who had lost their belongings, assets. The conflict has reduced access to basic services (health, nutrition and WASH) and the humanitarian organizations experienced a number of impediments in accessing the vulnerable conflict-affected people (men, women boys and girls especially children under five years, in (Gira, Kokori, Dulo and Mayewe) of Mvolo County due to insecurity or bad roads. As a result many of the health facilities are not fully functional due to lack of essential medical supplies and shortage of health workers. A. Based on the above described IDP situation there remains a large gap in providing essential health, nutrition and WASH services. It is therefore against this background, that this proposed project is aimed to provide one mobile medical team servicesand provide support to two PHCUs to provide essential health services to 10,000 of 13570 IDPs and the host community in the thee locations in Mvolo County.

Needs

Since July 2017, Mvolo County experienced heightened cases of insecurity and it still hosts an estimated 5078 IDPs living within the 83493 host community across the county. The conflict has reduced access to basic services (health, nutrition and WASH). As a result many of the health facilities are not fully functional due to lack of essential medical supplies due to frequent stock outs and a number of health facilities were looted. In addition Mvolo County experiences shortage of health workers due to high turnover. The locations most affected are (Gira, Kokori, Dulo and Mayewe). Again there are poor infrastructures especially Kulu and Kombi, which need rehabilitations or additional tents, to provide MMT services. Hence the project will Provide complementary health and WASH services in two PHCUs and through a MMT. These would include treatment of common diseases (malaria, pneumonias, diarrheas) support to GVB surviors.and management of SAM and CMAM among programme beneficiaries and sanitation facilities will be incoporated in the PHCUs

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Beneficiaries will receive information through the weekly meetings. Women’s safety committees will ensure key project services messages and concerned are identified and reported and appropriate strategies are jointly developed to address any concerns.Quarterly review meetings will also provide opportunities for information sharing. Children will be reached through schools.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed to a specific beneficiaries context. For instance, at the women and girls’ friendly spaces women and girls can easily share reproductive health and GBV concerns, lessons and build social support systems that addresses individual issues. In the targeted community women and girls are rarely involved in decision making, to enhance participation separate meetings for women and girls will be conducted and encouraged when addressing the project activities. Men and boys will also be separately encouraged to take a lead in encourage the community to benefits from the services.Age and gender specific messages will be designed with beneficiaries on key project activities and deliverable e.g. safe referrals and available services.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

To reduce potential risks of SEA all staff will be trained on PSEA. Appointment of PSEA Focal Points across the project sites will be done who will also conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men of communities of concern will ensure they are aware of their rights and entitlements posters, videos, community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas and recommending protective actions to combat against SEA will be a key focus in all project cycle phases. All project staff will sign a code of conduct that will spell out what is expected of them as relates to SEA matters. An effective and anonymous complaints mechanism to allow and encourage individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely will be strengthened. All complaints should be immediately brought to the attention of the Program Director either directly by the beneficiary or by the person to whom such an incident is reported or through the PSEA focal points..

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

CMMB will also recruit a Complaints and Feedback officer who will be responsible for CFMs at all sites As protection measure, topics on protection will be included in health messages to the beneficiaries, their right to the services, their dignity and integrity. Beneficiaries will be advised to report any protection concerns to the help desk. Staff will ensured beneficiaries take limited time at the service delivery point to allow them travel back home. Nutrition sites are integrated within the health facilities which are safe for the beneficiaries. Other concerns such as water point, toilets are available within the facility for the beneficiaries segregated for male and female. CMMB also implements protection activities where TSFP/IFP services are integrated. Special consideration is given to vulnerable beneficiaries such as children, disabled and pregnant mothers. Staff worked closely with Project management committee to identify and support the most vulnerable group. To further support protection concerns, beneficiaries, are advised to report any protection issue through project management committees and feedback desk at the facility. For complain and feedback, CMMB has already established help desk in every facility. This is to allow beneficiaries can channel their complain or feedback and report any security/protection issues.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are several, often overlapping, areas in which community engagement is promoted. Among these in Mvolo are members of the Food Distribution Committee come from the targeted communities that support food distribution act as a channel for feedback which will improve project implementation. Community nutrition volunteers (CNVs), whose role in community screening and referral, and defaulter tracing is key to project success and Community-based Mother Support Groups will do individual and group counseling on MIYCN. Finally The project will work with other locally-based stakeholders such as HHPs, Boma health teams in tracing of beneficiaries and linking them for care.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

There are several specific opportunities for women to participate equally alongside men in the project. Firstly, all trainings will be provided for both males and females and both sexes will be given equal opportunity during the selection of CNV. Mother Support Groups (MSG) will by their nature and mandate will be all women. A Project Management Committee will include both men and women. However, women will be preferentially selected for leadership position such as deputy chairperson and secretary. This will empower women and ensure they have a decision-making voice in the nutritional status of their family and the wider community. CMMB will ensure that project staff respect and uphold the safety, dignity and integrity of women by orienting these staff on SEA, GBV and Protection issues. CMMB has set up complaint desks at each site in Mvolo where beneficiaries can submit gender-related concerns. Finally, CMMB’s HR policy ensures gender consideration in hiring in terms of equal opportunity.
