Project Summary

The deteriorating conditions of farmers and their heavy daily losses put them in the face
of serious issues threatening their food security, as the agriculture sector is suffering from
going through a very critical stage as a result of the limitations posed on this sector. The
main challenges facing the agriculture represented in high prices of inputs, its poor
quality, poor farm structures and the inability of farmers to rehabilitate and upgrade it, as
well as electricity deficit and the associated high cost and the high cost of alternative
sources of power.
PARC has worked on assessing the main challenges, priorities, and needs of the most
affected farmers in the targeted areas. The targeted areas were selected by cross-
referencing the FSS Focus Matrix with a newly conducted needs assessment of the
various vulnerable communities and are focused primarily in the North Gaza, Gaza City,
and Middle Area Governorates.
The results of the assessment has been reflected in the proposed interventions and
treated as the theme that governs the implementation of the project activities. In its
ultimate state, the project will act as a booster to the living conditions of the affected
groups and will contribute to enhancing their food security and the food security of their
families.
The project has incorporated the concepts of the Water, Energy, and Climate Change
Nexus along with other useful frameworks to design an intervention that will directly
address the challenges faced by the target groups. Additionally, the project is based on addressing Country HRP Strategic Objective 3, the most recent MSNA and the FSS sector Focus Matrix and Strategic Objectives.
PARC has carefully selected and designed activities that aim to uplift and enhance the food security and livelihoods of the targeted groups:
-The frst activity focuses on rehabilitating agricultural wells and rainwater harvesting
ponds by renovating and/or rehabilitating the structures, adding submersible pumps,
and PV solar systems to power the pumps.
-The second activity will rehabilitate the external structures associated with poultry and sheep farming and equip each farm with an PV solar system that will help cool, ventilate, and light the chicken coops and sheep pens.
PARC is actively promoting the responsible and sustainable use of natural resources among its benefciaries to serve as an example of good agricultural practices in their communities.
PARC’s interventions will directly support 150 vulnerable HHs (840 individuals, 244
Males, 235 Female,184 Boys,177Girls). Of these individuals, 150 are farmers, 75 are
poultry breeders, and 75 are sheep breeders, in addition to 30 farmers will be biniftted from RWHP and 2 agricultral water wells. All activities
will be coordinated with the relevant authorities such as the FSS, Ministry of Agriculture,
Palestinian Water Authority, Food and Agriculture Organization, and existing Community
Protection Committees

1 - Needs

According to the latest statistics issued by the World Food Program on September 2022, 64.4% of people in the Gaza Strip are food insecureStrip, or about 1.36 million people. According to the (WFP, 2022), Severe food insecure people in Palestine face a significant consumption gap and lack the means to meet their basic needs such as food, shelter, and clothing. They are unable to close the significant consumption gap using economic means or coping mechanisms that have already been depleted as a consequence of the ongoing and prolonged crisis.. According to the results of the Multi Sectoral Needs Analysis (MSNA), the results of the oPt FSS HNO 2022 showed that 80.7% of the HHs Rely on less preferred/less expensive food, 37.4% Borrow food, or rely on help from a friend/relative, 44.3% suffer from Limit portion sizes at mealtime, 37.8% Reduce the number of meals eaten in a day, and 27.6% Restrict consumption by adults. The repeated wars on the Gaza Strip, which mainly destroy the infrastructure that supports the Gazan economy, starting with the war of 2009 and ending with the war in 2021, Gaza faced a setback followed by a setback due to the brutal destruction of facilities, economic facilities, farms, agricultural lands and agricultural holdings, which are considered the main engine of an economy suffering from a huge number of scourges. The deteriorating conditions of farmers and their heavy daily losses threaten to cause the collapse of the agricultural sector, which is the main provider of the food basket needed by the residents of the Gaza Strip and considered a source of livelihood for a large segment of its population. Where according to the (PCBS, 2021) on its report on the Performance Of The Palestinian Economy During 2021, As Well As The Economic Forecasts For The Year 2022, the contribution of agriculture in the national GDP in 2022 is expected to be 6.6 Million dollars, compared to 7.1 million in 2020. PARC has recently conducted needs assessment in October 2022 targeting five main groups of farmers namely Poultry Farmers (72 in the middle and Gaza governorate), livestock farmers (78 in the middle and Gaza governorate), open land farmers benefitting from water ponds (60 in north Gaza), and wells owners (21 wells owners located in middle Gaza governorate). The assessment showed that the targeted farmers suffer from deteriorated conditions as a result of the challenges imposed upon them due to the ever-declining economic conditions. One of the main aspects that were commonly assessed among the farmers is that all of them suffer from poor economic conditions represented in absence of fixed income, growing level of expenditures whether they were related to the production process or related to securing the basics need of the family, poor ability to boost their productivity and cover their basics needs, and high dependence on debt to finance their livelihood. All of these have resulted in placing a heavy burden on the lives of the farmers, knowing that most of them depend heavily on agriculture as their main source of income. As a specific highlight per each group of farmers, the results of the assessment showed that in regard to the poultry farmers, most of them face different challenges including power outage, poor farm structure, high prices of inputs, which result in increase in the mortality rates and eventually result in sever losses for the farmers. The main needs identified includes the rehabilitation of the farm structure and the provision of heating source, and the provision of solar power energy especially given that the winter season require high reliance on heating which poses a huge burden on the farmers. In regard to the livestock farmers, the main challenges that High fodder price, inefficient medicines, poor farm structure, poor quality of local varieties, and poor extension services. Where the priority needs of the farmers include rehabilitation of the farm, as well as the provision of solar powered energy which serve different purposes including heating, lightning, and ventilation of the farm. As for the open land farmers benefitting from water ponds, the assessment showed that the main challenges they face include Soil diseases, lack of electricity, poor Fertilizer quality and high prices, poor Agricultural input quality and high prices, poor water quality, and low selling prices. Where water problems were considered the main problem that affect their crops which prevent them from selling their products at reasonable prices due to its poor quality. Based on these challenges, the main needs identified include rehabilitation of rainwater harvesting ponds which will result in improving the quality of water used in irrigation the crops, which will eventually enable the farmers to produce high quality crops that can be sold at reasonable prices in the local marker, as well as stimulate their export readiness in the future. Finally, in regard to the water wells, the results of the assessment showed that the targeted farmers serve on average about 50 farmers per well. The assessment also showed that of the main challenges facing the well owners include inability to operate the wells as it needs serious rehabilitation work, and the high cost of operating the well due to electricity cutoffs and lack of alternative power sources to operate the wells.

2 - Activities

The project will be integrated with current PARC food security, livelihood, and economic resilience interventions in Gaza Strip. The proposed intervention addresses the needs identified within the oPt HNO and is aligned with the Country HRP SO3 and FSS #1. The key activities are: Activity 1: Rehabilitation of agricultural water wells and rainwater harvesting ponds (RWHP) (including provision of PV solar system) , PARC will rehabilitate 2 agricultural water well and 30 rain water harvesting ponds with the provision of rehabilitative services including PV solar system for agricultural wells and RWHP. PARC will provide rehabilitative maintenance and supply photovoltaic (PV) solar systems to two large-scale agricultural water wells (20-30 HP), The rehabilitation of the water ground well includes: Engineering evaluation /design report; Installing of submersible pump; Replacement of polyethylene riser pipe; Rehabilitation of the electrical room; and Fitting and accessories (water meter, valves etc). The supporting solar systems will be installed to decrease energy costs and maintain the running of the water pumping, which will recover the production of more than 200 dunums cultivated by 80 farmers households (448 individuals; 228 males, 220 females), with good water quality suitable for irrigating different crops from Gaza city and Middle area especially vegetables that have good market potentials. Additionally, PARC will provide rehabilitative services and supply PV solar systems to 30 RWHP serving 30 farmers in North Gaza (Biet Hanoun and Beit Lahia). The ponds will be reinforced with a geomembrane liner. Geomembrane ponds tend to be highly durable (lasting more than 25 years) as the geomembrane material is resistant to chemicals, heat, and physical stresses. The pond will include connections to the greenhouses’ drainage system to collect rainwater and a connection to the irrigation systems of these greenhouses. Additionally, the farmers will be provided with protective mesh to decrease the water pollution in the pond, especially from algae blooms. It is important to mention that PARC will devote lessons learned from previous interventions while implementing this activity The estimated total cost of the maintenance and PV systems (30,000 USD for each well and 4,000 USD for each pond) was based on the actual bill of quantities (BoQs), current market prices, as well as experience of PARC conducting similar activities in this feld. The activity will be coordinated with MoA and the PWA, and other active stakeholders implementing similar interventions. Priority will be given to the wells and ponds which were most affected by the various shocks that have occurred in past years, as well as those serving the highest number of small-scale farmers. Activity 2: Rehabilitation of poultry and sheep farms (including provision of PV solar systems) PARC will support 150 vulnerable and/or shock-affected small-scale breeders (840 individuals; 428 males, 412 females) with critical rehabilitation and provision of PV solar systems to power their poultry and sheep farms (75 poultry, 75 sheep), The targeted areas are the Gaza City and Middle Area Governorates. PARC will rehabilitate 75 poultry farms (25 females and 50 males) with solar systems consisting of 1kw solar cells, an inverter, 12-volt batteries, 200 amps, and ventilation hoods to reduce the spread of diseases and increase air circulation inside the farms. The farmers will also be provided with a 0.5 HP water pump to disperse water mist of the roof of the farm to reduce summer temperatures. The solar systems will provide around the clock electricity to the farm to provide lighting, cooling, and ventilation that will reduce production costs and increase the resilience of the farmers. Farmers will also be provided with training on how to use and manage their solar systems. The average cost of rehabilitation (2500 USD per farm) PARC will also support 75 sheep breeders (25 females and 50 males) through, first: rehabilitation of external farm structures such as roofs, safety nets, metal frames and breeding equipment. Second, providing breeders with production inputs such as concentrated feed to increase productivity of the sheep. Third, installing solar systems with a capacity of 2kw and all its associated equipment. Fourth, providing the necessary veterinary tools for sheep breeding such as high-quality veterinary medicines. The average cost of rehabilitation (4000 USD per farm) To ensure achieving the desired impact, and maintaining sustainability, as well as abiding to the do no harm, the project activities will be implemented in close coordination with target farmers and the target communities through the existing (CPCs) and ensure that intervention aligned with existing Community Protection Plan, the selection process will be participatory to the possible extent through involving all related stakeholders, and it will be transparent and easy to access, as PARC will open the announcement using different online platforms and CBOs to ensure reaching the targeted population easily, Upon the receival of the applications, PARC team will review, filter, and sort the applications to produce the initial list, which will be further crosschecked with the FSS to ensure that the candidates. After that, PARC will start the field process to validate the received applications, where in this stage, all applications will be completed and all supported documents will be gathered and added to the application, and those who scored the highest scores will be selected and shared with the FSS to get approval on the final list of beneficiaries. PARC will select all beneficiaries based on a set of vulnerability criteria, and taking into consideration gender, age, and disability considerations in all phases of the project. Priority will be given to female headed households at least 10% in all activities in the targeted areas.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

“Do No Harm" as seen to be part of ensuring accountability towards the local communities used and monitored during any intervention implemented by PARC including participation, information sharing, enabling complaints and "do no harm”, environment protection and gender mainstreaming. The designed project interventions and implementation strategies adheres to the social, political, and economic context of targeted populations and is based on comprehensive understanding of the targeted contexts through the conducted participatory needs assessment and PARC do-no-harm approaches. In regards to social sensitivity, PARC understands the conservative local Palestinian context and will ensure that in all project phases the announcements and meetings will take place in a wide, easily accessed area where women can feel comfortable to join and share their needs. For the political side, all proposed interventions will take into consideration the sensitivity of its location in which a low-profile working approach will take place. Finally, at economic level the project interventions aid to support local population to empower their production abilities and access to their assets. Continued consultations will be conducted with the project target groups/stakeholders and HHs to analyze potential risks that the Project team will take into consideration while progressing/implementing the project. PARC believes in equal opportunities for all beneficiaries; ensuring equal access of information, neutrality and assistance through the project cycle starting with the inclusion of all relevant stakeholders in the need assessment. Coordination with the local authorities, CBOs, FSS and community leaders in the project planning and selection process to ensure optimal community participation, outreach, and transparency in service provision. PARC ensures a transparent selection process which avoids social conflict during the implementation of the project. The project is designed based on Human-Rights Based Approaches (HRBA) including women, youth and Persons with Disabilities (PwDs). HRBA; focusing on the actual needs of people and integrating principles of accountability, transparency, meaningful participation, nondiscrimination and linkage to global, regional and national human rights instruments are an integral part of PARC work.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PARC ensures that aid and impartial access reaches the most vulnerable since the design phase when conducting a need assessment at communities. Gender mainstreaming and protection approaches are well integrated within the project activities. In need assessment PARC ensures to engage local community members, local governmental bodies and other relevant stakeholders to have a comprehensive understanding of the targeted community’s needs, including the participation of women and women led cooperatives and clubs. The findings of the need assessment define the proposed interventions. This is then followed at the implementation phase of the proposed project, in which PARC starts its intervention with formulating a Project Local Committee that represents the targeted population and include key actors from the locality to ensure optimal outreach, transparency and ownership to vulnerable community groups. The project Local Committee and PARC’s team will be responsible in ensuring the access to information to all community members through a clear gender sensitive project announcement in the mosques, village councils, and PARC’s social media platforms. PARC team will develop a well-defined selection criterion that responds to the needs of the targeted population and ensures accessing the most vulnerable (Children, PwDs, elderly, women headed HH… etc). An open call of applications will also ensure the meaningful access, empowerment and transparent process in selecting final beneficiaries which will also be in participation with Project Local Committee. This participatory approach emphasizes ownership, transparency, good governance, gender balance at beneficiary and community levels. Weekly meetings will be held between the municipality, project local committee, community members and PARC field officer for each community. PARC accountability system follows a clear mechanism of complaint registration, reporting and solving, is accessible by the municipality and local community members. Regular follow-up of complaints received from communities, and Municipality are performed with PARC’s M&E unit, including when necessary, addressing OCHA for larger external complaints concerning security and overall occupation related to protection risks. PARC will share any human rights-based violations to relevant clusters.
