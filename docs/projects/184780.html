Project Summary

Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely affect at least 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. The majority of displaced people have self-settled in over 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in urban, semi-urban and rural areas across the country. People displaced to these sites are living in precarious conditions with the majority not having their basic needs met due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support. According to the detailed site assessment finding and site verification assessments conducted by the CCCM cluster, more than 85 per cent of sites are informal settlements settled on private land with the majority of sites located in urban areas. The lack of access to land coupled with a weak land tenure system, multiplicity of land ownership, land disputes, weak judicial systems to seek redress, and a lack of proper national land policy further exacerbates the vulnerability of IDPs. This has led to frequent incidents of forceful evictions pushing IDPs into worsened situations requiring both emergency and prolonged assistance. IDP returns are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services. CCCM Cluster monthly service monitoring and complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) trend analysis indicate similar service gaps and issues raised. Most IDP sites recorded needs mostly in Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) support, Shelter and WASH assistance, but not excluding other sectors such as health and education which also show considerable service and assistance needs. While as the service monitoring cuts across IDP sites and highlights the needs of all IDP populations, CCCM cluster reports indicate that the majority of those most acutely affected are vulnerable groups; women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups and people living with disability (PLWD), putting further stress on that demography of displaced families and limiting their ability to cope.
The constant threat of eviction in IDP sites destabilizes the ability for long-term community development preventing viable durable solutions to occur, particular in conflict locations such as South West State. As of August 2021, 92,042 individuals have been evicted from their settlements stemming from a total of 2,304 incidences of forceful eviction perpetrated by landowners. Furthermore, 115 of households in IDP settlements (17 per cent of Banadir IDPs) believe that they are currently at risk of being evicted from their shelters. Inaccessibility to land tenure security continues to function as a primary obstacle preventing IDPs from establishing permanent settlements
Camp Coordination and management will be enhanced in target settlements through training and infrastructure development. Post-training meetings among key stakeholders will touch on camp wide issues such as health, conflict mitigation, peaceful coexistence and service availability. It will also ensure equitable access to services, improve the quality of life of the IDPs during displacement while advocating for solutions and preparing them for life after displacement. Multi-purposes social spaces will be constructed in populated settlements with the aim of establishing structures for improved coordination and service delivery at site level. Service mapping exercises will be undertaken to map access and service delivery in newly established IDP sites in target areas. In the delivery of the proposed package, HIDIG will seek Integration with the protection, WASH, CCCM, Shelter and other clusters to ensure complementarity, value for money and efficiency of the response.

1. - Projects activities

1.1 HIDIG will organize CCCM training for 40 camp and community leaders from target settlements in Baidoa and Beletweyne. Participants will be drawn from the different fabrics of the society in target settlements with strong attention to female participation and gender balance. The trainings will adapt the CM Toolkit developed by the Global CCCM Cluster as applicable to the local context and in-line with the recommendations of the Somalia CCCM Cluster. It will also include topics such as camp set-up and management, protection of persons with specific needs, gender-based violence, information management, community participation, environment, sensitizing on camp wide issues such as health, conflict mitigation, peaceful coexistence and service availability. The training will equip participants with skills necessary for improved CCCM. Follow-up meetings will be held at the end of each training where camp and community committees discuss issues pertaining to improved site management and coordination. Throughout project life-cycle, HIDIG will continuously coordinate with the CCCM Cluster to ensure complementary and effectiveness of the response.1.2 HIDIG will construct 30 multi-purpose community spaces with seating options to facilitate community based activities and committee meetings in IDP sites across Baidoa and Beletweyne. These structures will not only support CCCM activities but will also serve as a safe space where IDPs can resolve interpersonal conflicts, conduct social ceremonies and rituals and raise awareness on matters of concern to them. Each space will be equipped with 10 plastic chairs and 3 plastic tables for efficient use1.3 HIDIG will conduct service mapping in newly established settlements in Baidoa and Beletweyne as needed on monthly basis to identify priority needs and access to services and facilities. HIDIG will gather data from settlements and will seek support from the Shelter/CCCM clusters for data analysis and reporting. Service mapping exercises will help humanitarian actors make informed decisions on their programs and to effectively enhance service delivery at site level.1.4 HIDIG will conduct proper site planning/re-planning where necessary during the construction of ESKs to enable safe and secure access to essential services and facilities while ensuring appropriate privacy and separation between individual household shelters and to ensure that pathways exist to provide all-weather access to individual dwellings and facilities

HIDIG have PSEA policy and this will guide the internal check and balance during the project implementation to enable mitigates any form of violence against girls, women and boysiii. Exposure on high risk settlement by beneficiaries –HIDIG trough coordination with the cluster and the government will ensure proper site monitoring and verification of sites risk and thus any eventuality will lead to relocation of the poor beneficiaries to safer sites iv. Violence exposure as a result of distance and location of services – HIDIG will coordinates with other humanitarian actors to ensure services are provided within the camps to avert any such risk of beneficiaries been exposed, the organization will also ensure the site mapping period that the sites have the necessary amenities to reduces the risk of beneficiaries been exposed in seeking services to further distancev. HIDIG will mitigate the issue of land ownership through proper sites identification and coordination with both the indigenous communities as well as the government representative and CCCM cluster hence this will provide proper guideline on the set of the project initiation period hence reduces the risk of land disputes among the IDPs and host communities in the target district
