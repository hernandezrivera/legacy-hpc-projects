Project Summary

Provide Unconditional food assistance (in-kind or cash-based transfer) to crisis-affected vulnerable Libyan IDPs, returnees and non-displaced populations in the East, West, and South of Libya.
WFP through local partners provides food assistance to food insecure and vulnerable people in Libya, including newly displaced persons through the inter-UN Rapid Response Mechanism. In 2020, WFP reached over 155,000 unique beneficiaries across all regions – 38 per cent more than the annual target, due to emergencies developing in Libya and increased socio-economic burden on people living with COVID-19 fears and restrictions necessitating unconditional food assistance to the increased number of vulnerable people in Libya.
Prioritization exercises are applied to the project based on the duration of displacements, the assistance provided by local authorities or other food security sector members, and accessibility and the selection of assistance modalities was guided by protection risk assessments and quick needs assessments depending on whether populations had access to cooking facilities.
Under the 2021 HRP. WFP plans to prioritize its unconditional life-saving food assistance to a monthly average of 68,064 vulnerable Libyan, including those crisis-affected, IDPs, returnees, non-displaced populations. Apart from the monthly general food assistance, WFP will keep its capacities to respond to urgent food needs of 37,239 newly displaced persons through the emergency Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) implemented jointly by IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF and WFP (up to 20,000 beneficiaries) and WFP own emergency responses (up to 17,239 beneficiaries).
Beneficiaries themselves, as members of local crisis committees, are involved throughout the food distribution process. Targeting is implemented geographically, mainly based on the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment findings and household demographic characteristics such as gender, age group, and the presence of women, men, boys and girls living with disabilities as well as Household Food Consumption Scores.
The general food assistance (GFA) basket is designed to provide families with 30-day food entitlements covering 75 per cent of daily energy requirements per person per day; the basket included enriched pasta, fortified wheat flour, rice, white beans, tomato paste, fortified vegetable oil and sugar. WFP Libya has rolled out the commodity electric voucher to the general food assistance since April 2020. Under this project, 27% of the monthly general food assistance beneficiaries (18,666 beneficiaries in West and East) will receive their food entitlement via the commodity e-voucher. 100% of the Emergency Food Assistance will receive their food assistance through in-kind transfer.
WFP will put its programmatic quality control and assurance measures in place, which include the following:
I) training of its Cooperating Partners and its own staff in cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, PSEA, Protection and Conflict Sensitivity;
ii) WFP’s output monitoring through the verification of distribution reports and spot-checks and WFP’s outcome monitoring through annual post-distribution monitoring;
iii) WFP’s distributions of leaflets and visibility materials to raise the awareness of participants and non-participants about the Common Feedback Mechanism.

1 - Needs

Food security remains a priority for the vulnerable populations in Libya as conflict continues to generate displacement. An estimated 604,307 individuals people (including 70,264 Libyan IDPs, 332,453 Libyan non-displaced persons, 71,287 Libyan returnees, 123,090 migrants, 7,213 refugees) are identified as in need of food security assistance. Of those, an estimated 78 per cent are Libyans – whether displaced people, returnees or host communities. Trend analysis between 2018 and 2020 shows a significant improvement of the food security situation in most of the country until COVID-19 crisis. However, the current situation in Libya has affected the food security of non-displaced Libyan households the most, with a 102 per cent increase in the number of people in need of food security assistance (332,453 in 2020 versus 164,000 in 2019). A higher proportion of female-headed households (49 per cent) adopted either crisis or emergency coping strategies than their male counterparts (15 per cent). The majority, of both male and female-headed households, spent savings (65 per cent female, 46 per cent male) and borrowed money (65 per cent female, 24 per cent male). Reducing health expenditure, expenditures on essential non-food items and taking on additional jobs were also adopted with the proportion of female-headed households more than doubling the proportions of their male counterparts in the use of these strategies. More than half of the Libyan population employed one or more livelihood coping strategies to deal with the shortage of food. The majority of the Libyans reported spending savings. Also employed by many Libyans were borrowing money, selling household assets, taking on an extra job and reducing health expenditures. 36 per cent of the Libyans adopted either crisis or emergency coping strategies. The adoption of the emergency coping strategy was highest in the Eastern and Southern regions across all the population groups. Alkufra recorded the highest level of engagement in illegal income activities. Among the returnees, more than half (59 per cent) adopted either crisis or emergency coping strategy to cope with food shortage. This was exceptionally high in Benghazi and Alkufra. All these further accounts for a deterioration in people’s well-being and living standards.

2 - Activities

Using the geographic severity needs and vulnerable groups identified in the HNO and in consultation with local crisis committees, the Ministry of Social Affairs branches and the local NGOs, the activity will prioritize response interventions addressing the specific needs of different population groups and targeting IDPs, returnees and non-displaced hosting communities with a priority to female-headed households, or large households with pregnant and lactating women or girls, children under two, elderly persons, people with chronic illnesses and/or people living with disabilities. The activity will prioritize 68,064 most vulnerable Libyans across Libya, with a priority in Benghazi, Misrata, Murzuq, Sebha and Tripoli. In case of emergency, WFP will prioritize the newly displaced by providing immediate and life-saving food rations in the framework of the inter-sectoral Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). WFP will also mobilize its own capacities and resources to provide its emergency food assistance to urgent cases who do not fit the scope of the RRM, such as non-displaced persons affected by unexpected shocks such as COVID-19 lockdown and prolongedly displaced persons in needs for food assistance. Along with the project implementation, WFP will ensure that the practical programmatic control and quality assurance measures shall be put in place, which includes: i) WFP’s training of its Cooperating Partners and its own staff in cross-cutting issues such as gender, GBV, PSEA, Protection and Conflict Sensitivity; ii) WFP’s output monitoring through the verification of distribution reports and spot-checks and WFP’s outcome monitoring through annual post-distribution monitoring; iii) WFP’s distributions of leaflets and visibility materials to raise the awareness of participants and non-participants about the CFM.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.1) WFP displays posters sharing the CFM number in all food distribution locations and also shares this information verbally with all beneficiaries throughout the assistance cycle; 4.2) As part of its conflict-sensitive assistance, WFP conducts analysis and consultations with local authorities and Local Crisis Committees before expanding unconditional food assistance to any location. This includes ensuring access to all affected communities and speaking to different groups to enable their participation. WFP also coordinates its food assistance to vulnerable Libyans with food assistance to vulnerable non-Libyans in the same geographical areas; 4.3) As part of its conflict-sensitive assistance, WFP analyzes before beginning food assistance in any location. This includes ensuring access to all affected communities and speaking to different groups to enable their participation;4.4) COVID-19 guidelines are shared verbally and through posters throughout training venues. Masks and disinfection gels are provided at food distribution points, and waiting areas and food receipt points are re-arranged to facilitate social distancing;4.6) Sensitive beneficiary data is only shared between WFP and its partners through secure means and never via email. Access to this data is strictly limited;4.9) While WFP engages with multiple stakeholders when selecting beneficiaries, all must be chosen based on quick needs assessment findings and vulnerability scorecards; 4.10) WFP consults a range of actors during the quick needs assessments, project implementation and quality control call phases. This includes interviews with locals from a variety of backgrounds; 4.11) WFP facilitates access to food assistance for people with disabilities where possible. This includes having sign language available or other support such as food deliveries by porters as needed;4.18) Though thorough analysis, WFP has determined to use the Family Booklet Number as the standard identifier for beneficiaries of unconditional food assistance. For one-off life-saving assistance, WFP does not require the documentation for newly displaced persons to enjoy their food entitlements;4.21) Before and during the implementation of unconditional food assistance regardless of in-kind transfer or commodity e-voucher, WFP continuously monitors the local market prices through the Joint Market Monitoring Initiatives. WFP's local partners participate in the JMMI by collecting local market prices on a monthly basis so that WFP's food assistance must not adversely affect the food accessibility of non-beneficiaries in the targeted areas; 4.23) WFP sets the assistance period at 6 months. At the end of the assistance period, household vulnerability scorecards are updated so that only vulnerable households shall be transferred to the next 6-month food assistance cycle. The better-off households graduate from unconditional food assistance, and WFP facilitates their participation in conditional food assistance to enhance their income-generating capacities and resilience.
