Project Summary

In response to the critical conditions estimated by the latest IPC round for acute malnutrition in Ikwoto, AVSI’s intervention intends to save the lives of malnourished vulnerable groups - pregnant and lactating women, and children under five years of age – by increasing access to timely lifesaving management of acute malnutrition and enhancing malnutrition-preventive MIYCN counselling, for both displaced people and the host community.
AVSI proposes to have continuous treatment of moderate acute malnutrition in targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) sites with further expansion of services in the hard to reach locations without health facilities. While ensuring universal treatment for acute malnutrition is a priority, AVSI will also focus on the prevention package to address other multi causative contributing factors to acute malnutrition. The prevention efforts will focus on child care practices including improving quality of food consumed by children and treatment and prevention of childhood illness through MIYCN counselling of PLW and caregivers of children (6-59months). Support will be given to access food diversity for vulnerable households through the establishment of kitchen gardens. AVSI currently uses an integrated approach therefore health, food security, and WASH projects that are already being implemented by AVSI in Ikwoto will continue to be linked to nutrition interventions to holistically address acute malnutrition. Integration strategies will include: linking malnourished vulnerable households to short-term resilience food security assistance (GFD); the establishment of homestead small scale vegetable production; provision of preventive and curative basic maternal child health care services such as malaria testing for children, immunization, and antenatal services; the provision of WASH NFI for caretakers of malnourished children and drinking water and hand washing facilities for all nutrition; the establishment of health clubs and routine nutrition and health promotion awareness campaigns in primary schools.
This intervention will also provide food assistance to the only referral point for management of HIV/AIDS and TB in Ikwoto County: St Theresa mission hospital. The hospital is also a stabilization centre for managing complicated cases of acute malnutrition. Food assistance will help encourage caretakers to comply with treatment duration stay in the inpatient care unit and provide the required body energy and accelerate recovery from illnesses. Through all its nutritional interventions for 2020, AVSI aims to:
- Increase equitable access to quality nutritional services that provide prevention interventions and early detection and treatment of malnutrition for children under the age five, school-aged children, young girls and pregnant and lactating women
- Improve institutional capacity in nutrition information systems and knowledge-generation to facilitate scale-up of nutrition interventions in Ikwoto county
- To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

According to AVSI’s June 2019 SMART Survey conducted in Ikwoto and in accordance with the latest FSNMS findings, a significant number of households were found to be at risk of food insecurity where only 38.6% of the surveyed households had acceptable food consumption, and 61.1% of sampled households had low diet diversity. 95.8% had a low reduced coping strategy index. The SMART survey also revealed a Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate for the former County of Ikwoto was 9.2% (6.6 – 12.8 95% C.I.), and the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate was 2.0% (1.0 – 4.0 95% C.I.). GAM indicates a poor nutrition situation while SAM is classified as critical according to WHO classification. Results also showed a significant difference between boys and girls in the prevalence of acute malnutrition estimated by Weight for Height Z-score (p-value = 0.0291). Boys were more malnourished compared to girls with GAM rate at 12.5% for boys and 5.9% for girls. This suggests a higher vulnerability to acute malnutrition by boys compared to girls. 26.9% (22.4-31.9 95% C.I.) of children were found to be underweight while 4.3% (2.7-6.6 95% C.I.) were severely underweight. The rate of chronic malnutrition in Ikwoto is also worrying with a level of stunting considered "serious" at 31.9%. In fact in EES, Ikwoto County in particular, chronic malnutrition is the highest compared to the entire country, highlighting the need to prioritize stunting prevention interventions. The high rates of malnutrition in South Sudan are attributed to several factors including high levels of food insecurity, poor infant and young child feeding practices as well as high morbidity due to a weak health system in the country. Poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum dietary diversity: 15%) and high prevalence of diseases (up to 40%). Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services during the rainy season due to poor road infrastructure constrain service delivery to areas already afflicted by high malnutrition levels. According to IPC key message of August 2019, in EES, an estimated 560,000 people (52.6% of the State population) were facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August. Ikwoto County was classified to be in Crisis. The outcomes were driven by low cereal stock and high food prices, the situation was also further worsened by reduced physical access to markets and poor purchasing power. According to projections, food security should improve within EES during the typical harvest season, Sep-Dec 2019.This reduction is attributed to the seasonal availability of harvests and livestock products. However, in some locations, particularly the predominantly pastoralist areas of Ikwoto (Lomohidang South Payam), incidents of cattle raiding are likely to contribute to household vulnerability to food insecurity. The overall improvement is believed to be a short lived relief for some of the population as figures are likely to return to around 50% again during the post-harvest season of Jan-Apr 2020 due to decline in harvested grains where households start to rely more on markets for food, but face continued high prices. The onset of the lean season will be characterized by depleted stocks and reduced access to livestock products and wild foods. Currently Ikwoto has got 27 functional health facilities with only 4 PHCCs capable of providing comprehensive integrate maternal child health care, most of the facilities have got inadequate skilled personnel and basic equipment’s hence poor health care system. Nutrition services implemented currently is targeting treatment of acute malnutrition which is not comprehensive and with minimum integration. The nutrition sites are not adequate and not easily accessible to majority in hard to reach settlement. In addition, there is only one stabilization center in the county which is not supported and lacks most of the vital required facilities and equipment.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

AVSI’s effort in the field of protection and food security are oriented to ensure the respect of human rights to life and health. The combination of such response aims at fostering the conditions for people to know their rights and to have the means to make them.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

One objective of AVSI’s projects in EES intends to reinforce the resilience of displacement-affected people by improving the nutrition, food security and education of returnees from Uganda. AVSI has recently conducted two surveys, one with refugees in Uganda and another in South Sudan, to assess the needs of displacement-affected people, including their perspective on repatriation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

AVSI, given its steady presence in EES since 2005, has had the opportunity to establish over the years a deep relationship with local authorities, both at the county and at the state level. AVSI foresees the presence of staff that support local authorities in defining needs, implementing intervention strategies and putting into action planned activities. State ministry of health and county health department will play very important roles in the implementation of the project to ensure sustainability in terms of ownership and governance of the health facilities, local resource mobilization, support supervisions, maintenance of health workers, provision of essential drugs and other medical consumables, etc. Community Nutrition Volunteers and mother support peer groups will also be involved in the mobilization, screening and follow up home visits. AVSI’s interventions have the purpose of strengthening the local governance structure, making the project more sustainable and durable over time, both by nurturing and strengthening the capacity for management, organization and monitoring of the situation of food insecurity and malnutrition of the direct beneficiaries and more generically than the population of the counties, both by ensuring that this (a situation of food insecurity and malnutrition) may be present in the future and be reflected in local, state and national policies.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The active participation of AVSI in the Nutrition Cluster, both at the national and state level, guarantees the alignment of its response with the priorities indicated by the humanitarian system and international cooperation agencies, in line with the country's political developments.For the purposes and activities envisaged, this proposal integrates the objectives and recommendations of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 and the Nutrition Cluster Objectives 2020: SO1 - To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women; SO2 - To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition; SO3 - To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups. AVSI’s intervention will increase access to quality nutritional services that provide prevention interventions and early detection and treatment of malnutrition. Additionally, AVSI will improve institutional capacity in nutrition information systems and knowledge-generation to facilitate scale-up of nutrition interventions in Ikwoto County. Finally, in all its interventions, AVSI closely coordinates all activities and programming to ensure a successful multi-sector approach. Activities are also aligned with the priorities mentioned in the main national planning documents, such as the National Development Strategy 2018–2021 and the National Gender Policy 2013.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The conditions of protection are far from stable, let alone safe. In Ikwoto County, AVSI’s multisector survey found that around 15% of children and adolescents are reportedly subject to repeated physical abuses and harsh punishments, 8% were discriminated against, and 7% sexually abused. Moreover, mental distress is also quite common, with almost half of the population seeking the repeated intervention of some healer (whose qualifications are difficult to assess but presumably inadequate). 34% of women and girls report GBV such as physical assault (24%), rape (4%) and forced marriage (19%). These figures are most likely much lower than reality due to underreporting. In the face of such threats, leveraging on the expertise gained in relation to its education projects, AVSI promotes the development of capacity in the field of protection for all its project officers, actively seeking the inclusion of women in all its interventions, including with local civil associations of women. AVSI implements projects that integrate education components, as a means to provide protection to young children, and FSL projects, to ensure the sustainability of such support.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The proposed intervention is an essential component of a wider, multi-sectorial approach in Ikwoto County. AVSI continues its vision of implementing development projects, in strong collaboration with local authorities and international donors, aimed at facilitating the abilities of the local community to rebuild their livelihoods and resilience. Community involvement and best practice promotion will contribute to a multiplication effect which leads to not only greater impacts per intervention, but also to more durable impacts in the long run. Additionally, AVSI’s ongoing and long-term projects in education are well connected to and embedded within other interventions of promoting health and nutrition through knowledge transfers and referral systems that will lead to positive behaviour change hence reducing the problem.AVSI strives to work always very closely with local stakeholders as described above. As such, through a well-established infrastructure, much of the nutrition and health coverage in the county will continue under the County Health Department’s supervision. AVSI is confident of its local partner’s transformative capacity and the resilience of the systems that is being established over the last few years.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Firstly, as is often reported from communities, humanitarian assistance is at times channeled to more organized communities, leaving other communities (even more vulnerable populations) less assisted. To minimize this, AVSI always attempts to cover large as well as small communities in both central and more peripheral locations. For the second risk, rather than lack of awareness of referral services, there is more a problem of lack of access to psychological support to traumatized children and victims of GBV. AVSI focuses on prevention and mitigation by providing safe spaces to children, promoting female education and gender inclusion in general. Lastly, the delivery of humanitarian assistance may always spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. To mitigate this risk, AVSI is always transparent in its selection criteria and strives to implement activities that have sustainable and long-lasting benefits for the entire community.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 5 of such surveys which, along with other researches aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. The most recent example of one of AVSI’s feedback mechanism happened in the Lake States in August where AVSI staff validated the finding of two researches on challenges faced by Persons with Disabilities (PwD) and on Gender Based Violence. The 3-day validation meeting was attended by key stakeholders including PwD. AVSI ensured that at least half of the 50 attendees were females in positions of authority at the insitiutional or community level.
