Project Summary

The extensive use of explosive weapons since the beginning of the crisis generated explosive hazards, posing a grave threat to kill or injure. Civilians, humanitarian response operators, and livelihood activities. The contamination with Unexploded ordnance (UXO) in the areas affected by the crisis caused injures and loosing life. Across the country, an estimated 8 million people are exposed to life-threatening explosive hazards, including Aleppo and Deir-ez-Zor Governorates. Such hazards range from landmines, to booby traps and unexploded remnants of war and have devastating consequences on the lives of children and their families. In 2017 in Aleppo, 383 people were killed and hundreds more injured in similar hazards, having returned to east Aleppo after fighting subsided (https://www.unicef.org/mena/stories/unicef-protects-children-and-their-families-across-syria-against-explosive-hazards.In Deir-Ez-Zor, after 5 years of fighting and periods of siege, in November 2017, the GoS (Government of Syria) took control of the area. Infrastructures are heavily damaged, basic public services suspended, and large part of the city is contaminated by Explosives Remnants from War (ERW). In Rural Aleppo, as big part of it was under fight and siege, infrastructures are heavily damaged and high level of contamination of ERW still exists.
Therefore, GVC decided to launch the “Risk Education Program” in contaminated areas and in areas of returns, where people will be forced to deal with the risks related to mines and explosive hazards.
GVC intervention will aim at proving communities and returnees with the proper knowledge and methods to prevent the accidents of explosion contamination.
The planned intervention is in line with GVC’s humanitarian commitment started in 2014 and focused on Risk Education to teachers since 2017, targeting Aleppo Governorate. The planned project will build upon the following achievements: GVC Risk Education training was conducted in Aleppo city and rural and targeted 800 teachers (male and female), as in each training session 20 teachers attended. A pre test and post test took place in order to evaluate the teachers knowledge of the Risk education as subject and its component; the outcome of the tests showed that in each training 15 teachers did not know anything on risk education, and the rest 5 teachers they either had wrong information or they only knew the kind of mining without any clue of how to avoid and behave properly. At the end of the training 99% of the teachers were assessed as aware and acquired an adequate attitude in the Risk Education. .
All the above encouraged GVC extend the program to target different categories of beneficiaries through different kinds of activities, such as awareness session and theatre activities.
The action will continue targeting Aleppo, while extending to Deir-ez-Zor Governorate.
The “Risk Education Program” is using professional standard where a diverse range of methods are implemented, targeting different categories of beneficiaries that are able to spread the messages to the community as a whole. More specifically, this program will be delivered to teachers, children, and community (men/ women/ persons with disabilities/ elderly) through training and awareness campaign, using material (brochures, guidelines, kits, video contents etc.) provided or elaborated in collaboration with Ministry of Education/Directorate of Education.

Needs

The contamination in Syria is threating the lives, restricts freedom of movement, prevent communities from conducting life-sustaining activities and accessing land, property and services. The Rapid Assessment on Mine Action (RAMA) survey made by UNMAS in November 2017 reached 6,024 communities, 5,455 of which were populated, which represents 100% coverage of Syria. The findings presented below are based on surveyed populated locations, unless otherwise stated. A total of 24,068 KIs (Community Leaders, Health Worker, NGO Worker, Civil Defense, Military Technical Unit) were interviewed in 5,455 locations. Although a great majority of the KIs are people in key positions who are expected to have the best knowledge of the communities they live in, they can only share their best estimates. It is therefore important to note that community assessments are subject to an undefined margin of error since they are based on the best estimates of KIs interviewed.As November 2017 (reporting period) Aleppo has the highest number of contaminated locations, with one quarter of its communities reported to be contaminated. The majority of reportedly contaminated locations are located in the eastern part of the governorate, with 94% of contaminated communities reporting that agricultural land is blocked, 80% of contaminated communities report landmines; 62% report UXO/AXO, 33% report cluster munitions and 19% report IEDs. The 47% of communities in Aleppo report casualties, with movement within the community cited as the most common activity prior to accidents. 90% of Aleppo’s contaminated communities reported agricultural land to be contaminated, while 37% reported housing and apartment buildings to be contaminated, while ownership and rental disputes are the most commonly reported issue in contaminated communities in Aleppo. As November 2017 (reporting period) 17% of the communities in Deir-Ez-Zor reported contamination, the third highest proportionally after Ar-Raqqa and Aleppo. All contaminated communities reported access roads to be contaminated, and 96% of communities reported being afraid to move within or outside their location. Deir-Ez-Zor is the only governorate to have reported contamination but no explosive hazard removal or risk education. It is also the only governorate where all contaminated communities reported had no functioning land registry.When communities were asked whether the fear of explosive hazards prevented them from conducting certain activities, the most commonly cited activity was farming (72% of contaminated communities), followed by the grazing of animals (66%). Communities also reported being afraid of sending children to play (43%), travelling within or outside their community (40%) and returning home (29%) in the case of displaced persons. Besides secondary data reported by UNMAS, GVC staff collected information and stories of teachers involved in on-going Risk Education training, and understood how urgent is the need of communities (including vulnerable groups such as children and women) in Rural Aleppo for acquiring knowledge on the topic.

Implementation of activities

Activities linked with Protection Objective 42.1 Conduct risk education for at-risk groupsIn this activity, GVC will do training for teachers (n.940) and educators(n.100). More specifically, the 2 days Risk Education training will be either part of the Active Learning Training (total duration of each course is 7 days) or delivered as a specific 2 days’ training. The teachers selected in coordination with DoE, as relevant public service providers, are considered the main messengers to distribute the information on risk education to their children in schools. We esteem that each teacher will transfer the information to 50 students reaching 47.000 students, using guides and material received during the training course and additional teaching tools provided by the project. Trainers delivering the course will be assisted by GVC staff, who will give proper support and ensure constant monitoring and good quality of the information provided. Besides the above mentioned intervention, GVC is planning to implement a Risk Education campaigns in the targeted areas, relying on the support of community outreach volunteers and/or SARC (Syrian Arab Red Crescent) volunteers. All the people responsible of delivering the campaigns will be previously trained on RE and the proper ways to approach people and give information on the topic. During the campaign, around 50,000 people will acquire awareness and confidence on types of mines, cluster munitions, unexploded ordnance, warning signs, related dangers and how to prevent them; a fake minefield where a demonstration of manual clearance of mines, description of all equipment will also take place. Awareness material (flyers, posters, brochures etc.) will be distributed and displayed in public spaces and key locations (e.g. mosques) in the villages/neighborhoods.In order to attract children, who are one of the most vulnerable groups and more likely exposed to the hazard, recreational activities will be organized as a part of the campaign: interactive theatre events will take to promote behavioral change in boys and girls and delivered by SARC volunteers previously trained on RE. At least 50% of the overall beneficiaries will be school-age children, including vulnerable groups such as OOSC and returnees.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates:1. Assumption: Security context in Deir-Ez-Zor and Aleppo targeted area remains stableRisks: Security situation deteriorates to the point to prevent the implementation of some of the activities 2. Assumption: Access to target areas is safe for staff to conduct activities Risks: Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some intervention area due to military operation3. Assumption: Internal return of people sensibly increases because of improved political and safety situation (mostly in Aleppo rural and Deir-Ez-Zor)Risks: Major change in demographic in the area of intervention due to population movements requires modification in the strategy.4. Assumption: Collaboration of the Authorities to set up criteria for the selection of the beneficiaries, to monitor the activity, and approve it.Risks: Major change in strategy of authorities and local partners (in particular SARC/ MoFA and MoE) toward the planned interventions and reject it, and/ or delay in approval and bureaucrat requests.5. Assumption: Recruitment of trainer, volunteers and key persons and attendance to the training sessions are assured as planned Risks: difficulty to find qualified trainer mainly in Deir-Ez-Zor.Mitigations: 1. GVC ensures a continuous coordination with local partners SARC, Ministry of Education and international specialized agencies (UNDSS) in order to monitor the situation. GVC adopted a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident. 2. Policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, Working Groups and Coordination Mechanisms in order to keep us updated on the reasons which may refrain teachers, educators and volunteers from attending training sessions and, generally, discuss any issue occurring in the implementation as it is planned, and/or adjust the training sessions in accordance with any Sector decision. 3. Thanks to dialogue and exchange of information in coordination meetings, GVC monitors the demography in the targeted areas and liaise with other agencies in order to adjust target/content/way to implement the activities according to changes in the composition of population 4. GVC ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of the planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working. Plans of intervention are shared and discussed in order to increase acceptance. 5. GVC ensures directly the monitoring and evaluation of the activities, plan to do training of trainers using already existing material, in coordination with UNICEF and MoE and in line with guidelines provided in the RE working group meetings, thus getting GVC staff trained on the approved standards and material.
