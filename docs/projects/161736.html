Project Summary

The insecurity experienced by displaced and refugee children can have damaging physical, social and psychological consequences affecting their well-being and development. In contexts of forced displacement, parents and caregivers may have difficulties in caring adequately for their children when livelihoods options have diminished, and essential services are no longer operational. Parental distress greatly affects and impacts the well-being of their children. Changes in daily life and routine (such as school interruption), sudden and abrupt separation from family, friends and familiar places, as well as other child protection risks can greatly impact a child’s psychosocial well-being. Children who have been exposed to distressing events during conflict or displacement or who are victims of abuse, exploitation, neglect have a right to physical, psychological recovery and social reintegration in an environment that fosters the health, self-respect and dignity of the child. Therefore, it is essential for IOM support in coordination with other UN agencies to implement actions to preserve and improve the well-being of displaced children, by mainstreaming psychosocial support in all aspects of its work as well as implementing specific psychosocial support programme. The mitigation of immediate and long-term risks and consequences for the mental health and psychosocial well-being of individuals, families and communities is a core part of IOM mandate. This requires strong collaboration between specialists and sectors to ensure a holistic and child-cantered response that fosters the well-being of all children.

1 - Needs

Due to the broken familial structures, children are exposed to many risks and psychological distress which affect their well-being. The project aims to promote a more supportive environment that protects and promote their physical and mental well-being. In order to foster a protective environment, it is required to enable communities, families, with the necessary skills to protect children and increase the manpower capacity to provide specialized services for children as well as promoting positive parenting competencies. -The psychosocial approach ensures that the social considerations that affect the wellbeing of the population are properly addressed by the different actors by facilitating the access of the living standards that a culturally appropriate. There is a need to raise awareness on the psychosocial needs of the affected population that will facilitate transformation on access to the basic services such as food sustainability, water access, education, health care and other protection needs. Part of the mainstreaming and awareness is to empower the community about their rights, and information giving on available services such as accessing of legal documentations by use of the mobile teams. -The project is geared towards creating an enabling environment to promote recovery and resilience by promoting coping mechanisms of the affected population. This will be done through different community-based activities, livelihood support, and strengthening resources by recognizing the capacities of the communities. This will increase the ability of the care givers to transform resources into valuable activities.

2 - Activities

Activities Activity 1.1 Support the deployment of 120 psychosocial support mobile team members, including to newly accessible areas and areas of return in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa state. Activity 1.2 Provide culturally appropriate structured recreational activities for children, adolescents and caregivers, activities will be tailored according the age and gender categories Activity 1.3 Conduct informal education for adults and youths through psychosocial support mobile teams and facilitate access to formal education through referrals to education actors. Activity 1.4 Provide focused psychosocial support services through counseling and support groups for children, adolescents and caregivers provided by trained psychosocial support mobile team members. Activity 1.5 Provide training on MHPSS and child protection approach intervention for relevant stakeholders and partners involved in the field of mental health and psychosocial support. Develop guidance notes on MHPSS and CP, referencing the already existing materials of the sector. Activity 2.1 Support vulnerable persons including children and adolescents with increased access to specialized services and follow up for individuals suffering from mental disorders (through supporting referrals and treatment, through the deployment of governmental mental health care workers) Activity 2.1 Provide periodic trainings and weekly supervision for the psychosocial support mobile teams. Activity 3.1 Mental health and counseling services for care givers, vulnerable children, and families. Activity 4.1 Conduct awareness raising at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues within the community.
