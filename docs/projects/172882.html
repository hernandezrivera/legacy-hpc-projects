Project Summary

The seeks to provide life-saving and resilient WASH-IWRM, Education and Protection to 340,853 beneficiaries, 255,533 (WASH), 28,980 (Protection) and 59,060 (Education). IAS will target returnees, host communities, and those affected by conflict or recurring natural disasters in South Kordufan, South Darfur, West Darfur, Red Sea, Khartoum, and the Blue Nile. IAS will target vulnerable populations such as women, children, and children with disabilities, and the elderly considering gender-sensitive issues. IAS will utilize an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) approach. Activities to be implemented under WASH will include drilling of new boreholes equipped with hand pumps and construction of solar-powered Mini Water Yards. IAS, within this project will provide life-saving education services for 59,060 beneficiaries (52,000 indirect beneficiaries, 4340 direct beneficiaries), in Khartoum Um Bada, Jabal Awlya targeting 640 out of school and vulnerable children ( 160 in each locality), 40 teachers ,5 PTAs (30 members each) - Blue Nile, Altadamoun, Ed Damazine, Alrusayris, Wad almahi targeting 640 out of school and vulnerable children,5 PTAs (30 member in each), 40 teachers – west Darfur, Beida, Alginena, Sirba targeting 480 out of school and vulnerable children, 5 PTAs, 40 teachers -South Darfur, Beleil, Mershing, Nyala shamal, Um Dafoug targeting 640 out of school and vulnerable children, 5 PTAs(30 members each), 40 teachers -south Kordofan, Dilling, Habila, Al tadamoun targeting 480 out of school and vulnerable children, 5PTAs (30 members each), 40 teachers -Red Sea, beneficiaries and vulnerable children in Red Sea, Agig, Haya, Jubait Elmaadin, Tokor targeting 640 out of school and vulnerable children, 5 PTAs ( 30 in each), 40 teachers. Protection will be targeted in SK (Dilling, Habila, altadamoun) and BN (Altadamoun-BN, Eldamazien, Alrusyris, Wad Almahdi) localities; intervention will be through awareness sessions in PSS approach and provide PSS services to children with disabilities. For the affected population, including children with disabilities. IAS will implement activities aimed at addressing GBV. These will include construction of two Women centers in Dilling and Wad Almahi, targeting 1500 vulnerable women and girls and income generating projects informed by market surveys and needs identified through community consultations targeting 3500 beneficiaries, IAS will provide vocational training for 80 women and provide different services to 400 GBV survivors in the 7 localities in SK and BN. The project will conduct awareness sessions on Gender Based Violence at locality levels (1500), conduct public community awareness campaigns through radios, theatres and community meeting targeting 5000 people and strengthen the referral pathway (target 2). GBV activities will be implemented in Blue Nile and South Kordofan; Damazine, Ruseries Tadamon and Wadelmahi in Blue Nile and Dilling, Habila, Dalami, Al tadamon, Abujebeiha, Talodi, Alliri, Um Doreign, Algoz & Kaduguli in South Kordofan. IAS will also participate in state and national GBV coordination meetings. IAS will also mainstream protection, specifically to address the various challenges faced by children. The project will promote awareness on children's rights, provide referral services and also seek to strengthen the referral pathway. IAS will also participate in state and national coordination meetings. Assistive devices will be distributed to 350 children with disabilities in the 7 targeted localities. COVID 19 assessment will be conducted in the targeted localities to identify 800 vulnerable beneficiaries provided with COVID 19 hygiene kits (200 in each locality), including children with disabilities. IAS will mainstream peace building, gender and climate changes mitigation strategies in all the projects.

1 - Needs

In the Blue Nile (Adamazien, Alrosyris localitis 6,305 individuals in need of water, FSL, Protection, Education (IAS, Blue Nile Assessment, November 2020). 50,230 people in Beleil, Mershing, Kas, Gereida, east Jabal Mara are in need of WASH and education services. Furthermore, majority of IDPs had failed to re-establish their livelihoods such as Agricultural production, pastoral, and other income-generating crafts since being displaced six years ago. Food shortages and lack of access to seeds and tools, both IDPs and returnees do not have adequate income generation activities; in the WASH sector (IAS, South Darfur Assessment, Jan 2020). The Inter Agency Assessment report in West Darfur January 2020 revealed huge gaps in WASH including presence of open defecation and limited access to water for some communities and most hand pumps were not functioning. In another assessment, 66% of households in West Darfur face challenges of access to clean water and at least 26% of the respondents reported having no latrines in their homes. The fecal handling and handwashing practices were also poo (World Relief, April 2020). An inter-agency assessment in South Darfur in East Jabel Marra in November 2020 established huge needs in WASH; all localities are in urgent need of water and WASH facilities as many boreholes have broken down. Some households were using unprotected water sources, Schools did not have water facilities, most of the IDPs did not have toilets and practiced open defecation in and around their dwellings. Some areas in South Kordofan as still not accessible to aid workers and thousands of people from the SPLA N areas have no access to basic needs. According to the HNO HH overview 2021, only 35% in South Kordofan of the total population have access to drinking water from an improved source with safety distance without being sexually abused; in Red Sea, West Darfur, South Darfur, and Blue Nile 65% of the total population have no access to drinking improved water source. According to Education HNO 2021. Women and the aged are marginalized and prevented from assuming more influential roles in beneficiary societies. Needs in the education sector continue to increase mainly in SD and West Darfur. The most vulnerable children and adults are those with special needs. Children of displaced communities add pressure to existing education facilities that cannot accommodate new students due to limited space and resources. As a result, children are at risk of dropping out of school, becoming exposed to additional risks, violence, and child labor. Reporting of GBV is limited and the referral pathway are weak, in some instances nonexistent. In 2020, an additional million children experienced a prolonged absence from school due to COVID 19 school closures. HRP food security sector 2021 has identified 7,979,722 PIN, targeting 6,561,201 - 55,972 vulnerable, which need food assistance and lack of seeds and tools. The physical safety and psychosocial well-being of girls and boys affected by emergencies in Sudan remain significantly compromised due to the protracted exposure to violence, disruption of social support systems, increasing poverty, and the persistent lack of access to quality social services necessities. In many states, CBCPNs were established sometime in the past but didn’t receive appropriate training to enable them to identify and report child protection issues. In addition, Psychosocial distress is not identified and appropriately handled (South Kordofan SCI Needs Assessment, December 2019, & Blue Nile IAS Assessment, November 2020). In Khartoum, at the height of the floods emergency in 2020, over 100,000 people lost their shelter and livelihoods. There are huge protection needs in all the targeted localities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to our experience and lessons learned in the multi-year DFID funded IWRM projects in Darfur and eastern Sudan, IAS uses IWRM principles to enhance the environment in the design, implementation, and closing of our WASH humanitarian projects. For 2020, IAS will be implementing sand-dams using the community-involved catchment approach. The beneficiaries make up the catchment committees, and they will help determine while coordinating with WES the decisions governing all things related to the intervention, including environmental actions. Rather than continuing to use up existing precious water by drilling more boreholes, this approach guides rainwater (90% of which is normally lost) through the use of sand dams and other subsurface dams to replenish existing wells. The environmental impact for safe water provision is naturally very positive in terms of supporting healthier lifestyles, a better quality of lives, and supporting plants and agricultural projects, etc., but with the use of cement and construction materials, great care will be given to cleaning up after each project site; that each water point intervention area will be cleaner than before IAS commenced the project. Inclusive community/ main actors project design, initiation, and planning meetings will be the stage for environmental protection and planning advocacy. Beneficiaries will be tasked with developing a long-term environmental plan and will sign-off and be accountable to the authorities on their progress. For every water point, IAS will conduct Environment Impact Assessments (EIAs). Taking the data from these surveys, we educate targeted beneficiaries to take the necessary steps to improve the environment. Communities will be involved in cleaning up water points and latrine construction sites. Fences and shrubs will be set up around the area. Conservation of natural resources, especially water, will be central themes in WMC meetings throughout the project, including close-out activities, including inspection, ensuring that the environment activities were carried out and the environment enhancement plan was completed for the future each beneficiary community where IAS is intervening. As well as budgeting for water point maintenance needs, WMCs will be requested to work with the community to budget environmental enhancement activities. Finally, those receiving environmental advocacy and planning training will be encouraged to bring their knowledge into the local schools.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In emergency WASH responses, IAS will continue to facilitate meaningful participation and engagement of affected communities, including training and messaging throughout the program, including child protection needs assessment, response planning, and monitoring. In carrying out community-based protection services, IAS will pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner, conducting community safety audit and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. IAS will maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services, consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selecting services, and feedback after service provision. Partners will also be guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability boys and girls, children without adult care, ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to all communities' needs. Child Protection services and response interventions will continue to be based on a quality and integrated approach, including coordination and capacity building with other humanitarian actors to support Protection mainstreaming and a do-no-harm approach as well as principled response. Besides, IAS will build on and integrate, wherever possible, feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and support the interagency coordination mechanism to respond to relevant complaints. IAS will continue working closely with partners to monitor beneficiaries' feedback, share information, and conduct community consultations, ensuring adequate accountability to affected populations (AAP). Child Protection training for partners will often include sessions on AAP and Child Protection response. During the implementation of this project, IAS will ensure equal participation of all vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities and other vulnerable populations, in all decision-making processes concerning needs identification. As COVID 19 hits globally, IAS will ensure safeguarding the poor and vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities, through continuous massages that will be provided within all project implementation activities. During latrines rehabilitation, facilities will have enough lights to ensure girls' and children's safety during usage. During WASH activities implementation, IAS will position the water facilities in the appropriate location accessible to women and girls to ensure their safety from sexual exploitation and protect vulnerable from being trafficking and be subjected to join armed groups.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IAS will follow the WASH cluster approach by promoting the minimum commitments to safety and dignity for affected people, following the guidance of the Global WASH Cluster and the HCT. The minimum obligations are operationalized in the accountability framework developed by WASH partners, which guides partners to 1) provide the minimum information to the public, 2) involve the community in decision making, 3) learn from feedback and complaints, 4) encourage appropriate staff attitude and behaviors. 5) use information from project learning, and 6) develop programs based on participatory assessments. The WASH cluster will continue to guide partners in adopting the framework by sharing best practices and peer learning. The implementation of the framework will be monitored through a self-reporting mechanism, while the cluster will also advise partners on relevant indicators they can include in their monitoring efforts (including post-distribution monitoring). As part of its community-based response, IAS will engage and involve affected populations through, among others, establishing community-based protection networks and committees, including IDPs, women, and youth, to foster two- way communication and feedback with the humanitarian community and mobilize capacities toward the Protection of all members of the community, particularly the vulnerable. Community protection initiatives also involve the community's participation in raising awareness of crucial protection issues relevant to women, girls, boys, and men to prevent or resolve conflicts and foster peaceful co-existence. IAS will also establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, such as protection hotlines that provide information on services, suggestion boxes in child-friendly spaces, safe spaces for women, and regular trends analysis. Other accountability mechanisms include the Accountability and Impact Assessment Framework of the GBV Sub-Cluster, as well as Child Protection Minimum Standards, which supports accountability between partners and meaningful child participation in program implementation. Every WASH intervention will include SEA mechanisms.
