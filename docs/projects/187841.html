Project Summary

The protracted crisis in Syria has resulted in over 7 million children and over 213,000 teachers are in need of education assistance in Syria (note 1). At national level, the data reveals that 18% of children are out of school and half of the children who are not in school have never enrolled; these children face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor.
Thus, UNOPS proposes a project to support the increased access to education for the communities with high vulnerability in Idleb governorate, by rehabilitating public schools, providing school furniture and supplies, equipping the rehabilitated schools with renewable energy solutions, and delivering the related training.
It is expected that this project will directly contribute to the Strategic Objectives (SO 1: Available & Access: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non- formal education for crisis-affected children and youth and SO 2: Quality: Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth) of the Education Sector in the Syria HRP 2021.
The location was selected based on the accessibility and the severity of needs as per the HNO with all the localities being rated 4 on the severity scale. The communities of Idleb are all witnessing continuous influx of returnees, that is increasing the demand for basic services. As for the education infrastructure, Idleb has the highest ratio of classroom to student numbers (1:178), compared to the average ratio in Syria (1:54). In addition, over a third of schools operate in triple and quadruple shifts, as a way of addressing the overcrowding and physical distancing.
In addition, overcrowding is one of the critical issues to address under the school reopening framework (note 2) prepared by the Education Sector in Syria. The framework outlined some necessary measures to be taken prior to the reopening of schools, which include strengthening the existing protocols for physical distancing such as staggering the start and close of the school days, staggering feeding time, moving classes to temporary space or outdoors, and considering the possibility of additional classroom space.
Finally, in terms of children with disabilities, only half of the children with a reported health condition, injury, or disability reported school attendance, compared to 84% of children without these issues.
Through this intervention, it is expected that five public schools will be rehabilitated. The specific scope of work may include school rehabilitation, and provision of furniture and supplies and additional prefab classrooms. To fill the gaps in the shortage of electricity in schools, the rehabilitated schools will be also equipped with solar energy solutions through the procurement, installation and commissioning of photovoltaic (PV) hybrid systems (controller, solar panels, inverters, monitoring systems, mounting system etc.) and the provision of operations and maintenance training to technical staff in school.
UNOPS will support school-aged children via improved access to safe and protective spaces and provide equitable access to qualitative education services in the targeted areas, taking into consideration the inclusiveness for children with disabilities. This project will be designed and implemented, taking into account gender sensitivities with particular attention to girls and accessibility for children with reduced mobility i.e. privacy, access ramp, seated toilet and grab bar. It will comply with INEE, Syria Education Sector, and MoE standards.
Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be one of the key stakeholders of the project implementation in order to ensure community ownership and capacity development of communities for sustainability, which will also contribute for enhancing resilience and APP.

4 - Needs

After 11 years of crisis in Syria and the severe damage it caused to the infrastructure, rehabilitation of essential services infrastructure remains critical to Syria’s recovery. The education services remain highly prioritized and are a critical bridge between relief and early recovery. Supporting education services in a protracted crisis necessitates simultaneously addressing immediate needs and fostering coherent service delivery through a contiguum of emergency and early recovery approaches Given the context, this project will prioritize interventions that focus on vulnerable children in high severity scale areas (areas rated 4 on the severity scale), out-of-school and children with disabilities in Idleb Governorate. According to the meeting with the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) and Idleb Governorate in January 2022, UNOPS received a list of 70 schools in need of rehabilitation in Idleb, and built the proposal targeting the five schools in Khan Shekhon district of Idleb.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Exclusion of students with disabilities who can’t access schools during the project implementation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures 1) Schools will be improved to ensure ease of access to students with disabilities.2) Schools might be structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1) Facilities are assessed by UNOPS technical engineers to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. 2) Sufficient WASH facilities provided and maintained, gender and age segregated. 3) UNOPS has a dedicated Health, safety, social and environmental management (HSSE) Officer on the project site to supervise the aspect of HSSE.3) Students exposed to protection concerns during the rehabilitation and installation work in the project site. (Likelihood: Poor; Impact: High). Mitigation: 1) UNOPS will select schools in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the students when attending school. 2) The project implementation work will be carried out after/before the regular school time to the extent possible, for ensuring safety of students and teachers. 4) Communities/authorities prevent access to schools; and ii) checkpoints/barriers which delay delivery of services. Likelihood: Poor. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1- community engagement and outreach activities through an establishment of PTAs; and 2- protocols with authorities on rights of children.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment (SEAH) risks are present in the direct contact between UNOPS and it’s contractors’ staff and local community members (beneficiaries) during the rehabilitation works and installation of equipment. An on-site SEAH risk assessment will be conducted before initiating rehabilitation works and installations at schools and mitigation measures will be included in the project Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP). All UNOPS personnel will take a mandatory online PSEA training upon joining the project while contractor personnel working on the project will receive a PSEA training after having signed their contract with UNOPS. The contractor in turn shall train all workers engaged in the project from their side, as well as have them sign a Code of Conduct. Works contractors will also be responsible for conducting regular refresher sessions for their workers as well as for putting up PSEA awareness raising posters adapted to the local context on all work sites to facilitate reporting of cases. For this purpose, the posters shall include the referral pathway for reporting cases, i.e. the contact information and uptake channels of the project GRM. In addition, SEA clauses will be included in all UNOPS personnel and contractor/supplier contracts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Security situation in Syria is assessed by United Nations Department for Safety and Security (UNDSS) as permissive for UN operations for the duration of the project in the targeted governorates; Syrian authorities will ensure access to the working sites, and provide the necessary permission and clearance to UNOPS to receive, deliver and install the procured goods before project ends;COVID-19 situation does not deteriorate further. Risks:Political situation deteriorates, resulting in limitations to freedom of movement.Access restrictions are imposed, limiting ability to respond to the needs.Project implementation delays, mainly due to delays in obtaining the necessary approvals from the Government may lead to larger financial risks where personnel costs will be incurred ahead of the delivery.COVID-19 related restrictions on movements imposed by the Government.UNOPS will maintain operational readiness and position staff to support alternate activities, as required. Coordination will continue to ensure tasks are reprioritized in accordance with needs.UNOPS will maintain an active engagement with MoE and other relevant authorities and advocacy to access is granted.UNOPS has a robust internal control framework with internal and external oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities.UNOPS will continuously monitor the situation, keeping all stakeholders informed and will maintain close coordination with the Government of Syria.
