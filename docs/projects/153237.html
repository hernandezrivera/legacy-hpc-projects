Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to save lives and alleviate suffering through safe access to services and resources with dignity by targeting delivering quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to children of 0-59 months of age with SAM and MAM respectively including PLW with MAM in Duk County.
The county underwent through spreading conflict coupled with increase in severe food insecure households, high prevalence of morbidity and outbreak AWD related diseases; deterioration of economy and inflation that has contributed to worsening of nutrition situation in both IDPS and host communities in Duk County. This situation has resulted into GAM at 25.3 %, MAM at 23.4% and SAM at 6 % which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention (ACF SS report 2018).
Therefore, treatment of SAM for children below five years and MAM for children below five years as well as PLW continues to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk in Duk County.
The target groups for this project will be 22,830(Boys= 11,643 & Girls=11,187) children of 6-59 month of age and 3,997 PLW in Duk County.
This project intends to reach the target numbers of 1,583(M=798, F=785) SAM and 14,828(M=7,600, F=7,228) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively as well as 3,997 MAM cases for PLW.
The proposed project objectives will be 1) To deliver timely quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk, reaching at least 85% of SAM and 76% of MAM in girls and boys 6 59 months; 62% of PLW and 60% of elderly in need in Duk County; 2) To increased access to Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition programs preventing under nutrition among the most vulnerable and at risk, reaching at least 88% of PLW in need in Duk County; 3)To increased access to integrated Nutrition, Health, WASH and FSL activities in Duk County with critical levels of global acute malnutrition (GAM 25.3%).
The proposed activities will be: Treatment of SAM and MAM among under-five children (Target SAM 1,583(M=798, F=785) and (Target MAM 14,828(M=7600, F=7228)); Treatment of MAM among PLW (Target PLW MAM 14,828(M=7600, F=7228)); Training community health workers on creating and facilitating MIYCN education, information sessions, and mother support groups (Target 40 M=20 & F=20); Train health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition (Target 40 M=20 & F=20); Screening/testing and treatment of malaria and other illness (URTI, UTI) in OTPs among children with Severe acute malnutrition (Target 14,828(M=7600, F=7228); Treatment of children with vitamin A deficiency and infested with worms and participate NIDs (Target 23,000 M=11,600, F= 11,400); Provide WASH -NFIs supplies (soap, jerry cans) to the nutrition sites ( Target 1,583(M=798, F=785)); Establish kitchen garden at nutrition sites for demonstration (Target 7 sites); Linking with targeted distribution for livelihoods and GFD responses for malnourished children based on criteria for targeting including CMAM exits; Establish a community complains and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the 7 OTP/TSFP sites; Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the 7 OTP/TSFP sites

Needs

The overall food security situation in the country continues to deteriorate as a result of the protracted conflict, poor economic conditions, disruption of livelihoods and challenges in the delivery of humanitarian assistance. Farmers are facing new challenges in crop production which have adversely affected their ability to access food from own crop production. Climate change is a major element which has affected farmers with agricultural seasons being characterized by long mid season dry spells which lead to crop loss. Farmers are also unable to access nutritious high yielding seed varieties due to impassable roads and dysfunctional markets. Occasional violent inter clan conflict between the communities and Murle cattle raiders has also led to food insecurity due to temporary displacement which has seen farmers abandoning their fields during the peak of the agricultural season.Cultural values in the targeted areas are also a major determinant of child care and feeding practices. The targeted areas are inhabited by ethnic groups who are traditionally agro pastoralists. The majority of the community depends on subsistence crop farming for their livelihood activity. The main crops cultivated are sorghum, sesame, ground nuts and maize which lack dietary diversity. Due to its lengthy presence in the targeted areas, JDF has observed key social and cultural determinants of malnutrition children under the age of five years as: Cultural practices, such as food sharing, sending infants to the cattle camps, cultural beliefs towards pregnant women prohibiting them not to eat eggs, liver and wild animal meat since it has effect to unborn babies , introducing water to babies younger than 6 months old, and polygamy families has limited resources to look after large family due to economic hardships; lack of knowledge and understanding of nutrition and malnutrition; and the effects of hygiene practices and cultivation practices and dietary practices.In addition, there are limited financial investment for CMAM, IYCF and VAS by MoH and are 100% funded by donors with fragmented delivery of immunization and VAS since VAS is not prioritized with lack of government leadership and ownership of CMAM, IYCF and VAS campaign; lack of implementation of existing guidelines. Furthermore, hard to reach areas in Duk county have inadequate supplies to implement CMAM, IYCF and VAS campaign with in adequate consistent technical staffs for provision of CMAM, IYCF and VAS campaign associated with few of them trained on CMAM.There is also missed opportunity or lack of integration of services into nutrition screening at community level The spreading conflict coupled with increase in severe food insecure households, high prevalence of morbidity and outbreak AWD related diseases; deterioration of economy and inflation, has contributed to worsening of nutrition situation in both IDPS and host communities in Duk County. This situation has resulted in GAM at 25.3 %, MAM at 23.4% and SAM at 6 % which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention (ACF SS report 2018).Acute malnutrition is a life threatening condition that requires urgent treatment since most families have exhausted their resilience and nutrition related copying mechanisms resulting into girls, boys and women resorting to activities that put children and women at increased risk and relying on humanitarian assistance for their survival. If this is not supported, pregnant women will have adverse birth outcomes due to malnutrition. Therefore, treatment of SAM and MAM for children below five years continues to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk children and PLWs. In order to reduce high incidences of malnutrition related mortality and morbidity, SAM and MAM management will be complemented with preventive responses (BSFP and Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition) targeting vulnerable and at risk children living in Duk County.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The accountability to the affected people is essential ingredients for protection analysis for responsible use of power, combined with effective and quality programming that recognizes the community’s dignity, capacity, and ability for self-determination as well as strengthening accountability, investments in direct and sustained engagement with communities play a key role in securing protection outcomes. This in turn requires will a transparent and continuous two-way communication that captures records and relays the expressed risks, needs, capacities and aspirations of communities. Actions and approaches will enable communities to participate meaningfully in key decisions throughout the programme cycle: assessment and analysis, planning and design, resource mobilization, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.AAP will be streamlined in all staff work plans (without restricting AAP outputs to a specific job title or individual) and that senior management exercise AAP oversight.The project will taking into the account of a broad spectrum of the community that must be able to participate in decision-making in a way that is inclusive, non-discriminatory and has real influence in the humanitarian response; for communities to engage meaningfully and to make informed decisions, the project will establish two-way communication. Dialogue, furthermore, cannot be limited to the simple exchange of information but needs to contribute to programme design, management and delivery, pursued regularly and aligned with the different phases of the programme cycle; a broad spectrum of the community will have the opportunity to assess and, where feasible, alter or sanction the activities and decisions of humanitarian actors. Being accountable thus involves direct inquiry with communities on the quality of humanitarian action, its fairness and effectiveness; tailoring protection outcomes in accordance with the context, thereby avoiding ready-made approaches and generalizations; Empowering communities, as rights-holders rather than as beneficiaries of aid, to exercise their rights and comply with their duties; Recognizing and enabling marginalized community members to be represented and partake in meaningful participation in programme design and implementation; Assuring equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes.The project will conduct continuous assessments which will investigate gender specific differences and needs in the prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition. Information collected from assessments and performance reports will be dis aggregated by gender, so as to identify any unique needs to the gender. This information will be shared to beneficiaries through community meetings, Nutrition out reach activities and mother to mother support group sessions as well as through IYCF counseling sessions.The project consider gender mainstreaming to ensure there is gender equity, that boys, girls, men and women are fully involved in the implementation. Data collection will be segregated to allow analysis by gender to help inform better the trends, of malnutrition of different gender, hence been able to provide specific intervention to the more affected gender group.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The protection analysis emerging from a community- and rights-based approach that is underpinned by a comprehensive system of accountability will inform and shape the overall strategy, enable the design of appropriate preventive, responsive and environment-building interventions by protection actors. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, including as part of the Joint Needs Assessment, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms;The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context-appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centres; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian programmes
