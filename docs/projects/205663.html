Project Summary

Health Action Africa (HAA) planned to continue providing lifesaving WASH activities aimed at meeting life-threatening, severe needs of the population affected by floods, sub-national conflicts, high malnutrition rates, disease outbreaks, GBV threats and above all counties receiving high numbers of returnees Populations with limited WASH infrastructures. For the Year 2024 HRP, HAA will implement the projects in; Panyikang & Fashoda Counties, Upper Nile State. HAA already have static presence in these counties.
The project will support the provision of access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and aims to reduce gender-based violence-related issues. Since women and girls are the primary, direct users of WASH services, the project will contribute to GBV mitigation among the targeted population
The interventions will include but not limited to
• Provision of WASH Standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to parents /caretakers of children screened and confirmed with/ for SAM in the nutrition centers
• Provision of safe drinking water in the nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM
• Enable vulnerable affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate services
• gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers
• Provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design;
• Safe water provision through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children
• Hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns)
• Capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs)
• Distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community
The activities will include a detailed needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies of the affected population. The main priority is to provide emergency WASH necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH-related diseases, contributing to malnutrition and limited access to water. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected population by ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive and responsive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, including people living with disabilities, etc. With the involvement of our M&E department, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through an existing beneficiary feedback mechanism.

1. Needs

The needs analysis working group (NAWG) conducted an evidence-based and contextualized analysis of essential needs and reported that the underlying vulnerability of counties in severity level 4 (IPC) /PIN 2024 is Band 1 with an Indicator threshold (7.0 -7.9) indicating a higher severity score of the humanitarian conditions. , Panyikang & Fashoda Counties in Upper Nile State all fall under this categoryThe humanitarian situation remains dire and is still impacted by instability, economic stagnation, and fragility. Poverty is ubiquitous, exacerbated by conflict, displacement and external shocks. The food insecurity levels remain high due to the impacts of the economic crisis, flooding, low agricultural production, conflict, and insecurity in some areasAccording to Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022), the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. The report notes that covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, such as poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. People’s access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low in South Sudan. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and lack of accessibility to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions, with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E. According to OCHA 2023 Situation Overview, South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. As of 13 October 312,958 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since April, with Joda/Renk being the main PoE. Of these people, 89 per cent were South Sudanese returning to a country already hosting over 300,000 refugees and 2.3 million internally displaced persons, with limited livelihood opportunities. DTM /IoM estimates in Sept 2023 indicates, some of these Populations have already reached to Panyikang and Fashoda in Upper Nile State. The returnees Population continue to arrive on daily bases across these counties. This influx forces humanitarian partners to make difficult choices about how to address growing humanitarian needs with limited resources. There is widespread open defecation across these counties. Health Partners projected high increase in WASH induced Morbidities if Humanitarian assistance is not timely provided to these Populations. The Health Partners have reported outbreaks of Hepatitis E, Diarrhea & Measles. This HRP 2024 proposal is therefore designed to support the provision of access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and aims to reduce gender-based violence-related issues. Since women and girls are the primary, direct users of WASH services

2. Project activities

Health Action Africa (HAA) will intervene in Sectoral Objective 1, 2, 3 & 4, with the following activities: CLWSH/CO1: Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with NutritionThe key activities that will be implemented are; Provide WASH Standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to parents /caretakers of children screened and confirmed with/ for SAM in the nutrition centers , Provide safe drinking water in the nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM; Conduct treatment of water for safe consumption in the nutrition centers Provide sanitation services in nutrition facilities Provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC down to the household level; Ensuring small stock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent small stock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction; Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community; Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level.CLWSH/CO2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. The key activities include; Conduct household treatment of water for safe use Conduct hygiene promotion (hand washing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns); Provide capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs); Conduct distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community; Provide training to hygiene promoters; Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations; Create awareness on appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases;CLWSH/CO3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation. The project will Provide gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers; Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls after appropriate risk analysis; Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls after appropriate risk analysis. CLWSH/CO4: Community members’ exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure. The project will support the Rehabilitation & Construction of water points with flood-resilient design, support the rehabilitation and construction of latrines with flood –resilient designs and above all, the project will support disability inclusive sanitation facilities across the counties.
