Project Summary

According 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan indicated that there were about 4.9 million school-age children in Somalia, of whom more than 3 million were out of school. 1.4 million crisis-affected children, 705,615 of whom girls, and marginalized groups living under the most challenging circumstances in IDP settlements urgently needed education assistance in 2021. In 2021, Education Cluster partners targeted 374,973 (168,738 girls) crisis-affected school-aged children with education in emergencies assistance on firstly crisis-affected children enrolled in schools and at high risk of dropping out; and secondly crisis-affected school-aged children who are out of school mainly due to displacement. When the partners provided safe learning spaces that ensure safeguarding of children and promote cognitive and skills development to prepare children to be active and resilient members of their community.
SCC will work closely with the community to develop and implement a code of conduct for teachers and other education personnel that addresses sexual harassment, abuse and exploitation. SCC will use creative strategies to proactively recruit and retain women teachers (e.g., entry through classroom assistants programme, part-time positions). SCC will ensure that women teachers are equally able to participate in school meetings and professional development where possible, ensure that women teachers are placed in high-profile positions. SCC will provide special attention to children with disabilities (CWD) are often unable to access educational opportunities because of social stigma, inadequate facilities, trained teachers and poor infrastructure. This project will address to construct new 10 schools of 2 classrooms each and the rehabilitation of 5 schools in Kaxda & Daynile. A total of 20 latrines with gender sensitive with ramp and handrails will be rehabilitated in Kaxda and Daynile districts of Banadir Region. This project will reactivate SHF/SCC schools in Kaxda and Daynile. WASH facilities will be installed for each school in kaxda and Daynile of Banadir region. Each school will be constructed 4 gender-sensitive latrines (2 for girls and 2 for boys) with handwashing facilities and inclusive children with disability . Each classroom will be made CGI to measure 6x8 meters and provision of school furniture of 14 benches and 14 desks for each classroom. A total of 3,000 (1,650 Boys and 1,350 Girls) of school-age IDP children with children disability will have access to emergency school feeding programe providing one-time meal for five days a week for three months. A total of 3,000 (1,650 Boys and 1,350 Girls) will have access to safe drinking water and hygiene awareness sessions. The project will recruit and train 60 emergency teachers (40 males and 20 female) and a total of 105 CEC members (45 males and 60 females) will be engaged and trained in school management, child protection, and school safety for school improvement and retention. Community mobilization will be included in the project since COVID 19 pandemic has been affected in IDPs and community enlarge. A total of 3,000 (1,650 Boys and 1,350 Girls) benefiting from emergency teaching and learning materials, a total of 60 emergency teachers (40 males and 20 female) will be supported with emergency incentives for one year, 15 child protection focal points will selected from the schools and trained on psycho-social support and referral pathways. The project will recruit 2 supervisors to work for all 15 schools. His/her responsibilities include: Managing teachers performance and works, managing school schedules, Reporting to Project manger, evaluating performance and providing feedback, identifying and applying monitoring tools of SCC and helping to resolve issues and disputes during supervision. school employees and the connection to SCC office. A total of 150 adolescent girls will be received menstrual hygiene management training and sanitary kits.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

All SCC staff, newly recruited, field officers, and all other parts that are part of the project like CEC members, training facilitators, teachers, and contractors will sign the SCC SEA Code of Conduct (CoC) to mitigate the risk of SEA cases which may occur in any place any time. Any infringement of the code will result in the termination of the contract and other relevant charges. Through these networks, the risks to the project implementation will be identified through involvement by the project team, appropriate stakeholders that will include an evaluation of environmental and security factors, organizational culture, and the project management plan including the project scope. Careful attention will be given to the project deliverables, assumptions, constraints, cost/effort estimates, resource plan, and other key project documents. A risk Management Log will be generated and updated as needed and will be stored electronically and as hard copy printed on monthly basis in the project file. After identifying the risks SCC will assess the range of possible project outcomes and a matrix will be used to determine which risks are the top risks to pursue and respond to and which risks can be ignored as there is no big implication to the project outcomes. The probability and impact of occurrence for each identified risk will be assessed by the project manager, with input from the project team and SCC education team.
