Project Summary

To save lives and protect the safety and dignity of 43,333 vulnerable ind. affected by flooding or conflict in Pibor, Akobo, and Ayod Counties, CRS will provide in-kind and/or cash-based assistance to ensure access to life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter. CRS will target its response to assessed acute humanitarian needs for shelter materials or life-saving NFIs in communities affected by shocks especially conflict or flooding, where women, children, persons with disabilities and others with unique vulnerabilities face extremely elevate risks of malnutrition, disease, hunger, and rights violations including sexual violence. CRS will prioritize displaced or returnee populations, but may also consider inclusion of host community per demonstrated needs and context. CRS will adapt SNFI cluster vulnerability criteria to select participants, and may differentiate assistance per assessed HH needs. For 2025, based on ISNA data, SNFI cluster is targeting 27,271 ind. in Ayod, 23,463 in Akobo, and 45,684 in Pibor who will need SNFI assistance; CRS plans to reach 12,500 in Ayod, 12,500 in Akobo and 18,333 in Pibor. Individual Responses in each county will be based on acute assessments (preferably joint IRNA) in response to specific shock/displacement events. CRS will prioritize use of cash-based, voucher-based, or hybrid modalities to deliver NFIs or shelter materials, especially local materials, if rapid assessment demonstrates that markets are functional and accessible and cash is feasible/preferred option (most likely in Akobo). Where feasible, cash will be more efficient and cost effective, supporting local markets, promoting early recovery, and contributing to resilience. CRS will work to increase community capacity to implement DRR approaches and will apply a conflict sensitivity framework to ensure the project does no harm and reinforces efforts to build peace and resilience. Assistance will be tailored to each specific context/emergency, but CRS will likely focus on delivering (through either cash or in-kind modality) : 1) Standard or light Emergency Shelter Kits 2) Standard lifesaving NFI kits for newly displaced, 3) HH NFI replacement Kits for protracted displacements 4) shelter upgrade with local materials for protracted displacements. CRS will leverage SNFI Core Pipeline for in-kind materials; in case of challenges with the pipeline, CRS is well prepared to procure and deliver to all target locations. CRS will implement a comprehensive community engagement strategy, centered around active and passive feedback and community response mechanisms (FCRMs) customized to needs/preferences of diverse groups, to ensure strong AAP. CRS’ community engagement approaches will be integrated with a flexible and robust monitoring and evaluation system, including strong PDMs. The project will apply a conflict sensitive, gender-responsive “Do No Harm” approach, considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, girls, and PWD. CRS will ensure representation of youth, women and other vulnerable / marginalized groups in leadership structures. CRS has worked across greater Jonglei since independence, implementing SNFI, food assistance, livelihoods, nutrition, health, social cohesion, DRR, and conflict mitigation. CRS recently implemented BHA, EU, WFP programs in these counties that included SNFI distribution. CRS will implement 2025 WFP FLAs in Ayod, Pibor. CRS has established field bases to reach all areas of target counties, strong acceptance, and a stellar reputation with local communities and stakeholders; CRS leverages our local experience, reach, and expertise in this challenging context. CRS maintains robust systems to ensure accountability and program quality. CRS' established supply chain and logistics capacity, including field warehouses, ensures CRS can timely purchase and deliver required supplies and equipment. Staff, office space and vehicles are shared across programs to maximize cost-effectiveness.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in Ayod, Akobo, and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) remains severe, with crisis/emergency conditions driven by large numbers of IDPs and returnees, persistent inter-communal conflict, macro-economic challenges depleting purchasing power, four consecutive years of flooding, and limited agricultural production. Many displaced or affected are completely dependent on humanitarian assistance to access even the most basic life-saving shelter and NFIs. Vulnerable populations, especially those that have been displaced or isolated by floodwaters and destroyed roads, as well as returnees/refugees, face hunger alongside dramatically elevated risks of malnutrition and disease due to lack of adequate shelter, clothing, hygiene and cooking supplies, and essential household items; children, vulnerable adults such as PLW and PWD are disproportionately affected. For 2025, based on ISNA data, SNFI cluster is targeting 27,271 ind. in Ayod, 23,463 in Akobo, and 45,684 in Pibor who need SNFI assistance; CRS plans to reach 12,500 in Ayod, 12,500 in Akobo and 18,333 in Pibor. Individual Responses in each county will be based on acute assessments (preferably joint IRNA) in response to specific shock/displacement events. However the following indicates anticipated needs. Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor counties are historically under-developed, isolated, and seriously affected by insecurity and intercommunal conflict. Jonglei State and Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA) are among the most food insecure areas of South Sudan: approximately 60-80% of the population currently faces food security crisis or emergency (ICP3+), indicative of the extreme vulnerability and high level of humanitarian need in this area. IPC Projections for lean season 2025 (April-June 2025) indicate that Ayod, Akobo, and Pibor counties will experience emergency food security (IPC4) conditions. (IPC predictions released 18 Nov 2024). Given the high likelihood of conflict, flooding, drought, returns/displacement to impact food security in 2025, the severe degradation of livelihoods due to recurrent shocks over multiple years, and the poor macro-economic conditions (including steady inflation of food prices + devaluation of SSP), there is every reason to anticipate severe emergency and large-scale displacements again in 2025. Humanitarian needs are severely elevated due to recurrent, overlapping sub-national violent conflict and climate-related shocks, including extreme flooding. Akobo, Ayod and Pibor have been severely affected for 4 consecutive years: large areas were inundated, destroying community and household assets like homes, waterpoints, crops, food stores, seeds/tools, livestock, schools, and health facilities, cutting off some areas from all land/air access, and driving displacement. In 2024, flooding has affected estimated 1.4 Million people, including ~125,000 in Akobo/Ayod; with nearly 150,000 ind. displaced across Jonglei. (OCHA, 15 Nov). South Sudan has experienced decades of multi-level violent political conflict, inter-communal conflict over resources, and rivalries among ethnic groups. Routine revenge killings and cattle raiding displaces communities, forcing them to concentrate in smaller locations with limited access to shelter, NFIs, clean water and power, hygienic sanitation facilities, etc. Per the latest IOM data (DTM 14 -2023), Akobo was hosting 17,338 IDPs / 14,492 returnees; Pibor - 43,847 IDPs / 31,355 returnees; Ayod - 16,116 IDPs / 31,106 returnees. However, this figure is very low: CRS field staff collaborating with RRC officials report that in 2024 at least 10,000 more returnees have arrived in Ayod and at least 50,000 more in Akobo, due to Sudan crisis or conflict in Gambella Ethiopia. Widespread need for assistance will remain for the foreseeable future, and it is essential to sustain or scale-up humanitarian assistance to save lives, alleviate suffering, and ensure protection and dignity.

2. Project activities

To save lives and protect the safety and dignity of 43,333 vulnerable persons affected by displacement, flooding or conflict in Pibor, Akobo, and Ayod Counties, CRS will provide in-kind and/or cash-based assistance to ensure access to life-saving NFIs and emergency shelter. Based on acute assessments (preferably joint IRNA) in response to specific shock/displacement events, assistance will be tailored to each specific context/emergency. Priority Activities will include: 1) Needs Assessment, prioritizing cluster-endorsed tools and coordinated assessment with RRC and other local actors. Applying participatory methodology as feasible. 2) Market assessment, cash feasibility assessment, and rapid response analysis as needed in coordination with other actors. 3) Community Engagement and consultation process, 4) Establishment of FCRMs with community input 5) Community based targeting / registration; 6) Distribution of Standard Emergency Shelter Kits 7) Distribution of Standard lifesaving NFI kits for newly displaced persons; 8) Distribution of HH NFI replacement Kits for protracted displacements. 9) Sensitization on proper use of NFI and shelter materials, to include protection, hygiene and other key messages 10) Training for community members and committees on key DRR topics; 11) PDMs. Targeting is explained in project summary section. CRS will prioritize use of cash-based, voucher-based, or hybrid modalities to deliver NFIs and Shelter materials where rapid assessment demonstrates that markets are functional and accessible, and that cash is a feasible and preferred local option. Where feasible, cash will be more efficient and cost effective, and will support local markets while promoting early recovery and contributing to building resilience among beneficiary households and communities. In addition, CRS will work to increase community knowledge and capacity to implement Disaster Risk Reduction approaches, and will apply a conflict sensitivity framework to ensure the project does no harm and reinforces efforts to build peace and resilience. CRS will leverage the SNFI Core Pipeline for in-kind materials, but in case of challenges with this pipeline, CRS is well prepared to procure and deliver to all target locations as needed. The project will adhere to a “Do No Harm” approach while considering the unique vulnerabilities and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls.: CRS humanitarian programming centralizes protection and conflict sensitivity, while integrating robust PSEA measures. CRS will ensure this project promotes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and community members through the following measures: 1) CRS consults men, women, boys, girls from diverse groups and incorporates risk assessment which applies gender and conflict sensitivity lenses, including considering risks around GBV and intra-household conflict/violence. 2) Regular monitoring activities actively gather information about the experience of project participants and other community members during programming, providing the project team with data which is triangulated with field monitoring visits and feedback received through CFRMs, to understand any unintended consequences of the intervention and adapt programming accordingly. 3) CRS interventions commonly target women per vulnerability criteria, and provide services, interventions, and assets that are culturally appropriate for women and girls to utilize, own or govern. 4) All CRS staff as well as local authorities and community leaders trained on reporting / referral systems in place for GBV, code of conduct, free assistance they should expect, targeting/inclusion method. 5) Service points located to ensure vulnerable beneficiaries can access with safety and dignity, without traveling long distances. 6) Beneficiaries and community members sensitized, particularly during distributions, on the available CFRMs and how to report concerns.
