Project Summary

Through the proposed project, Oxfam plans to enable the most vulnerable families in the targeted subdistricts to have access to the essential non-food items (Winterization kit) Depending on local market functionality, people preferences, and availability of appropriate FSPs, distribution modality will be through cash, noting that Oxfam had performed market assessment in several communities in the proposed subdistrict and found that cash modality is preferable and feasible. The number of cash rounds will be given according to the CWG standards. According to the to NFI sector and HCT Cash Working Group recommendation in December 2021 , the value of monthly cash transfer will be USD 166 per family per installment. The cash transfers will be given for up to 2 months, through 2 installments.
Oxfam will target 14,000 HHs, at least 30% of which will be women headed households This will benefit a total of 70,000 individuals in need. The monthly distribution value will be given in Syrian Pound using the Central Bank preferential rate at the time of distribution. Oxfam will coordinate with other humanitarian actors to avoid overlapping in and ensure complementarity in geographical targeting, their coordination will be done through NFI Sector, CWG and even through bilateral meetings. Oxfam will make sure to engage the communities in the selection of the most vulnerable families. Therefore, Oxfam will form a community committee where at least 30 to 50% of participants will be women. The committees will be responsible of identifying the most vulnerable families through agreed criteria , the criteria will be clear to the community in order to avoid any misunderstanding and/or tension about the targeting. Oxfam will follow its internal procedures to do the verification process, impact and market prices will be monitored regularly.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 HNO, the overall estimated number of people requiring NFI support by the Sector for 2022 has reached 4.91M PiN, which is 4.4 % more increased than 2021 when it was 4.69 M PiN. The number of People in Need is expected to continue to increase to reach 5,05 M in 2023 especially under the economic downfall & C-19 impact. 70% of the HHs in need are facing more difficulties and decreased affordability to meet basic needs including clothing and other NFIs, particularly during harsh winter seasons, when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving. HNO 2022 analysis considers the unaffordability of NFIs as the main driver of need, with 67% of the population reporting that they are unable to afford NFIs available in the marketplace. Oxfam assessment found that the crisis-affected communities are in need for NFI support especially for IDPs & those returning to their home. Assessment’s findings show that cash-based assistance would be feasible in the targeted subdistricts. Cash modality is aligned with the targeted HH preference who already reported using markets as their source for purchasing food& NFIs. In addition, findings show that the market would be able to respond to an increased demand, traders have a good capacity to absorb the increase of demand by 100% &restock can be achieved in a short period of time. Besides, the assessed markets have maintained good supply of items from main source central markets and have enough stock to fulfill the needs of BFs.

5 - Implementation of activities

As Oxfam has competent staff in the hubs and based on their experience and needs assessment findings, Oxfam will do rabid need assessment before starting the activities implementation and will coordinate with other working agencies to avoid overlapping. The activities will be as follows: Community mobilization, sensitization, and committee’s formation. The project will be implemented in a manner that deepen connecting factors and minimize the dividing factors in the targeted communities. Identification, registration, and verification of most vulnerable HHs IDPs and Host Communities for cash-for-non-food transfer Assistance, as per the defined selection criteria. Up to 2 tranches of Cash for NFI transfers will be provided to 14,000 HHs through the 2-year project duration, with at least 30% female headed households, through licensed and vetted FSPs. Monthly price monitoring and analysis to measure the impact of cash injection on the prices of essential commodities and services will be conducted in the local markets. Oxfam MEAL team will perform daily DDMs during the whole distribution period. Also, Oxfam will measure the impact of the projects through PDMs in addition to the monitoring and evaluation of cash towards NFI, winter assistance.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEA by humanitarian workers and potential other beneficiaries: strict feedback mechanism, and all staff will be thoroughly inducted on protection and safeguarding issues and redlines. Oxfam conducts risks analysis at the beginning of the project cycle to identify PSA potential risks and put mitigation measures to ensure that safe programming approach and Oxfam safeguarding polices are considered and enforced in all the proposed activities and this risks analysis is updated during the project cycle to ensure if the identified SEA risks are managed and if new SEA risks are raised during the implementation phases. Oxfam Safeguarding focal points conducts SEA induction sessions for all staff, volunteers, implementing partners and all personnel deal with Oxfam (suppliers and service providers). During the project implementation MEAL and Gender teams conduct regular field visits to refer any SEA concerns to the safeguarding focal points in ethical and confidential way. Also, Oxfam establishes a help desk in the locations of activities to ensure a safe and accessible space for beneficiaries to raise their voices through the available feedback and complaint mechanism, all complaints as well as SEA are followed with confidentiality by the Safeguarding team under the direct supervision of the Country Director.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: – Risk of compromised impartiality and independence - Occurrence of gender-based violence -The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. -Potential of fraud and aid diversion,amongst Oxfam staff, FSP and partners -C19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. -Movements restrictions as C19 preventative measure by governmental authorities hamper the delivery of humanitarian assistance. Assumptions: -No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. -Security situation in target areas allow movement ability of Oxfam staff and volunteers to operate. -Oxfam is supported by relevant authorities to continue with the cash distribution Programme. -C19related risks can be sufficiently managed.Mitigation measures:Oxfam’ll ensure the humanitarian principles are in place through coordination with relevant humanitarian actors and GoS,Oxfam to ensure Gender considerations are in place during designing the projects and took all mitigation measures to prevent GBV risks,Oxfam to plan accordingly taking into consideration the fluctuation in the exchange rate with contingency plans ,Ensure all Oxfam staff&supplier have signed and committed to the code of conduct with awareness sessions
