Project Summary

SEMA plans to build on the previous projects in order to proceed in providing preventive and responsive CP services in 5 CFSs in Azaz, Afrin, Albab, Jisr Ash-shughur, and Idleb sub-districts including:
1-Structured PSS to the children(B&G) and youth (M&F) through using "Child Resilience" and “Youth Resilience” curriculums for children and youth to develop their self-resilience and social skills such as cultural and recreation skills.
2-Document and provide services for child survivals and at-risk cases including children with disabilities, where a supervision mechanism will be in place to support caseworkers to provide safe, ethical and competent case management services.
3-Providing emergency ECF to enable immediate response when it is needed.
4-Provision awareness-raising campaigns that will cover CP issues (Child labor, Family separation, dropping out of schools and dealing with CWDs, and the prevention and response to COVID-19) through outreach teams.
5-Supporting 4 Community Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in order to respond to violations affecting children. CPNs will be composed of youth and adults (M&F) including people with disabilities volunteering to protect children in their communities.
6-Providing rehabilitation program for children with disabilities in 3 locations in addition to parenting skills for CwDs parents.
7-Providing children under 8 years old with Early Child hood Developing program using IRC curricula “Ahlan Simsim and Reach Up and learn“ to enhance their social and emotional skills.
Moreover, SEMA will mobilize their Health, Nutrition, Physiotherapy, Specialized Mental Health services to build an internal referral pathway in order to contribute to a family strengthening approach.
Activities will be adapted according to COVID-19 consequent restriction of movements, and in line with (Guidance Note on GBV and CP Service Provision during the time of COVID-19) and (Ethical Closure Matrix), COVID-19 precautionary measures in line with WHO standards will be applied in face-to-face activities.
IN CASE OF EMERGENCY
SEMA will use one harmonized training kit developed by the protection cluster for the emergency response to make use of existing capacity and reduce duplication.
Each organization will implement an agreed mobile team structure. Mobile teams will target areas with high numbers of new IDPs and adapt locations based on the changing needs. Activities will include prevention and response services, which will be mainly (PFA, and dissemination of key messages on (CP), referral to protection or other services, high-risk cases provided with direct support service, provide specialized services, conduct rapid protection needs assessment. Coordination will take place with actors in Turkey and Syria at the district level. building on our capacity to provide integrated protection services. Each organization will work within their respective districts to provide coordination with other protection actors to ensure sufficient coverage of priority areas with high numbers of new arrivals. In addition, we will coordinate within our respective districts with non-protection actors to facilitate referrals, share information about protection services, and engage in joint activities such as distributions. SEMA will have dedicated focal points in Syria and Turkey.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023: More than 6 million children, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. The ongoing hostilities, protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services continue to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children and caregivers.Grave violations against children remain a major concern including risk of being killed, injured, recruited, used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. By September 2022, 1,895 grave violations were verified by the MRM in Syria.Insecurity and economic hardship exacerbate child protection concerns, fueling harmful coping mechanisms. Eighty-five per cent of people live in communities where child labour is reported . According to CP monitoring report ,86%of KI’s reported children in need of focused non-specialized services in Sharran /Afrin, while in Albab this reached 82%. 73%KI’s In Sharran and 68% KI’s in Albab reported Parents/caregivers in need of specialized services. 91% KI’s in both Sharran and Albab, reported children engaged in type of work prevent them from go to school.50% KI’s reported children living in CHH engaged in child labor in Albab. 76% Ki’s in Sharran, and 84%KI’s in Albab reported child living in HH with PWSN engaged in child Labor.41%KI’s in Sharran and 48% in Albab reported child being separated from their usual caregivers. *comment box.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented through 5 CFS and outreach teams (M&F) in Albab, Azaz, Idleb, Sharran, and Aljanoudeyh. PSS animators will provide structured PSS, ECD services, and parenting skills. The 5 CFS will be static, the outreach team will take each facility as a starting point for their activities in the surrounding communities. In each location, there will be 1 CP case management supervisor, three case specialists, and two outreach teams each one consisting of four outreach workers who will identify and refer the cases to the CFS and provide semi-structured PSS and awareness-raising sessions to (B, G, M, F) on CP issues and the prevention of Covid-19. One SNR specialist will provide Rehabilitation Program for Cwds in 3 locations. SEMA will ensure good access to female youth through the provision of transportation.CM Supervisors will conduct supervision to ensure the quality of Case management service and ensure that all emergency Case Fund documents are in line with CP Sub-Cluster guidelines. SEMA is committed to submitting the 4Ws on monthly basis as one of the important reporting tools. THE SEMA MEAL team will conduct different activities to monitor the work progress in each CFS including the following tools: Monitoring visits- Bimonthly, satisfaction surveys, FGDs, and pre-post-test of the training and PSS sessions. *‘Updated comment box.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SEMA ensures the privacy of the beneficiaries and vulnerable groups through adjusting and keeping the data of the beneficiaries secured, being responsible for the principle “Do-no-Harm” to the beneficiaries, and keeping their dignity. There is effective feedback and complaint mechanism and beneficiaries are made aware of how to access it. This mechanism includes developed practices and channels for reporting Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The concept of PSEA is also considered in the awareness-raising topics offered for children and adults to raise their awareness on their rights and safety and the safety measure to report such misconduct. IEC materials, banners, and posters will be available in all facilities and distribution points. Staff will be oriented in the contracting stage on SEA issues and will sign the CoC. Refresher training for the staff will be conducted on a regular basis. All complaints will be taken into consideration and will be investigated following SEMA’s investigation policies, Disciplinary measures will be taken against any breach of CoC.
