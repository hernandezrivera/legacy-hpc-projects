Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to address the immediate WASH related needs of the most vulnerable and isolated populations especially Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and the host communities living in vulnerable, hard to reach community sites in Jonglei and Upper Nile States.
Africa Development Aid (ADA) will target a total of 7,200 individual beneficiaries living in the two 2 Counties. It will address the urgent WASH needs of the vulnerable populations while empowering the community to become technically knowledgeable in WASH infrastructures as well as role models for community hygiene best practices.
The proposal is premised on the high prospects of peace holding in South Sudan in 2020, especially after the 2018 signing of the revitalized peace agreement between the Government and the opposition parties (IO, SOA, political detainees) plus the expected formation of Government of National Unity (GNU) in November 2019. Based on these assumptions, 2020 will witness arrival of returnees from various internal and external locations to their respective places of origin.
On-and-off years of civil war destroyed almost all sectors of the economy, including WASH infrastructure and management systems throughout war-ravaged communities. Fangak in Jonglei State and Panyikang in Upper Nile State are amongst the highly needy locations in terms of basic WASH facilities, yet they are expected to be epicenters of 2020 returnees from several PoCs in South Sudan and from immediate neighborhoods of The Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya and Uganda.
Mass returnee populations and IDPs in these two locations put serious strain on an already burdened WASH infrastructure and management systems, thus creating new WASH dynamics to handle. ADA intends to respond by improving and increasing access to safe drinking water. Besides, it proposes to enhance proper, culturally acceptable sanitation and hygiene practices that promote dignity for the targeted population in Fangak Jongolei State and Panyikang Upper Nile States.
ADA intends to conduct mass community mobilization and sensitization on community WASH concerns. Other activities include: training of water management committees; production and distribution of WASH IEC materials; construction or rehabilitation of community WASH facilities; provision of water purification kits; promotion of CLTS in communities; distribution of basic hygiene and MHH kits to schools and vulnerable groups; and other basic training in promoting community hygiene for safe, healthy and secure communities, as a central vision of Africa Development Aid.
ADA seeks to use WASH Cluster tools, management structure at different levels, partnerships, networks , logistical and technical backups as well as other agencies on the ground to create meaningful synergies in realizing the 2020 WASH HRP in the two States. ADA has the staff, experience, hardware and practical presence in the two States, thus creating a conducive environment for successful operations

1 - Needs

Since the outbreak of conflict in 2013 and protraction in 2016, large parts of South Sudan, in particular Upper Nile and Jonglei state, are facing food insecurity, malnutrition, and lack of safe and adequate water, inadequate health services and displacement of civilians according to HRP 2020 (OCHA). Despite efforts to bring peace, areas like Fangak and Panyikang have continued to witness pockets of conflicts thereby displacing more people across the counties. Besides displacement, the conflict has destroyed livelihoods, water supply infrastructures and services, health care systems and people’s way of life and farming practices. As a result, 5.7 million People have no access to safe drinking water and are in need of WASH assistance (WASH cluster 5W dashboard – August 2018) and nearly 2.6 million children are in need of water according to UNICEF South Sudan situation and response bulletin of 31st August 2018 and are likely to die of diarrhea each day. Across the counties (Fangak, Nasir, Ayod, Uror, Pigi/) and Panyikang most host communities and IDPs rely on hand pumps and contaminated water sources such as rivers, swamps and ponds. From the 15 hand pumps in Fangak county, only 11 are functional; 3 are functional out of 7 in Nasir, 22 are functional out of 28 hand pumps in Uror county, 7 functional out of 14 hand pumps in Ayod and no hand pumps exist in Pigi as per September 2018 IOM WASH and NFI inter-agency assessment report conducted in Diel. The functional hand pumps have increased strain due to added pressure from the IDPs on the several water points. These increases the time the hand pumps are continuously used without rest, without preventive maintenance and with lack of or no spare parts – leading to constant breakdowns. In most of the water points, water safety plans are not implemented and water safety chain is disrupted often resulting in contaminated and unsafe water that people continue to drink due to no better alternatives to drink from. None of the existing assessed boreholes, have operational management committees in place, which increases the risk for misuse/mismanagement and mechanical breakdown, and unsanitary conditions around the facilities except those targeted by ADA and other NGOs. Furthermore, long queues, distant water points have given people no better solution but to have alternative sources of water by fetching directly from the rivers, swamps or ponds without any form of treatment which highly exacerbate the risk of outbreak of water borne diseases. Clean, safe water has become a scarce resource and often the available one is of poor quality. Insufficient water means that personal hygiene needs are not always met which accelerates the spread of diseases. Poor sanitation facilities access and practices are one cause of water borne disease outbreaks. About 90% open defecation is the practice across the targeted counties. Feaces of children litter homesteads and smells from near-by bushes along the pathways are pronounced. Institutions like schools supported by NGOs, PHCC/U operated by NGOs and NGO compounds mostly have latrines. According to assessment conducted by DRC (the Danish Refugee Council) in December 2018; new displacements have been reported each day in Fangak with the new revitalized peace agreement seemingly encouraging those who had previously left the area to return, whilst IDPs from disparate areas were reported as relocating to the area due to the perceived safety and security available within the returnees from sites in Sudan. Access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene is poor in Fangak. There are no or limited number of functioning boreholes. Functioning ones are normally situated far from population centers. The facilities are shared by many villages leading to long queues and protection issues to women and girls. People widely practice open defecation and hygiene knowledge is limited. This condition has been worsened by long dry spells with drying up of swamps and ponds.

2 - Activities

In the given context and anticipated projection of 2020, ADA has prioritized the following activities to meet the needs of the people in Fangak in Jongolei State and Panyikang Uper Nile State.1. Stakeholders mobilization meeting to explain the project to targeted beneficiaries, its time frame and the exit strategy. 2. Repair and rehabilitation of identified water points. ADA will work through the village pump mechanics. These will be established at village level, trained and capacitated to carry out the repairs and rehabilitation of the boreholes. 3. Distribution of household water treatment and safe storage products. (PUR, Aqua tab, filter clothes and buckets). ADA will respond to emergencies and locations far from safe water points and depend on surface water sources like rivers and swamps by distributing PUR, filter clothes to render their water safe for drinking and buckets for safe collection and storage of the water so as to maintain safe water chain at the household level. 4. Water quality monitoring of the newly rehabilitated and repaired water points and at household level by using pool testing kit and those treated by PUR at household level will be monitored to ensure quality is not compromised. 5. Construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities at institutions: example schools and health facilities to enhance sanitation practices among children. ADA will work through school hygiene, feeding centers, market and churches to carry out hygiene promotion. It will promote construction of hand washing stations and practice of hand washing among the children. 6. Initiating and establishing community led total sanitation (CLTS) approach at the community level. ADA will provide support and training to community members to enable communities achieve open defecation free (ODF) status thereby making their communities and environment safe from fecal or oral route contamination. 7. Carry out Hygiene promotion. Through house to house visits, public sessions, hygiene campaigns; tools and approaches that are culturally acceptable ADA will be employ IEC materials that are translated into the local dialect. Awareness will be raised through community hygiene promoters (CHP) who will be trained and equipped by ADA to disseminate good sanitation and hygiene practices, Gender Based Violence (GBV) messages in targeted communities. 8. Distribution of WASH NFIs like soap for laundry and personal hygiene, hygiene kits to restore dignity of vulnerable women and girls. ADA will distribute soaps for laundry and personal hygiene according the sphere standards and hygiene kits to the most vulnerable people who will be targeted through set criteria of selection. These items will go a long way to improve people’s hygiene and restore dignity of women and girls in the community.9. Provision and distribution of menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits to school children and vulnerable children under adolescent age in the community.10. Visibility. (IEC) T-Shirts for water management committees, community hygiene promoters and school hygiene clubs, posters, banners and signposts. These will be written in the local dialect for the communities to understand and use the message practically.12. Formation and training of water management committees (WMC) The committee will have 7 members per hand pump with a ratio of 5 women to 2 men to manage or rehabilitate boreholes.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes. The beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized by taking into consideration the humanitarian principle of do not harm; avoid giving humanitarian assistance to beneficiaries when doing so exposes them to a danger or harm. Affected populations will be able to access WASH facilities when and where needed, and in conditions of safety and dignity. Everyone will be entitled to the full protection of rights available to the population in general. In order to ensure that the displaced and returnees are not deprived of their human rights, the local authority and the response teams are obligated to respect and provide special measures of protection and assistance to IDPs and returnees that correspond to their vulnerabilities in order to ensure that beneficiaries are treated equally with respect to non-displaced citizens. Beneficiaries rights will be legally recognized, respected and promoted through this project design in accordance to the United Nations Deceleration on Human Rights, international human rights treaties. Cluster /WASH due diligent guidelines will be used to mitigate risks associated with the project. Safety audit guidelines as well will be used to manage cases related to PSEA and protection concerns during implementation. In February 2017 South Sudan came up with a framework for returnees; providing special consideration to conflict-affected persons including children, women, widows, persons with disabilities, etc. in the provision of public service, including access to health, HLP and education services and granting the host communities the same protection and humanitarian services. The Cluster and ADA will be accountable to the beneficiaries and the donors to fulfill their mandate to provide WASH services on basis of need, by ensuring proper assessment and verification of need and faithfully delivering the services.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes. This project aims to meet the urgent needs of the displaced affected communities. The affected communities are the core target of this project. It will ensure equal access to all the affected populations. All the responses will be conducted through consultation with the affected communities and equal access will incorporate the principle of do no harm. All the affected population will have opportunity to contribute significantly to the implementation of the project. Prior information will be passed to the community on a potential date for project sensitization and mobilization on the project start, objectives, selection criteria and target beneficiaries, separation of responsibilities, project expectation or outcomes and exit strategy to ensure all vulnerable households and authorities are updated. However, responses on the ground will pay particular attention to the needs of the most vulnerable groups including women and girls, plus people with special needs. During the implementation the project team will engage with targeted beneficiaries to learn how the project meet respective their needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, ADA jointly work with the government mainly RRC/ROSS through project coordination and joint missions which are planned collectively aligned to the government priority plans. Prior to project inception or entry, project meetings for implementation will be conducted with the local authorities in the project areas, both at the State, County, Payam or Boma levels as safety audits measures for projects links with government plans. The communities in question were consulted prior to the project design phase through needs assessment and existing community feedback mechanisms and the interests, needs, aspirations and future goals were included into the programming and implementation plan. This project complements the many contributions of the national government, Jonglei and Upper Nile States and WASH cluster plans and objectives on the communities needs in Fangak and Panyikang Counties. It recognizes and will endeavor to work with existing network of local community structures; utilizing the support of local leadership and other partners such as ADA, ACF, PAH C&D and FCA in Fangak and Panyikang areas as well as the beneficiaries to ensure that only the most vulnerable populations receive the project inputs and skills. During the beneficiary selection stage, ADA will consult all partners with ongoing related projects in the counties, regarding them as the primary channel for sharing and crosschecking of information on targeted beneficiaries.The community members and people affected will be represented, engaged and encouraged to participate at all stages of the project and they will be facilitated to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance particular attention to the gender, age and diversity of those giving feedback. The capacity building of the village hand pump mechanics for sustainability of the existing hand pump repair will be selected through the recommendation of the line ministries .

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, this project is aligned to the national plan or/framework for returnees, IDPs and Refugees reintegration and it is consistent across clusters in the country. The humanitarian relief programming and coordination is regulated by the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission at the national, state and county levels in conjunction with UNOCHA. The WASH cluster and OCHA does the alignment and coordination with support of cluster state focal points to ensure that project goals, objectives and activities are in line with national objectives and that the implementation criteria does not deviate from planned cluster methodology. The project developed its goal and objective from the overall cluster general objectives which were developed in consultation and participation of national ministry of Water resources and Irrigation priorities for 2020. The project aligned these to its organizational strategic plans and priorities for Fangak and Panyikang counties. The WASH cluster consulted the refugees and returnees lead Agency -UNHCR to ensure the cluster priorities are well aligned to the national returnees/refugees framework.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the security situation of Fangak and Panyikang counties is currently stable as a result of reduced armed conflicts and the revitalized signed peace agreement between the warring parties, creating an enabling environment for participation and engagement in productive economic and social activities. Local authorities are part and parcel of the project design and will be the primary entry points for safety and security guarantees and clearance for both humanitarian workers and community members. The project aims at increasing access to safe water by rehabilitation and repair of existing water points. These rehabilitated boreholes will be located closer to the people reducing considerably the walking time for women and girls who are traditionally assigned the role. (Some people were reportedly walking up to 10 hours to fetch water in 2019!) This in turn will reduce the risk of harassment, including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). The new boreholes will in addition decongest the existing functional boreholes from long queues thereby promoting use of all- year-round safe sources of water by the people who would have been discouraged by the long queues and would have used unclean water from swamps and ponds. The focus of the project will be empowering the community members by involving them at every stage of the intervention and providing training to community structures especially village hand pump mechanics (VHPM), water management committees (WMC), community hygiene promoters (CHP) and school hygiene clubs that will be able to maintain and manage WASH infrastructure, sanitation and hygiene promotion in their locations. The linkage of the project to community sustainability systems: local technicians, local community and school hygiene promoters, locally available, in-country supplies promises sustainable solutions for local situations. Besides, the WASH intervention will continuously seek technical services from nearby Humanitarian Hubs for synergy.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, ADA is implementing a three-year project renewed annually, advocating towards WASH resilience approaches and ending emergency live saving responses with support from CARITAS GERMANY. Therefore, ADA will continue to monitor the project and statically support beneficiaries’ ongoing practices with coaching and mentor-ship especially of lead farmers to be able to provide continuous guidance to beneficiaries in their respective communities as needed after the project period ends. Other sector gaps that may be identified will be reported through different cluster forums with the capacity to respond to the specific needs. The one year period, in addition to ADA’s experience in Fangak and Nasir Counties and involvement of other partners, will help the team to identify and map other service providers, ensuring that all the gaps identified are reported to specific partners for further intervention as needed. In addition, ADA will apply its funding strategy to solicit for additional donor resources for these locations to ensure that vulnerable people that may be left out are included. The community and people affected will be represented, engaged and encouraged to participate at all stages of the project and they will be facilitated to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance, with particular attention to the gender, age and diversity of those giving feedback. The project team will stipulate to the community the exit strategy right from the beginning of the project implementation. The exit strategy will be planned with the stakeholders, engaging and encouraging them to participate at all stages of the project. They will be facilitated to provide feedback on their level of satisfaction with the quality and effectiveness of the assistance with particular attention to the inclusion of vulnerable persons, gender, age and diversity of those giving feedback.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ADA will conduct intensive mobilization and sensitization to reach out to all intended communities to ensure members of the most vulnerable community are included in consultations and/or humanitarian services/assistance. Use various channels of communication including through churches, markets, schools, hospitals, local authorities to ensure all members of the community in all project locations receives or hears the information. To factor in inclusiveness, fair representation and a participatory character, and above all jointly identify and mitigate risks, the project will establish a community committee comprising of all vulnerable categories of affected persons including the disable, elderly, youths, women, and girls. All the risks selected above will be discussed in the project consultation meetings and incorporated into decision making, implementation and monitoring. ADA will work with GBV partners on the ground for any psycho social and referral pathways support for GBV. Local community security systems will be consulted and alerted on issues of enforcing local security, especially for the most vulnerable. This will be coupled with public sensitizations on PSEA plus GBV matters. Public WASH facilities will be located in places where access is clear and within close distance to homesteads. Where applicable, rules will be set on when and not to fetch water, when security concerns are raised locally. Local complaints and compliments desks will be set, with possible suggestion options on how to improve WASH services in respective communities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Through stakeholder mobilization meetings, ADA will define and documents expectations from the most vulnerable members of the community. The expectations would include vulnerable people’s information needs and preferences for channel and means of communication with them. These will be processed into action points for follow-up during implementation, monitoring and feedbacks. Feedbacks will be obtained from the vulnerable people through direct group interviews, house-to-house surveys and key informant interviews. Project information for local consumption will be presented in local dialects and communications will basically be oral and in local languages – both for giving info ad receiving community feedbacks. ADA will ensure that its staff identify themselves to the beneficiaries and other stakeholders. ADA will provide information presented in languages, formats and media that appropriate for and accessible to and can be understood by the people and other stakeholders. ADA has a strategy for disseminating information to beneficiaries about the existing complaint and feedback mechanism. Monitoring and evaluation strategy examines how beneficiary’s feedback shaped the project delivery. ADA will conduct end line survey at the end of intervention to evaluate the project impact in terms of accountability, feedback, success, failure of the project and recommendations.
