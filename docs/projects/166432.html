Project Summary

In response to gaps in receiving general food assistance (GFA) resulting from barriers created by the COVID-19 pandemic, Relief International (RI) will support individuals and their families affected by the virus with complimentary food access, including those who are current and former ITC patients, and in home-quarantine in camps and host communities. As the ability of these persons to access GFA or livelihoods is inhibited due to self quarantine or separation from isolated breadwinner, RI will ensure provision of fresh nutritious foods continues to support recovery after discharge, assist family members while parents and guardians are isolated, and provide ongoing assistance for households undergoing self-isolation at home who are unable to leave their homes and access GFA. Moreover, to provide these food items RI will champion its existing supply chain of approved Bangladeshi host community farmers and petty traders to ensure continued financial resources flow to the local economy so heavily impacted by the country-wide lockdown. RI’s efforts will build upon its broad rapid response to COVID-19 here in Bangladesh. This includes RI’s operation of an Ukhiya-based, UNHCR-funded 144 bed Isolation and Treatment Center (ITC), and network of community health workers (CHW), mobile medical teams (MMT) and community protection volunteers (CPV) in the surrounding host community and camps 3, 5, 6 and 8W.
Once a Rohingya refugee or HC Bangladeshi has recovered at the RI ITC and is discharged, RI will support them and their family with a weekly complimentary fresh food basket (FFB) with nutritious fruits, vegetables, and other items. This will support the individual who is still weak, in need of complimentary food, and likely missed recent GFA, to strongly recover. This support will continue on a weekly basis for up to one month until they and their family are able to resume a relative level of stability.
Upon arrival at the RI ITC, RI will assess if new patients have left vulnerable family members unable to access GFA and in need of support at home. For refugee and host community cases, RI will relay this information to the relevant GFA partner (if applicable) and support the GFA transition from pick-up to home drop-off. If the household is not enrolled in GFA, or home drop-off is not available, RI will provide regular GFA to meet their needs while their head of household is absent. For the family members, primarily children, adolescent girls, women, elderly and disabled persons whose parent, spouse, relative or guardian is receiving treatment, RI will provide a weekly complimentary food basket. In cases where children are temporarily orphaned, such as those of female-headed households (FHH), RI will refer to CPVs to ensure continuous check-ins to support their wellbeing.
Lastly, RI will distribute weekly FFBs to Rohingya refugees and HC Bangladeshi households that are receiving home care, as they will likely make up the largest group of affected persons. This will primarily focus on female-, child- and elderly-headed households and households with a disabled person or pregnant or lactating woman (PLW). In the aforementioned catchment areas, RI MMT and CHW will be providing continued care and will be able to continuously assess the status of these individuals to ensure coverage by RI Food Security & Livelihoods Team (FSL).
As previously noted, RI will utilize its existing supply chain for this initiative established in the WFP Farmers Market Pilot program: Expanding Market Access for Rohingya Refugees in Camps 2E and 7. Through a preexisting list of vetted and approved farmers and traders who have demonstrated sufficient capacity to provide all necessary food items, RI will be able to quickly mobilize these individuals in compiling FFBs with a variety of fresh, nutritious food items. The integration of these communities to support one another's dire needs during this critical time will create mutual benefit and foster social cohesion.

Needs

As the humanitarian community prepares to respond to the looming COVID-19 pandemic which has gripped the world, primary focus - and rightfully so - has been centered on the medical response, with serious gaps emerging in how Rohingya refugees and host community Bangladeshis will maintain adequate food security. The primary concerns are that 1.) General food distribution (GFD) requires physical movement and contact - restricted practices in communities affected by the virus; and 2.) pickup is required by a household’s principle or alternate member who now, according to age, health and social realities, will be the most likely to be affected by the virus and isolated from their families while they receive treatment. This means that the ability to collect GFD for households who either have a family member undergoing treatment, or themselves are in self-quarantine, will be greatly reduced or even fully stalled. Such a disruption in food security will be especially devastating for children, adolescent girls and women who are vulnerable to being taken advantage of with the removal and isolation of their parents, spouses, relatives or guardians due to the virus. Households that are female-, child- and elderly-headed or include a disabled person or pregnant or lactating woman (PLW) are already extremely vulnerable to food insecurity. As a result, the removal of principle and/or alternate individuals, or designation of the household as a whole for self-quarantine will put further strain on an already fragile situation. For the humanitarian community and the government of Bangladesh to avoid increasing local transmission of the virus and ensure its impact does not inhibit Rohingya refugees or Bangladeshis in accessing critical food, efforts must be made to adjust to the new realities that the virus and its treatment will have. Coordination:RI has consulted with WFP and the Food Security Sector in designing this approach to avoid duplication of coverage, and will ensure constant communication to coordinate with existing GFA efforts for target households. While the status of many households in accessing food assistance - both within camps and the host community - remains unclear, RI will be prepared for all circumstances to support those impacted by COVID-19. As RI’s ITC will support patients from multiple, non-RI catchment areas, RI will be flexible to modify and expand programming where necessary to provide assistance.

Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Provide weekly complimentary fresh food basket (FFB) for one month to Rohingya Refugees and host community Bangladeshis that have recovered at the RI ITC and are discharged Output 1: Improved resilience and hastened societal reintegration through nutritious food support to newly recovered, former COVID-19 patients weakened by virus in need of assistance and unable to access GFA provided in their absence. Activity 2: Provide weekly complimentary FFB for Rohingya refugee and host community Bangladeshi children and disabled persons in their homes, whose parents or guardians are receiving treatment, primarily concerning female-, child- and elderly-headed households and households with a disabled person or pregnant or lactating woman (PLW), who therefore may be unable to fully access GFA. These households will also be referred to CPVs to frequently assess well being and potential protection concerns. Output 2: Continued food assistance and safety of children and other vulnerable family members separated from their parents or guardians and unable to access GFA. Activity 3: Provide weekly complimentary FFB for Rohingya refugee and host community Bangladeshi individuals and families with 1) mild COVID-19 symptoms in self-quarantine and are receiving home care through RI MMTs and CHWs; or 2) were potentially exposed and therefore designated to self-quarantine, with special focus on female-, child- and elderly-headed households and households with a disabled person or pregnant or lactating woman (PLW). Output 3: Continued food assistance and reduced risk of locally transmissitting virus for households unable to leave their home to access GFA due to COVID-19 concerns. As RI has been working over the course of the past six months with the farmers and traders who will be sourced to compile the FFBs, each has already been enrolled in mobile banking with The City Bank, ensuring all payments are mobile and do not risk virus transmission. RI will ensure all measures are taken to ensure the beneficiary and staff safety during this process, maintaining safe and sanitary handling of food items to compile FFBs, while supporting all staff with adequate training and personal protective equipment (i.e. masks, gloves, etc.) when interacting with beneficiaries. Coordination and Gap-Filling:For households in Activities 2 and 3 who will be unable to access their GFA pickup either due to absence of the principle and alternate member receiving isolated treatment, or have been designated for self-quarantine at home as a household, RI will refer these individuals to WFP and the relevant GFA partner in that catchment to adjust their GFA provision method. As the status of home-based delivery of GFA for these individuals unable to access e-voucher or in-kind distribution outlets remains unclear during the pandemic, RI will be flexible and prepared to either 1) assist the relevant partner in transporting the GFA items to the household itself; or 2) provide intermediate coverage in place of GFA through provision of FFBs. This concept applies to host communities as well, where new programming is supporting food assistance for households, but may not be oriented towards households in self-quarantine or who are missing parents or guardians as a result of treatment.

(Target: 80%) Accountability to Affected Populations:RI will maintain accountability to affected populations by ensuring that each aspect of the program adequately meets beneficiary needs and priorities in an effective and dignified manner. Given the unique context of the pandemic, many standard tools of community engagement and feedback mechanisms will not apply, and therefore all RI engagement with communities will occur with proper safety precautions to avoid putting the health of beneficiaries at risk. Upon program mobilization, RI will coordinate with WFP on determining the appropriate composition of the FFB, ensuring sufficient diversity of nutritious food items in line with sector standards. Simultaneously, RI will conduct a rapid assessment through KIIs with beneficiaries from each gender and age group to ensure the items planned for distribution are both desirable and can be prepared with standard household resources. During implementation, staff and volunteers taking part in distributions will receive protection training from RI Child and SGBV Protection teams. This will include how to identify potential protection concerns at the household level in need of referral, and how to communicate with vulnerable populations. To ensure safety, RI will maintain that only female staff and volunteers interact with FHH and child-headed HH during door-to-door engagement to support beneficiaries’ safety and wellbeing. Moreover, any such households will receive regular visits from RI CPVs, increasing the ability to better engage the specific needs of these groups. Each FFB will include an information card describing the goods and will include a hotline number which can be reached to voice complaints or provide feedback. This not only includes issues concerning quality and safety of food, but also the ability to report complaints such as harassment or abuse which may have occurred during distribution. RI will conduct post-distribution monitoring (PDM) throughout the program to assess progress against indicators and receive feedback to improve implementation. Indicators will measure the status, impact and quality of the FFB distribution, assessing if the correct quantity is being delivered, whether or not they are having a positive effect on individuals’ health, and if the goods provided are in line with beneficiary preferences or need to be adjusted. RI data collectors or CHWs will follow up with all households receiving distribution, ensuring all individuals unable to provide feedback through other mechanisms can do so. Based on best practices currently used by RI Health, Child Protection and SGBV Protection teams, RI will employ age-appropriate methods to gather inputs from individuals of all ages and gender. Trained data collectors practicing safe distancing will gather information in a culturally appropriate and gender sensitive manner, ensuring feedback is solicited effectively through language barriers or illiteracy. KIIs will also occur for Bangladeshi host community farmers and traders as well to solicit feedback and gauge their level of satisfaction in participation in order to adjust programming where necessary.
