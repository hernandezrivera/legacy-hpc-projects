Project Summary

According to HNRP 2024, an estimated 9 million South Sudanese will need humanitarian assistance due to critical needs. The acute food insecurity and malnutrition crisis is worsening, exacerbated by economic instability, climatic shocks such as widespread flooding (a 10% increase since 2023), and protracted conflicts and insecurity. The influx of returnees and refugees from the Sudan conflict further exacerbates the situation, straining an already fragile country. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people faced Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) levels of acute food insecurity. Of these, 1.71 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels, and 41,000 faced Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) levels. The Catastrophe-level population includes 10,000 residents of Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and approximately 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan's ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this represents a 500,000-person increase in the number of people facing Crisis or worse (latest published IPC report 2024).
The PIN 2024 indicates that an estimated 5.01 million individuals in the country require WASH services. Of these, the WASH Cluster has identified 2.25 million people as their primary target population for humanitarian WASH assistance. These individuals are primarily located in four states: Unity, Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Northern Bahr El Ghazal, which are frequently affected by communal violence, natural disasters (primarily floods), and food insecurity.
According to IRNA findings from Mayendit and Rubkona (August 2024), many households have been displaced to higher ground due to recent floods. It was established that 3,659 households in 158 villages in Mayendit were displaced by the flood as of August 31st, 2024. According to a CH assessment conducted on September 27th, 2024, in Rubkona County, over 500 households in Rubkona/Bentiu Payam had been displaced. The flood situation has been worsened by returnees’ influx from Sudan estimated at over 44,000 individuals who have settled in the main town of Bentiu/Rubkona Payam with other sheltering at POC. The displaced households reported the lack of shelter, access to sanitation services, and essential household items. In Mayendit, 72% of displaced households were not treating their water due to a lack of treatment chemicals. Hand-washing practices are low, and 91% practice open defecation. In Leer, most households rely on contaminated water sources as boreholes were submerged. Additionally, hygiene remains a significant issue, with the majority of the community practicing open defecation (Leer IRNA report, September 2024). The influx of flood-displaced populations and returnees has also put pressure on existing facilities, including shelters, health facilities, sanitation facilities, and water resources, further exposing households to vulnerabilities across the priority 1&2 counties in the greater upper Nile states.
Coalition for Humanity has a static presence in the four states and proposes to reach a total of 332,100 people through the following integrated WASH and nutrition interventions to save lives:
1. Construction of flood-resilient latrines in IDP camps, returnee holding sites, and nutrition facilities to ensure durability, safety, and easy access for all, especially women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities (basic level of service)
2. Drilling and solarization of boreholes in communities and nutrition facilities to improve access to safe and clean water within a 60-minute collection time.
3. Conduct hygiene promotion activities in IDP sites, nutrition facilities, and communities. This will ensure hand washing at critical times with running water and soap (basic service level)
4. Provision of WASH standard kits to SAM and MAM caregivers.
5. Distribution of lifesaving WASH kits to affected populations.
6. Conduct safety audits with women and girls before selecting water points and latrine sites.

2. Project activities

Coalition for Humanity has identified the following activities to address the needs of affected populations in greater upper Nile.1. Construction of Flood-Resilient Latrines: CH will construct flood-resilient latrines in IDP camps, returnee holding sites, and nutrition facilities to ensure durability, safety, and easy access for all, especially women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities (Basic level of service).2. Borehole Drilling and Solarization: CH will drill and solarize boreholes in communities and nutrition facilities to improve access to safe and clean water within a 60-minute collection time.3. Hygiene Promotion: Conduct hygiene promotion activities in IDP sites, nutrition facilities, and communities. This will ensure hand washing at critical times with running water and soap (basic level of service)4. WASH Standard Kits Provision: Provide WASH standard kits to Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) caregivers.5. WASH Kit Distribution: Distribute WASH kits to affected populations.6. Safety Audits: Conduct safety audits before selecting water points and latrine sites to ensure the safety of women and girls
