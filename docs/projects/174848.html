Project Summary

Whilst humanitarian efforts are striving to provide help to IDPs and supporting temporal learning, the global crisis of COVID-19 in Nigeria has again affected the entire education system leading it to a complete shut down and forcing children to stay home. global crisis which poses immediate threats to children’s rights to survival, development, learning, protection, and participation. With very few social safety mechanisms provided for marginalised girls in IDPs and rural communities, more children (girls especially) are exposed to high risk of violence,abuse and exploitation within their communities which has negative effects on their physical and psychosocial wellbeing.
The impact of the crisis on the education system has been severe, leaving generations of children without opportunities to learn and even more vulnerable. Since the conflict erupted in 2009, 611 teachers have been killed, 19,000 teachers displaced, 910 schools damaged or destroyed, and more than 1,500 schools forced to close. As a result, an estimated 900,000 children have lost access to learning while 75 percent of children in camps do not attend school. 70 percent of girls of primary school age are out of school in Borno State - the highest percentage in the country. Of those who do attend, 72 per cent are unable to read upon completion of grade six.The further impact of the Nationally mandated school closures in March of 2020 compounded this crisis, further complicating access to education, learning and psychosocial support, among other key services provided to children through schooling.
Children living in host communities and IDPs camps are exposed to various forms of abuse such as child labor, survivor sex, abuse and exploitation as they fend for themselves during this pandemic. Thousands trapped in conflict zones and hard to reach LGAs faced high risks to their survival, development, learning and protection. The increased risk of early and unwanted pregnancy, forced/early marriage and threats to health and nutrition due to reduced access to education and learning as a result of school closures has increased the exposure of most families into poverty due to limited available opportunities for livelihoods, and socio-economic activities. This heightens the exposure to protection risks for children in homes, IPDs, and host communities.The closure of schools according to the Education sector in the North-East has affected close to 46 million students throughout the country. 4.2 million students in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States in North East Nigeria, adding to the already existing 2.2 million out of school children recorded by the sector. With the existing challenges children faced in accessing education in the northeast, about 400,000 IDP children who were attending some form of learning in the camps and host communities have already been affected by the closure of schools.
Unless mitigated, the pandemic risks undermining progress made on achieving the Sustainable Development Goals and puts an entire generation of children at risk of not fulfilling their potential. Currently there is a unique opportunity to reach conflict-affected children in IDP camps and host communities with education services through distance-based learning modalities. Leveraging the momentum of investments into radio learning made under Covid-19 school closures.
The need to expand Temporal Learning Structures in safe and affected LGAs, Provide teachers with monthly incentives for teaching, Strengthen teachers’ capacities in psycho-social support, effective pedagogy and inclusive education, mines risk education and detection and referral of children with disabilities as well as COVID-19 prevention/response measures will help towards the expansion and improvement of basic education for all.

1 - Needs

The present need for protective and inclusive Education in the Northeast falls within the Nigeria Education Sector2 COVID-19 Response Strategy in North East that seeks to support distance-based learning modalities which expanded under Covid-19 school closures such as radio learning, tablet-based trainings, pre-loaded USB keys for both hard to reach areas and children who are conventionally otherwise occupied during lessons airing times (girls).This is also supported by the Addendum on Covid-19 Response Plan 2020 Borno Adamawa and Yobe States Nigeria-Northeast,. The experience of managing outbreaks with droplet transmission is not strong in the Northeast Nigeria and the strain on health-care facilities, overwhelmed by capacity constraints, underlines the need for rapid actionThe Federal Ministry of Education guidelines for schools and learning facilities after COVID-19 Pandemic closures further outlining measures needed to ensure safe return to schools across all states in Nigeria. The Global Humanitarian Response Plan for COVID-19 lists conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe as high risk, vulnerable populations, and the multi-year Buhari Plan, the Presidential Committee on the North East Initiative (PCNI), the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for North East Nigeria (often referred to as the Durable Solutions Strategy), and three volumes of the UN-EU-World Bank Recovery and Peace Building Assessments are key strides that have been taken in this direction to bridge the gap.

2 - Activities

TPO will complement the activities undertaken in the target LGAs under the First Emergency Response for Covid-19. The situation has changed as the schools are re-opening and it is imperative to provide protective learning environment for the children; This will be done through following key interventions; Rehabilitation of 52 Temporary Learning Spaces/ classrooms Establishment of 8 blocks of gender segregated latrines (children with disabilities friendly)Payment of incentives for 240 teachersTraining of 240 teachers on PSS, effective pedagogy, mine risk education, inclusive education, covid-19 prevention and mitigation measures and detection and referral of children need case management services. Distribution of 500 PPEs to 52 schools. TPO will continue the engagement of 9 (5 males, 4 females) referral focal persons (RFPs) across proposed project locations in two deprived LGAs in Borno state of Nigeria. These volunteers were recruited to facilitate referrals between education authorities, schools and school communities and health and other social services like protection and to also provide psychosocial support to children. This will strengthen case management and referrals for cases that require medical, legal aid,Nutrition and those in need of psychosocial support. The volunteer’s referral focal persons shall be trained on child safeguarding and how to provide psychosocial support to the children to mitigate the psychological impact of COVID -19 on children especially during the school closure period. Mentoring sessions will be arranged for teachers and mentoring support will be provided through mentor teachers, composed of LGA officials and representatives from State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), who will be trained on coaching and constructive observation and feedback.TPO shall also collaborate with the Mine Action Sub Sector to train teachers on Mine risks education in the two LGAs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project will ensure all four key protection principles of protection,Best interest, Participation and Do-no-harm are monitored during implementation. This will be done by defining who is at risk, what vulnerabilities and capacities do learners, communities and stakeholders have relative to the threats, ways in which the project has been adapted in response to these, strengthening referral mechanisms and provide specialist support in preventing or recovering from violence and exploitation. Existing protection structures at community level shall be leveraged on in ensuring children's protection and enhance learning.
