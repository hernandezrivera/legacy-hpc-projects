Project Summary

This intervention provides group information and individual counselling, legal assistance and capacity building to refugees and vulnerable host community members on housing, land and property rights and responsibilities and collaborative dispute resolution options.

Needs

Housing Land and Property: In a formal needs assessment conducted in late 2018 on tenancy practices in the Ukhiya and Teknaf camps, an overwhelming number of respondents indicated that they pay rent for the shelter they reside in, and that rental payments, rent extortion, and the threat of forced evictions are serious concerns for them. NRC will collaborate with the IOM Shelter/NFI HLP focal point to conduct a more formal mapping of land tenure and collaborate with protection sector actors, including UNHCR and IOM, to understand the dynamics of land conflicts and existing collaborative dispute resolution options before commencing ICLA activities around HLP, including collaborative dispute resolution. Gender, age and diversity: ICLA information sessions are held separately for men and women, with women's sessions held in a range of locations that are determined in collaboration with site management, the camp protection focal point, and women themselves. Locations include women and girls safe spaces and private women's shelters. Special consideration is made for women with mobility limitations when identifying information and counselling locations. Men's sessions are held in existing multipurpose spaces, but ICLA staff conduct house to house visits prior to the session to see if there are would-be-participants who cannot visit the multipurpose space for mobility or other reasons. Individuals unable to visit the multipurpose spaces receive individual information and subsequent counselling sessions.

Activities or outputs

1. Information sessions for refugees and host community members on HLP rights, tenancy rights and responsibilities, and collaborative dispute resolution options 2. Individual counselling sessions for refugee and host community individuals on HLP rights and collaborative dispute resolution options 3. Legal assistance to refugees and vulnerable host community members in resolving HLP disputes. 4. Capacity building of justice duty bearers, including CiCs, mahjis, and imams on dispute resolution using existing mediation structures. Conflict sensitivity/do no harm: Prior to the commencement of any HLP related activities, NRC will engage in conflict analysis to understand the dynamics around tenancy practices, to understand what the risks are, and to seek the input of a range of stakeholders (CiC, UNHCR, IOM, site management, protection focal points, Shelter/NFI). NRC will also ensure that HLP CDR practices take advantage of existing dispute resolution mechanisms in the camps and do not create parallel channels. DRR: NRC ICLA will include relevant DRR messaging in information sessions, which include an overview of camp services. NRC's emergency response plan includes the release of ICLA field staff from their ordinary duties to assist other humanitarian agencies in a disaster response. AAP: NRC will maintain a hotline for complaints and feedback, as well as a physical box in camp locations where beneficiaries may register complaints if they are unable to use the phone. Social cohesion: NRC HLP activities support the resolution of tenancy and land conflicts, which typically involve refugees and host community members---often with host community members as (legal or illegal) landlords and refugees as tenants. NRC will also work with the HLP Taskforce to identify structural solutions to land conflicts to reduce land tensions and improve the ability of refugees and host community members to coexist. Sustainable outcomes: NRC ICLA programming seeks to build the capacity of both beneficiaries and local authorities to handle issues in the future without NRC support. NRC information and counselling sessions are designed to give beneficiaries in both the refugee and host communities the knowledge to resolve issues themselves, and to pass this knowledge onto family and community members, with legal assistance provided only in instances where beneficiaries are not able to act on their own. Capacity building of local community leaders (mahjis, imams, community governance leaders) is intended to equip them with knowledge about dispute resolution options for the future as well. Use of existing camp facilities: NRC has not acquired any of its own facilities in camps. Instead, all services are conducted in existing camp facilities, such as multipurpose spaces, women's and girl's safe spaces, and private shelters. NRC staff are seeking permanent desk space inside of shared facilities that will be staffed as a legal help desk during working hours. Coordination with authorities, local partners and civil society: When beginning ICLA activities in a camp, NRC staff work closely with the CiC office, site management, and protection focal points (to date this has been entirely in IOM camps, but will include UNHCR camps in the future) to understand issues in that camp so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs. In addition to working with the CiC offices, at the request of IOM NRC is providing capacity building support to Pulse, who provide legal assistance to trafficking victims. Inter-sectoral linkages and collaboration: NRC works closely with the Shelter/NFI sector on planning for HLP interventions, and will participate in an HLP Taskforce once one is established. PSEA: NRC is active in the PSEA Network. NRC has also engaged the PSEA Network to train camp volunteers.
