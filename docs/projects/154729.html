Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Iraq is entering a new phase where the people especially the most vulnerable one are suffering the effects of war. Education and Children Rights remain a critical need considering that still there are 3.3 million people affected. Many families continue to face challenges which prevent them from returning in their homes, they fear safety and security and lacking services in their areas of origin. Many of these families chose to remain in camps and will continue to need support under the camp consolidation strategy. Al Mortaqa aim to improve the Education system in Iraq through Education project in integration with Child Protection. The focus will be on Governorates that have critical needs for humanitarian intervention (especially camps located in these areas). The intervention will be done in alignment with last Humanitarian Response Plan and the strategy of Education and Child Protection Cluster. Al Mortaqa aim to implement project that consistently provide specialized child protection assistance. After the assessment of children in risk and survivors Al Mortaqa in partnership with other actors will provide PSS activities and case management services for children that live with conflict and displacement-induced trauma. Resilience activities with children will be conducted during the project implementation as well as will provide sustainability by building the capacities of stakeholders and communities through training on child protection and education themes. 10,000 school age children (ages 3 to 17) will be target as beneficiaries for the education component. In addition, the project will target community members who want to participate in teaching and are willing and able to take an active role in planning and implementing NFE. Also we aim to engage in the project the community’s members, man, woman and children (with gender balance participations) and provide for this target group capacities building that will contribute on changing behaviors and build knowledge on child protection and education approaches. The project will also provide assistance by humanitarian and public services.
The most vulnerable children who are not attending formal and non-formal school will be supported by project to access Education through improving the quality of life in camps and out of camps. In coordination with partners in filed Al Mortaqa teams will support the communities’ structure for helping children find appropriate education alternative options that can continually contribute their learning.
The project target also the returned children and teacher staff that are less helped and affected by conflict though the main focus remains the communities in the camps Through the project implementation as national organization we aim to provide and facilitate activities that improve the quality of education and find sustainable solutions by targeting both children and teachers. Local stakeholder such as headmasters, teachers, child committees, PTAs, government authorities, DoE and other non-formal stakeholders will be engaged as primary beneficiaries as well as partners during all project implementation.. The project will focus in providing direct and specialized support to vulnerable conflict-affected individuals; special focus will be given to woman and girls. The direct support is focused on providing case management and psycho-social services. The intervention will also enable the community (especially woman and girls) to access humanitarian assistance and services as well as establish long term solution as per needs identified in location that Al Mortaqa will run the projects.

Needs

Over 3.2 million children aged three to 18 have been affected by the conflict in Iraq. The displacement of people in Iraq over the past three years has been unprecedented and affected Education system in dramatic way. As per IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix March 2018 there are over 5.4 people have been displaced since 2014, 1.7 million newly displaced in 2017 alone. As of 30 March 2018, people remain displaced across Iraq are 2,205,252, while 3,635,598 have returned. Approximately 50% of the affected population are children. Education sector is one of the main affected in Iraq. As per cluster information shared on mid-2017, over three million children do not attend school on a regular basis and more than 1.2 million children, including 90% of children in conflict-affected areas, are out-of-school. All affected children have witnessed violence and suffered disruption to their learning (referring OCHA, Iraq Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018). Educational facilities have been destroyed and teachers are not available. Teachers also suffered underpayment and lack of investment in their development. As per Al Mortaqa assessments children who stay away from quality Education are a target of child labor, early marriage and provide a contingent of not-employed people. Many children living in displacement/refugee camps do not have access to quality education and availability of schooling is a critical factor in families’ decisions to return. In the areas where schools are open they operate in multiple shifts meaning that children are able to attend for only a few hours a day, severely impeding their ability to catch up on what they have missed. Child are prevented to access Education due to the lack of space, unsafe environments, low quality of teaching, poor governmental policies and support, in addition to significant protection based concerns and barriers. Lack of access to integrated, protective educational opportunities is also source for social tension between host and displaced communities (referred on OCHA, Iraq Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018). Protection issues will continue to be significant issue, particularly in communities where local populations are perceived as having supported ISIS. There are specific concerns for the long-term futures of children recruited or used by parties to conflict, those in detention, and those born to ISIS members who are at risk of being abandoned. Hundreds of thousands of people, including tens of thousands of children, have been exposed to extreme violence, danger, and uncertainty, resulting in stress and trauma that will require immediate and long-term support and care. This had traumatic impact in children life and their Education. Based on Cluster (MCNA) and Al Mortaqa assessments data in the Governorate Salah al Din (especially in Shirqat and Baiji) more than 25% of families have involved in child labor their own children with +6 age. The number of data for this categories are lower in Ninewa Governorate but still critical responded needed, with 12% of families that involve their children working especially in location of Mosul, Telafar and Sinjar. In the Governorate of Anbar district Falluja more than 4% IDPs children in and out of camp have missed their documentations. Also it is reported that 7 % of children in Ninewa specifically in Mosul, Telafar and Sinjar out of camps and returners have already missed their documentation to any kind (the information is taken by the data reported by Sub cluster protection and Al Mortaqa assessments). We consider critical the high number of children that are non-enrolled in formal education or in non-formal education. Through the protection project we aim that our teams work in field with families, children and relevant actors. Al Mortaqa will provide training for parents and caregivers for better care and protection for their children, this also will contribute to prevention of long term affect as well as will contribute to behavior changes. REACH findings reflect that 30 % at least one children from 6 to 17 age (IDPs out of camp, Returners and IDPs in camps) in Governorate of Anbar location Falluja are not enrolled in formal on non-formal education. The number for this target is more critical in Governorate of Ninewa especially in locations where Al Mortaqa will be focus such as Mosul, Telafar and Sinjar. Al Mortaqa consider to give some special focus children with psychosocial distress considering the high need for this intervention. Large number are identified with this need as per assessment of Sub cluster.

Activities

Al Mortaqa will focus to increase access to quality formal learning opportunities for children in areas displacement and areas of return. Non formal Education will be used to competent and support the children enrollment on the formal Education. All the intervention will allow for transition into recognized educational pathways. Also will be worked to make schools and learning environments are protective and responsive to the needs of conflict affected children, adolescents and youth. With the purpose to have gender balance activities and make them aware of their rights, how to access humanitarian assistance and public services, and overcome barriers to achievement of durable solutions Al Mortaqa will be implementing as per below activities. • Learning and teaching materials will be provided to of conflict affected children, adolescent and youth. This will enable children to attend school without having the complexity of not having proper materials. • Conflict affected children, adolescent and youth (3-17) accessing quality and inclusive formal and non-formal education by making school a child friendly environment where children rights are protected. • Families that have economic difficulties will be supported with cash assistance in order to cover transportation and other education related costs of the conflict affected children, adolescent and youth (3-17). • In integration with CP project children will benefit from PSS and individual case management at school level. Awareness raising events will take place targeting themes related to Education and CP. • Girls and boys at risk will be identified and referred to specialized services as well as provided with case management through the proper pathways. •. • Al Mortaqa will support to open and close the families tracing requests. • Community centers will be established in order to serve as community based child protection structures established and the centers will be supported to sustain in long term. • As part of child empowerment and participation will be established child and adolescent clubs that will address CP issues in the community. • Adults such as parents, community stakeholders and children will be trained in CP issues. • Training women and men non child protection staff trained on CP approaches. •Awareness raising on gender inclusion. • Provision of livelihood opportunities by prioritizing women.• Also providing training for professionals’/ CP workers who provide proper CP assistance. All the intervention will be built to impact school age children, their families and community Provide vocation training for youth and woman. The second CP objective will be focused in community empowerment and advocacy for affected children and communities. The purpose is to coordinate with authorities, humanitarian and recovery actors to address protection issues in accordance with protection norms and applicable legal standards. This will happen in collaboration with community members and will establish long term solutions.

Protection Risk Analysis

Several protection risks can affect the population in the vulnerable areas affected by war, schools can be vulnerable and not secure safety of children. Construction and rehabilitation can impact significantly the life of children if proper design and engineering monitoring is not in place. Corporate punishment is a reality in schools therefore that impacts directly children life. Livelihoods options that are not controlled for families inside and outside the camps expose the IDPs to seriously risks especially related to child labor, withdrawing children from school and also can increase selling their personal assets. During the projects implementation experiences of Al Mortaqa in many camps inform us that issues related to protection can happen due to heavy armed forces being present in the camps which are without major constrains. This can cause arbitrary arrest, pressure on humanitarian actors and harassment (including sexual harassment).. A risk for families that are forced to leave the camps is that the children will likely lose a year of study in the transition. Culture behavior that existed between different communities might bring access prevention on the service of child protection like children born by mother raped, access prevention on the service of child protection like children born by mother raped and less participants female during the activities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Community members and school staff will be consulted in the detailed implementation plan as well as throw-out of all project implementation. Focus groups (with gender balance) will be held with girls, women, man and boys where ideas will be shared and taken in to account. Needed changes will be done to the project based in their feedback. The best interest of the beneficiaries will be a priority. Community based facilitators and volunteers will be recruited by community who is benefiting from the project. The changed that will take place based on their feedback will be shared back with the community groups. All the facilities will be design and constructed with proper community authorities’ approval and collaboration. The existing structures such as schools, local NGOs, formal and informal community groups, social workers, nurse, police, etc. will be consulted and involved in the project stages. ALMORTAQA as a part of the Education and CP clusters will be responsible for adopting the clusters strategy which will build further accountability to affected populations through the first key activity; assessing projects against the needs and feedback expressed by the target populations. Accountability mechanisms, such as complaints and feedback lines and monitoring systems will be established to permit for transparent follow-up for project's activities. People receiving assistance continues to provide feedback and guidance to humanitarian partners. As usual during ALMORTAQA previous project, Call center will be adopted as mechanism to dial with all beneficiaries’ issues and to ensure coordination to respond to the beneficiaries need, and the campaigns will refer to the call center. Also ALMORTAQA has the capacity to handle referral internally through its LC project and refer to relevant actors,ALMORTAQA will ensure to undertake the commitments on AAP into consideration and as follows:Leadership/Governance: Demonstrate their commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into country strategies, program proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and highlighted in reporting.Transparency: Provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its affected populations over information provision.Feedback and complaints: Actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.Participation: Enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence. ALMORTAQA will seek for feedback of the targeted AP on the overall project's activities, the performance of our teams and how they were involved in the process, strong and weakness points of the project's delivery process in all the targeted areas, threats and hazards our team had to face during the process, challenges, constraints, recommendations, as well as the impact of the indirect beneficiaries regarding the activities provided at their areas.ALMORTAQA’s trained M&E staff will specifically monitor and report on Beneficiary verification process, Direct project activities and Beneficiary satisfaction.
