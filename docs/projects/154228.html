Project Summary

Guided by health needs assessments and requests from the Ministry of Health (MoH) to fill gaps in service provision, IOM is currently providing comprehensive primary healthcare (PHC) services across Iraq in coordination with the Health Cluster, along with referrals to specialized secondary and tertiary health services.
IOM has been requested by Departments of Health (DoH) to continue to support and provide lifesaving primary health services including reproductive health and emergency services, referral and health facility support, and provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to conflict-affected and liberated areas across Iraq to support returnees and host communities and continue camp-based clinic responses for internally displaced persons (IDPs). IOM will support the MoH DoH through building their capacity for preparedness and response, and offering services. In line with Health Cluster objectives 1, 2 and 3 this project thus aims to increase the well-being of IDPs and highly vulnerable people and their communities. It responds to the critical health needs resulting from the extreme conflict during 2014-2018, that resulted in millions of people experiencing distressful events, disruption of social networks, loss of property and harsh living conditions.
IOM’s support includes PHC in camp clinics, non-emergency referrals from camps to secondary/tertiary health facilities inside cities, and health facility support/capacity building training sessions. Services will be offered to two main vulnerable categories of people:
- Displaced people in camps, temporary, formal and informal settlements: offered primary healthcare consultations, obstetrics gynecology (OB-GYN) and pediatric consultation through static clinics in camps; and non-emergency referral will be offered. MHPSS services will be offered though static community centers, and referrals supported when specialized services are not available in camp.
- Returnees and vulnerable host communities: supported by enabling primary healthcare clinics (PHCCs) and hospitals in the areas of return to support free services to affected population, through minor revitalization and operational costs. PHCCs will also be supported to integrate MHPSS services though trained staff and revitalized MHPSS units.

Needs

As of Oct 2018, 4,090,242 individuals have returned to their areas of origin, predominantly Anbar, Ninewa, Salah al Din, Diyala, and Kirkuk. However there remain 188,277 IDPs in camps supported by IOM (Qayarrah, Haj Ali, Shekhan, Garmawa, Al-Qadissiyah complex, Debaga, Al Mateen, Yahyawa and Karbala IDP camp, and Hassan Sham U3, Sari Blind). The multiple waves of conflict experienced in Iraq resulted in millions of people experiencing distressful events, disruption of social networks, loss of property and harsh living conditions. These stressors can result in anxiety, depression, low self-esteem, increased domestic violence and deterioration of previously existing mental health conditions. The conflict also disrupted the provision of MHPSS services in PHCCs and at the community level, creating a further strain in a system with limited specialized services. Gaps in quality individual and group counseling, specialized mental health consultations, integration of mental health in PHCCs and psychosocial activities have a strong impact in the ability of IDPs and host community to function and be active members in the society. The conflict decimated health facilities and the capacity of the Gov to deliver basic services in conflict-affected areas, which is now affecting people returning to their area of origin. The high rate of returnees has resulted in increased need of health services and decreased provision.IOM health staff in coordination with local health providers and government authorities have identified vast health needs. The most critical health conditions identified are respiratory infections, gastroenteritis, urinary tract infections-specially females, skin infections/infestations and chronic diseases such as hypertension and diabetes mellitus. Immediate health services are needed to cater for the growing number of returnees. The destruction of health facilities, and influx of returnees has resulted in increased need of health services and decreased provision. Hospital, primary health centres were subjected to major damages - a WHO HeRAM 2018 assessment of PHCCs in Ninewa revealed that 13% of PHCCs fully damaged while 17% partially damaged, contrary to that 34% partially functional while 22% are not functional. There are huge weakness in referral services in hospitals and at the PHCCs level. Anbar, Salah al-Din, Diyala and Kirkuk- Hawija- need support with structure and functionality aspects. DOHs need to be provided with commodities and services to provide high quality of care. IOM is participating in the strategy to transition out of direct health care provision and revitalize the health system to make it more resilient.As per general statistics on mental health after emergencies, up 3-4% of the population present severe disorders and need advanced services, 15%-20% suffer mild or moderate mental health disorders and a large part of the population experience normal distress or other psychological reactions. Highly vulnerable returnees and host community members will face adjustment needs that require the same level of support.Women and children living in hard to reach areas are a vulnerable group less likely to access health services and requiring more specialized care, either by specially trained general practitioners or obstetrician/gynecologists and pediatricians. For example, in Haj Ali and Qayyarah Emergency sites, more than half of the patients are children who suffer from dehydration and diarrhoea in summer, with rising rates of scabies and other skin diseases due to poor hygiene. Adolescent girls, persons with disabilities, and the elderly are at high risk of complications from chronic diseases. There is a critical need for referral transportation for E sites and camps to secondary health facilities in Ninawa, Erbil, Salahddin and Kirkuk per identified need at the different IDPs camps. Activities will be tailored to reach children under five, adolescent girls, persons living with disabilities, the elderly and people at high risk of complications from chronic diseases, who remain the most vulnerable population groups. The risk of communicable diseases such cholera, measles, and polio require a timely surveillance system in all locations; IOM will continue to support and strengthen the current EWARN system, to detect and respond to disease outbreaks.

Activities

With this project IOM will provide an estimated 105,280 consultations and 7,680 referrals with assisted transportation for non-emergency& Emergency cases. 8,000 MHPSS sessions will be provided and 1,000 cases supported. 16 health facilities will be supported, with IOM interventions such as consultations, capacity-building, medical supplies and medical equipment, and MHPSS initiatives such as guided group discussions, sports, religious and cultural events, educational and recreational activities (considering gender, age dynamics and needs) and 550 participants trained (450 on health interventions, 100 on PSS). More than 180,000 IDPs in the targeted camps will have access to primary health care, referral assistance and secondary health service by targeting hospital units and training as a part of supporting health national capacity building. DoH health staff will be trained in Mental Health Gap Action Programme (MhGAP) and Problem Management Plus (PM+) programmes, developed by WHO, followed by supervision . They will be supported to integrate MHPSS into primary health care services, offer basic support and reinforce referrals. Interventions will be implemented in parallel and coordinated through the Health Cluster and with the FFIS UNDP program, which is supporting infrastructure rehabilitation and equipment of damaged facilities. MHPSS interventions will be coordinated with the MHPSS Technical Working Group and the Protection Cluster, offering services at different levels in line with IASC guidelines. The awareness and community-based services in the MHPSS programmes will be addressed under the protection cluster, collaboration with the Ministry of Youth and Sports (MoYS) and Ministry of Education (MoE) will support the mainstreaming of MHPSS services in different settings. The activities are:1- Continue providing IDPs in camps and highly vulnerable people with good quality essential healthcare services, including treatment of common diseases; vaccination (will be coordinated with UICEF); referrals and; reproductive health services to women (will be coordinated with UNFPA).2- Provision of gynecological consultations. IOM appointed female trained doctors and nurses with good experience dealing with Gynecological conditions according to MoH standards in most of the camps clinics.While for Qayyarah Airstrip camp clinic it has been provided with Sonar service for all IDPs in Qayyarah including Jadaa camps and Haj Ali camp to decrease referrals and complement with other medical clinics in the different camps providing that more than 75% are ObGyne cases receiving the sonar consultation. Also, Both clinics ( Qayyarah airstrip and Haj Ali ) are supported with referral service for non-emergency cases to nearest specialized RH facilities supported by UNFPA.3- Referrals of complicated cases (both emergency and non-emergency) from camps to secondary and or tertiary health care services in the cities of corresponded camp’s governorate, factoring in the unique needs for people such as those with disabilities, or age groups.4- Short term support to health facilities (hospitals and main PHCCs) with medical equipment and minor revitalization, especially in return areas.5- Capacity building training for DoH staff in providing good quality health service, to support clinic handover processes, and to increase their capacity in MHPSS, including specifically to address and respond appropriately to the needs of women, men, boys and girls according to their experiences and needs. In all trainings under this project, IOM will be coordinating with the MoH and the appropriate UN Agency.6- Continue support for IDPs living in camps and highly vulnerable people with MHPSS services, tailored to the experiences and priorities of each gender or age group. This includes specialized services and focused non-specialized (counseling and support groups). By way of planning an exit strategy, IOM will coordinate closely with health authorities (DoH) to take over the service at the medical unit with transitional period of at least of one month, IOM had closely cooperated with DoH Ninawa, Erbil, Duhok, Kirkuk, Anbar and Salahddin during the last 3 years for health service run by IOM and provided through DoH personnel, who underwent several on-job training as capacity building strategy, for IDPs in camps and out camps. All provided health service have been subjected to close and continuous monitoring and evaluation to ensure best quality of services based on outcome QoC assessment missions. Being active partner in all sub cluster groups at the level of governorates enabling IOM to discuss and update all health providers including DoH about the exit plan and period of handling the service to DoH. IOM health coordinator discussed the handing the camp clinics in Qayyarah and Haj Ali with DoH Ninawa and there is an agreement of transitional steps to hand over gradually with assurance of proper stock of medications.

Protection Risk Analysis

The following could be expected in 2019:• Access constraints due to armed conflict, security considerations such as check-points to some locations, whether sudden onset or prolonged, such as Basrah, Hawija, Southern/Western Mosul, etc. which may restrict appropriate service-delivery. • Risks of placing people in more vulnerable positions due to provision of assistance i.e. doing harm. Those identified as particularly vulnerable such as women and children continue to be particularly vulnerable to further violence and exploitation.• Continuous violation of the civilian nature of camps by armed forces and groups; Attacks to health personnel or facilities.

Mitigation Measures

• IOM has over 1,300 staff operating directly in all governorates within Iraq, providing significant access to at-risk populations, including in areas of high insecurity. IOM has established relationships in GoI and KRG with key government focal points and maintains strong relationships to ensure necessary bureaucratic processes for support. IOM can rely on existing access and coordination arrangements in all governorates to support access to beneficiaries. Through taking a safety-first approach, IOM will continue to coordinate with the DoH, camp’s management and partners at the sub-national level to deliver the required assistance as soon as possible. • Advocacy and sensitization of humanitarian principles; occurrences taken to camp manager and health and protection clusters to take the appropriate measurement to minimize harm to all. Staff are trained on following do not harm principles in interventions and signed a code of conduct. They are receiving PSEA training and have a reporting system in place. Under Protection programming, IOM is already capacitating front line emergency staff through internal and external trainings and practical support to effectively identify and respond to protection risks within emergency operations to ensure the delivery of humanitarian assistance that incorporates protection principles and reduces risks to promote safe and dignified access for all affected populations.• Events will be shared with health cluster and WHO through WHO’s MEASURES program and camp manager will be informed as well, along with relevant Gov Authorities when applicable. In the delivery of MHPSS services, personnel are trained on escalation of violent situations and follows security regulations. A security guard will be engaged in any community centers at risk of possible incidents.

How is your project accountable to affected population

• Feedback from beneficiaries, DoH, partners and IOM medical and MHPSS staff throughout the implementation of IOM’s health and MHPSS projects have been used to identify the main beneficiaries’ needs that this project will address as part of the Health response included in the HRP. Assessments including MHPSS have been conducted in Mosul and Tel Afar, and will be finalized in 2019 in Hamdaniya and Tel Afar. • IOM staff guarantees an adequate information sharing system through constant presence in the field and daily direct contact with beneficiaries, to collect and address their needs or complaints and adapt to the evolving surrounding situation.• Continuous M&E: Throughout implementation randomly selected beneficiaries (over 18 years) complete a short, confidential survey questionnaire describing demographic data (age, gender) and satisfaction with services received including accessibility. The results are compiled monthly by field staff and shared with the programme manager to monitor their satisfaction and ensure they are being met and that target populations are being reached (ie. Women and children), this inform distribution of HR and medical expertise with supplies. This is as important tools of project evaluation and feedback mechanism, with the goal to cover 3% of all project beneficiaries. MHPSS teams use a survey to measure impact and satisfaction, most significant change interviews for selected beneficiaries, evaluation forms and pre and post-test for trainings. All teams submit a monthly narrative report and a training report after each training has finalized. Psychometric tools PHQ-9, SDQ and SQR-20 are used regularly by psychologist, psychiatrist and social workers to measure progress. • Communication with Communities (CwC) will continue to be used to reach out to affected people, including vulnerable groups (women of child-bearing age, pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities, the elderly, people with chronic illnesses) to spread messages on issues of public health concern. • IOM participates in the IDP Call Center, who receive information from beneficiaries, and submits to Health Cluster who requests investigation and feedback. More efforts will be directed to enhance problem resolution rates.• IOM will strive to hire female health and protection professionals, while investing in capacity building female professionals during the project. Gender disaggregation is critical in IOM’s standard operating procedures for collection and analysis of health data.
