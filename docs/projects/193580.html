Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the education cluster HNO strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and is aligned with the MoE Education Strategy Plan. The project aims to ensure that boys and girls have equitable safe access to inclusive and quality educational services in vulnerable MoE schools in Gaza Strip (East Khanyounis, Rafah, Gaza, and North Gaza). This project fits under a multi-year NRC strategy to ensure that children will be bridged with formal education, have safe access to education and contribute to create safe learning environments mainstreaming psychosocial support throughout the whole intervention. NRC will regularly coordinate with education partners and be part of the Strategic Advisory Group in Gaza. The intervention will focus on: i) Psychosocial support to help strengthen protective factors for children. The psychosocial intervention is delivered through NRC’s flagship Better Learning Programme (BLP) curriculum, providing support to the Ministry of Education on creating an additional capacity with a cohort of BLP Master Trainers; provision of capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in MoE schools; provision of emergency kits to schools; awareness sessions for parents on the importance of mental wellbeing; and provision of a “Supporting the Supporter” package for teaching and non-teaching staff. ii) Strengthen first phase responses supporting MoE and schools on the development and adoption of contingency plans; creation of NRC emergency team to be able to respond to emergency situations. iii) No-formal education interventions (remedial classes an catch-up classes) to bridge out of school children with formal education or support children who have experienced prolonged learning loss and tackle drop-out in formal schools. In the event of no access, NRC will adopt blended modality through digital platforms to keep children engaged and provide continuous learning. iv) Provision of technical support to education sub-cluster in Gaza through as co-lead in the Strategic Advisory Group. v) Conduct situational analysis on OOSC needs, gaps and priorities, as well as contributing in enhancing their access to quality basic education. vi) Conduct a situational analysis on adolescent/youth needs and priorities, in addition to contributing in enhancing vulnerable youths’ empowerment and skills, capacity and resilience development. The action is also contributing in improving both skills and knowledge of teaching and non-teaching staff on PSEA principles. During the intervention, gender inclusion will be mainstreamed throughout the project providing information sessions to parents on the importance of education for boys and girls, promoting gender balance in the activities, tackling gender barriers in coordination with parent and teacher associations, teachers and school principals. NRC will work towards an inclusive education programme with attention to children with disabilities coordinating with specialised partners such as Humanity and Inclusion to conduct regular assessments on how to improve access to education for children with disabilities, trainings for teachers and sessions for parents' engagement.

1 - Needs

Approximately 600,000 Palestinians (56 per cent females) across the oPt, including around 13,400 Children with Disabilities (CWDs), need humanitarian assistance to access education in 2022. Nearly 74 per cent live in the Gaza Strip and over 26 per cent in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. Among the longstanding issues affecting access to education include insufficient and/or inadequate safe school infrastructure. The Gaza Strip is most affected with around 65% of schools operating on a double-shift system. In 2020- 2021, these challenges were compounded by successive COVID-19 pandemic surges, leading to prolonged closures of schools and kindergartens, as part of the measures adopted to contain the pandemic (HNO 2022). The escalation of violence in Gaza, in May 2021 and August 2022, degenerated the education situation leading to the early termination of the school year and delayed the start of the school year for 2022/2023. Accordingly, 1.3 million children in the oPt lost at least three months of schooling and most have not been able to engage in summer or extracurricular activities. While the Ministry of Education (MoE) and UNRWA put in place various distance learning programmes to mitigate the effect of COVID-19 to learning, these were not fully accessible to many children living in homes without internet connectivity, or who could not collect the printed self-learning materials being distributed by schools. In June 2021, NRC education assessed 100 PA schools for the purpose of identifying the most vulnerable ones for emergency response. The assessment confirmed that Covid-19 is one of the main factors impacting students’ psychological well-being. 99% of school reported having people infected with Covid-19 in their schools, and this led to depression, stress, anxiety, violence and low academic achievement. In addition, the assessment confirmed a number of factors leading to distress and fears including the household environment that substantially affected students’ well-being. Teachers and counsellors lacking the skills and response methodology to identify and responding to children requiring support among others. In September 2020, a rapid assessment through phone survey was conducted across the Gaza Strip on 30 teachers from 30 schools out of the 118 targeted in the ongoing BLP program. The findings indicate that: 81% of teachers were still in need capacity building particularly skills for in running the virtual classes and managing the lessons content. 60% of teachers confirmed not to have received the MoE led training in virtual classes. Only 21%of students are active in virtual classes a factor; attributed to the lack of ICT equipment. 90%of teachers confirmed that, not satisfied by students’ participation in virtual classes, with 100%of teachers confirming a lack of appropriate skills and techniques for improving and evaluating students’ participation in virtual classes. From the foregoing assessment as well as the evidence from the education cluster and NRC experience there is a need for a clearer and improved technical capacity and leadership in designing and rolling-out the distance-learning scheme, including catch-up classes for those at high risk of dropout. Support is particularly required in regard to content, pedagogical approach as well as tools and materials for education professionals and students. At household level, material support (ICT) to families with school-aged children who have limited access to distance learning. According to Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment- MSNA conducted by REACH in July 2022, 81,2% of Gaza refugee households and 76.7% non-refugee households reported a need for catch-up learning due to school closures due to Covid-19 or other reasons. Of the 7.1% of households with school-aged children not attending school the most frequently reported reasons for non-attendance are: household cannot pay related school expanses, lack of interest in education, illness, children working to support household, school cannot accommodate children with disabilities. Of the 9.1% of households not planning to enrol school aged children the most frequently reported reasons for non-enrolment are: household cannot afford school related expenses, child not interested in school, school cannot accommodate children with disabilities, child needs to support families at home. 5.7% who reportedly dropped-out of school during 2021 – 2022 school year are 2.7% girls and 5.3% boys and the main reasons are: child labour, cannot afford costs, lack of interest, disability specific needs not met. The condition of unsafety and security concerned 23.8% of the respondents when children travel to/from school and studying in schools. 93.5% of households perceived that children with mental and physical disabilities faced additional challenges in accessing education services. As additional challenges were mentioned: bullying, infostructure not adapted, classrooms not adapted to need, transportation constraints, teacher training, curriculum not adapted. Regarding to psychosocial support available in school, 66.2% households reported that PSS is provided by school counsellors, 15.1% no such support available, 10.1% teachers trained on PSS, not sure 9.9%, information on external PSS 0.2%.

2 - Activities

NRC has a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank and Gaza. A framework agreement has been signed between NRC and MoEHE outlining an overall commitment to ensure the adequate implementation of the project. The activities are clustered in five main areas based on the findings of vulnerability/ needs assessment. The needs assessment will target schools in which NRC has been operating, and it will engage community, parents, children, teaching/non-teaching staff and school management. 1)Psychosocial Support: 100 MoE schools' benefit from PSS training, delivered by trained teachers and counsellors at school level. Based on identified needs, schools will be provided with emergency educational kits, in line with MoEHE requirements.10 (6M-4F) MoEHE Subject Supervisors and Head of Department will be trained as Better Learning Programme (BLP) Master Trainers. Master trainers will support the process of supervision and coaching of teachers during the implementation. 339 (139M - 200F) teachers will benefit from BLP1-2 training on Increasing Learning Capacity among pupils in ongoing Crisis, and Group Sessions Improving Study Skills.2,325 (950M-1,375F) teachers will be engaged and benefit from BLP1-2 activities, receiving a training from other teachers in a cascading modality. 113 (43M-70F) will be trained on BLP3 training focusing on Fighting Nightmares and sleeping problems. 1,130(460M - 670F) students will benefit from BLP sessions led by teachers under the supervision of NRC education team.904 (369M - 535F) students will benefit from the group and individual sessions of BLP3 delivered by trained counsellors.15,375 (6,344M-9,031F) students will benefit from BLP group sessions. 292 (119M -173F) teaching and non- teaching staff will benefit from a structured PSS service through the local partnership with Gaza Community Mental Health. 2,260 (1,506F) parents benefit from awareness sessions on the importance of mental health on how to support their children, importance of education. 2)Remedial classes: NRC education team and teachers will identify students with low academic achievement. The process of identification will take into consideration the current school achievements and level of attendance that might help to identify a risk of drop-put. NRC will set up classes on remedial learning within formal schools and conducted by support teachers selected by NRC in coordination with the MoE. The classes will focus on main basic subjects: Math and Arabic. The target group will be students from grades 4-8. Students’ learning outcomes will be monitored throughout competency-based assessments and in the event of remote learning, students will benefit from additional devices or printed modules to avoid learning disruption. NRC will engage with parents and formal teachers organising regular meetings to share progresses, challenges or concerns, and collaboratively agree on steps forwards. 3)Schools Contingency Planning: part of the needs assessment exercise is prepared to figure out the level of target schools preparedness planning against emergencies and crises. Accordingly, NRC will work on supporting the - establishment of most vulnerable schools Emergency response committees; (3 members: 1 teacher, 1 school management, 1 school counsellor), as well as building the capacities of school’s emergency teams for possible contingency plan implementation. Part of the contingency planning is supporting schools in carrying out simulation and 1 school-based campaign exercises based on each school needs and updated contingency/emergency plan. 4)Continue work in co-leading the Strategic Advisory Group and support education sub-cluster in Gaza in steering the priorities of EIE. NRC will support in the coordination and contribute in developing and adjusting the strategic framework, priorities and work plan of the Cluster. 5)Bridging out of School children to formal education: based on a situational analysis and desk review, NRC will design activities to support out of school children to go back to school, with a children education pack focused on helping children to transit from a status of out of schools to enrolled into formal education. The activity will focus on catch-up classes. The non-formal education provided by NRC in formal schools will also be an opportunity to have a more gender responsive programme working together with school principals, teachers and parents on the importance of education for boys and girls, as well as organising school visits with Humanity and Inclusion to provide recommendations when needed on school improvements to be more inclusive for children with disabilities, or trainings for teachers on inclusive education for children with disabilities.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, allbeneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general classroom based intervention approach targeting all children in target schools. Its suitable for all children in any context, as it is designed to reach large numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience among them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilized. iii) The third dimension is a specialised clinical one that is designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, children with or without special needs are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach. For the inclusive education and contingency plans component and catch up classes, the project will target the most excluded children (boys and girls) at school level. The project team will work at directorate and school level, to define the criteria for those children involved and benefited by the project
