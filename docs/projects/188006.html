Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements gender-sensitive livelihood early recovery initiatives targeting the most vulnerable households to enhance their resilience and self-sufficiency to the multi-faceted prolonged crisis.
Action Against Hunger conducted 31 livelihood technical needs assessments and several markets assessments over 7 governorates. AAH assessments confirmed that the main livelihood activities in the rural areas are related to crop cultivation and livestock in addition to employment as a daily wage earner in agricultural-related activities. However, the income generated by the households is insufficient to cover the basic needs of food and non-food items, therefore, households are implementing a number of food and livelihoods based coping strategies to survive. According to “CARE_Syria Food Security Report, February 2021”: Over the past decade of the conflict, the humanitarian response has largely centered on short-term, immediate relief. However, in light of persisting crises and rapidly deteriorating circumstances, it has become clear that donors and the humanitarian community could create larger-scale, more sustainable impacts by shifting their approach to support Syrian women’s self-sufficiency, growth and security. Therefore, Action Against Hunger will prioritize female-headed households who will receive vocational training on adding value to milk in order to increase profit rather than just relying on selling raw milk. AAH’s assessment in Dar’a governorate indicated that milk is sold to individuals who transport the milk to Damascus because there are no factories for milk processing in Dar’a. The assessment also indicated the milk is sold for a low price, hence a reduced profit to the households. Action Against Hunger will collaborate with the Arab Center for Studies in Arid Zones and Dryland (ACSAD) to provide vocational training to female-headed households on adding-value to milk. ACSAD will allocate a venue in one of their field offices in Dar’a governorate; AAH will equip this venue with the necessary inputs and equipment to establish a training site for the trainees. The selected female-headed households will receive a theoretical and practical session on adding value to the milk. Topics will highlight the importance of milk and dairy in human consumption, proper and safe collection and handling of milk, dairy processing and preservation. The training material will be prepared jointly by AAH and ACSAD. The training will be conducted by a dairy production expert over 5 days; including a session on the establishment and management of potential income-generating activities related to adding value to milk. An incentive kit containing will be distributed to each female-headed household at the end of the theoretical and practical training; this kit will help each female-headed household to start her own business.
In 2023, AAH and its partner will fundraise to conduct the same ERL activities targeting new beneficiaries with the same criteria. The same budget of 2022 is expected in 2023, however the estimated number of beneficiaries will likely be less than the target in 2022 considering the inflation affecting the purchase power of the SYP and the prices in the market. Overheads costs cover AAH support costs in the country office and in HQ at AAH standard rate.

4 - Needs

In 2022, 14.6 million people need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. In December, the national average price of WFP’s standard reference food basket recorded fourth consecutive month registering the highest ever recorded price since monitoring started 2013, reaching SYP 220,112 (USD 88 at the official exchange rate of SYP 2,500/USD). AAH during 2021, conducted 31 Livelihoods Technical and market assessments in 7 governorates. Findings show that households cannot cover the basic needs of food and non-food items; therefore, they are forced to implement several negative coping strategies to survive increasing GBV risks as child labour for boys and early marriage for girls. AAH assessments confirmed that the main livelihood activities in the rural areas are related to crop cultivation and livestock in addition to employment as a daily wage earner in agricultural-related activities. Farmers are coping with losing their main source of income by renting the lands or selling part of the land while livestock breeders sell part of their animal flock to cope with the lack of pasture and the high and unprecedented increase in the cost of animal feed. Smallholder farmers are suffering from the lack to access to high-quality agricultural inputs due to decreasing their purchase power and the unprecedented increase in inputs cost. Women headed households are particularly vulnerable as they lost the breadwinner and have little income generating opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

AAH in collaboration with ACSAD will provide vocational training focusing on adding value to milk. Female headed households will be selected based on criteria prioritizing households having children under 5, women indicating the willingness to establish their own business. ACSAD will provide the training venue, raw milk (from the animals raised in their field office). AAH will provide the needed inputs/tools/equipment to equip the facility for adding value to the milk. The produced milk by livestock in the field office will be used by the female-headed households during their practical sessions. The selected female-headed households will receive a theoretical and practical session on adding value to the produced milk. Topics will highlight the importance of milk and dairy in human consumption, proper and safe collection and handling of milk, dairy processing and preservation. The training material will be prepared jointly by AAH and ACSAD. The training will be conducted by a dairy production expert over 5 days. The training will include a session on the establishment and management of income-generating activities related to adding value to milk. An incentive kit containing will be distributed to each female-headed household by the end of the theoretical and practical training. A PDM report will be prepared. Outputs are measured against defined indicators to quantify the impact through baseline/endline surveys. An accessible feedback and complaint mechanism is rolled out.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behaviour or linking sexual favour with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.
