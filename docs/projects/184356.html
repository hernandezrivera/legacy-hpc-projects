Project Summary

The goal of the proposed project is to sustain retention of crises affected children and ensure increased access to education for vulnerable displaced and nondisplaced boys and girls in Benadir Benadir (Kahda and Daynile Districts), Galgadud(Adado and Cabuwaq districts) Mudug(Galkayo and Galdogob districts) and Nugal(Garowe district) regions of Somalia. The proposed action will be in line with Somalia’ Education Cluster’s Response Strategy Note for 2022. Children in the proposed locations experience key challenges such as lack of school infrastructure, lack of trained teachers, lack of teaching and learning materials, as well as scholastic materials. There is also lack of capacity of community education committees (CECs) in managing the schools. To ensure the centrality of protection, Mercy Corps, the proposed project will focus on the most vulnerable and marginalized school age children including children living with disabilities (CWD) both in rural and poor urban communities, displaced and refugee children in the target districts. Equally, female children of adolescent age, will be targeted with life skills, provision of dignity kits.
Though this project, Mercy Corps will provide direct emergency education support to 20,000 displaced and non-displaced school aged children in 60 schools within the target districts. This support will not only improve retention and access to quality education but also ensure a protective learning environment for both girls and boys in the target districts. The project will address the negative effects of hunger through school feeding during school days in the target schools to retain children in school and boost their ability to concentrate during class lessons.
To increase access to education, the project will include establishment or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces with gender friendly and inclusive WASH facilities, provision of school furniture and teaching/leaning materials, teacher incentives and teacher training in inclusive, protective, and socio-emotional teaching and learning, as well as training of Community Education Committees in school management and school safety.
To mitigate the impact of shocks such as drought, the proposed project will focus on providing emergency teacher incentives to maintain teachers in schools during crisis and lower the cost of education. Additionally, the project will contribute to retention of crisis affected children through provision of emergency school feeding as well as provision of safe drinking water and hygiene promotion.
To enhance the wellbeing of the target beneficiaries, the proposed project will map out Child protection community services and referral pathways in the target districts, train teachers and CECs on child protection and psychosocial support; provide recreational materials and will establish Child protection focal points in target schools in line with the Integrated Child Protection Framework.
Covid-19 response activities will be integrated with the project including the provision facemasks for children and school personnel in line with WHO and UNICEF guidelines on age appropriateness of the facemasks for school children. In addition, Covid-19 awareness sessions will be incorporated into the school-based community mobilization sessions and visits. Schools will be provided with WASH supplies including handwashing soaps and hand sanitizers to enhance Covid-19 preventive measures in project target schools.
Where and when possible, the project will prioritize the most sustainable solutions and engage with development actors to bridge the humanitarian-development divide. This includes activities to strengthen the capacity of partners and MoE staff in response planning and coordination. The project response is aligned with the MoE Education Sector Strategic Plans and is aiming to complement the efforts of the government to strengthen the capacity of the education system to respond to the impact of the crisis.

1. - Projects activities

Mercy Corps will align its strategy with that of the cluster on two strategic approaches including retention of crisis-affected school children and increased access to education for vulnerable internally displaced children and children in host communities. The project focuses on schools as an entry point for life-saving assistance to vulnerable children and is integrating the provision of WASH and protection services at the school level. Mercy Corps will ensure centrality of protection by paying increased attention to school-aged children living with disabilities (CWD) and response interventions will be designed to enhance their access and inclusion to education. This will include ensuring school infrastructure are improved to allow easy access for the children with special needs. Equally, Mercy Corps will develop advocacy tools to encourage parents with CWDs get enrolled in schools. This will be done with close consultation with government to ensure the right incentives are identified to improve this enrollment. Mercy Corps will also ensure that at least 30% of the targeted beneficiaries are female (women and girls). Where and when possible, the project will prioritize the most sustainable solutions and engage with development actors to bridge the humanitarian-development divide. This includes activities to strengthen the capacity of partners, CECs and MoECHE staff in response planning and coordination. This will ensure that at least 30% are female teachers and CEC members. The project response is aligned with the MoECHE Education Sector Strategic Plans and is aiming to complement the efforts of the government to strengthen the capacity of the education system to respond to the impact of the crisis. Mercy Corps shall undertake a market analysis/study to determine availability of the teaching and learning materials as well as the scholastic materials. Cash Transfers (CTP) will be encouraged. To meet the identiﬁes need, the following activities are planned to implement. Objective 1: Ensure emergency and crises affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment.1.1 Rehabilitation of Classrooms with the provision of 15 sets of desks per classroom1.2 Setting up temporary Learning spaces1.3 Conduct community mobilization and hygiene promotion campaigns in 6 districts1.4 Construction/rehabilitation of 15 units of gender segregated latrines (2 stances) with hand washing facilities.Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion2.1 Provision of feeding for 7000 children in 24 Schools for six months2.2 Provision of incentives for 300 teachers (target 30% female teachers) and use this to encourage female teachers to join the profession. 2.3 Provision of incentives for 60 head teachers2.4 Provision of facemasks for 60 Schools2.5 Provision of emergency teaching and learning materials to 10000 students in 30 Schools2.6 Installation of pipe water extension for 8 schools2.7 Provision of WASH supplies to 60 Schools.2.8 Provision of feminine hygiene kits for 3000 adolescent girlsObjective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system3.1 Training of 120 CEC members and local authorities (targeting at least 30% women) on Management child protection, school safety, SGBV and psychosocial support3.2 Training of 120 teachers (both male and female with at least 30% women teachers) on pedagogical skills, child Protection and Psychosocial support based on INEE standards 3.3 Training of MoECHE staff (both male and female) and cluster partners on Emergency preparedness, response planning and Coordination3.4 Transport allowance for MoECHE officials (at least 30% felame) conducting quality monitoring of project activities (6 districts X 2 sessions X 2 officials)3.5 Provision of recreational materials to 40 schools

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash is most preferred since it gives the project participants an opportunity to make choices and hence feel dignified. For activities such as support to CECs through school grants, this allows them to make choices on what the cash disbursed to them can be used for. It also helps to build the local market and hence inject cash to the local economy. This in turn helps the market structures to emerge leading into more resilient markets even in the absence of external support.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Mercy Corps will undertake a market analysis/study to determine availability of the items - teaching and learning materials, scholastic materials, dignity kits and even text books as necessary. The project will also work to promote local private sector to see opportunities in education sector and therefore invest in building the local markets.
