Project Summary

Since the conflict began in 2011, Syria has faced the worst humanitarian crisis. Our project addresses one of the most pressing needs for people affected by the war: access to safe and clean water.
In 2024, ADRA is planning to intervene in several types of activities, such as water system rehabilitation, borehole rehabilitation and solar systems installation (in locations where household-level access to water is limited due to electricity outages), High water tank rehabilitation to ensure proper pressure in the water network, implementation of reverse osmosis unit to provide clean drinking water in the eastern Ghouta where there is high contamination with nitrate due to the damages in sewage network and the absence of sewage water treatment plants also the usage of fertilization in agriculture.
This project will improve access to safe water and sewage services for approximately 1,879,117 beneficiaries (375,823 households). The targeted category includes newly returned returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities across the governorates of Lattakia, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, and Deir-Ez-Zor. The beneficiaries will be selected on a detailed needs assessment, and those with the highest need and underserved by the humanitarian community will be prioritized for assistance.
The project will use a participatory and integrated approach to ensure that the needs of men, women, boys and girls are taken into account from the project design stage, throughout the implementation and during the post-implementation monitoring of the work. Children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will be solicited during the initial needs assessment for the project and in the post-implementation stage. In addition, each age and gender group will be able to provide timely feedback to ADRA on the intervention through regular contact with ADRA monitoring staff.
The needs of young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will also be considered, as children, adolescents, and middle-aged and older adults may require more water for cleaning and cooking.
ADRA has already secured 25% of the estimated budget for planned 2024 WASH activities.
