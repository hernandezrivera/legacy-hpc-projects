Project Summary

The project will provide unconditional cash transfers to 1,000 HHs most food insecure households (approximately 6,000 individuals) in Jowhar and Balcad Districts in Middle Shabelle from January 2022 to December 2022. USD71-MEB for Middle Shabelle/household/month in Middle Shabelle will be provided through electronic cash transfers in tandem with World Food Programme cost of household food basket per region and aligned to established guidelines within the Somalia Food Security Cluster. Kaalo Development Solutions (KDS) will use mobile cash transfers given their rapidity and relative cost effectiveness to advance economic access of the beneficiaries to sufficient, safe and nutritious food to meet their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life. KDS will draw on established protocols and best practices to ensure local merchants and mobile cash agents are not overwhelmed by demand for food and requests for cash outs. This will include staggering payments throughout the month and ensuring buy-in by local businesses/agents. Whilst operating in different districts, KDS has been carrying a range of interventions in the areas of food security, nutrition, livelihoods, education, health & hygiene, and youth. KDS will lead markets monitoring exercises throughout the program to ensure availability of food is never jeopardized and to track inflationary pressure that could result from a cash transfer program.
Building resilience and durable solutions are context specific and will be defined by the type of shock experienced and local social, economic and environmental context. The search for Durable Solutions should begin in the immediate aftermath of a disaster and this programme will thus be informed by Community Based Disaster Risk Management Planning (CBDRM) processes. CBDRM will identify priorities that can be supported to save lives and facilitate transition to early recovery, long-term stability and resilience by allowing the voices of vulnerable communities to inform desired durable solutions outcomes including food, nutrition and livelihoods diversification initiatives. The project will allocate resources to each target district for implementation of prioritized durable solutions initiatives that improve access to water that can subsequently support food production and income generating opportunities for target communities.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The cash transfer programming that KDS will employ will be digital payments delivered directly to beneficiaries’ mobile phones. Feasibility studies conducted by KDS and augmenting findings by the Somali Cash Consortium reflect that the households have mobile phones that can receive electronic money transfer and the digital cash can be transferred to local merchant shops in exchange for household goods, such as food, water and non-food items or can be exchanged for hard currency This method of humanitarian delivery has been shown by previous experiences and by studies to be favored by aid recipients as it allows them to make choices and purchase what they need most. Since it is delivered via mobile phone networks, it is also more easily traceable and does not need to be physically transported, ensuring that there is less risk of it being stolen. Food represents the majority of what the recipients spend their money on, but no family is the same and flexibility to buy what is most crucially needed allows more people to survive with dignity. Repaying small debts to households that shared their food in previous months helps keep social support networks intact, while buying medicine for an elderly family member.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

During the response analysis and planning, KDS actively participates in the Sub-National Cash Working Group and market assessments to determine the modalities and delivery alternatives of support. Market functionality and access have been important variables for CBI interventions as a pre-requisite for CBIs. A minimal expenditure basket has been utilized to inform the transfer values for beneficiaries based on the recognized needs across sectors, and market price monitoring has continued to influence the transfer values. Contingency measures have been put in place in CBI programming to strike a balance on modalities (cash/in-kind) owing to insecurity, as markets might easily be disrupted or inaccessible because of instability. As a member of the SNCWG, KDS will continue to participate in joint assessments (needs and market) to inform CBIs and will work to establish shared platforms (joint FSP procurement, joint post-distribution monitoring, joint targeting, and so on) across agencies to deliver assistance more efficiently. The Mogadishu-Balcad-Jowhar highway is the main thoroughfare between the two towns. It's now being under construction. Lessons learnt from the post distribution monitoring are used to improve future project that KDS is planning to undertake.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures for (a) minority clans/ethnic groups not being included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance and (b) risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure.The project will use the CBDRM methodology as its strategy to address multiple divisions amongst people and within communities that may be active or latent resulting from competition over resources such as land or emanating from differences in clan, gender, political ideologies and other related issues including status as returning ex-combatants, displaced persons, refugee returnees or host community members. The aim will be to create opportunities for inclusion of minority clans in decision making through for a series of interactions between various groups in the community to promote deliberative dialogue, mutual understanding, trust, empathy and resilient social ties. Through CBDRM, diverse members of the community are able to express and process their voices into peaceful and prosperous development initiatives which create an incentive for working together towards inclusive equitable sharing of resources and for monitoring any threats to local stability that may result from land disputes. The project will target key people in leadership structures and large groups of people who will provide broad–based support for social cohesion and be capacitated on facilitating mutual dialogue for dispute resolution and peace building. The CBDRM approach will create dialogue platforms for citizens and local stakeholders and build consensus on issues of concern such as exclusion of minority clans and land disputes with clear mechanisms for learning, monitoring and evaluation. Individuals and community’s involvement in the CBDRM will create a sense of ownership of local development initiatives for reducing tensions. The CBDRM approach assumes that different individuals/socio-economic groups/ethnic groups/clans/stakeholders are able to realize their differences/common needs and co-create the desired future together. The key components that will be promoted by the project’s CBDRM approach will include the following: • Security: the approach will seek to bring together the police, civil society and local communities across the clan and gender divide to jointly take responsibility for and develop solutions to local safety and security. • Socio-economic recovery: the approach will seek to provide community-based solutions towards the equitable provision of services on food, water, sanitation and hygiene. Many of these initiatives will be designed and implemented with particular attention to fostering social capital, cooperation across the divides and the foundation for reintegration and reconciliatory processes.• Personal change: facilitating self-identification into diverse clan and socio-economic groups allows beneficiaries to begin to identify underlying grievances and individual priorities; providing individuals with the skills and opportunity to discuss these priorities amongst themselves and with service providers will lead to individual empowerment. This will reduce tendencies to view political, civic processes as a zero-sum game, and open spaces for positive engagement. • Relational change: facilitating dialogue among and between clans/ethnic groups, IDPs and host communities will encourage cooperation and reduce the potential for violence and conflict between groups and individuals. Traditionally marginalized IDPs and ethnic groups will begin to view engagement as an effective means to address priorities and concerns. Traditionally powerful groups will humanize members of marginalized groups and accept collaboration as a means to realizing mutual goals. The self-identification of the targeted groups and the explicit recognition of the voices of all actors encourages a change in gender dynamics, allowing women to express themselves and providing a mechanism for gendered priorities to be addressed.
