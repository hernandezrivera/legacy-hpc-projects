Project Summary

This project aims to support the WASH response and the immediate needs of most vulnerable displaced populations in the northwest of Syria by providing emergency WASH assistance in camps and improving the basic WASH infrastructure and WASH conditions in IDP sites in northwest Syria through:
i) Improving access to drinking water by rehabilitating 15 water systems and installing connections to IDP camps.
ii) Rehabilitation and construction of 15 sanitations systems in informal IDP sites.
ii) Provision of temporary water supply, solid waste management, desludging, water storage tanks and garbage containers to newly displaced and/or highly vulnerable
populations living in 30 IDP camps.
iv) Operational support to 270 latrine units including water trucking, maintenance, solid waste management and desludging
The project will contribute to the two objectives of the WASH cluster which are:
• Support to water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria.
• Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behaviour and practices of most vulnerable people
Overall, the project aims to support 220,000 beneficiaries with humanitarian WASH assistance and improvements to basic WASH infrastructure in and out of camps in NW
Syria. Project locations are Harim, Dana and Salqin.

4 - Needs

According to the latest CCCM cluster updates (December 2019), there are 2.42 million IDPs in northern Syria while more than 1 million displacements where tracked in 2019, representing an increase of 66% compared to 2018. The CCCM cluster has registered 426 IDP sites, which now host over 740,000 IDPs (over 137,000 families). Women and children make up 80% of the IDP population. According to HNAP recent figures in February 2020, there are 586,000 newly displaced individuals from Southern Idleb towards Northern Idleb and Western Aleppo. According to a comprehensive WASH assessments conducted between June and August 2019 by WASH cluster partners, access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites, as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response and smooth transition into regular programming, in particular in NWS where large scale movement of IDPs is continuously observed. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are dependent on continuous WASH service provision. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. Countrywide, 42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, and in extreme case of Idleb up to 85 per cent . Communities not served with public water networks are in general at higher risk of unsafe water consumption, use less water than people well served via free/low cost water networks and spend significantly more of their incomes on purchasing water.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be directly managed, in cooperation with WASH Cluster Coordinator, by IOM Turkey cross border team based in Gaziantep with technical and administrative support from IOM sub-office in Gaziantep.IOM will work in partnership with well-experienced NGOs in each specific activity in camps and camp-like settings as implementing partners, selected from active members of the WASH and CCCM Clusters. IOM will provide technical support and monitor the project implementation on a daily basis through direct communication with the Implementing partners. For the rehabilitation and construction of water and sanitation systems, implementing partners will be asked to conduct topography studies for each IDP site, provide detailed needs assessment, site plans, implementation/ distribution plans, bills of quantities, and scopes of work. IOM technical staff will review and revise the project proposals and the above-mentioned documents and ensure the project idea and design meet the set objectives. IOM will also pay attention to Housing land and properties (HLP) dynamics and will mitigate possible tenure risks and disputes, by asking implementing partners to go through land ownership identification process and sign agreements with land owners / local councils. Each agreement should include physical and construction works to be completed, purpose of the intervention and approval from the land owners / local councils. Third-party monitoring mechanisms will be utilized to monitor and evaluate project activities. Warehouse verification, distribution and post-distribution monitoring activities will be conducted by third parties for at least 30 percent of the overall distributions and activities. Disaggregated beneficiary data will be verified through regular reporting by cluster members through the 4W reporting format to the WASH cluster and through IOM weekly progress reports. Exit interviews with men and women beneficiaries will be also conducted at project sites.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As water-trucking is linked to market variables, the distributed water quantities and quality can be affected, leading to non-compliance with SPHERE standards. Thus, to ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils. Ensure and comply with agreed context/SPHERE standards (minimum humanitarian standard at 15 litre/person/day) and establish water quantity tracking & monitoring system involving beneficiaries. Moreover, there is a potential risk due to the movement of water-trucking, desludging, and solid waste collection trucks in the congested camps. This issue can be mitigated by consulting/engaging community regarding the services modality/ frequency, truck routes and schedule; Establish clear routes and schedule for desludging trucks that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training (include operational safety/PPE-if the service required) with truck drivers/operators. Water network operation usually accompanied with an issues that form protection risks such as unequal distribution of services, theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation), Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction, and theft of chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups. Nonetheless, these issues can be fulfilled by stablish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Include community and authorities in targeting; Ensure sensitive information is handled with care; MoUs to be agreed with local authorities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key challenges for IOM and humanitarian partners operating in-country remain 1) Security risks that hinder the access to target locations are beyond project’s control. Mitigation measures include context analysis through active participation in OCHA access working group and IOM’s internal mechanisms of context analysis. 2) Risk of not having beneficiaries participate in the project’s activities are also beyond IOM’s control. Assumptions is that beneficiaries are still willing and able to participate in the project. Mitigation measures are through the open communication lines available between stakeholders that will allow continuous flow of information in a transparent and confidential way when needed. In addition, IOM will ensure beneficiary participation at all stages of the project, involving all gender and age groups (women, men, boys and girls) as well as community members with specific vulnerabilities such as persons with disabilities. The participation activities include needs assessments and post distribution monitoring through interviews, focus group discussion and surveys, consolations on vulnerability criteria, consolations on the design of latrines.3) Application of humanitarian principles: The risk of aid diversion by armed groups exist although thus far not reported by IOM IPs – IOM and IPs will be requested to monitor, track and report back to donors on possible aid diversion incidents. Assumption is that humanitarian principles are applied and adhered to for all stakeholders. 4) Risk of not renewing security council resolution remains very high. Closure of formal border crossings will highly affect cross border operation in NWS. Mitigation measures includes prepositioning of humanitarian assistance inside Syria as well as preparation of contingency planning.
