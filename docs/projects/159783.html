Project Summary

According to Humanitarian needs overview(HNO) 2020 the lack of protection from violence, abuse and neglect remains a key consequence of the current humanitarian crisis in Somalia for up to 3.2 million people. Out of those, 1.9 million are living in displacement and 1.2 million people belong to host communities. Whether directly exposed to armed conflicts or violence, or the destitution resulting from climatic shocks, affected communities face serious risks to their physical safety and integrity, and access to their rights.
The objective of the project is to strengthen case management for survivors of Gender-based violence(GBV). WOCCA will be working in Middle Shebelle,Hiraan,Bay(Baidoa) and lower Juba(Kismayo). The objective of the project is to strengthen case management for 600 survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV).The project staff who will be providing immediate assistance to the survivors and vulnerable population will be trained on case management and survivor centered approach.
WOCCA will use the recent data collected in Somalia service mapping to link and coordinate with other service providers to provide the survivors with immediate care.300 survivors and vulnerable person at risk of GBV will be also provided with tailored material assistance, basic emotional support and follow up on the services provided. We will also be providing 5500 solar lanterns to reduce risks to the most vulnerable members of the community and also increase outreach and build networks by training traditional birth attendants, imams and elders to promote awareness of GBV risks and to mitigate exposure to Gender-based violence.The community members (11,000) will be given awareness raising and basic GBV knowledge. The service providers,teachers,community leaders and religious leaders will be trained on GBV risks and referral pathways.The women at risk of GBV will be trained on life skills education to allow them cope up with difficult situations and overcome stress and pressure.

Project Activities

-Case management for SGBV (600) survivors including referrals by the case workers and recording cases in the GBVIMS. -Provide material assistance to 300 GBV survivors and 150 vulnerable persons at risk of GBV based on agreed individual assessments. -WOCCA will also use the referral pathways in place to refer survivors who give consent to legal assistance. -GBV caseworkers(5) and community support workers(10) will receive case management training on caring for survivors focusing on GBV survivors as well as incorporating training on survivor-centered approach. -11,000 community members sensitized in person and through media on GBV risk, consequences, available services, mitigation and increase their knowledge and understanding of basic human rights among the affected communities.-Provision of portable solar lanterns to 5500 of the most vulnerable IDP households who are most at risk of GBV during the evening period. -Community outreach training for Traditional birth attendants 70, community elders 40, imams 40, local authorities 50 and WASH committees 30 on GBV core concepts, protection guiding principles and referral mechanisms.-Protection mainstreaming training for 150 service providers including teachers and community health workers to ensure a coordinated effort for timely and efficient referral of cases. The training will also target new service providers captured in the newly updated service mapping. -Train 100 Women at risk of GBV on Life skills education and coping mechanisms.-Set up four (4) GBV centers for psychosocial referral.

Mitigation Measures

The protection rights and needs of estimated 3.2 million people, of whom 64 percent are children, and 48 percent are women and girls, are affected by armed conflict and violence and destitution triggered by climatic shocks, including prolonged drought conditions and floods. People in need of protection include households with significant proportion of persons with heightened vulnerability households with vulnerable members such as people with disability, medical conditions, children, elderly or pregnant/lactating women.Large portions of the displaced communities in need are members of marginalized groups, who lack support network in the areas of displacement or residence. Surveys conducted in 2018 confirmed that they are actually the majority of IDPs in Dollow, Kismayo, Mogadishu and Baidoa, four cities with large IDP populations (DDG). Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries by humanitarian actors, discrimination from host communities, lack of access to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement. IDPs lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. The project will therefore work towards mitigating the risks of exclusion. The project will also Support provision of protection services to affected communities, including in hard-to-reach areas and in IDP sites, targeting the most vulnerable, especially those at risk of exclusion. Protection of affected communities and access to protection services will be enhanced through regular protection monitoring and analysis, strengthened referral pathways, more protection that is systematic mainstreaming, expanded community engagement, capacity strengthening of humanitarian partners and increased evidence-based advocacy with duty bearers.

Please provide further explanation

Protection remains a huge concern in Somalia. SGBV cases is still on the rise according to the GBVIMS managed by the protection cluster During first two quarters of 2019 reported GBV incidents were physical assault (59%) followed by rape (14%), sexual assault (12%), psychological/emotional abuse (7%), denial of resources (5%), and forced/early marriage (3%). WOCCA has established direct relations with clan elders, protection and GBV cluster members and local authorities in targeted regions to gain access, enhance program awareness and monitoring on issues including complaints/feedback about operations. WOCCA closely engages local leaders and government officials to avert any security risk and possible threats. The project will help in training the community members comprised of men, women, girls, boys and even religious leaders on GBV risks, referral pathways, and services provided by WOCCA and other actors in the area. The project will also include vulnerable groups like the IDPs, women and youth in awareness raising to make them feel not left out. The project will have reporting mechanisms like suggestion box and mobile phones where the hotline will be shared with the IDPs to make it easy for them to report cases of SGBV to WOCCA for immediate help. The project will leave behind a well informed and educated community reducing the number of people undergoing GBV risks. WOCCA will train community leaders on psychosocial support meaning they can give proper counselling to those in the community with GBV issues even after WOCCA exits showing a long term solution. The community support workers can easily direct survivors for medical referral because they will be trained on referral pathways in the area. The religious leaders are the most respected in the community with the training they will be given on GBV they will continue to guide the community not to do any harm especially when it comes to rape, FGM and child marriages. The religious leaders will also train the community what Koran says on SGBV and the consequences of outdated practice like FGM.
