Project Summary

Conflict related sexual violence is probably the most prevalent form of violence in the ongoing Anglophone Regions of Cameroon which unfortunately is not properly reported and addressed because most victims do not self-identified as victims. GLOWA estimates that there are more than five thousand armed men in the region (both Non-state armed group and state security agents away from their families) engaged on combat. These sexually active combatants most of whom consume varied forms of drugs regularly abuse girls especially out-of-school and young women sexually resulting to children born thereof. They equally initiate them into drugs and other rituals of invincibility commonly used by combatants. The inability of those affected by conflict related sexual violence to self-identify as victims coupled by the targeting of victims by opposing sides of the conflict does not encourage them to speak out and seek support. Equally the regular occurrence of incidents of legal prosecution of survivors by state authorities for failure to report confirms the fact that both sides of the conflict consider victims as accomplices confirming limitations on rules of engagement and the increasing recognition of conflict related sexual violence as a war crime.
The project to Advance the Rights and Respond to the Needs of Survivors of Conflict-related Sexual Violence seeks to provide rehabilitation services to some 250 survivors of conflict related sexual violence who have become single mother as a result. The project shall build a database of the experiences of survivors to raise the profile of conflict related sexual violence in the project area. It shall provide economic opportunities through vocational training and small business initiatives to 250 survivors. It shall equally strengthen the operational capacity for GLOWA in the provision of holistic services to survivors of CRSV and work to break the myths and stigma associated with CRSV and children born thereof.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Eight years since the outbreak of violence in the crisis that hit the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon in 2016, conflict-related sexual exploitation and forced pregnancies has remain one of the unrecognized and unacknowledged forms of violence. Girls and young women trapped in sexual exploitation have been erroneously considered by opposing sides as enemies targeting them for life threatening violence and sometimes killing. Victims and survivors including children born thereof tend to be treated as undesirable social group based on the circumstance of their pregnancies and/or births. Children born as a result of CRSV suffer rejection from their mothers and weak mother-to-child bond. Survivors of conflict related sexual violence are equally being initiated into drugs and other rituals for the invincibility of combatants. The consequences on victims and survivors gain intensity with the lack of adequate response to the needs of survivors from humanitarian actors at different levels. Survivors of our ongoing support program of GLOWAHaus speak out to be in need of enhanced access to affordable and appropriate services, such as livelihood and economic empowerment activities, pro-bono legal advice, birth registration and legal adoption etc. GLOWA’s Consultations and dialogue with survivors have highlighted the recurrent requests for economic security through livelihood support for both survivors and children born as a result. Our project therefore aims to contribute to the implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 1325 and supporting resolutions as well as to the fulfillment of Cameroon’s obligations under the Committee on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) and the Convention on Preventing and Combating Violence against Women and Domestic Violence (Istanbul Convention), amongst other international instruments.
