Project Summary

Recurrent flooding in NW Syria severely impacts physical access to IDP sites by rendering roads completely or partially impassable. Heavy winter rains trigger flooding, primarily affecting self-settled IDP sites which do not have the appropriate infrastructure to withstand this elemental onslaught. Flood-damaged roads negatively impact the physical access for IDPs as well as the safety and dignity of residents. This further limits access to markets, livelihood opportunities, health, and other critical services.
The project will provide critical improvements to roads infrastructure through the following key activities:
1. Under ERL programming, BINAA will establish the inter-cluster advisory group that includes representatives of CCCM, SNFI, WASH, ERL, and Logistics Clusters, representatives of the NGOs operating in the Idleb governorate, and BINAA focal persons. The group's mission is to report emergencies requiring a response from the Rapid Response Team "RRT", in addition, to coordinating to avoid overlapping. This group will continue to conduct assessments including technical details for the potential locations of damages, in addition to the database of people who could not reach out in each location throughout Idleb governorate to determine the places most exposed to winter and flood damage. Based on findings, BINAA RRT will cover these needs ensuring rapid response for residents in the Idleb governorate to emergency maintenance work for both roads leading to camps and communities.
2. Establishing a ready-to-be-deployed Rapid Response Team to provide emergency infrastructure restoration service around IDP sites throughout Idleb Governorate during winter.
3. Rapid response mechanism training will be conducted for each sector, including the project supervisors, engineers, contractors, and local community representatives, to ensure clear understanding and prompt response to emergencies.
4. Deploying the team around Idleb governorate to conduct 500 emergency infrastructure restorations to flood-affected roads near IDP sites during winter throughout Idleb Governorate for critical flood-affected roads that connect IDP sites to host communities.
5. Training the local communities on Asset Management to improve the sustainability of infrastructure and increase social cohesion.
The interlinked ERL and Logistics activities have been developed in coordination with the ERL, SNFI, CCCM, and Logistics Clusters as well as with NGOs and UN agencies, including UNHCR, to ensure the project is integrated with ongoing and planned works to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter in IDP sites.
The project will observe the principle of “Do No Harm” through monitoring the markets, high coordination with stakeholders, and coordinating with ERL Cluster other NGOs to avoid duplication/overlapping.
The project is 24 months.
The project/Emergency infrastructure restorative service will directly benefit 46,352 HHs (278,375 individuals) deployed in IDP sites throughout Idleb Governorate.
BINAA has a proven track record in the rehabilitation of roads and drainage systems, as well as in the creation of livelihood opportunities. Overall, BINAA will be responsible for managing the project and its oversight against programmatic and fiduciary risks through efficient contractual arrangements and administrative support from teams in Gaziantep. This will strengthen overall risk management and ensure timely implementation of the project along with the high quality of activities and deliverables.
BINAA brings in the value addition of its expertise in infrastructure projects in NWS. BINAA's Early Recovery team, who is highly experienced in infrastructure projects, will provide technical guidance and expertise to ensure the quality and appropriateness of infrastructure works as well as better outcomes for beneficiaries.
The added value of BINAA is anchored around their extensive experience in ERL and rehabilitation activities in Idleb go.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021,- 74 per cent of the available basic infrastructures’ facilities are reported damaged, with 53 per cent of them reporting significant damage. The poor conditions of access ways and roads affected by ten years of conflict, in addition to the harm caused because of natural events such as heavy rains, negatively affect the possibility to boost the speed of local economic recovery.- The number of IDPs has increased. This has led to additional pressure on social and basic services, leading to a potential rupture in the social fabric, Health and education services quality and a decrease in social cohesion.BINAA conducted a Rapid Need Assessment within Aleppo and Idleb for most accessible areas asking LCs, HHs, and Key Informants about the number of points in the internal roads in their village that led to a complete or massive cut off in the 2020-2021 winter, and on average, about 1875 points affected by rainwater/flood were reported during targeted locations /for 5 months/
