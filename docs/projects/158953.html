Project Summary

The intervention will target Al Shaimaa area (Beit Lahiya) and Kherbit Al Adas area (Rafah) to address bad WASH situation (76% of the budget) with a complementary component that will address the related waterborne and nutritional health diseases for the children under 5 years (24% of the budget).
This application is drafted in line with WASH, Health and Nutrition Clusters’ main strategies and interventions. It aims at improving equitable access to WASH services for vulnerable families, while providing the nutritional health services for the affected children under 5 years and their caregivers. Women’s needs have been addressed and considered in the need’s assessment and priorities identification.
The activities will include installation of sewer lines, HH connections, repairs for HH WASH facilities, hygiene promotion and material support, distribution of water storage tanks and treatment for children who suffer from waterborne diseases. A total of 7244 individuals will be directly reached through both WASH and Nutrition interventions.

1 - Needs

In September 2019, DCA/NCA partners, MA'AN, Al Ahli Arab Hospital (AAH) and Near East Council of Churches (NECC), had conducted an assessment including a sample of 70 families in the targeted areas. It aimed to address the socioeconomic vulnerabilities, the accessibility to safe water, adequate sanitation and hygiene practices, the waterborne diseases of children under 5 years. The assessment included home visits, interviews with women, men, children’s caregivers and elderlies, hemoglobin, heights/lengths/weights tests, key informant interviews and consultations with the actors operating in the areas. The assessment revealed that 98.6% of the target populations are abject poor. The areas' population includes 28.4% children under 5, 0.7% elderlies, 1.2% people with disabilities, 1.5% women headed HH’s, and 7.7% pregnant or lactating women. Assessment shows that 69.1% of the families have poor/unsafe water storage tanks, 38.2% have poor/unsafe potable water storage tanks, 98.5% have toilets in poor conditions which are neither healthy nor safe, 97.1% have kitchens in poor conditions which are neither healthy nor safe, 27.9% are using cesspits having 94.7% of the cesspits are in poor/unsafe conditions, and 3.6% are using Septic Tanks having 100.0% of the Septic Tanks are in poor and unsafe conditions.Environmental and hygienic aspects of the living conditions in the targeted areas are poor in terms of wastewater infrastructure and most importantly, cleanliness and personal hygiene practices that preserve their health and environmental borne diseases. Only 18% of target group wash their hands with soap at critical times, while the detergents are available only in 12% of target group homes. The cesspit flooding in the target locations distributes pathogens in or close to the domestic area and can infect people via hands, food and water.Upon the assessment, 16.7% of the children are malnourished (sever and moderate wasting/stunting/underweight) which is much higher than the nationally reported figures and the internationally accepted figures (2.5%). The prevalence of Anaemia is 31.5%. Weak immune system of babies; imbalanced diet and high risk of infection through pathogens due to deteriorated WASH situation lead to high rate of anaemia, wasting and chronic malnutrition.The bad WASH conditions are associated with infectious diseases especially diarrheal disorders and parasitic infestations which lead to malabsorption of nutrients and consequently malnutrition. It is revealed the history of diarrheal episode in the previous month was present in 32.4% of targeted children. Among sick children in target locations, 66% presented with respiratory infections due to bad housing conditions. 11% present with infectious diseases and parasitic diseases due to bad hygiene and WASH resources. 53% of the stool analysis were positive for Gardia, E. Histolytica and Yeast, indicating poor sanitation and wash facilities.The bad hygiene conditions are manifested in sites that accumulate stagnant water—such as sinks, toilets, waste pipes. Poor hygiene conditions of kitchens, utensils, bathrooms, combined with shortage of hygienic material inside homes were recognized. The economic burden is one of the main causes of the bad hygiene situation among the surveyed sample. The women in 16.3% of the HH’s reported concerns about children hygiene, safety and security due to the conditions of WASH Facilities. All families who include disabilities and elderlies reported concerns due to the conditions of WASH facilities that are not adequate for the use of those vulnerable groups. The poor WASH conditions affect adversely the psychological wellbeing of women. Women have the responsibility of emptying the cesspits at time of sewage flooding which overburden women. Supporting the access to safe and adequate WASH services is a priority for women to enjoy well-being by living with dignity.

2 - Activities

WASH Activities: - Installation of 1200 meter (10 Inches) of sewer in Al Shaimaa area (100 HH’s - 592 individuals including 320 children, 140 women, 132 men)- Installation of 50 houses sewage connections targeting 50 households in in Al Shaimaa area (296 individuals including 160 children, 70 women, 66 men)- Rehabilitation of Sanitation facilities including toilets, sinks and water connections in both targeted areas (100 HH’s: 592 individuals including 320 children, 140 women, 132 men)- Distribution of 50 Roof Storage Tanks (1000 liters’ capacity) in both targeted areas - (296 individuals including 160 children, 70 women, 66 men).- Distribution of 70 Potable water storage tanks (200 liters’ capacity) in both targeted areas - (414 individuals including 224 children, 98 women, 92 men).- Hygiene material support and awareness raising in both targeted areas (950 families – 5624 individuals including 3040 children, 1330 women, 1254 men). The content of the hygiene kits will be determined in consultation with the communities. It can include Plastic Squeegee, sanitary napkins, Liquid and sponges for dishes washing, Dettol and Hand washing soap liquid. Nutrition Activities: - Screening activities for 2200 children under 5 years (to identify children with waterborne diseases– (1080 girls, 1120 Boys)- Treatment for 1100 children suffering from waterborne diseases (540 girls, 560 boys)- Nutritional health awareness targeting children’s caregivers (4000 individuals including 3200 women, 800 men) The intervention will mitigate the protection risks manifested in potential health hazard resulting from the deteriorated WASH conditions and will address women needs as the main responsible for rationing water inside the home and other hygiene related responsibilities. HH’s include children under five who suffer from waterborne diseases will be prioritized. Targeting criteria will include:- HH with no or unsanitary sanitation facilities, such as lack of handwashing sink, HH’s using unsafe cesspits and septic tanks, etc.- Vulnerable families with children under 5 with symptoms of waterborne diseases.- Socio-economic vulnerability considering poverty, family size, female headed HH’s, etc.- Protection criteria, WASH Facilities are not prepared to consider the special needs of elderlies, women, people with disabilities, WASH Facilities imposing threats/risks to children and women.The maintenance works will address women security and privacy, child protection, supporting the wellbeing of elderlies and people with disabilities. The communication material for both hygiene and nutritional health awareness will be tailored to consider the different roles and responsibilities for men, women and elderlies inside the HH. Awareness raising will focus on households’ safe wastewater disposal to prevent water borne diseases. Specific support will be provided for women in identifying and applying best hygiene, sanitation and nutritional health practices for activities specific to their roles and responsibilities such as safe water handling, safe personal hygiene, and safe children excreta disposal, handwashing, etc. The hygiene material support will consider the specific hygiene needs for women and girls particularly teenagers, pregnant, elderly and women with disabilities. Special attention will be made by trained female staff to deal with GBV incidents, where detected cases will be referred to specialized services through referral pathways of the protection cluster/CPWG.The intervention has been coordinated with CMWU, municipalities, WASH and Nutrition clusters.MAAN will be implement the WASH activities in both areas. NECC will implement the nutrition activities in Rafah, while AAH will implement the nutrition activities in Beit Lahiya. DCA/NCA will ensure proper coordination among the implementing partners to ensure harmony. The intervention responds directly to both WASH and Health cluster objectives.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA will support partners to increase the capability of the complaints and feedback mechanism to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. DCA/NCA and partners launched a complaints and feedback mechanism which provides safe and easy access for the targeted communities to raise their thoughts, feedback and complaints at project implementation level. The system will enable the beneficiaries to participate effectively in the project implementation and monitoring processes and inform possible correction throughout project implementation. The system uses ICT for receiving feedbacks either by online link, verbal feedback or recording complains received by other channels such as the hotline and feedback boxes. The system includes clear procedures to guarantee the accuracy of receiving, recording and documenting the complaints and feedback. The system includes restricted procedures to ensure privacy, confidentiality and information security to deal with gender-sensitive complaints. Eventually, the system supports to ensure the timely and effective response to all complaints and the follow up of the response aftermath to check if the taken actions were appropriate to address the complaint/feedback by doing the needed corrections. Community engagement committees will be formed to build community confidence and networks and ensure efficient and continuous participation of the community in the implementation and monitoring processes and to inform decision making. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project design recognizes the rights and needs of beneficiaries in terms of safety, security, and dignity and proposes activities that foster the realization of these needs and rights. Furthermore, it adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to addressing the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. The project activities will enhance the safe access of families with malnourished children to appropriate WASH assistance relevant to their immediate needs including water storage and supply, better hygienic environment and hygiene messages and hygiene items appropriate to their context and living conditions. The provided activities will support to alleviate the public health risks that the targeted families are facing especially women and girls. It will also improve their living conditions, improve the nutritional status of children, mitigate the impact of violation and enable them to have better access to water and sanitation services and most importantly live in dignity and equality. The monitoring process for the project will include regular field visits and random calls to check if beneficiaries are satisfied with the services provided to them and that they are treated with dignity and respect. During the inception period, the project elaborate an environment and social impact assessment (ESIA) that will be conducted in order to ascertain the possible environmental and social impacts of the infrastructural subcomponents; detail the possible environmental and social impacts of the construction and maintenance works; and to manage, mitigate, and monitor any possible negative impacts during the design, construction and operational phases of the project; and where possible enhance positive impacts. This will include to conduct consultations with the various stakeholders, to develop an environmental management plan that covers the all phases of design, construction, operation and closure. All necessary safety and security measures will be taken into consideration as usual when implementing the project activities to prevent incidences and reduce safety and security risks. DCA/NCA and partners will also keep close monitoring for the security situation in the Gaza Strip and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. Timeframe and activities will be adjusted in case any emergency happens to avoid security and safety risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to WASH and nutrition services w for the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable families. This will support to alleviate the public health risks that these families are facing especially women and girls. It will also improve their living conditions, improve the nutritional status of children, mitigate the impact of violation and enable them to have better access to water and sanitation services and most importantly live in dignity and equality. DCA/NCA and partners adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions considering the humanitarian principles. Partners will undertake an assessment to select the project beneficiaries based on clear selection criteria. The assessment will be conducted by trained field workers, who have the required skills and expertise to collect precise data, under the supervision of the M&E team. The collected data will be analyzed and scored following an accurate statistical methodology and using Excel to ensure the accuracy of selection. The final selection results will be also verified and approved by partners’ management and DCA/NCA. All selected beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society.The nutrition partner will conduct health screening in order to select the families with malnourished children. These families will be subjected to socio-economic assessment to address the socio-economic vulnerability of the families and to select the poorest families. The socio-economic assessment will measure the family income through calculating food and non-food consumption and compare it with the family income resources. The form also addresses families coping mechanisms, children access to education and different child protection concerns. A clear selection and scoring criteria will be elaborated to ensure the selection of the neediest families to benefit from the project activities. The criteria will give priority to the women headed HH, HH with elderly people, PwD and pregnant women. The following actions will be considered to ensure providing equitable access to the basic services for men, women, boys and girls: 1. Ensuring significant involvement of women through the participation in the community engagement committee.2. Having safe procedures to guide the handling of gender-sensitive complaints through the complaint’s mechanism3. Orienting the project team on gender policy and GBV incidences. 4. Ensuring that the developed baseline tools address the perceptions and concerns of women.5. Ensuring that the baseline information contained gender segregated data
