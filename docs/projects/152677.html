Project Summary

The humanitarian needs in South Sudan in particular nutrition situation remains at critical level which has progressively deteriorated in the past 4 years due to sever food insecurity, increased burden of communicable diseases and limited access to health services, limited safe water and sanitation facilities, wide spread insecurity and displacement of people with 63% of population estimated to be food insecure. Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in South Sudan ((IPC phases 3, 4 & 5; Feb-April 2017).
In Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau and Raga Counties), the nutrition situation remains at critical level (GAM rate > 15.7%) and is likely to worsen due to recurrent conflict and restricted access, while in Greater Equatoria (Terekeka and Budi Counties), the nutrition situation in remains at critical levels. Additionally, FSNMS assessment round 20 indicated GAM rate for Wau at 19.6 (SAM 6.2%) and Raga 15.6% (SAM 4.5%) all above critical levels and FSNMS round 20, report indicated Budi and Terekeka counties with GAM rates of 20.9% (SAM 5.9%) and 7.7% (SAM 1.7%) respectively. This nutrition situation require intervention to prevent situation progressing into farming as per the IPC projection. Recurrent Tribal/clan conflicts and insecurity results into destruction of livelihoods and displacement of people, and hence worsen the situation.
The burden of malnutrition has a direct impact on children less than 5years, women of child bearing age (15-49yrs) including Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Therefore, the need for management of sever acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM), pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in these Counties is still huge as indicated by critical GAM levels and is likely to worsen without intervention and it’s on this basis that AFOD is submitting a proposal to implement nutrition activities in the above selected counties to avert death, nutrition related complications and promote health.
This project will support management of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) and preventive services (behaviour change communication on maternal, infant and young child nutrition - MIYCN). The target beneficiaries are children aged 6 – 59 months, pregnant and lactating women, women and care takers of children 0 – 23 months for nutrition education
AFOD will adopt combination strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static, mobile OTP and TSFP site, including use of expanded criteria in case of supply shortage, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening and assessment of immunization status using child health card), WASH (provision of safe water, soap for hand washing education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL (vegetable gardening, cooking demonstration, and post-harvest handling that preserve food nutrients), Education (establishment of health and nutrition clubs in schools to promote nutrition education) and protection of beneficiary by community for complementarity and synergy and preposition of supplies for desired treatment outcome. Key stake holders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complain desk, community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback.
Broad Objective:
To provide treatment for SAM and MAM for children 6-59 months, PLWs and MIYCN services for caretaker of children under 2 years in Raga County to prevent death related to malnutrition and promote health
Specific objectives;
1) Improve access to quality integrated management of SAM and MAM in Wau, Raga, Terekeka and Budi Counties
2) Increase access to Maternal and Infant Young Child Nutrition services for Pregnant and Lactating Women and care givers of children under 23 months in Wau, Raga, Terekeka and Budi Counties
3) Strengthen nutrition information system and staff capacity in Wau, Raga, Terekeka and Buidi Counties
4) Promote integration of Nutrition services into WASH, Health and FSL for synergy and complementarity in Wau, Raga, Terekeka and Budi Counties.

Needs

Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in South Sudan ((IPC phases 3, 4 & 5; Feb-April 2017). In Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau and Raga Counties), the nutrition situation remains at critical level (GAM rate > 15.7%) and is likely to worsen due to recurrent conflict and restricted access which interferes with service delivery in most of the locations and displacement of the community in hard to reach location. FSNMS assessment round 20 indicated GAM rate for Wau at 19.6 (SAM 6.2%) and Raga 15.6% (SAM 4.5%) all above critical levels. IPC food security projection indicated the state to be in emergency (phase 4), requiring intervention to prevent situation progressing into farming. In Greater Equatoria (Terekeka and Budi Counties), the nutrition situation in remains at critical levels. FSNMS round 20, report indicated Budi and Terekeka counties with GAM rates of 20.9% (SAM 5.9%) and 7.7% (SAM 1.7%) respectively. In Terekeka County, the situation is likely to worsen due to recurrent tribal/clan conflicts that results into destruction of livelihoods and displacement of communities with limited access to food, safe water and sanitation facilities. In Budi county, there is poor road networks, continued insecurity in the areas and limited agricultural activities, and the situation is likely to worsen as indicated by IPC 2018 projected food security outcome of phase 4 (emergency level). Therefore, this basis that AFOD is submitting a proposal to implement nutrition activities in the above selected counties.

Activities

• Conduct biweekly community mobilization and screening to increase uptake for nutrition services• Admit and treat SAM, MAM & PLW in the OTP and TSFP program• Refer SAM cases with complications to stabilization centre• Conduct home visits for children enrolled in nutrition program with poor treatment progress• Conduct nutrition training for nutrition, health, and non-skilled staff on new CMAM guideline• Train Health workers and community volunteers on the MIYCN package• Conduct individual MIYCN counselling for PLWs and caregivers of children 0-<23 months• Establish and support mother to mother support groups • Print and distribute MIYCN guidelines and counselling cards to OTP/TSFP sites and for mother to mother support groups• Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community etc.• Conduct nutrition sensitive activities including, kitchen/backyard vegetable gardening, gender and protection mainstreaming and hygiene promotion activities• Screen SAM cases with fever for malaria in all static OTP/TSFP sites• Link SAM/MAM cases that tested positive for malaria treatment• Provide safe water in all OTP/TSFP sites for appetite test and hand washing• Conduct community dialogue meeting with community to obtain feedback and improve nutrition service delivery• Conduct join support supervision with stakeholdersConduct malaria for cases enrolled in the programscreen children 6-59 months for immunization status and refer appopriately.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Project monitoring measures access in terms of availability of services needed, availability of service providers to offer the services and physical access to the services e.g. distance or time taken to get services, insecurity and poor roads affecting access to services. Although, most nutrition facilities remain accessible to the population, inaccessibility due to heavy rains and insecurity remain a major barrier to access to services by beneficiaries. AFOD in collaboration with the local authorities and other partners will continuously identify barriers to access by beneficiaries and will continue to work together with all other clusters in all responses to address the barriers identified and ensure integration of services. AFOD will further strengthen the current coordination mechanisms with partners through appropriate information sharing, updated service mapping and collective decision making.
