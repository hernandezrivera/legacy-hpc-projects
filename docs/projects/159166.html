Project Summary

The project aims to deliver life-saving WASH assistance to reduce acute needs among most vulnerable settlements and communities and provide/restore sustainable access to safe water services in targeted settlements and communities. More than 20% of the country’s population (2.7 million people) require support to access basic WASH services in 2020. Of this people in need, about 2.5 million are in severe need of humanitarian WASH assistance (1.7M children, 0.4M are women, 0.4M men). Additionally, about 0.1 5M of them are people who will require a specific attention (0.1M are elderly and 0.4M are people living with disabilities). IDPs represent 32% of the total WASH people in need for 2020.
Access to basic WASH services remains a priority for around one third of the IDPs (0.8M) in the country. In those settings, users of WASH facilities, especially women and children are usually at higher risk of facing violence or other protection related issues. As country’s vulnerability to climate change is projected to increase, lack of durability of water and sanitation outputs continue to limit the sustainability of other sector outcomes. About 49% of the country’s population do not have access to a regular and stable improved water source for drinking and only 52% among them walk less than 15 minutes to reach their main drinking water source, while another 36% of Somali population do not have enough water per day to cover their basic needs both for drinking and domestic use.
Unreliable access to water source and Inadequate access to WASH services is a leading cause of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) and Malnutrition, especially where health services are too few and/or too distant. It is also a cause of relapse when treated patients and children return to their communities where safe water and sanitation coverages are limited, resulting in limited sustainability of health/nutrition programs. Lack of access to water and adequate sanitation in schools is also a factor contributing to poor school attendance. Lack of access to water for humans and for livestock remain one of the leading causes of displacement, conflict and diseases. Only 38% of the population have access to their own household latrine or share it with one or two household (10%) or with three households or more (28%) or do not have access at all (24%). About half of the households (45%) report problems with domestic waste near their household. Coping mechanisms for sanitation include sharing a latrine with more households than in normal conditions (28%), open defecation (17%) and using un-hygienic/un-clean facilities (13%).
In addition, only 26% of households across Somalia report availability of water and soap at latrines and 38% at home. In case of non-availability of soap, people wash their hands with soap substitutes (19%) or do not wash their hands (13%). Other coping mechanisms include washing hands less frequently (9%), borrow cash or hygiene material (5%) or relying on humanitarian assistance (4%). Availability of water and soap in toilets was reported as the main barrier to healthy practices by 61% of households. Also, only 25% of households have access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) material at home or can access it through their local market.
Availability of water container is also limited, with 25% and 72% of household respectively reporting their non-availability for transporting and household storage. When water is scarce, people reduce domestic water consumption (37%), reduce drinking water consumption (9%) and/or rely on seasonal unsafe water sources (9%) as main negative coping mechanism. Worryingly, 7% of households request their children, especially girls, to walk on longer distances and/or fetch water more frequently than when water is more available. The project is expected to address some of the above gaps and reach a total of about 250,000 individuals without double count (10% of the total target for WASH sector).

Project Activities

1.Extension/rehabilitation of existing water supply system; The project will rehabilitate strategic water points in consultation with the different sectors of the affected communities.2.Establish and train gender inclusive Water management committees and equip them with tools and materials to operate and maintain the water supply systems, for local ownership and sustainable management of the facilities beyond the intervention period. 3. Rehabilitation/construction of shared latrines for the affected communities in the target locations. This will be done through a consultative process that will involve women, men, girls and boys in the selection of locations and designs of all facilities so as to conform to their culture and preferences. Separate and lockable (from the inside) latrines will be constructed for males and females indicated by pictograms. 4.Establish and train gender inclusive hygiene mobilizers to improve hygiene practices among target communities. All hygiene promotion activities will target both men and women to ensure equal access and will be conducted at household level in local vernacular language. 5. Purchase and provision of water containers for transport and storage to poor and vulnerable households in disaster affected target locations. 6. Provision of appropriate Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTSS) for 2 months minimum; HH water treatment and purification tablets will be provided to affected communities in consultation with the women and men so as to appeal to their test and preferences. 7. Emergency water trucking with temporary distribution systems for 2 months minimum; Emergency water trucking will be done as the last resort for the hardest hit communities and institutions that are facing acute water shortages. This will be done in close collaboration and consultation with regional WASH cluster Coordinators.8.Purchase /distribution of hygiene kits to disaster affected households that will target vulnerable women and girls and will include specific items such as items for managing menstrual hygiene among poor and vulnerable women/girls.9. WASH in Schools-Provision of emergency water, sanitation services and hygiene promotion activities to schools affected by disasters.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization:Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance.CARE adopts a broad-based consultative approach in its assessments, project design and implementation. Broad based in the sense that every section or group of the community members are consulted. Special attention is given to members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups in a manner to create animosity and draw hostility from the majority groups. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes.CARE engages broad based gender inclusive relief community committees at village levels and project sites. Women, girls and boys are part of these project committees and they are coached/mentored and encouraged to articulate and bring out challenges facing them to the decision-making table at the village level. 3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. CARE’s Staff and partners are trained to on PSEA. Protection is mainstreamed in all CARE’s interventions and there are dedicated staff who conduct high level awareness campaigns on prevention of all forms of Gender Based Violence (GBVs) at community level. 3c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. CARE ensures access to humanitarian assistance and services does not result to exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence in terms of distance and location of the assistance and services. The project team will consult all gender and age groups when selecting the site for water and Sanitation facilities. Water and sanitation facilities will be located in sites that are close proximity to the homesteads. Latrines will be located in properly lit areas and with good view from all sites. Male and female will have separate latrines and they should be lockable from inside. Hand washing stations will be gender and age appropriate 3d. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection:Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. CARE adopts a “Do no harm approach” so as to ensure delivery of humanitarian assistance does not affect peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources. CARE conducts involves community leadership in the design and implementation of the project. Clear guidelines and beneficiaries’ selection are shared with communities at public gatherings at the start of the project. All vulnerable groups from all sections/clans including host community and IDPs will be targeted proportionately to avoid backlash from any group that will be left out of the intervention 4b. Protection mainstreaming: Beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution. This will be mitigated by having an establishing mutually agreed beneficiary selection criteria that identifies the most vulnerable groups from the target communities. This will be followed by rigorous public vetting and house to house beneficiaries’ verification exercise and ensure only those who met the agreed criteria are registered and receive humanitarian assistance. 4d. Protection mainstreaming: Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms. CARE has functional and robust feedback, complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM system in place for reporting beneficiaries’ concerns. Please provide further explanation

The proposed target locations are sufficiently safe and stable. CARE has a risk management strategy which identified potential risks in target areas. This strategy has an elaborate appropriate risk management matrix and mitigation measures to minimize the risks. Regular reviews are done to ensure the risk matrix remains relevant to the changing dynamics. The project is linked to the government priorities as CARE has strong and cordial relationships with the government and local community leaders. CARE coordinates and collaborates its interventions with key government agencies both at federal and State levels. The program teams collaborate and involve local community leaders in identification of target villages based on needs identified through CARE’s assessment and FSNAU reports. Gender-inclusive village committees support CARE in identification, selection and verification of beneficiaries using agreed-upon criteria. The project will build on CARE’s experience and cordial relations at grassroots level across Somalia. The proposed interventions, are expected to meet the immediate humanitarian needs of the affected population. These will also be link up with other ongoing activities in the development projects aimed at reducing risks and vulnerabilities and building resilience to future shocks. This will promote complementary/synergy and cushion beneficiaries from slipping back to crisis. In order to increase disaster risk reduction strategies, CARE will ensure timely, coordinated and high quality assistance to communities in target areas where disaster losses pose a threat to people’s life and livelihoods. This coordination will involve building capacities local state agencies in disaster responses in providing early warning systems of alert before the onset of droughts, floods, cyclone etc. The project has inbuilt sustainability as it will establish and train gender inclusive Water management committees and equip them with tools and materials to operate and maintain water supply systems. The rehabilitated water of points will be handed over to Water Management Committees (WMCs) for local ownership and sustainability. All WMCs members will be trained on operation and maintenance and equipped with basic tools. The respective ministry of water in each State of the federal government will also be involved in the handover of the water facilities to the WMCs and they will sign a tripartite agreement (CARE, Ministry of water and WMCs) to ensure continuity after the project. CARE will facilitate accountability to communities affected by crisis, and improve the quality of services provided by monitoring quality of output using the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) as a framework. Conformity to CHS will be verified and internal processes assessed to establish if they meet the actions and organizational responsibilities set out within the CHS.
