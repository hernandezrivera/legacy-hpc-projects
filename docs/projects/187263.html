Project Summary

Over 2.18 million people remain displaced across north-east Nigeria due to the ongoing insurgency by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) and subsequent conflict; mostly unable to return to their areas of origin. For many, their means of livelihood and houses have been completely destroyed. The limited availability of land, due to the proscribed civilian space within the protracted insurgency, has also rendered humanitarian interventions very challenging. Most of the Internally displaced persons (IDP) camps are overcrowded and cannot be expanded due to security concerns, competing priorities for government owned land as well as tracts of flood prone lands rendered unusable during the rainy season.
IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) also identified 1,918,03 returnees (including returnees from abroad and IDPs that have returned to their LGAs of origin). It is evident that IDPs, returnees as well as the local host population have been greatly affected by the conflict and there are IDPs that are still unable to return to their places of origin due to multiple reasons, including lack of land, housing, and livelihood opportunities. With the ongoing return of IDPs to their place of origin and relocation close to their areas, housing, land, and property (HLP) issues are evolving and dynamic.
IOM is actively engaged in land advocacy across LGAs and works closely with the Camp Coordination Camp Management (CCCM)/ Shelter& Non-food items (NFI) Sector in the prioritization of the land advocacy efforts. IOM will continue to advocate for and work on HLP rights in close coordination with the HLP Sub-Working Group that functions under the umbrella of the Protection Sector in north-east Nigeria. During the project implementation, IOM ensures incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety, and dignity for the returnees, IDPs, and host communities. IOM will provide services – in proportion to need and without barriers (e.g., discrimination), with special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty in accessing HLP rights and services. Land for accommodating displaced populations is allocated by the State Emergency Management Agency SEMA and National Emergency Management Agency NEMA or the relevant local government authorities. Before construction, IOM ensures that land issues are cleared by the Local Government Authority, SEMA/NEMA and the CCCM/Shelter-NFI Sector. IOM ensures that proper site assessments and planning is carried out in conjunction with negotiating land issues with NEMA/SEMA Sector partners and LGA Authorities.
In 2021, IOM also conducted HLP assessments and in-depth tenure mapping to identify problems related to HLP in Borno State. The most significant issues identified were eviction due to lack of rent payment, return of the property's original owner, disputes, and damaged or destroyed houses resulting from the insurgency. In 2022, IOM aims at expanding these assessment exercises to Adamawa state as well, given the relative stability and high number of returnee populations in the state. The HLP need assessments will enable to understand the HLP dynamics in particular areas where a significant number of IDPs are returning or resettling. .
Under this project, IOM will continue to study and identify significant bottlenecks associated with HLP and propose corresponding solutions to contribute and achieve durable solutions targeting returnees, IDPs, and host community members. Furthermore, IOM will work towards mainstreaming the IOM approach to Housing, Land and Property issues across IOM program implementation in north-east Nigeria. The proposed activities will build on IOM ongoing activities in north-east Nigeria with technical support from IOM Field HLP expert and Global HLP Advisor.

1 - Needs

Despite the protracted emergency context, an increasing number of individuals choose to return from urban locations of displacement to home areas in military garrison towns in rural LGAs. The Government of Borno State, the largest IDP hosting State in the country, announced their intentions to close the IDP camps and promote relocation and return of IDPs to their communities of origin and other “safe zones”. Between May and November 2021, three camps, namely NYSC, Farm Centre and Bakassi Camp have been closed in Borno. In line with the 25-Year Borno State Development Plan, and the related return and relocation agenda, these camp closures are likely to continue. This has increased the number of IDPs returning to their place of origin or to LGA capital close to their original places. In most cases, it was observed during a recent IOM assessment that the houses of these returnees are occupied by other displaced persons, or their houses are damaged and inhabitable, or it is not accessible. This results in an increasing frequency of competing land ownership claims emerging from returnees who find their personal or family property occupied by IDPs either in an official capacity (allocated through the government process outlined above under emergency measures) or through de facto occupancy by displaced persons. Most legal squatters on lands, secondary occupants, and IDPs living in rented houses are at risk of forced evictions. This forced eviction is mainly due to their inability to pay rent and the protracted conflict and displacement. Most north-east Nigeria locations, especially in Borno state, have remained inaccessible to humanitarian actors as well as to returning IDPs, making the return of IDPs unmanageable. Even those returning in most cases find their houses, land, or building occupied. Furthermore, it is important to note, that most of these movements are not to the communities of origin but to peri-urban areas such as garrison towns, where the IDPs have not lived before, and where no provisions have been put in place for newly arrived IDPs to access basic necessities or services. This further necessitates the need to work on emerging HLP issues. Lack of land documentation and property mapping in return areas is a significant challenge affecting returnees and IDPs. Because most of the communities displaced during the crisis have lost their belongings, including their documentation for the property they own, or they might not have them from the start. There is a lack of comprehensive regular assessment to understand the dynamics of changes in return locations. More individuals return spontaneously or voluntarily, looking at the situation in their place of origin. Besides, humanitarian actors' access in most deep field locations is restricted because of insecurity and travel restrictions. All these necessitate the need to continuously conduct a situational assessment on HLP issues to understand the context dynamics. In 2021, IOM recruited an HLP Advisor with a legal background to support HLP integration in various programs and contribute to land advocacy through Stakeholder engagement and representation of IOM at HLP forums. Access to land advocacy will continue in 2022 to contribute to decongest existing camps and identify new lands to accommodate newly displaced and returning IDPs. IOM will also engage a local Non-Governmental Organization (NGO) as an implementing partner who will support and conduct HLP activities as access to the deep field locations are becoming difficult due to insecurity. The engagement will also enhance capacity of the national NGO actors on HLP activities undertaken. At the request of the NE Ambassadors Group (NEAG) and the Resident Coordination and Humanitarian Coordinator, IOM, in coordination with UNHCR, is leading the development of a Durable Solutions Compact. This project will be aligned with and contribute to the broader efforts towards these inter-agency efforts on durable solutions.

2 - Activities

IOM will continue to engage in HLP advocacy across NE Nigeria working closely with the CCCM/Shelter/NFI Sector and the HLP Sub-Working Group. Key activities will include: 1. Conduct advocacy on land. IOM HLP team will support CCCM, WASH, Livelihood, and Shelter/NFI Units and the CCCM/Shelter/NFI Sector with access to land advocacy in deep field locations by engaging relevant government authorities and with traditional council and private landowners to access land authorization/consent for land use for the IOM programme. IOM will continue to work closely with HLP Sub Sector Working group on land advocacy and to proactively address HLP issues associated with lands before use to ensure that all lands are authorized for use by relevant authorities and documented. 2. Conduct HLP workshops for the implementing partners to build their capacity as part of broader inter-agency and multi-sectoral efforts towards the resolution of internal displacement in north-east Nigeria’s Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States. 3. 3: Conduct awareness-raising for local authorities and community leaders. Awareness-raising sessions on evictions and how to manage cases of forced eviction will be conducted in locations where refugees and IDPs return, targeting local authorities and community leaders in the project area. Throughout, IOM will move further towards a community-based planning approach, to ensure that interventions use participatory approaches, allowing IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community members to choose their preferred solutions, define their own priorities and actively participate in the choice, planning and implementation of HLP activities.4. Provide women, girls, boys and men information and awareness counselling and legal advice on HLP.5. Support IDPs, including those returning to their LGAs of origin or resettling, with cash for rent and rent subsidy. The selection of beneficiaries and locations will be based on detailed site and needs assessments and the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) return intention surveys. Detailed assessments are conducted to identify needs, gaps and associated risks during targeting and assistance. IOM has recently carried out an assessment to determine the availability of housing stock and the general cost of rent and what factors determine the rental amount across some locations in Borno state. Two interventions have been designed to address the needs identified. They are: (i) Cash for rent: the target will be IDPs who are renting but are at risk of eviction due to inability to pay rent and HH that have already been evicted. (ii) Rehabilitation for rent: this will target IDPs currently renting houses with significant structural damage and below habitable standards. The houses will be repaired in lieu of rent for about three years, depending on the annual rental amount and the extent of repairs carried out. 6. Conduct an assessment on the functionality of existing statutory, traditional, and emerging ad-hoc dynamics to understand the project implementation areas' situation. 7. Conduct HLP mapping and develop HLP database. A comprehensive mapping will be conducted to deepen understanding of the extent and type of HLP issues inhibit sustainable return in locations receiving refugees from Cameroon and returnees and IDPs. This will be followed by a development of a database containing tenure information of IDPs and returnees to help actors involved in relocations/returns to resolve their disputes. 8. Strengthen case management system and establish a referral pathway. IOM will work to strengthen the case management system within the existing formal and informal institutions and strengthen and/or establish a referral system to address the disputed cases and coordinate with dispute resolution institutions.9. Support IDPs, including those returning to their LGAs of origin or resettling, with the processing of title documentation and other forms of HLP documentation.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, elderly, and persons with disabilities, among others. Mapping of community structures and having focus group discussions with IDP representatives, Persons With Specific Needs such as disabled, child headed households, pregnant and lactating women as well as other vulnerable groups will ensure the community is fully involved in the decision making process. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. IOM has already taken several steps to ensure accountability to affected population: findings from beneficiary surveys, some of which have already been incorporated into the design of this project. IOM also actively uses practical recommendations from beneficiary feedback surveys as advocacy tools at the sector level to influence change.
