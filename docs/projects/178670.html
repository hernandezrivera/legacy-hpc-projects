Project Summary

The response aims to increase the resilience of vulnerable communities in rural Salamiyeh where basic social services have been severely affected throughout the crisis and, more recently, exacerbated by the deteriorating economy and lack of fuel in the country. Consequently, vulnerable communities in rural Salamieh lack access to basic and quality WASH services.
The response is in line with HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 3 “Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.” - Specific objective 3.3. “Access to basic services such as electricity, water, education or health is improved”.
The targeted areas are part of another project funded by SHF, where AKAH has been supporting water trucking services since last October 2020. Through the regular monitoring process and the established feedback mechanism, reasons for water shortage were addressed in consultation with the local communities and the potential solutions were discussed with the water authority in Hama. In the Q1 progress report for SHF, one of the targeted communities – Eastern bari- was specifically mentioned to show the dire need for sustainable solutions.
The response targets the villages of Eastern Bari, As-Saan, Tal Jdeed, and Fritan located in Eastern Bari and As-Saan sub-districts in Salamiyeh district to improve the existing water production and supply system by installing hybrid PV systems to produce electricity and run the existing boreholes for at least 10-13 hours per day. The total population of the target villages will benefit from the response. OCHA’s population figures are used to determine the total population which is approximately 22,000 – however worth noting that recent consultations with the water authority indicate that the total population may be higher (approximately 34,000).
In the targeted locations there are seven (7) boreholes with a capacity range of 12-20 m3 /hr working daily for a maximum of 3 hours due to the applied electricity rationing system in the area and lack of fuel needed to utilize alternative energy resources. The produced daily quantities of water from the targeted boreholes is about 45-168m3 /day, which is insufficient to meet the daily needs of the population. In total the daily share per person is less than 15 liters. By installing the hybrid PV systems, the daily pumping capacity is expected to improve the pumping efficiency by 60-70%.
The project will establish a community-based monitoring and operations support mechanism that aims to enhance the participation of communities in managing local resources, addressing the low efficiency of water services; designing solutions and raising awareness on water saving and improving the services’ efficiency and continuity.

4 - Needs

Vulnerable communities in rural areas of Salamieh District (80% living on less than $2/day), do not have the minimum WASH requirements to meet basic needs and protection from diseases spread, with a particular concern on the health risks of girls and women. Lack of proper sewage systems and contaminated water has contributed to increased cases of life-threatening water-borne diseases, such as Hepatitis A and leishmaniasis disease (400 cases recorded in Salamieh District in 2020). Water scarcity has led to negative coping mechanisms of households and increased dependency on water trucking services. In 2021, the severe lack of fuel and deterioration of the economy negatively impacted the operations of WASH services (i.e. power cuts for around 20 hours/day). The selected communities of As-Saan and Eastern Bari sub-districts have the highest populations and high dependency of temporal alternative resources of water like water trucking; As-Saan, eastern Bari, Tal Jded and Fritan villages. It is estimated that targeted communities receive less than 10 litres of water/person/day, which is below Sphere minimum standards (15 litres/person/day). Currently, there are 7 boreholes serving these communities; 4 in As-Saan village (12, 14, 14, 16 m3/h), one in Eastern Bari village (12 m3/h), 2 in Tal Jded and Fritan (15 & 20 m3/h). The boreholes work for 2-3 hours/day only, producing between 45-168 m3/day of water, which at best does not cover more than 10-20% of the daily needs. AKAH is trucking 320m3 /month to As-Saan and Eastern Bari villages, which is unsustainable. According to Hama Water Authority, two boreholes are serving Eastern Bari (24m3 /hr), one is currently supported by another agency to install PV system.

5 - Implementation of activities

AKAH will implement the activities in cooperation with the local communities, the planned activities are: A. Provision and Installation of the Hybrid Solar Systems including establsihsing the protection measures in each location. B. Communities participation and ownership: A community-based monitoring and operations support mechanism will be established in the targeted communities and run by local committees that will assist the Hama Water Authority in managing the projects and work with the communities on enhancing the ownership and accountability practices; the committees will be trained and provided with the capacity and materials required to fully meet their responsibilities.C. In cooperation with the local committees, awareness raising activities on water saving, energy efficiency and saving, and the renewable energy will be implemented in the targeted locations. D. Monitoring and Evaluation process, including Regular technical assessment of the systems performance and support the regular maintenance works and regular monitoring and follow up processes and FGDs with the local communities.E. Document the community engagement exercise, showing the results of enhancing the community participation and ownership in improving the efficiency of managing the local resources and services provision at the community level.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In Salamieh District, the risk of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) is considered high. AKAH has a plan for MRE and ERW incidents’ victims' assistance. A communication with UNMAS has been launched to discuss the areas of cooperation and the priorities in the targeted areas in Salamieh district. The involved WASH staff and the local-communities committees will be trained on the mitigation measures along with the implementation of the AKAH-supported Mine risk education (MRE) program in the area for raising awareness among the beneficiaries. AKAH will ensure that the ’Do no Harm’ principle is systematically applied and the local communities are continuously contacted and engaged in assessing the impact of the project and addressing any protection issues. The local committees might be exposed to a risk of being not fully accepted by the communities and criticized, in similar cases, AKAH in cooperation with AKF, is providing capacity building to the committees on addressing the community’s behavior and the mitigation measures required to reduce these cases.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Market price fluctuation affects activity implementation. The donor will be constantly consulted and informed on the situation and potential solutions.Shortage of fuel may have an impact on some operational activities; i.e. workers or materials transportation to the sites. Fuel shortage might also cause additional energy cut-off hours in the targeted areas. AKAH will consider these situations throughout the project and ensure proper solutions in coordination with the contractor and water authority. COVID-19 pandemic might affect the project, either due to infections among team members or government restrictions on the movement or institutional work. AKAH will provide the project team with PPEs and awareness sessions on the prevention measures. In case partial/full lockdown is applied, the timeline will be reviewed and amended accordingly in coordination with the water authority and the donor. Lack of specialized workers to do the installation in the targeted sites. AKAH currently – in cooperation with AKF- is implementing a project targeting agricultural areas with solar systems to improve irrigation systems, the work is managed by technical local teams trained. The same modality can be utilized for this project.Local communities are not fully cooperative with the committees. AKAH will provide capacity building to the committees’ members on behaviour change related communication at the local level and will rely on the existing social structures to raise the communities’ awareness of the ownership and the participation aspects.The installed systems may be exposed to damage due to human or natural reasons. AKAH has considered providing protection metal fence in the system's design.
