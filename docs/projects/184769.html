Project Summary

In Qandala District of Bari Region, majority of communities rely on shallow wells, and/or trucked water. TC GATI brought heavy rainfall, which has led to flooding which damaged sewerage systems causing them to overflow, potentially contaminating water supply. Access into the affected areas remains challenging, and it can also be expected that the supply of trucked water may be disrupted. Access to sanitation (latrines) was already inadequate, and open defecation was widespread. These factors combined increase the risk of waterborne diseases such as acute watery diarrhoea. There is likely to be a need to provide safe water through the distribution of water treatment chemicals (aqua tabs) and storage items (buckets, jerry cans). According to preliminary reports, the affected population’s access to basic health services has not been impacted considerably, with emergency mobile teams still able to operate. Somalia remains in a state of protracted crisis which increases the impact of the challenging climatic conditions, amidst inadequate coping strategies and development assistance.
There is limited expectation that the government and associated other actors support the affected population due to administrative challenges, security and political constraints.
Tentatively 1,900 HHs (11,400 people) will be targeted through this project – specifically those affected in Qandala district of Bari region in Puntland. This equates to 50% of the affected population as per Humanitarian Needs Assessment report 2021 (Qandala rapid interagency assessment led by OCHA and participated by WFP and other partners including our field staff) indicate that close to 5,800 HHs have been affected. Targeting will include drought – affected pastoralists, conflict and drought IDPs living in makeshift shelters and fisherfolk whose livelihoods that need immediate assistance, as well as vulnerable groups including women headed households with many children, the elderly, people with disabilities and critically sick and people injured by Cyclone GATI. This will also include disaggregation of HH data; and actual projections on forecast on HH size. Planning for the Worst case scenario, an estimated 1,539 HHs including IDPs/Refugees vulnerable to the cyclone are affected and exposed to critical Emergency WASH risks due to overwhelmed/collapsed social services in Puntland State of Somalia.
Moreover, the core problem that the project seeks to address is the extremely weak livelihood, education and health outcomes currently faced by target populations in Qandala district due to inadequate and unsustainable water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and practice. By addressing these WASH issues alongside its work to address other core problems within the sectors of Food, Income and Markets (FIM), Education and Nutrition, Concern intends to make a significant contribution to an overall objective of addressing the high incidences of poverty and vulnerability and the limited quality of life affecting these populations.

1. - Projects activities

Under this HRP, the planned project by SORDO will implement three activities targeting critical locations in Qandala district that are expected to be affected by the current and anticipated other shocks including conflict, cyclones and flash floods:1. Water trucking and provision of clean drinking water2. Hygiene promotion, treatment of unprotected water sources and provision of household water treatment and safe storage products (HWTSS), and 3. Emergency Rehabilitation/ upgrading of shallow wells and other seasonal rural and spring water pointsActivity #1: Improved access to safe drinking water and quality control in conflict and drought affected IDPs and pastoral communities. Activity description: 1,900 HHs which is approximately 11,400 people (50 % women) drought and conflict affected populations disaggregated by sex and age in Qandala district will have improved access to safe water and enabled to improve water quality at source and household level through emergency water trucking, rehabilitation of existing water points, disinfection, promotion of household water treatment and safe storage and repair of non-functional water sources.SORDO will targeted most vulnerable community members and beneficiaries from the target community in Qandala district. The selection of beneficiaries will be done through consultative process. people with disabilities, people with special needs, elderly people, minorities, women and children and other vulnerable people will be given priority. SORDO will provide emergency water trucking to the affected community for a period of 30 days for 1,900 families (11,400 people) providing 45 liters of water on daily basis (7.5l/person/day). A minimum of 7.5 liters of water per person per day is required to sustain life and comply with the minimum hygiene requirements to decrease the risk of WASH-related diseases, including COVID-19. In addition, SORDO will ensure the quality of water that will be delivered to the targeted beneficiaries through water sources chlorination and will ensure water sources are safety. Activity #2: Hygiene promotion, treatment of unprotected water sources and provision of household water treatment and safe storage products (HWTSS). Activity Description: During emergencies and crisis, especially droughts and natural shocks and when water points dry up and become very little and sometimes are contaminated, there is expected to be increased cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD). And as a access to clean water and increased shortage of clean and safe water persists the need to treat both water sources and that of households becomes of vital importance. Activity #3: Emergency Rehabilitation/ upgrading of shallow wells and other seasonal rural and spring water pointsActivity Description: In emergency periods; such as droughts, seasonal and temporary water points and shallow wells dry up from increased demand for water where they require to be renovated and or rehabilitated to ensure functionality and support ongoing water trucing especially in coastal settlements where spring wells can be renovated to increase yield. Also, emergency rehabilitation/upgrading of shallow wells and other seasonal rural and spring water points helps that most of the rural settlements water points are ready to harvest better water in the coming second season. This will ensure to sustain the supply of clean water, mitigate potential water scarcity by increasing access to clean water and mitigate the secondary impacts such as displacement and disease outbreaks, including COVID-19.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In the implementation of this WASH Project, consideration will be given to women and girls, and how they can become agents of change. Their expertise and knowledge on resilience will be tapped. Boys and men who are at risk of being recruited by militia groups will not be left out. Based on the existing gender disparity in Somalia, SORDO, as the implementing agency, will use all local available information to reduce negative impacts from existing gender beliefs. Gender based risks such as women excluded from undertaking activities due to cultural or other reasons. SORDO sets targets for female inclusion in all activities and project staff ensures these targets are met; communities including all genders (women, men, boys and girls) are sensitized to this requirement prior to implementation. The same mitigation measures also apply to minority clans and other marginalized community members who will also be included in both project consultation and implementation to make sure they are not excluded from receiving humanitarian aid and assistance. Communities and stakeholders will be provided with training on how to identify locally customised intervention strategies (waste management approaches, establishing and strengthening of community structure e.g. WASH committees, school committees etc.) and how their work is going to change their communities. The empowered communities with appropriate infrastructure supported under this project will have an improved resilience and coping mechanism thus enabling a dignified life.Meanwhile, with regard to potential access and security risks, SORDO has a set of security procedures that allows its personnel, especially local staff to travel across many settlements in Qandala district which will be fully implemented throughout the project implementation cycle in the target district. SORDO also has a robust security structure and community linkages and operational presence in the area which allows it to protect its staff. In case of any security risk that emerges, SORDO will ensure that the mitigation measures for security risks are fully adhered to by all its staff.
