Project Summary

In Zimbabwe, women, children and men live in a multi-risk environment, facing slow and rapid onset emergencies. Most people are at risk of multiple hydro-meteorological hazards namely drought, flooding and diarrheal diseases epidemics (Cholera and Typhoid). The projection of the El Niño phenomenon will negatively affect the provision of safe water thereby increasing the risk of WASH related diseases among the vulnerable communities. An erosion of coping mechanisms in an increasingly uncertain and unpredictable socio-economic environment has exacerbated vulnerabilities. Floods and drought (exacerbated by climate change), high rates of disease such as typhoid and cholera all contribute to increased levels of risk. According to the Rural WASH Information Management System (RWIMS) of October 2018, 28.8% of the water points nationally are seasonal and only 12.74% are good yielding and perennial. The same RWIMS report points out that 30% of water points (mainly hand pumps) are fully functional. The risk of drought will further increase the burden (especially for children and women) of fetching water. As of April 2019, the ZIMVAC Assessment report showed that 18% of households travelled more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest main water source. Matebeleland South, Matebeleland North, Midlands and Masvingo provinces showed the highest number of households travelling more than 1km to fetch water. The Humanitarian Response Project is specifically targeted at WASH needs arising from and aggravated by the recurrent cholera outbreaks and eminent drought.
