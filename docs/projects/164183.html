Project Summary

UPP will address the urgent and life-saving health needs, as well as basic acute needs of primary health care in 4 different areas, including non-communicable diseases.
UPP and KRC will support four Primary Health Care Centers (PHCs) established between 2017 and 2019. These 4 sites were identified due to their strategic value of service delivery points covering a heavily underserved catchment area of 214,000 people (PIN data, December 2018). According to UPP/KRC historical consultation pattern, approximately 40,000 direct beneficiaries accessed the facilities in 2019.

4 - Needs

In November 2019, an assessment on envisioned needs and gaps in health care services provision of 10 health facilities in North-East Syria has been conducted by UPP Medical Team, with the objective of devising a strategy for the facilities previously funded by a UN agency and whose funding remains uncertain for 2020. The health needs assessment was carried out in Abu Qalqal, Al-Malikeyyeh, Ras Al Ain and Deirez Zor areas. Some key findings of the health need assessment are as follows: Health services provided in the facilities includes internal, paediatrics, gynecologic consultations, reproductive health counselling, basic laboratory service, emergency management and referral, basic laboratoryservice, free medication, and community health program. Abuqalqal is the only facility providing nutrition consultations while all facilities apart from Sur provide community management of malnutrition (screening and referral linkage). Recruitment of CHWs in Deir ez Zor is a challenge due to tribal tensions in the area originated by the on-going conflict. UPP/KRC prefers not to exacerbate tensions with an hiring process that could easily distort how the population perceive the organization and therefore pose a threat to the staff supporting the clinic. Unfortunately, the program cannot be resumed for the moment. From September to December 2019, the total number of outpatient consultations was 43,520, with a monthly average consultation of 10,880 consultations. Data show an increment in the number of consultations with a consistent pattern in almost all facilities. This is due to the increase in the services provided, in the increased amount of health care workers per patient and also due to the inauguration of different health posts between September and November. In all areas, the majority of patients are female and under 5 children, especially in those facilities with well-functioning gynaecological services.

5 - Implementation of activities

UPP approach consists in expanding partnerships and engaging in capacity building as part of individual partner NGOs strategies. This is to ensure continuity of response and to enable a quick and effective shift in modality should UPP losing access or international staff presence be reduced. To this end, UPP is in the process of renewing its partnership strengthening a mirror structure that entails a progressive shift of responsibilities and applied knowledge from UPP international staff to LNGO.UPP/LNGO aims to provide continuity of care to beneficiaries in Abu Qalqal, Al-Malikeyyeh, Darbasiya, and Sur PHCCs while addressing growing health needs due to recent displacement. The intervention will start on the 1st of February 2020. The action will target host communities, IDPs, Refugees and people with disabilities. Services provided would include RH, NCDs, nutrition screening and referral, laboratory analysis, community medicine, gynaecological, paediatric and internal consultations. Remote tools are in use to assure good quality monitoring and triangulation of information, such as the use of weekly checklists per facility/structure by focal points. Monthly reports will be produced and shared with the donor, in addition to the formal reporting system required.Project’s experienced staff and UPP support structure will ensure clinical supervision, adequate M&E and correct implementation of the supply chain. UPP Protection Coordinator will provide guidance and oversight for the Protection Mainstreaming and Prevention package of activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Main risks in the field are related to patient abuse and gender-based violence. In line with ProM standards and LNGO-UPP ProM strategy, health services will be provided in a safe, dignified and non-discriminatory manner and in accordance with protection priorities identified by the inter-agency working groups. Action onset, a ProM staff capacity assessment will take place to further inform the planned ProM activities and ProM strategy. ProM and GBV audits will take place regularly throughout the action to identify and mitigate key protection concerns. In order to monitor ProM strategy implementation, communication with the communities will be strengthened through beneficiary satisfaction surveys (BSS), FGDs and KIIs with beneficiaries and FCRM channels. Capacity building of health staff on key ProM principles will be ensured. Specific vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration when conducting community consultations. Furthermore, UPP will continue liaising with the humanitarian community, building synergies at a cross-sectoral and inter-agency level for an increasingly coordinated, transparent and reliable humanitarian response.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

UPP and KRC mandates are non-political, humanitarian, and social, as the organization is guided by humanitarian principles in its response to humanitarian crises. In conducting its operations, UPP is committed to do no harm, to overlook nothing, and to denounce and combat discrimination. The principle of "do no harm" obliges UPP to prevent and mitigate any negative impact of its actions on affected populations. Equally important is UPP's commitment to rights-based and community-based approaches that include efforts to engage and empower persons of concern in decisions that affect their lives. The assumption behind the action is that context situation allows supported health facilities to be operative 24/7 with qualified staff deployed to provide life-saving, primary health care and emergency referrals to the war-affected population, including the wounded. The action emergency system has been built based on this primary assumption and proved to be effective in saving lives during Peace Spring Operation (PSO). Furthermore, medical referrals have been effective due to the collaboration between civil and military actors, NGOs and private/public hospitals.The main contextual risks are the following:- Further escalation of violence between SDF and GoT, extended to territories beyond the agreement reached, and increased waves of population displacement (medium/high risk);1. Flexible response: to respond to significant instability and displacement, a risk mitigation strategy transversal to all facilities is the capacity to relocate PHCCs according to need.2. Local acceptance: unrelated to the geopolitical situation, a moderate risk posed to the implementation of the action would be the potential lack of acceptance and trust of CHW
