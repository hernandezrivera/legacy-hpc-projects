Project Summary

UNOPS proposes a project to restore critical public services, through the provision of integrated solutions, ranging from rehabilitation of public service buildings, installing renewable energy systems and repairing access roads/sidewalks with solar powered street lighting to support the vulnerable populations affected by the crises.. This project will target public service facilities with priority needs (severity 3/4/5 level) in Aleppo, Homs, Hama and Damascus Governorates. UNOPS also plans to undertake in-depth socio-economic context studies across Syria in 2024-2025.
Within the ERL sector, UNOPS has extensive experience in the region, including the Cash-for-Work project for creating short-term employment opportunities in Jordan, integrated restoration of critical services in Yemen, Iraq, and Lebanon, rehabilitation of PHCs, hospitals, schools and social protection facilities, and provision of solar energy solutions across the region.
In Syria, UNOPS is currently implementing infrastructure projects, focusing on rehabilitation works, supply and works of solar PV systems in one of the Homs Grand Hospital buildings (the Kidney Building), and provision of renewable energy solutions in PHCs and schools in Hama. In addition, UNOPS commenced the Nexus Response Mechanism (NRM) project, aimed at reducing communities’ vulnerabilities through programmes that increase their resilience through capacity development to prepare for future shocks, while addressing their immediate needs and challenges, across Syria.
UNOPS’ proposed interventions include 1) provision of renewable energy solutions to critical public facilities in order to address electricity shortage, 2) repair of access roads to the rehabilitated facilities, aimed at improving safe and secure access, 3) undertaking socio-economic context studies. (NOTE: renewable energy and rehabilitation works specific to schools and PHCs are planned under the Education and the Health Sector respectively). It is envisaged that UNOPS’ intervention will support the target communities to equip them with sustainable energy supply for strengthening their capacity to manage their community assets. Improved roads would also help the communities to better deal with climate change. Improved access roads would significantly reduce protection risks on residents, returnees, IDPs) such as GBV, potential health-related concerns, particularly for children. Road floods derive mostly from unmaintained roads, drainages, water pipes as well as lack of maintenance mechanisms that make these roads deteriorate over time and susceptible to climate-induced disasters. Unmaintained drainages are also home to a number of sanitary issues and diseases. Due considerations need to be made for environmentally resilient solutions with local mechanisms to sustain such key infrastructures resilient against climatic shocks.
This plan will contribute to the ERL Sector’s Objectives and activities as follows:
Objective 1. Support the maintenance & Improve equitable access to basic services, and Objective 3: Strengthen social cohesion by empowering communities to lead early recovery processes together
The activity costs have been calculated based on UNOPS’ ongoing project experience, the technical needs assessments and the market surveys, conducted in 2023. The estimated cost of the provision of renewable energy solutions includes the design and installation of PV systems for electricity supplies including the steel structure and solar panels (Note: the activity cost was calculated per facility). The cost of the social-economic context analysis was estimated based on the scopes of the ongoing project.
In order to mitigate PSEAH risks, UNOPS plans to conduct gender-responsive needs assessments with female and male beneficiaries, including marginalized groups in order to identify their specific needs as well as perceived SEAH risks.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

UNOPS will take measures to protect the security and safety of the beneficiaries. Particular attention will be given to SEAH risk management through robust PSEAH training programmes to the project team and the selected contractors and strict adherence to UNOPS’ environmental/social safeguards that include PSEAH safeguards. Given the focus of the project on marginalized populations including women, children and PWD, it is crucial to include PSEAH measures in the project. PSEAH is particularly important in projects with high degrees of direct contact between humanitarian actors (including contractors implementing on behalf of the UN) and local community members (beneficiaries) as in the case of the proposed project. In addition, protection risks have been identified and mitigation measures have been developed. The main risk would be related to potential exposure to violence and abuse to the beneficiaries, particularly for students (girls and boys) during the implementation, when the UN staff and contractors commence the work on-site. The mitigation action would be to conduct PSEAH awareness training to the project team/contractors, and design the work plan to avoid or reduce contacts with the beneficiary (e.g. the work schedule is planned for after school hours, school holidays,etc.). UNOPS will also put grievance mechanisms in place, including hotline, complain box in the project sites, and take remedial actions. According to the Project Risk Analysis, the identified risks are as follows: 1) Fraudulent activities occur causing UNOPS operations to be suspended. 2) Delays in design works (if original drawings of the building structure are not available/provided to UNOPS). 3) Sanctions and lengthy approval procedures. 4) Lack of awareness of contractors on health and safety measures at the project site, which may trigger incidents on site. 5) Deteriorating security situation and kinetic environment that experiences frequent incidents which might hinder access to project sites. UNOPS takes mitigation actions against these risks, which are 1) Implementing a robust internal control framework with oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities. Regular monitoring, quality assurance activities, reporting systems in place. Vendor due diligence and performance monitoring in place. 2) Coordination with the relevant authorities for their continuous support to provide the required technical information. 3) Proactive local assessment and availability for materials during the design phase and getting the required approvals ahead of time to avoid delays. 4) Mandatory HSSE training and inclusion of HSSE requirements in the tendering documents, and the selected contractors will be guided by the UNOPS team. 5) Close monitoring of the situation and coordination with UNDSS, and operational readiness and positioning staff to support alternate activities, in accordance with Business Continuity Plans.
