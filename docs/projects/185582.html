Project Summary

IOM’s Protection Project for the 2022 JRP connects three strategic objectives with activities that aim to benefit the most vulnerable Rohingya and host community persons, specifically to reach older persons, widows, single-headed households, persons with disabilities, and persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, and expression, and to target authorities and partners in a way that does no harm, assists based on needs, and prioritizes respect for human rights, safety and dignity.
IOM will continue serving as the Protection focal point lead in eight camps, directly implementing most activities. As the Protection focal point, IOM will coordinate with camp authorities, Site Management and other key camp-based actors to support refugees in addressing their issues and reinforce the do no harm principle and humanitarian good practices. IOM’s protection monitoring activities will facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to all humanitarian assistance, dignity and human rights. IOM will strengthen community-based protection through community engagement, awareness raising, and sensitisation, case management and specialized Protection services for individuals in need. IOM will engage and assist older Rohingya men, women and those with disabilities, because of their greater need for tailored protection support to overcome environmental barriers to access and during emergencies. In 2021, for example, among the total number of Rohingya beneficiaries assisted by IOM, 13% were older women and 9% were older men, which is disproportionately higher than a camp population’s average approximately 4% for this age category. IOM’s core counter-trafficking (CT) activities will be prevention, protection and capacity building, which will be implemented in 17 camps and 10 unions of host communities under Cox’s Bazar district. These activities intended to reduce the trafficking incidents and risks in the camps and host communities through comprehensive activities that follow the governmental National Plan of Action 2018-2022. The CT activities will foster collaboration among stakeholders who respond to human trafficking cases. The aforementioned integrated approach to Protection strives to have sustainable impact and improve peaceful coexistence between various communities. The activities conducted by IOM in 2022 will respect the prevention measures for the COVID-19 pandemic.
For disaster risk reduction, IOM will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps, and relevant authorities to identify extremely vulnerable individuals or households needing specialized assistance and preparation before a disaster. IOM will verify the needs of extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs), coordinate services, and support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. IOM will continue leading the Protection Emergency Response Units.
IOM’s protection mainstreaming activities will promote a holistic lens, incorporating age, gender, and diversity needs of the affected population in Cox’s Bazar into programming through capacity building, training, as well as activity monitoring and modification. Finally, IOM will continue to co-chair the Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group, and be a proactive member of the Protection Working Group (PWG), the Protection Task Team, and other related working groups.

1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar continue to face formidable challenges, needing Protection and other life-saving humanitarian assistance. Among the 896,879 Rohingya population, 80% are girls, boys, women, and elderly men and women; 4.25% of individuals were identified with at least one specific need; and 1% are persons with disabilities according to population data records. Frequently, refugees approach protection actors to have their disability status updated in databases and to access specific support.The J-MSNA (Oct. 2021) underscored how limited self-reliance activities rendered the refugees highly dependent on humanitarian assistance and with unmet needs. It noted the reduced Protection footprint between April and September due to the government’s COVID-19 prevention measures. The deteriorated community engagement and safety and security environment was cited by an ACAPS-NPM analysis on COVID-19’s secondary impact and potential consequences of the May 2021 containment and risk mitigation measures. Violent incidents and conflict occurred between and within Rohingya and host communities. Influential community leaders, who extort, physically punish and psychologically coerce the refugees, grew more controlling of humanitarian services. Recent trends indicate that gang culture, petty theft, abduction, and similar other crimes have risen. Gangs seemingly carried on without impunity, gaining influence and illicit profits and instilling wide-spread fear among the community. The unsafe camp conditions frustrate many refugees who told IOM that they want a community-based approach to help them combat such threats. Noted by the J-MNSA and IOM protection monitoring, refugees who rented land from host community families struggled to pay increased rental fees. Housing, land and property (HLP) issues were particularly vexing in Camps 19, 24 and 25, where IOM observed that families, who struggled to pay higher rents, started to send their children to work in houses, grocery shops and restaurants. The army’s completion of a fence that encircles all camps restricted the unauthorised movement of refugees outside the camp, limiting the scope of work available to them. The fencing also impacted several host community families, who lived inside the camps, restricting movement and fomenting dissatisfaction and evictions of refugees. IOM’s protection monitoring highlighted new needs caused by the government’s relocations of refugees to Bhasan Char, the authorities’ closure of Camp 23, the catastrophic fire, and the monsoon flooding across many camps. Affected refugees experienced family separation, disrupted humanitarian services, secondary displacement and upsetting moments. There is a need to continue counter-trafficking efforts through awareness raising and capacity building of government authorities and humanitarian actors, and victim identification and assistance. Some trafficking victims identified by IOM said they left the camps because of limited livelihood opportunities. According to IOM’s case data, 40% of the caseload included Rohingya women, girls, and boys and 60% of the cases were Bangladeshi men, women, and children trafficked for mainly forced labour. Self-reliance through skills development and income-generation for Rohingya and Bangladeshi trafficked victims along with community awareness sessions and capacity building training for stakeholders are needed to address and prevent human trafficking. Community-based protection activities that promote inclusion, participation, and meaningful access, and measures to mitigate harm are necessary for Rohingya and host communities. Exacerbated by COVID-19 and its response, the vulnerabilities and thus the various needs of Bangladeshi and Rohingya men, women, boys and girls require a comprehensive and fortified Protection approach while enhancing dignity and fulfilment of human rights and capacities at the individual, household and community level.

2 - Activities or outputs

1 - Continue protection monitoring through direct observation, focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to collect and analyse data on risks, incidents, and trends in ten camps. Particular attention will be given to how the refugees, particularly older persons, persons with disabilities and other EVIs meaningfully access humanitarian assistance. The findings will be used to advocate for improvements and change.2- Identify, refer and follow up on trafficking cases, vulnerable households or persons, or persons who have protection needs. IOM will also engage relevant actors to address registration, HLP, food, WASH, Shelter/NFI matters to support the refugees. IOM will support victims of trafficking with the skills development training and livelihood support to stabilize their situation in the community and avoid revictimization.3- Continue strengthening community-based protection mechanisms (CBP) through a network of Rohingya volunteers and Community Protection Committee (CPC) members, supporting protection activities, such as referrals, outreach, risk mitigation action through information-sharing and close engagement. The CBP enables the refugees’ participation through the exchange of ideas and techniques to address sources of harm, including social conflict. CBP activities will also promote social cohesion and capacities among the communities. IOM, partners, volunteers, and the CPC members will adjust IEC material based on feedback from the refugees and conduct outreach activities on counter trafficking and human smuggling issues to prevent, sensitize and develop a sustainable prevention mechanism through community-based mobilization.4- Conduct capacity building activities for counter-trafficking that are in line with the national legislation and government plan of action 2018-2022. Through sensitization and mobilization, IOM will support the Counter Trafficking Committees (CTC), local administration, law enforcement agencies (LEA), Rohingya community leaders, and other relevant stakeholders, involving them in the prevention of trafficking and empowerment of trafficking victims with safety, dignity, and opportunity. IOM will continue technically supporting the government to lead the CTCs in the district and serve as the co-chair of the Protection Sector’s Anti-Trafficking Working Group. 5- Contribute protection technical expertise to the disaster preparedness and emergency response by collaborating with camp-based sectors and agencies, focusing on unique needs of EVIs. IOM will identify EVIs in need of additional support or relocation because of disaster risks and conduct outreach to promote individual and household preparedness and response plans. IOM will serve as a focal point in the Protection Emergency Response Units (PERUs). After a disaster, IOM will assist refugees with re-establishing meaningful access to humanitarian services, monitor protection trends, including any changes to the human trafficking context, for new or emerging threats and risks and apply mitigation measures. 6- Continue protection mainstreaming by conducting regular refresher trainings on protection principles and Inclusion techniques for IOM staff, volunteers, and partners to facilitate beneficiaries’ meaningful access to humanitarian services. IOM will continue being a key member of the Age and Diversity WG, the Gender Diverse Population WG, and the Gender in Humanitarian Action WG. IOM will consult daily with the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) desks that are operated by IOM Site Management about Protection related complaints and address beneficiaries' needs on denial of access to different services related to documentation and other barriers to access. IOM shall continue supporting the PSEA Champions based in 18 camps of the IOM AOR, conduct training for staff and partners about SEA and the obligatory reporting system for staff, and serve as the PSEA Network co-chair.
