Project Summary

the project is structured from two maternity hospitals, 4 PHCs, 1 mobile clinic, 5 EPI centers, and 1 prosthetics limbs clinic.
1 maternity hospital, 3 PHC, 1 mobile clinic, 5 EPI centers, and prosthetics limbs clinic are currently supported and will continue during 2019 t a total cost of (3,300,000 USD)
This project focuses on improving access to healthcare and providing primary healthcare
and maternal healthcare services with a special emphasis on women and children, and
vulnerable populations who have been affected by the crisis in Northern Syria.
The motivation of this project is to continue to provide child-friendly health services in the
already established locations to the population; PHCs are the first point of service for
patients and will refer patients to secondary and tertiary centers as needed. Currently, the
PHCs consist of three clinics (pediatric, gynecology, and general medicine/internal
medicine) in addition to laboratory and a pharmacy.
Kafar Takhareem hospital presently provide laboratory services and, and better ensures
patients aren’t making health decisions based on financial resources.
Killi, Maaret Al-Namaan, Maret Al-Nasan, Aljineh (PHCs) and the Kafar Takhareem and
Sarmada maternity hospital currently has a delivery room and act as referral centers for
deliveries. There has been a substantial need for first services aid due to the conflict and
so we operate dressing rooms in PHCs.
Kafar Takhareem and Sarmada hospitals each contains a pediatric clinic,
obstetrics/gynecological clinic, two operation rooms, a delivery room, 2 inpatient rooms,
an incubator room for new-borns, a laboratory, blood transfusion service (for mothers)
and a pharmacy to provide emergency maternal and newborn care. In addition, there is a
kitchen and laundry room.
As well as Routine vaccinations activities started in PHCs and Kafer Takhareem through
UNICEF and WHO with coordination with Idlib health directorate and Syrian immunization
group SIG.
There is a centralized medical warehouse in Maaret Al Naasan that is responsible for
supplying the health facility pharmacies with the necessary medications, consumables,
and equipment and tracking their stock and consumption. The warehouse will be used as
a distribution point to provide medical equipment and assistive devices.

Needs

According to the WHO Surveillance System of Attacks on Healthcare (SSA), 139 verifiedattacks on health care took place in Syria during 2018 resulting in a reported 101 deathsand 189 injuries, compared to 112 verified attacks in 2017, reportedly resulting in 73deaths and 149 injuries. Attacks and resource constraints impact the functionality ofhealth facilities. September 2018 Health Resource Availability Monitoring System(HeRAMS) data indicated that across Syria 46 percent of health facilities were either nonfunctionalor partially functional, with 167 facilities completely destroyed, and a further419 facilities partially destroyed and in need of rehabilitation. The national ratio of fullyfunctional Primary Healthcare Centers (PHCs) of 2.28 per 50,000 population is aboveSphere Standards, but the distribution is highly unequal, with 101 of 270 surveyed subdistrictsreporting having no fully functional PHC, and an additional 9 sub-districts notmeeting minimum standards. Nationally, less than one functional hospital bed isavailable per 1,000 population, which barely below Sphere standards and very low whenmeasured against a global average of 3 beds per 1,000 population. The availability of andaccess to hospital services also differs considerably between communities. BetweenNovember 6 and 15, 2018 the field team conducted a total of 215 exit interviews withwomen who had received services at the 7 different supported medical facilities in Idleband Aleppo. The focus was on the activities provided, Satisfaction and needs therecommendations were, raising the level of privacy of consultations by conducting theexamination in a more isolated room or expanding of family planning room, provide Modernmedical equipment (ultrasound) and finally Provide medication such as (vitamins,inflammation,) During the assessment we have seen some of the FP-PAC activities areprovided through the gynecologist clinic and the delivery room/ operation room, the mainfinding was the need for the supplies/equipment not available in the facilities, the need fortraining in some methods and the community policies & traditions is affecting some ofthe activates such as vasectomy. The recommendation for the issues that we have facedwill be providing training to the staff and CHWs in order to transfer it to the communitylevel, the need for supplies/equipment can be managed by providing a baseline ofconsumption. The raising awareness session, reaching for community leaders and FGDswill give a better understanding of FP-PAC services to the community. Syria Relief staffconducted a Physical therapy gap survey guided and supervised by the health cluster, thelocation in northern Syria, 17 centers responded with a monthly of 11,851 sessions. Thereis a need for equipment and it was shared with the health cluster. 6 out of 17 facilities requested running cost, while 5 of 17 centers requested more equipment and 4 requested assistive devices.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks againsthealthcare collected through SSA during the period of January - September 2018documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout theyear 2017 (122 incidents). Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declinedsignificantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcareproviders and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long-term effecthampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change ofcontrol. Mitigating measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism forhealth facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties tothe conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of internationalhumanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare to the healthcluster to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. GenderBased Violence GBV: Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the onlypoint of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBVrelated emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclosethe abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must havesafe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offersurvivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Mitigatingmeasures: -Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violenceis considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population shouldhave access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package(MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff are trained onproviding first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identificationof GBV cases, and safe referrals to GBV centres - Presence of female staff in healthfacilities increase access of women and girls to health services Protection from SexualExploitation and Abuse PSEA: Humanitarian workers have access to goods and servicesthat put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, somehumanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerablemembers of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive andcommit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational andprogrammatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard ofbehaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexualexploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective communitybased complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective andcomprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEAamongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment andperformance management of all humanitarian staff. Duty of care: In the Syria contexthealth care providers were always amongst the first responders and the last to move outof areas witnessing increased conflict activities or change in control, something madethem subject to physical harm and mental stress exacerbated by the protracted nature ofthe crisis and years of operating under continuous emergency conditions. Furthermore,similar to the rest of affected population, they were subject to displacement, insecurity,reprisals, and loss of livelihood. It has been estimated that Syria has lost up to 50% of itspre-crisis qualified medical personnel capacity due to immigration, displacement, violence,and criminal activities. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the security situation is stable and helps to deliver the project services. Risks:Security situation deteriorates (airstrikes, shelling, inter-fighting between NSAGs) andcontrol over the area of operation changes which might cause a delay in implementing theactivities: Mitigation: SR may suspend some activities till the situation is stable. The staffis trained in and adhere to security protocols. SR employs program and monitoring stafffrom the area so they can move more freely. Strong community support will bemaintained. If the control changes, Syria Relief will negotiate access for other areas.Considering the need for health services for men, women and children across northernSyria, the service provided can be targeted to another location and SR may move tomobile units instead of static HFs temporarily. All Syria Relief’s offices and field personnelimplementing the activities have emergency contacts and receive updates on the securitysituation. SR informs donors and partners of relevant activities and security incidents.Risk: Staff and beneficiaries might be attacked or injured causing attrition or limits to theimplementation of activities. Mitigation: All the activities that Syria Relief implements arebased on the skills and competencies of the field staff inside Syria along withstrengthened support from Senior staff based in Turkey. Access: Syria Relief maintains agood relationship with the communities it operates in allowing access and movement ofstaff from and to project location easily. Coordination: Syria Relief coordinates its projectactivities with NGOs, through UN-led clusters as well as through direct coordination withNGOs and local authorities. Assumption: Lack of skilled professionals in these villagesand the different salary scale among organizations Mitigation: We have experienced lackof professionals and it is the same for other organizations, especially for gynaecologistsand anaesthesiologists, in the villages we are working. We have resorted to recruitingdoctors from neighbouring villages and areas, and recruiting lesser skilled workers(midwives, anaesthetic technicians). We can also look in Turkey for doctors who arewilling or looking to move back to Syria. In the case of gynaecologists, whilegynaecologists are rare, there are doctors available in the areas we are working, but theyare demanding higher wages. One option would be to hire them part-time at higher wages.And we are coordinating with other actors in our area to provide the same salary scale forthe doctors, because the high salary given by some actors is affecting and causing thedoctors to refuse to work with a less amount of money.
