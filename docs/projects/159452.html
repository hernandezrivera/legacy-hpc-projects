Project Summary

The median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition has remained serious (10 -14 Percent) in the past four seasons: 13.8 percent in 2019 Gu, 12.6 percent in 2018/19 Deyr, 14 percent in 2018 GU and 13.8 percent in 2017/18 Deyr. Despite the serious nutrition situation, some livelihoods has critical rates of malnutrition due to food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin A supplementation and poor child care and sub-optimal feeding practices. The 2016 Infant and Young Child Nutrition (IYCN) assessment found that only 33% of infants below six months are exclusively breastfed while 46% of mothers continue to breastfeed their infants up to 12 months, only 15% continue to breastfeed the child up to 24 months.
In the 2019 post Gu food and nutrition assessment, pockets of GAM above the emergency threshold of 15 percent were reported in 10 out of the 33 areas surveyed. The majority (51.7 percent) of malnourished children cases concentrated in the six regions of Banadir (14.8 percent) Shabelle (11 percent), Bay (8.8 percent), Hiran (6.1 percent), Woqooyi Galbeed (5.6 percent), and Gedo (5.4 percent). Morbidity among children was high (≥ 20percent) in 15 out 33 population groups surveyed. These regions also realized below average rainfall and the expected harvest is 68 percent lower than the long-term average since 1995.
The proposed interventions will cover treatment (Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program for malnourished children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women - PLW) and prevention (Blanket Supplementary Feeding Program for children 6-23 months and PLW, Mother, Child Health and Nutrition (MCHN) for children 6-23 months and PLW in the districts where GAM (Global Acute Malnutrition) is above 15%.
An estimated 1,008,500 children under the age of five years are likely to face acute malnutrition through June 2020, including 178,400 who are likely to be severely malnourished.
The World Food Program is the leading UN agency in Somalia in fighting hunger. In Somalia, WFP together with the Ministry of Health, nutrition cluster and the partners supports nutrition interventions in the treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and prevention of acute and chronic malnutrition. Treatment of MAM prevents children from mortality and deteriorating to Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM), which requires extensive medical support; prevent deterioration of maternal nutrition status and subsequent low birth weight.
Targeted Supplementary Feeding program targets children 6-59 months and pregnant and PLW with MAM. In IDP and host/urban populations in Dollo, Mogadishu, Baidoa, Guriel, Dhusamareb, Galkayo, Hargeisa, Berbera, Bossaso, Qardho WFP collaborates with UNICEF and MOH to offer an integrated package of nutrition and health. This program follows the Ministry of Health guidelines for treating acute malnutrition in Somalia.
WFP also implements preventive program:
Seasonal Blanket Supplementary Feeding (BSFP) a seasonal program implemented in areas with high rates of acute malnutrition and food insecurity. The purpose of the program is to reduce the incidence of malnutrition. The target group is non-malnourished children 6-23 months and PLW
Mother and Child Health and Nutrition Program (MCHN) aims at promoting growth by focusing on the first 1,000 days of life which contributes to prevent acute and chronic malnutrition and break the inter-generational cycle of malnutrition. Pregnant mothers also receive an incentive if they deliver their babies at health facilities ensuring safer deliveries under the supervision of a midwife/or skilled health worker. The program also aims at increasing the utilization of antenatal and post-natal services as nutrition services are offered within the health centres. The target population is children 6-23 months and PLW in the districts where Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is more than 15%.
Nutrition and health messaging is integrated across all WFP nutrition programs.

Mitigation Measures

WFP has a large presence in Somalia and works with local partners to provide assistance in all regions of the country that are accessible to humanitarian partners. This means that beneficiaries do not have to leave their places of residence to access WFP assistance. The use of cash-based transfers and SCOPE cards also allows beneficiaries to access their entitlement at locations most convenient to them.Before the onset of any project, WFP carries out consultations with the target communities, local authorities, and partners to discuss the overall objectives, duration and mode of implementation of the project. The eligibility criteria for the project, including vulnerability and the rationale behind the targeting is discussed so as to avoid any backlash that could lead to violence against the recipients of assistance, both domestically and from the community. To mitigate against exclusion and marginalization, WFP employs community-based targeting principles and processes through which the most vulnerable and under-represented members of the communities, including IDPs, are able to participate. Communities are consulted on the validation of vulnerability criteria, and formation an all-inclusive selection committee for the identification of most vulnerable household at the village level. When carrying out community consultations and targeting, specific attention is paid to age, gender, minorities, disability and clan representation from across the community. WFP supports the participation of both men and women as well as other vulnerable groups by providing safe spaces for these groups to provide feedback (e.g. women-only focus discussion groups).WFP works closely with its cooperating partners to ensure the safety of the men and women receiving assistance through in-kind assistance and cash based transfers. To avoid overcrowding, and reduce travelling distances and queuing time, beneficiaries have the option of redeeming their transfers at each of the facilities where they receive the nutrition treatment and prevention services. WFP has established networks of over 1,000 retailers/cash points throughout the country are, ensuring that beneficiaries do not travel more than 4 km in high-risk areas (and 10 km in safer locations) to collect their entitlements. WFP has an average of 30 active food distribution sites per district and 135 per region for collecting in- kind food. WFP provides also provides cash assistance in batches on different days and over extended periods. WFP will continue to work closely with local authorities and service providers to enhance security measures at distribution points and ensure the provision of culturally appropriate water and sanitation (WASH) facilities.WFP has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) which focuses on acts committed by WFP employees or others associated with its work against the people it serves. WFP’s cooperating partners (CPs) are required to abide by WFP’s regulations and to adhere to its commitments against harassment and abuse. WFP (in collaboration with the Gender-Based Violence-GBV sub-cluster in Somalia), has developed a GBV referral and programming protocol that outlines WFP's corporate strategy on GBV, particularly on reporting and referring cases for medical, legal or psychosocial assistance, as well as working with GBV survivors in terms of programming. To complement field-monitoring visits, WFP has a robust complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) that handles complaints and feedback from all beneficiaries and all stakeholders involved in the assistance process. The CFM not only aims at strengthening accountability to communities but also to identify a range of protection, fraud and diversion issues as well as other concerns related to delivery of WFP assistance.

Please provide further explanation

Many IDP, urban and host populations struggle to meet their adequate nutrition needs in times of crisis or even after crisis subsided. Most IDP face chronic food insecurity due to lack of reliable livelihood opportunities and exposure to shocks over many years. In response to this, WFP Somalia Country office has moved towards multi-year, comprehensive Interim Country Strategic Plan – ICSP (2019 – 2021) that enables WFP to forge the long-term multi-stakeholder partnerships that are central to humanitarian, development and peace objectives. In the framework of ICSP, WFP address the root causes and better support longer-term sustainable solutions (through nutrition and resilience and nutrition sensitive programming) while effectively addressing immediate humanitarian needs.In Somalia ICSP, WFP has adopted a multi-sector approach, based on the comparative advantage of bringing together agriculture, livelihoods, health, food assistance, nutrition and WASH as the most effective of reaching those furthest behind. WFP had secured rolling yearly funding like KFW (German fund) and RBA (Rome Based Agencies) that demand the participation of the governments playing a crucial role in laying the foundation for resilience and longer-term development. Similarly, WFP has embarked on strengthening the working relationship with national counter parts to build governments’ resilience and adaptive capacities to address malnutrition. This makes WFP nutrition programmes well placed to not only contribute to both humanitarian and development objectives, but also leverage its presence, tools and dual mandate to enhance its contribution to peace. The ICSP aligns with National Development Plan priorities to support a nationally led development and peace process and supports the capacity of the government at Federal, regional and local levels to provide durable solutions for the citizens. The SUN initiative supported by WFP, UNICEF and other UN agencies and has created a multi-sector platform to address malnutrition in Somalia. Likewise, WFP and UNICEF supports the Ministry of Health in the harmonization of the Community Health Workers tools and training. The outreach component is a critical part in the Essential Package for Health Services that cuts across all the levels of health service delivery. Under the leadership and primary responsibility of the Ministry of Somalia at the Federal, State and local level, harmonised outreach strategy ensures involvement of the populations of concern in health and nutrition programs. This approach fosters community based and participatory approaches allowing the affected population to participate in the planning, ownership and management of durable solutions to resolve the health and nutrition problems affecting them. WFP will continue collaboration with the government on programme implementation and capacity strengthening on policy development and implementation and monitoring of the nutrition activities. The government has taken the leadership of nutrition services delivery both at the Federal and at the federal member state level. WFP will work together with the government to support the holistic approach to nutrition programming.
