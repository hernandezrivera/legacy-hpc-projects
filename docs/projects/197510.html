Project Summary

Mehad (Ex-UOSSM France)’s response focuses on providing lifesaving and life-sustaining services to affected people in NWS by supporting the provision of primary and secondary healthcare services. The project intends to support two fixed primary health care centers (PHCs), two mobile clinics (MCs), and one internal medicine hospital in Idleb and Aleppo governorates (Ariha, Harim, Al-Bab, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts). The project was designed in alignment with people’s needs, health cluster objectives, and funding gaps identified by the health cluster and directorates. The project is designed to provide the targeted population with equitable life-saving and preventive healthcare services, enhance the well-being and welfare of people and strengthen the capacity of local health professionals. The project goal is to contribute to decreasing the morbidity and mortality of the people living in NWS through:
- Increasing accessibility and affordability through equipping the health facilities, hiring the project staff, and providing free-of-charge services.
- Increasing the availability of the services through ensuring the provision of communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases, and basic RH services.
- Enhancing the quality of these services through adhering to international health standards and WHO technical guidelines, and improving the capacity of the health providers.
- Enhancing preventive health approach through raising public awareness on health topics and adopting best practices regarding hygiene and nutrition.
The services delivered by Mehad through this project will be as per the technical standards of WHO, primary and secondary health cluster technical working groups TWGs, and in accordance with the Mehad operational policies and SOPs. To achieve this output, Mehad will actively attend health cluster meetings and the TWGs relevant to the project objective and will capture every possible opportunity through its network with other partners and health actors to maximize the technical capacity of the project health staff regarding health programs such as the Non-Communicable Disease program NCDs, Re-visited EHSP of WHO, Minimum Initial Service Package MISP of Reproductive Health in emergency settings. Moreover, project activities will be reported to the health and nutrition cluster reporting platforms i.e., 4Ws.
The project was also designed to ensure the inclusion of people with disability, the implementation of the PSEA principles, and providing gender and age-sensitive services.
In addition, Mehad will put efforts to collaborate with other partners such as providing coordinated and timely referrals and supporting the SIG teams to reach more people with routine vaccination or specific vaccination companies like COVID-19 or Cholera, etc.

3 - Needs

Syria remains one of the most complex humanitarian emergencies in the world and has been characterized by ongoing hostilities and the worst displacement crises of our time. The Syrian protracted conflict led to the widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply, and irrigation systems. According to HNO 2022, The fragile health system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies and chronic challenges which affect the availability and quality of health services across Syria, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. Moreover,12.23 million people are in need of health services; 1.33 million of them are children U5 while 3.38 million are WRA, and over 500,000 are older people with NCD and likely to require health services. Also, the prevalence of disability is nearly double the global average, those persons not only require specialized care, but also are at a greater risk of Covid-19 pandemic outcomes, which increase the burden on the health systemThe health cluster has performed a prioritization exercise based on a request from Mehad to confirm the priority of the PHCs in Barisha (Supported by IOM from March 1, 2022 till now), and Bab AlHajar (Feb. 2022) in addition to two MCs in Jisr-Ash-Shugur and Ariha (Prioritization exercise approved on September 9, 2021). The HC confirmed the need to support these facilities to provide their services based on prioritization exercises

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to contribute to continuity of essential life-saving health services to ensure equitable access of people in need and improving quality of services.The above-mentioned health facilities will provide free of charge preventive and curative services in the targeted districts. Mehad will ensure that all health facilities proposed under this project will be supported with the running costs, the equipment and the medical/non-medical staff required to provide quality assured services at these faculties. Also, Mehad will provide the health services according to WHO standards and under the supervision of the Mehad technical team and will have a capacity building plan to ensure the adherence to these standards. Moreover, Mehad will conduct MEAL activities through its team who will also be responsible to monitor the mainstreaming of PSEA principles, the inclusion of the people with disability and the accountability to the affected population. As for the primary health care facilities (Two fixed PHCs and two mobile clinics) proposed in this project, essential primary health care services will be provided according to the WHO essential package and that included child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, Non-Communicable Diseases NCD, MHPSS and nutrition. As for the internal medicine hospital, specialized internal medicine services will be provided through outpatient and inpatient curative specialized cardiology, pulmonology and neurology services

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The following risks were identified based on Mehad's joint experience working in NWS since:1. Risk: Limited/ interrupted access to intervention areas due increased insecurity and/ or loss of access due to change in control. Mitigating actions: 1) Close monitoring and participation to info-sharing mechanisms and meetings 2) Security related SOPs in place; 3) Remote monitoring mechanisms in place; 4) Maintain certain level of flexibility to suspend the work partially in the hospital when possible; 5) Ensure accurate inventory of assets within the hospital and establish plans to relocate assets as necessary; assess security of area and staff and take action to cease or continue support based on risks; 8) Ensure the maintenance of the humanitarian standards and the neutral positioning.. Risk: COVID-19 spread among staff and beneficiaries. Mitigating action: 1) Reduce number of staff at facilities/clinics or work on a rotation plan; 2) Provide adequate PPE supplies and enhance IPC measures at clinics; 3) Implement social distancing measures in waiting areas and entry point screening; 4) Suspend activities requiring gathering of beneficiaries or break the beneficiary groups to smaller ones. Improve relationships with all stakeholders through seminars and training processes that may have targeted them. Solving outstanding problems early and not ignoring them to prevent their negative development.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Mehad is an active member of the PSEA network and it is committed to protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and thus it is very serious to adhere to the PSEA standards and to make it inclusive to accountability measures.To achieve that, all staff of the proposed projects will be required to sign a PSEA code of conduct at the contacting stage after being fully oriented on PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms. On the other hand, posters and banners about PSEA will be adopted and visible for beneficiaries in all project facilities and the PSEA videos published by PSEA network will be shared on Mehad social media accounts i.e: Facebook.Mehad will raise the awareness of the beneficiaries at the levels of facility and community on how to recognize SEA cases and how they have the full right to report any of these cases through the PSEA network complaint hotline/ email which Mehad make them visible for all beneficiaries in the health facilities. Also, the PSEA focal point will conduct frequent training to the project staff on PSEA standards and the best practices to achieve high engagement between service providers and beneficiaries. Mehad is aware of the importance of the presence of female employees to conduct and follow PSEA activities, particularly when approaching female benefices. PSEA focal point will ensure the mainstreaming of PSEA standards and policy in all project activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -The border between turkey and Syria remains open for humanitarian assistance and staff. -The security situation is stable to provide the services and there are no major movement or displacement occur or a change in the controlling map between the military forces.-The local health authorities keep supporting the provision of services through the project. -The staff of the project are competent to provide assured quality services.Risks:Overlapping in the provision of the services: Mehad will make sure to maintain high coordination levels with the health cluster and local health authorities, i,e, HDs, and will also make sure to attend the health cluster meetings in addition to the relevant technical working groups TWGs. Through these coordination channels, Mehad will exchange information and ensure all parties are updated.Cross-border resolution is not renewed: Mehad will keep advocating with other partners in all possible platforms on the criticality of the resolution renewal. Mehad will also participate in all workshops conducted to discuss the contingency plan in case of a non-renewal scenario.Lack of health workforce availability: Mehad will announce the project job vacancies on all possible and relevant recruitment platforms and will also reach the health directorates to validate their credentials. Risk: Loss and/or damages to program equipment and supplies and/or staff injured or killed due to airstrikes or other security-related incidents.
