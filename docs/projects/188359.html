Project Summary

The proposed project will implement an integrated intervention that is specifically in response to protection risks and concerns in the target areas of Azaz, Kafar Yehmul, and Kafr takharim to reduce the vulnerabilities and enhance the resilience of the affected population of most vulnerable groups from IDPs and host community including PWDs . the interventions will be as below:
1- Continuing the support SENED pre-existed CPCM Static Facilities in Azaz, Kafar Yehmul, and Kafr takharim communities, which is friendly for CWDs.
-Continuing referral of identified cases (B:200, G:200), which will be through Mobile Teams, focusing on cases who fall under protection and early recovery criteria to specialized services as an integral part of case management, in coincidence with updating the referral pathways regularly, service mappings.
2- Continuing to provide case management services (B:150, G:200), addressing children's vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, and lack of appropriate care, family separation (Including expected separation due to COVID19) and immediate risks of worst forms of child labor, child recruitment and child marriage using CP-Sub Cluster SoPs.
Supervision mechanism will be put in place, as qualified supervisors will be responsible to support the case managers in technical competencies and practice, promote well-being and enable effective and supportive monitoring of case managers.
3 - Conduct parenting sessions for 600 (M:200, W:400) parents and care-givers of Children with Disability with and without disabilities helping understand, prevent and respond to protection risks and the specific needs of their children using Special curriculum developed by SENED organization,
This activity aims to assist caregivers to cope with their Persons with Disabilities’ psychological issues and regain confidence in the face of challenging circumstances. the sessions aim to raise the parents’ awareness about the necessity of dealing with their child’s disability in an earlier time which contributes to the provision of a better and effective rehabilitation process.
4 - SENED will Provide 300 children (B:150 , G:150 ) with special needs rehabilitation programming (SNR) through conducting full assessment which is necessary to develop and implement individual educational plans “IEP”. This assessment and IEP will be conducted by trained and supervised SNR specialists and will include the child developmental skills (social, emotional and verbal) and other behavioral skills.
5- Provision of psycho-social support (Outreach)
The working mechanism of the mobile team will focus on three interventions which are:
Enhancing inclusion of children with disabilities and their peers without disabilities: PSS workers will prepare a plan for implementing recreational activities with children (with and without disabilities). Those activities will take place in camps, schools, orphanages, and non-formal education centers to enhance the inclusion of children and their caregivers who will be invited to the center and receive awareness raising sessions. The recreational activities will reach children with disabilities and their peers without disabilities .
- Providing (in-kind) Emergency case Fund to 150 cases according to their specific needs as a response to their vulnerability needs using CP-Sub Cluster Emergency case Fund SoPs.
- Continuation to Provide lifesaving activities including case management, referrals and PSS:
women, and adolescent girls including vulnerable divorced, widows, and those with disabilities will be targeted with GBV case management and counseling sessions.Sessions can be conducted physically or remotely as per the severity of COVID-19 and consequent restriction of movements and in line with (Guidance Note on GBV Service Provision

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021: It is estimated that more than 5.7 million children reported severe need and child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. The HNAP 2021 report states that over 10 years of conflict, infrastructure degradation, and ongoing displacement in Syria have exposed millions to physical injury and trauma while compounding risks for persons with pre-existing or non-war-related disabilities, undermining their access to essential services and support. More than one in four individuals (28 percent) aged 2 and above have disabilities in Syria. The rate of persons with disabilities inside Syria is 10 percentage points higher than the 18 percent average of lower-income countries and significantly higher than the global average of 15 percent. 2 in 5 (39 percent) of households with at least one member with disabilities report that they are missing a household member due to war or non-war related deaths, movement within or outside of Syria, being in the place of origin (for IDP families only), imprisonment, or unknown reasons. The more members with disabilities, the more likely households are to report absent members; only 3 percent of households without members with disabilities report the absence of 2 or more members, compared to 10 percent of households with one member with disabilities and 19 percent of households with multiple members with disabilities.
