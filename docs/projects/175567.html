Project Summary

UNH is a dual mandated organization that provides development & emergency response. Based on our experience in emergency response, developed this project following consultative meetings with the respective state MOHs, CHDs, local authority, and affected population including elderly and persons with disability.
UNH’s Humanitarian Respond Plan for 2021 targets Pibor, Tonj North, Magwi, Yei & Terekeka counties targeting Host communities, IDPs & returnees. These counties except Magwi & Yei are inhabited mainly by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, interclan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility and humanitarian crisis. Yei experiences sporadic armed conflicts between government and rebel forces hindering access outside Yei town. People of Yei & Magwi are farmers, will have conflicts with pastoralist who are crazing cattle in the farmland.
Health needs of Terekeka, Yei, Magwi, Pibor and Tonj North enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, infection prevention/control measures, food insecurity, COVID-19 pandemic and threat of disease outbreaks and flooding in past two consecutive years in Pibor.
IPC report released in December 2020 puts Yei/Magwi in IPC 3; Pibor/Terekeka/Tonj North at IPC 4 with about 5-10% population of Pibor/Tonj North in IPC 5 for October – November 2020. Pibor/Tonj North expected to be in IPC 4 while Magwi/Yei/Terekeka in IPC 3 from December – March 2021. The food insecurity couple with continued violence, contracted economy, impact of COVID-19, poor health indicators, inadequate WASH facilities will drive malnutrition rate, morbidity/mortality.
In 2021, UNH will support PHCCs/PHCUs & mobile clinics to provide lifesaving & basic health services to the vulnerable population. Will implement following activities which are in line with health cluster specific objectives and will contribute to the HRP objectives.
• Implement emergency preparedness, contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases including COVID-19.
• Provide Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases, capacitate health workers (HWs) on EPI.
• Build capacity of HWs on BeMONC & provide BeMONC services.
• Provide life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through static/mobile facilities & build capacity of HWs on provision of lifesaving essential health care.
• Integrate & provide MHPSS/SGBV services.
• Strengthen IPC in our health facilities.
• Conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs of affected populations & strengthen feedback mechanism to ensure beneficiaries provide feedback on the project.
• Provide technical/operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision.
• Provide community health services using BHI and strengthen the linkage with facility-based services.
• Provide services for people with disability.
• Conduct community sensitization, disseminate IEC materials include those for COVID-19.
• Participate in RRM.
• Coordinate with core pipeline managers (WHO/UNFPA/UNICEF) to provide emergency kits.
• Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.
• Provide management for SAM children with medical complication.
• Support Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care & provide report on the attacks.
Direct project beneficiaries 27,240 (Terekeka 2,320; Yei 3,576; Magwi 6,028, Pibor 10,489; Tonj North 4,827). Indirectly about 10% of the population will benefit from the response.
To implement those activities, UNH will need 1,675,260 USD in 2021, aligned with health cluster’s strategy on the cost per beneficiaries ($61.5). Will work with other health, Nutrition/WASH/FSL/Protection partners to avoid resource duplication, ensure synergies and value for money, will engage CHD & development health partners in each county to take over at the end of the project to ensure sustainability.

1 - Needs

January 2021 HNO/Health Cluster (PIN), estimated 4,940,938 South Sudanese & 310,000 refugees/asylum seekers need humanitarian health services in 2021, population targeted for health response 2,416,667. UNH response in Terekeka, Yei, Magwi, Pibor & Tonj North targeting Host communities, IDPs and returnees. Number targeted for emergency healthcare in Terekeka, Yei, Magwi, Pibor & Tonj North is 33,135, 51,103, 60,288, 73,046 & 53,636 respectively.Inhabitants of Pibor, Tonj North/Terekeka mainly pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, interclan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility and humanitarian crisis. People in Magwi/Yei are farmers, conflict arises due to pastoralists from other states grazing cattle in their farmlands and returnees whose land have been occupied. Health needs of Terekeka, Yei, Magwi, Pibor and Tonj North enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, infection prevention/control measures, food insecurity, COVID-19 pandemic and threat of disease outbreaks. Pibor County has 36 health facilities, 15(41%) supported through UNICEF leaving most of the HFs non-functional, 2 HFs provide inpatient care, no CEmONC, limited BEmONC, low EPI coverage (Pibor CHD Jan2021 report), vulnerable people in remote areas have no access to lifesaving healthcare. Measles transmission persisted especially in Gumuruk/Lekuangole, outbreak declared in February 2019, reactive campaigns done in 2019 and 2020. County devastated with heavy flooding for past two consecutive years, couples with intercommunal violence leading to massive population displacement, loss of lives/livelihoods, destruction of infrastructure, impeding access and service delivery. August 2020 IRNA report indicated 95,838 (39,868 Female, 36,801 Males, 19,168 children U5) affected by the flood/intercommunal violence. Tonj North, World Vision under HPF3 supports 13 health facilities out of 18 (Tonj North CHD Jan2021). No emergency partner in the county. County experiences cycles of intercommunal violence and current violence which erupted in January 2021 has displaced significant number of people, vulnerable population including women, girls, boys, elderly and people with disability have no access to lifesaving health services. County is prone flooding. Magwi, 24 HFs supported by HFP3 out of 41; 8 out of 17 not supported were vandalized during 2016-armed conflict (Magwi CHD Jan2021 report). COVID-19 cases continue to rise, 297 cases confirmed including 7 health workers (Weekly bulletin 15- 21 February), porous borders points where a lot of trucks pass per day and voluntary return of refugees increase the risk of COVID-19 and now EVD. Magwi hosts significant number of IDPs/returnees. Returnees lack access to lifesaving health services, with COVID-19, most are now vulnerable.Yei, HPF supports 10 health facilities out of 19 (Feb2021 CHD report), sporadic armed fighting between government and rebel hinders access to facilities outside Yei town. COVID-19 cases now on the rise with 34 confirmed cases cumulatively. Voluntarily people are returning from Uganda/DRC through porous border points increasing the risks of COVID-19 and EVD importation. Terekeka, HPF supports 31 health facilities out of 47, functional health facilities provide moderate services. Most of the nonfunctional HFs are in hard-to-reach areas increasing vulnerability. HMIS exist in all the counties, reporting progressively improving (IDSR WK3 2021). December 2020 IPC puts Magwi/Yei in IPC3, Pibor/Terekeka/Tonj North in IPC 4 with 5-10% population of Pibor/Tonj North in IPC 5 for October- November 2020. Projection for December 2020-March 2021 puts Pibor/Tonj North in IPC 4; Terekeka/Yei/Magwi in IPC 3, pockets of population of Pibor in IPC 5 while for April -July 2021, Pibor, Tonj North and Terekeka in IPC4 with pockets of population in Pibor and Tonj North in IPC 5, Magwi/Yei in IPC3.

3 - Project indicators

The indicators for this project are in line with health cluster and linked to Ministry of Health Information Management System (HMIS). UNH uses MOH HMIS tools for data collection and reporting. The tools are standardised and collect all the indicators proposed under this project, these registers will be positioned in all UNH facilities (both mobile and fixed sites) and will be used as means of verifications for the indicators. The HMIS tools collect information on all the proposed project indicators, we will refresh, mentor and support our project staffs on the proper use of the HMIS tools. These are the sources of data from where reports are generated.• Indicators on EPI (Measles and Penta3) will be monitored through MOH EPI registers and evaluated through vaccination cards. Report will be generated monthly.• Malaria incident rate; MoH OPD morbidity registers will collect information on malaria cases, will be monitored/reported through EWARS on weekly basis.• Number of deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants; MoH Delivery Registers (Birth log) will collect information on deliveries, will be monitored/reported through DHIS2 on monthly basis.• Percentage of functioning health facilities provided with supportive supervision; will use the MoH Quantified Supervisory Checklist, will be conducted and reported quarterly.• Number of Primary healthcare centers with (BEmONC)/500,000 population; information will be collected and reported monthly using Health Service Functionality.• Number of SGBV survivors receiving CMR services and mentally ill persons receiving MHPSS services; MoH OPD morbidity registers will collect information on CMR and MHPSS services, will be monitored/reported through DHIS2 on monthly basis.• Data on number of persons with disability receiving services will be collected and reported through health cluster disability tracking tool and shared monthly.• Percentage of alerts, rumors and outbreaks investigated within 48 hours of notification, this will be monitored through EWARS on weekly basis.• Percentage of facilities with community feedback mechanism, feedback will be collected, analyzed and monitored on monthly basis through 5Ws.Data will be collected daily at the facilities/mobile clinics, compile by the data clerk/field officer and send to CHD on weekly basis, at the CHDs where all reports are checked, proofread and corrected as necessary. These reports are then entered into the DHIS2 and send to State MoH then to national MoH. Indicator tracking; M&E activities will be included in the developed workplan under this project, will have a distinct Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP) and Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT). The DIPs and IPTT will be closely tracked on monthly basis to ensure accountability and performance. Each facility supported under this project will have targets set for key indicators that will be tracked on monthly basis and data visualization (charts, graphs) will be posted on the walls of the clinics and our main office. Monthly analysis will be conducted, performs and underperforming facilities investigated, weakness identified and will be addressed to improve program performance.We will convene and conduct quarterly program progress review meetings with focus on the key project indicators, feedbacks provided and shared, recommendations drawn from the meetings will be used to improve our strategies in service delivery. During the meetings, State MoH and CHD representatives, Relief and Rehabilitation commission, WHO, stakeholders, beneficiaries including people with disability, women, elderly, boys/girls, community/youth/religious/women leaders and association of people with disability will be invited. The project targets will be measured against the achievements to determine whether the project is on track or not, this will help to measure the success of the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

United Networks for Health has PSEA policy which guides UNH employees including its board of trustees, consultants, volunteers, collaborators, suppliers, sub-contractors and anyone who has signed an agreement with UNH. We have trained our staffs on PSEA and signed the policy; new recruits will be oriented. We have and will print more banners, posters and flyers on PSEA which will be placed in the offices, health facilities, mobile posts and given to literate population accessing our services to get information on PSEA.We have integrated information on PSEA on our routine health education conducted at the health facilities and communities. We will strengthen the system to ensure that our beneficiaries are well informed about UNH policy on PSEA. People with disability and elderly will be reached through mobile services where they will be informed on PSEA.We will include a session on PSEA during monthly and quarterly review meetings where affected population/beneficiaries including their leaders- youths/religious/women/community/chiefs are always invited and will be requested to disseminate the information on PSEA to the communities.We will orient the local management and community committees (members from the affected population) on PSEA to ensure dissemination of PSEA to the communities.UNH will collaborate with protection actors in our areas of operation to ensure beneficiaries are reached with key information on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We have accountability and feedback mechanism in place, will ensure the system is robust, confidential and safe. Will raise awareness and inform the vulnerable population (people disability, illiterate, women and girls including the elderly) to know their rights and entitlements. We will encourage these vulnerable groups of people to provide feedback through Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), the RRC has representatives at all levels from State to Boma levels, they will be informed to collect and share feedback from the vulnerable groups.We will engage and encourage the most vulnerable persons to provide feedback through their chiefs, community/religious/youths/women leaders and associations of people with disability. Today most of the illiterate and other group of most vulnerable people have mobile phones where telephone networks available, will share with them the hotline for providing feedback and will encourage them to use their local language which will be recorded and interpreted in case the receiver does not understand the language. We will ensure that the receiver calls back to get details feedback since the most vulnerable may not have adequate airtime units.We will also encourage the most vulnerable to provide feedback through the members of household they trust who may have access to UNH provided means of collecting feedback.Our mobile team will be trained and task with the responsibilities of collecting feedback from the most vulnerable since they will be reaching to the hard-to-reach areas and providing services to the most vulnerable who may have no access to the fixed health facilities.We will always encourage the participation of the most vulnerable people in the monthly and quarterly review meetings so that they provide feedback.

Accountability to affected populations is one of UNH’s key commitments under the core humanitarian standards, and we are therefore committed to ensuring that affected populations are meaningfully involved in projects and have the ability to give feedback and make decisions about anything affecting them.UNH had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups and minority groups- elderly and people with disability) of Pibor, Tonj North, Terekeka, Yei and Magwi, needs of each groups of people incorporated into this proposal, no activity will be carried out in an isolation/fragmented manner as the proposed project aims to adopt a comprehensive approach based on participation of all stakeholders. They will actively participate in the implementation phase, joint monitoring and evaluation, monthly/quarterly review/feedback meetings will be conducted where affected persons give feedback on the quality of the service delivery. 80% of our local staffs implementing UNH ongoing project in targeted counties are from the affected population and will continue with our philosophy of empowering the affected populations hence will recruit most of the staffs for this emergency response locally. 45% of our staffs are females and will encourage and recruit more women at all level of positions in this project. 7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project has been designed following consultative meetings with the communities and their leaders, people with disability/its associations and elderly also included. The project will be launched and the beneficiaries will be informed so that they know the project to enable them provide sound feedback.Feedback provided by the beneficiaries will be analyzed to guide the project implementation. Feedback can be positive showing the satisfaction by the beneficiaries in such a scenario the team will be encouraged to continue with implementation ensuring all in line with the planned activities and indicators. In scenario of negative feedback, the management will quickly do further analysis and if it is within the scope of the project then will reprogram the implementation plan internally to ensure improvement in performance to achieve the desired deliverables within the set timeline, will also increase the level of monitoring to keep the project on track. In case the feedback analysis shows the demands of the beneficiaries are beyond the scope of work stipulated in the project document, then further consultation will be conducted swiftly with the beneficiaries (women/men, girls/boys, people with disability of different age groups and elderly) and the leaders at different levels (state to Boma levels) to understand the deviation of the needs of the affected population. If the needs are paramount and beyond our scope of work then will contact our donors and request for project revision with clear justification and evidence from the beneficiaries. This will help to ensure that resources are channeled to address the humanitarian needs of the affected populations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.) is high. UNH is health partner with a number of health care professionals. We will integrate basic messages on GBV, child protection, eviction and explosion hazards in our awareness messages which will be conducted at the fixed and mobile health facilities as well community. We will collaborate and work closely with protection and mine action partners on the ground to ensure synergies and enhance referral. We will also work with other health partners and refer survivors of SGBV/GBV to their facilities where we don’t provide comprehensive care (at mobile and PHCU levels).South Sudan has diversity of cultures where in some areas the most vulnerable especially people with disability, elderly, women, girls and boys are excluded in decision making and accessing humanitarian assistance. We normally conduct assessment to identify and map out the most vulnerable groups in our project areas, we do mobilization and also involve the chiefs, community /religious/women leaders and association of people with disability to identify and invite the marginalized groups for consultative meetings ensuring that their needs are taken in consideration while developing the proposal and are engage throughout the project cycle. People with disability, elderly and those in hard-to-reach areas will be reached through mobile team who will ensure their needs are identified and they participate in decision making for this project. The project will be launched and the state, county and Payam authorities and the affected populations will be informed about the objectives and targets of the project so that all including marginalized groups are well informed about the project. We will provide humanitarian assistance to the marginalized groups without discrimination and those with limited access to the services will be reached through mobile services. There are instances where county authority would push an emergency responder to response in the areas of their interest (villages where they come from) not based on the needs. To mitigate such external interference, we will brief and share the project documents with the state and county authorities and let them know the target beneficiaries. The authorities and the communities will be sensitized about the project and project target beneficiaries. Our team being South Sudanese understand the context well and based on UNH code of conduct will work with the local authorities transparently to reach to the target beneficiaries. Any external forces interfering with the beneficiary’s selection will be reported and action taken appropriately. Land disputes and insecurity caused by it is not uncommon in our project areas. Such disputes normally lead to insecurity affecting delivery of essential basic services, loss of lives and destruction of infrastructure. In Yei and Magwi, there is high risk of land disputes as most of the returnees will find their land occupied and getting them back may lead to disputes. People of Yei and Magwi are farmers and will have problem with pastoralist who are crazing the cattle in the farmland. We will work closely with the peace and reconciliation commission at county level who will able to detect early warning signs of such disputes and resolves the problems amicably before escalating. The inhabitants of Pibor, Tonj North and Terekeka are mainly pastoralists, land disputes normally arise over the grazing land. In all these counties we will work closely with community leaders and county peace commission to detect early warning signs and respond to it appropriately. UNH is non-political organization, does not take sides, will continue to work closely with community leaders, state/county authority, UNOCHA and UNDSS to ensure safety of humanitarian workers and access to provide lifesaving services.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

UNH team providing health services are guided by UNH code of conduct and PSEA policies and humanitarian principles of Do No Harm. We response and manage all those at risks- Children and women and GBV survivors without discrimination ensure all are treated equally with respect and dignity as we provide holistic care looking as a person as whole. We will work and collaborate with protection partners and all the development and emergency health partners in our project locations. Will integrate basic information on child protection, general protection and GBV/SGBV in our routine health awareness conducted at the facilities, mobile posts and communities, will also print and put posters and banners with information on protection and referral pathways at the facilities and public places, this will ensure that those at risks and GBV survivors are timely referred to protection partners and to health facilities for medical care and mental health psychosocial services. We will strengthen referral pathways between communities and health facilities/protection partners and between protection and health partners to enhance referral of GBV survivors from the communities to the health facilities/protection partners and from protection partners to health facilities to ensure the protection services are offered to those risks and GBV survivors in time and also referral of the survivors to the safe houses where necessary and the protection actors taking lead in the legal aspect of the services provided.We will orient the community/youth/religious/women leaders, chiefs and association of people with disability on risks, mitigation measures and referral pathways to reduce stigma hindering reporting cases of SGBV and enhance early referral of the GBV survivors for protection services.We will conduct radio talk shows to create awareness on child protection and GBV, also work with schools to create similar awareness in schools and market places in our areas of operation.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

UNH implements Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services of Primary Health Care of Ministry of Health which addresses malnutrition among children and pregnant and lactating women. December 2020 IPC puts Magwi and Yei in IPC 3; Pibor, Terekeka and Tonj North in IPC 4 with about 5-10% population of Pibor and Tonj North in IPC 5 for October- November 2020. We conduct screening for sign of acute malnutrition among children under 5 to ensure early detection and management. This helps to minimize progression of MAM children to SAM and SAM getting medical complications. Will also manage SAM children with medical complications in our health facilities to reduce mortality and ensuring good treatment outcome. We will integrate information of Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) on routine health education and promotion conducted at the fixed and mobile health facilities. Will work closely with nutrition partners and strengthen referral systems to ensure that MAM and SAM children identified in UNH facilities are referred timely to nutrition partners to get the required services, also SAM children with medical complications are referred to UNH facilities for proper diagnosis and management. Will work and collaborate with Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) partners to integrate information of food security in our routine health education. Have a demonstration farm at the facilities so that mothers and caregivers can be oriented and given vegetables seeds for gardening at the compound to combat and minimise cases of relapses.Will work with WASH partners to improve access to clean drinking water to the vulnerable communities which will reduce waterborne disease and malnutrition.Working with WASH, Nutrition, FSL, Protection and other health partners in the project locations will ensure synergies and value for money.
