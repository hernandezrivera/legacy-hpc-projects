Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to provide access to basic and improved livelihoods over a two-year period (2022-2023) in order to bring about more profound change for 279,336 IDPs, returnees, and overburdened at-risk groups in the severely neglected sub-districts of the areas listed below than is possible with short-term projects:
Saraqab, Ariha, Teftenaz, /Idlib Governorate
Ghabagheb, As-Sanamayn, Busra Esh-Sham / Dar’a Governorate
Abu Kamal, Ashara, Mayadin, Khasham, Tabni, Muhasan, Deir Ezzor / Deir Ezzor Governorate.
The consequences of the conflict and the ongoing Covid-19 are particularly severe, as many parts of different governorates have been turned into hard-to-reach areas where people suffer from lack of basic needs. This is due to the enormous destruction, including socio-economic infrastructure cut off from access to markets, loss of livelihood and income, increase in unemployment rate, lack of essential goods, and decreasing purchasing power and sharp devaluation of the currency. In addition, food security continues to deteriorate due to declining crop yields and economic and climatic shocks. This reconstruction and livelihood support will improve the livelihoods and socio-economic conditions of 383,400 people in areas with high displacement rates, and expand livelihood interventions to mitigate the impact of the COVID-19 crisis and recent economic deterioration. This is done by strengthening resilience through financial support, including grants, seed capital, and subsidized loans to high-potential businesses for job retention and creation; providing vocational and skills training opportunities; creating long- and short-term job opportunities; building capacity for unemployed youth; and providing income-generating activities. Providing cash assistance to children with disabilities, building the capacity of community advocates for ERL needs. Distribution and installation of solar systems, rehabilitation of power grids. In addition to rehabilitating local economic infrastructure (markets, warehouses), repair and rehabilitate social infrastructure and rehabilitate roads and associated drainage systems. Also remove debris and waste.
It should be noted that the project is consistent with HNO 2022-23 and the Sector Strategic Objective for the ERL (SO1 and SO3) and the priorities:
Strategic Objective 1: Provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable people with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs.
Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities
The intervention areas may be increased according to the increase in the areas that will beunder control in the years 2022-2023

4 - Needs

Due to the humanitarian crisis, an estimated14.6million people are in need of humanitarian assistance.76% of households report that they are unable to meet the basic needs of their families.80% of available basic socioeconomic infrastructure is damaged.The vulnerability of the population has worsened compared to last year due to the impact of theCOVID-19 pandemic.In addition, the overall poverty rate is estimated to be close to90%. In addition, the informal exchange rate has weakened by0.3% due to the ongoingCOVID19 and has increased to3.486 SYP/USD(WFP, November 2021)Unemployment is estimated at 65% of the working age population and over 60% of youth, and the income deficit of households where the head is a person with a disability, including female-headed households, is estimated at nearly71%.(HNO 2022)According to HNO22and the assessment conducted in the field,3,032,907were reported for Early Recovery and Livelihood Support in the targeted areas.It is worth noting that livelihood support represents50% of the total needs. Therefore, the RSRP aims to reduce the dramatic loss of livelihoods and improve the socio-economic resilience of affected households.Priority will be given to scaling up support to people with disabilities and women with their own households to improve access to livelihoods, as well as in high-need areas where access to basic services is most limited, where there are high levels of destruction,and where there are spontaneous returnee/high influxes of IDP’s

Procurement Process.
