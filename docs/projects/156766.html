Project Summary

Given the scale of the Syria crisis, and the high priority to addressing child protection needs, the demand for strengthened coordination in the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (AOR) under UNICEF’s leadership continues . There are currently more than 90 child protection organizations (comprised of Syrian NGOs, INGOs, SARC, UN agencies) that are part of the existing hub levels child protection coordination groups. Continued investment in coordination is needed towards building a community of practice, monitoring and providing strategic oversight for a well-coordinated response for the protection of boys and girls in humanitarian settings across the country. Coordination helps to make sure that child protection responses are prioritised, efficient, predictable and effective, hence the criticality of this project.

Needs

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crisis ever. Humanitarian assistance is provided by a large variety of actors, operating both from within Syria and from neighboring countries. Three coordination hubs have been established, in Damascus, Gaziantep and Amman, as well as a Whole of Syria overall coordination system, that ensures common strategies and best use of resources from the three hubs. As Cluster Lead agency for Child Protection, Unicef must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hubs and at Whole of Syria levels are met through appropriate support. This project presents accordingly the activities and related budget allowing to fulfill these coordination tasks to ensure following objectives• Predictable sectoral leadership and coordination • Increased predictability of geographic coverage and sectoral responsibility• Demonstrated accountability (for sector wide performance).• Increased field level capacities to address gaps.• Maximized coordination and partnership and buy in from the major humanitarian actors to ensure a more strategic and coherent response in support of national led efforts. • Enhanced ability of the Strategic Steering Group to lead a more strategic and coherent response, and better influence how available resources are prioritized UNICEF has dedicated coordination capacity in the Syria hub only. At the WOS level, as well as Turkey XB hubs UNICEF staff is double hatting as Child Protection Area of Responsibility Coordinators (50%) and technical programme staff responsible for cross-border child protection programming (50%). IM specialists support WOS and hub-based coordination and are also double hatting with programming and/or education cluster. With the rapid changes in the areas of control there is a growing need to also strengthen sub-national coordination in the 5 field offices of Aleppo, Qamishli, Tartous/Lattakia; Homs/Hama and Rural Damascus throughout 2019. In each of these locations there is a need for additional investment in capacity to reflect the growing demands on the Syria Hub in terms of additional partners, changes/expansion in coordination structures, new child protection protocols and more national oversight. Currently, UNICEF programme staff in each of these hubs are double hatting with coordination responsibilities. These staff, along with their sector members, require tailored capacity enhancement activities to strengthen skills and knowledge in line with the global child protection minimum standards.

Implementation of activities

This project covers both the staff costs related to the coordination function, including information management, as well as the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: i) support service delivery based on needs; ii) inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis; iii) plan and implement cluster strategies; iv) monitor and evaluate performance; v) build national capacities; and vi) support advocacy efforts. In 2019, the Child Protection AOR will continue to pursue a complementary strategic approach to reach girls and boys with the required flexibility within a rapidly changing operational environment. The coordination structures will build upon the investment of previous years to improve equitable access to quality child protection interventions through two main intervention priorities: 1) improving the quality of community-based child protection through support to community-based structures and psychosocial support interventions; and 2) expanding the reach and improving the quality of child protection services for children most at-risk and survivors of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Optimising child protection outcomes through multi-sector responses, strengthening the workforce and generating evidence on child protection issues to inform programming and advocacy with stakeholders are some of the key strategies that will be used by the Child Protection AOR to support these two intervention priorities. More specifically, activities under this project include:• Coordination and IM staff (in case UNICEF will not obtain the full amount required for a sector coordinator, the agency reserves the possibility to have the coordinator performing also agency program management functions): 1.1 Hub coordinators; 1.2 WOS coordinator; 1.3. IM staff.• Coordination activities (such as planning workshops, technical events etc) in support of the core coordination functions.• Capacity building to strengthen skills and knowledge of CP actors and service providers to provide quality child protection services through targeted and ongoing trainings and follow up.• Knowledge/evidence generation to supporting the AOR with reliable evidence generation on core child protection issues).• M&E
