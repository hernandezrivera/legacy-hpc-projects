Needs

The ongoing conflict in eastern Ukraine leads to the increasing number of internally displaced persons, the destruction of infrastructure, as well as an increase in poverty and vulnerability of the population. The conflict has had serious economic and social consequences, including restricting access to livelihoods and significantly reducing the incomes of both communities and individual families, which can be attributed to the humanitarian crisis. Carrying out the functions of the humanitarian mission on non-government controlled territory of Ukraine, DDC regularly receives requests from individuals, public organizations and social institutions to provide assistance in the form of food or cash to purchase food. Among the vulnerable categories in NGCA, are single pensioners with the minimum monthly assistance (1500 people, men - 610, women - 890), families with children with specific needs (1000 people, men - 430, women - 570), low-income people with chronic diseases (1000 people men - 415, women - 585), persons with specific needs (1500 people, men - 615, women - 885). In addition, IDPs living in collective centers, unemployed, single women, raising underage children, children deprived of parental care, persons without the fixed place of residence, or who have lost their documents also apply for the assistance. This equals up to 112,000 people, (men - 46730, women – 65270).Food is the main expense of these vulnerable groups and they have critical limitations in the acquisition of a variety of household goods, clothing, housing, utilities, and even treatment. At the same time, a significant part of the population is limited even in purchasing the necessary basic food to meet the daily kcal requirements. All of this can have a devastating impact on the health of the elderly, pregnant and lactating women, children and people with chronic diseases.Charitable assistance is often provided on an ad hoc basis, and an acute and yet stable need for such resources requires a systematic approach to the solution of the issue of providing food assistance - for example by supporting a free food infrastructure, and by opening a social canteen based on DDC for most vulnerable categories of citizens. In one day, a social canteen can provide hot dinners for at least 350 people. This could help reduce the incidence of mortality and morbidity among the local population.It should also take into account the high initiative of individual groups of citizens and organizations that are currently providing such assistance, as well as other communities interested in continuing and opening the activities, but requiring basic support for organizing such assistance. For public catering projects, in addition to the hot meals (which they will prepare) special equipment (furnaces, boilers) and proper hygienic conditions are necessary (household chemicals, "consumables" - gloves, bathrobes). Another solution to the food problem may be the food voucher programme, which will allow to redistribute funds in the family in favor of other, not less important, items of expenditure. Confidence in solving the issue of purchasing food, at least for a while, will allow households to plan their family budget more freely. It will free up time to find qualified work and will open new opportunities in solving family problems.

Activities & outcomes

Within the framework of the project, several activities are planned - most of which have already been implemented by DDC in NGCA for more than two years. From September 2016 to the present, under the agreement with INGO, there is the distribution of food kits to the vulnerable people (Donetsk region NGCA). In June 2017, a food voucher programme was also implemented. The suggested project includes the following activities:Output 1a) Selection of 5 initiative groups and charitable food institutions ready to participate in programmes for the accessible food for the poor. The advantage will be given to organizations that will open points of free food (social canteens) in the most conflict-affected areas (Gorlovka, Debaltsevo, Yasinovataya, Kievskiy and Petrovskiy districts of Donetsk, or other locations). Procurement and distribution of equipment for cooking. Drawing up the organization work plan for providing free food to the poor. During the year, approximately 4650 people will receive the assistance (1900 men and 2750 women), including persons without a fixed residence, single pensioners with a minimum monthly assistance, low-income, people with chronic diseases, IDPs, people with specific needs and other categories of citizens. DDC will work to engage beneficiaries in the planning and design part to ensure the planned activity is appropriate and in line with not only FSLC criteria but also beneficiary requirementsb) Informing citizens about the programme of providing free food for poor citizensOutput 2Creation of stationary and mobile points of free food for the needy on the basis of DDC, selection of locations. Daily preparing of hot meals and organization of work of stationary and mobile points of free food. The location of mobile points will be determined on a daily basis, taking into account the needs. It is assumed that they will be organized in the locations near the line of contact, where people are most in need of food and will not overlap with social canteens (Output 1). In total, during the year, at least 1860 people will receive the assistance (755 men and 1105 women), falling under the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster criteria of vulnerability and also beneficiary requirements.Output 3a) In the framework of food voucher programme it is planned to select and coordinate two locations where the project will be implemented. b) Formation, coordination with local official institutions and verification of beneficiary lists;c) Distribution of 5000 vouchers in 2 selected locations. Vouchers are planned to provide to the following categories of citizens:- single pensioners with a minimum amount of monthly assistance - 1500 people (610 men and 890 women);families with children with special needs - 1000 people (430 men and 570 women); low-income people with chronic diseases - 1000 people (415 men and 585 women) ;persons with specific needs - 1500 people (615 men and 885 women);Output 1,2,31. Coordination of activities with local communities, OCHA mechanism and the FSLC.2. Daily monitoring, telephone survey of beneficiaries and local communities on the progress of the project and its results. Post-monitoring after its completion;3. Monthly financial and narrative report.4. Evaluation of the implemented project and preparation of the final report.
