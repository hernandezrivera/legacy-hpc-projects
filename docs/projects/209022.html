Project Summary

Nine months into the conflict which erupted in April 2023 between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) the total estimate of Internally Displaced People (IDPs) across Sudan has reached 6 million as of January 2024 (IOM DTM). 54% of them are women, and over 50% of them under 18. The majority of IDPs are staying with host communities. Hundreds of thousands of people have arrived in Gedaref and Al Jazirah, along Madani road, fleeing Khartoum. Even though 3.5 million persons fled Khartoum, an even more important number remain in the city, and needs in the capital remains very high, while access to services is very limited. Following intense fighting in December in Al Jazirah state, Sudan's breadbasket, and the fall of Madani to the RSF, more than 509,796 people have been (re)displaced since then, with more than 60% fleeing to neighboring states (Gedaref, Sennar, White Nile, ...), putting an additional burden on IDPs already present settled in these states and host communities. The remaining 40% were displaced or re-displaced within the state. The disruption of harvest threatens national food availability. The cholera outbreak which was declared in Gedaref state in Sept. 2023, has spread to 10 states, with close to 10,000 suspected cholera cases, with 275 associated deaths reported as of January 20th, 2024 (WHO).
The integrated project's goals are to improve immediate access to free, quality, emergency medical care to internally displaced populations and host communities, to seek to contribute to a reduction in mortality and morbidity by increasing access, coverage, use and quality of Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition, with the support to 1 Health Facilities (HF), 1 in Gedaref and 2 Mobile Health Teams (MHTs), one in Gedaref and one in Khartoum.
Activities:
1) Health : provide Basic infrastructure, drugs/medical equipment and financial support to health facility, with the provision of essential pharmaceutical commodities following standard quality process for purchase, storage, distribution and management, and nonmedical equipment and supplies to health facilities, their financial support and necessary rehabilitation. PUI will continue to coordinate with UNFPA and WHO for the provision of SRH and IEH kits.
-Training and capacity building for MoH staff
-Provision of medical consultations through MHT and HFs, with preventive and curative services for common communicable and vaccine-preventable diseases, provision of quality Sexual and Reproductive Services (SRH) by implementing Minimum Initial Service Package for Sexual and Reproductive Health (MISP) objectives. PUI will support ante-natal care (ANC) and post-natal care (PNC) deliveries and Family Planning consultations. PUI has been a partner of UNFPA for the last two years and will ensure the distribution of dignity kits, including necessary items for menstrual hygiene kits, through MHT.
-Support referrals to higher level health care services, based on continuous service mapping
-Community based health intervention, with a network of 30 Community Health Volunteers from the targeted communities. CHVs will be trained and provide community awareness sessionson prevention of most common diseases, infection prevention and control, use of SRH services, prevention of outbreak diseases, vaccination, through door-to-door visits, under supervision of PUI’s team.
2) Nutrition:
-Integrated management of malnutrition at MHT / HF levels, with OTP/TSFPs at HFs and MHT
-Support referrals to higher level health care services for SAM with medical complications to the most accessible Stabilization Centre
-Community based nutrition intervention: the trained CHVs will screen, refer and raise awareness on nutrition issues, at community level, in the HF supported by PUI and in the MHT. CHVs will implement collective IYCF awareness sessions at gathering points (markets, HFs, schools and worship places).
PUI will coordinate with UNICEF, WFP for nut supplies.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

PUI projects and activities are designed and implemented around an integrated approach, guaranteeing the centrality of Protection and gender issue as cross-cutting themes to ensure an intervention quality requirement. As per the guidelines of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC), protection principles are incorporated into each single project, and promote meaningful access and safety and dignity in all activities. The following pillars of Safe and Dignified programming are mainstreamed throughoutall sectors and activities, and all PUI staff have or will be trained and will receive refreshment training on Safe programming principles and safeguarding policies:: 1. Prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks 2. Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services -in proportion to need andwithout any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who maybe particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3.Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints 4.Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rights To prevent causing harm and ensure the safety of the beneficiaries, PUI will conduct sessions on safety measures to adopt during the repairs and after the work. These will also be developed with the aim to reinforce the beneficiaries’ autonomy and capacity to protectthemselves in case of endangerment. PUI will ensure creating a safe and dignified environment. PUI aims at identifying the most vulnerable individuals or groups when leading assessments and surveys, to be able to appropriately analyze their needs and feedbacks, and PUI will develop strong accountability mechanisms as well. PUI will develop efficient accountability mechanisms, with the local authorities andcommunity leaders. PUI will apply the “Do No Harm” principle across all its activities. PUI will also ensure that the supported health facilities are safely accessible, particularly for the vulnerable groups. An entrance accessible for people with disability, and a safe waiting area will be dedicated to women. Across all its intervention at health care facility level, the health and nutrition staff will able to identify unattended cases in need of PSS and base protection assistance such as child protection related issues, GBV cases, and severely traumatic experiences. PUI will activate the identified and relevant referral system when necessary.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Premiere Urgence Internationale's commitment is to make sure that communities, beneficiaries, and among them the most vulnerable members, can provide feedback, make suggestions or express, file complaints if required. PUI has developed a community-based feedback system that consists of multiple entry points such as complaint/suggestion letter box, sometimes a free-of-charge helpline, a helpdesk, and community volunteers' networks. PUI also regularly conducts key informant interviews (KII) and FGDs to understand the evolving context of its intervention areas as part of the monitoring strategy. Besides, PUI works alongside community leaders and representatives to gather inputs that influence the program design and implementation, the targeting methodology, the gaps, and unaddressed needsanalysis. These channels are reviewed yearly following beneficiary feedback to ensure the mechanism is inclusive, context-specific, and accessible safely and confidentially by women, girls, boys, and men. Accountability teams are composed of both male and female members who speak the most popular language of the area of intervention to allow beneficiaries to easily express their concerns. To ensure a consistent feedback management process across the intervention areas, SOPs emanating from theorganizational accountability field guide are in place to track and respond to complaints and feedback. The SOPs describe steps for managing both non-sensitive and sensitive cases (e.g, fraud, corruption, PSEA, child protection).

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

PUI will ensure that the field staff share with the community PUI’s commitment to accountability to the affected population on issues such as staff recruitment and training, code of conduct, information sharing and transparency. All PUI staff receive the organization Code of conduct and all PUI ethical policies, including PSEA. PUI has a zero tolerance towards all forms of abuse, exploitation, mistreatment and moral or sexual harrassement. All persons entering in contact with Première Urgence Internationale collaborators, members, partners, suppliers, associates, and beneficiaries in relation with its activities must be protected to the greatest extent possible against deliberate or involuntary actions and failures which would expose them to risks of abuse, harassment or sexual exploitation and any other prejudice. PUI will ensure cases can be reported. All representatives and collaborators of Première Urgence Internationale undertake in all circumstances to respect the provisions, norms, and commitments to report any concerns, suspicions, or allegations of PSEA and any violations of this Policy in accordance with the wisthleblower mechanism and recommended timelines, as detailed in PUI Ethical Framework. The signature of the code of conduct includes a commitment to respect all policies and to maintain highethical standards. PUI will set up a CFRM adapted to the local context to collect the views and complaints of a wide range of stakeholders (affected populations, communities, local civil society actors, international humanitarian actors, local authorities or national governments and donors) and take appropriate action. It will be designed in line with the protection principle of Do No Harm, in consultation with partners and affected communities, taking also into account available internalresources (financial and human) and with the objective of gradual scaling up. PUI thus aims at guaranteeing the availability of appropriate Feedback/Complaints channels, while ensuring effective complaints handling, in order to avoid creating frustration or mistrust with regard to the proposed mechanisms. Depending on the context, this mechanism consist of i) Community Mobilizers, who would be important messengers conveying feedbacks and complaints from the population, themselves being part of the community ii) Complaint boxes, especially in health facilities ii) Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees iv) Focus group discussions led by PUI teams. Whatever the mechanism(s) chosen, PUI will inform the population of the existence and functioning of the complaint and feedback mechanism. This information will be disseminated through several channels(i.e.through community relays, during surveys etc.) in order to ensure that the targeted community can make use of it as soon as the first activities are launched and Inform the population in the intervention area of the anti-fraud, anti-corruption and PSEA principles.
