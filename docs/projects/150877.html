Project Summary

DRC proposes to undertake a life-saving and sustainable assistance to Disaster Affected Communities (DACs) through provision of emergency shelter kits (ESK), non-food items (NFIs), transitional shelter and durable shelter solutions. DRC defines DACs as people affected by displacement internally and those that are returning seeking for reintegration. Therefore DRC targets new IDPs, the IDPs in protracted situation, returnees, refugees and host communities. DRC understands that the primary displacements in Somalia is as a result of natural disasters and man-made conflict; but also, secondary displacements are now a common reality due to either military offensive or forced evictions by government. These displacements are the key driver of humanitarian shelter needs in Somalia. The IDPs are currently residing in urban and peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions, overall improvements to their living conditions and improvement to their chances to access livelihoods and durable solutions. On the other hand, DRC targets Host Communities - to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups and also because many of them are equally vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. For IDPs in protracted situation, DRC offers transitional shelter assistance. This also includes negotiating for extension of land lease/tenure to allow for a longer stay until full re-integration is sought. Returnees are also targeted with durable housing solutions in which case DRC proposes permanent shelter. For permanent shelter, DRC will ensure an integrated approach where are needs - WASH, shelter, livelihood, protection and education are included in the settlement planning. To enhance livelihoods, DRC will offer life-skills to DACs to enable them enhance their socio-economic well-being. Therefore, DRC will target newly displaced populations with emergency shelter and NFIs. For IDPs in protracted situation and returnees, DRC will seek transitional shelter and durable solutions respectively.

Needs

Somalia faces an enormous internal displacement crisis, fueled by drought, conflict, forced evictions and floods. There are around 2.6 million people are internally displaced in Somalia currently residing in urban and peri-urban informal settlements. These are crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. The IDPs remain in need of improved Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions, overall improvements to their living conditions and improvement to their chances of accessing livelihoods and durable solutions. Out of the 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia, 2.3 million can be concluded to be in need of humanitarian shelter assistance. Evidence from the JMCNA 2018 data collected from 58 districts in Somalia show that 80% of the IDPs live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents, or under trees; 35% reported damage to their shelters in the last three months; 70% reported that the shelters they live in have no internal separation; 16% reported thefts from their shelters; and 80% were NFI vulnerable. DRC categorizes the current needs into three - those newly displaced IDPs seeking life-saving emergency response; the IDPs in protracted situation living in emergency shelter for more than six (6) months in deplorable conditions that are in need transitional shelter. The other category are returns who are seeking for durable solutions. The last category are host communities who need a mixture of shelter assistance and NFIs. DRC recognizes age, gender and diversity in her programming and will therefore, during project implementation collect AGD disagregated data to help inform the cluster and humanitarian community in Somalia, the number of people assisted in each category.

Activities

Based on the needs, DRC will address both life-saving, transitional shelter and durable solution needs facing DACs. For the case of newly displaced IDPs, DRC will assist with emergency non-food items (NFI). The standard emergency NFI package will include plastic sheeting, blankets, jerry cans, sleeping mats and kitchen sets. DRC may give the NFIs through cash so that beneficiaries can decide based on their needs. DRC will also assist with ESK. The ESK includes plastic sheets, supporting poles and a rope for tying down the structure. This provides the most basic shelter which can offer protection from extreme weather. The ESK helps the displaced household to put up by a 4m x 4m shelter structure within the first few days of displacement. For IDPs in protracted situation, DRC seeks to improve their living conditions by assisting them with transitional shelter and NFIs by cash provision. For returnees and refugees DRC seeks to improve their housing and living conditions by providing permanent shelters that includes - a two-rooms house with veranda and toilet. DRC will also do a full settlement plan to include improved access to basic services such as roads, clean water, electricity, school, market, health clinic, and police post where possible. As this provides an avenue for durable solutions - DRC seeks to engage in promoting public private partnership (PPP) in provision of services to ensure sustainability. For instance, for water provision, DRC will ensure that a water utility company is established that will provide the water services and a reasonable tariff set to enable returnees, host community and institutions afford the services. This will also be the case for solar generation, health, education and other services. DRC will develop a selection criteria based on AGD as well as vulnerability to ensure that assistance goes to the ones who deserve it most. DRC will train government on DACs rights ensuring that they assume their role as duty bearers.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This submission is inline with the shelter cluster 2018 objectives 3, that is aimed at offering durable solutions for IDPs that have opportunities to locally integrate and IDPs/ Refugees returning to their places of origin and therefore contributing to strategic objectives 3 and 4 (SO3 + SO4) of the shelter cluster in Somalia. DRC is cognizant of the fact that, there are four (4) categories of people in need of either humanitarian assistance or durable solutions depending on their situation. In the context of Somalia, DRC targets Refugees, Returnees and IDPs (Internally Displaced Persons) as well as host communities. The targeting of latter group (Host Communities) is done on two fronts - one is to ensure peaceful coexistence between the local population and target groups; and two is that many of the host communities are as vulnerable as the displaced population. DRC is aware that most of the areas where IDPs settle are very temporal and will therefore not warrant durable solutions programming. However, in specific locations DRC and other humanitarian partners have advocated for permanent shelter and the respective regional governments or through federal government have reached an agreement to get land for resettlement. DRC is also keen to partner with other shelter agencies like UNHCR to improve the housing and living conditions of returnees by creating permanent shelters where land has been provided. For such permanent housing solutions, DRC will seeks to provide a two-unit housing with verandas and toilets as well improved access to basic services such as roads, clean water, electricity, school, market, health clinic, and police post where possible. As this provides an avenue for durable solutions - DRC seeks to engage in promoting public private partnership (PPP) in provision of services to ensure sustainability. For instance, for water provision, DRC will ensure that a water utility company is established that will provide the water services and a reasonable tariff set to enable returnees, host community and institutions afford the services. This will also be the case for solar generation, health, education and other services. Some of these areas include Gaalkacyo, Baidoa, Bosasso, Kismayu and sections on Mogadishu. DRC will also look into Housing, Land and Property (HLP) Rights. In Somalia there are three categories HLP disputes - landowner disputes; landlord-tenant disputes; and IDPinternal disputes. DRC will get into advocacy with government and through clan leadership, ensure that IDPs are able to access the HLP rights without any form of discrimination.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The IDP status is often strongly correlated with extreme economic vulnerabilities and insufficient social network, where the extended family members and relatives are unable to provide sufficient support. Majority of IDPs residing in spontaneous settlements come from marginalized clans with unequal access to resources. Weak clan protection and lack of resources to support positive coping strategies, makes them vulnerable to secondary exclusion, abuse and extortion from power-holders with interest to commercialize aid. DRC will consult DACs during both project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation, through existing, well-functioning community structure, where needed improving their inclusivity, to give voice also to traditionally marginalized groups, women, members of marginalized clans, youth. DRC will ensure all beneficiaries are aware of DRC feedback/complain mechanism. Being aware of the power relation between displaced and settlement managers, consultation will be done both with community leaders as well as regular residents, to increase multitude of perspectives on needs and priorities. Where appropriate DRC will adopt an area-based approach, ensuring the intervention benefits the whole community, regardless of displacement status and duration, and supports social cohesion, rather than further divisions. During mobilization sessions DRC filed staff with sound understanding of the community dynamics will provide detailed information on vulnerability-based selection criteria. DRC has strong framework for advocacy opportunities, on the issue of evictions from IDP settlements and collective centers; and promotion of land tenure security for IDPs at the onset of an emergency. For the case of returnees and IDPs in protracted situation, DRC will engage with both clan elders, regional and federal governments to provide land with title deeds to enable this category of DACs access durable solutions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

DRC will ensure proper protection mainstreaming through the activities proposed in the response, aligned with other protection activities as well as the Action Plans between the FGS and UN to prevent and respond to grave child rights violations; FGS Gender Policy.DRC will ensure that specific needs of women, children and elderly are included when designing and implementing the shelter and NFI activities. NFI content will be tailored to those needs, supporting dignity, based on consultation and will include solar powered torches, to mitigate risks related to movement after sunset. Shelters will be equipped with lockable doors and enabling access to people who use mobility devices will be considered during their erection. Distributions plans will include mitigation of most common risks, making sure the distribution sites are accessible and safe, risk of looting is minimized. Beneficiaries will be informed about their entitlements prior to the distribution and pictorial user instructions will be provided to ensure knowledge on use the safe use of distributed items. Applied vulnerability criteria for prioritization of assistance will include singe women, single-headed HH, as well as PLD and elderly, especially if they live alone. DRC will also reinforce activities that focus on women and their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes in conflict affected areas by leveraging women’s roles and leadership in expanding the humanitarian space, ultimately promoting community resilience, and furthering protection of civilians and civilian assets. HLP rights for women will be prioritized whenever title deeds or land rent agreements are signed. Accordingly, DRC will engage women in shelter, CCCM, WASH and protection activities recognizing that they (women) are key stakeholders in implementation of the project activities. Where cash for work will be implemented, activities will be designed to allow participation of both men and women, as well as PLD.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

DRC is aware that, abuses against civilians, including wide spread sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), recruitment and use of children, indiscriminate and disproportionate attacks on civilian areas and infrastructure, explosive hazards and forced displacement, remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in Somalia. Additionally, natural disasters further affect the already fragile coping capacities. Most affected groups include women, children, youth and marginalized communities, especially the most vulnerable (older people, persons with disabilities, female-headed households etc.) Through the Protection Cluster DRC is spearheading the protection monitoring initiative – establishing a Somali-wide PM system that will monitor key protection concerns across Somalia to identify trends and patterns of protection risks. Based on this, DRC will further engage in joint advocacy with other protection actors, as well as the international community to address these protection concerns. DRC will support survivors of abuses esp. GBV through case referral and case management and provision of psycho-social support. Child Friendly Spaces will be established to provide a safe space to children affected by conflict. DRC will also support community-based protection mechanism and leverage existing positive coping strategies in terms of protection.
