Project Summary

The project’s overall objective is to enable early recovery and enhance the resilience of at-risk smallholder farmers and households in rural and urban areas so that they are food, income and nutrition secure in the face of increasing multiple and chronic shocks and stressors.
This will also protect the development gains achieved and yet to be achieved in the implementation of a various personal and development initiatives, ensuring that they do not lose their assets, livestock and/or crops.

Needs

Climate change is increasingly impacting the livelihoods and food security of rural households negatively, as erratic rainfall patterns cause crop failure and livestock losses. The 2017/18 season was characterised by poor rainfall distribution and a prolonged mid-season drought. This resulted in below average national cereal production. The seasonal outlook forecast for the 2018-2019 growing season has reported an anticipated onset of El Nino during the 2018/19 rainfall season particularly below-average rainfall is most likely from October 2018 to January 2019. Due to this phenomenon, the below-normal rainfall has been predicted for the bulk of Southern Africa with enhanced probabilities of above-normal temperatures (NACOF, 2018). This will compromise any livestock and crop initiatives by farmers and development organisations that are rain fed dependant. There will also be a risk of migrant crop pests including fall army worm, quelea birds and seasonal livestock diseases such as January disease cattle. In addition to the impact anticipated from El Nino and climate change impact, livelihoods have been affected by worsening economic situation in the country, with inflation and increase in cost of goods exceeding to 300%. This has undermined the capacity to purchase basic needs such as inputs for the current farming season and basic livelihoods requirements. The value retained in savings, livelihood activities and sales made from any produce has also been depleted by the effects of the inflation. Households and small enterprises need urgent support to improve affordability of basic inputs for the current agriculture season, and also to develop a robust response to protect the value of their current assets, savings and produce.

Activities

Activity 1: Early recovery response, restoration of livelihoods, emergency employment and enterprise recovery. Sub-activities for rural households will include:• voucher based inputs for livestock survival feeding,• voucher-based inputs support for small grains,• diversify and provide suitable alternative livelihoods options and employment opportunities,• rehabilitation of community livelihood infrastructure and productive assets through cash for work for labour endowed households,• unconditional cash transfers for labour constrained households during the lean season, and• early response mechanisms and inputs to impeding dangers like army worm attacks, and wild fires.Sub-activities for urban households will include:• unconditional cash transfers for vulnerable food insecure and economically constrained households • complementary livelihoods strengthening support through high value products training in mushroom & vegetable production, which requires minimal land and capital and is not labour intensive, with pre-secured markets for the produceActivity 2: Provision of and supporting access to IT based information on market situation updates and emergency livelihoods and emergency employment opportunities. Sub-activities for both rural and urban households will include:• Linkages to a market information, logistics and transport app - Enduna to improve securing of quality markets for products• Strengthen business skills for small enterprises through entrepreneurship training • Strengthen key supply micro enterprises like agro-dealersThe project will thus minimise the impact and depletion of development gains in the respective areas, and also seeks to enhance household positive long term coping strategies and livelihoods sustainability through tailor-made early recovery interventions for both rural and urban households. Indicators and targets• Number of households receiving voucher-based agricultural inputs.• Number and percentage of affected population that gain access to formal or informal financial service• Number and percentage of micro enterprise owners in affected areas received skills training• Number of people benefitting from rehabilitated community infrastructure and assets• Number and percentage of affected population benefitting from IT-based informational support• Number of vulnerable people benefitting from unconditional cash transfers• Number of urban households receiving unconditional cash transfers and engaging in complementary livelihood activities • Percentage of key supply enterprises strengthened
