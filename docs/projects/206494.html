Project Summary

Due to attacks by NSAG, Ibo District and Quissanga's coastal and island areas faced a sudden increase in IDPs, straining resources and endangering the balance between people and the environment. This situation greatly affected access to vital resources like water, food, and livelihoods, that were already impacted by the cyclone. To tackle these challenges, IO aims to support displaced and hosting communities in Ibo and and Quissanga. This project focuses on sustainable water, sanitation, and hygiene interventions to reduce diseases incidence, enhance safe water access, eliminate open defecation through CLTS, and promote better hygiene and menstrual practices. Ultimately, the project aims to support the sustainable recovery of communities by strenghtening the capacity of local authorities and improving wash-related infrastructure.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Ibo District and Quissanga coastal and island areas historically maintained low population density, primarily inhabited by fishermen adapting to limited resources. However, due to attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAG) in recent years, Ibo district faced a significant influx of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). This sudden population surge strained already limited resources, threatening the delicate balance between human presence and the environment. The population nearly tripled, stabilizing at around 35,000 people, severely impacting access to water, food, and livelihood opportunities. Similar challenges persist in the neighboring District of Quissanga, particularly in coastal and island areas.In addressing these challenges, Istituto Oikos (IO) aims to support displaced and hosting communities in Ibo and Quissanga. Aligning with the principles outlined in the Community Recovery and Resilience Plan (CRRP) initiated by UNICEF in 2022, IO approach prioritizes sustainable water, sanitation, and hygiene interventions. The project methodology integrates data collection, including geological surveys, community engagement, formation of local committees, and collaboration with other sectors. By leveraging the CRRP principles of prioritization, localization, and following an integrated approach, the project aims to reduce diseases incidence, enhance safe water access, eliminate open defecation through Community-Led Total Sanitation (CLTS), and promote improved hygiene and menstrual practices. Ultimately, the project goal is to boost the sustainable recovery of communities by strengthening the capacity of local authorities and enhancing wash-related infrastructures.The intervention will be discussed with other organizations working in the district to promote synergies and to avoid overlap, in order to reach the most in need.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Awareness Raising CampaignThe project will conduct an awareness campaign focused on hygiene, menstrual hygiene, and sanitation practices. Through the dissemination of messages on safe water use, sanitation, and hygiene, the campaign targets specific areas to prevent the spread of waterborne diseases. Special emphasis is placed on menstrual hygiene, including the promotion and distribution of locally produced hygienic items like reusable menstrual pads and soap. These campaign will target especially youth, students and women to involve them as agents of change towards other members of the communities. 2. Continuation of CLTS ApproachIO will continue to implement the CLTS approach to eradicate open defecation and overcome cultural resistance. This intervention involves ongoing efforts to engage communities, implementing specific strategies to encourage behavioral changes related to sanitation practices. In addition, the capacity of the communities to maintain and improve their latrines will be strengthened. Particular attention will be given to vulnerable group, especially to people with specific needs and children. 3. Hygiene and Water AccessThe project focuses on enhancing hygiene practices and improving water access. This includes installing HH RWHS on transitional shelters rehabilitated in the framework of a IOM funded intervention, and on vulnerable households houses. In addition RWHS can be installed on public building, such as schools. Additionally, the distribution of essential items, such as Certeza, is aimed at aiding vulnerable groups and ensuring adequate water quantity and storage. Finally, existing water committees will be supported to guarantee proper maintenace and usage of already existing water points. 4. HH Water TreatmentTo address organic contamination in water sources in Quirambo, the project promotes the use of slow sand filters for water purification. This initiative targets specific areas where water analysis identified significant organic contamination, with the aim of improving water quality and safety. 5. Hydrogeological Surveys and Alternative Water SourcesConducting hydrogeological surveys and identifying alternative water sources are vital components of the project. In areas like Mefunvu and Quissivi, lacking suitable water sources for consumption, efforts are focused on surveying and characterizing aquifers to explore potential water sources. This aims to alleviate the reliance on buying water from external sources due to water scarcity. 6. Future Infrastructure DevelopmentFuture plans involve the construction of water infrastructures based on forthcoming studies results, particularly from the hydrogeological study of Quirambo and Matemo Islands, with anticipated data availability in early 2024. This initiative aims to address water challenges by strategically developing infrastructures based on research findings. 7. Emergency response and coordinationDue to the frequency of population movements caused by both conflict and natural disaster, IO will be ready to intervente to tackle this situation by contributing to the overall humanitarian response organized in case the situation happens, providing materials or services on the base of the needs identified case by case. In addition, IO will foster the coordination of activities in the island: IO already cover the role of Wash, Shelter and humanitarian focal point for the district of Ibo, and will continue to support and facilitate the activities of all the different actors involved.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project adopts a comprehensive approach to identify and prevent potential risks, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the communities in Cabo Delgado. Collaborating closely with local institutions, the wash cluster, and partners, the project utilizes existing feedback mechanisms like Linha Verde for real-time feedback on service provision, product quality, and information requests, allowing for swift response and resolution of concerns. The project actively engages communities through consultations, focus groups, and participatory data collection to identify and mitigate risks. These participatory processes will not only identify constraints but also empower communities to actively contribute to risk identification and mitigation strategies. Tailored sessions foster understanding and promote new habits to mitigate risks. In addition, Protection mainstreaming is integral to the project's design strategy, focusing on preserving safety, dignity, and equitable access. Oikos adheres to the 'do no harm' principle and Oikos staff is trained in identifying solution that respect these principles. Moreover, Oikos coordinates closely with protection actors, addressing gender-based violence (GBV) and other protection needs through referrals, ensuring a proactive approach to safeguarding vulnerable populations.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

This action plan is designed based on the inputs provided by target group identified through ongoing community engagement and participatory methods. It emphasizes collaboration with communities, valuing their resources and traditional knowledge to ensure community acceptance and active involvement. The project will create spaces for interaction, such as public consultations, focus gender groups and community data collection, to identify constraints and ways forward. Furthermore, the project prioritizes gender balance by aiming for 50% representation of women in fieldwork hiring, and in water committees. Emphasizing women's involvement in Hygiene Promotion campaigns ensures informed and inclusive practices, recognizing that their participation is crucial for appropriate and accessible WASH services aligned with their needs.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project ensures inclusion of vulnerable households, especially including people with disabilities, through active participation of local stakeholders in beneficiary selection. Oikos implements proactive measures to prevent risks and ensure inclusion in WASH activities. Consultations with individuals with disabilities and specific needs allow identify specific construction requirements and adaptation. Oikos actively involves people with disabilities in designing WASH infrastructure for improved accessibility, incorporating features like handrails and seats. Additionally, a community survey ensures inclusive infrastructure, aiming for long-term inclusivity even in the absence of disabled individuals currently.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmental conservation is a priority for Istituto Oikos, particularly within the scope of projects conducted in National Parks. Our solutions prioritize an environmentally friendly approach by utilizing locally sourced materials whenever feasible. Additionally, we advocate for a 'reduce, recycle, and reuse' system, particularly in WASH activities.For instance, in Ibo district, plastic generated from distributions or other activities will be directed to the plastic bank established by Oikos with the support of IOM. This initiative aims to prevent further environmental pollution by responsibly managing waste. Moreover, Istituto Oikos adhere to the country's environmental regulations and to Oikos internal environemental policy. Our project deliberately avoids promoting materials sourced from non-renewable resources, striving to minimize the use of non-reusable materials to curtail excessive waste production. By prioritizing sustainability and adherence to environmental standards, we aim to ensure our initiatives align with the principles of environmental conservation and responsible resource management.
