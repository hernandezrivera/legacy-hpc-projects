Project Summary

This project aims at improving the food security and nutrition situation of vulnerable
households targeted by the intervention while protecting and gradually restoring their
agriculture-based livelihoods. The project will strengthen the food production capacities of
a total of vulnerable households in the Governorates of Aleppo and Idleb. Priority will be
given to women-headed households.
the proposed interventions will also encourage the targeted households to plant legumes
with their cereals to improve soil health and fertility, besides diversifying their income
sources. This practice will minimize the erosion of soil and compensate for the reduced
use of fertilizers due to their high prices and/or unavailability.
This intervention will strengthen the production of vegetable crops widely used in the diet
of Syrian people to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring
agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and
recover from shocks. By integrating immediate relief with livelihood-recovery activities, the
proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of the resilience of the targeted crisis
affected people and their host communities.
SECD Organization will target Atareb, Idleb, Harim, Salqin, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, and Mhambal
sub districts in Aleppo and Idlep governate through the distribution of 4000 agriculture
input kits, 10 of Solar powered irrigation system.
The project will provide some workshops for training those who are interested in receiving
these kits and Solar powered irrigation systems. so that they will be able to use them
correctly and efficiently inside their Farms. 4000 households will be targeted by this
project; some of them are already agricultural workers, while about 200 person will be
trained to start this kind of job for the first time, the training will be about:
1-Winter cropping techniques.
2-Modern irrigation systems.
3-Solar energy system needed for irrigation.

4 - Needs

Based on the 2021 Food Security Assessment, 14.2 million PiN will not be able towithstand the ongoing economic burden and new shocks,13.8% of them has a poor FoodConsumption Score , with 41.4 % just at the borderline. In areas affected by conflict in thenorth-east, north-west, and parts of southern Syria, agricultural production will be furtheraffected. The food security situation among IDPs, Vulnerable Resident Populations andreturnees is expected to remain poor throughout 2022 due to the degradation oflivelihoods and socio-economic constraints. In January/2022, The MEAL team conductedAgriculture Needs Assessment in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates, covered 6 sub-districts,across 32 villages, for families who planted last year. The assessment was mostly formen and a little for women. Most of the targeted families were resident families, most ofthem have gardens. After analyzing the assessment of the families that benefited fromthe last year, we found they were happy with this support and had a great impact on theinside and they still need this support, they desire to increase the workspace and tocontinuous support for them to improve their life. 93% of people believe that the gardenproject is successful and should be expanded to cover a larger area and fields. Accordingto the plan, 95% of the target families planted seeds at the specified time. Most of thefamilies who planted winter vegetables and their seeds were from humanitarianorganizations.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented on many steps as mentioned in the time frame below.Firstly, we need to conduct a need assessment in target areas for three weeks. Secondstep is hiring trainers who will give some training courses for the beneficiaries in asuitable place that we will rent for this purpose. At the same time, we will start toprocurement the winter vegetable homestead kits which require some trucks and workersto achieve the mission. After distributing the items to the beneficiaries, a specialized teamwill visit the beneficiaries at homes regularly to provide any assistance and respond toany questions raised by beneficiaries. Along with the visits, an evaluation team will beconducting a post distribution assessment to fill any gap and fix any problem. Finally, areport will be written and prepared for feedback to the donor.
