Project Summary

Access to education remains as a significant challenge in South Sudan in the context of protracted conflicts. The entire education system, which was already under-resourced, with a low percentage of children enrolled in schools, is further weakened by the long lasting
Conflict, which has pushed hundreds of thousands of children out of school. One in three schools has been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since 2013. South Sudan has at least 2.2 million out-of-school children who are exposed to protection risks
and may need psychosocial support. Based on projections, this number could easily
Increase to 2.4 million by 2020, if no action is taken.
The Government closed all educational institutions since March 202, as a precautionary measure, to mitigate the risk of human to human transmission of the COVID-19 and protect children from being affected and to minimize further spread the virus in the community. Currently, only the candidate classes are in school while the other pupils are still at home. This means that 1.9 million school age1 children currently enrolled in formal and non-formal schools will not be able to attend regular schooling. This is in addition to approximately 2.4 million children estimated to be out of school in 2020.
Therefore, in view of the above this project seeks to provide safe, protective and inclusive
education to conflict affected boys and girl in South Sudan in alignment with HRP with learning activities taking into account strict adherence to the COVID19 preventive measures.
Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3 and Education Cluster Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3.
World Vision proposes to implement the project in the counties of Baliet, Fashoda, Melut
and Renk (Upper Nile); Tonj South, Tonj East, Tonj North (Warrap); Yambio, Nagero,
Tambura (Western Equatoria) and Juba in Central Equatoria which are geographical areas
Affected by conflict, displacement or facing acute food insecurity levels. WVSS continue to give focus to emergency affected rural and urban areas with significant IDPs/refugee/returnees and affected host community presence.
It is worth noting that WVSS has presence in these locations and is able to deliver quality
Education programming. World Vision ECW, Irish Aid ,UNHCR, SSHF1 and JPF CEN education projects cover majority of the locations are part of ECW .
However, not all schools are covered by the project, so there is room for collaboration.
WV is willing to share information with partners working in those respective areas such UNKEA,LIZDA,HDC ,RUCAP etc .
In line with the Education Cluster Response Plan, WVSS will ensure that through this
project, 25,500 girls and 24,500 boys have access to quality education and 1,500
teachers (765 female and 735 male) and PTA’s will be trained and supported with
Supplies and materials. WVSS will also prioritize establishment of learning structures
with gender-segregated WASH facilities, and other supplies/materials/kits that will be
supportive and will enhance motivation of children to go to and remain in school with a
Particular emphasis on girl’s education. In addition, recreational opportunities will be
established and implemented to support the recovery of children and youth and promote
their overall wellbeing. All learners will be given equal access to education despite
Gender, disability or ethnic background.

1 - Needs

As stipulated in the 2021 HNO, the protracted crisis coupled with COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 resulted in unprecedented challenges and closure of the schools in South Sudan affecting 1.9 million children. The closure of these schools not only interrupted educational progresses, but also affected livelihood and hindered children’s access to essential basic needs and services including school feeding, health information on disease prevention, protection and access to clean water and sanitation services. The sectoral objectives’ major activities require inter-sectoral commitments and re-enforce the education cluster to develop sectoral objectives those are inter-connected with strategic objectives and inter-sectoral specific objectives in order to redress the needs and demands of schoolchildren, teachers, and school community through provision of protection, WASH, and school feeding and nutrition interventions. Cluster objectives are also linked to inter-sectoral specific objectives, go beyond lifesaving, and promote community resilience through building capacity in emergency preparedness, response planning and engagement in decision-making, accountability, ownership, and sustainability of education programmes. The country is already facing range of humanitarian emergencies from flood and violent conflict to economic instability and severe food insecurity situation. The closure of schools further exacerbated the burden and influenced partners to extend their support to the entire caseload and prioritize response based on vulnerability, capacity and availability of resources to implement education programmes. COVID-19 response activities are entirely new and require special arrangements with additional resources to implement education in emergencies’ activities in order to bring back children to schools. Support to learning environment, teaching and learning, psychosocial support programming, infrastructure improvement, school WASH activities, teacher training and provision of incentives exerts more resources than the previous response to meet the needs and protect children from the COVID-19 pandemic in schools.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish accessible, gender and age-friendly accountability and feedback. The mechanisms will systematically include participation of affected population (individuals with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) in project planning and review and will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services. In the targeted schools, accessible, gender and age appropriate feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established to enable children to share their issues and views. This will be a continuous mechanism that will continue to be active beyond the project period. Accountability assessment will be conducted to determine the preferred accountability mechanisms, which include complaint help desks, suggestion boxes, meetings as well as reporting issues directly to staff. Informed by this assessment, the project will establish the mechanism at schools and in CFSs. These diverse mechanisms should offer options for persons with disabilities, limited literacy, and for speakers of minority languages to report on both sensitive and non sensitive issues, and also allow literate and non literate as well the most vulnerable to access the mechanisms. Children will also be sensitized on the availability and location of feedback and complaint mechanisms to enable them understand what to report and where. The project will acquire feedback after implementing project activities to determine the extend to which beneficiaries are satisfied with the service provided. This will be done through focus group discussions and post distribution monitoring (PDM) of supplies. The project will be deliberate to conduct FGDs for these special groups of persons with disabilities, illiterate and the elderly, to know how they are being impacted by the project, and where we can adjust to fit in their needs to benefit maximally for the project interventions

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

World Vision has a Programme Accountability Framework (PAF) with four pillars that holds the organization accountable to the affected population they seek to assist. Through the PAF framework, WVSS will ensure timely information sharing with the affected population; consult the affected population on any issue before making decision; promoting participation of the affected population at every stage of the project cycle and establishing relevant feedback and complaint mechanisms. World Vision will ensure that all the staff involved in the implementation of this project are trained on humanitarian accountability standards (PAF, Sphere Standards and Core humanitarian standards) so that they will deliberately involve, inform the beneficiaries and seek their opinions at all stages of the project management. The project beneficiaries will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process and the quality of services they are getting from the organization. It is also planned that the project team will provide guidance to the beneficiaries on the nature of complaints to launch and the available feedback/complaint mechanisms put in place to collect and redress grievances. World Vision will put in place child friendly feedback/complaint mechanisms, suggestion boxes, help desks, dedicated phone lines and email address for this purpose. Printed posters containing these addresses shall be distributed by the organization and placed in all Payams and meeting venues for ease of access by the beneficiaries and the general public. This project will also use conflicts sensitive planning to apply the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the project will ensure active and consistent communication and feedback from key stakeholders, such as local officials and government staff, at both county and state levels. Coordination will entail meeting with and communicating project activities to key stakeholders to ensure that activities are appropriate and well supported. Additional meetings and discussions will take place as needed to ensure any follow-up on issues, challenges, or areas that need improvement. World Vision has had a longstanding presence in Malakal, which resulted in solidifying good relationships with key stakeholders, providing the foundation for better accountability systems with the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through the accountability mechanisms established, the M&E team will get feedback from teachers, pupils and parent and use this feedback to adjust the project to benefit children more. Feedback will also be gathered from the vulnerable members of the community by deliberately interacting with them and including them in sample population when conducting surveys, monitoring visits and post distribution monitoring. They will also be encouraged to voice their views through the complaint feedback mechanism that would be set in the community. Feedback will be discussed at the office level as well as with the community and appropriate action agreed upon will be implemented to improve the project’s service delivery. This will entail either feedback good practice to be continued or adopted in other areas or a complaint on something that require to stop or be improved.
