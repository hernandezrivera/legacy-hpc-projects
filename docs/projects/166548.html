Project Summary

Zimbabwe is currently reeling from many challenges that include drought induced food insecurity and poor performance of the economy. It is estimated that about 7 million people in urban and rural areas are currently in need of humanitarian food assistance . Food insecurity projections for February to June 2020 revealed that about 4.34 million rural population need urgent food support . The food insecurity situation is also prevalent in urban areas in which about 2.2 million people are project to be in need of assistance . The poor performance of the economy and the rising inflation is compounding more on food insecurity as it affects consumer spending power. Food prices are pegged against the united states dollar (US$) which becomes too expensive for the majority of Zimbabweans who are not employed.
The COVID-19 global pandemic national lockdown since the 30th of March 2020 has increased the consequences of food insecurity across the country. The performance of the local markets for food have since been constrained. According to Weekly Market Monitoring Assessment report of 12 May 2020 by WFP and food security partners; the most preferred staple cereals maize grain and maize meal were not available on the local markets. The supply of other commodities such as rice, cooking oil and beans was declining while the prices were increasing due to demand. Most households are facing challenges to cope up with increased prices and are finding it difficult because their livelihoods and income sources were cut-off since the introduction of the lockdown . Households are currently restricted to travel to distant areas in search of food and other livelihood coping strategies such as casual labour and petty trade.
In light of the high prevalence of acute food insecurity, there is an urgent need to extend the current food assistance programs to reduce food consumption gaps for food insecure people especially those in IPC 3 and above (Crisis and Emergency). It is against this background that Zimbabwe Council of Churches (ZCC) is appealing for funding to support vulnerable food insecure households with cash based transfers (Cash in transit), COVID-19 hygiene kits, personnel protective equipment. The proposed project will increase awareness on COVID-19 through multimedia platforms.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

The proposed activities includes cash based transfers integrated with COVID-19 activities consisting of provision of personal protective equipment (mask, gloves, protective jackets) to stakeholders involved in distributions (NFIs), provision of hand washing soaps to beneficiaries (NFIs), awareness raising on COVID-19 through multimedia platfrms, and training of Village Health Workers and Church pastors on COVID-19. The project will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security cluster humanitarian organisations and local leadership. The organisation will attend all coordination meetings that will be called by government departments, other partners and the FoodCluster.
