Project Summary

Africa Development Aid intends to provide quality protection services to crisis-affected children and their caregivers in Ayod, Pigi, and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State, Malakal, Renk and Panyikang Counties of Upper Nile State. Emergency Response to 600 Unaccompanied and Separated children through family tracing and reunification services. Emergency response to 21,000 children, adolescents and caregivers through community-based mental health and psychosocial support interventions, conduct parental sessions on child protection issues affecting children in the community. 3750 children with different protection concerns will receive case management services through cash based initiatives. Conduct 30 awareness raising on child protection issues, formation of community based child protection committees to identify key protection concerns and help in advocacy on how to solve issues affecting children in the communities. Establishment and update of Child Protection referral pathways and training of women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff including ADA staff ) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE) these are inline with Protection cluster objects on response plan to child protection issues for both returnees and host communities in South Sudan. A total of 30,000 people (15,000 Males and 15,000 females) will benefit from this project. Out of this, 15,000 will be children (7,500 girls; 7,500 boys) and 15,000 will be adults – 5,000 males and 10,000 females. However, out of the total beneficiaries 30% will be from the host community while 30% will be from Returnees and 40% from IDPs.

1. Needs

Following the onset of armed conflict in Sudan between RSF and SAF, from 01 to 31 May 2023, a total of 65,399 individuals (13,810 households) reported to cross into South Sudan, representing 74% of the total number (89,010) of returnees and refugees that came into the country since beginning of the Sudan crisis between opposing military factions, (the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), adding extra burden on the already fragile situation especially in areas with high number of returnees and refugees. Between May and October 2022, communities in Greater Upper Nile faced various shocks and stressors, including recurring conflict, violence, climatic shocks, and limited public service infrastructure. Notably during this period, two high-intensity conflict periods drove large-scale displacement, limited humanitarian access, and had repercussions for affected populations’ food security and livelihoods, and access to services. In northern Jonglei and Upper Nile states, conflict between armed groups – which began in August 2022 – continued through December 2022 along the White Nile.Mass violence in August 2022, December 2022 and January 2023 sparked reprisal attacks in northern Jonglei Counties of Pigi/Canal, Fangak and Panyikang Counties in February and March 2023, these let to massive Child abduction and other protection issues. Rainy season flooding in October 2022 also compounded needs in the region during the reporting period. While flooding had affected 258,000 people in the region as of October 2022, marking a 33% decrease from the same period in 2021, floodwaters were still responsible for the displacement of tens of thousands of people. For the April-July lean season period, people in 29 of 32 counties in the region were estimated to be facing emergency level (Phase 4) food insecurity as per the Integrated Phase Food Security Classification (IPC) analysis. Other Child Protection concerns including families separation, child abduction, gender based violence, cases of early/child marriages, child abuses, psychological distress among others. These 6 counties of Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Ayod (Jonglei State), Panyikang, Malakal and Renk Counties, IPC analysis estimated that 74,141 people would face catastrophic (Phase 5) (REACH October 2022-March 2023).

2. Project activities

Through this Project, ADA will do Emergency Response to children through case management services (including through cash based initiatives). Emergency Response to Unaccompanied and Separated children through family tracing and reunification services. Emergency response to children, adolescents and caregivers through community-based mental health and psychosocial support interventions, conduct parental sessions on child protection issues affecting children in the community. Children with different protection concerns will receive case management services through cash based initiatives. Awareness raising on child protection issues, formation of community based child protection committees to identify key protection concerns and help in advocacy on how to solve issues affecting children in the communities. Establishment and update of Child Protection referral pathways and training of women and men (community leaders or humanitarian and government staff including ADA staff ) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). ADA will Recruit and capacity build 54 CP staff (27 M, 27 F) including existing staff from Renk, Fangak, Pigi, Panyikang, Malakal and Ayod Counties and train them on revised CPMS, CPiE, CPIMS+, PSS and Case Management SOPs. Case workers will identify; document; assess the protection needs of children; plan and implement interventions on the identified needs including referral to other actors for services, and follow up of cases using global child protection case management minimum standards. PSS facilitators will be trained on basic skills like how to communicate with children, signs of children facing distress or other forms of abuse, psychological first aid, planning and management of mobile & static Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) activities that are age, gender and culturally appropriate. Provide psycho social support activities including parental sessions and also select and train community-based child protection networks and foster parents on CPiE for all the targeted locations. Strengthen case, management approach including provision of cash-based assistance for most vulnerable cases of UASC, J4C cases as well as to their traumatized parents. 3,750 most vulnerable children (1875 boys & 1875 girls) will be supported with cash-based Assistance (CBA) as part of case management support. Identification will be done using priority list from Standard Operating Procedures for case management. The well-being of all children with critical protection concerns will be monitored through follow up visits to ensure new protection needs and risks are prevented or responded to in good time. 600 UASC/missing children will be identified/registered, documented and referred for family tracing. Provide psycho social support (PSS) services to 15,000 children and some of their 6000 traumatized caregivers through provision of mental Health and Psycho social Support Services e.g., recreational activities, livelihood services and parenting skills sessions via mobile & static response. Conduct 30 awareness sessions on prevention of family’s separation, COVID19 and harmful traditional practices prevention to 30,000 adults and children, these will be the same targeted parents and children for PSS. Identify/train 240 community volunteers (CBCPN & Foster Parents) and 240 other actors on CPiE in Ayod, Pigi, Panyikang, Malakal, Renk and Fangak Counties. Establish strong referral pathway in six targeted counties, this will include service mapping to know which partners is doing what to ensure children access other services that the project will not provide, eg. child survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) will be referred to GBV actors etc.ADA intends to target 30,000 people (M=15,000 and F = 15,000) in all six targeted Counties through the HRP 2024 whereby 15,000 will be children and 15,000 adults.
