Project Summary

HAGI at this response as a opportunity to support the physical, social, environmental and economic conditions to create a more resilient community. The purpose is to increase the number of safe learning environments to host the arrival of IDPs safeguarding the access of education of host community and support the communities and government to maintain this balance in sustainable and participative manner. Accordingly, HAGI will ensure quality education to concerned people including, IDPs, school related staff, students, children with disabilities and communities to be more equipped with the skills/knowledge regarding disaster risk reduction and prevention which is specific to their own context. In the wake of persistent fragility and economic crisis, an estimated 3.4 million (51 per cent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This is a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. In addition, there are 98,500 school-aged refugee children, of whom 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support. Children and youth, aged between 3 and 17, represent 44 per cent of South Sudan’s population. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic instability, displacement and widespread flooding. The capacity of the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) to respond to the needs of children remains limited. Only 6 out of every 10 school-aged children attend school. Less than 45 per cent of those attending are girls. The current trend in girls’ enrolment in schools in South Sudan is alarming. Girls are more likely to be excluded from receiving education as parents prefer to send boys to school. This increases the risk of girls ending up in an early marriage. In 2019, 17 per cent of girls were enrolled in pre-primary, 58 per cent in primary and only 25 per cent of girls received a secondary level education. In October 2020, education was identified as the most needed by children in one-third of almost 2,000 assessed settlements across South Sudan. Significant issues affecting education are as follows;
1. Limited numbers of Instructors to meet student’s needs.
2. Low wages for teachers hence the turnover is high.
3. limited monitoring and evaluation system in place to assess the quality of teachers.
4. Communities underestimate the value of girls’ child education leading to child marriage.
5. Sporadic displacements due to flooding and intense inter-communal violence that lead to changing patterns of settlement.
6. Inadequate class rooms and offices.
HAGI intervention will target 10,000 people (1,500 men, 1,500 women, 2,500 boys and 4,500 girls) with assistance gear towards young mothers who are back to school through Cash-Based interventions, rehabilitation and construction of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) and gender segregated WASH facilities for emergency affected schools, provision of essential teaching, learning and recreational materials, psychosocial support services, psychosocial first aid and schools club activities, conduct teachers training to improve quality of learning outcomes in the schools, training of Teachers on child safeguarding, psychosocial first aid and Psychosocial Support Services, establishing/strengthening referral services in schools/temporary learning centers, feedback mechanisms and referrals of children with protection needs and reporting, training of child protection (CP) focal points and linking with community-based child protection committees, school management committees, and provision of suggestion boxes in schools, provision of girls with dignity kits and other essential items to encourage stay in schools, establishing/strengthening child protection Help desks in schools and conducting enrolment, Out of school, survey and assessments.

1 - Needs

HAGI look at this response as a opportunity to support the physical, social, environmental and economic conditions to create a more resilient community. The purpose is to increase the number of safe learning environments to host the arrival of IDPs safeguarding the access of education of host community and support the communities and government to maintain this balance in sustainable and participative manner. Accordingly, HAGI will ensure that the quality of infrastructure be better and all concerned people including, IDPs, school related staff, students, children with disabilities and communities to be more equipped with the skills/knowledge regarding disaster risk reduction and prevention which is specific to their own context. Since conflict erupted in South Sudan in December 2013, four million people have faced displacement, with 1.47 million remaining displaced within the country and close to 2.2 million people crossed into neighboring countries bringing the total population of refugees from South Sudan to unprecedented levels to providing an enabling environment for service delivery. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2020), some 2,784,276 girls and boys between 3 & 18 years of age in conflict-affected areas are estimated to not have access to pre-school, primary and secondary education in 2020. In addition, some 42,902 teaching personnel and members of school management committees are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to results of the last biometric verification conducted by FAO, ACTED and WFP in Yirol East, Yirol West, Awerial counties of Lakes State are still hosting 69,567 IDPs, and over 18, 087 (26%) of this population is school-age children. HAGI is also implementing FAO FSL, pastorals education projects in Yirol East and Yirol West with different donors including FAO, ECHO, and EU. In lake state, HAGI has built over 25 learning spaces through partnership with FAO, UNICEF and ECHO; in addition, HAGI established more (20) school demo plots with funding from FAO. HAGI is currently supporting 10 schools in Yirol East through FAO, the project involves establishment of learning spaces, rehabilitation of 10 classrooms, distribution of 200 desks, payment of teacher incentives, admin/logistical support to county education department and teacher training components. Most importantly, HAGI has ongoing & anticipated HAGI projects until the end of 2020 in these areas. Improving the health & learning performance of school-aged children. Experience gathered in recent years has showed that enrolment and retention rate in schools are very difficult to maintain. The Government announced on 21 of March the closure of all educational institutions for a period of one month, as a precautionary measure, to mitigate the risk of human to human transmission of the COVID-19 and protect children from being affected and to minimize further spread the virus in the community. This means that 1.9 million school age1 children currently enrolled in formal and non-formal schools will not be able to attend regular schooling. This is in addition to approximately 2.4 million children estimated to be out of school in 2020.Therefore, in view of the above this project seeks to provide safe, protective and inclusiveeducation to conflict affected boys and girl in South Sudan in alignment with HRP with learning activities taking into account strict adherence to the COVID19 preventive measures. 7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will establish accessible, gender and age-friendly accountability and feedback. The mechanisms will systematically include participation of affected population (individuals with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls) in project planning and review and will facilitate the provision of feedback from affected people on the services. In the targeted schools, accessible, gender and age appropriate feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established to enable children to share their issues and views. This will be a continuous mechanism that will continue to be active beyond the project period. Accountability assessment will be conducted to determine the preferred accountability mechanisms, which include complaint help desks, suggestion boxes, meetings as well as reporting issues directly to staff. Informed by this assessment, the project will establish the mechanism at schools and in CFSs. These diverse mechanisms should offer options for persons with disabilities, limited literacy, and for speakers of minority languages to report on both sensitive and non sensitive issues, and also allow literate and non literate as well the most vulnerable to access the mechanisms. Children will also be sensitized on the availability and location of feedback and complaint mechanisms to enable them understand what to report and where.#Feedback from vulnerable community members who are not literate will be collect through the existing Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs).

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HAGI has a Programme Accountability Framework (PAF) and AAP with four pillars that holds the organization accountable to the affected population they seek to assist. Through the PAF framework, HAGI will ensure timely information sharing with the affected population; consult the affected population on any issue before making decision; promoting participation of the affected population at every stage of the project cycle and establishing relevant feedback and complaint mechanisms. HAGI will ensure that all the staff involved in the implementation of this project are trained on humanitarian accountability standards (PAF, Sphere Standards and Core humanitarian standards) so that they will deliberately involve, inform the beneficiaries and seek their opinions at all stages of the project management. The project beneficiaries will also be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process and the quality of services they are getting from the organization. It is also planned that the project team will provide guidance to the beneficiaries on the nature of complaints to launch and the available feedback/complaint mechanisms put in place to collect and redress grievances. HAGI will put in place child friendly feedback/complaint mechanisms, suggestion boxes, help desks, dedicated phone lines and email address for this purpose. Printed posters containing these addresses shall be distributed by the organization and placed in all Payams and meeting venues for ease of access by the beneficiaries and the general public. This project will also use conflicts sensitive planning to apply the principles of Do No Harm. Additionally, the project will ensure active and consistent communication and feedback from key stakeholders, such as local officials and government staff, at both county and state levels. Coordination will entail meeting with and communicating project activities to key stakeholders to ensure that activities are appropriate and well supported. Additional meetings and discussions will take place as needed to ensure any follow-up on issues, challenges, or areas that need improvement. HAGI has had a longstanding presence in Aweil Centre, Rumbek Centre, Yirol East and Yirol West, which resulted in solidifying good relationships with key stakeholders, providing the foundation for better accountability systems with the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Through the accountability mechanisms established, the M&E team will get feedback from teachers, pupils and parent and use this feedback to adjust the project to benefit children more. #The feedback collected from beneficiaries will be collated, analyzed and lessons learnt will be used to adapt the project.# to ensure adequate records of feedback/complaints from the beneficiaries for follow-up, HAGI team will establish accessible CRFMs (complaint response feedback mechanisms) such as complaint forms, complaint desks, hotlines, complaint boxes and complaint committees based on the area of applicability. Complaints and feedback will be registered and followed up with the communities on ongoing bases and used to adjust project activities. The records of complaints and feedback are kept and regularly analysed by MEAL team in Juba, whose officers track each case and advise/support the programme team. Under the supervision of Senior Management, the implementing programme team conducts regular review meetings and adjust the project implementation to changed circumstances or according to constructive feedback..
