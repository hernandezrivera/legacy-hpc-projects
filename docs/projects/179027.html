Project Summary

The planned intervention contributes to the reduction of morbidity and mortality, and improve overall health services for conflict-affected Syrians and IDPs. It will also focus mainly on the provision and improvement of primary health care services, secondary health care, referral system, physical rehabilitation, health education and specialized services.
The scope of QC 2021 is to support the core health services represented by the primary health care services (the plan is to support 16 PHCs so far in line with the essential health package, these centers are distributed between (Aleppo, Idlib, Tell Abiad, and Ras Al Ain). Currently supported centers with Response ID are: B0203064, K1102027, K1102029, H0804005, G0704041, G0703168, G0704042, B0204103, B0203057, B0203074, G0700088, G0703063, B0204075, G0704036, G0700058.
There will be three specialized hospitals (one general and two for gynecological and pediatric services) operating as follows: 1 general hospital in Idleb, 1 women's hospital in Aleppo and 1 women's hospital in Al-Hasakeh.
All projects are planned through self-implementation by QC field teams in targeted areas; partners also involved in certain circumstances and the joint implementation modality is existing.
So far $12.7M USD is secured from different sources. This fund of $12.7M will be utilized for the procurement of drugs and consumables, fully supporting 16 PHCs (salaries, operations, drugs, and consumables) for one year, fully supporting three hospitals (salaries, operations, drugs, and consumables) for one year, Operate two central medical commodities warehouses, purchase and operate 10 ambulances, construct and equip emergency department in Afrin hospital, one nursing and midwifery school, one physical rehabilitation and artificial limbs center and many types of medical equipment for different purposes. The project target is 4M populations over 12 months.
In order to ensure project quality, resources management, and accountability towards beneficiaries, QC MEAL staff will follow-up regularly on project activities to ensure activities are implemented per to the scope of work, linked appropriately to the needs, and going in line with the time frame. At the early beginning, QC MEAL staff will set an M&E plan as per to project log-frame. All the means of verifications and the frequency of MEAL visits will be scheduled with clear roles and responsibilities for each activity.
M&E Tools:
- Health staff attendance to facilities will be monitored by the admins and Health supervisors with attendance sheet as per the -Registration Form of the beneficiaries.
- Patient satisfaction surveys will be conducted to get feedback on the quality and need-based distributed items as well as the other health services provided.
- Staff satisfaction surveys will also be conducted to better understand obstacles and difficulties if any, and to keep the staff motivated and up to the task. Moreover, QC will conduct lessons learned and quality review workshops (3 times in project life-cycle), this will be Monitored through field visit and documented in a detailed report.
QC and its partners (Ataa, Syrian Forum, Onsur and SRD) maintain a strong working relationship with the local health authorities and Local Councils (LCs) in areas of operation in Syria which currently serve as one of the strongest resources for information on health needs in each area. QC continues coordination with humanitarian actors in the area in addition to close collaboration with OCHA, WHO and Health Cluster,
QC is in constant and continuous coordination with NGOs (HIH, IDA, SAMS, SEMA, UOSSM, SHAM, SR...) and INGOs (WVI, IMC, IRC, HI) ensure reach to updated information that is important for health service delivery and activities.
The proposed intervention will also complement the actions of other humanitarian health actors delivering assistance in the same areas through continuous communication with health directorates and other coordination platforms.

4 - Needs

The scale, severity, and complexity of the humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive due to continued hostilities in localized areas and new and protracted displacement. Most of the IDPs escaping hostilities by moved to urban centers and IDP camps in northwest Idleb; with an estimated 400,000 people displaced in northwest Syria and nearly 390,000 people displaced in northern Syria. OCHA Accessibility and cost of transport also impede access to services for persons with disabilities among beneficiaries who received assistance. However, 86%percent of surveyed households who sought health services in the previous three months reported having to pay for care most often for medication. The availability of medicines and treatment costs are also leading determinants of child mortality in Syria(HNO). QC intervention and because of its comprehensive nature will target the whole community groups including (Women, girls, men, and boys) with different ages. The assessment conducted by QC through contacts with local health directorate, communities, and other stakeholders indicated the need for continuity of the support to health facilities by staffing, provision of medical supplies and equipment and improvements and covering the running costs. Need assessment conducted in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates for QC health projects indicated that drugs is a priority in 68.8% of total facilities followed by medical equipment with around 18.8% while medical consumables come at least with around 12.5% of the total. The inadequacy of health services in Northern Syria exposes women to risks of morbidity and mortality due to lack of maternal health services, including the ANC, safe delivery and BEmOC services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will focus on implementing the following activities: 1- Increasing access to primary health care through the provision of the Essential Package of Primary Health Services, including reproductive health services and child health care ( boys and girls) 2- Procurement and supply of medicines, consumables and medical equipment for trauma, obstetric care for women, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions and strengthen the medical supply chains, including emergency supply stocks at districts warehouses and health facility levels. 3- Increasing access to treatment for people who live with (NCD) 4- Training of staff on key essential services such as management and primary and secondary prevention of non-communicable diseases, treatment of NCDs, basic surgical and obstetric care, task shifting of basic services to staff to provide care in their home communities when mobility restrictions or health facility are found. 5- Cover the cost of salaries of medical staff and provide incentives to attract and retain qualified medical staff from both sexes 6- Provide running costs of the hospitals and health centers 7- Support and improve the current referral system in the targeted area 8- Monitoring and evaluation of the health program activities where QC has a MEAL system in Gaziantep and inside Syria to monitor and evaluate the planned activities. In addition to the health facility-based information that will be collected as part of the HIS, QC will organize regular submission of 4Ws reports to a cluster 9- Monitoring and evaluation of project activities will take special consideration to seek feedback from the beneficiaries and other stakeholders. QC will report to the cluster at Gaziantep level on the implementation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The Whole of Syria (WoS) Health Cluster conducted a review to identify the most pressing health-related protection risks facing health actors and the affected population and suggested a set of mitigating measures. Risk: Attacks on health facilities and health care staff.Mitigating measures: While neither the health cluster nor humanitarian actors can guarantee protection from attacks on health care, health actors are encouraged to avail themselves of mechanisms for visibility, advocacy and accountability as follows: Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks.Risk of Gender Based Violence GBV.Mitigating measures: Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Specialized health staff will be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Risk of lack of Duty of care. Mitigating measures: implement staff care programs which provide psycho-social support services and resources to staff. Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights.- QC team is aware of and follow Covid-19 rules, health cluster protocol and WHO recommendations.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Through all project locations, environmental risks (floods, earthquakes, etc.) may have an effect on the implementation of the activities in the case that a major disaster happens in one of the areas of implementation. The likelihood of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on project activities will be very high. QC through its staff, will have a preparedness plan and mitigation mechanisms in all the activities and this to be shared with all staff . In this intervention, QC assume that the implementation of the activities will go smoothly in all targeted locations. In regard to this, some risks might be beyond QC control. The reinforcement of health facilities does not provide sufficient protection for patients, exposing them to physical harm/risk. *These can be mitigated by: putting in place task-shifting guidelines as part of care protocols - providing Training and adequate follow- up of competency - decentralizing services, clear messaging to communities around risks associated with health care facilities and underground care. Risk of Increased morbidity and mortality rate due to increasing the opportunity of diseases and medical conditions that needs professional intervention. *Mitigating measure: - Hire a qualified medical staff - Distribute medical supplies and consumables. The risk of increasing pressure on health services influx of IDPs is very high. Program activities will be negatively impacted when the number of IDPs in some areas suddenly increases. * Mitigating measure: - Expand and strengthen the available health services to meet the needs of new IDPs. More coordination with health actors on the ground to maximize use of the available resources to meet the increasing needs.
