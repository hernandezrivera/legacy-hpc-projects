Project Summary

GOAL’s primary purpose for 2019 is to deliver quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk girls and boys 6-59 months and PLWs in Melut and Ulang Counties, Greater Upper Nile. Protracted conflict and displacements in South Sudan have contributed to insufficient crop production, worsening levels of food insecurity and malnutrition throughout the country, with most areas in at least IPC 3 "Crisis" phase of food insecurity. Communities in Melut and Ulang are particularly vulnerable to malnutrition; the counties were classified as IPC 3 “crisis” (Melut) and 4 “emergency” (Ulang) phase of food insecurity as of September 2018, and although Melut is expected to improve to IPC2 during the last quarter of 2018, both counties are anticipated to deteriorate to IPC3 (Melut) and IPC4 (Ulang) at the start of 2019. Recent analysis indicates GAM rates of 21.7% (Melut, WVI SMART Survey March 2018) and 20.1% (Ulang, UNKEA SMART Survey March 2018), which are above the emergency threshold rates for treatment of malnutrition. To respond to these needs, GOAL will implement management of acute malnutrition in Melut and Ulang. Further, GOAL will provide MIYCN counselling services to prevent under-nutrition for most vulnerable and at-risk. GOAL will conduct enhanced nutrition situation monitoring, analysis and utilization of early warning information for timely coordinated response decision making. GOAL will also support at-risk communities to sustain their capacity to cope with significant shocks, while reinforcing protection, promoting access to basic health services, and integrating FSL and hygiene activities for the displaced and other vulnerable people.
GOAL proposes to provide nutrition services through a network of 21 TSFP sites in Melut (9 sites) and Ulang (12 sites) Counties, where GOAL has been operational since 2014 and 2007 respectively; in Melut, these services will be integrated with GOAL’s primary health services through 2 clinics and 1 mobile outreach to provide a comprehensive package of care. GOAL will strengthen linkages between partners implementing management for severe acute malnutrition (SAM), referring identified SAM cases to OTP for treatment and receiving beneficiaries discharged from OTP into TSFP for continuum of care. GOAL will enhance integration across sectors to provide comprehensive services for beneficiaries. GOAL will focus on integration of food security and livelihoods (FSL) into nutrition services by linking TSFP beneficiaries to ongoing FSL programming to address drivers of poor health and nutrition in the community. GOAL will also incorporate integration of WASH, aiming for clean water supply, safe and hygienic latrines and waste disposal facilities and handwashing stations at the nutrition centres, using WASH FIT (Water and Sanitation for Health Facility Improvement Tool). GOAL’s programming will focus on enhanced integration of protection into project activities to safeguard beneficiaries. GOAL health facilities in Melut are equipped to respond to and manage any cases of gender-based violence (GBV) presenting at the facilities; GOAL will also strengthen the referral mechanism to InterHealth, the Protection partner in Melut, and ARC, the Protection partner in Ulang. Further, GOAL will promote community utilization of its FCRM as a means for beneficiaries to voice feedback, questions, or concerns about programming, or to raise protection issues; GOAL will continue raising awareness of the mechanism to ensure beneficiaries are informed of the methods available to them in providing feedback to implementing partners.

Needs

Protracted conflict and increasing levels of food insecurity, morbidity, and difficulties in accessing humanitarian aid have contributed to a deteriorating nutrition situation in South Sudan; the latest World Bank poverty assessment report (2017) raises urgent concerns about malnutrition amongst children, with an estimated 1 million children under five years expected to be acutely malnourished and almost 300,000 severely malnourished. Despite this need, and because the conflict in South Sudan led to a disruption in the newest nation's systems, government institutions face challenges in effectively providing for its people. Further,urther, because the country has minimal economic opportunities, the government is unable to invest in necessary systems, including nutrition services. The fragile health system is characterized by lack of skilled staff, supplies, equipment and leadership at all levels, resulting in inadequate access to life-saving health and nutrition services. Community vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening food security situation country-wide; although Melut and Ulang Counties, classified as IPC phase 3 ("Crisis") and phase 4 (“Emergency”) in September 2018, are expected to experience modest improvements between October and December 2018, the most recent IPC analysis predicts they will deteriorate back to phases 3 (Melut) and 4 (Ulang) during the first quarter of 2019; these projections predict they would be at least one phase worse off without humanitarian assistance. Alarmingly high GAM rates have been observed alongside significant gaps in acute malnutrition coverage throughout Upper Nile State; in Melut GAM prevalence is 21.7% (SAM 4.9%, MAM 16.8%; WVI SMART Survey, March 2018), while in Ulang a GAM rate of 20.1% (SAM 2.7%, MAM 17.4%; UNKEA SMART Survey, March 2018) was reported among children 6-59M and GAM 18.8% among PLWs. These GAM rates in both counties are above the emergency threshold of 15%, thus treatment of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) for children below five years continue to be a key need for saving lives of most at risk children and PLWs. As a result, GOAL proposes to provide nutrition services through a network of 21 TSFP sites in Melut (9 sites) and Ulang (12 sites) Counties where GOAL has been operational since 2014 and 2007 respectively; in Melut, these services will be integrated with GOAL’s health services through 2 clinics and 1 mobile outreach to provide a comprehensive package of care to prevent children and PLWs further deteriorating into SAM. GOAL will also integrate FSL, WASH, and Protection components into programming. GOAL will refer TSFP beneficiaries into ongoing FSL projects to address drivers of poor nutrition at the household level. GOAL will refer any GBV cases to relevant Protection partners, while taking steps to promote safe access for beneficiaries to its services; GOAL will conduct refresher training for all staff on safeguarding and PSEA, while also encouraging communities to utilize its feedback, complaints and response mechanism (FCRM) as a resource to give feedback or voice concerns about programming.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Promotion messaging is tailored to relevant audiences to provide gender and age sensitive information regarding sexual and reproductive health, MIYCN practices, hygiene promotion, among others; these messages are designed to improve community health, nutrition and hygiene practices.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL will incorporate protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in all levels of programming; it will conduct routine PSEA and safeguarding training for all staff to educate on what constitutes sexual exploitation, proper reporting mechanisms if SEA is suspected, and ways to mitigate risk of SEA in programming, to better safeguard beneficiaries at all times; following training, all staff will sign the PSEA, Child Protection, Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies to reflect their understanding of the consequences of breaches to these policies. During awareness campaigns, special emphasis will be placed on SEA so that communities are well-informed on various methods they may use to report suspected or perceived cases of SEA, which include GOAL’s internal reporting email (speakup@goal.ie), external mechanism (goal@safecall.co.uk) and South Sudan’s countrywide reporting through the IASC PSEA Task Force.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with stakeholders in its field sites to inform them about programme implementation and provide an opportunity for feedback about its projects. These consultations are used to improve subsequent programming and inform project design. Throughout the life of the project, awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints about the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

There is an existing feedback, complaints, and response mechanism (FCRM) in place, and GOAL will target raising awareness within communities of the FCRM and its use as a vehicle for beneficiaries to voice their feedback or concerns about programming. In particular, GOAL will focus on ensuring vulnerable groups, including women, elderly and disabled persons, are aware of their rights, entitlements, and methods to report issues related to programming, child protection or sexual exploitation and abuse.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with communities to discuss programme implementation, achievements, and opportunities for improvement; additionally, community members may utilize the FCRM to provide feedback or complaints, which are routinely monitored for appropriate response.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s programming will focus on enhanced integration of protection into project activities to safeguard beneficiaries. GOAL health staff in Melut have been trained on clinical management of rape (CMR), and health facilities have requisite kits to respond to and manage any cases of gender-based violence (GBV) referred to the facilities. In 2019, GOAL will conduct refresher safeguarding training to additional cadres of staff as well as strengthen the referral mechanism to county Protection partners (InterHealth in Melut and ARC in Ulang).

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers barriers vulnerable groups, including pregnant women and children, may face in accessing care; it seeks to address these barriers through provision of mobile TSFPs to provide treatment to those unable to access health facilities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s humanitarian responses will routinely assess, analyze and monitor the risks to civilian safety, and take appropriate action to mitigate risks. Programme managers will ensure the programme brings human rights standards and principles to the centre of all interventions, recognizes programme participants as rights-holders, as well as focus on vulnerable and marginalized groups to ensure their safety and dignity. Adequate measure will be put in place at the distribution sites to ensure distribution takes minimal amount of time, including adequate staffing, clear communication to beneficiaries about when to come for supplies, and triaging the area to make sure sufficient supplies are available at the time of distribution. Furthermore, GOAL will ensure that adequate gender-disaggregated latrine facilities are available at facilities where programming is implemented to protect dignity of the users.
