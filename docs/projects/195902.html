Project Summary

Maram Foundation (MF) aims through this project to provide adequate and full spectrum of humanitarian lifesaving multisectoral assistance with full CCCM core activities to 15,229 IDPs - 2,745 families/ HHs in total, including (4397 G, 4452 B, 3339 W, 3041 M, 897 PWD), in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates.
Where the project's areas of intervention include Four location ( 4 planned camps); in Idleb (Kafr Jales, Shiekh Bahr PC and Maaret Elekhwan PC), and Aleppo governorates, in the Euphrates Shield area - (Ihtemlat PC), in northwest Syria (NW Syria).
- In Ehtemlat planned Camp, the number of beneficiaries/ IDPs reached to 1029 HHs/ 5252 IDPs.
- In Sheikh Bahir planned Camp, the number of beneficiaries/ IDPs reached to 372 HHs/ 2175 IDPs.
- In Kafar Jales planned Camp, the number of beneficiaries/ IDPs reached to 714 HHs/ 3602 IDPs.
- In Maaret Elekhwan planned Camp, the number of beneficiaries/ IDPs reached to 630 HHs/ 4200 IDPs.
Thus, Maram will continue the following up and serve as the camp management agency, and set in place proper staffing/management personnel to conduct CCCM activities, including care and maintenance to basic infrastructure, registration/data collection, vulnerability mapping, verification, establishment of gender-balanced camp governance/ participation management structures, and accountability mechanisms, routine site renovations, and strengthening fire mitigation and response in the identified Planned Camps.
In addition to enhance community participation and form committees, strengthen the capacity of staff and committees through providing relevant trainings for them, build accountability mechanism towards IDPs by established mechanism for coordinated multisectoral services provision, established mechanism for community engagement and accountability to IDPs.
This project also works on strengthening household and communal coping mechanisms in camps and developing spontaneous exit strategies through providing tailored livelihood activities for better solutions (Vocational trainings and In-kind grants).
It will consider the different priorities and needs of IDPs, and the planned intervention will mainstream protection principles especially (Do no harm) throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, and older adults.
MF’s extensive CCCM experience is demonstrated by establishment and the ongoing successful management of Planned Camps in NW Syria.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection crisis compounded by over ten years. the conditions continued to deteriorate and appear to be worsening. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Referring to SYRIA HNO 2022 the expanded sectoral analysis. For CCCM, the people in need 2M (Female 1 M, Male 0.9M)- 52% female - 48% male/ 58% Children (0-17), 38% Adults (18-59), 4% Elderly (>59) - 2% PwDs. These percentages reflect the extent of the needs and the necessity of intervention. Only during the period from 1 to 30 Sep 2022, more than 12,000 IDPs took place in north-west Syria, with departures principally occurring from Dana, and other notable departures from Idleb, as well as Atareb. The main locations where IDPs arrived to included Dana, Maaret Tamsrin and Idleb sub-districts.By referring to CCCM database, regarding the analysis of the different reasons for new displacements in NWS, where the main factors for new displacements were loss of income (62%), reduced access to shelter/raise of rents (15%) and reduced access to health services (10%), anticipation of future conflict escalation (17%). For this purpose, MF strives to continue providing comprehensive CCCM packages in line with the vision of the Humanitarian Response Plan to provide safer and more dignified shelter solutions, for thisMF M&E unit will continue assessing the needs and the gaps to be addressed.

4 - Implementation of activities

MF through this project aims to provide 15,229 IDPs - 2,745 families with core CCCM services.The project activities aligns with CCCM cluster priorities, by provide lifesaving services through services provision coordination mechanism, and enhancing the safety provisions by continue strengthening fire mitigation and response, care, functional and maintenance to basic infrastructure in the identified planned camps. and by enhanced accountability to IDPs through establishment and implementation of CCCM mechanisms.Also by strengthening HH and communal coping mechanisms in camps through providing tailored livelihood activities (450 IDPs with Vocational trainings and In-kind grants).Hence, the activities will be as below;-Providing streamlined humanitarian lifesaving assistance, multi-sectoral assistance to the affected IDPs and strengthen the basic infrastructure support in identified sites.-Accurate registration process.-Regular maintenance of site infrastructures.-Enhanced accountability to IDPs through establishment of CCCM mechanisms.-Set up accountable complaint and feedback mechanism (AAP).-Establish community committees, making sure participating of women.-Capacity Buildings for the camp staff and Committees.- Fire Mitigation Prevention& Response.- An expanded mobile CCCM approach / CCCM mobile teamsAll activates will consider the different priorities and needs of IDPs from different ages including men, women, boys, girls, elderly people and PwDs.
