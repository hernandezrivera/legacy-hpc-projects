Project Summary

Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) proposes an Education in Emergencies project that will respond and provide improved access to safe, inclusive and protective lifesaving education among 2500 girls, 2500 boys, 500 men and 500 women by improving the quality and equitable access to education to ensure continuity of relevant education services among targeted children/ youth, strengthening the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth.
SPOCI proposes to achieve by scaling up already achieved activities through current UNICEF education funding and sustaining the momentum of activities by establishment/ rehabilitation of 10 learning spaces/ classrooms, provision of school meals, establishment/ rehabilitation of 10 gender segragated latrine facilities equipped with 10 hand washing facilities, prepositioning through UNICEF/ Cluster pipeline and provision of scholastic learning and teaching materials/ EiE kits, capacity building and trainings of 40 teachers and volunteers, trainings which will encompass community focal points and authorities on psychosocial support, trainings and equipping of SMCs, PTAs on school governance and management.
Proposed activities are also in line with the 2019 Education Cluster strategy and will ensure delivering a WASH Nutrition integrated package integrated to address the high level of malnutrition, by targeting SAM/MAM children and their families. These will be further integrated with other Health-related priorities, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health, and ultimately resulting in high malnutrition. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse. The project will be taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches.
SPOCI has an office in Pibor town with adequate and experienced staff, with existing strong grassroot networks, that will scale up current UNICEF EiE programming and sustain momentum of activities.

Needs

School-aged children (3 to 17 years) represent around 48% of the population in the country, which is faced by deteriorating economic conditions, unprecedented food insecurity levels with at least 5.2 million people projected to be food insecure in January-March 2019. This coupled with intermittent clashes, prevalent child and women abduction and cattle related fighting in Pibor continue to critically undermine provision of education services in the county, among other regions (UNICEF Country Report, 2018; IPC Sept 2018). Pibor is among other counties in the country where the needs of boys, girls, men and women are compounded by violations of their rights including killings, unlawful detentions, family separation, neglect, sexual /gender based violence and forced recruitments into armed forces and groups. At least 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited by armed actors/ factions and groups in 2017, up from 17,000 in 2016. Despite recent 2018 releases spearheaded by NDDR and UNICEF which brought the total number of children released from armed forces and groups in 2018 to 806 (214 girls); there are still reports which indicate continued recruitments of children in Pibor among other regions (UNICEF South Sudan May 2018 Situation Report; 2018 Child Protection/ CAAFAG Coordination Meeting Humanitarian Updates).Boys, girls, men and women including children in schools are in addition at risk of water borne diseases, as the country is reeling from the longest cholera outbreak in the country , with 20,438 cases with 436 deaths. Among these, 18 per cent were children under five years, 28 per cent were children aged five to 14 years. Pibor being prone to flooding, presents a key hotspot location attributed to poor access to safe water, hygiene and sanitation coverage, especially among children (Cholera Sitreps, 2016/17/18). Recent rapid assessments in Pibor indicate possible IPC 5 outcomes in 2018, food insecurity has been identified as a risk factor for GBV, especially sexual violence, with women and girls forced to move long distances in search of food and water, exchange sex for food or turn to do petty jobs in insecure locations which increases their risk to sexual violence; and further contributing to school dropout rates and retention including demotivated teachers and volunteers (Education Cluster Assessment 2016; Protection Trends 2018; FEWNET Food Security Outlook 2018).Despite SPOCI among other partners responding to education in Pibor, a lack of qualified teachers, irregular or non-payment of teacher salaries/ incentives remains a key concern leading to teacher abseentism and demotivation. This is coupled by attacks on schools, destruction and looting of schools including armed occupation, children and teachers including recruitment of boys and men into armed groups/ forces. SPOCI proposes to respond to above needs by scaling up already achieved activities through current UNICEF education funding and sustaining the momentum of activities. The project will be taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches. SPOCI has established presence on grouns with a coordination office in Pibor town with adequate and experienced staff, with existing strong grassroot networks that will scale up current UNICEF EiE programming and sustain momentum of activities.
