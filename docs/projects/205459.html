Project Summary

This project is designed to effectively contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods of the most vulnerable and affected IDPs, host communities and returnees’ population in Rumbek East, Rumbek North and Rumbek Centre of Lakes State, Rubkona, Guit, Mayendit and Leer Counties of Unity state. The project plans to target 28,000 HHs equivalent to 168,000 beneficiaries from vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities. It is designed to make use of the windows of opportunity for targeted population facing acute food insecurity. It is very much consistent with the FSL allocation both in terms of the Priority, Geographical scope, and the seasonality of the activities.
The major livelihood activities in the selected area for response is support for livestock, crop, vegetable and fish production with resilient light training. A total of 168,000 vulnerable individuals will be targeted and selected from the targeted areas. These will include men, women, boys and girls selected from the IDP, returnees, agro-pastoralists and host population. Additionally, the targeting will consider women headed HHs, PWD and families that have persons with special needs. The targeting will be done with gender lens and consideration of incorporation of Accountability to affected population. A total of 100 CAHWs will be trained and 140,000 livestock will be targeted for treatment and vaccination. The program implementation will ensure that Do No Harm principle is adhered to as transparency and accountability to the affected population is placed right at the center of beneficiary targeting and provision of support. Humanitarian Aid for Development extensive presence and history in Lakes and Unity state coupled with an in-depth understanding of the community conflict dynamics and tailored approaches to specific communities will mitigate risk and reduce any potential negative impacts of the project and any protection issues.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple intersecting crises which is having a profound and adverse impact on food security in the country. Some of the difficulties the nation is experiencing include insecurity, subnational violence, developing regional and national socioeconomic challenges exacerbated by the global economic slump, and the effects of climate change. The FEWS NET reported that emergency levels of food insecurity would persist in several counties during the lean season harvest amid returnee influx. (FEWSNET August 2023). The ongoing conflict in Sudan continues to compound an already dire situation, forcing thousands of people to cross into South Sudan. As of 31 August, 252,242 people had crossed into South Sudan, and is anticipated to rise to at least 600,000 through end of the year. Of these, 91 percent were South Sudanese returning to a country hosting over 300,000 refugees and 2.3 million internally displaced, with limited livelihood opportunities. Access to primary services for people living in South Sudan and new arrivals remains strained, further complicating the provision of humanitarian assistance (WFP Situation Report#316, August 2023).In Unity State, IPC analysis for the period of September 2023 to July 2024, during the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, the food security situation will seasonally deteriorate in the State and Ruweng Administrative Area with an estimated 744,00 people (66% of the state’s population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 202,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 542,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period, 6 counties (Guit, Koch, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Rubkona) will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Similarly, in lakes state, during the lean-season projection period from April and July 2024, the food security will deteriorate, with an estimated 753,000 people (59% of the State’s population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 253,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 500,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this projection period, all counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with only Wulu County being classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurityWidespread events of cattle raiding as well as altered marketing/migration routes occurred in most areas of major conflict/insecurity. Due to inadequate veterinary services, livestock diseases have significantly affected livestock productivity and threatened to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists. This has been further exacerbated by conflict disrupted migratory pattern which end up creating new disease outbreaks, inadequate pasture and water, lootings among others. According to FEWSNET, August 2023, In parts of Upper Nile, Unity, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal bordering Sudan, cases of increased competition and conflict over limited grazing resources are reported, linked to the atypically extended presence of Sudanese pastoralists. Conditions are likely to further deteriorate with the expected arrival of more Sudanese pastoralists in these areas due to ongoing conflict in Sudan. In line with FSL Cluster Overall Objective of decreasing the number of people in need of FSL humanitarian assistance by 2025, HAD will provide complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support including training on improved agronomy practices and fish handling techniques to the communities in Rumbek East, Rumbek North and Rumbek Centre, Rubkona, Guit, Mayendit and Leer Counties where indicators show a continuously degrading situation.

2. Project activities

• Promote emergency food production through main season fishing, agriculture seeds & tool distribution through provision of livelihood kits that include fishing equipment, vegetable seeds and crop seeds. • Reducing pest and crop disease through farmers training on integrated pest and disease management, FAW surveillance, detection, prevention and eradication. • Livestock vaccination for disease prevention and treatment. • Support value chain addition through milk, fish preservation and marketing.• Training and capacity building through both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills) and vocational training
