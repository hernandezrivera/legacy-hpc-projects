Project Summary

This Action aims at implementing lifesaving responses for the displacement affected communities. The project aims to increase access to safe water and improve sanitation and hygiene in an effort to reduce the transmission of AWD/cholera and water borne diseases for the host community, IDPs and returnees in the regions of Middle Shabelle, Bay and Bakool. The current WASH gaps cited in thethree regions are: depletion of water source, lack of improved sanitation, high water prices, inadequate quantity and quality of water due to recurrent drought across the country and influx of IDPs which increase the demand for WASH facilities. INTERSOS intends to intervene to ensure rapid and sustained WASH services at key health and nutrition centers as well as in any strategic basic services' provision facility in areas highly affected by displacement. For this reason, schools will be targeted to both retain and reintegrate children in schools in a safely and dignified manner, with a focus in providing wash services for girls (i.e. sanitary pads) to ultimately improve hygiene standards and access to education. During the intervention, Water Committees, composed equally by women and men to represent equally the community, will be trained to manage wash facilities and scale up hygiene promotion to prevent potential AWD/Cholera outbreak, thus to build resilience and promotion of preparedness and disaster risk reduction mechanisms.
INTERSOS is committed to ensuring that its programs are gender sensitive and achieve the Gender Marker scale. Women and girls have been adversely affected by the conflict, mainly due to breakdown in community protection mechanisms. Therefore, particular attention will be given to Lactating, pregnant women and children (0-5 years old) visiting Nutrition Centers, who will be provided with Hygiene Kits. Menstrual hygiene products and specific targeted items will be included as part of the hygiene kits.
To guarantee communities' engagement, consultation in all planning and implementation phases of the project will be constant. In addition, water distribution points and sanitation facilities will be established in areas that are safe and appropriate for women to access and will be designed considering people with disability in the community. INTERSOS will use its established female community network to divulgate hygiene promotion messages. A feedback and complaints mechanism will be put in place and beneficiaries provided with a hotline number in order to ensure accountability towards the affected population.

Needs

It is estimated that 4.6m people in Somalia are in humanitarian need. 1.5m of them are food incesure (IPC3 & IPC4). Due to the draught and conflict in 2016-2017, internally displaced people in Somalia are today estimated to be approximatly 2.6m. According to the WASH Cluster, 1.7 million people require urgent WASH assistance in remaining drought acffected regions and about 600,000 are in need in recently flooded districts. Access to an improved water source remains low nationwide, with regions such as Bay and Bakool registering the lowest (25%). Lack of access to water, sanitation and hygiene has a huge impact on human health including diarrhea which is the leading cause of death in children under five globally and its constant presence in low income settings may contribute significantly to under nutrition. Even tough access to water is granted in most of urban settings, the water is often unsfae and/or contaminated. Poor sanitation is worrying in Somalia, and not only in IDP settlements. As reported by the WASH cluster, despite the outbreak of AWD/cholera last year, hand washing is practiced by only a third of the population. Due to the recent rains above average , and lack of rehabilitation of wash facilities, the risk of outbreak of water born deseases and cholera in the coming months is still very high, especially in the regions targeted by this Action (Middle Shabelle, Bay, Bakool). Based on this assessment, INTERSOS proposes to implement WASH integrated project with health, nutrition and edcuation for the purposes of tackling the prevalence outbreak of AWD/Cholera and improve the health standard of the communities in Bay, Bakool, and Middle Shabelle regions of Somalia. Schools will be considered a critical entry point to provide wash services and to contrubute to an education outcome. A protection approach will be embedded in all activities to prevent and mitigate risks of violence, abuse and exclusion linked to access of clean water and sanitation.

Activities

Activity 1: Clean up and solid waste disposal campaigns. Each social worker will be equipped with a waste management kit. Activity 2: Rehabilitate/construct and maintain WASH infrastructure in line with cluster standards (6 health centers, 6 schools, 12 TLS and CTCs). Each institution will receive a complete wash package. The construction/rehabilitation of water supply/storage points and Latrines (including hand wash facilities) will respect Sphere and Wash cluster standards. Ad hoc Hygiene promotion will be organized to diffuse key Hygiene messages (with a specific focus on menstrual hygiene). Activity 3: Mapping of water sources at district level and Rehabilitation of community water sources (shallow wells, deep boreholes or others).A water points assessment will be implemented along with district authorities in 6 Districts targeted by this Action. INTERSOS will prioritize water points which respect Sphere standards and wash cluster guidelines in terms of safety, security and accessibility. Activity 4: Emergency latrine construction and latrine desludging for in 24 IDP camps respecting the ratio of 5 HHs x latrine. Latrines will be equipped with hand wash facility and lockable doors.Activity 5: Establish 24 Water Management Committees (WMCs) at community and IDP level to manage WASH facilities and conduct hygiene promotion. Each committee will be trained on wash facilities management and reparation, general administration of Water management committee and how make Hygiene promotion in communities and IDP level.Activity 6: Water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing programme. Water treatment will be guaranteed via the distribution of Aqua tabs, while water chlorination will cover water storage tanks. Water treatment monitoring will be guaranteed via FRC monitoring control (WHO Standards).Activity 7: Hygiene promotion campaign in 6 targeted Districts on drinking water collection and storage, hand washing, hygienic sanitation.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The Project aims at promoting durable solution/resilience of displacement affected communities. This objective will be achieved by ensuring that displaced communities who intend to integrate in their current area of settlement have access to clean water and to sanitation facilities. In order to achieve this result, the project will be integrated with other ongoing actions in the areas of intervention to promote access to basic services for the targeted communities such as schools and hospitals in order to build their resilience and to promote long term solutions to displacement. The proposed Action is linked to the second IASC criteria on durable solutions to guarantee an adequate standard of living. The targeted population group will be the IDP community, as well as returnees and vulnerable host community members in areas affected by displacement. INTERSOS will transfer responsibilities to the relevant local authorities/community members during the project implementation through enhanced coordination and engagement with the government and with the affected population. INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger, its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility for the delivery of humanitarian services. Moreover, the design of the project is implemented in coordination with the relevant government authorities and through a systematic engagement of the targeted communities in order to promote community ownership and sustainability by using existing community structures, such as the water management committees, in order to define priorities, implement activities and monitoring of the services' delivered.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

At the beginning of the project, specific space will be reserved to discuss with all the project staff protection risks related to each projects’ activity. This exercise will help to identify possible threats and to avoid or reduce their impact on the project.At level of planned activities, the main risk is related to the confidentiality about the information and the proper verification of the beneficiaries. In this regard INTERSOS guarantees that field staff will undertake follow up trainings on the database management, verification, protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection coordinator. The registration and verification of beneficiaries will be done through a committee to ensure fair representation of the most disadvantaged community members. Moreover, the relevant local authorities will be fully involved from the onset of the project in order to minimize protection risks related to the implementation of the activities in the targeted locations. In order to avoid risks related to miscommunication and wrong expectations, activities will be planned in advance with engagement of all the main stakeholders and beneficiaries.Furthermore, different population groups will be targeted (returnees, IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and conflict based on population's groups. This approach will be paramount in displacement affected communities in order to promote social cohesion in the areas of operation.All the recruited staff will be closely supervised by the Program Manager. Periodical peer to peer meeting will be fostered in order to share good practices and lessons learnt. INTERSOS staff will be trained on the internal policies on child protection and protection from abuse and sexual exploitation. A feedback and complaints mechanism through a hot line number will be used during the entire implementation of the program and complaints will be reported to the Program Manager and investigated.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Disaggregated data to effectively inform programming. INTERSOS will identify groups who face difficulties accessing services (e.g. women, girls, persons with disabilities...) and specific time will be reserved to discuss at the beginning of the project all the activities which could be critically affected by social exclusion. This exercise will help to identify possible threats and to avoid/reduce their impact on the project and it will be conducted together with local authorities and relevant stakeholders.At level of planned activities, the main risk is related to the exclusion of vulnerable population groups such as women and children in all phases of the project, therefore, vulnerable groups will be strongly engaged at the planning phase of the project in order to ensure their opinions are embedded in the programming. In this regard INTERSOS guarantees that field staff will undertake trainings on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection coordinator. The relevant local authorities will be fully involved from the onset of the project in order to minimize risk of exclusion of women and children in the targeted locations. Project's activities will always focus on gender and age mark and ensure proper engagement of all the population. Priority during beneficiaries' selection will be given to women and children.Moreover, different population groups will be targeted (returnees, IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion based on population's groups. All the recruited staff will be closely supervised by the Program Manager. Periodical peer to peer meeting will be fostered in order to share good practices and lessons learnt. INTERSOS staff will be trained on the internal policies on child protection and protection from abuse and sexual exploitation. A feedback and complaints mechanism through a hot line number will be used during the entire implementation of the program and complaints effectively investigated.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Project's activities will ensure proper engagement of all the population groups with priority to women and children.Different population groups will be targeted (returnees, IDPs and host community) in order to mitigate risks of exclusion and to promote successful reintegration and long term solutions. This approach is paramount in displacement affected communities in order to enhance social cohesion and peaceful coexistence. The relevant local authorities will be properly engaged in activities design and implementation as well as informed of ongoing activities and developments through periodical meetings conducted by Project Manager and Field Coordinators. This will minimize the risk of misunderstandings and overlapping of activities with other partners and the government. Most of all, community representatives will be engaged in order to promote project's ownership and to support local solutions to emerging challenges as well as communitye's decision making. Wide representation of the community will be taken into account especially over decisions on distribution of resources. A proper engagement also of village and community leaders will mitigate security risks thus allowing INTERSOS Staff to safely implement activities in all the targeted locations.Monitoring the evolving security situation in areas of operation will be also critical in order to ensure the safety of the staff implementing activities.Risks of fraud and corruption will be minimized by delegation of duties and sharing of responsibilities, as well as monitoring visits and quality control of activities implemented with the ultimate aim of avoiding conflict.Beneficiaries will be provided with hotline numbers to refer complaints. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address human errors thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services such as cash transfers.
