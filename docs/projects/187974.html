Project Summary

More than ten years of conflict, economic degradation, and persistent displacement has exposed millions of people in Northwest Syria to ongoing shocks and deprivation. Flood and incidents impacted many sites in northwest Syria (NWS), The top three affected sub-districts were Maaret Tamsrin, Jisr Ash Shugur, and Dana, and the main urgent needs included tents, non-food items (NFI), and graveling. As a result of these incidents, many IDPs lost NFI, furniture, and personal belongings. Areas with concrete shelters were reported as being more resistant, while the least resistant areas were newly established or relocated IDP sites, or sites in flood-prone areas such as valleys or agricultural land. Heavy rains and strong winds affected the roads to and within the sites. IDP families with the support of the civil defense, local authorities, and/or NGOs were able to open rain drainage and remove water from the tents. Sewage systems were also flooded causing sewage water to enter and damage tents. This project proposes a holistic approach to enhance resilience and strengthen the humanitarian response, addressing shelter needs through a local-based and infrastructure-focused approach in Maaret Tamsrin, and Dana districts. The project aims to achieve the following outcome: Improve Shelter and living conditions among conflict-affected HHS in Maaret Tamsrin, and Dana district, enhancing access to safe, dignified, comprehensive, and gender-equitable Shelter solutions through:1. provide an essential infrastructure rehabilitation for 10 IDP camps in Maaret Tamsrin and Dana districts(roads, drainage system, tents leveling, and culverts) 20,950 individuals will be targeted. 2. Provide a better-dignified shelter solution to 2 IDP sites in Maaret Tamsrin, this type of shelter will provide more adequate space inside the unit. more dignity and privacy, provide spaces for people and children these units will secure sustainable protection from the weather factors according to the good floor insulation and to the high quality of the used materials for the unit, 2,750 individuals will be targeted. The Shelter activities have been developed in coordination with SNFI Clusters, and in consultation with WASH and CCCM clusters to ensure the project is integrated with ongoing and planned works to increase resilience in targeted locations. First, 18 km of roads will be rehabilitated in Dana, and Maaret Tamsrin camps, second, establishing drainage systems and culverts in IDP sites in Maaret Tamsrin and Dana to mitigate flooding and improve access to services. third, the project will construct 500 dignified and safe transitional shelters utilizing locally available resources. Overall the project will directly benefit 23,700 inds (12,324 M, 11,376 F) living in 12 IDP sites in Dana and Maret tamsrin. IYD team, who are highly experienced in infrastructure projects, will provide technical guidance and expertise to ensure the quality and appropriateness of infrastructure activities as well as better outcomes for beneficiaries. IYD will work closely with Shelter cluster and Syrian national NGO which has experience with a proven track record in infrastructure works, rehabilitation, and reparation of roads and drainage systems. IYD will work through local contractors to gravel roads, construction of culverts, and/or provide tent insulation, to mitigate the risk of seasonal flooding impacting NWS camps residents and to enhance their access to humanitarian assistance and services. The following steps will be considered in the implementation of the project, Topographic surveys, Leveling the muddy and dirt roads, Implementing the gravel base layer, Implementing and leveling the top layer, Laying the culvert concrete pipes, Casting the reinforced concrete above the pipes, and in the entrance of the culvert, Implementing steelworks above the culverts, Implementing of tent floor insulation (Raising Tents). IYD will be responsible for the management of the project by HQ office

4 - Needs

According to the shelter cluster, CCCM cluster, HNO reports, and to have a full overview and better understanding of the needs for all the population groups in targeted locations, a rapid need assessment was conducted by IYD staff. The results of these assessments can be summarized by:1-- Because of the harsh winter season and based on the need to increase the access to the camps, it is recommended to improve the roads out/in the IDP camps by laying layers of gravel and damping it well to make a solid surface resist the weather’s conditions2- According to the nature and muddy land of the camps’ sites and as a result of heavy raining where floods form and wash out the tents, it is highly recommended to establish a drainage system inside the camps to protect the tents and roads from flood and keep the access active in all situations.3- Since the existing tents are directly established on the farmland it is recommended to elevate the ground of tents to secure good insulation and good protection from the rain.3- The existing type of shelter units are not suitable for dignity life, lacking and poor for the minimum requirements for living conditions, no privacy, no protection from weather conditions or from outside actors, all inhabitants reported about the acute needs to improve this type of shelter to match the minimum living standards. 4- in the absence of lighting in the IDP camps it is recommended to install a lighting system in the camps to provide protection for women.

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD planned to implement this project through the intervention in the following main activities. First rehabilitation 18 km roads inside10 IDP camps and establishing 5 culverts. Second ground insulation for 1.370 tents in 10 IDP camps. Third, establishing drainage system in 10 IDP camps. Fourth improve 500 shelter units to better-dignified shelter made from sandwich panels and steel structure. Fifth installing lightning system in 10 IDP camps, IYD, through the coordination with shelter cluster, CCCM cluster, and local authority, will implement this project in the proposed targeted locations; the implementation of the activities will be through local vendors and suppliers, all project materials will be from the local market, the workers will be from the targeted locations, this will give the project advantage to contribute in the income of the IDPs in the camps. IYD team will share the project information with the IDPs before starting through the project staff and project IDs as a type of community engagement; a topographic and breakdown technical study will be conducted for the flood mitigation in potential locations. The technical engineers will work closely with the suppliers to ensure the good quality of the implementation, M&E team will conduct regular monitoring for all activities to ensure all is going well, weekly reports from the program and M&E will be sent to the main office in Turkey, the senior project manager in HQ will follow the implementation regularly.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: The potential for discrimination against beneficiaries of PWDs, due to the staff doesn't understand the specific protection risks that persons with disabilities face while implementing the field activities. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly. Mitigation: IYD will Assess the current attitudes and knowledge of staff and provide training on disability inclusion measures to enhance their confidence and efficacy in the field. Training will aim to ensure that staff understands the specific protection risks that persons with disabilities face, how to mitigate or reduce these. Risk 2: The project activities may cause harm for some women, girls, and PWDs, as well as other vulnerable groups during their participation in these activities either through a cause of action or failing to take necessary actions. Likelihood: Medium Impact: Highly. Mitigation:1 - Analyze programs from different perspectives will be conducted by asking the local community for feedback on what is not going well and ways to improve it, in addition, a constant assessment will be taken if project activities are having negative consequences on the beneficiaries and may threaten their safety and dignity.2 - IYD will establish robust feedback and complaint mechanisms. Such as: Customize a text messaging hotline and secure feedback box. Conduct face-to-face interviews with beneficiaries. Organize community meetings to discuss the beneficiary list and gather feedback. Conduct FGD with beneficiaries.

Zero tolerance means that active measures will be introduced to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse by IYD and that appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against all persons who are found to have violated the IYD standards


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

3,809 displaced families benefited from shelter infrastructure activities through road graveling, flood preventative, out of them 670 displaced families received tent leveling. 500 Vulnerable families have been provided with local dignified shelter solutions and Site improvement. Assumptions: IYD can procure the required materials and undertake the activities. IYD has access to the proposed project's areas and has support from the local authorities. Community acceptance to project activities. The security situation will remain relatively calm. IYD has experience in infrastructure projects implementation (graveling, rehabilitation, WASH services, etc.). Risks: Closure of borders with Turkey and increase the material's prices. Change in the security situation. Intervention from the local authorities in the project's activities. Late winter and continued rain, which will lead to delays in the implementation of activities. Mitigations: Coordinate with the local councils to ensure the safety of IYD’s employees and beneficiaries. IYD has a good relationship with community’s leaders, this will help us push back and prevent any attempts to interfere with the project’s activities.
