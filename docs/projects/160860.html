1 - Needs

Help e.V has been present in Yirol East since 2016 following vulnerability and needs trends and constantly monitoring WaSH & Nutrition Data of the population. This monitoring has been coupled with a nutrition pre-harvest SMART survey in June 2018 in Yirol East and the result showed increased food insecurity at the household level, and high rates of acute malnutrition with of GAM rate of 22.6% and SAM rate of 6.5% among children 6-59 months. Acute malnutrition prevalence among PLWs measured by MUAC (<23 cm), was 25.7%. Infant feeding practices were poor with only 25.8% of children 6-8 months receiving complementary foods and almost no dietary diversity; Vitamin A supplementation (61.9%) and deworming (56.8%). Malnutrition remains one of the top morbidities for children under five years of age in Yirol East. Moreover, in August 2019, FSNMS R24: Projected a High prevalence of acute malnutrition as observed during the lean season of 2019 with an estimated 10,000 people in Yirol East county of former Lakes State are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Help e.V proposes Multi-dimensional and integrated WASH, FSL and protection intervention to continue strengthening and capitalizing the current partnership with SSHF in South Sudan in link with both organizations mandates to provide lifesaving assistance through comprehensive humanitarian relief with a strong protection component that with tackle root problems of beneficiaries of this intervention while enhancing community resilience;- Prevention, detection and care of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) amongst children under 5 years old and of malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women, through 12 OTP's and 3 Outreach posts;- Dissemination of Community-based Management of Malnutrition (CMAM), ensuring community-based MASS MUAC Screening, MIYCN awareness-raising and Mother MUAC Strategy. - Reinforcement of a Community Outreach Network to ensure Protection monitoring, referral mechanisms and awareness-raising of the local population on protection rights and needs, health and hygiene; protection mainstreaming within Help’s humanitarian operation and capacity building in protection, PSEA, AAP and GBV of Help’s staff and nutrition personnel.The present request is justified with a thorough knowledge of the area of intervention and its needs. As a whole CNW and CNV’s will integrate Help’s outreach component to ensure closeness with the community by diffusing key messages. CNV’s will particularly focus on PLW’s and Mother Care Groups animation while CNW’s will ensure the whole outreach component. Another gap is since September 2018 – June 2019, there was an influx of IDP’s from Rumbek East & North due to inter-tribal fighting between the two clans and are now settling in Yirol East. According to the report from the RRC, the process of registration was on-going. With no food in the community, these IDP’s will be adversely affected as the overall nutrition situation is already serious and dire need. 20191001_IOM_DTM_R6_first_resultes_IDP_RET_Dataset_admin1_2_3. Moreover, Yirol East county, health and nutrition services are predominantly manned by people of lower cadre (CHWs, untrained semiliterate Nutrition Assistant CNWs, or CNV’s with much lower qualification to provide quality services), with CHW/MCHW accounting for about 44%. Help e.V will hire staff with requisite skilled staff in capacity on nurses and clinical officers for the position of nutrition supervisors, officers and managers.These important needs require a multi-dimensional response which includes early identification and treatment of cases through active screening of SAM children, community sensitization on prevention of malnutrition, promotion of breastfeeding and hygiene practices. Aiming for a sustained impact, this multi-dimensional response must be part of an integrated approach tackling WASH, food security and centrality of protection.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

HELP have already installed event product latrine to some health facilities and the work is ongoing to cover all the 12 health facilities within Yirol East County.To minimize more time taken in the waiting areas by the beneficiaries, HELP has aligned a uniform program follow-up days in all the health facilities to be Wednesday for OTP while TSFP will be on Thursday for under-five and Friday will be PLWs. During the follow-up date, all facilities staff and volunteers will be made available and tasks will be divided accordingly to minimize prolong stay in the health facilities as beneficiaries wait to be served. Every beneficiary will be treated as an individual with individual needs without discrimination and stigmatization because of their status. All HELP staff were trained and committed to protecting the rights of beneficiaries that covers the prevention of and response to sexual exploitation and abuse policy. All staff signed not to get involved in any form of sexual exploitation/abuse. All beneficiaries will be made to understand his or her condition and some mitigating factors to the same conditions to improve the knowledge. Clients will be treated equally without fear or favour of specific groups of people HELP staff and volunteers will not get involved in any form of political participation. All beneficiaries will be directed to where they can get better service for special cases like rape with clear referral pathway. To some point, beneficiaries will be asked to give feedback on how they feel about the service they are receiving. After that, the report will be used to improve on identified areas of intervention.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Due to the critical malnutrition rate (GAM 22.6%) revealed by pre-harvest SMART survey (June 2018) conducted in Yirol East, long term underdevelopment in the state, including state infrastructure, and the on-going vulnerability to shocks such as conflict and flooding, it is likely that nutritional support will be needed in Yirol East County, for the foreseeable future. However, in spite of this, HELP is working on a long term exit strategy which also incorporates an exit strategy during the present project if it became necessary. HELP in Yirol East works closely with the County Health Departments, and the nutrition programme is integrated into the health system, with TSFP will be run within the health facilities where program food commodities will be distributed by health facility staff. As part of the broader nutrition programme, HELP also provides training on nutrition to health facility staff, so that nutrition can be further integrated into the health system. This training includes treatment and prevention of severe and moderate acute malnutrition, screening for malnutrition, IYCF and good hygiene practices. By integrating the nutrition programme within the health system, HELP can work to ensure that the project is sustainable, as capacity will be built up so that when HELP does exit, the knowledge and skills will remain. Also as part of its broader nutrition programme, HELP works in communities to reduce malnutrition, promoting IYCF and behaviour change messages around health and hygiene, so communities are able to identify malnutrition risks and take steps to prevent them. This will ensure that communities are better able to prevent malnutrition, and also ensures greater awareness of malnutrition and empowerment to advocate for nutrition services to be offered as part of the basic health services outside of HELP’s support when the project ends.
