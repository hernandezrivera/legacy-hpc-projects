Project Summary

This project aims to ensure access to education and psycho social support, and reduce protection vulnerabilities by providing emergency education opportunities for crisis affected boys and girls to return to normalcy with minimal disruption to learning.

1 - Needs

The presence of explosive remnants of war and conflict related debris poses an additional hazard for children on their way to and from school and in the school environment. According to Mine Action statistics provided by Protection Cluster, over 1,000 casualties have been recorded as a result of landmines and other ERWs since 2014, as land mines continue to be planted in the ongoing conflict. In 2018, 43% of civilian casualties were attributed to mine and ERW-related incidents. Mine related incidents remained the leading cause of child casualties in 2018.The presence of military installations near schools is a concern. In several settlements, children are in danger while attending school because the military installations are potential targets. According to Education Cluster information mentioned in Draft HNO 2020, 66% or a total of 606 education facilities reported that they have conflict related or non-conflict related safety and security concerns in the vicinity of the education facility area/site or on the children's commute to school According to the Education Cluster attacks on schools continued on a regular basis in 2018 and 2019. Since the start of 2019, the Ukraine Education Cluster has received reports regarding 32 conflict-related incidents resulting in physical damages to school infrastructure, 2 education facilities that have been temporarily closed, 8 cases of military presence in close vicinity to the education facility and 9 incidents resulting in threat of death or injuries to students, teachers and parents. In 2019 the number of incidents doubled compared to the total number of attacks throughout 2018. Another worrying trend is that dynamics of such incidents is almost equal to 2017 when 33 education facilities were damaged in the period of January - September. According to Draft HNO 2020 most schools during 2019 reported that they observe impact of the conflict on their student’s ability to learn and/or well-being. Many schools do not have a full time psychologist, and teachers themselves are also in need of psychosocial support and guidance on self-care. Since the conflict began, many schools are lacking teaching materials and learning materials. Children in the NGCA are of particular concern, as protracted conflict is putting a generation of school graduates at risk of diminishing opportunities to continue higher education outside the NGCA and to enter the job market. According to the Education Cluster estimates in 2019 the number of children and youth who received school certificates in NGCA since the beginning of the conflict and till 2019 is 120,000 (including 9 and 11 grades). Each year the conflict continues, a larger number of students complete their secondary education in the NGCA without widely recognized certificates.According the Education Cluster School Survey from September 2018, 54 per cent of education facilities reported the impact of the conflict on their student’s ability to learn or wellbeing. That is why all necessary steps should be done to advocate for signing of Safe School Declaration which will protect schools from attacks. Within 20 km of the 'contact line' this indicator is 65 per cent. This indicator has not changed from 2017 when 52 per cent of all education facilities and 74 per cent within 15 km of the 'contact line' reported the same. That is why it is very important to build teachers’ capacity on such topics as provision of PFA, Inclusive education, Conflict Sensitive Education and other similar topics.

2 - Activities & outcomes

• Schools and pre schools infrastructure rehabilitation • Capacity building for teachers on identified priority topics (Incisive education, Gender awareness, Child Safeguarding, Conflict Sensitive Education, Education in Emergencies) • Provision of psychosocial support to conflict –affected children and youth (i.e. HEART, Resiliency and recreational activities, Life Skills for Youth etc.) • Provision of learning kits to conflict –affected children • Provision of teaching and learning materials to conflict –affected schools • Advocate for the government to endorse and implement the Safe Schools Declaration • Implementation of the concept of Schools as Zones of Peace • Capacity development of local authorities (conflict‑sensitive education, referral mechanisms, reporting on attacks on education, inclusion) • As the Co‑Lead of the Education Cluster, support the coordination mechanism in both GCA and NGCA . Outcome Indicators • % of targeted girls/boys who have access to formal education • % of male/female teachers with increased knowledge and skills • % of targeted schools with active School Management Committees# of public statements/commitments of the key government officials (MoES, MoD, Verkhovna Rada and Presidential Ombudsman, relevant Parliamentary Committees) in support of the SSD • % of targeted boys/girls, school staff increase feeling of safety in school.
