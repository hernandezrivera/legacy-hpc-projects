Project Summary

According to the latest December 2020 IPC report, high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, crop pests and livestock diseases, deteriorating economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 pandemic with negative economic implications, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods (IPC December 2020; WFP Market Price Bulletins 2020). The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets more so in market dependent counties such as Wau and partly Yei (WFP Market Price Bulletins 2020).
Despite progress in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, which has been followed by the re-designation of POC sites into IDP settlements (OCHA, December 2020);there have been an increase in numbers of returnees back into their original homes especially in Yei, Wau and Jur River (IOM DTM Updates, 2020). However, these will put much further strain of host communities, who are still reeling from severe food insecurity (WFP Market Bulletins, 2020). Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020, and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season (IPC December, 2020).
Proposed activities will enable targeted 60,000 beneficiaries (10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 girls and 20000 boys), who will be supported through prepositioning of emergency provision of crop, fishing and vegetable livelihood kits and livestock support kits via FAO pipeline; including capacity building/ establishment of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites. Targeted populations will include people with special needs. These initiatives will support recovery and improvement and equitable provision of have diversified access to food, incomes and improved livelihoods in addition to strengthening community capacities to coping with threats.
Targeted beneficiaries will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches and do no harm approaches.
Activities will ensure delivering a package of integrated activities through linkages with other sectors and complementing programs in WASH, Education, Health, Protection to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, by targeting SAM/MAM children and their families; coordinate Relief and Rehabilitation Commission other NGO partners. SPOCI will further integrate the project with other Health-related priorities such as COVID 19 mitigation, WASH, acknowledging that poor WASH services are a key driver to poor health, and ultimately resulting in high malnutrition. This is in addition to regular assessments to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

According to the latest December 2020 IPC report, high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by flooding, insecurity, population displacements, declined crop production, crop pests and livestock diseases, deteriorating economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 pandemic with negative economic implications, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods (IPC December 2020; WFP Market Price Bulletins 2020). The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets more so in market dependent counties such as Wau and partly Yei (WFP Market Price Bulletins 2020). High food insecurity levels have been compounded by ongoing armed skirmishes in Yei its environs between various armed actors have led to multiple displacements of an estimated 32,000 IDPs who have been displaced from villages such as Lasu and Mugwo; in addition, there are reported new returnees arrivals from the DRC (IOM DTM, 2020). Jur River continues to grapple with livestock related fighting as Pibor flooding extremes have been compounded by cyclical waves of inter-communal violence and age set fighting among youth (OCHA Updates, 2020; Flooding Reports 2020). This is in addition to protection concerns which include destruction and looting of property including pastoralist – farmer aggravated violence in Yei and Jur River (Protection Trends, 2020; NGO Forum Updates, 2020; REACH Situation Overview Reports, 2020). This has been exacerbated by additional bureaucratic impediments, operational interference and access constraints and increased cases of GBV along major roads; including mines presence (MAG Updates, 2020; NGO Forum and REACH Updates 2020). Despite limited progress in the implementation of the revitalized peace agreement, which has been followed by the re-designation of POC sites into IDP settlements (OCHA, December 2020);there have been an increase in numbers of returnees back into their original homes especially in Yei, Wau and Jur River (IOM DTM Updates, 2020). However, these will put much further strain of host communities, who are still reeling from severe food insecurity (WFP Market Bulletins, 2020). Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020, and the much lower than required humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, will likely result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season (IPC December, 2020). SPOCI is currently implementing an SSHF/ FAO funded projects in Western Bahr el Ghazal , Pibor and recent SSHF actions in Yei. SPOCI proposes to scale up emergency food security and livelihoods via emergency provision of crop, fishing and vegetable livelihood kits and livestock support including capacity building/ establishment of kitchen gardens at nutrition sites. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in proposed counties and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Working Group and M & E Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs. Activities will ensure integrated programming with WASH, Education, Protection etc to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, coordinate with RRC among other partners. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration AAP, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPOCI shall refrain from, and shall take all reasonable and appropriate measures to prohibit its employees or other persons engaged and controlled by it from exchanging any money, goods, services, or other things of value, for sexual favors or activities, or from engaging in any sexual activities that are exploitive or degrading to any beneficiary. All SPOCI staff have received training on PSEA and have signed organization’s PSEA policy including newly recruited staff. SPOCI does not tolerate any form of discrimination against ethnic groups, clans, minorities or communities. SPOCI shall ensure that the selection of beneficiaries is transparent and conforms to established selection criteria and is applied objectively and without prejudice.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Implementation committees shall include other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, women, girls and men including illiterate ones who shall be guided and supported to ensure tgeir views and concerns are taken into consideration.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Key community representatives, leadership structures and RRC will be engaged and consulted including various assessments conducted which key recommendations highlighted included but not limited to provision of inputs (crop, vegetable and fishing kits) and livestock support; in addition to having complaints and feedback desks at Boma levels for collection of views including complaints on implementation; which shall be taken into consideration.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review how complaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SPOCI has policies, Accountability to Affected Populations Feedback and complaints in addition to a code of conduct relating to PSEA and will ensure appropriate awareness measures among beneficiaries in regard to their rights on feeding back on the project and any complaints raised including on PSEA which shall be communicated through Boma level feedback and complaints desks, IEC materials that shall include hot lines and email addresses for reporting any cases of PSEA. In addition, key AAP and PSEA focal points will be identified within the community to support in feed backing on AAP issues while confidential reporting of PSEA cases. SPOCI will in addition through its referall pathways, support referral of cases through relevant agencies which shall include protection partners, health partners among others. FSL actions especially areas such distributions and trainings will be mapped in collaboration with protection actors after conducting safety audits including linkages with other agencies to ensure safe and accessible to all target beneficiaries – women, girls, men and boys; which shall be dis-aggregated to promote safety; in addition to people living with disabilities. SPOCI will ensure communities are consulted, sensitized and involved in beneficiary targeting and selection whose criteria will include both displaced and host communities, people with special needs etc with selection agreed upon to avoid/ mitigate potential conflicts in the community. Implementation will be conducted in nearby locations and will be selected in close consultation with women and local authorities including ensuring appropriate safety audits are conducted to ensure safety of beneficiaries particularly women and girls to mitigate potential protection concerns. Each distribution point/ location will have feedback and complaints desks in addition to follow ups in place for raised concerns. SPOCI will in addition conduct peace messaging, messaging on UXOs/ mines etc and coordination with government agencies including other focal points such as the NGO Forum, UNDSS among others.
