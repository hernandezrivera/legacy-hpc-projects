Project Summary

The project seek to work in collaboration with locally-led organisations to champion humanitarian action to provide child protection and enhance community resilience. Additionally, it will empower local actors to take ownership of child protection initiative and ensure their sustainability in the long run. The project is therefore designed to facilitate the growth of local organisations operating in the fragile regions of cameroon (NWSW, Far North and the East) by providing them with training/capacity building opportunities, mentorship, coaching and guidance on increasing their chances to access funding to respond to the needs of vulnerable children.
By empowering local organisations and leveraging their knowledge, resources and networks, addressing the specific needs of children becomes very possible. Through this approach, Street Child of Cameroon shall provide child protection services to 25,000 children and 5,000 caregivers through her partners. The services will involve the establishment/strengthening of 30 Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms/Networks, provide child protection case management services to 1,000 children, adolescent life skills to 5,000 children, 15 safe spaces will be established to provide MHPSS to 1,000 children, livelihood support to 500 adolescent mothers, family business will strengthen 1,500 families, 2,000 caregivers will be provided with positive parenting training (including foster parents) and 500 teachers will be trained on MHPSS and mainstreaming child protection in schools. Above all, 500 local actors will be trained child protection mainstreaming.
The outcome of these interventions will ideally lead to the wellbeing and safety of children in these areas. This could be achieved through the implementation of effective strategies and interventions that address the specific needs and perspectives of the local communities.
Specific outcomes could include a reduction in child abuse and exploitation, increased access to education and healthcare for children, strengthened child protection systems, and the empoweerment of local actors to take ownership of child protection intitatives. Ultimately, sustainable solutions will be prioritised too ensure the well-being and safety of children in conflict affected zones, both during and after the project’s implementation
This approach will further encourage community ownership and sustainable solutions, as local actors will be actively involved in decision-making processes and implementation; hence enhance overall capacity of local organisations to cope with emergencies and promote long-term recovery and development.
