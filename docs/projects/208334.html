Project Summary

The year 2023 was marked by numerous protection incidents affecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. In addition, significant flooding has exacerbated pre-existing child protection problems such as GBV. Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existing difficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of opportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64% of the current displaced population are children.
This project will support formal and community-based CP systems, placing particular emphasis on (i) capacity building of adolescent girls and boys as agents of change for conflict and violence prevention, peacebuilding, and the promotion of intergenerational and interreligious dialogue, (ii) capacity building of deconcentrated CP structures in a humanitarian/development Nexus approach focusing on case management, referral, psychosocial support, and family/community reintegration of vulnerable children girls and boys and (iii) increased coordination at all levels, under the leadership of the Regional Delegation of Social Affairs (RDSA).
The key result of the project which is in line with the objectives of the protection sector, is that conflict-affected children and caregivers are protected from violence, abuse and exploitation in armed conflict settings and live in a protective environment in the targeted localities of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Kani and Mayo Danay Divisions in the Far-North Region of Cameroon.
The project builds on previously established dynamics and community structures and will provide PSS to children and caregivers. PSS will be provided both through a one-on-one approach (through social workers or other trained child protection actors) and through community-based approaches, such as CFSs, adolescent clubs, and intergenerational dialogue.
Implementing partners will continue to identify unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). Based on previous trends and on partners capacities, the project plans to assist UASC, ensuring and providing those children with psychosocial support, alternative care arrangements, reunification with their families of origin and reinsertion in their communities. All will benefit from close individual follow-up.
To address both social norms but also to ensure smooth community reinsertion, the project will work at strengthening community mobilization and will organize community activities around prevention of GBViE including activities to abandon child marriage, to promote intergenerational dialogue and social cohesion. Based on the projections, a total of 70,000 persons (women, men, girls, boys) are planned to be reached through community mobilization activities. Partnership with religious and traditional leaders, including female stakeholders will be an important channel to display messages and address complex issues. In complement, the work on youth empowerment and participation will enable to merge with the other members of the communities and promote prevention and protection of children from violence and exploitation.
The issue of conflict-affected children without a birth certificate was confirmed to still be a major issue. The project aims at supporting conflict-affected children in obtaining a birth certificate. 60 former CAAFAG are expected to be identified, will receive PSS, and will be supported UNICEF will continue to closely work with civil society implementing partners, providing them with capacity building, supporting the setting up of SOPs for case management and referral. UNICEF will also strengthen the coordination mechanism led by RDSA.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project aims at protecting children against violence, abuse and exploitation in emergencies. Conflict and floods have negative impact on the well-being of children and their caregivers. In the Far North, the main threats against children and their caregivers are family separation caused by forced population movements and the abduction of children often related to the forced recruitment by NSAGs, with heightened risk of sexual exploitation for girls. Most of the children and adolescents formerly associated with NSAGs struggle to reintegrate into the community, which causes further physical and psychological harm. Girls are exposed to various forms of sexual violence, child marriage, and early pregnancy, often as negative coping mechanisms and because of traditional social and cultural practices. Incidents of GBV, physical violence, negligence, and abuse also define the child protection risks that humanitarian actors will respond to. Child protection activities will include case management, the strengthening of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs), provision of psychosocial support to children, adolescents, and caregivers to prevent violence, negligence, and abuse against children in all three divisions affected by the conflict and floods. Child Protection partners will assist children and caregivers with mental health and psychosocial support, including through the identification, referral, and individual case management vulnerable children. A dedicated and specialised support will be provided to children formerly associated with armed groups to facilitate their reintegration to the community. A comprehensive Case Management will be provided to vulnerable children and support to families to better protect their children as well as the capacity building of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms. Activities will also include support to issue birth certificates to conflict-affected children, interventions for the prevention and mitigation of risks and response to GBV affecting children and providing Humanitarian beneficiaries with PSEA measure.
