Project Summary

This project will support 500 farmer households (3000 individuals: 644 men, 677 women, 824 boys, 855 girls) with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting. This support will improve bread production (the staple food for Syrian people) which has been negatively affected by the lack of wheat and high prices of production supplies. The project will strengthen local production capacity and local markets, therefore, the self-reliance of the community to be stable against external conflicts.
This project will also support 400 vulnerable households including female-headed and child-headed households living in peri-urban and rural areas by distributing vaccinated chicken and chicken feed to 200 families (1200 individuals: 257 men, 270 women, 330 boys, 343 girls) and distribution of sheep and fodder to 200 families (1200 individuals: 257 men, 270 women, 330 boys, 343 girls), in addition to supporting them with the required knowledge and basic advice to achieve the best possible production of eggs, milk, and meat.
This will strengthen and re-establish productive small backyard chicken and sheep flocks to support local food production and to improve household nutrition, especially for children and pregnant women, and to open up additional sources of income for rural and peri-urban women through small-scale marketing of eggs, milk, and meat
The project will also support 100 women-headed families (600 individuals: 129men, 135 women, 165boys, 171girls) by vocational training, Tools and equipment, and raw materials to prepare dairy products, in order to start their own projects and secure incomes to cover their families’ needs.
the project will also include Rehabilitation of key irrigation infrastructure for agricultural plains of Azmarin village, including the main intake pumping station (3 main pumps, control panels, and main station building), main water pipes (60cm diameter, 660-meter length) which shifting water to the main water collection basin (100 m3), open agriculture channel system (2500 meter) and water closed channels which distribute water from open channel to the nearest farms (10,000 m) along with their valve and gates.

In 2023 we are planning to support 1000 farmers (6000 individuals: 1288men, 1354 women, 1648 boys, 1710 girls) with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting, in addition to supporting the marketing phase by buying 50% of their yields, which will be milled, baked, and distributed to vulnerable families.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2022, Syria continues to suffer from multiple and complex socioeconomic difficulties, which are exacerbating food security and livelihood needs. The overall estimated sector PiN for 2022 - of food insecurity plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021. HNO 2022 also addressed that according to preliminary findings from FAO, wheat production for 2020-2021 season is estimated at 1.045 million tons, down from 2.8 million in 2020, and only a quarter of pre-crisis level of 4.1 million tones 2002-2011. Furthermore, there is an anticipated shortage of wheat seed for the upcoming 2021/2022 season and the critical needs in seed should be addressed to ensure that food production is sustained, and the humanitarian caseload is eased. HNO 2022 also mentioned that People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, only 10 percent of households have an income above the cost of Syria’s Minimum Expenditure Basket. HNO 2022 showed that Livestock sector continues to suffer from high feed/fodder prices and lack of access to pasture and water resources due to drought-like conditions. Veterinary services in Syria remain weak, combined with high costs for veterinary medicines, resulting in extensive destocking.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

**The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm to some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: M. - Impact: H. -Mitigating: -We will not distribute at a late time of the day. -We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute them in each village separately. -During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. -Ensure that complaint mechanisms for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email, etc.) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties. **The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. -Likelihood: w. - Impact: H.-Mitigating: 1- We will choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. 2- 4- Ensure that the complaint mechanisms for reporting SEA/GBV are accessible (WhatsApp, email,..) and that focal points for receiving complaints understand how to discharge their duties.3-In case any exploitation happens, BINAA will take appropriate action to protect persons from retaliation where allegations of SEA/GBV are reported involving our personnel (suspending staff till the investigation is conducted, referring the victim to PSS, and even witness protection program if needed) and investigate allegations in a timely and professional manner.
