Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to the WASH sector 2025 objective and plan to address immediate needs for vulnerable, crisis-affected populations through improving access to safe water, sanitation facilities and improving hygiene. This will provide the affected population with safe and sufficient WASH service contribute significantly to preventing waterborne diseases and reducing risks of outbreaks such as cholera and AWD, directly saving lives, alleviating suffering and preventing mortality. Gender-sensitive interventions directly address the unique needs and vulnerabilities of different genders. Similarly, facilities adapted for those with disabilities ensure inclusivity and accessibility. The project will respond to WASH needs in IDP camps and communities as well as locations or settlement affected by natural disaster or outbreak. It will ensure free and equitable access to safe drinking water and latrine in secure places, enhance their knowledge on improved nutrition security

Activities under this proposed intervention includes the construction and/or rehabilitation of water points, the construction and/or rehabilitation of gender-appropriate sanitation facilities and the promotion of proper hygiene practices through WASH’EM, Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) approach to eradicate open defecation, Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) and emergency hygiene promotion sessions.
The project will target all group but will prioritize vulnerable and marginalize group especially women, girls, boys, children and people with special needs. Women and girls will be consulted during all stages of activity design and implementation and will be integral agents of change for improving safe hygiene practices.
Through coordinating with other actors and stakeholders in Borno and Yobe state, other gaps and needs will identified and cover. This intervention is targeting the most vulnerable, conflict-affected individuals in communities of Borno and Yobe state.

1 - Needs

The convergence of armed conflict, displacement, flooding, food insecurity and cholera outbreaks in north-east Nigeria has significantly amplified the WASH-related humanitarian needs. The armed conflict has led to massive displacement, with many IDPs living in makeshift camps that often lack adequate WASH facilities. Overcrowding in these camps strain existing water sources and sanitation infrastructures. The situation has been further exacerbated by seasonal and yearly floods heightening the risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera. The HNO 2024 indicate almost 5 million people have been identified in need of humanitarian WASH assistance across the BAY States with a significant deterioration of the severity compared to last year, from 27 to 34 LGAs in critical situation (phase 4). Women and girls accounting for 52% of the population in need often are tasked with fetching water far away, potentially exposing them to protection issues. Children accounting for 60% of population in need are highly impacted making them susceptible to WASH related diseases and malnutrition. Persons with disability require adaptive WASH infrastructures-water supply and sanitation, through which their dignity and hygiene are upheld. Access to safe and sufficient quantity of water remain limited in nearly all of the BAY states which affecting the population groups in the IDP/informal settlements, returnees and host communities. With 17% of assessed households reported having less than SPHERE standard of 15l/p/d with 5% of assessed households having to walk for more than 1 hour round trip to access water. These limitations with access to water within SPHERE standards especially impact women and girls who primarily are tasked with the responsibility of fetching water for the family exposing them to protection concerns especially in locations where water collection point is far away from the community. Sanitation across the IDP camps remain a lingering challenge and major need arising from difficulty in FSM reducing usability and access to latrine with 71% of assessed camps having latrine to person ratio above 1:50 beyond SPHERE not reaching standards. Of the assessed households, there was an overall 69% of the population with access to soap for personal hygiene while 31% of the population reported lack of access to soap for personal hygiene. Cholera remains critical need and challenge within WASH across the three BAY states with historically high-recorded cases in Borno and Adamawa.

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved protected access to safe water supply for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno states. In close coordination with the Government, RRT, RRM working group and the WASH sector-working group, the response will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) to the vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality monitoring testing including free residual chlorine will be tested for all water supply to ensure the quality of the water. The IDP camps will be supported with operation and maintenance of water points to ensure continues safe water supply. In communities, WASHCOM will be formed and trained with 40-50% of female in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation and LGA level revolving fund mechanism will be establish to improve the sustainability of camps in Yobe and Borno state. Activity 02: Multi-unit communal gender separated latrines will be constructed and rehabilitated in IDP camps in safe places with light to ensure the safe access. Male and female Hygiene and sanitation volunteers will be trained and supported to monitor the cleaning and maintenance of latrines. Solid waste management will be conducted through distribution of waste bins, trainings and coordinating with BOSEPA for waste collection. In communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on household latrines. Following a transect walk focused on the negative aspects of open defecation, the Community Lead Total Sanitation approach will be launched. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their own resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that communal latrines in both camps and IDP host communities are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved safe access to hygiene practices within affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Hygiene promotion activities will be delivered with different approaches in emergency phases and resilience phases. In emergency, rapid hygiene session conducted by hygiene promotes and volunteers through group session and HH sessions. WASH EM approach will be implemented to improve the hand washing practices. In resilience building phase the beneficiaries will be reached through mix approaches of PHAST, CHAST, CLTS and sanitation marketing to ensure sustainability of the practices. BABY WASH approaches will be implemented to improve the WASH practices and prevent children from WASH related disease. In addition to that the use of IECs and other communications materials will be adopted in sensitizing the beneficiaries through house-to-house visits, FGDs, campaigns to be conducted by a trained and equipped gender balance community hygiene volunteers and CHIPs. A gender sensitive hygiene messages will be conducted.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The safety of beneficiaries will be prioritize in the delivery of WASH infrastructures such as water points, latrines and showers construction. ACF will involve all vulnerable group from the design to final project implementation. The design of all infrastructure will be gender sensitive; all latrines will be segregate, constructed in safe access location with ramp and handrail including flood light and gender mark. While water points will be site with consent of women, girls, boys and people with special needs with ramp at collection point. Following an environmental analysis, ACF will priorities the construction of latrines that are proximate to households and with adequate lighting. As women and girls are partially or totally responsible for collecting water, water points will be located in places convenient for women and girls. ACF will work to increase women’s voice and leadership by working with the state and community structures. We would ensure women are active participants in project decision-making and monitoring by conducting household surveys. In addition, ACF will ensure all partners working for the project understand and undergo a GBV and safeguarding training.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF has a well-established accountability system with a feedback and complaint response mechanism within the MEAL Department, which aims at improving the quality of interventions and promoting Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) commitment on AAP, and ACF’s goal of institutionalizing the Mission's Accountability Framework (Including Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) in all functions and operations alongside system level cohesion, coordination and learning. To ensure accountability to affected population in this project, ACF will ensure that participation, feedback and complaints mechanisms are integrated in all aspects of this project to provide a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication to the affected populations. These channels will facilitate reception of feedback and complaints and to inform affected populations about procedures and processes that affect them so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. ACF will capitalize on the result of Humanitarian Accountability Assessment (HAA) to determine the existing systems and structures of complaints referral as well as to determine the communities’ preference in terms of channels to put in place to enable them provide feedback/complaints relating to the intervention, while maintaining do-no-harm principle. Through the established channels, ACF will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern (both positive and negative) are analyzed to inform protection, assistance and solutions to the programming, and corrective action is taken as appropriate. Furthermore, ACF will ensure constant sensitization of the affected population on the established channels and how to use them for earing their views and opinion regarding the intervention.
