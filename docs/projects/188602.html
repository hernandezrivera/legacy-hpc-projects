Project Summary

The WoS Food Security Cluster/Sector of Syria links the hub-level clusters/working groups (NES, NWS and Damascus) offering technical and operation support and provides analysis of needs, response and gaps at a WoS level. Mercy Corps currently hosts the FSS NGO co-Coordinator. In 2022, the sector will build on its work of previous years and will focus on reviewing and updating needs periodically, response and gap analysis to operational planning and developing contingency/preparedness plans. The sector will continue to harmonize all aspects of the response such as assessments, analysis, geographical targeting, selection criteria (including targeting youth for livelihoods) and monitoring. The sector will lead cross-learning initiatives on outcome indicators monitoring, response modalities and cross cutting issues. The sector will also deliver on the key outputs for the Humanitarian Programme Cycle as well as feed into strategic discussions and decisions. Inter-sector work will focus on a variety of areas including (1) linkages and collaboration with WASH, NFI, Nutrition, Early Recovery and Protection sectors (2) deeper focus on protection analysis with the inclusion of the protection matrix at sector and projects level and ongoing collaboration with the Protection sector (3) an integration strategy aimed at linking people on food assistance with livelihoods support from other sectors such as the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector (4) joint needs analysis and advocacy, common messaging to communities and assessing convergence at household level with nutrition and other closely linked sectors (5) improved linkages and coordination with ongoing cash-based transfer activities and to help guide new activities as well as learn from ongoing and past activities.

4 - Needs

Syria 2022 PIN figures estimated food insecure + those at risk of food insecurity increased from 14.2 to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity, 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity, 8 million living in camps deemed to be 100% food insecure, an increase of 470,000 people since 2021. This is alarming given FAO projects wheat yield drop 50% since last year, which is 25% of pre-crisis levels. General analysis on food consumption scores (FCS), reduced coping strategy (rCSI) and diet diversity, show food security has consistently worsened over the past two years. People consuming an inadequate diet increased from 21% in 2019 to around 44% by the summer 2021. rCSI levels is an indicator monitoring resilience to face-off future shocks, where a higher score relates to lower levels of capacity of coping. Consumed diets (diet diversity) highlights a clear reduction of consumption of proteins, fruits, dairy and pulses while sugars, fats and vegetables remains largely unchanged - a significant worsening of the diversity of the diet, now largely composed of cheaper and non-nutrient rich foods. The WFP national average standard reference basket a household in Syria can afford with their monthly minimum wage shows erosion of purchasing power over the past couple of years, which steadily increased as the COVID-19 impact coupled with the intensified depreciation of SYP.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a cornerstone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff are trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. All coping mechanisms, with the possible exception of sending children to work or to beg in opposition-controlled and Self-Administration areas, increased at a steady rate since October 2019. In particular families subject their daughters to early or forced marriages in conflict situations as a means to relieve financial burden, and because a husband serves as additional protection from pervasive sexual violence. However, girls married at younger ages are at high risk of complicated pregnancies and deliveries, the children have worse health outcomes, are at higher risk of intimate partner violence, and on average receive less formal education than girls married as a consenting adult.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates contingency planning with the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT), which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps’ operational planning strives to minimize risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2022 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence has been reducing, creating potential destabilizing event in the delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return home, leading to questions of operational viability across the North in 2022. Mercy Corps contingency plans with donors and partners for adaptable programming. Covid-19 infection rates are rising across Syria and with hospitals quickly reaching capacity, GoS and other authorities’ mismanagement of the pandemic may lead to the situation further spiraling out of control in all areas. If local authorities fail to enact policies that prioritize public health and enforces precautionary measures, then local healthcare systems in governorates, who notably have very limit capacity, will face a grim scenario.
