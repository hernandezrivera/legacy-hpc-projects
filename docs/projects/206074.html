Project Summary

The proposed response plan seeks to contribute to food security and livelihoods of 58,307
communities in need (IPC3+) in Akobo, Uror, (Jonglei state), and Panyijiar county Unity state
through the implementation of CO2#: Increased food production through in-kind support
of farming inputs, fishing kits, livestock restock treatment and vaccination, CO3#:
Capacity Building on Good Agronomic Practices for both trainers and farmers, providing
context-specific value chain inputs and training to increase food production, market
interventions, fishery and farming value chains through an investment of post-harvest
handling equipment, climate resistance agriculture, disaster risk reduction, amongst
others.
This plan of 2024 to reach a total of 13,000 households (individuals; 24% women, 16%
men, 36% girls and 24% boys) consisting of host communities, returnees, refugees and
IDPs (Beneficiary type will be determined through a baseline). In 2024, INTERCEDE plans to
protect the livelihoods of vulnerable rural populations and improve food security through
the provision of livelihood support during the main planting and dry season (crop and
vegetable seeds, tools, swing kits), support food security information, analysis and
coordination and strengthen absorptive capacity and build resilience at household and
community level.
This HRP project will complement INTERCEDE’s ongoing resilient programming to provide
immediate quick impact activities while protecting the gains of resilience programming.
Protection and Gender will be mainstreamed throughout the project's lifetime. The Do No
Harm approach as enshrined in INTERCEDE safe programming approach will be
mainstreamed by ensuring participation and accountability to the affected population.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces a dire humanitarian crisis, where acute food insecurity andmalnutrition levels have risen to alarming rates, particularly in the states of Jonglei andUnity. Despite entering what is generally considered a favourable post-harvest period,projections from December 2022 to March 2023 paint a bleak picture. An overwhelming7.8 million people, representing 64% of the nation’s population, are projected to sufferfrom Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity (OCHA 2023). Inparticular regions, including Akobo, Ayod and Pibor counties of Jonglei and Panyijiar countyof Unity State, individuals will likely encounter Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) conditions if thecurrent trends persist. The ongoing conflict, particularly in Northern Jonglei, threatens tointensify livelihood disruptions, impede trade, undermine market functionality, and hinderthe delivery of humanitarian aid. This bleak scenario is compounded by substantial cerealdeficits, inflated prices of staple foods due to a shortage in market supply, deterioratingroad conditions that impede market access, and a continuing macroeconomic downturn.Among all states, Unity State is expected to endure the harshest impact, with anestimated 77.2% of its population projected to struggle severely with food insecurity,closely followed by Jonglei at 74.2%. Approximately 1.4 million children under five areexpected to face acute malnutrition by June 2023, with a staggering number at risk ofsevere acute malnutrition. Jonglei State, in particular, reports a Global Acute Malnutrition(GAM) burden of 22%, with Unity State not far behind at 14% (ICP Report 2022) Accordingto the IRNA Report 2022, Uror County has experienced years of intensified inter-communal conflict, cattle raiding, and general insecurity, which have formed the perfectstorm for recurrent displacement, further made worse by a combination of heavy rainsand massive floods. These main drivers have increased vulnerabilities and reducedaccess to essential goods and services in Uror County, diminishing its most vulnerablepeople’s resilience over the years. Women and girls are increasingly at risk of gender based violence, including sexual violence, while searching for food or an income tosupport themselves and their families. Intra-domestic tensions are also increasing,heightening the risk of intimate partner violence. Adolescent girls are facing increasedrisks of child marriage. Their exposure to these risks increases when communities facegrowing humanitarian needs driven by conflict, violence, climate shocks or foodinsecurity. Akobo town was heavily affected by influx of the returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia camps due to insecurity and insufficiency. As such, There is shortage of food stock in the market as the number of the population in the county has increased there is no food stock observed and seen. The market is empty, with very few groceries, no fishing gear, no goats, chickens and vegetable gardens at the riverside (IRNA Report on Panyijiar January 2023). Panyijiar County has been hit by flood that have caused displacement to theaffected population and made them more vulnerable. A significant number of peoplewere displaced and properties were looted and destroyed. All livelihoods were looted, andsome of them were killed. Shelters were damaged and burned including markets.Increased post-harvest losses along the agricultural value chains (planning, production,harvesting, storage, processing, and marketing) have been significant recently, estimatedat 40-50 per cent (World Bank, 2020). Strengthening skills and investment in post-harvestinfrastructure (processing equipment, adequate and efficient storage systems) andsupporting farmer organizations and cooperative movement would go a long incountering post-harvest losses.

2. Project activities

Activities are planned under CO2 and CO3. CO2: Enhance food production will be attainedthrough. i) in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable, and fishing kits to 11,661 IPC3+households (Akobo, Uror and Panyijiar), a) Mobilization ofcommunities, b) PIC formation and training, c) Identification and registration ofbeneficiaries d) Distribution of inputs, f) Post distribution monitoring, reporting. INTERCEDEwill enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetableand crop livelihood input and fishing kits. Fishing kits will be provided to locations withaccessibility to fishing grounds to enable them to participate in fishing to meet their foodneeds and income supplement during crisis times. Rapid awareness on the proper use offishing kits will be conducted to ensure that fishing is conducted in a way that does notdeplete fishing capacity, reduce exposure to water pollution by responsible fishing anddisposal and improve fish handling methods and hygiene to improve the nutrition ofhouseholds; 2) provide crop and vegetable kits and tools. Vegetables, crop seeds andassorted tools will be provided as standard kits. 5 types of preferred vegetable seeds willbe provided to the beneficiaries in a total of 8 Kg per household. A beneficiary preferenceof seeds will be conducted, and ecological and culturally adapted types of seeds will beprioritized. The distribution of seeds will be conducted both in Integrated Rapid ResponseMechanism and static operations in locations with elevated IPC phase 4 and 5 and inwhich INTERCEDE has established presence. Simple awareness creations on farmingpractices and methods as part of capacity building will be conducted on the spot duringthe distribution to ensure the recipient households know the production practices andmethods; 3) Distribution of fishing kits and assorted vegetable seeds as standard kits.INTERCEDE will distribute fishing and vegetable kits to the most vulnerable households tomeet their livelihood assistance during the dry season. This will reduce dependency onfood and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorbshock by 1) capacity building to beneficiaries receiving livelihood support such as GAPsand improved fishing and preservation. The project provision of seeds and tools such asfishing, vegetable and crop seeds will be complemented with capacity building to improveskills and knowledge towards improving production and productivity. CO3: Resiliencebuilding and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids. i) Strengtheninglivestock, fishery, and farming value chain through a) Infrastructural support on fishpreservation infrastructures (fish drying racks, salting facilities, cold storages, andsmoking facilities to 1000 fisher folks a) Farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, exchangevisits and farmers’ shows b) Establishment of the demonstration plots c) Vocationaltraining across the 5 targeted project locations Community mobilization and sensitizationd) beneficiary identification and registration, training iii) Market development Training onSILC community groups. Climate action, including assessment of climate risk, Mappingof climate-related risks and dissemination of anticipated risks/disasters, construction orrenovation of flood control dykes.
