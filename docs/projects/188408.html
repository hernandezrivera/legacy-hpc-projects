Project Summary

This intervention takes a participatory, community-based approach to recovery programming, ensuring market-sensitive livelihoods is provided to vulnerable Syrian households. Increased income, assets and consumption hold the key to promoting an increase in individual economic well-being. Social well-being stems from human capital and positive social relations. The vocational training and on-the-job training or apprenticeships are provided to persons with disabilities, youth and women, who will be offered a selection of enterprise and employment pathways. For those seeking to set up their own small/micro enterprise, a further package of business skills training and business grant will be provided based on their business proposition. Moreover, vocational training graduates are supported to seek employment with small-medium businesses. All skills trainings are identified based on a rigorous assessment of labor market to ensure addressing local sectors of economic growth, gaps in skills, barriers facing persons with disabilities accessing livelihood opportunities and women’s role in the market.
Small and micro-business owners whose business has been negatively affected by conflict are supported to relaunch their businesses through a combination of skills, business grants and coaching. Business trainings are provided, particularly for those new to labor market such as youth, women & persons with disabilities. Moreover, Cash for Work schemes are identified to provide benefit to the entire community. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities is ensured through community participation in selection of types of businesses needed in community, beneficiary selection and monitoring project progress. All activities are mainstreaming COVID-19 Key messaging in activities package according to WHO Guidelines.
Inclusion of persons with disabilities mainly women with disabilities will be ensured through using twin track approach, the logic of intervention will combine a package of activities aimed at empowering persons with disabilities and building more inclusive and accessible working environment at community level. Activities will be based on assessment of barriers faced by persons with disabilities. While the organization will conduct several actions to overcome barriers such as awareness raising on persons with disabilities rights, tailored case management support, and intensive coaching support. Participation of women will be supported through awareness and advocacy with their families, provision of female trainers and skills training for home-based businesses.
Complementary services such as Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided based on community needs & vulnerability assessments. The organization provides technical support to other livelihoods agencies to ensure the design and implementation take an inclusive approach and that they provide persons with disabilities with equitable access. This support also includes guidance on the meaningful engagement of older persons and women in activities.

4 - Needs

The situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated, with an estimated around 68% of population are in need for food and agriculture assistance. The spread of COVID-19 and application of the Caesar act sanctions, brought an unprecedented increase of market prices. Prices increased by 236% coupled with a reduction of HHs’ incomes while 50% of the household income is spent on food (HNO 2021). A high rate of unemployment (50%) further hampers income generation especially among vulnerable groups and persons with disabilities. The deterioration of economic conditions is the main driver of displacement, according to HNAP data, 71% of people surveyed declared that their monthly incomes insufficient to cover basic needs. This affects their resilience capacity at 3 levels: i) creating new protection concerns (4% of HH are recurring to child labor and 3% are selling properties),b) reducing their productivity capacity (28% of HH are selling assets and goods and 9% their means of transport); c) reducing their resilience and ability to deal with future shocks (71% of HH are borrowing money and 41% are spending their savings). From a Disability, Gender and Age (DGA) perspective, key concerns from surveyed persons. were i) community attitude and cultural behaviors limiting persons with disabilities and women from involvement in Livelihoods activities; ii) accessibility barriers related to inaccessible transport, and infrastructures; iii) attitudinal, information, and communication barriers.

5 - Implementation of activities

Cash-for-Work participants are the mostly vulnerable HHs including at least 25% of women and 15% of people with disabilities will be ensured. After consultation with the community member, the CFW project type will be selected. Labour Market Assessments (LMAs) will be conducted in the selected area to discover the local-level economic growth sectors, new economic opportunities, and skills in greatest demand. This data will feed into a response options analysis for livelihoods program design in line with the needs of the local labor market. LMAs will also identify specific barriers faced by women, men, young and older people and persons with disabilities in accessing employment and make recommendations for addressing them. Provision of business skills training, grants and coaching for small and micro business recovery for reestablishing businesses will be offered to eligible applicants. Provision of vocational skills, life skills and business skills training will be offered to eligible applicants. Vocational skills trainings will be based on the LMA findings of sectors of economic growth and skills in demand in the local market. Vocational skills training (VT) will be certified where feasible, and include an apprenticeship or on the job training and establishing links to local employment opportunities. Young people and women will be prioritized for VT and apprenticeships. Beneficiaries graduated from the VT and/or apprenticeship will receive a startup kit.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Protection of partner staff: the INGO ensures having a low visibility profile and adhering to a confidentiality note related to partner contact details.• Applying PSEA policy for staff, partners and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. Also, raise awareness on the organization’s policy on PSEA and complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. • Community tensions arising from beneficiary selection: The INGO will share and discuss vulnerability criteria and weighting with community through participatory processes. The INGO outlines clear processes and guidelines for beneficiary selection to partners’ staff.• Participation of Women in livelihood activities within vulnerable household causing intra-family tensions. The INGO will closely monitor family dynamics and conduct awareness sessions for families.• Exclusion of persons with disabilities: Inclusion workers will be embedded within projects to provide specialized support to fully participate in livelihood opportunities, in parallel with community and HH awareness-raising of disability and women’s rights. Providing guidance and support for service providers on skills to interact with persons with disabilities.• Location of livelihood services can have threats of violence. Conduct participatory security assessment involving community members, including representatives of DGA groups and Ensure security risk assessment as part of programming.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Loss of access: LLH programming will be prioritized in relatively stable areas, in communities with severity raking 3 or below. Also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects its operations.• Lack of acceptance, resulting of security incident: The INGO and its partner adopt community-based approach and are well-perceived on ground and ensure to keep close coordination with local authorities and positive relations with community. • Intended beneficiaries are psycho-socially not ready to participate in productive activities. People in need of psychosocial support will be supported through the following: Basic Psychological First Aid and the group PSS are integrated into LLH activities.• The eventual spread-out of COVD-19 and its implications on the operational capacities. To mitigate this risk, the organization and partners will ensure the following: i) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available; ii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging in the overall package of activities; iii) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster.• Loss of funding and budget reduction due to exchange rate loss is managed by the INGO continuous negotiation with the donor and the use of USD value for money transfer and payment. Regular market assessment to adapt LLH activities accordingly, accountability towards beneficiaries and stakeholders and donors.
