Project Summary

WFP has been assisting the displaced people from merged districts for over eight years now. However, 2016 and 2017 observed a considerable number of displaced people returning to their areas of origin after the launch of the KP merged districts Sustainable Return and Rehabilitation Strategy (SRRS) in April 2015. In response to the phase wise plan around 2.6 million (266,261 individuals have returned to their areas of origin in KP merged districts successfully, with around 699,978 registered individuals (15,833 families) still displaced. An estimated 255,906 individual returnees are members of 42,651 female headed households. As the government plans to continue with the return of the remaining IDPs in 2019, WFP’s response will need to be aligned with government’s merged districts SRRS for returnees in KP merged districts. WFP will also continue with its relief food assistance to IDPs and returnees which include women (23%), men (23%), Boys under 18 (30%) and Girls under 18 (24%) and the extremely vulnerable.
WFP’s assistance to families returning to their areas of origin will ensue that basic needs continue to be met despite adverse availability and access conditions at home, thereby allowing recovery efforts to proceed, unhindered by immediate food security concerns. The recovery assistance will be initiated upon completion of the six months return food package. The recovery activities include rehabilitation of their livelihood assets through food for assets and trainings. A significant proportion of women will benefit from training on the latest food security and livelihood best practices in their local context. The assistance in recovery phase will be conditional upon work or training.
Activities or outputs
Relief food assistance is provided on a monthly basis with the general family food basket consisting of cereals fortified wheat flour or rice (80Kg), pulses (8Kg), salt (0.2 Kg), and vegetable oil (4.5Kg).

Special consideration is given to the provision of food assistance to the most vulnerable groups in the communities, including the elderly, sick, disabled, children, pregnant and lactating mothers, and female and child headed households. WFP ensures that general food distribution as well as all programmatic activities are conducted in a safe and dignified manner and in adherence to the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy (2012), including basic humanitarian and do no harm principles. Separate distribution counters and waiting areas are established at distribution points where female staff are deployed. Assessment teams aim to include women where possible and beneficiary data is disaggregated by gender.
Cash and food transfers will be the primary modalities of assistance. Cash transfers will be used in post shock recovery to help short term food insecurity and encourage market functionality. Food assistance for assets will utilize cash as a modality of support and areas will be targeted based on food security needs, according to local contexts and market conditions. WFP’s VAM unit will further strengthen food security monitoring systems through participation.

Needs

WFP has been assisting the displaced people from merged districts for over eight years now. However, 2016 and 2017 observed a considerable number of displaced people returning to their areas of origin after the launch of the KP merged districts Sustainable Return and Rehabilitation Strategy (SRRS) in April 2015. In response to the phase wise plan around 2.6 million (266,261 individuals have returned to their areas of origin in KP merged districts successfully, with around 699,978 registered individuals (15,833 families) still displaced. An estimated 255,906 individual returnees are members of 42,651 female headed households. As the government plans to continue with the return of the remaining IDPs in 2019, WFP’s response will need to be aligned with government’s merged districts SRRS for returnees in KP merged districts. WFP will also continue with its relief food assistance to IDPs and returnees which include women (23%), men (23%), Boys under 18 (30%) and Girls under 18 (24%) and the extremely vulnerable. The recovery assistance will be initiated upon completion of the six months return food package. The recovery activities include rehabilitation of their livelihood assets through food for assets and trainings. A significant proportion of women will benefit from training on the latest food security and livelihood best practices in their local context. The assistance in recovery phase will be conditional upon work or training. The relief and recovery activities are extremely critical to sustain the returns and support the families with lifesaving food assistance, as the food and nutrition situation in KP merged districts remains a concern. The food insecurity situation though improved still hovers at 24% with an increase in Mohmand, Orakzai and NW tribal districts. In the last KP merger districts Vulnerability Assessment indicates that overall 36% households have moderate to severe food insecurity in KP merger districts and sub divisions, whereas 16% have severe food insecurity. Last Protection Cluster conducted an analysis indicating that up to 28% of returnees who had signed a Voluntary Return Form (VRF) and received assistance had either not returned or had voluntarily moved away from KP districts following their return; lack of livelihoods being the second highest cited reason for this.

Activities

Relief food assistance is provided on a monthly basis with the general family food basket consisting of cereals fortified wheat flour or rice (80Kg), pulses (8Kg), salt (0.2 Kg), and vegetable oil (4.5Kg). Special consideration is given to the provision of food assistance to the most vulnerable groups in the communities, including the elderly, sick, disabled, children, pregnant and lactating mothers, and female and child headed households. WFP ensures that general food distribution as well as all programmatic activities are conducted in a safe and dignified manner and in adherence to the WFP Humanitarian Protection Policy (2012), including basic humanitarian and do no harm principles. Separate distribution counters and waiting areas are established at distribution points where female staff are deployed. Assessment teams aim to include women where possible and beneficiary data is disaggregated by gender. Cash and food transfers will be the primary modalities of assistance. Cash transfers will be used in post shock recovery to help short term food insecurity and encourage market functionality. Food assistance for assets will utilize cash as a modality of support and areas will be targeted based on food security needs, according to local contexts and market conditions. WFP’s VAM unit will further strengthen food security monitoring systems through periodic situation analyses with the Government, market and price monitoring which will form a basis to appropriately target the most vulnerable areas and communities in need of support. WFP is already coordinating closely with all humanitarian partners at the field level to avoid duplication of targeted beneficiaries and overlaps. All activities will seek to optimize gender equity by promoting women’s participation as well as support behavioral change to improve access and control over commodities for better food and nutrition security
