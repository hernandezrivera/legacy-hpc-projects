Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is continuing to deteriorate yearly due to persistent natural and man-made disasters. This situation has continued to cause more shocks and stresses to the people, mostly women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities. To help address some of these situations, CEF will implement FSL integrated with child protection in Rumbek Center, Cueibet and Aweil East Counties of Lakes and Northern Bahr El Ghazal Sates in South Sudan which are considered extremely severe in food security and child protection issues according to HNO 2023 assessment report.
In this project, CEF will strive to achieve the stated project goal through three outcomes of improved resilience of the target Communities through food production and productivity., increased and diversified income for youth, women, and men and Increased child protection services to women and children.
The project will adopt a multi-faced approach combined with relevant cluster approaches to achieve these outcomes. The participants will be part of the project at all levels, training will be conducted based on learner-centered approach where discussions and sharing experiences takes precedency. Participants will be provided with the necessary agricultural inputs and supplements in addition to skills and knowledge to increase production and productivity. There will be established demonstration gardens and Community base structures for smallholders’ farmers and child protection committees. The project will form Project Management Committees (PMCs), Asset Management Committeees (AMCs) and Community-Based Networks (CBPNs) to manage the project affairs and assets created for sustainability.
The project will ensure that gender and inclusion play greater role in the project implementation process. The project monitoring will be conducted regularly and when needed jointly with all stakeholders. At the end of the project, there will be project evaluation/ review session to measure performances, document lessons learn and provide recommendations for challenges encountered and best future programing.
Problem Statement
As of the independence in 2011 and after the signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement in 2018, South Sudan has not been at peace within itself. According to humanitarian need assessment of 2022. the humanitarian situation is continuing to worsen with an estimated 9.4 million in 2023 as opposed to 8.4 million people in 2022 are in dire need of humanitarian support. The report further stated that, South Sudan hosts nearly 337,120 refugees mainly from the Republic of the Sudan. It added that, out of the 9.4 million people affected, 24% are women, 53% children and 15% are people with disabilities.
Above normal rainfall for the fourth consecutive year have destroyed or submerged crops in several counties in Lakes and Northern Bahr El Ghazal States, contributing to a lower crop production than last year. As of October 2022, some 290,039 people have been assessed to need food and livelihoods assistance in these Counties. The severity of food insecurity is compounded by the depreciation of the local currency and the global impacts of the conflict in Ukraine and now in Israel. The cost of the Minimum Expenditure Basket has increased by 15 to 30 per cent since last year despite the availability of local harvests, while increases on wage rates have been minimal. The highly food insecure people are in locations with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate related shocks (severe flooding and dry spells), the macro-economic crisis, conflict, public health challenges such as measles and cholera outbreaks, climatic shocks, insecurity, and low agricultural production.

1. Needs

According to IRNA cluster report of January 2022, in Aweil east County a total of 11580 People were displaced by the conflict which took place between the mesirya and the Dinka Malual communities in Yith-Pabol of Ajiep Payam. The situation has caused increase in prices of food and non-food items in the local markets within the areas. Food shortage was also observed at the household level as daily meals have drastically reduced to one meal a day. It was also observed that people are moving from one village to another in search of foods. There has also been loss of farm tools by households from all the villages, most of these tools were either burnt down inside the households or looted during the attacks.The attacked has claimed 24 lives of people lost, 11 children went missing and many others wounded. Meanwhile nearly 2620 households (HHs) were displaced to the nearest villages of Warnyiel, Majok yinthiou, War-agany, Riang-awei, Angoot, and many others ran to difference locations as far as Warawar, Malualkon Wanyjok Madhol, Malualbaai and other places seeking for safety as their homes including food items in the granaries, example grain, groundnuts were burned down to ashes.In Rumbek center, total population 4,334 households are identified and in need of food, seeds and farming tools (IRNA, June,2021).According to IRNA report of September 24-25, 2021, a total of 8,651 HHs were assessed and found to have been devastated by floods in 9 payams of Cueibet County. Crops failure is imminent because of the unprecedented levels of floods which devastated peoples’ farms. This has led to lack of food in most of the affected locations. Some of the displaced households are surviving by gathering Pumpkin’s green leaf from the gardens. Livelihood sources have been devastated too. Chickens and goats have died because of wall collapsing and there are stories of sickness of some of the domesticated animals such as foot & mouth disease.For few who were given humanitarian assistance depend on that food for 3-4 days which is not enough for everybody in those locations since the food distribution done was a targeted one. Adults and young boys and girls may spend days without food.In these three counties, the people most affected and need of immediate and long-term food assistant are the women, children, elderly, and people with disabilities.

2. Project activities

 Training of community animal health workers (CAHWs) on animal health and livestock production (disaggregated by sex). Procurement and distribution of complementary wares (bicycles and protective gears) and veterinary kits to CAHWs to enable provision of animal health services through a cost recovery mechanism. Procurements and distribution of small livestock (sheep/goats) to households. Training of individuals households on improved animal production and health practices using Pastoralist Field School Approaches (PFSA) disaggregated by sex. Training of community-based extension workers on best agronomic practices and climate SMART agriculture (disaggregated by sex). Training of members of women groups on improved horticultural production and farming as a business. Procurement and distribution of vegetable kits to households (members of women horticulturally based groups). Training of youth, women and men on income generating activities and provision of in-kind support. Formation of Village Saving and Loan association (VSLAs- 10-20 members each). Identification and provision of support to Women Fishing Groups (WFGs) to developed business plans through provisions of grants through revolving funds. Formation and support of group-based post-harvest management facilities (10 members per group- disaggregated by sex). Provision of appropriate labor-saving technologies on post-harvesting and processing to women. Training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHW) on inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonoses e.g., RVF. Provision of timely food security information.
