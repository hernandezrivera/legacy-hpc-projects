Project Summary

The aim of this project is to avail its beneficiaries with Housing, Land and Property (HLP) support, to provide technical and expertise information to crisis affected families in IDPs, returnees and host communities in Jere, Biu, Damboa and Gwoza LGs of Borno State. This project targets a total of 5,000 individual beneficiaries with proper segregation as to gender and age in the four (4) LGs. The main activities will include: Disseminating legal advice and counselling on HLP rights; legal assistance, support, counseling and effective management of cases; conduct workshops, system strengthening, capacity building, raise awareness on legal implications surrounding HLP in general with reference to ADR in a community-based approach. Protection without Borders seeks to leverage on a multi-sectoral integration response within the Cluster such as GBV and CP and sister clusters such as Shelter/NFI.
Prior to this intervention, Protection without Borders is driven by experience legal practitioners with a spice of humanitarian response experience, this gave the team the privilege to long understand the laws, rights perceptions of what is tenable within Borno state and will implement this project with the best of knowledge and expertise. POWIB is also fully knowledgeable on land tenure systems and HLP rights which they will cascade/disseminate in a simplified manner to the families, community stakeholders, religious leaders and other important actors through the most appropriate method. to achieve an impactful outcome, POWIB will conduct further local assessments of HLP rights and customary tenure systems in the LGs.
POWIB is willing to work with relevant agencies, stakeholders, like Board of Town Planning, community leaders, local authorities and religious leaders in other to easily locate resources like water points, drainages and gutters and prevent misused of land that might result to disasters, crisis, conflict and other risk. Owing unto the pool of years POWIB has garnered in Borno state, the organization has a wealth of experience of implementing projects in the abovementioned LGs this is also coupled with their method of engaging the community to be in the know of the implementation of this project.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Response Plan 2023, the long raging conflict in the Northeast of Nigeria have further weakens the rule of law and have deteriorated the legal system which in turns escalate criminality and other vices within the community. One disturbing factor of the above mentioned assessment increased number of women inaccessibility of both ownership and possession of houses, lands and properties. The communities have further taken a shape to deprive children, single mothers their rights and thereby increase their vulnerabilities. Also, according to the HRP 2023, many communities were unable to resettle because flooding factors in most of part Borno state which does not only affect the resettlement but also other rights relating to HLP. Furthermore, the influx returnees and IDP returns enmasse were only reported to be supported by community members, friends and families. Flood-driven displacement which was strongly connected with livelihood-driven displacement as livelihood collapse was presumably a direct consequence of flooding and it increase the concerns of HLP in a drastic way. Moving forward, considering limited availability of land and the disruption of cultivation due to land rights issues and flooding, the 2022 harvest is unlikely to result in long-term improvement in the food security situation. Despite improvements in shelter conditions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) across the State. Reflective of this, although decreasing, reporting of improvised shelter types as the main reported shelter type for IDPs remained high in assessed settlements and host communities in Jere LG. Returnees, resettlement and numbers of IDPs in host community in Gwoza and Biu LGs emerged as another driver of displacement; it was reported that access to land became inaccessible, houses scarce and properties inaccessible. Stepping forward as shelter needs remains high in flood-affected communities, findings suggest the need that HLP rights needs to be address in other to address shelter needs. The need further emphasize on the relevance of peaceful co-existence between members of the host community and returnees/IDP as they are both entitle to an undisturbed use of HLP in the community.

2 - Activities

• Conduct HLP training for the community members comprising of adolescent, young men and women, older women and men both IDPs and host community on Human rights, HLP rights, legal frameworks including traditional customary laws. • Provide capacity building support and system strengthening on land registration and policy development. • Provide options with HLP issues on legal remedies particularly claims of lost documents, institutions, and land registration. • Provide legal counseling and psychosocial support including community sensitization and awareness-raising on HLP rights.• Support families and community with HLP related issues to access alternative dispute mechanisms including mediation, negotiation, and arbitration. • Assist in facilitating the referral of person/families with HLP needs to NFIs and other humanitarian partners to access the needed assistance. Objective: host communities, IDPS, returnees, including women and persons with disabilities, have increased knowledge of their HLP rights, ability to exercise such rights and uninterrupted usurpation of HLP. Indicators: • # of persons of concern reached through HLP awareness raising, workshops and outreach activities. • # of persons/families with HLP related needs referred to NFIs partners on the ground and assisted with shelter materials. • # of persons/families reached through HLP awareness-raising. • # of persons supported with legal counseling. • # of training of HLP rights conducted at the community/LG level. • # of LG stakeholders, community representative and religious heads trained on how to resolve the HLP related concerns. • # of person/families with HLP issues facilitated to access alternative dispute resolve and legal assistance.
