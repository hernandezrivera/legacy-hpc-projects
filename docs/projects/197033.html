Project Summary

The intervention is aligned with HRP 2022-2023 Strategic Objectives 1, 2, 3 and intends to complement nutrition sector efforts in Idleb and Hama. The project aims to implement aid support life-saving services for the treatment, preventive and awareness activities targeting children under five years and PLWs in Hama(Suran -As-Suqaylabiyah-Karnaz-Muhrada) &Idleb(Khan Shaykun)for two years (2022-2023) to reach 23,085 beneficiaries for 2023. In addition to address some of the key determinants of malnutrition by promoting improved IYCF practices and healthy behaviors associated with unnecessary deaths among vulnerable populations in the above areas. By integrating the CMAM program with the implementing partner's existing PHC clinics in the targeted areas, which will be supported by an outreach team to screen and identify cases of malnutrition among boys, girls, and women among IDPs and the host community. CMAM clinic will also be established in the target areas supported by a team of trained volunteers from the project implementing partner to address the unmet needs of acutely malnourished boys, girls and women among vulnerable returnees, refugees and IDPs in the target areas. With coordination with the nutrition sector ,TdH will focus on equitable and inclusive access to essential, life-saving humanitarian assistance based on vulnerability targeting. As well TdH will focus on identification of malnutrition cases through screening with appropriate referral.The targeted areas do not have CVA interventions , for that reason TdH will provide CVA to ensure social protection with cash availability for vulnerable households for 8 cycles planning to start distribution on february. The focus will be on improving availability of diverse nutritious food for PLWs with MAM cases and who is at risk to be MAM to improve maternal dietary diversity .The project will provide focused training for local partner and Ministry of Health volunteers to strengthen their capacity to provide structured CMAM and IYCF services and activities to those in need, case management and referrals, in addition to the capacity building activities for local partners through training of volunteers and community members, and support to facilities and communities for counseling on infant and young child feeding practices recommended by WHO. be divided into urban and rural areas, with coordination with existing partners (NGOs) and local partners to identify and divide sectors for outreach activities. In terms of treatment, the local partners' centers are located in the targeted areas and are accessible to beneficiaries according to Sphere standards. Also, the distribution of PPE (personal protective equipment) to volunteers, staff and beneficiaries as a protective measure for work in the event of an outbreak of the disease COVID. All activities will be coordinated with the nutrition sector and working groups to avoid overlap or duplication as TdH is an active member of the nutrition sector.Routine data will continue to be monitored using the revised 4Ws tool to measure progress towards planned targets. All the activities will be supervised on-site by a TdH staff and will be carried out in close coordination with local partner in order to guarantee a continuous and fruitful collaboration.TdH Project Manager and M&E Officer will conduct field visits on a regular basis and dealing with process monitoring basically, the methods that will be used to ensure implementing partners and donors know whether the beneficiaries have been targeted properly and whether they are receiving the correct services in a timely manner through monitoring the efficiency, effectiveness and accountability of the project.
Program indicators will be tracked according to work plan and logical framework, report progress and highlight areas of concerns. AAP participatory process will continue fully engage communities and provide qualitative information to complement the quantitative data

3 - Needs

According HNO 2023,Approximately 5.9 million people including 3.75 million children (2.2 million girls and 1.55 million boys) and 2.1 million women are in dire need of nutritional assistance in Syria, an increase from 5.5 million people in 2022. The PIN figures have shown an increasing trend since 2016 indicative of a fast-deteriorating nutrition situation,2022-23 HRP Sector Chapter Template (DRAFT). In 2021 the nutrition sector observed an increasing trend in malnutrition compared to 2019. According to the SMART survey report, July, 2021) the prevalence of acute wasting MUAC< 125 mm and/or bilateral pitting oedema) increased from 0.9% to 4.7% since the 2019 SMART survey results. As per HNO 2022- Nutrition Sector Severity, the total population in the project two targeted locations: Hama (Suran, As-Suqaylabiyah, Karnaz and Muhradah) and Idleb (Khan Shaykun) is 126,474 people including 1,167 children suffer from acute malnutrition and 1,897 PLWs suffer from MAM. Therefore, TdH will provide nutrition services to vulnerable mothers and children<5 in the mentioned areas based on severity of needs 4 and 5. As well, the targeted areas do not have CVA interventions, for that reason TdH will provide CVA to ensure social protection with cash availability for vulnerable households that have PLWs with MAM cases.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activities to be implemented in the project includes: Support facility- and community-based screening for acute malnutrition, Support treatment of acutely malnourished children with MAM, Support treatment of acutely malnourished children with SAM; Support treatment of acutely malnourished PLWs Counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practice PLW being reached with IYCF in child protection sessions like mother-toddler and positive parenting, as well as community outreach; Provide micronutrient supplementation to children for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies; Provide micronutrient supplementation to women for prevention of micronutrient deficiencie; Train and support center staff as needed for IYCF and psychosocial care practices on two separated trainings.M&E activities include field visits on a regular basis and dealing with process monitoring basically, the methods that will be used to ensure implementing partners and donors know whether the beneficiaries have been targeted properly and whether they are receiving the correct services in a timely manner through monitoring the efficiency, effectiveness and accountability of the project. Along with 4Ws, quarterly reports and spot checks. the project will be linked to other TdH interventions in food and Health mainly in addition to the distribution of PPEs that is linked to WASH in areas of implementation to have more impact and sustainability

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PR1: The beneficiaries may have no access to the centers . M1: TdH will consider this point upon the selection of the center location and specifications in addition to the involvement of women and girls in the activities timing and center shifts to ensure their accessibility during the day. PR2: The volunteers may not understand the cultural context . M2: TdH will ensure female-to female communication, and male to male communication between staff and beneficiaries to respect the cultural background of the area of intervention and improving access to services, majority of the volunteers in IYCF & CMAM are females for the sensitivity of the topics , in addition TdH will ensure that majority of staff and volunteers will be selected from the same area of intervention to have community acceptance, and culture background. PR3: COVID outbreak during the activities M3: TdH will provide PPEs to staff and beneficiaries during the activities , and will ensure dividing the beneficiaries into different shifts and groups to avoid any potential gathering PR4: abuse from staff to beneficiary during the project implementation. M4: all TdH staff and local partner volunteers will undergo a CP training, and PSEA training, in addition, all of them should sign the code of conduct, in addition to the assignment of the CP commit


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

TDH adapts a zero-tolerance policy against sexual exportation and abuse, and to ensure all PSEA standard are respected: • All TDH staff have signed the Code of conduct upon recruitment. • All TDH staff are oriented on PSEA upon recruitment on PSEA topic via induction sessions • TDH has its own internal reporting mechanism on PSEA, and has clear reporting channels that are accessible for all staff • All TDH staff including field staff that is working directly with beneficiary are regularly trained on PSEA, and reporting channels. • Beneficiaries are oriented on PSEA reporting Mechanism, through multiple channels (lines, Posters, email, Focal points). • TDH includes Gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection services in its intervention to support women facing sexual Abuse.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Local partner lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities. M1: SARC displayed an outstanding ability to implement humanitarian projects despite constraints and limitations, However, if SARC lost the access to project locations, TDH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with local actors, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue. R2: bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities M2: TDH already obtained approvals to work in Northern and southern area of Syria, in addition, TDH will advocate through the local partner to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU. R3: delay in receiving the fund due to the sanctions on the banking system in Syria. M3: TDH has different banking accounts in Syria with different pipelines to receive the fund from different sources. R4: new quarantine by the local authorities due to COVID outbreak . M4: TdH has on-line policy for approvals and working from home, also, the PHCs will follow COVID measurement protocols , in addition, TdH health Advisor will conduct training to TdH staff and local partner volunteers for COVID measurements during humanitarian response and will distribute PPEs to both beneficiaries and staff. R5: Fraud Usage of cash assistance by households. M5: The cash will be provided via banking system to ensure tracking, TDH will follow up with them via PDM.
