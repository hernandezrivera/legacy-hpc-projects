Project Summary

SPEDP proposes a 12 months response plan to provide life-saving emergency shelter and non-food items aimed at meeting the emergency critical needs of 13,500IDPs, 10,500returnees and 6,000non displaced and host communities in Counties of Greater Yei, Central Equatoria State. Our total target is 30,000 individuals and categorized by sex as; 10,500men, 9,000women, 5,100 boys and 5,400girls). SPEDP will implement its planned responses in Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo keji Counties, however, if the emergency would raise in other prioritized Counties, we are ready to change and implement the intervention where it will be most needed. The project will highly regard a consultative approach with humanitarian partners and local stakeholders in the vulnerability criteria to the identification and selection of the project beneficiaries.
This project is designed to complement our ongoing Health (Ebola response) project in Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo Keji Counties supported by World Vision (Core Group), education project in Yei and Kajo Keji and Shelter/NFI project in Morobo and Kajo Keji supported by SSHF/UNDP. We aligned our intervention to the shelter and NFI specific objective; 1. Improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations with new vulnerabilities; 2. Enhance the community’s dignity by increasing their ability to respond to new shocks and building on existing skills; 3. Support most vulnerable returnees, host communities/affected non-displaced, and IDPs with durable solutions, rebuilding lives through shelter and NFI solutions.
SPEDP teams will coordinate and engage in joint response missions with responding partners in Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo Keji Counties and also continue building experience on cash based intervention modality with partners implementing cash intervention projects. Through a collaborative approach, SPEDP will collaborate and coordinate with all the partners in Kajo Keji humanitarian hub for effective service delivery through joint response missions to fill emergency shelter and non-food items gaps in the targeted communities. Most importantly, SPEDP is playing a role of Co-state focal point for Central Equatoria state hence it will regularly engage in close collaboration with state focal points and shelter/non food items actors in Greater yei for effective planning of joint response missions as well as desk research on HLP issues, and conflict mitigation measures, though HLP may not be an issue since most of the returnees are settling in their areas of origin.
The main priority is to provide gift in-kind (GIK) shelter and non-food items together with cash based intervention (CBI) modality to newly internally displaced people, returnees and vulnerable host communities to improve their lives from acute shocks, the team will focus on shelter, skills training, and resilience building. SPEDP will request in-kind emergency shelter and NFI materials from the core pipeline managers based on the verified needs and preposition the items to the project locations through the logistic cluster and SPEDP assumes the role of distribution together with shelter/NFI partners present in Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo Keji respectively. The project design aims to integrate with other sectors like health in Ebola hotspots or HIV risk areas for holistic response. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people ensuring that services are delivered in a gender sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, people living with disabilities, etc. With the involvement of our protection and M&E teams, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through existing beneficiary feedback mechanism, to address GBV and protection threats during provision of shelter and non-food items, and ensuring service.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan crisis is a complex and protracted humanitarian crisis resulting from 5 years of armed conflict, recurring natural disasters and severe economic crisis. Civilians bear the brunt of the conflict and have been systematically targeted by the warring parties. People have lost their homes and livelihoods, and basic infrastructure has been badly damaged. Of the country’s estimated population of between 11 – 12.3 million people, new research by IOM DTM (30th of September 2019) finds 1.5 million people internally displaced persons in South Sudan and more than 2 million south Sudanese have taken refuge in neighbouring countries like Uganda, Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya and Democratic republic of Congo, with little hope of returning to their homes in the near future. Despite the peace-agreement reached on 12th September 2018. Nonetheless, fighting continues in many parts of the country by factions that are non-signatories to the peace agreement hence severe humanitarian crisis continues to threaten millions of lives. Over 7.1 million are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. South Sudan is also prone to recurrent natural disaster, particularly flooding as a result of cyclic heavy rainfall. Years of armed conflict have made local communities increasingly vulnerable and events have a severe impact, aggravating food insecurity, health risks and epidemics (e.g. Cholera etc) as well as disrupting local economy. Perhaps, there has been general reduction in conflict-related incidents and associated insecurity across Central Equatoria in the last 10 months (REACH April to June 2019 report), hence lower perceptions of protection threats, continued returns of IDPs and refugees, although the low level of returnees to Yei indicates that people remain wary about the likelihood of future insecurity. Nonetheless, Persistent food insecurity continued to prompt outflows from Central Equatoria to Uganda and the DRC. Following long periods of food insecurity, it is likely that many households have low resilience, and are unable to apply successful coping strategies when they experience shocks. In Kajo Keji County, the authorities reported 25,000 returnees, including refugee returnees settling in Kangapo and 36,000 returnees also arrived in Liwolo, Kerwa and Sokare and humanitarian organizations have provided household items and shelter to almost 1,000 households in Kajo-Keji central and Liwolo, (OCHA-16th September 2019 report). Much as there is security stability in Yei and Morobo at the moment, the population remain at very high risk of Ebola transmission as these counties share borders with DRC said World Health Organization. It is on this background that the project is designed to respond to the immediate emergency shelter and NFI needs of the population in the counties of Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo Keji to enable them to catch up with resilience and rebuild their livelihoods.

2 - Activities

The response plan will implement life-saving emergency shelter and non-food items activities, targeting 30,000 beneficiaries suffering from acute shocks as a result of displacement and other severe vulnerabilities. Interventions will include: Close collaboration and coordination with Central Equatoria state focal points, shelter/non-food items and cash based intervention (CBI) actors, protection actors including health actors in Ebola hotspots or HIV risk in the proposed areas of intervention for effective planning of joint response missions, desk research on housing and land property issues. SPEDP’s static teams together with other responding partners on ground will be conducting needs analysis and market assessment to figure out the needs of the affected communities in Yei, Morobo, Lainya and Kajo Keji Counties. This will be followed with beneficiaries’ verification and registrations that advise on active distributions of the gift in-kind NFI and/or shelter materials to 25,000 beneficiaries and cash based intervention (CBI) modality to 4,500 beneficiaries. Members of the community will be trained as trainers and others hired as enumerators, crowd controllers, security guards, help desk to register complains. Women and other vulnerable groups like the elderly, disabled and chronically ill will be prioritized to receive their items during distribution. Distribution points will be allocated in safe places, close, and accessible to beneficiaries so that women do not walk long distances that will expose them GBV risks. Distribution arrangements (time, place, schedule, size and weight, etc.) will not discriminate against vulnerable or marginalized groups. After a period of two weeks from the time of distribution, post distribution monitoring will be conducted to evaluate the effectiveness, appropriateness and coverage of individual responses, and thus accountability to beneficiaries. SPEDP being a Co-state focal point will be conducting sub-national coordination meetings with partners operating in Central Equatoria to monitor their interventions and update the cluster as well as engage in close collaboration with other state focal points and shelter/non food items actors in various locations for effective planning of joint response missions as well as desk research on HLP issues, and conflict mitigation measures.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the categories of the beneficiaries are; non displaced and host communities, IDPs, andreturnees. These thus involves all vulnerable groups in the counties. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are particularly vulnerable as all their assets have been depleted or destroyed due to conflict and displacement. The categories of IDPs who will benefit from the intervention include:- Women: While attempting to save their children from starvation, malnutrition and disease, women are subject to conflict-related security threats including sexual violence; their vulnerability is enhanced when they are displaced and forced to migrate. Many women have fled the fighting alone with children. Pregnant and lactating women: This group is particularly vulnerable. With access to limited food, maternal and child health is compromised. At this stage the project will aim to improving this. Children and young people: There are many unaccompanied children and child or young adult headed households. Children under 5: This group remains vulnerable to malnutrition and access to sufficient support and services. With displaced parents of having access to limited foods supply, this group is most affected nutritionally. Older people: Older people often bear the brunt of conflict because of their frailty and reduced mobility. People with a disability: Like older people, are particularly vulnerable to violence due to reduced capacities (i.e. hearing, vision, mobility) and are often missed out of distributions due to access. Men: Most of the fleeing men have lost all their livelihoods and are unable to provide for their families. This results in anger and frustration sometimes leading to an increase of gender based violence. Targeting them focuses on restoring family relations and dignity.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project will use local community based structures and systems. training of the local population on the use of local available materials for shelter to empower the communities. Once project is completed skills are already transferred to the communities to ensure continuity of the project using local resource persons and domestic resources.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the national cluster objectives and it will recognize the urgent need to meet the social protection needs of specific vulnerable groups such IDPs, refugee returns based on UNHR 2019-2020 Regional RRP to prepare refugees returns to partake in peace building, promoting social cohesion between refugee and host communities and national reconciliation efforts in South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, there is currently peace and the area is experiencing population returns both IDP and returnee population. according to the Situation over view: REACH Jan-Mar 2019, the areas experience increase in population returns. SPEDP is working in these proposed locations and found the protection environment safe and stable for this intervention. SPEDP teams will ensure protection concerns are mainstreamed to ensure safety and stability of the project and ‘do-no-harm principle’ i.e. any form of assistance will not exacerbate or create more risks to already vulnerable populations, legitimize conflicts or fuel further conflicts rather direct assistance towards ‘connectors’ in affected populations to foster a sense of community cohesion. The intervention will ensure project participants’ of safety, rights and dignity alongside material assistance.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project is linked to local community systems triggered by emergency needs. There are a lot of multi-hazard factors; floods, localized insecurity, depleted community assets /shelter, Protection and degenerated livelihood amidst deteriorating health concerns. This interventions will reverse the emergency trends and over sustainable recovery solutions in the severity counties targeted.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaints and feedback will be acted on according to established protocols agreed with stakeholders and incorporated into programme learning. SPEDP team will establish help desks at shelter/NFI distribution points to allow for illiterate beneficiaries to provide verbal feedback. The field teams will regularly engage with community members (including beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, men and women, adults and youth) to invite their honest feedback. The feedback mechanisms are part of SPEDP’s Accountability to Affected Population commitment. In this approach, the beneficiaries are urged to lodge complaints/express grievances prior to and after distribution of the shelter and NFIs including on the general programming of this intervention.
