Project Summary

Improving the food security & Livelihoods condition for the most vulnerable people in North West Syria through: 1-Direct food assistance: food baskets & bread distributions 25,000 HHs, 2- and provision of in-kind livelihoods grants 1500 HHs. total : 26,500 HHs.
This will contribute in Increasing sustainable access to FR for those most vulnerable IDP & host families in NW Syria including IDPs, & host women headed households, and their children.
And, Improve the ability of vulnerable and conflict-affected men and women in Syria to meet their own basic needs through the restoration of sustainable livelihoods opportunities. Beneficiaries receive the assistance on-need based.

Needs

With the increasing number of IDPs arriving from Syria’s Eastern & South regions, North Hama, Idlib & North and North West Aleppo has become impacted with newly arrived families. IDPs arriving from different parts of Syria or who previously arrived from Aleppo and Rif Damascus are poor with limited access to work opportunities. A majority have been forced out of their homes, whether it be from coalition airstrikes or other forms violence committed during war. High number of the IDPs whom reached Idlib have settled in the camps & main cities, but due to the overcrowding in Idlib’s large cities a large portion have begun to also reside in the countryside, almost 67% the total population in Idleb are People in Need. This includes 37% of the population as acutely food insecure and 30% of population as at risk of food insecurity. In addition to physical and financial access to food, displacement and conflict have been key drivers to food security in Idleb (Alameen DNA, 2019) A majority of Idlib areas are considered of low economic status with little access to work opportunities Food basket distribution will be targeting areas considered the most in need. Many of these areas are filled with newly arriving IDPs that have not been reached. Also Livelihood opportunities have been devastated by the conflict, leaving communities without the ability to absorb shocks or the financial means to meet their basic needs of water, food, and fuel, which in many areas have become exorbitantly expensive in the context of the crisis. For example, in Idlib province, the price of rice and wheat has more than 400 % since the onset of the crisis and a majority of the population cited lack of resources to buy food as a major barrier to accessing it. This has left vast proportions of the population reliant on continued humanitarian assistance to meet their needs. It is estimated 10 million people are now in need of new or restored income generating activities countrywide if they are to return to a position of self-sufficiency. The situation is particularly severe In Idleb. For example where 80% of households fall under the extreme poverty line ($1.10 per person per day). Whilst many markets are still functioning in Ariha, the ability to access labour opportunities has been severely restricted due to a physical and financial inability to access the markets, lack of capital and the forced sale of productive assets as a short-term negative coping strategy. On top of this, many professionals have fled the area, many are wounded and many more are engaged in fighting. There is thus both a need and an opportunity to empower and support livelihoods, particularly for women and youth in Ariha society, many of whom have lacked access to formal education or training opportunities over four years of bitter conflict. (Al-Ameen DNA) Data need Assessment Unit.

Implementation of activities

1- Al-Ameen will maintain coordination with the humanitarian actors having similar operations in the project area to avoid assistance duplication. 2- pre-registration: Targeted families will first be identified. Families will be selected based on Al-Ambe beneficiary selection criteria, this includes determining their existing need under food & monthly income, the family profile & category, displacement condition and time, and food consumption score. 2-1 Al-Ameen will undertake pre-registration activities, this includes registration of potential beneficiaries and preparing the pre-registration list, and establishing a committee in each community and engaging the local councils to be represented in these committees to ensure transparency and engaging the community in the selection process, through the pre-registration process Al-Ameen team will ask questions related to the family condition, displacement & detailed questions under the food consumption, the answers of these questions will be basic to identify and measure the vulnerability & fitting the project beneficiary selection criteria. The registration in this process will be in centralized point on the community level, the data will be collected through families filling electronic questioner by Al-Ameen staff members. All, families will be able to register in this process, although general information on the beneficiary selection criteria will be provided by Al-Ameen before the pre-Registration process for both the IKGs & FR distributions. 3- Filtering the collected data: Al- Al-Ameen will identify the beneficiaries based on the beneficiaries selection criteria. Then Al-Ameen will apply filtration for the collected data 4- conduct door to door visits to verify the collected information and prepare the final list of beneficiaries, that according to each field visit and interview with families whom already provided information during the pre-registration process. 5- Each family identified as a beneficiary of the food assistance or the IKGs, will be given an electronic barcoded card which they can utilize to obtain FR each month at the FDP.or to receive their IKG The Data Management Officer in Gaziantep will prepare these cards through barcode generation tools. The print of the cards will be done inside Syria. 6- The final distribution list & beneficiary contact list will be printed for each location and available Excel format list will be prepared. 7- Transport the food baskets “ & IKGs” to Al-Ameen warehouses, In line with the above, Al-Ameen currently has a warehouse. 8- Transport the food baskets or the OKGs to the distribution points: Al-Ameen will do reception, secure storage, and handling as per Al-Ameen quality standards and practices for final distribution to the eligible beneficiaries of the commodities.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Al-Ameen identifies the key protection risks prior to the launch of the intervention to ensure mitigation measures are in place to reduce the likelihood and impact on the population. To reduce the risk or tensions arising amongst the targeted communities, during the initial application and vetting stages, Al-Ameen will communicate with all stakeholders to ensure wide understanding of the proposed intervention, including the selection criteria in place to identify the beneficiaries. For the IKGs specifically, Al-Ameen establishes a Steering Committee composed of various entities to ensure full transparency of the beneficiary selection, and when applicants are not awarded with the grant, Al- Ameen provides detailed feedback on the submitted business plan including remarks on why the project was not selected and how to improve for potential future selections. Special attention will be given to the female-headed household in order to respond to their specific needs and ensure gender equality and avoid any risk of discrimination in the implementation of the project.
