Project Summary

The project objective is to respond to the Health cluster and the Nutrition working group objective to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving and gender responsive health services to the most vulnerable communities and vulnerable women and children under five in West Bank including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
This project aims to improve the accessibility and quality of essential and lifesaving health care services for Hebron residents. The project will include both responsive and remedial interventions to meet the needs of children, especially neonates, and pregnant and lactating mothers.

It will address critical gaps in the health sector response for women and children in the West Bank, especially in Hebron (H2 and Area C) communities, including the need for the maternal and child health, and neonate services.
In addition, the proposed activities are aligned with the UN strategy for Hebron and responds to the latest multi-sectoral need assessment conducted by OCHA, and the neonate services assessment recently completed by WHO. The project will address the gaps through the provision of critically needed equipment, medical consumables and drugs. The project will also address the needs for ambulatory services, through conducting basic life support training to front line health professionals and community members, to enhance their skills and competencies to respond to immediate life-threatening incidents, this will include neonate resuscitation training, and infant and young child feeding in emergencies training.
The total number of women, children and men that will benefit from the HRP funding will be 40,000 across the H2 area and two localities in Area C (Qilqis, Khalet Ad Dar) in Hebron district over a twelve-month period. It is estimated that at least 14,750 high-risk pregnant and lactating women and 10,000 children under age of five (5,000 boys and 5,000 girls) will benefit from urgently needed lifesaving health interventions. Additionally, 300 female front line health care providers and community members, and 200 male front line providers and community members will receive lifesaving training interventions and preparedness training. Some 14,750 men will receive primary health care services as part of the minimal essential package of services.

1 - Needs

Across the oPt, health related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to health care due to a combination of chronically depleting medical supplies, insufficient human resources, and imposed access restrictions. An estimated 1.3 million people need humanitarian health interventions across the oPt. In the West Bank, 377,803 people need humanitarian health interventions. The systematic isolation of communities as a result of movement restrictions, has meant communities are cut-off from basic primary healthcare services and even emergency care during times of need. With a rise on attacks against healthcare facilities and increasing movement restrictions, particularly for ambulances and paramedics, the frontline healthcare system and those in need of timely healthcare assistance, remain under threat. In Area C, H2 and the seam zone, 162,663 people need humanitarian assistance. In East Jerusalem, almost one in every five people needs some form of humanitarian assistance, with mental health as the most expressed need. A key issue for the fulfillment of the right to health in H2 in Hebron is insufficient availability of health services, poor emergency services and primary and secondary health care for pregnant women and children. There is a high need for psychosocial support services especially for children under 5 and youth affected by violent events, especially the disabled. Moreover, movement restrictions severely impact accessibility to health services, with key concerns including waiting time at checkpoints and closures of areas, security issues at hospitals and delay in access for ambulances. In a multisectoral household assessment of essential services in H2, health services of the residents in H2 was affected by several factors. Over 40% of the surveyed households explained that the crossing of checkpoints to access services was the main impediment. While another 52 % explained that the cost of health services was the reason. Another 41% explained that the cost of transportation was the reason. 30% mentioned harassment by settlers on the roads. These challenges, along with a lack of proper infrastructure, equipment, adequate staffing, affect the quality of health services in H2. In another assessment on the neonatal services in Hebron, the results showed there are insufficient neonatal services in this area, with only 45 governmental incubators, of which 5 are isolation beds at neonatal units available for Hebron. This leads to a high number of cases being transferred to hospitals in the northern West Bank and East Jerusalem, increasing the risk of medical complications and disabilities in newborn babies. Al-Mohtaseb hospital has the potential for improving access to secondary health care for residents of the H2 area, mainly in emergency services and neonate lifesaving interventions; however, the installment of new equipment is required. Furthermore, an estimated 3,000 boys and girls under age of 5 in Area C, H2 and Hebron, including at least 1,000 children are acutely vulnerable and are at high risk of life-threatening conditions and developmental delays and disabilities. This situation is similar among pregnant (38%) and lactating women (28%). Even though all residents including men, women, boys and girls under the age of 5 and disabled people are affected by movement restrictions and barriers to seeking health care, women’s accessibility to essential reproductive and maternal health services form a critical challenge for these communities. Since women are also more likely to have care responsibilities towards other household members, they disproportionately bear the burden of restricted access to medical services for the whole family. Hence, there is an urgent need to provide support to the population in H2 in Hebron to avoid a further degradation in essential and quality health care services, particularly for high risk pregnant and lactating women, neonates and children under five.

2 - Activities

With UNICEF support, relevant stakeholders and NGOs will address identified gaps and improve access to adequate lifesaving maternal and child health interventions through both responsive and remedial activities in Area C, H2 and Hebron. UNICEF in partnership with relevant stakeholders and local NGOs will support acutely vulnerable groups in Area C, H2 and Hebron communities to have access to maternal and child health services, and ambulatory interventions delivered at the facility or through community outreach services to meet the needs of the most vulnerable people. Moreover, UNICEF will ensure the implementation of the minimum package of essential health services for communities that require access to primary healthcare services, working jointly with relevant stakeholders and local NGOs. Furthermore, the project will ensure complementarity with implementation of the WHO and UNFPA HRP projects and will jointly contribute to the implementation of Every Newborn Action Plan 2017-2022. To reach the 2020 HRP objective to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in West Bank/East Jerusalem, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities defined under the Health and Nutrition cluster will be implemented: HC Act:.1: Respond to the needs of the most vulnerable people by ensuring that they have access to essential secondary level health care in West Bank. (Responsive)1. Provision of maternal and child health related medical equipment and essential supplies drugs and medical consumables to primary health care clinics operating in Hebron H2 area such as Karantina and Um aldalyeh clinics, and Al Muhtaseb hospital. 2. Provision of critical medical equipment to neonate unit at Al Mohtaseb hospital3. Provision of critical emergency/ambulatory equipment to emergency unit at Al Mohtaseb hospital 4. Provision of basic life training for health providers and first aid training and kits to community members; women and men in H2 and Area C communities mainly in Qilqis, Khalet Ad Dar HC Act:2: Provide access to healthcare for the most vulnerable children under the age of 5, neonates and pregnant and lactating women through life sustaining interventions at the facility and community level and Minimal Initial Service Package (Remedial) Provision of early screening and detection of developmental delays and or disabilities among children through training of medical staff at H2 PHC facilities such as Karantina and Um Al dalyeProvision of community / parent education services, to enhance parents’ skills in dealing with children of developmental delays and disabilities. In H2 and Area C communities (Qilqis, Khalet Ad Dar) This project is designed to contribute to gender equality and gender-sensitive programming, focusing on the needs of gender-specific vulnerabilities faced by high risk pregnant and lactating women and their new-borns, and boys and girls under five. Data will be collected, analyzed and disaggregated by gender and by age group. Gender will also be mainstreamed by working with other clusters to ensure that clinical management of GBV is improved, and that sanitation facilities within health facilities meet the specific needs of women and people with disability. Early engagement of selected communities will be enhanced by involving communities and their representatives at all stages of project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Target beneficiaries have been consulted during the project design through focus group discussion and will be continually involved in its implementation. Channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing will be established through satisfaction survey and recording goods of services delivered to the end user, so that assistance is delivered in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.
