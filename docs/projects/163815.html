Medair will focus on rehabilitating and/or re-equipping primary healthcare facilities in areas with high needs, prioritizing hard to reach and newly accessible areas, such as Deir-Ez-Zor (DEZ), Northern Aleppo, southern Idleb and underserved areas of South Syria (Dar’a and Quneitra). Given the high needs and likely reduction in the presence of other actors, Medair also hopes to expand programming into either Hasakeh or Raqqa. Following infrastructure rehabilitation and/or re-equipment, a 12-month programme of monitoring visits and capacity building training for staff will maximise the quality of service provision.
The community health programme (CHP) will target key health, hygiene and nutrition behaviours through messaging at household level in the clinic catchment population.
For the 8 PHCCs rehabilitated and/or re-equipped by Medair in 2019, the supervision, training and CHP will continue into 2020. Medair plans to rehabilitate and/or re-equip up to 10 PHCCs during 2020, with 8 locations selected and MOH approval received for 5 to date.
MHPSS services, healthcare for people living with disabilities and initial support of GBV cases will be integrated into the services provided by the PHC facility. Nutrition screening and treatment will also be integrated-details of this have been submitted separately as per sector guidance.
Service provision is by existing public sector health staff. Medair Health Officers (4) conduct field visits and training. Support staff consist of Health Coordinator, Health Advisor and senior management.To ensure sustained access to healthcare, Medair will exit the location at the end of the support period by handover of each PHCC to national or regional health authorities.
Medair provides capacity building training for both clinic staff and CHWs working in the clinic catchment area. 325 healthworkers and 320 CHWs will be trained in 2020. The training package for both is extensive, and delivered over a 12 month period by Medair staff, qualified MOH staff, and external trainers. Medair provides transport /accommodation costs for trainers if needed. Healthworker training comprises 28 days per governorate (140 training days in 2020), covering key gaps (RH (ANC, PNC, FP), mhGAP, PFA, Disability inclusion, NCDs, and IMCI). Trainee incentives are 5000 SYP/day, trainer incentives 6000 SYP.
CHWs receive 8 days of initial training and 6 refresher days, delivered per clinic (112 days total during 2020) . Supervisors also attend M+E days. Topics include ANC/PNC, nutrition, MHPSS awareness, PFA, hygiene, communicable diseases, NCDs, GBV awareness, and disability awareness.

4 - Needs

Health systems remain heavily damaged, resulting in a severe lack of access to primary health care (PHC). Of the 12.89M PiN for health, 62% are in areas of "critical" or "catastrophic" needs (HNO 2020 Draft). Only 52% of PHCCs are fully functioning (HeRAMS, Q2 2019), and in NES only 36%. (Health sector Oct 2019). Of 28 functioning PHCCs in DEZ, only 6 are connected to the water mainline and only 3 have a generator (HeRAMS 2018). While multiple actors rehabilitate clinic infrastructure, few provide equipment or support for services to resume. Only 69% of functioning PHCCs nationally had a blood pressure machine (HeRAMS, 2019) while non-communicable diseases such as hypertension affect at least 20% of the population (WHO, 2018). Additionally, staff shortages are a major barrier to the provision of quality PHC. On average in Syria, only 8 doctors are available per 10,000 people. ALP, RAQ, DAR, IDL and RD governorates have the most acute staff shortages. Task shifting is limited by legislative restrictions.Reproductive Health (RH) remains a challenge as well. Facility based delivery rates are below 70% in rural areas such as DAR and DEZ (MSNA, 2019). In rural Homs, only 58% of women had received timely postnatal care (PNC) and only 28% used a modern contraceptive (Medair survey, 2019). Exclusive breastfeeding rates are only 28.5% nationally (SMART, 2019). Among children, the most common diseases are diarrhoea and ARI (EWARS/Medair, 2019). A household survey in Homs found only 29% of children under 5 with fever were taken to see a healthcare provider in a timely way (Medair, 2019). National coverage for measles immunization is 73% (MMR2), too low to ensure herd immunity. Clinics are poorly equipped to support GBV survivors due to restrictions on GBV training and treatment. Additionally, mandatory reporting of GBV survivors continues at GoS PHCCs, creating a major barrier to accessing medical/psychological care.

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will rehabilitate and re-equip 7 PHCCs and re-equip up to 3 more to ensure delivery of the Syria BPHS, focusing on MCH with integrated nutrition and MHPSS services. Priority will be given to clinics in high need areas. Rehabilitation of clinics will include appropriate WASH, ensure privacy and provide accessibility for PLWMI/caregivers. Outpatient curative consultations are provided with a focus on vulnerable groups incl. CU5, PLW, elderly, people with MHPSS needs, and PLWMI. Initial management of medical, surgical and obstetric emergencies and referrals to secondary care will be provided, as well as NCD screening, diagnosis and management. EPI services will be strengthened. EWARS notifiable cases will be identified and reported. Accessible and sustainable MHPSS will be provided through PFA and mhGAP provision in the PHCCs. ANC/PNC including safe delivery planning, family planning, STI treatment, and initial management of GBV cases (including CMR if permitted) will be provided. Medair will implement a curriculum for staff involved in facility-based and community programs. The focus will be on meeting key gaps in the current health system: RH (ANC, PNC, FP), GBV, IMCI, NCDs, PFA, mhGAP HIG, disability inclusion and nutrition. Where security and access approvals allow, a CHP will accompany each supported PHCC. Trained CHWs will share targeted messages at HH level (e.g. vaccination, RH, GBV awareness, NCDs). The aim is to improve key health and hygiene behaviours. Monthly supervision visits will be done jointly with the DoH, subject to access and approvals. Standardised tools including WASH FiT, Medair’s clinic services score and supervision checklist are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic registration data will be collated at least monthly. Beneficiary feedback (incl. complaints box, FGDs, exit interviews) will be collected where possible. A HH survey to assess key health and nutrition behaviours will be conducted in at least 2 locations

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Confidentiality of sensitive beneficiary data is breached, risking stigmatisation of individuals: train staff on data protection; clinic staff to ensure privacy during consultations. • GBV disclosures during consultations are not handled appropriately, causing harm: train clinic staff and CHWs on dealing sensitively with GBV cases and identify referral pathways at each location- -• Reluctance of community leaders and to accept GBV awareness-raising: involve influential parties from the start of health programmes. Clearly communicate issues and encourage community contribution. Support social structures. • Risk of GBV/abuse/threats towards CHWs: make HH visits in pairs.• PSEA (actual or alleged) by clinic staff or CHWs: provide training on Medair’s PSEA policy and reporting mechanisms to clinic staff and CHWs, AAP mechanisms including complaints box and exit interviews.• Inequity of access to clinics between men/women: Awareness of wider community context e.g. transport availability, market times. Ensure service providers available at times that suit women as well as men.• Inequity of access to services between boys/girls: Train clinic staff/CHWs. Monitor data for adverse trends. HH visit awareness-raising of importance of access for all children.• Head of households reluctant or PLWs unwilling to be screened, access services, or receive HH visits: CHWs mainly female (85%) to achieve acceptance for HH visits.• Inequity of access to clinics for people with mobility impairments: accessibility adaptations e.g. ramps, accessible bathrooms to be installed during rehabilitation; disability awareness and inclusion training to all staff and CHWs• Children at risk from accidents during building works: Explanation in advance to communities; Contractual requirements for tidy work sites. Site engineer to oversee contractors day by day. Spot checks.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS -No significant change in the political/security situation; GoS continue to control recently retaken governorates-Medair receives quick approvals from relevant bodies (SARC, GoS, ministries)-UN agencies able to provide requested GIK for nutrition-Fluctuations of Syrian Pound able to be offset against hard currency (USD) -Medair is able to recruit enough qualified staff/contractors-Acceptance of authorities and communities during interventions-Continued cooperation and coordination by authorities, UN agencies, INGOs, NNGOs, and SARC-Equipment in specifications listed by MoH is available at the time of procurement-Supply chain remains reliable and necessary goods approved for transport-MoH approves training plans, and allows Medair to conduct assessments-MoH provides timely information/approvals on PHCCs in need of rehabilitation/re-equipment; needs can be triangulated. RISKS - Temporary suspension of programme if insecurity is too high, delaying project delivery - Medair cannot secure approvals within timeline outlined - Central Bank imposes regulations which constrain Medair’s ability to maintain adequate cash flow to pay programme expenses - Medair staff or contractors commit fraud or do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines leading to a loss of financial resource. Mitigation:-Conduct robust, regular context analysis as part of security management.-Keep security planning up to date and train a crisis management team for each location. -Remain flexible in project locations if access to a particular location is refused.-Proactive coordination with Ministries and SARC, other agencies and UN to avoid duplication.-Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders.-Train all staff on procurement guidelines, conduct systematic internal/external audits to assess adherence, spot checks for fraud, Medair site engineer present daily during rehabilitation
