Project Summary

This project endeavours to fulfill the Logistics Cluster’s mandate to support humanitarian partners in reaching affected populations in the context of the ‘triple threat’ COVID-19, Locust and Floods emergency. Somalia’s operating context is particularly complex due logistics infrastructure and continued insecurity as well as restrictions on border crossings; and although Somalia is reasonably accessible by air, the cost of air operations is extremely high – and often is a deterring factor for several organisations and partners.
In 2020, as a consequence of the COVID-19 pandemic, commercial and humanitarian supply chains remained under strain due to limited accessibility which has significantly restricted the transportation of passengers and vital cargo for the humanitarian community. Therefore, this project aims to strengthen the humanitarian community's ability to save lives and address needs through timely and reliable logistics services, facilitation of operational coordination and information sharing, thereby facilitating the efficient and rapid transportation of urgent relief items for the humanitarian community's emergency response operations.

Project Activities

The Logistics Cluster aims to provide logistics coordination, information management, and – whenever a systemic gap is identified - facilitate access to common logistics services (air, road, sea transport as well as storage) on behalf of the humanitarian community. Leveraging the logistics expertise of WFP, the WFP-led Logistics Cluster plans to meet, based on demand, the requirement for support from all responding partners in Somalia, ensuring an effective and efficient humanitarian response through optimizing available funding and resources, to reach affected populations. The project aims to strengthen the humanitarian community's ability to save lives and address needs through timely and reliable logistics services and information sharing, thereby facilitating the efficient and rapid transportation of urgent relief items for the humanitarian community's emergency response operations. Additionally, this Project aims to strengthen the logistics capacity of the humanitarian community in Somalia in order to improve the wider response to future emergencies. This capacity strengthening will mainly take the form of logistics trainings, including warehouse management trainings – from basic to advanced – specifically tailored to the Somalia operating context, and the consolidation and dissemination of best practices so organisations can make informed and evidence-based logistics decisions.

Mitigation Measures

WFP - as the Logistics Cluster lead - will endeavor to mitigate any primary and secondary risks that may be associated with its facilitation of service provision. The risk management infrastructure of WFP will be leveraged to support the mitigation of any associated risks with Logistics Cluster activities. WFP’s risk mitigation strategies include the prioritisation of emergency planning and rapid mobilisation of resources, as outlined in its Corporate Risk Register while complying with humanitarian-wide and industry-based standard operating procedures such as the Sphere project and IATA’s Dangerous Goods Regulations. In addition to strengthened assurances, WFP also uses service level agreements and standard operating procedures to manage cost-sharing and cost recovery to minimize the risk of financial loss and delayed payments. For service provision including logistics services, WFP has also established a vendor and partner due diligence procedures and performance capacity assessments to pre-screen suppliers and partners, and will ensure that all contractors and contracted service providers adhere to the PSEA standards.
