Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to prevent and mitigate protection risk through community engagement including protection monitoring in the conflict-affected Counties of Pigi/Canal in Jonglei State, Leer, and Mayendit in southern Unity. MHA will actively involve in the identification of various protection-related risks and will prepare the community on how they can prevent or minimize the impact of the risk to the acceptable level if cannot be eliminated. The project beneficiaries are IDPs, returnees who are currently returning to their place of origin in Leer, Mayendit, and Pigi counties, the project will also be focusing more on the vulnerable individuals or groups experiencing different forms of protection risk including the negative coping strategy. These groups will need to be targeted with an individual’s protection assistance either with direct cash support or materials support depending on the needs. identify the trends or fluctuation of population movement through tracking the movement of the IDPs/returnees into the areas which would help the partners and the cluster to decide on the overall humanitarian situation and response. Assessments gather data on issues of safety, protection, needs, meaningful access to services, and freedom of movement of beneficiaries to access services and to identify the specific protection concerns that will need to be addressed promptly. Engage with existing community-based protection structures and collectively involve them in the identification of dynamics, risks prevention and mitigation so that critical protection gaps are jointly addressed.
The project aims to engage with to achieve a long term protection outcome, youths play key roles in community peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence in including prevention of GBV if they are provided with skills and knowledge. youth make public opinion regarding the essence of peace and humanity, can lead peace movement against racism, and militarism, spread the idea of peace and non-violence, work for democracy, human rights and gender justice. Strengthen the fight against discrimination, poverty, gender injustices, malnutrition, and hunger, provide voluntary service to the people during human-made and natural disasters, participate in public policymaking, create public awareness about the problems facing their communities and they can make a network of multi-cultural co-operation in economic, social and cultural areas among others.
MHA has designed unique strategies for sustainable peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence mostly among the big influx of Returnees coming back in Pigi/Canal of Jonglei, Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State. MHA will promote dialogue among the conflicting communities, community meetings, and Youth recreation activities utilizing the MDD approach targeting both Youths in Schools and out of school. MHA will also improve social-economic standards for youth who spend most of their time on unproductive activities which sometimes force them to engage in bad protection coping mechanisms.
They will be trained on skills like carpentry, hairdressing, handcraft making, tailoring, mechanics among others depending on market research survey findings which MHA will conduct to find out apprenticeship opportunities.
MHA has been operating in Leer and Mayendit counties since 2017 implementing a general protection project, our activities are being coordinated with other actors who working are within the area to avoid overlapping and duplication and that will continue to ensure that the limited resources are utilized correctly. We extended our protection monitoring services to Pigi/Canal this year under SA2 in which MHA is currently the only general protection actor in the area( Diel) and has become part of deep field coordination in New Fangak, therefore the protection monitoring services will be extended to Ayod. MHA is planning to establish a presence in Ayod toward the end of the year.

1 - Needs

Leer and Mayendit counties of Unity State were amongst the areas that were not prioritized for 2019 SA2 despite the huge number/influx of the returnees into the area. That has left protection gaps and other basic needs of the conflict-affected population unaddressed. A total of 34,000 individuals IDPs who returned into the areas between October 2018 to 2019 have been recorded by MHA in both Leer and Mayendit counties. That number added to the already existing caseload and eventually increase demand for protection needs. The return is believed to have been triggered by last year's signing of the revitalized peace agreement between the opposition and the government and has given hope to the conflict-affected IDPs/returnees to come back home to start their livelihoods as the situation changed with some vital signs of stability. The same has been noticed through our static presence in Pigi/Canal and this an opportunity to switch from the emergency phase to capacity building activities as communities are now seeming to be capable of adapting to new realities due to the changing contexts. They need to be introduced to resilience activities and sustainable projects.MHA wants to take the opportunity to apply a resilience activities approach which shall be built on the existing community-based protection networks (CBPNs) formed under SA2 in 2018 and SA1 in 2019 in Pigi/Canal, Leer and Mayendit counties, this project will emphasize on the risks that exist and how they will be tackled through full involvement of the communities in which the communities will be able to solve the issues that are affecting them by themselves. This approach will allow MHA to address risk at multiple levels, it will involve youth to be part of the problem-solving in the issues related to revenge killing, cattle raid, GBV and It will address some negative cultural norms and practices, economic, environmental, and social issues. It will also focus on the individuals to ensure they have the necessary tools to minimize the impacts of shocks and stresses. Moreover, this approach focuses attention on enhancing the capacity of local institutions, communities and individuals to adapt to shifting contexts and manage anticipated as well as unanticipated risks rather than merely focusing on vulnerabilities. MHA conducted Needs Assessment in Canal/Pigi in July 2019, we realized that Canal/ Pigi has been a destination for internally displaced people (IDPs) fleeing from Atar, Kaldaka, Malakal and other locations during the heightened insecurity in 2017. According to MHA data and WFP/NPA data, there is a high influx of returnees of newly arrived IDPs and Refugee returnees from different displacement camps within the country know as PoCs and those returning from neighboring countries such as Sudan, Ethiopia, Uganda, and Kenya. According to the REACH Assessment report March 2019, early marriage was also a key concern for girls, reportedly high in Canal/ Pigi, possibly driven by the need to secure more resources such as cattle through bride price. Cattle raiding continued to affect both men and young boys. Child abduction, often associated with cattle raids, was reportedly widespread and a key concern for protection regarding young boys. In January 2019, pockets of populations identified as being in IPC Phase 5 “Catastrophe” Canal/Pigi is among them which indicated high percentages of hungerKey MHA activities we plan to implement in 2020 include strengthen the local capacity to be able to prevent and mitigate protection risk and solving the inter-communal tensions, protection monitoring and assessment, community mobilization and awareness-raising, Identification of the people with specific needs and referral, Peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence activities mostly targeting youth economic and education-entertainment interventions.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

This project aims to have long term strategy so the project can be sustained after the end of implementation period ,the cornerstone of working with the community is to prepare them to take up the role and the responsibility of the project and to reduce the dependency on humanitarian for so long, if there will be anything that will need to be handed over to the community after end of the project, it would be done as per donor requirements. In the previous project, MHA had handed over the community centers to the community and the community is currently using those centers to conducts their protection activities based on the training and the skills provided by MHA before the end of the project.Again MHA is going to continue using a community-based approach up to the final stage of project implementation. MHA is going to maintain community-based Project officers, project assistants, and volunteers both in Leer, Mayendit and Canal/Pigi Counties, refresher training will be done as well to community-based protection networks (CBPNs) and local authorities. MHA protection team will directly work with trained CBPNs community members specifically in conducting protection monitoring, assessment, referrals, giving out financial/material assistance and reporting, train CBPNs on conducting community advocacy and awareness-raising/dialogues. MHA is surer that since the communities will be fully empowered to mitigate and manage their protection challenges, they will continue supporting their communities even when the contract expires. Transparency within the community with all the ongoing activities and clear timeframes will help in preparing for contingency planning when MHA will end the project. MHA will share, as necessary, given information with key partners and all stakeholders working in the project locations. Clear handover with local communities, authorities will be done to ensure a smooth transition.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The community feedback mechanism is in place and accessible in all proposed project locations established by MHA, in Canal/Pigi of Jonglei State and Leer and Mayendit Counties of Unity State CBPNS are already established and empowered. They have been one of our best source of information from the most vulnerable community members and some of the people are part of CBPNs. From the beginning of the project, MHA will continuously carry out rapid assessments that include services mappings and key informant interviews with stakeholders. These form the basis for any intervention and inception meetings held with humanitarian partners, CBPNs, community leadership structures to inform them of proposed programming, produce their feedback and support for services and reduce any potential for conflict within the community. Close collaboration with all key gatekeepers from the outset to ensure that they are supportive to programming and are included in decision-making processes. Regular meetings will be held in all locations of our Project targets to maintain positive working relationships with government, CBPNs and humanitarian partners as well as community leadership structures to address any issues and resolve conflicts in a timely manner as soon as it arises. During the course of the project, a beneficiary feedback and complaints response mechanism will be implemented to ensure equal participation of all groups. Community will be actively engaged through complaints and feedback mechanisms putting account their ideas and rights. Monthly feedbacks and complaints will be collected from beneficiaries. Consultation meetings will be held prior project implementation and during the implementation phase feedback meetings and mechanisms will be put in place to ensure timely provision of information to beneficiaries. Feedback is very critical to MHA because it brings everyone on board thus promoting project ownership and it re-direct the project back on track.
