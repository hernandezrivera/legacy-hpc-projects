Project Summary

The WASH component of the program is aimed at improving the overall health and wellbeing of 43,500 individuals including 4,787 IDPs. Water supply, Sanitation and hygiene education and promotion activities are designed to complement Nutrition and Livelihoods programs in order to deliver comprehensive health and wellbeing support to the targeted beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

Safe water supply sources, Hygiene education, Sanitation infrastructure, and construction training are some of the main needs of this population. Results from the 2020 baseline study, conducted in Abiemnhom, Mayom, and Mayendit by SP, indicated that 66% of HHs in Abiemnhom, 62% in Mayom, and 68% in Mayendit were able to access their primary water within a 30-minute round trip. Meanwhile, only 37% of HHs were able to do so in Maiwut. Per the report, the average HH water consumption was 13.7 liters per person per day in Abiemnhom, 7.3 in Mayom, 14.7 in Mayendit, and 10 liters in Maiwut. The assessment also revealed that only 42% of HHs in Abiemnhom, 8% in Mayom, and 40% in Mayendit have access to a functional latrine. Beneficiaries reported lack of water storage containers, distant water sources, and a long waiting time at the point of collection as the main contributing factors to low HH water consumption.

- Rehabilitation of 55 Hand Pumps - Upgrading 3 existing hand pumps to water yards - Installation of 1 new borehole as a water yard- Manual Drilling of 18 Boreholes- Establishment of 77 Water Management Committees - Training of 76 Hand Pump Mechanics- Training of 8 hand pump operators- Construction of 40 gender Gender-Segregated Communal/Institutional Latrines- Construction of 400 improved shared HH Latrines- Construction of 60 Gender-Segregated Bathing Shelters- Establishment of 76 Community Health Clubs- Conduct Refresher Trainings for 72 Health and Hygiene Committees - Conduct 80 Health and Hygiene Promotion Campaigns- Construction of 80 HH disposal pits- Conduct Trainings on Solid Waste Management at the HH and Community Levels- Conduct 48 Community-Led Debris Clean-Up Campaigns to Ensure Appropriate Waste Management- Water quality testing at completed wells and at point of use

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Project team will create referral pathways that can be used by vulnerable groups and conduct formative research to get feedback from these groups using the age, gender, and diversity approach as well as protection mainstreaming.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project will inform beneficiaries through inception meetings, joint monitoring exercises, and in response to queries through several focal points, including WASH management committees in the community.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be disseminated through posters pasted at strategic public spaces, and through addresses at NFI distribution points. Beneficiaries will be informed of their rights against exploitation and existing feedback and referral pathways.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback will be used to realign implementation design approaches to address the unique priorities raised by beneficiaries for the achievement of desired outcomes. The feedback will influence the monitoring plan to track those indicators that best show impact. Where relevant, periodic reviews will foster engagement between beneficiaries, SP, and the Donor to ensure alignment of beneficiary feedback with project implementation.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1) Risk is mitigated through designing project to ensure activities are held in locations with low security risks and that participants do not have to walk long distances through insecure areas to participate in project activities. Project staff register and distribute NFIs within the payams where the participants are based to ensure easy access and protection. 8.2) This risk is mitigated by siting WASH facilities close to households (not in remote locations) where women and girls feel secure to use facilities without exposure to sexual violence or abuse. The sanitation facilities will offer adequate privacy, with lockable doors, to maintain the dignity of the beneficiaries, especially those more vulnerable to exploitation and abuse.8.5) Risk is mitigated through targeting the vulnerable groups in communities and ensuring their voices are heard through establishment of feedback mechanisms. 8.10) Risk is mitigated by working with local government authorities to map out minefields in order to reduce risk of harm to project beneficiaries.
