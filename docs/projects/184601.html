Project Summary

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, about 4.6 million people are in need of humanitarian WASH support in Somalia, a country that has been in a state of protracted emergency for decades. The Gu rains caused floods in many parts of the country, displacing more than 400,000 and affecting a total of more than 900,000 people. Additionally, people’s livelihoods and resilience was affected by multiple waves of desert locusts. In 2020, the situation was further compounded by the effects of COVID-19. First cases in Somalia were recorded in March 2020. Unreliable access to water often from unimproved sources is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Access to an improved water source remains low, in drought affected districts such as Bardhere, water scarcity is a leading cause of displacements and conflicts. Nationally, the JMCNA found that 14% of non-IDP households and 21% of IDP households reported not having enough water for drinking. Furthermore, 32% of non-IDP households and 46% of IDP households reported not having enough water for personal hygiene. Additionally, JMCNA reported (HNO 2021), less than half of the households (42%) nationally reported having access to an improved latrine, while a quarter (25%) reported using an unimproved latrine, and the remainder (31%) had no access. Key hygiene behaviour such as hand washing after defecating and before handling food with running water and soap, keeping drinking water free from fecal matter, menstrual hygiene is still an issue in host communities and IDPs in Bardhere, Dollow, Ceel-Waaq and Garbaharey. Nationally, according to JMCNA data, only 58% of the households reported washing their hands with soap after defecation. Majority of households (72%) report not having a specific handwashing device (no tap of any kind). Furthermore, 45% of the interviewed households reported having no problems accessing menstrual hygiene products. Of those reporting problems, 42% indicated not having enough money to buy menstrual hygiene products whereas 20% reported that menstrual hygiene products are not available to purchase. In this project, HDC is aimed to improve access to WASH services for IDPs, host communities in Baardhere, Dollow, Ceel-Waaq and Garbaharey Districts. The proposed WASH services will be provided with recognition of people with disabilities, minorities and men, women, boys and girls, and the most vulnerable population through the provision of emergency water service/tracking to vulnerable settlements and communities affected by extreme water shortages and drought 5000 (2000 men, 2000 women, 500 boys and 500 girls). Construction and equipping of 16 flood proof water infrastructures (shallow wells) for communities affected by extreme water shortages and drought. Formation and training of water management committees 176(116 Men and 60 women).Construction of 600 gender sensitive latrines to reach 10,000 (3000 men, 5000 women, 1000 boys and 1000 girls). Construction of 100 disability friendly latrines. Conduct 12 general cleaning and garbage collection IDP and host community settlements. Procurement of sanitation tools (sets) for garbage collection 160 sets. Conduct quarterly (4) project support monitoring and supervision. Reach people 5,000 (2000 men and 3000 women) in vulnerable settlement and communities with critical hygiene items and key messages with specific focus on disease transmission and prevention. Provision of sustainable access to safe water services to displaced people 2700 (500 men, 1500 women, 350 boys and 350 girls) in targeted settlements and communities. The project will emphasis and directly target people with disability in all project levels and give them space to participate in planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. Focusing on men with disability, women with disability, boys with disability, girls with disability and elderly
