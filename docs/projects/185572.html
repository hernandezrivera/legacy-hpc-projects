Project Summary

The project will provide support to the targeted population which is identified by addressing the needs of the Rohingya and host communities through a recent study of FAO (FAO, 2021) as:
1. Develop portable skills for Rohingya refugees and improve farm productivity for host communities
The project will provide technical support to refugees in developing portable skills on homestead gardening that will capacitate and equip them with relevant transferable technological competencies for sustainable integration into the economy upon their return to Myanmar. The project will also provide inputs support to the host communities in practicing climate-smart homestead vegetable gardening production methods for year-round households’ consumption and improved nutrition.
In addition, the Digital Village initiative will be introduced to connect the farming families to agricultural information, ICT-based extension services, and financial inclusion. This will also promote a decentralized digital knowledge-sharing platform among rural farmers.
2. Strengthening market linkage and social cohesion
Livelihood activities in the host community will mainly focus on marketing system approaches to ensure sustainability and adaptation of methodologies of the smallholders. In this regard, the project will provide technical support to improve the operational capacities of six Aggregation Centers (ACs) to establish vertical integration of the smallholder farmers with the traders and retailers in accessing the WFP led camp market/outlets. The farmers will be linked with the WFP’s ongoing activities including Fresh Food Corners in Rohingya camps to localize the supply of agricultural goods. This will support the income resilience of local farmers and enhance the social cohesion activity between the Rohingya and host communities.
FAO will work to strengthen functioning of the aggregation centers and provide transports, tools and other infrastructural facilities to them in improving efficacy.
The project will also conduct an assessment in identifying barriers to social cohesion and mapping the dynamics including driving factors within and between host communities and Rohingya people.
3. Watershed and natural resource management
The intervention related to natural resource management will be implemented through a number of activities, such as land stabilization and reforestation which will contribute to the strengthen resilience capacities of communities by reducing climate-related risks, such as floods and landslides. The assessment under this output will provide guidance to project implementation and pave the way for future planning of interventions.
The project will also contribute to applying climate-smart agricultural practices and diversified income-generating activities for the local farmers and forest-dependent vulnerable communities. This intervention will help the project stakeholders in the adaptive planning and decision-making process for sustainable natural resource management, while simultaneously contributing to the conservation of biodiversity and better functionality of the ecosystem.
Gender, age, and disability Inclusion: Project interventions will be identified considering the needs of men and women with an account of concerns, choice, and extra workload of women. The identified trades will also ensure equal access to men and women based on intersectionality analysis including SADDD (Sex, Age, and Disability Disaggregated Data).

1 - Identified Needs

The population of Cox’s Bazar, especially living in Teknaf and Ukhiya, was already vulnerable before the Rohingya influx. The ongoing refugee crisis has put an enormous strain on the host communities. While also, the Rohingya refugees are living with poor nutritional status including children and women in the fragile environment. The COVID-19 pandemic has further worsened the situation.A study conducted by FAO in 2021 observed that 38.3% of households’ income in these sub-districts is dependent on agricultural (i.e. crops, livestock, and fisheries) practices. The COVID-19 pandemic has been severely affected the demand and supply chains of agricultural production. Moreover, the majority of marginal and smallholder farmers lost their capacities in purchasing agricultural inputs (i.e. seeds, fertilizers, feeds, and fingerlings), irrigation, and harvesting costs (FAO, 2021). The FAO conducted baseline study observed that the overall livelihood potential of the majority of the smallholder farmers is often limited by the loss of agricultural land, inaccessibility to improved technology, lack of knowledge, poor market linkage, inadequate extension and advisory support and insufficient private sector investment (FAO, 2019) .The study also observed that 50.7% of the responded households had low dietary diversity and belong to borderline or poor food consumption status (FAO, 2019). This is evident that the affected population is in dire need of support to improve their livelihoods and dietary diversity.Moreover, natural resources and groundwater reserves have degraded significantly in these sub-districts (FAO, 2021a) . In the aforementioned circumstances and contexts, the ‘People in need’ is explained focusing on the following areas:Targeting: The project primarily addresses the needs of a total of 10,000 targeted households (i.e. 50,000 unserved beneficiaries) comprises 2,500 HHs from Rohingya refugees (i.e. 12,506 individuals) and 7,500 HHs from host communities (i.e. 37,494 individuals). The interventions will include inputs support and technical assistance to enhance food security and diversity of Rohingya refugee and host communities. Approaches: FAO has identified three distinct approaches to achieve the FSS JRP 2022: (1) targets the smallholder farmers of host communities that are not currently in a viable position to enter into commercial farming. (2) targets the agricultural groups with higher potential for commercial or collective production. (3) targets the vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host communities who are vulnerable to food, nutrition and social harmonization. Watershed and natural resource management: Water and other natural resources have been put under considerable pressure and are a source of social conflicts. FAO will continue to address the watershed and natural resource management to play a major role in coordinating host community and refugee needs through its role as co-chair of the Energy and Environment Technical Working Group. Gender and cross-cutting issues: Targeted beneficiaries will participate during project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases. The implementing partners’ staff will be oriented on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) focusing on women empowerment and gender mainstreaming in all stages of project management. Accountability to Affected Population (AAP): FAO will continue to utilize its existing internal AAP systems to ensure that all activities incorporate measures of information sharing and recognition of vulnerable groups, as well as grievance gathering and address.
