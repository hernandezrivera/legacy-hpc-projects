Project Summary

The main objective of the proposed action is to save lives, improve food and incomes security of households’ and increase their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks.
The key interventions proposed through this action are cash/voucher assistances and market based agricultural inputs provisions and productions. The livelihoods support will help target beneficiaries in meeting lifesaving and immediate food needs and also contributing to agriculture productions and livelihoods diversifications and building disaster management capacity of vulnerable communities. The improved agricultural productions can increase the availability and affordability of nutritious food and hence improve dietary diversity to women and children. Combinations of food security and livelihoods support will help the target households to improve their food and nutrition status, build resilience and reduce vulnerability to malnutrition and other shocks.
The proposed action will be implemented for 12 months in four States of South Sudan, specifically Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil East), Jonglei (Akobo, Bor South, Nyirol), Warrap ( Abyei Area) and Eastern Equatoria (Torit and Kapoeta North) targeting a total of 60,000 people.

Needs

Exacerbated by severe economic crisis, armed conflict compounded by localized ethnic clashes in South Sudan have resulted in humanitarian and disruption of development efforts. This calls for holistic interventions in the short, medium and long term. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classifications (IPC September 2018) report shows that the food insecurity in South Sudan reached with an estimated 5.2 million people (49% of the population) projected to be severely food insecure including 1.76 million IDPs and 2.47 million refugees in neighbouring countries. The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring (FSNM) round 21 bulletin released in May 2018 also indicates more than three-fourths (76 percent) of the households across the country are facing moderate to severe food insecurity. Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency above threshold of GAM 15% and is expected to deteriorate even further as some areas are projected to experience extremely critical levels of acute malnutrition in the peak lean seasons. The crisis aggravated incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations having directly affected about 100,000 children. Approximately 1.8 million children and youth have not had the opportunity to attend school or education and training programs. The same report projects 2.4 million Children and youth to be out of school in 2019. As a result, many children and youths remain out of the formal education system due to limited or lack of education facilities especially in the rural areas and displacements resulting in young people with no or limited food and/or incomes. As the result of the crisis situation, it has been difficult to cultivate or harvest food, much of which has been destroyed in the course of fighting, market dysfunction in most areas, hyperinflation, and even lost their coping mechanisms to a point where relying on humanitarian aid is key for their survival. As a result, much of the population in the affected area has resorted to coping mechanisms and even negative copying means such as eating the crop seeds that they would have planted the next season, and skipping daily meals. This has affected community resilience in Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal States, Warrap and Eastern Equatoria that classified as (IPC 3 and 4) likely to experience severe food gaps, reduced dietary diversity, reducing meals and malnutrition.The proposed action gives priority to IDPs, returnees and host communities who are facing the heaviest burden by restoring to negative coping strategies. While designing the proposed action, Save the Children takes into account Draft - HRP objectives, priorities & indicative activities for 2019 for CLA and official recommendations of the government released on 31st of October 2018. Thus, this livelihoods based emergency project is proposed in response to the ongoing conflict and its associated impacts mainly severe food insecurity and malnutrition in Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South), NBEG (Aweil East), Upper Nile (Maban) and Eastern Equatoria (Torit, Lafon and Kapoeta North) State of South Sudan. The project targets primarily conflict affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition.

Activities

Save the Children proposes this action to respond to such a protracted crisis in South Sudan by ensuring integration of the project interventions at two levels: i) geographic – areas identified as hot spots using the common criteria or entry points (malnutrition, conflict) and ii) household or individual level – where a given HH or individual, in the identified geographic areas, benefit from one or more types interventions. The expected outputs and activities are: Output 1.1 Households benefit from provision of direct cash transfer, voucher and emergency livelihood kits meeting immediate needs Activity 1.1.1 Provision of food vouchers to households identified with acute malnutrition and in need of assistances until harvesting; Activity 1.1.2 Unconditional cash transfer to vulnerable/poor households of labour constrained, disabled, and affected by HIV/AIDS; Activity 1.1.3 Emergency vegetable kits (fast maturing types) to households along the riverine and accessing moisture areas; Output 1.2 Households benefit from crop and vegetable productions meeting their short and medium term needs; Activity 1.2.1 Distribution of emergency crop kits; Activity 1.2.2 Procurement and distribution of vegetable kits; Activity 1.2.3 Training of households on post-harvest management, protection, marketing of produces and use of adaptive technologies related to crop farming and vegetable gardening Output 1.3 Households engaged in fishery activities Activity 1.3.1 Provision of fishing kits Activity 1.3.2 Training of households on post-harvest management, marketing of produces and use of adaptive technologies related to fishery Output 1.4 Creation and rehabilitations of key livelihood assets through cash for asset schemes Activity 1.4.1 Creation and rehabilitations of communal assets (preparation of farming lands, ponds for vegetable gardening) Activity 1.4.2 Establish and support small scale irrigation user groups including construction and rehabilitation of small ponds for use in the dry seasons

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information to beneficiaries will be provided during project familiarization, joint planning, and monitoring. the key information to be provided are project objective, source of fund (donor), type of activities proposed, duration of support, targeting and beneficiaries selection procedures, their entitlements, roles and responsibilities of stakeholders and procedures about compliant feedback mechanisms.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Save the Children will conduct rapid need assessment to understand the context and analysis of socio-culture of target community. Based on findings, key messages will be provided to beneficiaries considering the context and also specific information that take into account the age and sex groups.

Yes

The project will use any opportunities of community level meetings to disseminate key messages and create awareness on protection of women and girls from any kind of harmful practices, any forms of abuse and sexual exploitations. Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Save the Children will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities and explore ways of strengthening or setting up complain and feedback mechanisms (CFM) to enhance accountability and community participation throughout the project period. The target groups for CFM will be formed with representatives from with girls, boys, women and men, together with community and government leaders and community structures from the onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and getting assistances considering their safety, needs and vulnerabilities. Any complaints from the beneficiaries about the project activities will be collected and feedback will be given by the Bomal level project management committees. In addition to existing experiences, we will work with the communities to determine a most appropriate and suitable complaint response mechanism (CRM) in order to facilitate beneficiary complaint/feedback collection, complaints handling and response mechanism. We will continue to build capacities of staff as well as communities to ensure they are aware of their rights and entitlements. Issues raised could be discussed during the monthly coordination meeting for joint actions where due necessary. Feedbacks from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, post-distribution monitoring, post-harvest assessments and meetings with beneficiaries.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will employ community based approach through engaging target communities and beneficiaries in the whole cycle of project - project familiarization and joint planning, implementation, monitoring and feedback processes for instance post distributions and post harvest and evaluations using participatory tools (household, focus group discussions).

The Proposed action will conduct risk analysis aiming at identifying prioritizing and planning for meeting specific needs of target beneficiaries. To realize this, consultations will be conducted with each sections of the community separately by employing conflict and gender sensitive checklists that focus of risk analysis for each homogenous groups (children, youth girls, youth boys, men and women) of the community.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed action will conduct assessment to identify the main barriers and power relationships how target beneficiaries are accessing resources, who is making decisions and how decisions are being made. For the analysis, Save the Children will develop specific checklists and conduct interviews with the different sections of the community using participatory tools to identify the key barriers and draw action points for informing implementations of the project.

Yes

The proposed action will conduct consultation or discussion with target communities and other stakeholders to identify the main issues related to inequalities or protection threats. Gender sensitive checklists will be developed and administered to identify issues of inequalities or threats and hence draw action points to inform implementation of the project.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will address inequalities during targeting and selection of beneficiaries, identifying and prioritizing their specific needs and receiving their feedback during post distributions monitoring. Protection issues will be addressed during selection of distribution sites, timing, and considering distances from home bases.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will employ standard monitoring tools that track beneficiaries reach through different interventions in a regular basis disaggregated by sex and age. Consultation with the beneficiaries will be conducted during post distributions and post harvest assessments. As part of the targeting and beneficiary selection process, communities will be consulted through community meetings to ensure that the families most affected by chronic food insecurity are included in the program. This will be ensured with due attention to inclusion of affected people such as disabilities, households with children under five affected by malnutrition, elderly and affected by HIV/AIDS.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety and security assessment will be conducted in the earliest phase of the project. The findings will be used to inform implementation of the project making sure that beneficiaries and staff are working in areas ensuring their safety. For instance, during distributions, community watch volunteers will maintain order and provide added protection for the beneficiaries.
