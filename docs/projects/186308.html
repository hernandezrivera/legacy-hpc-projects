Project Summary

The North West region and South West region of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that has resulted in system wide breakdown of infrastructure and services especially in the health sector. This crisis has resulted in the displacement of over 1.1 Million people, especially women and children1. Data from the MSNA conducted in February 2021, revealed that 199 (22%) out of 893 health centers were no longer functional2. Attacks on healthcare is a common feature of this crisis and remains a major threat to the availability of healthcare in the North West region and the South West region. There are limited specialized services to take care of victims of physical and mental trauma in these regions as the number of cases have been on the rise because of the crisis. This situation has been worsened by the COVID-19 Pandemic which has increased the burden on a health system that has already been fragilized by the humanitarian crisis. Three health districts (Ekondo Titi, Mbongue, Bakassi) have had cholera epidemics this year with case fatality rates sometimes reaching 40%. In the most of affected areas, health services are not available while less affected areas have health services of poor quality. This project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internal displaced populations (IDP) and affected host communities in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon affected by the humanitarian crisis by ensuring a timely and equitable access to health care services. The project will target 373,069 IDPs persons (51% of this target are women, 16% are children under 5, and 11% are people over 60 years old). The areas of intervention will be Fako, Meme, Lebialem, Ndian and Kupe - Muanenguba divisions in the South-West region and Mezam, Momo, Bui, Ngo-ketunjia, Mechum and Boyo in the North-West region. The project will emphasize on timely access to equitable health care through mobile clinics in areas with no functional health facilities and the use of vouchers in areas where health facilities are operational.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the North West region and South West region has remained unpredictable and volatile for the past 5 years since the onset of the crisis. The combined effect of COVID-19 and the crisis has had a huge impact on the fragile health system. Access to essential health care remains a challenge to most of the affected communities due to the complete or partial shutdown of health facilities, the distance to the closest functional health facility and the inability to pay the cost of health care. The Multisectoral Needs Assessment carried out by IOM in February 2021 showed that 22% of health facilities are closed in both regions and over 410,993 people were still displaced from their homes. The persistence of insecurity is causing additional displacements in these regions. There is an urgent need to improve epidemiological surveillance in these regions as the risk of outbreaks of diseases like Cholera, Measles, yellow fever and Monkey Pox remain high due to the poor living conditions of affected populations and poor vaccination coverage for routine vaccines. A total of 3 out of 18 health districts in the South West region are currently reporting cases cholera this year. The proximity of some of these health districts to parts of Nigeria under epidemics of laser fever is of great concern given that movement between Cameroon and Nigeria is difficult to control, it is important to strengthen the capacity of the health districts carryout response activities during outbreaks and community-based surveillance for early warning on public health events. Persons living with disabilities, pregnant women and teenagers are most vulnerable and hence they need assistance to access quality health care. Given that most of the affected populations are living under poor living conditions, they are exposed to many pathogens that increases the likelihood illnesses. The brutal nature of the crisis has resulted in physical trauma of many through gun shots or torture. A good number of people traumatized mentally and need mental health assistance. The project will have a mental health component to take care of the mental health needs of the affected populations
