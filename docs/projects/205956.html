Project Summary

Action Against Hunger strategy is designed to address the main causes of malnutrition through an integrated approach linking nutrition with health, FSL and WASH interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon communities' resilience and prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Under this project, the proposed activities include prevention, treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition in children U5 and PLW using the national guidelines; provision of basic health services at our Stabilization Centers (SC) and community-based healthcare using ICCM approach through the outreach clinics; provision of minimum WASH services to beneficiaries at nutrition sites and at community level. Additionally, Action Against Hunger will provide nutrition preventive services through early screening at the community level using the Family MUAC approach and referrals, increasing access to MIYCN services, deworming and Vitamin A supplementation as well as improving the access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH and FSL services. Innovative activities for malnutrition prevention will be implemented in the project areas in collaboration with UNICEF and WFP. The project will be implemented in line with the transition strategy by the Ministry of Health and the nutrition cluster. Action Against Hunger will work closely with the Ministry of Health and other stakeholders at all levels to ensure effective coordination and service delivery.
Disaster Risk Reduction is a key element of the proposed project given the locations and both natural and man-made disasters experienced frequently in these areas. South Sudan continues to face extremely high prevalence of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and we will use the findings from the GBV RM research to plan our interventions. Action Against Hunger proposes to a) continue its efforts to mainstream GBV and gender considerations in its Nutrition, WASH and health programs, and b) support the humanitarian community in South Sudan to remain accountable for GBV and gender-sensitivity. Action Against Hunger will strengthen nutrition surveillance mechanisms through accurate and timely reporting, conducting SMART surveys and monitoring and analysis of data for early and objective decision-making.
We will hold regular community meetings at project site including local leaders during planning and implementation of the project activities; to discuss about the types of services available; feedback mechanism in place and how they can approach our teams on ground to provide their feedback on the services on regular basis. All the modalities of the feedback mechanism implemented will be introduced to the community members to enable them to choose the modality. All feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project. Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our nutrition program and will be conducted using the tool provided by the nutrition cluster. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specially asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements.
To mitigate risk of SEA and GBV committed by and/or against staff and our beneficiaries, Action Against Hunger staff are bound to our Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero-tolerance approach to such cases.

1. Needs

According to the SMART Survey conducted in Gogrial West County in July 2023, the prevalence of GAM was 26.9%, which is the highest prevalence of malnutrition in the last five (5) years in the county. About 76.7% and 55.5% of the children received Vitamin A supplements and deworming tablets in the last 6 months respectively. About 98.3% of the children 0-23 months of age were breastfed and early initiation of breastfeeding was at 93.3%. Mothers who practiced exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) was at 96.5%. Introduction of solid and semi solid foods was at 77.3%.In Fangak County, the prevalence of GAM was 18.8% and SAM by weight-for-height (z scores) were 3.8%. About 66.3% and 60.8% of the children received Vitamin A supplements and deworming tablets in the last 6 months to the survey. All the children 0-23 months of age were breastfed and immediately breastfed according to their mothers or caregivers. Introduction of CF was 33.3% while 66.7% of the mothers have not introduced their children to complementary foods. In Aweil East County, the prevalence of GAM was 20.5 % (16.9 - 24.7 95% C.I.) while the overall prevalence of (SAM by weight-for-height z scores was 5.3 % (3.4 - 8.2 95% C.I.). About 72.0% and 68.1% of the children received Vitamin A supplements and deworming tablets in the last 6 months. About 89.3% of the infants were initiated immediately to breastmilk after birth and 97.7% of the children were exclusively breastfed for the first 6 months; 63.8% of the mothers introduced their infants to solid and semi-solid foods at 6 months, Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) for infant was 49.4% while Minimum Meal Frequency (MMF) was 41.6%.The latest IPC data shows that 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). . An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Jonglei state (61%) has been identified as one of the most food insecure states between September and November 2023 with more than 50 percent of their populations facing IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse).An estimated 1.65 million children 6 – 59 months are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and are in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2023 to June 2024. The total number of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 0.48 million and 1.16 million respectively. During the post-harvest period of October 2023 to March 2024, the acute malnutrition situation is expected to improve in 62 counties, remain similar in 12 counties, and expected to deteriorate in 6 counties. Despite these changes in the nutrition situation, the IPC AMN classification in 75 out of 80 is projected to remain the same, expected to improve in 3 counties including Aweil center from phase 4 to 3 each), and expected to deteriorate in 2 counties. Whereas, during the lean season period of April to June 2024, the severity of acute malnutrition is likely to deteriorate in 66 counties and remain similar in 14 counties.Contributing factors of acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases with fever, cough and diarrhea as the leading symptoms reported at 42.4%, 25.4% and 18.7% respectively; poor access to improved drinking water (58.4%) and inadequate feeding practices. To reduce the prevalence of malnutrition in CU5 and PLW, there is a strong need to strengthen the coordination with MoH at all levels, local authorities on ground right from planning upto implementation and monitoring. In 2024, the project will be part of the transition strategy where the implementation modality will be based on the category allocated to each county.

2. Project activities

Action Against Hunger will continue nutrition services in the catchment areas of Fangak, Duk Pagaak, Gogrial West, Kessengor (GPAA), Aweil East and Aweil Centre counties in line with the nutrition cluster strategic and sectoral objectives. The services will focus on both treatment and prevention of malnutrition in compliance with national guidelines. Key activities include: 1. Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. 2. Deliver blanket supplementary feeding programmes to vulnerable children. 3. Provide micronutrient supplements to young children and women. 4. Screen and treat girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by severe and moderate acute malnutrition. 5. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition. 6. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages. 7. Work with FSL Cluster to promote chicken raring, establish kitchen gardens and promote seed multiplication for vegetable gardens and support fish preservation. 8. Collaborate with health cluster to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea at the nutrition service delivery. 9. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites. 10. Link with Cash and Voucher Assistance programs to benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women. 11. Collaborate with health cluster in the management of diarrhoea, including providing Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhoea. 12. Undertake community engagement activities during the design, implementation, and monitoring of nutrition services to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations. 13. Promote school feeding among vulnerable communities and use schools to raise awareness on optimal nutrition practices. 14. Conduct regular program and field monitoring and supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making. 15. Conduct nutrition coordination meetings with the government, implementing partners, and donors.16. Innovative malnutrition prevention activities like:- Strengthening kitchen gardens with plantation of protein rich beans- Provision of eggs and milk to children under 2 and chickens to set up poultry to their families- Integrate Baby WASH in nutrition program for improved child development and uptake of best hygiene practices - Implement Nurturing Care Group at community level, with a focus on 1000 days and SBC lens for improved behaviour change for malnutrition prevention 17. Hold regular community meetings at project site including local leaders during planning and implementation of the project activities; to discuss about the types of services available; feedback mechanism in place and how they can approach our teams on ground to provide their feedback on the services on regular basis. All feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project. 18. Additionally Safety Audits will be conducted using the tool provided by the nutrition cluster to identify the potential GBV risks and plan for risk mitigation activities. The findings from the GBV Risk Mitigation research being carried out in collaboration with Ministry of Health, Ministry of GoCSW, UNICEF, Kenyatta University (academic partner) and national GBV service providers TOCH and AIRD, will provide way forward to make the nutrition program safer and more accessible for women and children.
