Project Summary

The project aims to address urgent challenges faced by internally displaced persons, returnees, and host communities in South Sudan, focusing on women and girls. It will operate in Fangak, Ayod, Uror, Canal/Pigi, Akobo, Nasir/Luakpiny, Panyikang, and Twic counties across Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Warrap States. These areas have been severely impacted by flooding, inter-communal violence, and protection issues.
The initiative intends to provide integrated life-saving protection services that prioritize assistance to affected populations, responses to gender-based violence (GBV), and support for sexual and reproductive health services, strongly emphasizing disability inclusion. This project will build upon existing programs funded by various donors.
A strong focus is on scaling up psychosocial support activities in temporary learning sites to build resilience among affected children. The project aims to encourage community-led innovations that promote ownership and sustainability in prevention efforts.
Furthermore, the organization acknowledges the urgent need for gender-based violence prevention and risk mitigation measures to assist survivors. They plan to implement primary GBV prevention programs, such as Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practice (EMAP) and Women’s Economic and Social Empowerment (EAE), to combat harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality. These efforts will be complemented by initiatives such as distributing dignity kits and safety audits to mitigate GBV risks. The project encompasses various thematic areas, including protection assistance, addressing child rights violations, mental health support, cash assistance, and a comprehensive response to GBV.
The overarching goal is to reach 55,000 beneficiaries ( 25,000 for Protection and 10,000 GBV), particularly those most affected, through initiatives designed to strengthen community resilience and empower women and girls as vital contributors to development. Monthly assessments will be conducted to identify gaps in protection responses, ensuring that findings inform ongoing efforts. Survivors of GBV will receive referrals and psychosocial support, which will be integrated into broader community projects related to education, health, nutrition, and WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene).
An updated database will track individuals with specific needs, while regular monitoring will evaluate the project’s impact. Community outreach and recreational activities will be organized to promote peaceful coexistence, fostering networks and inclusivity. Additionally, detailed assessments of the needs of internally displaced persons, returnees, host communities, and other vulnerable groups affected by severe floods will guide the allocation of relief items, reinforcing community ties through consultative surveys and peace-building discussions across various age groups.

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing numerous significant challenges that heighten the risks to its population's protection. Among the pressing issues are gender-based violence, forced evictions, child marriage, family separations, and unequal access to resources. The situation is further worsened by alarming attacks on civilians, restrictions on freedom of movement, severe flooding, hyperinflation, and the ongoing impacts of the crisis in Sudan, all of which have contributed to widespread displacement. Vulnerable groups, particularly children, survivors of gender-based violence, and conflict victims, are experiencing the most drastic deterioration in their health and living conditions. In 2024, humanitarian assessments conducted by the Protection Cluster in South Sudan revealed that around 11.29 million individuals are affected by the crisis, with 9.18 million—81% of those affected—at risk of encountering at least one of the five main protection threats. Returnees, numbering approximately 840,000, are identified as the most vulnerable, closely followed by internally displaced persons (IDPs), 81% of whom are also at significant risk. The violations of children's rights are of particular concern, as many children face abduction, family separation, and a range of abuses and exploitation amidst the influx of returnees and IDPs from Sudan. Nearly 3.7 million children, adolescents, and caregivers are at heightened risk of violence and exploitation, highlighting the urgent need for strong child protection services. The situation for women and children is alarming, especially given the high prevalence of sexual and gender-based violence in communities with many female-headed households. These women and girls often face intimidation when trying to report crimes, with challenges further compounded by ineffective judicial systems and deep-seated cultural biases. With the rising number of unaccompanied and separated children, there is an urgent need for life-saving child protection services tailored to their specific needs. Integrating gender-sensitive and protective support services into education, mental health, and reproductive health initiatives is essential. The Community Action Organization (CAO) plans to enhance its comprehensive case management services aimed at vulnerable children, focusing on family reunification and alternative care while also strengthening community-based reintegration efforts for children released from the armed forces. By establishing referral pathways and deploying mobile support teams, CAO aims to improve responses to serious violations against children and ensure the safety and well-being of the most at-risk populations in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

Protection: Activity 1: Create cohesive key messages based on insights from protection monitoring analysis and develop awareness-raising materials in collaboration with affected communities and protection partners. Activity 2: CAO will carry out community Assessment activities to identify and refer individuals with specific needs (PSNs). Activity 3: CAO will establish or strengthen community-based structures while implementing alternative activities to displacement, working closely with protection partners and the affected communities. Activity 4: Collaborating with protection partners, development and peace actors, authorities, and existing community structures can strengthen community resilience, promote PSEA, and facilitate peace-building initiatives and training through the AGD approach. Gender-Based Violence: Activity 10: To enhance awareness of gender-based violence (GBV), we will implement visits, interventions, and campaigns and leverage mass media. CAO aims to strengthen primary prevention efforts against GBV. Activity 11: Our strategy includes enhancing specialized GBV response services and referral pathways through collaboration with our GBV partners. Additionally, we will bolster the provision of psychosocial support by building capacity and working closely with the Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) working group. Activity 12: We seek to improve the reach and distribution of dignity kits by partnering with the Cash Working Group and integrating cash and voucher assistance into GBV programming. Activity 13: Furthermore, we plan to expand access to fuel-efficient stoves, revitalize the GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR) Fuel-Efficient Stove Task Force, and increase the availability of safe spaces for women and girls. Activity 14: CAO will conduct service mapping to strengthen GBV referral pathways and establish or update Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for GBV. Lastly, it will provide training to build capacity for GBV integration within sector responses.
