Project Summary

Populations affected by displacement in conflict areas in the, NW/SW regions are lacking shelter and NFI assistance to improve their physical protection during displacement or return. The NRC programme will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, households who need shelter assistance to improve their living conditions in the project implementation locations. Particular attention will be given to households that meet the following vulnerability criteria:
• IDPs and returness living in makeshift camps with shelters exposing them to bad weather;
• IDPs and returness living in host communities where they scramble for the very limited space available and share sleeping spaces;
• IDPs, Primary, Secondary Multiple Displacements and vulnerable Host community members
• Returnees (former IDPs), where houses have been vandalised or destroyed due to the crisis;
• Schools in conflict affected areas that are used as collective housing by the displaced communities.
Target group
Beneficiary targeting will also aim at reaching the most vulnerable households based on agreed upon selection criteria. Priority will be given to female headed households (HHs), unaccompanied minors and child headed HHs, and HHs with disabled members. Having clear beneficiary selection criteria will ensure that deserving beneficiaries have equitable access to NRC’s shelter assistance, and hence mitigate the risk of tensions if the assistance is solely addressed to a particular group.
The Shelter response in the South West, North West, Littoral and West regions aims to reach a total of 11,600 Households benefitting directly, some households will benefit from other different services at the same time hence the house holds beneficiaries will also have a double counting. While 11,600 households will benefit from this project, some activities will be double counted. In total, the direct beneficiaries with double counting will be 69,600 direct beneficiaries while those without double counting will be 6,000 households representing a total of 36,000(17,640 Male headed 18,360 Female headed) direct beneficiaries)

1 - Besoins / Needs

Shelter and NFI The shelter infrastructure in the North West, South West, West and Littoral region remains insufficient to meet the needs of the displaced populations (IDPs and returnees) due to either having been destroyed during conflict or poorly maintained. Majority of the affected population do not have legal documents (with majority of the affected population having lost their documents when fleeing) proving that they are the owners of these properties. This makes it difficult for them to claim ownership to the property and thus they find themselves unjustly thrown out of their properties. This is particularly the case for women. The required assistance ranges from immediate life-saving to protracted recovery. Increasing forced displacement has contributed to the weakening of family and community safety nets, the overuse of limited basic resources and services and to an increase in social tension. Older people, people living with disabilities, with chronical illness and pregnant and lactating women, are often left behind when families flee violence, increasing their vulnerabilities and depriving them of the social safety net on which their survival and wellbeing depend. Those left behind often remain in areas with no access to basic social services and unsafe for humanitarian workers to reach . The humanitarian needs in the NW, SW, regions of Cameroon are concentrated in divisions such as Fako, Meme, Ndian, Lebialem, Kupe Manenguba and Manyu Divisions in the SW region and Mezam, Ngoketunjia, Momo, Bui, Menchum, Donga Mantung and Boyo Division, in the NW region. NRC will be moving into additional regions that include Littoral and West Region. The main subdivisions are already characterised by poor living conditions, especially for the population that has fled to the bush. The instability in the area has affected timely humanitarian interventions especially where access to IDP sites in the bush is hampered by different security developments which remain harsh and unpredictable. The people affected by the North West and South West crisis translate to a total of 2.2 million persons with 1.6 million persons targeted for assistance. The two regions reported a total of 573.9K IDPs within or displaced from NWSW: while 383.6K Returnees (former IDP) in NWSW were reported as at end of September 2021 . These returnees are typically those who were displaced into the bush after localized conflict and return soon after, constituting of a regular pendular migration pattern. A majority of the displaced persons are women and children amongst whom are unaccompanied and separated children. People in rural and hard-to-reach areas are most affected because of limited or no access to medical, shelter, nutrition and WASH facilities and limited access to farmland due to insecurity. The locations where the IDPs have fled for safety include areas where they are living with relatives and friends in homes whose space is insufficient to provide the needed separation and privacy between the different ages and sex. In some situations, it is still common to find a home hosting almost 22 persons as depicted by NRC’s profiling data. The situation is reported to be worse in the bush where the IDPs sleep on the bare ground with no privacy and are exposed to harsh weather conditions and protection issues. Their vulnerability is further compounded by the lack of adequate household items, and the use of the provided plastic sheets (tarpaulins) to act as walls, which can easily be cut to allow unwanted intrusions. Besides, the plastic sheeting requires replacement after every few months as it is easily degraded by the hot climate. Inadequate lighting at night coupled with weak doors and windows and wall cladding materials for the IDP shelters, increases the risk of theft, night attacks and protection concerns.
