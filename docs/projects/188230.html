Project Summary

The project is divided into two main components:
first. Support SC/OTP/TSFP services in five fixed health facilities :
This is to ensure continuity and increased coverage of services for treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) among children aged 0-59 months; and to maintain continuity and increased coverage of services for management of acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women (PLW).
Second: 15 RRTs Approach:
- CMAM & IYCF programs :
1.Screening, treatment, referral and follow up children 6-59, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished
2.Provide emergency IYCF counselling for mothers with moderately malnourished children
3.Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women
4.Disseminate messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices,
5.Monitor distribution and use of breast milk substitutes
6.Support health/nutrition education and promotion through IEC materials in line with cluster recommendations during COVID 19 pandemic,
7.Nutrition Supplements distribution
8.Crafting innovative approaches to nutritional counselling, including supporting mothers in breastfeeding infants, through telehealth and mobile phone services;
9.Integrating nutrition supplementation with other routine child and antenatal services.
third. Capacity building
the employees who received the necessary training will be more able to perform in their jobs.
Most of the health and nutrition workers had some weaknesses in their technical skills.
Those training programs will be a good opportunity for them to strengthen those skills and refresh their information the thing will reflect on the quality of the services delivered in the field of IYCF & CMAM.
In coordination with the nutrition cluster and Physician Across Continent (PAC) and according to the inventories and need, AFAQ will receive the nutrition supplies through PAC by its quarterly forecast. Supplies requests will be shared with UNICEF, cleared technically by UNICEF nutrition team leader and approved by the head of the office. AFAQ will ensure their warehouses' locations are shared and subject to Third Party Monitoring (TPM) by UNICEF. All nutrition will be supplies stored away from other non-food commodities and hazardous chemicals. Nutrition supplies will be transported and distributed according to standardized measures that AFAQ has developed; also, mitigation measures will be place to avoid any losses or risks that might happen during the project period; however, in case of any supply losses, AFAQ will send an incident report for any supply loss ,diversion or misuse to UNICEF within 24 hours using UNICEF standard loss report template.

4 - Needs

The Syrian conflict has created one of the most severe and protracted humanitarian crises in the world; millions have been displaced both inside and outside the country. Entering the tenth year of the crisis, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. 13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance, of these, 6.76 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to essential goods and services.(HNO Syria Arab republic 2022). 2.8 million people are internally displaced persons (IDPs) and 1.72 million of these IDPs are living in displacement sites. (OCHA-Situation Report No. 31 – Sept 2021).The Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) database highlights 1,400 IDP sites that host 1,721,579 IDPs or 323,458 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 37,670 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs (Integrated Monitoring Matrix "ISIMM August 2021").According to the SMART survey report conducted in June 2021 in NWS, the Prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) (WFH <-2 z-score and/or edema) among children aged 6-59 months was 2.5%. The prevalence of GAM by (MUAC) was 4.7%.The result shows significant increase on GAM rate when compare to 2019 SMART survey the GAM was 0.9% (<125mm and/or oedema), and 0.73% (WFH <-2 z-score and/or edema).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

AFAQ coordinated with Nutrition cluster and humanitarian actors to determine the gaps and selected the in-need communities for intervening. AFAQ will assess the potential risks of women, girls, boys and men and people with disabilities to ensure that nutrition services are conducted with no harm. AFAQ is integrating protection mechanisms to understand the needs of community for targeting them through: Delivering services impartially to ensure reaching to all in-need BNFs without discrimination, with prioritizing the safety and dignity of BNFs and avoid harm.Identification of suitable BFSs, times and dates, considering needs of female and children. And secure BFSs like safe locations and movements. Hiring the majority of staff from females. Collecting and monitoring of data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Monitoring and overseeing potential barriers to accessing the Nutrition services. Conducting exit interviews to adapt the counseling and preventative nutrition services to respond the need.AFAQ will develop a service map that identifies all Protection service providers within the operational areas.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AFAQ have PSEA, Code of Conduct, Protection and Child Safeguarding policies and mechanisms to ensure every effort is made to protect all project participants. AFAQ’s PSEA Mechanism is designed around four core components: Awareness Raising, Prevention, Reporting and Response. All AFAQ staff must attend training about PSEA and code of conduct before start working.Approach to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which includes mitigating activities are , including but not limited to the implementation of PSEA policy, standards of behavior for organization’s staff, all implementing partners personnel and representatives, speciﬁcally forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures; complain PSEA mechanism and training activities for partners and staff of health facilities in NWS; PSEA needs assessments and surveysAFAQ will ensured equality and impartiality through the need and vulnerability assessment. where the IHR met community members of women, men, disabled, elders, and young through KII and FGDs modality to reach for actual needs. it aimed to participate the stakeholders in making decision. Also, during beneficiaries’ selection, the priority will be given to the most vulnerable groups including, women, Girls, Covid 19 patients, elderly people, GBV survivors, and people with disabilities.AFAQ will coordinate with local councils to prevent any interfering may affect BNFs selection or their access to provided services.
