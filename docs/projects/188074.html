Project Summary

Syria has passed a decade of devastating conflict that negatively impacts the situation in the Northern provinces and in particular the health system. Currently, a third of the health facilities are not fully functional and the health infrastructure has not been recovered to support the overwhelmed services. In addition, the COVID19 pandemic has further amplified the dire needs in the health sector and increased the load on the existing services in each wave of COVID19 cases.
THE OBJECTIVES:
- Decease the morbidity and mortality among the highest risk and vulnerable groups in the NWS.
THE PROJECTS OUTPUTS:
(1) Provision of blood bank service including donor recruiting and distribution. (scaling up the capacity)
(2) Provision of primary healthcare services. (improving the quality and switch to a fix-model facility)
(3) Provision of secondary healthcare services; surgical orthopaedic hospital
(4) provision of community COVID19 treatment services (part of COVID19 CM)
BENEFICIARIES:
This project plans to reach about 2.5 Million indirect beneficiaries (mostly from the high priority areas in Idleb and Aleppo). At least 40% out of direct beneficiaries will be women from the age of 20-50 and 7- 11% will be people with disability. These indirect beneficiaries will be reached by supporting primary healthcare, secondary health care, and supplying blood products to fill the gaps for the general hospitals, trauma hospitals, maternal hospitals, Thalassemia-NCD.
THE OUTCOMES:
(1) direct addressing of one of the top causes of maternal death in the NWS and one of the top 5 causes in trauma patients death.
(2) decrease untested blood transfusion done in the emergency because of the lack of central testing and availability of blood unit in the hospitals, thus decreasing the transfusion-transmitted infection (TTI) rate bearing in mind the increasing of HBV, HBC infection rate (based on WATAN lab service)
(3) increase the capacity of the existing blood banks by 2 folds to fulfil the gaps in Idleb.
(4) decrease the disability by providing specialized orthopaedic surgical care that will reduce the duration of limb fixation and provision of limb-saving surgery.
(5) compliment the essential primary healthcare services in Azaz and Afrin including RH, Dental, child care, community outreach.
(6) increase the preparedness and responsiveness to the COVID19 pandemic in the NWS.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk Analysis (PRA): • Protection risk 1: There is a risk of gender-based violence among the affected population which can become clear during the medical consultations. The rate is anticipated to be high in camp communities (10 camps are near the hospital) due to the overcrowding and lack of personal space. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High Mitigation: 1) increase the awareness and understanding of GBV case management among the medical staff through GBV training. 2) Ensure that social workers in the hospital know how to manage cases of GBV and how to link it to more specialized protection services. 3) Violence cases must be reported to the hospital manager with respect to the confidentiality of the personal information of the patient. A reporting mechanism between the hospital management and staff and WATAN will be established, to report on any occurring cases that indicate further protection needs. • Protection risk 2: There is a risk of a lack of gender awareness among the health staff in the hospital and the hospital not being a gender-sensitive and family-/women-friendly workplace which in turn can affect the retention of female staff and hence lead to not being able to ensure beneficiaries' culturally appropriate examination by female practitioners or under the presence of female nurses. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High Mitigation: 1) Ensure gender balance among the newly hired staff. 2) ensure separate rooms for the female staff


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Network and commits to protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse, in compliance with PSEA standard policy and procedures. New staff recruited will have to sign the new version of CoC released by the PSEA network recently and will be fully oriented about PSEA and the available complaint mechanism (also uploaded). Awareness-raising of the community about SEA will be a part of activities with the full illustration of available complaint mechanisms. PSEA focal points are responsible for mainstreaming PSEA into all departments and projects, through developing the measures and practices including feedback and compliant mechanism along with concerned departments. Posters and Banners raising awareness about PSAE will be used in all facilities. Videos released by the network will be constantly shown on partners' private social media (Facebook, Instagram, ..).New modalities of PSEA raising awareness - due to the COVID-19 crisis are followed, such as sending text messages and videos to the beneficiaries, online training, using Banners in the distribution locations will be also taken into consideration. The project ensures the community and beneficiaries' engagement in project designing, and planning since the beginning through the needs assessments and so on during the next phases, and through targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities including life skills. There will be multiple channels to report feedback and complaints privately.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

increasing of COVID19 cases: Mitigation: 1) IPC training, 2) PPE, 3) encourage the staff for vaccination 4)implement cluster recommendation for patients flow, 5) for the hospital, reassign the oxygen generator of WATAN to be the backup line of oxygen supply to overcome any shortage in oxygen supplies in the ORs. Diversion of humanitarian aid: Mitigation: higher commitment to humanitarian principles, through the awareness campaigns and workshops that WATAN holds frequently. Effective MEAL department procedures activating monitoring mechanisms to preserve the humanitarian aid within its frame. Risk of security incidences: Mitigation: 1) security team to keep updating and tracking any security threat in the area and deconflict the locations. 2) develop an evacuation plan. Risk of loss of assets and medical supplies: the risk owner: the facility manager in each site Mitigation: Monthly inventors, daily consumption tracking for the medical supplies, segregation of the duties in the medical supply management (between the logistic team and program team). The SOP of pharma management is attached. Risk of . Risk of transfusion-transmitted infection TTI (esp for blood banks): Mitigation: (1) 100% lab testing for all blood units (2) tracking the units through all phases.
