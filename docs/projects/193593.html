Project Summary

The overarching objective is to provide time-critical and quick-impact support to the vulnerable farming and herding households affected by man-made and natural shocks, including the COVID-19 pandemic and the global supply shocks induced by local and global crises such as the Russia-Ukraine war. Special focus will be placed on women and youth, and the integration of women in the decision-making process throughout the project. The project aims at ensuring the resilience and protection of the targeted communities and reviving their livelihoods, affected by the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, the May 2022 Gaza escalations and the food security impacts of the local and global supply shocks. This project falls under HRP 2023 Strategic Objective 3 and the Food Security Sector SO 1: “The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced" of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2022, as it improves the coping abilities and reduces shocks and environmental hazards of farmers and herders, with special focus on women and youth in those communities.
This project ensures accessibility and inclusion of all stakeholders regardless of their background, geographical location, or social status. A clear Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) confidential reporting process will be distributed and explained to all stakeholders, including beneficiaries and partners.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity in the WBGS is chronic and continues to be affected by prolonged conflict, economic stagnation, restrictions on trade and access to resources, and high unemployment and poverty rates (HNO, 2023). Multiple challenges threaten the resilience and livelihoods of herding and farming households (HHs), including uneven COVID-19 economic recovery and the widespread supply chain disruption caused by the Russia-Ukraine crisis. The consequences of global price increases are evident in the high and volatile price levels in Palestine. In mid-May 2022, importers reported availability of inputs stocks in the local market; however, compared to earlier this year, prices have increased for wheat (+73%), sunflower oil (+50%), barley (+26%), wheat bran (+39%), maize (+26%), dairy fodder mix (+23%), layers fodder mix (+22%), broilers mix (+26%), ammoniac (+7%) and NPK 13-13-13 (+20%). In the WB, 30% of herding and farming HHs (32% female-headed) face moderate to severe food insecurity (MSNA, 2022). These households face restrictions that continue adversely impacting their livelihoods such as lack of access to land and resources, illegal settlements and outposts, closed military zones and checkpoints obstructing their freedom of movement, frequent harassment and violence from settlers and demolition threats with more than 220 agricultural structures demolished up to September 2022. In 2022, up to 47% of these households (44% female-headed) reported decreased incomes, 16% reported resorting to life savings, 5% sold household assets and 4% sold productive assets to cope with the lack of resources to satisfy their basic needs (MSNA, 2022). Recurrent hostilities in GS further reduce the profitability and sustainability of herding and farming HHs agricultural activities. According to MoA estimates, the August 2022 escalations hit agricultural lands, greenhouses, livestock and poultry farms, resulting in $1m of damages and losses. In 2022, 60% of farming and herding HHs reported decreased incomes, 17% reported resorting to life savings, 7% sold HH assets and 4% sold livelihood productive assets to cope. Alarmingly, these HHs reported spending as much as 56% of total expenditures on food (58% for female-headed HHs), a level that categorizes these HHs as most vulnerable segments of the population (MSNA, 2022). Across the WBGS, women and men herders and farmers have been heavily impacted by disrupted global supply chains and resulting soaring and unpredictable costs and limited availability of staple foods and key production inputs, including animal fodder, seeds, and fertilizers. For example, women and men herders reported that fodder was available but at considerably higher prices than pre-COVID19 levels. The importance of small ruminants is indicated by their various productive contributions, socio-economic relevance, and stability to farming systems. Milk and dairy products majorly contribute to herders’ diets, income and overall HH livelihood and food security. Small producers (1-35 heads) account for some 60-65% of total holders. Producers with less than 5 animals account for 15% of total goat holdings, and those with less than 10 animals account for 26% of all sheep holdings. These herds are typically managed by family members, particularly women, who play a vital role in maintaining and increasing the food security of rural HHs. In HHs with small numbers of livestock, women are responsible for most activities, including collecting wood for heating and cooking, taking care of the livestock (herding, feeding, fetching water, maintaining a clean shelter), milking them, processing and marketing the dairy products. Hence, empowering herding and farming communities retains significant economic, dietary, cultural and gender inclusion importance. This project aims at protecting the endangered livelihoods of those women and men farmers and herders across the WBGS by building capacities to cope with global supply and environmental shocks through restoring and enhancing local production of animal fodder, cereals, legumes and sustainable agricultural inputs as well as immediate rehabilitation and provision of productive assets, in-kind and technical support. The project aims to target around 5 100 small scale female and male herding households through (i) provision of seeds of drought-tolerant fodder crops; (ii) provision of farm management input toolkits; (iii) provision of essential dairy processing and poultry farm equipment; and (iv) rehabilitation of animal sheds. The project also aims to target 2 300 small scale female and male farming households including 30 farmer and women cooperatives (700 women, 500 men, and 1000 youth members) through provision of in-kind and technical support to restore the cultivation of cereals and legumes in underutilized farm and restore local production of sustainable agricultural inputs such as organic fertilizers, seeds, seedlings, tubers as well as rehabilitation of rainwater-harvesting cisterns to restore the access of farming households to essential natural resources by improving the availability and access to water. The overall target will be 7 400 vulnerable small-scale female and male farming and herding households (38 150 individuals including 10 337 women, 10 478 men, 15 805 children, 1 530 elderly) and 30 farmer and women cooperatives (700 women, 500 men, and 1000 youth members) in the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

2 - Activities

The proposed interventions will systematically consider Accountability to Affected Population and PSEA standards. The selection of beneficiaries will consider concrete criteria to ensure prioritizing those who are in most need as well as the value and sustainability of the support for the resilience of the production activities and livelihoods of the families. This will also be in coordination with the relevant MoA units. FAO ensures that women-headed households are prioritized in all interventions and along the project cycle. Herding and farming communities will be informed about the project objectives, intended support, target groups and selection criteria through information sheets that will be made available to all community members including the elderly, people with disabilities, women and children. The sheets will include information on FAO grievance mechanism and channels of communications including for submitting PSEA-related complaints. The project plans to deliver these outputs and activities: Output 1: Protecting the productive capacities and livelihood resilience of vulnerable herding households against environmental shocks and consequences of local and global crises 1.1 Supporting 2000 vulnerable female and male herding HH in coordination with the MoA to protect and restore local fodder crops production by providing seeds of drought-tolerant fodder crops (for around 20,000 dunums) in WB. This will include tailored technical support to the targeted communities. Up-to-date animal fodder production knowledge and good practices will be introduced through technical extension delivery, piloting good practices and technologies and exchanging knowledge and experience using the Farmer Field School (FFS) approach which brings together a group of farmers or livestock herders, to learn on how to shift towards more sustainable production practices, by better understanding complex agro-ecosystems and by enhancing ecosystem services. Location: Eastern slopes of Yatta, Dura, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus and Tubas1.2 Providing farm management input toolkits (e.g. disinfection preventive material, salt blocks, wool and hoof secateurs) to 200 vulnerable herding HH in WB and 200 HH in GS. This will help prevent animal diseases and support animal well-being thereby reducing women’s care burden. Location: Eastern slopes of Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin and Tubas in the WB; Khan Younis and Rafah in the GS.1.3 Providing essential dairy processing equipment (e.g. milk jars, milk shakers, cheese tanks) to 600 HH in WB. As women are the main contributors to dairy product production, this intervention will help restore women’s productive capacities by improving the hygiene, safety and efficiency of their working conditions saving them time and effort. Location: Eastern slopes of Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin and Tubas1.4 Rehabilitating 1500 animal sheds (1000 in WB; 500 in GS) using the Build Back Better (BBB) approach to help restore the productive capacities of vulnerable herding HHs by protecting animal health and enabling easier barn cleaning. Location": Eastern slopes of Yatta, Dura, Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus, Jenin and Tubas in the WB; Khan Younis, North Gaza, Gaza, Middle Area and Rafah in the GS.1.5 Supporting 600 vulnerable poultry farms in GS with water sprinkler networks and small water pumps to help restore access to natural resources for vulnerable HH while building their resilience against climate change effects. Location: Khan Younis, North Gaza, Gaza, Middle Area and Rafah.Output 2: Protecting the productive capacities and livelihood resilience of vulnerable farming households against environmental shocks and consequences of local and global crises 2.1 Supporting 2000 vulnerable female and male farmers to grow 8000 dunums of underutilized areas for cereals and legumes production. This will include providing in-kind and technical support to restore the cultivation of cereals and legumes in underutilized farms, including intercropping in olive orchards. The FAO Direct Beneficiary Grants modality will be used to increase efficiency and ownership and contribute to the activation of local economies in the targeted areas. Location: Jenin: Araaba, Salfeet; Tubas, Dura and West Hebron governorate and Bethlehem At the inception phase of project implementation, awareness raising and community mobilization workshops will be held in the targeted communities for all population groups regarding PSEA-related issues and communication networks (indicator 6.1). 3 - Indicators

Throughout the selection process, FAO will target the most vulnerable and at-risk HHs affected by global supply shocks. Women-headed HHs, women’s cooperatives and HHs whose female members are actively engaged in agriculture-related activities in support of household income will be prioritized during the community targeting and beneficiary selection processes. Similarly, beneficiaries with disabilities and older people will be given priority during the selection process. FAO will be working with local partners, who will be selected at a later stage during the inception phase. The local partners will play an important role in the following activities: 1- Support in the beneficiary selection process,2- Support in distributing the inputs to beneficiaries, 3- Support in providing training. Each indicator will be gender-disaggregated. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018-2022. Participation of women will be closely monitored, and tailored adjustments will be made to ensure effective participation of women. Post-delivery monitoring will be conducted by interviewing a sample of female and male beneficiaries in order to monitor the use of the inputs distributed, prospects for sustainability of the intervention as well as the overall satisfaction of beneficiaries as part of FAO’s accountability to beneficiaries. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow-up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until the final decision and the subsequent follow-up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific timeframes, including preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution, and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post-delivery monitoring as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant. All project activities and results will be coordinated with the FSS to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned will be shared with FSS stakeholders. In addition, FAO will maintain coordination with the protection cluster and the Humanitarian Country Team in order to ensure alignment with and support of advocacy initiatives. All monitoring, reviews and reporting will be based on SMART and gender, and age sensitive indicators and targets ensuring accountability and coherence with gender mainstreaming throughout the project. Mainstreaming gender, age and environment within FAO’s programming is based on socio-economic/environmental assessments and analysis, utilizing a range of tools and analytical modules that are tailored to the WBGS context. FAO’s gender focal point, supported by FAO’s gender committee will play a key role in assuring that women and men associated with the project have equal opportunities to participate in and benefit from the project’s activities at all levels.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

FAO will use in-kind modality along with technical support for all activities under output 1 as herders depend on seasonal products, meaning they usually wait for the season to generate money and for that they can't buy the needed items in time. Hence, FAO will provide in-kind support through inputs distribution in order to have the material in time to improve their productivity. In addition, FAO will use the conditional cash modality for two activities (linked to indicator 5.2); the cistern rehabilitation (activity 2.3) and supporting farmers to grow underutilized areas for cereals and legumes production (activity 2.1). A call for application is announced at the locality level in coordination with the MoA for small-scale farmers to apply for each activity. The applications are assessed against the specific criteria identified at inception phase including the vulnerability criteria as well as prioritizing women and persons with disability. For the cistern intervention, there is a high need for water in distant areas and contractors do not easily reach these communities. Hence, FAO will be using the cash modality to rehabilitate the cisterns. For the farmer support, this modality will be used to increase efficiency and ownership and contribute to the activation of local economies in the targeted areas FAO together with a representative of the MoA and the local partner will visit the locations and estimate the required support needed. The beneficiaries will be given the cash amount in installments linked to achieving specific milestones. Once a milestone is achieved, a field visit is organized to ensure the successful completion and following that, part of the payment linked to this specific milestone will be released.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable women and men herders and farmers whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers and herder's is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries at risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered livelihoods of vulnerable female and male farmers and herders in the Gaza Strip by securing the availability of time-critical and dispensable inputs and services, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries. Priority whenever possible, will be given to vulnerable marginalized groups including women, people with disabilities, and older people. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during the implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from a further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with vulnerabilities will be prioritized. This is done through the following:• Formulation of local committee: a committee presenting all the CBO’s (women, men, youth), MoA and village councils, and municipalities; to ensure the social and technical criteria.• Announcement at community level for beneficiaries to apply for the project: for two weeks announcements are made at community events (such as after Friday prayers) and notices published in widely visible sources (posters in the market, local shops, women association center, health center and village council building etc). These announcements contain information about the project, including its location, methods and the selection criteria for beneficiaries, who are divided into two types: socio-economic and technical. The former includes household income, the number of household members and that the household is female-headed. In our experience, women who are the sole providers for their households stand to benefit more from the skills and income which an FAO project will provide than women in male-led households, even if such women meet (and even exceed) the other requirements. • Sites visit to assess potential beneficiaries The project also project seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
