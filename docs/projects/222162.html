Project Summary

Despite of comprehensive peace agreement signed in Khartoum South Sudan has been afflicted by ongoing conflict, emergent crises, and harsh environmental conditions notably seasonal flooding. These have contributed to weakening of the health system over the years resulting in reduced access to and utilization of quality primary health care services. South Sudan has experienced some of the worst indicators globally including a maternal mortality rate of 789 per 100,000 live births. The most vulnerable groups in the society including women and girls, children under five years of age, people living with disability (PWDs), mental health illness, and the elderly among other vulnerable groups continue to be among the most affected. The proposed project targets 33,661 people in need as follows in Payinjiar, 10149 (under 5 years 1522 and above 5 years 8,627) in Leer county 5,003 (under 5 762 and above 4,241) in Longechuk,6765 (under years 1004 and above 5761) in Malakal 6765 (under5 years 1004 and above 5761) and in Maiwut County 5074 (under5 years 761 and above 4,313) and 150 people with disabilities. The proposed project will target 1 static health facility in Majak PHCUs and mobile clinics supported. SSAID is also currently running one PHCU through SSHF funding and mobile clinic response through Vitol Foundation for the people affected by flooding. In leer, the project will target Gardor PHCU in Leer County, in Malakal 1 mobile clinic, one mobile in Longechuk and one mobile in Maiwut. The project will operate mobile clinics and static health units which will offer gap filling services in villages which are cut off from essential health services by seasonal flooding and the swamps. Currently SSAID will leverage on existing linkages to support clients accessing services in supported health through mobile clinic as result of flood response to reduce missed opportunities and ensure holistic care. Referral for tertiary level health care will be supported using existing referral pathways through the County Health Departments. Vulnerable populations including un accompanied and separated children (UASC) will be linked to existing SSAID to mobile response activities. Gender, age and disability will be prioritized in our health response in coordination of other partners.
The SSAID through recruitment of qualified health workers in particular to support maternal and health services will result in improved maternal and child health outcomes. The SSAID will integrate and strengthen the prevention of mother to child transmission PMTCT of HIV/AIDS and screening of pregnant mothers of Syphilis during Antenatal visits. All complicated new cases identified will be referred for appropriate care at the PHCC. The project will leverage on existing SSAID projects in the counties covered to provide integrated holistic care for vulnerable populations including women and girls. SSAID screened positive children for acute malnutrition will be referred to IRC/IMC static and mobile nutrition sites for continuity of care whereas women and girls will be subjected to a voluntary GBV screening at the consultation clinics and referred to the nearest GBV one stop center for psychosocial support and other services. The support will further include training staff on IDSR, EWARS, IPC, Disability inclusion in health care and CCSAS training to ensure quality health services are provided. The project will support the procurement of drugs and medical supplies inclusive of psychotropic drugs for the treatment of mental health disorders. Regular supportive supervision will ensure quality of care is maintained by conducting regular QOC assessments. The proposed project will conduct a rapid WASH assessment of supported health facilities and through consultation with key stakeholders including the health committees, the project will seek to rehabilitate solid waste management facilities. Medical waste generated will be disposed according to internationally acceptable standards

1. Needs

The adverse effects of conflict, erratic weather patterns, lack of skilled and adequate personnel, issues with access continue to undermine efforts to attain acceptable standards in health service provision in Greater Upper Nile and Unity State. With a protraction of the humanitarian crisis, communities are stretched in their efforts to cope with emerging needs, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality. Since May 2020, heavy rainfall has affected parts of the country especially in Greater Unity and Upper Nile states affected by flooding and the flooding continue until now. This has exacerbated the acute food insecurity and continues to impact the humanitarian situation. 60% of the affected counties are currently classified as facing extreme levels of acute malnutrition. This has had an impact on access to clean water and sanitation with resultant heightened risk of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea and cholera. According to Integrated Food security phase classification (IPC), an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3+) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 2.22 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5).Panyijar and Leer Counties in Emergency (IPC phase 3+) and Critical (GAM prevalence of 15.0 – 29.9%, IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4).These continuous and emergent crises have contributed to SouthSudan’s poor health indicators including an immunization coverage rate of 48%, under five mortality rate is 90.7 per 1000live births and a maternal mortality rate of 789 deaths per 1000persons. Majority of the population (about 56%) reside more thana 5km walking distance from the nearest health facility. An estimated 46% of the women in South Sudan deliver at home without accessing skilled birth attendants, this is further coupled to inadequate qualified health care practitioners, inadequate access to supplies and medical equipment, poor health workers competencies and morale. Sociocultural barriers have also limited uptake and utilization of primary health care services. The revitalized agreement on the resolution of the conflict in the republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) and the tension in Sudan has resulted in increased returnees’ population. According to the IOMDTM April-September 2024, most of the returnees are women(60%) and <18 years (54%). This increase in affected population is likely to stretch the existing health systems in Panyijaar, Leer, Maiwut, Malakal and Longechuk Counties. August 2021, multi-multi-sectoral IRNA was conducted in Payinjiar County due to the seasonal flooding continues to compound humanitarian needs in South Sudan in which SSAID was part of the assessment and also an Initial Rapid Need Assessment was conducted in Longechuk County from 15-19 July, 2021 to assess the general humanitarian situation on ground and verify the presence of IDPs and returnee. OCHA estimated 1,042,000 people were affected by floods in eight out of ten states in South Sudan, between July2020 and December 2020. Coupled with the slow recession of the2020 floodwaters, and the onset of the recurrence seasonal rainfall Panyijiar county increased spontaneously with an estimated (52,654).

2. Project activities

The SSAID will procure and distribute essential drugs, equipment, medical supplies to all target Primary health Care Units and the Mobile health clinics. This will also include costs for last mile distribution of the equipment and commodities. The SSAID will also recruit essential health staff for the running of mobile health teams/PHCUs including (1 clinical officers, nurses, dispensers, CHWs, MCHWs, card registers and EPI vaccinators, clerks, medical waste operators) .These staff will provide basic essential health services including consultations, skilled deliveries, immunization, and provision of essential medicines to the beneficiaries as well as triage and also integrated WASH in the health facilities. The staff at the health facilities will be provided with the capacity to conduct nutritional assessments and screening in particular for children under 5 years. Children screened positive for moderate or severe acute malnutrition will be referred promptly to nutrition sites to be initiated on treatment. The SSAID will provide capacity building of key essential staff on monitoring, prevention, detection and response to epidemic-prone diseases by conducting a training IDSR and EWARS. Staff will also be trained by SSAID staff on screening for people living with disability (PWDs) using the Washington group set of short questions, on disability mainstreaming and inclusion, MHPSS and WHO mhGAP training will also be conducted to provide staff with essential skills in managing mental health disorders in treatment and psychological care. With a good institutional capacity in GBV, the SSAID will provide training to health workers on Clinical Management of Rape in Clinical Care of Sexual Assault Survivors (CCSAS). Through existing conflict resolution mechanisms, Boma health committees and community leadership, SSAID will be document and escalate any reported cases of attacks on healthcare workers. The SSAID will support the referral of emergency medical and obstetric cases to the nearest Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care (CEmONC) facilities for prompt responses as well as support referral of PWDs, including clients on treatment for mental health illnesses, people living disability for further rehabilitation services.
