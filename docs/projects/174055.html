Project Summary

Since 2009, the Boko Haram insurgency has crippled northeast Nigeria, with Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States worst affected, forcing 2.2 million people from their homes. The Global Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) compiled by OCHA estimates that there are 7.1 million people in need in Northeast Nigeria, 80% of whom (approximately 5.6 million) are women and children. The protracted conflict has led to continual displacement with new arrivals and displaced populations residing in over-congested formal and informal camps. The IRC has seen first-hand the acute and grave protection risks and violations faced by those displaced from their homes and livelihoods. Of those affected by these realities, particular subsets including female-headed households and child-headed households and adolescent girls face heightened protection risks. These risks are exacerbated by a lack of adequate services in sites, which has been compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic. Family separation and loss of livelihood, as well as social isolation as a result of the pandemic are anticipated to significantly affect the living conditions of the affected population. This will further increase their vulnerability level to sexual abuse and exploitation.
The IRC is proposing a comprehensive and community-based approach to addressing acute protection needs including case management and access to assistance for survivors of gender-based violence (GBV). The IRC’s proposed intervention will draw on expertise from GBV subsectors. The proposed response will focus on providing case management through direct services and linkages to specialized services as well as Psycho- Social Support (PSS) for women and adolescents who have been affected by COVID-19 and its related issues. Despite the acute humanitarian issues in northern Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states, where the crisis has shown no sign of abating, there have been an increase in violence including GBV and SEA. and the current COVID-19 pandemic has further increased the risk of most women and girls and there other protection needs in these locations. In response to this, the IRC intends to scale up programming to improve access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk through the provision of comprehensive services. This includes appropriate health assistance, GBV case management, psychological first aid accompanied with referrals for appropriate services,such as shelter, food and legal services depending on the immediate needs of the survivors other forms of psycho-social support and provision of critical material support. Furthermore the IRC will work to enhance access to multisectoral services through establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms particularly during the lock downs as a result of COVID-19 outbreak in these locations, where women and girls are under lockdowns with their perpetrators and are unable to access adequate services when necessary. The proposed program is designed to ensure the IRC is delivering a comprehensive and GBV timely response and prevention activities in BAY States. The presented intervention is intended to contribute to women and girls’ safety and healing by ensuring that women and girls can access case management services that address the physical, psychological and social consequences of GBV to prevent further harm. The intervention will also engage community members and stakeholders in order to ensure women and girls’ safety and protection from physical and sexual violence and to promote a safe and supportive environment where women and girls can freely access services without being blamed, stigmatize socially excluded or fair of contacting the corona virus. Women and girls accessing the safe spaces will be involved in the production of sanitary pad and face mask which will be part of items included in the dignity kits. This will form a means of livelihood for them.

1 - Needs

Despite the Humanitarian has been an acute one in northern Adamawa (Michika and Madagali), Borno (Konduga, Magumeri, Mongonu, Gwoza and MMC/Jere) and Yobe (Damaturu and Gujba) where the crisis shows no sign of abating and there is an increase in violence including GBV, SEA, the COVID 19 pandemic has also increases the risk of most women and girls as well as other protection needs, especially for women and girls who are the most at risk and have limited access to services as well as protection or redress. These locations which are hard to reach and according to the Nigeria HRP 2019, are subjected to military and AOGs attacks, making it difficult for aid workers to provide needed services for the affected population. The IRC intends to scale up programming to improve access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk through the provision of comprehensive services including appropriate health assistance, GBV case management, psychological first aid accompanied with referrals for appropriate services, other forms of psycho-social support, provision of critical material support, as well as enhancing access to multisectoral services through establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms most especially during this time that COVID 19 outbreak has created much negative impacts in the lives of women and girls in these locations. The proposed program is designed to ensure the IRC is capable of delivering a comprehensive and GBV timely response and prevention activities in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The proposed intervention is intended to contribute to women and girls’ safety and healing by ensuring that women and girls can access case management services that address the physical, psychological and social consequences of GBV to prevent further harm. The intervention will also engage community members and stakeholders in order to ensure women and girls’ safety and protection from physical and sexual violence and to promote a safe and supportive environment where women and girls can freely access services without being blamed, stigmatized and socially excluded. In communities where no safe space exists, the IRC in cooperation with local authorities and community members will identify a safe space for both distribution and CM service provision. In each safe space, confidentiality of survivors will be supported by well-trained GBV caseworkers who meet with GBV survivors in a private space to provide case intake and ongoing management support. The primary goal of social work case management is to optimize client functioning, wellbeing, emotional and physical safety. Existing Women’s Action Groups (WAGs) member’s capacity will be strengthened in each of the WGSS as they will act as local focal points for women and girls seeking access to GBV services. WAGs, will be trained on basic case management aimed at enhancing their knowledge on preventing and responding to GBV related issues. . The IRC will utilize previously trained caseworkers who are providing case management, GBV prevention and response messaging on voluntary basis, to enhance the sustainability of the program.

2 - Activities

IRC will continue to respond to the needs of women and girls in its establish women and girls safe spaces (WGSS) and at the comprehensive women centers (CWCs) to increase at-risk women and girls’ access to GBV services as well as increase social support and linkages for additional services in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. GBV survivors who self disclosed or referred in these communities (Michika , Mubi North and Mubi South, MMC/Jere, Konduga, Magumeri, Mongonu , Gwoza, Damaturu , and Gujba ) wil have safe access to quality case management services and psychosocial support. Using a survivor centered approach to GBV case management. Psychosocial support activities will foster healing and recovery which will be done through counselling and adequate. The IRC will conduct the following trainings in Gujba, Michika, and Magumeri LGA to enhance understanding and promote GBV prevention and response through coordination and collaboration: Three 3 day GBV core concept trainings will be done for 60 community stakeholders stakeholders)). Three 5 day GBV basic case management training will be done for 60 participants including WAG volunteers. In order to mitigate their risk to intimate partner violence due to cash control, Women will be linked up to livelihood support most especially the GBV survivors. Monthly remote safety audits and strategies developed will be coordinated and implemented, outcome from the safety audits will inform our decisions in other program implementation and women and girls will be supported with risk reduction material. 1,500 at risk women and girls will benefit from dignity kits assistance. Through coordination with the community structures such as the EMAP members, SASA! Activities and women action group, 8,500 community members will be reached with GBV awareness. The IRC will engage the adolescent girls through the life skills activities using the girl shine curriculum developed by the IRC and adapted to north east, this will be a 44 sessions of different topics which include personal hygiene and reproductive health topics. Also their caregivers will be taking through 14 sessions of parental topics in order to support the adolescent girls at home.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project is designed to reduce gender inequality for women and girls by supporting them to recover from GBV, through engaging community activists to challenge social norms which perpetrate GBV and by increasing women and girls’ social networks, skills and decision making power. This project will include safety audits and listening sessions with women and girls which will identify specific GBV risk mitigation actions to be addressed by humanitarian actors. Dedicated prevention and response services for women and girls will be provided at women’s safe spaces, providing accessible assistance tailored to women’s and girls’ needs. Utilizing EA$E and EMAP evidence-based approaches, this project seeks to positively affect relations between girls/women and boys/men, promoting more equitable gender dynamics while minimizing any adverse effect upon women and girls which might have caused them as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic or displacement.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will integrate robust feedback mechanisms designed to be accessible to all women and girls. These will include regular remote safety audit in the locations and listening sessions with women and girls; suggestion boxes within women and girl's safe spaces who access case management will be provided client feedback opportunities to hold service providers accountable. All feedback from women and girls will be documented, analyzed and reviewed alongside Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) data with trend analysis conducted during monthly project meetings.
