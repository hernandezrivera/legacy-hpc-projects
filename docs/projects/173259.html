1 - Needs

The situation In North Darfur is worse, especially in remote localities like; Kabkabya, Umbaru, At Tina, Sarif Omra, and Kernui, where a negative impact is being far from medical supply centers and lack of trained Medicare staff are some of the critical challenges faced by the communities here. As per the consultation meetings HDPO conducted with the ministry of health in ELfashir, it was apparent that there are clearly huge gaps in terms of access to PHC in those localities; hence, pregnant women suffer from adequate and clean delivery services. Similarly, the communities use Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs). These are none trained or qualified midwives to deliver, leading to high maternal and newborn mortalities in these areas. During the rainy season, it becomes difficult for the residents to do the referral as roads are normally unpassable due to the bad road network especially during the rainy season. lack of health intervention led to even protection instabilities as many cases of the women reported being harassed while traveling from their villages to other places for getting medical support.In most of the locations described above, there are some good initiatives from the Rural Development Committee comprised of the communities themselves. These communities mobilize, contribute, and collect money to purchase ambulances to facilitate referral of cases to better health facilities, often far away from the communities. In an assessment conducted by partners in November 2019 in North Darfur, it was found out that up to 80% of the reasons behind such initiatives was to improve maternal health services, especially to support pregnant women to deliver in safe places. COVID19 has put its overburden on the existing facilities, pressure on the staff, and affected household income at the community level. The localities that are densely populated or interconnected with main roads have continued to suffer. Thus, HDPO will work in; Janoub Aljazeera, Madani El Kobra, Sharg El Gezira, and Umbader in Khartoum localities to support the joint effort of the CIVID19 response supporting SMoH to improve the delivery of primary health care services at PHCs. Some of which shall include; training of the healthcare staff, provision of the PPEs for wide ranges while deploying the trained volunteers for outreach awareness campaigns on taking the measures beside there referral activities. Intersectoral coordination at the above-mentioned areas will be given priority; hence the close working with WASH, Nutrition, and protection will be assured at different coordination levels.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provision of essential primary health care services: In the year 2021, HDPO will keep the operation of its existing 19 Health facilities in At Kabkabya, Umbaru, At Tina, Sarif Omer, Kornoi in North Darfur, and Janoub Aljazeera, Madani El Kobra, Sharg El Gezira, and Umbader in Khartoum. These facilities will provide primary health care services according to the essential primary health care services package (ESP) with special focus on;1. Repair and maintenance of the PHCs and community-level health care facilities 2. Provision of supplies and medical staff to run the system for a basic consultation 3. Training of midwives and provide the necessary package and kits 4. Selection of outreach workers and training in awareness campaigns and referrals. 5. Conduct the sector level agreed AWD and other outbreak prevention activities Medical Waste management through the installation of incinerators at PHCs 6. Distribution of PPEs materials and conduct COVID19 response in close coordination with MoH, SMoH, and the partners,

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is a term used by the UN and NGO community to refer to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.
