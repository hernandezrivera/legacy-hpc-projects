Project Summary

Première Urgence Internationale (PUI) undertakes around 180 projects per year supporting nearly 5 million people in 21 countries. PUI has been present in Maiduguri Metropolitan Council (MMC) since April 2016, and has developed a strong expertise to address the needs of crisis-affected populations through a community based comprehensive integrated response. An operational base was opened in Kukawa, Kukawa LGA in 2018 to deploy health, nutrition and protection activities in Kukawa LGA, and more recently in Monguno. This presence enhanced PUI capacity to provide a rapid health and nutrition response following the influxes of IDPs which have occurred in late December.
PUI seeks to implement live-saving activities, strengthen the local capacities, provide an appropriate response and give a primary place to the accountability to affected population in all implemented programs.
Given the persisting humanitarian needs within Borno State, and the increased security threats towards the civilian resulting in afflux of IDPs in Maiduguri, Monguno and other perceived safe LGA, the proposed project aims to improve access to basic services through an integrated health and nutrition response for conflict affected populations, based on a community approach.
PUI has been supporting a 24/7 reproductive health care services in two PHCC and ANC/PNC in three outreach sites in Bolori II MMC. PUI has plans to start sexual reproductive health in Monguno in 2020. Beside the 24/7 reproductive health care, PUI is also running ANC, PNC and under-five years immunization in the 5 health facilities in Bolori II in MMC.
PUI rolled out MHPSS activities in December 2018 in MMC and in February 2019 in Monguno. This was after PUI identified that IDPs seen at its supported health facilities are among the most vulnerable group to mental health issues. Over 62% of all consultations were delivered to IDP populations presenting with symptoms like somatic pains, sleeping difficulties and low mood.
Following the high burden of malnutrition in Bolori II, PUI is providing life-saving nutrition interventions since 2016 integrated with Primary Healthcare; Food Security and Livelihoods; Protection and Community Outreach; and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). PUI implemented OTP activities in collaboration with MoH at the PHCC Kukawa from August to December 2018. Following attacks and instability in Kukawa with health facility hosting Outpatient Treatment Program for SAM cases affected, PUI nutrition activities were relocated to Monguno. In Monguno, nutrition activities are and will be limited to Infant and Young Child Feeding intervention because there are several partners currently implementing nutrition activities including 5 OTP and 1 stabilization centre.
To ensure a clear and organized response to the COVID-19 outbreak, PUI will create isolation rooms in all its facilities supported; guarantee safety of health staff workers with appropriate training and provision of adequate medical equipment; diagnosis, refer and treat patients of common disease and COVID-19; develop IEC material and so on.

1 - Needs

Health service delivery continues to be hampered by the conflict, impeding movements of health workers, drugs and other medical supplies, and general breakdown of adequate health facilities and infrastructure (Humanitarian Needs Overview OCHA 2019). With an estimated 5.3 Million individuals in need of health assistance, and with only half reached as of July 2019. There is also lack of local capacity and resources to address health needs within poor host communities with high IDP concentrations especially in remote LGAs. Around 42% of medical facilities in North East Nigeria have been damaged either partially or completely with humanitarian aid partners offering health services either under trees, temporal mark shits or in the tents. Treatable communicable diseases are highly endemic especially in areas with high concentration of IDPs due to high congestion, poor environmental and hygiene conditions from originating areas, a weakened health system, and poor sanitation among other important infrastructure. Malaria has been one of the highest cause of morbidity and mortality in North-Eastern states with children under 5 years and pregnant women being the most affected. In Monguno, 82 % of the Key Informants surveyed reported malaria as a top health concerns in their community (Multi-sectoral Rapid Needs Assessment of New arrivals, REACH, February 2019). Malaria cases in Borno state are seasonal with peak being witnessed during the rainy seasons, hence the need to have appropriate preventive and curative measure to handle the malaria upsurge during the rainy season. In 2018, 10,571 cases of Cholera were recorded in 18 LGAs within the BAY region. The second half of 2018 showed the highest rates recorded since 2016 for Malaria, Measles, AWD, and ARIs . While in early 2019, Measles outbreak was reported within multiple LGAs in Borno State. Currently, as atof 30th Apriol 2020, the state has recorded 59 cases of COVID 19 with 5 deaths in the span of only two weeks in the ongoing global pandemic. Health workers and IDPs are specifically at higerhigher risk of contracting the disease and they need to be protected. This outbreak has potentials to disrupt other life-saving health activities with serious consequences to already limited health services including health workers when it affects health workers in the state. Given the frequent diseases outbreaks in Borno, there is need to strengthen surveillance system and ensure robust emergency preparedness and responsed is put in place ready to respond to any diseases outbreaks. Gender-Based Violence is widespread in the region. Urgent medical care is often required as GBV seriously affects all aspects of women’s health, especially for an estimated 700,000 women and adolescent girls of reproductive health age who might be at risk of sexual violence and exploitation (HumanitarinHumanitarian Needs Overview OCHA 2019). Essential, often life-saving, medical services for GBV survivors are not available in many affected areas. In instances where health facilities exist, these often lack the necessary equipment, drugs and trained personnel to provide timely adequate clinical management of rape survivors. Survivors of rape require post exposure prophylaxis for the prevention of HIV/AIDS to be administered within 72 hours of the incident, but clinical management of rape capacity is only available in eight out of 27 LGAs in Borno State (HumanitarinHumanitarian Needs Overview OCHA 2019). Mental health and psycho-social support remain extremely limited within the BAY region for example, mental health treatment services are only available at the Federal Neuro Psychiatric Hospital in Maiduguri. In PUI health facilities 60% of referred patients are IDPs, with 80% requiring an additional session or referral to psychiatric services. It’s estimated that only 48% of estimated needs are covered due to limited coverage coupled with cultural attitudes and stigma towards mental health.

2 - Activities

PUI will continue providing free-of-charge life-saving health care services and supporting primary health care facilities and health outreach sites. PUI will provide diagnosis, treatment, and referrals when required of communicable diseases through PHCs and Health Outposts through the delivery of curative services and case management. As part of the Primary Health Care Package, immunization will be implemented in each health facility supported by PUI. PUI will obtain all vaccines used in the supported health facilities for routine immunization activities from the MoH, which is being supported and provided with vaccines by UNICEF. PUI will also support MOH and UNICEF Mass Immunization Campaigns through logistics support and community mobilization. Outreach teams will be raising community awareness through community health education around hygiene promotion, communicable disease, and immunization. Public health surveillance of epidemic prone communicable diseases will be conducted through EWARS on a weekly basis. Identified diseases (such as measles, cholera, and meningitis) will be referred to health facility proper diagnosis and sending of alert to the Disease Surveillance Officer, information sending to the State Primary Health Care Development Agency and other national and international bodies. The surveillance system will incorporate COVID-19 case identification and reporting to the health facility, isolation and notification to the state COVID-19 task force. PUI is committed to support the response in order to contain the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and decrease morbidity and mortality in Borno state through: supporting facility management of COVID-19 while strengthening essential life-saving primary health care; enabling access to community level surveillance, diagnosis, case management, and referral of common disease and COVID-19; coordinate with state authorities and partners in delivering logistic support to COVID-19 case management.PUI will construct isolation areas within supported health facilities; provide PPEs for health workers in supported facilities; distribute 560 disinfection kits to facilities and disseminate integrated key messages on COVID-19 and associated risks through different means (radio broadcasting, social media channels, IEC materials, bulk SMS and use of megaphones in the communities). PUI will train 50 health workers on IPC guideline across supported health facilities and strengthen the network of community health workers for disease surveillance within communities.PUI has modelled its reproductive health interventions on the Minimum Initial Service Package. PUI will provide 24/7 maternal health care including comprehensive antenatal neonatal, and postnatal services to Pregnant and Lactating Women. This includes performing deliveries through trained health-care providers and midwives within PHCs. PUI will provide free-of-charge SRH consultation within health facilities, including BEmONC and family planning; medical care for survivors of SGBV will be available at the PHC level. Medical staff will be trained on the identification of sexual violence and appropriate response. PUI is collaborating with UNFPA for the provision of Reproductive Health Kit. PUI will integrate some Mental Health and Psychosocial support in the health activities. The activities will focus on training of health, protection and nutrition field staff. The staff will be able to recognizing psycho-social problems, warning signs of stress, PFA, and self-care. PUI will put a referral system in place where field workers can refer protection, and nutrition cases to the MHPSS team. Patients reporting with mental/psychosocial problems at the health facilities will be attended by PUI qualified PSW who will provide counselling, PFA and follow up. Patients who will require mental consultation will be referred to the psychiatrist specialist/nearest psychiatrist hospital.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PUI has a strong mechanism in place that relies on various channels. Feedbacks are mainly captured through: (1) Community Committees Meetings that are organised once a month with community leaders, elders, women and PUI program staff to discuss project activities, gaps and needs; (2) Community Volunteers Network which comprised all outreach workers who directly link PUI to targeted communities; and (3) PUI Complaint Mechanism which consist of helpdesks in the community and a toll-free helpline managed by dedicated trained staff.
