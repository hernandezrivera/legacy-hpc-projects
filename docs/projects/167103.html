Project Summary

The proposed project aims to respond to the urgent health care needs of internally displaced persons (IDP/ Personne Déplacée Interne) and to reduce morbidity and mortality among 6-59 months children with SAM in 16 health areas in the Niono district in the Segou Region of Mali.
As for the IDPs, mobile clinics will provide free and quality primary health care to the displaced persons, mobile teams will remain flexible and adapt their interventions in the event of IDP settlements in new sites or the outbreak of a situation requiring an emergency response. ALIMA/AMCP-SP will set up three mobile clinic teams to provide better access to IDP sites, each team consisting of 3 staff (1 nurse, 1 midwife and 1 caregiver). Additionally, 1 community health worker (CHW) per IDP site will be trained by ALIMA/AMCP-SP. These teams will be supervised by a medical referent (responsible for the project's medical activities), based in Niono. Health care will be tailored to the needs identified for each site, including those of pregnant women and children under 5 years of age, who are most vulnerable to the living conditions on the IDPs sites. ALIMA/AMCP-SP curative activities will be combined with preventive activities to reduce vulnerability, such as prenatal consultations for pregnant women, malnutrition screening for children under 5 years of age, routine Extended Program on Immunization (EPI) for children aged 0-11 months and training of women in malnutrition detection techniques using the MUAC (Middle-Upper Arm Circumference) by mother approach, in order to detect malnutrition earlier and more effectively and prevent complications for their children, as well as epidemiological surveillance. Assisted deliveries and complicated deliveries will be referred to the relevant health facilities (Centre de Santé Communautaire and Centre de Santé de Référence) so as not to destabilize the district's health care cost recovery system.
Regarding nutrition activities, ALIMA/AMCP will support 16 CSCom and 1 CSREF (16 ATFC and 1 ITFC) in the Niono District with the management of outpatient children with SAM, according to the actual PCIMA protocol, and the community sector will be strengthened, with a large-scale MUAC by Mothers training campaign for childbearing aged women, re-dynamization and training ReCos (Community Relays) on MUAC by mothers approach, and on defaults and drop-out rates monitoring.
In case of emergency, ALIMA/AMCP-SP will be able to quickly assess needs and react accordingly.
