Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates number of crisis-affected population in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria at 11.7 million people (49.8% male and 50.2% female) out of which 4.7 million and 5 million live in areas of major severity of needs (inter-sector severity 3) and acute severity of needs (inter-sector severity 4-6) respectively. The above population in need include 6.2 million protracted Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs); 1.4 million returnees; 324,000 IDPs in hard to reach areas, 1.6 million-population movement and the rest overburdened host communities in addition to 480,000 Palestine refugees. 67% of the 11.7 million population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria according to Syria Response Access Dynamic report by OCHA (December, 2018). Overall, 9 million of people are estimated to be food insecure and at risk of food insecurity by Food Security Sector.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to support sector coordination to ensure needs of 6 million food insecure and at risk of food insecurity is adequately addressed by all sector partners responding to Syria crisis from different operational hubs. The objective of the proposed action is to strengthen effectiveness and quality of the food security responses base on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination within the Food Security Sector and across other sectors. The proposed project will contribute to 2019 HRP Food Security Sector Strategic objective 4 "Support to food security sector coordination across all hubs" and the expected outputs of the projects are:-
Output 1: Accessibility of food security information for stakeholders response planning improved.
Output 2. Capacity for national and sub national food security sector strengthened.
Output 3: Capacity for sub national coordination of food security sector built.
This project is expected to contribute to improve planning (evidence based, timely, efficient and effective), and responses to needs of affected population and avoid duplication to ensure efficient use of limited resources.

Needs

Eight years into Syrian crisis, the cumulative number of food insecure population continue to remain very high. The complex nature of the crisis made worst by the recent drought of 2017/2018 and considered as the worst in three decades. The combined impact of the crisis and drought which include multiple population displacements, destruction of infrastructure and services, reduced production and productive capacities, loss of livelihoods, hyper inflation, high unemployment, loss of productive assets and strategies and loss of lives have contributed to reduce social, financial and physical access to food. Currently, 9 million people in Syria are estimated to be food insecure and in need of a range of food security and livelihoods assistance out of 11.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance countrywide ( HNO, 2019). The Food Security Sector co-led by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Food Programme (WFP) was established to coordinate responses to the sector. Operating from Syria and South Turkey, the sector coordinates activities implemented by more than 100 sector partners across all hubs and links with inter sector coordination. FAO and WFP as Cluster Lead Agencies jointly contribute to the sector secretariat, which includes coordinators, information management officers and technical experts. Annually, the sector develops strategies including Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) base on a number of assessments, technical studies and analysis undertaken by the sector, lead agencies and sector partners to guide responses. The current strategy of the sector aims to ensure food security for all affected Syrians through provision of food assistance, support to agricultural livelihoods to increase productive capacities and resilience of crisis affected households complemented with light rehabilitation of critical agricultural related infrastructures that support production. The overall goal of food security coordination is to ensure food security for all affected people in order to reduce the number of people adopting negative coping mechanisms while supporting early and medium term recovery of critical agricultural/livelihood assets and essential services related to food security. However, the challenges facing the country and the sector are complex requiring regular and extensive evaluation for appropriateness and timeliness of the responses, quantitative and qualitative analysis to better understand the dynamics of prevailing situation will be critically important to comprehensively address risks to the sector in a coordinated manner. Hence the importance of a robust coordination mechanism capable of facilitating timely and effective responses cannot be over emphasized. Identifying and reaching affected population, avoiding duplication, minimizing delays and providing appropriate support to sector partners is key not only to during the emergency phase but well into recovery phase too.Building on the achievements of the past years, the sector will continue to play a critical role in 2019 in ensuring a well coordinated approach centered on the commitment of partners for a coordinated responses through the IASC sector / cluster approach. This approach seeks to increase the efficiency and effectiveness of response through: (1) developing a principled, predictable and systematic operational planning processes; (2) ensuring greater coherence across the different operational modalities (i.e. cross line, cross border or regular programmes) through improved coordination; and (3) strengthening information sharing and monitoring of responses. Moreover the Food Security Sector is providing technical guidance to partners (in particular national actors) through sub national working group including training, development of technical guidelines (minimum packages for interventions), conducting of technical studies.

Implementation of activities

Currently FAO is operating in thirteen governorates – Aleppo, Al Hassakeh, Ar Raqqa, Dar’a, Deir e Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, As Sweida, Quneitra, Rural Damascus, Tartous and Lattakia. In governorates where UN staff presence is not permitted by UN security regulations, FAO operates through government seconded officers and NGO service providers who are directly supporting implementation of activities and report to FAO.FAO Representation in Syria will be responsible for the overall implementation of the activities, backstopped by FAO headquarters with the technical support of FAO’s multidisciplinary team in the Regional Office for the Near East and North Africa. FAO is working closely with MAAR to ensure that the project goal is achieved through proper technical expertise and monitoring capacity. The implementation will be further supported by:• A central coordination committee that includes representatives from FAO, relevant MAAR directorates, community-based organizations and food security partners. The committee’s role is to oversee project implementation and application of sector guidelines by partners.• FAO Resilience Officer in the targeted governorates will operationally and technically support coordination functions, training and monitoring of the project.The proposed actions will benefit from existing capacity, which can be scaled up at short notice whenever required . Below are activities that will be implemented per output.Output 1: Accessibility of food security information for stakeholders response planning improved.Activities1) Support conducting of relevant technical studies and analysis (HEA, CFSAM, IPC, FSAL, capacity assessment of sub-national food security and agricultural institutions and other surveys) to support need analysis and response planning.2) Develop sector/cluster strategies (response plans) to guide sector and cluster partners on most critical needs.3) Facilitate setting of standards and coordinate monitoring of food security situation (OMI), developing and expansion of minimum response packages for interventions.4) Monitoring implementation of the strategies and document lessons learnt. Output 2. Capacity for National and sub national food and agriculture sector strengthened:Activities 1) Improve planning, agenda setting and time management of the sector meetings. 2) Facilitate regular information sharing and mapping of interventions to optimize complementary of interventions, collaboration and accountability to affected populations and to avoid overlaps;3) Encourage different stakeholders' participation in food security and agriculture sector/cluster coordination platforms including governments, donors and partners to harmonize responses and improve targeting;4) Assess gaps in the sector/cluster, support sub national coordination and ensure linkages with national coordination mechanisms;5) Strengthening institutional and technical capacity to support functionality of the Food and Agriculture sector including tailor made training to improve understanding of the sector/cluster approaches.Output 3: Capacity for sub national coordination of food security and agriculture sector built.1) Assign a dedicated coordination experts at sub-national levels to co-lead the sector with WFP where feasible.2) Assess coordination needs and equip sub-national partners with relevant technical tools, standards and guidelines.3) Train and mentor sub-national sector/cluster coordinators, information management officers and partners on key technical guidelines and strategies to enable them to apply the knowledge in their day to day coordination roles.All the activities under each output will address specific needs of different gender and age groups.

Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Aid workers being targeted as a result of being aid worker or coming from particular ethnicity/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection: Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population examples IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth base on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors; Mitigation measures: establishing a local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place a clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and processes; ensuring an effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance: Mitigation measures: establish community based beneficiaries selection and community based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do not harm principles including GBV and PSEA risks mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized. Training will take account of the differential requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Guidance will be provided to partners to target returnees also so that their re-settlement will be aided. Where IDPs will be selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible friction between the two. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place to provide communities with pathways to express their concerns and grievance. This will be used to make adjustments and improvement on the implementation of activities. Special attention will be provided to the returnees to help them in their settlement and resuming their production activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and assumptions beyond project control and their mitigation measures:1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation; Probability: Likely; Impact: Severe; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; Advocate with RC/HC and other instances to regain access. Develop contingency plans in coordination with Service Providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities; Probability: possible; Impact: significant; Mitigation measures: Continue with advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities. Advocacy with other organizations to include new arrival in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross border movements and operations; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Ensure that Service Providers (SP) and partners have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements; Probability: Likely; Impact: major; Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities. 5. Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the period/area concerned; Probability: Unlikely; Impact: Significant; Mitigation measures: Assistance is balanced and not directed only to one specific group. Identification of beneficiaries is done in partnership with local communities including IDPs and host communities. Selected IPs that are accepted by local communities and aware of conflict sensitivity.6. Risks: Increased control of land and water by parties in conflict; Probability: Likely; Impact: Moderate; Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from a range of sources. Accurate choice of target areas ensuring that farming is possible and beneficiaries will undertake the activity. Advocacy with local authorities by SPs. If necessary, move to alternative locations previously identified7. Risks: Possible climatic shocks during the implementation period (drought in particular). Probability: Possible; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: Select distribution areas in different agro-ecological zones and in irrigated areas to reduce risks. Distribute adapted varieties, which possess specific desirable traits, including drought tolerance.
