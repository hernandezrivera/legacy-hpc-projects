Project Summary

The aim of the project is to provide immediate food security and livelihoods assistance to IDPs & Host communities’ households in stress and crises in Galgaduud Region, Dhusamareb district to access to essential food items. The project will be implemented in Dhusamareb District with a total target population of 31,800 (9636 women, 7791 men, 6487 boys and 7886 girls). In addition, the project will provide Restocking to 500 rural HHs and farm input to 2200 HHs to farmers benefitting around 4,000 women, 3,234 men, 2,693 boys, 3,273 girls affected by floods and drought to protect assets and generate income for their households. During the project implementation, TUOS will conduct periodic monitoring and evaluation exercise will be conducted with the aim of checking the changing dynamics in the target area and need for changes in the project implementation. The project targets women, children, girls, boys and men of all ages. The selection committee will be trained on the selection criteria as such to promote inclusion of all gender and age group in the target community. Pregnant and lactating women and children under five years of age have special health requirements and suffer severe consequences from under-nutrition. TUOS also will mobilize the community to understand and basis of the project and the target group. TUOS will also be working with community elders, local Administration and cluster partners for successful project implementation.

Needs

Most recent Post Gu 2018 assessment of the FSNAU 2018 indicated that 4.6 million people are acutely food insecure (IPC phase 2 or worse). With OCHA’s successive strategic prioritization, approximately 40% (or 1.1 million) of people classified under IPC 2 are being envisioned for assistance through the recovery and resilience frameworks funded by development partners. Galgaduud Inter Cluster assessment report 2017 noted that an estimated 65 per cent of livestock in the region was lost due to severe drought between November 2016 and April 2017. The effect is still being felt since majority of the households affected are still in need of humanitarian assistance. The assessment found a reduction of more than 35 per cent of livestock prices due to the migration, which also affected livestock dealers who depend on the market. The livestock market in Cadaado town now depends on goats, sheep and camel from parts of Mudug region, while livestock markets in Dhuusamareeb and Guri Ceel depend on livestock from Hiraan region. Some 50 per cent of Caabud Waaq market also depend on livestock from villages bordering Ethiopia. Despite assistance from local communities, business sector, diaspora and humanitarians, it was observed that food insecurity is common among IDPs and most of them survive on one meal a day. The situation of the displaced has worsened following the refusal of Somali currency notes by business people in Galgaduud region, business transactions are mainly conducted through mobile money transfers and in US dollars. However only one out of every five households has a mobile phone. This makes it difficult for the majority to get assistance from relatives in far places. Since the EVC has replaced Somali currency, the movement of IDPs to urban areas in search of casual labor jobs has reduced because the majority do not have mobiles phones. This situation has not changed much and it is not expected to change by a significant percentage in 2019.

Activities

1. Identify and register 5,300 households with malnourished children, lactating women, pregnant women, elderly, sick and new arrived IDPs, identified by community leaders and women groups to prevent a deterioration of the situation and specifically to avoid people in phase 3 from slipping into phase 4 or 5. 2. Distribute unconditional cash transfer to 5,300 households representing 31,800 persons (9,636 women, 7,791 men, 6,487 boys and 7,886 girls). 3. Support agro-pastoralist communities through rehabilitation of 5 silted canals with Cash For Work Activities aiming to increase the food security to the vulnerable households and rehabilitation of productive assets. 4. Provide assorted farm inputs and tools to 2,200 farmers representing 13,200 persons (4,000 women, 3,234 men, 2,693 boys, 3,273 girls) to promote crop production. 5. Provide restocking to 500 pastoralists’ households representing 3,000 persons (909 women, 735 men, 612 boys and 744 girls) who lost their animals during the drought; each household will receive 10 goats. 6. Train 200 farmers and 50 pastoralists equally shared by men and women (125 men and 120 women) for the best farming methodologies and technologies and animal husbandry respectively.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Dhusamareb District is currently relatively stable though the clan conflict dynamics in the region is volatile. This however will not affect in anyway the activities in the target villages. TOUS has operated in the target area for a long time and the project implementation has not been adversely affected by conflict. The project is in line with Somalia 2017-2020 durable solutions strategy in place. The project will also support the long term government intervention on health programming which aims at reducing negative health impact in the region. Training of community health workers as part of the project activities will help in sustaining health and Nutrition intervention in the district. Training of farmers will help in promoting sustainability of the project with continuous food production when the environment is conducive for farming.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Displaced persons in IDP camps are more vulnerable and may risk being profiled and neglected by the host community due to clan affiliated social connections. To avoid any form of exclusion in the project, TUOS will employ a Do-No-Harm approach principle and help the community to select project committees which will comprise of all the target group representatives. Beneficiaries targeting and selection committee will be formed from each group, that is host community and IDPs. A selection criteria will be agreed upon before the project starts and discussed upon with the donor for approval. Emphasis will be given to the most vulnerable individuals and households such as IDPs regardless of their age, gender and clan. TUOS will conduct due diligence of the list provided by the selection committee to ascertain the inclusiveness of the target beneficiaries. Periodic monitoring and evaluation exercise will be conducted with the aim of checking the changing dynamics in the target area and need for changes in the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Food security interventions may fail to reach the most vulnerable and, in some cases, may actually lead to further harm. The risk of exclusion based on gender stereo type and age is prevalent in the community. The project targets women, children, girls, boys and men of all ages. The selection committee will be trained on the selection criteria as such to promote inclusion of all gender and age group in the target community. Pregnant and lactating women and children under five years of age have special health requirements and suffer severe consequences from undernutrition. TUOS also will mobilize the community to understand and basis of the project and the target group.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Women and children are the most vulnerable group during clan conflict and violence in the target area. To mitigate the effect of being adversely affected by the conflict when it occurs, TUOS will be working with community security team to protect women and children from attack. Also food assistance will be provided at the community level or IDP centers thus avoiding the need to travel for such services. TUOS will also be working with community elders in the target areas such that the community elders themselves will be negotiating among the warring groups to allow services to continue if any of the target areas will be affected.
