Project Summary

Under this project, Dorcas aid international will continue providing protection activities through different community centers in Aleppo. The community centers are protection projects that target beneficiaries in 3areas: a) in Ba’aiden, Jebel Semaan, Aleppo, specifically in Hullok neighborhood.
This project aims to raise awareness and increasing knowledge on protection subjects, children rights and non-violent communication. It also aims to increase resilience and adequate response to GBV and CP among Syrian community especially women, girls and boys in Aleppo and RD.
The overall objective is to strengthen the resilience and protection of conflict affected children, adolescents and their families in Syria through ensuring a protective environment that is conducive to their protection, psychosocial wellbeing and positive coping.
This goal will be reached through Case Management, PSS sessions, parenting without violence sessions and life skills.
The project will offer protection services to be available for vulnerable displaced and returnee population including older persons and persons with disabilities, it will support families to identify suitable protection measures to live safely in their communities. The proposed intervention aims at addressing the immediate protection needs for vulnerable Syrian, IDPs, returnees and displaced living in Aleppo, in addition to delivering Covid-19/Cholera related awareness and prevention messages. Dorcas will reach out to vulnerable individuals living in the areas. Identification will be done through a network of existing community centers staff with experienced social workers and community workers conducting individual needs assessments.

3 - Needs

Humanitarian needs remain critical in Syria and more specifically in Aleppo where Dorcas has been working since 2015. Within the affected population, protection issues disproportionately affect groups such as women, children, especially adolescent boys and girls, older persons, persons with disabilities and other vulnerable, marginalized or socially excluded people and groups. Following the multisectoral needs assessment (MSNA) that was carried out by Dorcas in October 2021 in 10 different neighborhoods of Aleppo Governorate, Jebel Saman district and sub-district, it shows that the community in question suffers from gender/age segregation when it comes to multiple issues, thus pinpointing towards the presence of at-risk groups or individuals who are more vulnerable than others, most notably women and girls. Multiple aspects of lack of human protection are found in the community ranging from harassment to explosive hazards, all of which contribute to increasingly diminishing living standards. Dorcas will therefor focus its Hanano and Hullock. In the implementation 2022 and 2023, Dorcas will focus on protection and Covid-19 and cholera will be mainstreamed throughout all the interventions.

4 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas has been working with families & individuals in Aleppo and RD since 2015 in collaboration with local partners. In Aleppo, Dorcas is doing direct implementation under the umbrella of the Presbyterian church. Through the partners, Dorcas' role is to support them through strong & focused capacity building measures throughout the project duration, following up with partner staffs through coaching, training to ensure quality of the sensitive program. Dorcas/partners team are already present & will be continuing the implementation through the protection teams that can build rapport with affected communities, distribute dignity kits to vulnerable women in an effort to build trust, assess protection risks, jointly identify protection response measures, plan and roll out community-based protection services to communities. After the crisis, psychosocial needs increased. Recently, it was also noted & due to the COVID-19 outbreak & the economic stress, MHPSS needs have increased. Through the community centers, beneficiaries will be informed through counseling or group meetings on protection services available, the team will refer cases, when possible, to other service providers. The following services will be provided awareness raising, early identification and safe referrals, case management to GBV & CP cases, activities focused on prevention, mitigation including PSS, training related Interpersonal therapy, PM+ problem management+, Counseling, Stabilization & relaxation technic

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Security risk: Physical risk to individuals & assets from acts of war, violence and crime. Aleppo and RD are Modern risk area. No major clashes or security conflict has been reported in the past year. Mitigation: Though personal classes & conflicts can not be eliminated, still Dorcas & their partners implement high level of alert & prevention measures to ensure risk minimization. Dorcas has a safety officer who coordinates with INSO & other agencies to obtain relevant security information & make informed decisions. The range of options how to assess & deal with security threats is laid down in Dorcas’ Safety and Security Manual. To ensure safeguarding of staff, Dorcas' project manager are trained in personal security and staff safety management. Dorcas security officer monitors the situation regularly & collaborates with the partner operating there. Risk: Discrimination among beneficiaries Mitg: Inform beneficiaries about the complaint process and the support provided by Dorcas and that services are provided to all equallyRisk: beneficiaries are at risk of sexual exploitation and abuseMitg: all staff have signed the COC, beneficiaries are informed about the complaint mechanism


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Dorcas develops many proposals & activities based on information received from its feedback systems & the needs of the community expressed by those coming to the CC. Dorcas’ 2-way beneficiary feedback system was established in the CC to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, & evaluation of the project and Dorcas makes sure it is well implemented and monitored. Feedback & suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring & subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against SEA. Dorcas ensures beneficiaries have multiple ways to provide feedback, including a hotline, text messages, emails, & box established within the CC. Dorcas’ long-term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, & widows are reached through its services. Dorcas will continue providing trainings related to PSEA to staff due to high turnover, awareness and IEC materials will be delivered. All staff have signed the COC, any new staff member attends induction training on integrity. Dorcas also is providing capacity building to partners organizations to capacitate them and make sure COC and PSEA are followed properly.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:Enabled access to targeted areas is maintained with acceptable security conditions during the intervention.The political and economic situation of the country remains at a level allowing effective humanitarian action.The Covid-19/cholera pandemic remains contained in Aleppo where the implementation will take place. Risks:Restrictions imposed by the Government of Syria on humanitarian interventions, including humanitarian access.Civil unrest in project locations prevents full geographic coverage as anticipated.Confirmed increasing number of cases and/or potential outbreak of Covid-19/cholera in project locations.Significant in-flux of conflict affected populations into project locations leads to over stretch of project delivery. Mitigation:Dorcas/the partners will closely monitor any safety and security concerns in targeted areas and act upon its Risk Mitigation Plan.Dorcas/partners will ensure strong participation in field coordination mechanisms (clusters and working groups) and engage with other partners and/or stakeholders in project locations to avoid any duplication of efforts.Dorcas/Partners will ensure most vulnerable populations are being selected and targeted through beneficiary selection criteria, including PWSNs and PWDs.Monitoring and evaluation of the implementation done by the MEAL department on regular basis
