Project Summary

CAOFISR will continue its coordination and management of Basateen Al Sheoukh IDP Camp in order for the camp residents to receive the timely distribution of aid and shelter, while mitigating risks and hazards within the camp. Due to the expressed need from the families, in addition to the CCCM activities, CAOFISR will be focusing on resilience based activities that will assist families with returning home.
This project's objective is "This project is aimed at supplying the camp population with life saving support through CCCM activities, while simultaneously providing them with vocational trainings that will assist their return home." The main objective is to allow for CAOFSIR, as the CCCM actor, to empower the camp population to begin functioning independently, identify needs that can also assist that process, and coordinate the assistance. Current mechanisms will be updated and reinforced to ensure the sharing of information is efficient, and that the government takes a larger role with managing the population in need."
Protection is the main concern for CCCM, and through all activities, the mitigation of risks and hazards are done through the program activities. The activities are aimed to support the camp population by maintaining the database of the camp population, identifying needs within the camp, referring individuals to the correct authorities, mitigating risks and hazards, supplying individuals with financial aid through CFW activities, providing trainings and awareness session on GBV and gender equality and establishing government structures. The goal of this project is to create a plan forward for the families in the camp so that they become less dependent on aid and the responsibility of their livelihood shifts from the CCCM partner, to themselves.
Since there has been a large expressed intention for the current camp families to remain in Shirqart, CAOFISR will have a team of individuals that are dedicated to analyzing the current market, and developing vocational and business trainings for camp residents to assist their departure from the camp. These activities will include:
-conducting market research in Shirqat, surveys... etc.
-vocational and business trainings, targeting 600-800 men and women
-linking them with possible job opportunities
-CFW activities
-assistance with legal documents
-advocating to the government about their needs
-social cohesion projects (art show and football tournament)

Needs

CAOFISR has been the CCCM partner in Basateen Al Sheuokh Camp since its opening in October 2017 with an original population of 2500 individuals. The camp's original purpose was to support the families displaced from the Hawija operations, but because there was a change in the tactics, many of those families fled to other camps, which is why Basateen's population remained so little. Today Basateen has a population of 1500 individuals that have expressed their need to remain within the camp. The camp's population consists mostly of families that were displaced from the areas liberated by ISIS and secondary displaced families from other camps or informal sites. The destruction of the surrounding areas of Shirqat is quite significant and the families were already having financial difficulties before the liberation operations. With the additional expenses of rehabilitation, the families are not able to afford these costs. In addition, the services within the areas of origin are not fully operational to support their return. CAOFISR has been trying to support the families with CFW opportunities, but it still is not enough to support their return, but with this project, we plan to shift our focus to supporting their departures with the resilience team. After many discussions with the government and the cluster, it was concluded that Basateen will remain open due to the fact that the families within the camp are very financially unstable, and they come from remote areas without much services. (Please refer to the Camp Sweep performed by CAOFISR and IOM in November 2018). The camp sweep has drawn the following conclusions from the 285 families interviewed: 1. 79% of families are from Salahaldin, 15% from Ninewa and 6% from Kirkuk 2. 30% of the families in the camp have been there since the opening of the camp in October 2017, which means there is a large need to support their return home3.90% of the families do not plan on returning home4. Reasons for not returning: 75% no financial means, 61% damaged homes, 50% area is unsafe The intention survey is a large indication of further support needed for the camp residents to return home.

Protection Risk Analysis

-Living in a camp poses many health risks, and not easy to contain because the families are living closely. -The water is used by all families, so if the water is not kept healthy, all families risk being infected by illnesses-Fire safety is important because of the closeness and flammability of the tents -Children playing around the site, there are many hazards that must be contained, and also the ability to leave the camp-security risks from the outside host community (may be caused by jealousy of aid) -GBV within families -families feelings not cared for and neglected

Mitigation Measures

-Maintenance of the site and facilities, the technician will attend to daily-Referrals to health and protection partners when problems arise-The water is maintained daily, and the RO operator is constantly ensuring it is healthy to drink-each block is equipped with a fire extinguisher in case of fire-Security is at all gates and monitoring the fences to ensure that holes have not been made-CFS have been installed for children to play -Community involvement through the maintenance of the committees with ensure the families feel apart of a community and will allow for the families to share any issues with the management to ensure the camp residents feel at home, cared for and safe. -Through coordination meetings, CAOFISR will be able to address all partners and the cluster system on needs for the camp to mitigate risks to the camp management -Regular meetings with the local authorities will ensure the camp is safe guarded by the security forces and there is no threat to the lives within the camp, and also to gain any support from the government-Regular awareness sessions given to the camp residents is important for mitigating health and protection risks

How is your project accountable to affected population

Camp management have set up 3 committees (men, women and children) to address their needs. Camp management records all the meeting minutes that is then reviewed by the Programme Coordinator. The PC then either addresses the concerns or requests of the committees or consults the partners responsible of those concerns or requests. Camp management reviews these needs and caters the outreach to ensure their needs are met if possible. We work closely with the other partners to ensure they are meeting those needs as well. The families can also supply feedback or complaints at any time to the camp management team anonymously. Our community mobilizers are giving daily sessions to camp residents on GBV and gender equality, along with hygiene promotion. The hygiene promotion is also catered to women's needs. Our staffed are trained to identify whether women are in need of protection assistance and offer assistance if needed. They then refer to protection partners that specialize in the care of these situations.
