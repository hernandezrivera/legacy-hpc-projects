Project Summary

In response to growing humanitarian needs in Lebanon caused by the socio-economic impacts of the economic crisis, the proposed intervention will provide life-saving assistance to 315 vulnerable Lebanese households living in Palestinian gatherings in Beirut (Said Ghawash, Daouk and Sabra) and South Lebanon (Kfarbadda, Jal el Bahar and Wasta). MCC together with its national implementing partner Popular Aid for Relief and Development (PARD) will distribute monthly food assistance in the form of a food voucher for a period of six months from September 2021 through February 2022. A higher proportion of beneficiary households will be from the targeted Beirut gatherings (64%) where there is higher reported incidence of food insecurity.
Each household will receive a food voucher of 220,000 LBP per household member, in line with the current WFP transfer value and the estimated food security sector fresh food basket cost, to be redeemed at shops contracted by the project in the project areas. The food assistance will be accompanied by nutrition and hygiene awareness sessions and distribution of multi-sectoral messaging on nutrition and hygiene, as well as mental health and reproductive health.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

The project is designed to contribute directly to strategic objective 1, saving lives, by providing essential support to the most vulnerable Lebanese households. The project will distribute food vouchers to 315 households, approximately 1,700 individuals for a period of six months. Multi-sectoral messaging (COVID-19, Reproductive Health, Mental Health, EPI Immunization, WASH & Hygiene, nutrition, PSEA) developed by relevant agencies and working groups such as UNICEF, WHO and the PSEA network will be provided to all beneficiaries through awareness raising at distributions and household visits. Monthly nutrition workshops will also be provided and cover topics such as food safety, infant and young child feeding practices, and nutrition needs of pregnant and lactating women.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

MCC's Code of Conduct requires staff to commit to preventing sexual violence and fostering an environment in which sexual violence is not tolerated. Strong complaints mechanisms are implemented in all MCC projects and a protection and safeguarding focal point is embedded in the MCC Lebanon office. MCC requires that all partners have policies and procedures regarding the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. The proposed project will enable equal access to assistance for the targeted beneficiary population on the basis of the established vulnerability criteria. During vulnerability validation, selection and follow up surveys and assessments, project staff will be able to identify specific risks and vulnerabilities of beneficiary household members and refer them though established mechanisms to trained social workers or to other agencies. The modality of implementation will be adjusted as needed to meet the needs of any beneficiaries with special needs.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

All project implementation processes, and procedures have been designed with the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in mind. Feedback and results of satisfaction surveys are reported to project management and are used to make required improvements to subsequent distributions. In regard to all activities undertaken in any Palestinian Gathering, PARD works with locally established women’s committees, comprised of members from both refugee and host communities (Lebanese and PRL), to collect community input on, composition of assistance, vulnerability criteria, outreach and assessments. Beneficiaries will be invited to provide feedback through follow-up phone calls or home visits and satisfaction surveys conducted post-distribution. MCC and PARD have established feedback and complaints mechanisms in place for all beneficiaries and stakeholders though a hotline, whatsapp and complaints boxes at distribution sites.
