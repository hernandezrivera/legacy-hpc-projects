Project Summary

With the vision of "building hope and health for life," the Alaska Health Project South Sudan (AHPSS) has had a static presence in Fangak County since 2008. The mission of the organization is to improve health outcomes by providing clean water and supporting agricultural initiatives for vulnerable populations within Fangak and Canal/Pigi Counties and the surrounding region.
To date AHPSS has successfully drilled 61 successful boreholes in Fangak and 3 in Canal/Pigi. According to Fangak County RRC, there are an estimated 80 total boreholes in Fangak County, meaning AHPSS is responsible for creating 75% of the county’s total borehole infrastructure. In addition, there are very few boreholes in Canal/Pigi and AHPSS is estimated to have drilled around 50% of these wells having drilled only three. No other partner in northern Jonglei has the capacity to create new water infrastructure and understands the geohydrology of the region like AHPSS.
In addition to drilling new boreholes, AHPSS specializes in other aspects of new water infrastructure including constructing elevated platforms for water tanks, installing solar and solar submersible pumps, installing tap yards, and constructing flood-resilient platforms for boreholes.
AHPSS also actively maintains all water infrastructure in the Fangak county including conducting repairs and disinfecting infrastructure. Furthermore, AHPSS provides trainings for pump mechanics and maintains a network of woman led waterpoint management committees.
This year AHPSS program is expanding. AHPSS is planning to drill an additional 46 boreholes (34 in Fangak, 6 in Canal/Pigi, and 6 in Manyo). AHPSS recognizes that Manyo County in Upper Nile is priority 1 location for the WASH cluster with almost 99% of the population taking untreated water direct from the river or puddles. For this reason, AHPSS will attempt to reach Manyo this program year to establish some of the first durable water infrastructure in the region.
In addition, we will rehabilitate 36 boreholes which were previously submerged by flooding or have tested positive for Hepatitis E. AHPSS plans to construct 20 flood resilient platforms and convert 4 existing boreholes to water yards. AHPSS will repair an estimated repair an estimated 50 boreholes, train 40 new waterpoint management committees, conduct water quality testing at 150 water points and disinfect 150 water points
AHPSS also has a new protocol to disinfect waterpoints. Each time a borehole is created, repaired, or worked on in any way, we disinfect the waterpoint. An estimated 150 waterpoint disinfections will take place this program year.

1. Needs

Fangak County and Canal/Pigi have been a priority location for the WASH cluster in 2023 according to reports. Fangak and Canal/Pigi and both IPC phase 4, and WSC phase 4, and 2023 HRP priority 2. According to the 2023 HNO, Fangak and Canal/Pigi were identified as the counties in South Sudan with the greatest WASH needs. The report found that in Fangak, 90 per cent of the population reportedly use rivers or puddles as their main water source (2023 HNO, 62). According to the 2024 South Sudan WASH HRP, Fangak and Canal/Pigi are both intersectoral severity extreme. These locations are both priority 1 counties according to the WASH Cluster HRP Targeting Guidance as they are locations affected by multiple hazards. In addition, Manyo County in Upper Nile is priority 1 location for the WASH cluster with almost 99% of the population taking untreated water direct from the river or puddles. As noted in the 2023 HNO, poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases in places like Fangak. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs. Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe drinking water which jeopardizes the benefits gained through the nutrition program. Adding to the urgency of the WASH situation is the conflict in Sudan which is displacing people to South Sudan. The RRC in Fangak County is reporting that from April 25th 2023 to October 15th 2023, 2328 households containing at least 13,868 individuals have arrived in New Fangak with more expected on the way. These returnees have put pressure on the WASH infrastructure. According to the RRC, the number of latrines and water points is completely inadequate to meet the needs of this vulnerable and growing population. Fangak RRC has noted that returnees being registered in New Fangak are settling in other locations within Fangak County like Old Fangak, Paguir, Manajang, and other locations. This would suggest that the need for additional boreholes is widespread throughout Fangak County and the surrounding region. In addition, while there have been no official numbers reported, an IRNA report compiled after a September 2023 assessment of Canal/Pigi notes that Canal/Pigi is experiencing a “High influx of returnees from Sudan”. Reports suggest that these returnees are settling in places along the river like Atar and Diel in Canal/Pigi. Both Diel and Atar are sites where AHPASS has previously drilled functional boreholes, and we currently have supplies in the pipeline to drill additional boreholes in these locations. A recent Outbreak of Hepatitis E in Fangak County resulted in an RRT traveling to Fangak to test water from boreholes to determine if it was contaminated with Hep E. Of the 27 water points tested, 19 tested positive for Hepatitis E. AHPSS has begun disinfecting these boreholes. Another 4 tested negative, and 4 were inconclusive. This suggests the problem of Hepatitis is widespread throughout the county and all boreholes in the county should be disinfected this year. According the to 2023 HNO, GVB needs were highest in 11 counties through South Sudan, Fangak County was one of these locations. In addition, the 2023 HRP outlines Fangak as one of 12 counties to be given priority for child protection. This focus on Fangak highlights the need for GBV to be carefully considered when implementing new WASH programming. AHPSS recognized this need and will renew efforts to ensure waterpoint site selection is inclusive and considering the needs of all vulnerable groups when selecting sites for new boreholes. AHPSS will conduct 76 safety audits and establish 40 new water user committees lead by woman and representative of all groups in the community.

2. Project activities

AHPSS is planning to drill 46 boreholes (34 in Fangak, 6 in Canal/Pigi, 6 in Manyo). In addition, we will rehabilitate 36 boreholes which were previously submerged by flooding or have tested positive for Hepatitis E. AHPSS plans to construct 20 flood resilient platforms and convert 4 existing boreholes to water yards. 76 safety Audits will be conducted and AHPSS will repair an estimated 50 boreholes, train 40 new water point user committees, conduct water quality testing at 150 Boreholes, and disinfect 150 water points. This project will directly address WASH Cluster SO1: by constructing new waterpoints and rehabilitating existing water points in nutrition facilities. In program year 2023, AHPSS drilled new boreholes at 5 nutrition sites and 2 PHCCs in Fangak County. In program year 2024, AHPSS will target an additional 4 nutrition sites in Fangak and 2 in Canal/Pigi, and 3 in Manyo. Thus, the project will address WASH Cluster SO1; Contribute to reducing prevalence of malnutrition, and WASH SO 2; Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality by integrating the WASH response with health and nutrition activities and by creating new waterpoint infrastructure at nutrition and health facilities in Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Manyo. This project will address CO 2; Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties and WASH Cluster SO 3; Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster. Specifically this project will address CO 2.1 Number of people having access to an improved water source focused on Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Manyo. According to the sphere standards, the project will provide 33,000 individuals in Fangak County with an improved water source which is 70.3% of the total cluster target of 46,931 individuals. This will include 6000 individuals having access to one of the 4 new water yard to be constructed, 17,000 people having access to one of 34 new boreholes to be drilled, and 10,000 people having access to a one of 20 boreholes to be rehabilitated with a flood resilient platform. In Total, AHPSS will rehabilitate 36 boreholes in Fangak County which have been submerged due to flooding or have tested positive for hepatitis E. In Canal/Pigi, AHPSS plans to drill 6 additional boreholes targeting 3000 individuals this year. Drill locations will target areas in the north of CP along the Nile. Preliminary reports have suggested that returnees are settling in these areas, however, the numbers are inconclusive. The AHPSS drilling program in Canal/Pigi will provide 3000 individuals with improved water source which is 4% of the total cluster target of 81,751 AHPSS also hopes to drill 6 new handpumps in Manyo targeting 3000 people, or 4% of the total WASH target of 75,173. The AHPSS project will also address GBV WASH Cluster SO 4; to mitigate WASH-related GBV and disability inclusion. This will be done in part by conducting 76 safety audits. A safety Audit will be conducted prior to the drilling of each new borehole and prior to rehabilitation of a water point or construction of a flood resilient platform. In addition, a female lead water point user committee with be established and empowered to assist in the management of the water point. The project will target 8,844 child female and 6,138 Adult Female in Fangak County, or 57.6% of the total cluster target of 26,006. In Canal/Pigi the project will target 765 child female and 630 adult female which is 3.2% of the total target of 43868. Finally, the project directly addresses WASH Cluster SO 5; Provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster by creating sustainable access to water in communities living in Priority 1 or 2 which are regularly affected by flooding. Fangak,Canal/Pigi and Manyo are priority 1 location which experience regular sever flooding, influx of returnees and IDP, and chronic conflict.
