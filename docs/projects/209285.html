Project Summary

The escalation of conflict in Sudan between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) added a new dimension to an already deteriorating humanitarian situation in the country as, ten months on, the violence continues to make healthcare delivery increasingly challenging, with unprecedented needs emerging in such a short period. About 25 million people – of whom over 14 million are children – need humanitarian assistance and support, about 15 per cent of the total population of the country – have fled their homes since the conflict started. They have sought refuge within Sudan or in neighboring countries. Disease outbreaks are increasing in the face of disruptions of basic public health services, including disease surveillance, functioning public health laboratories and rapid response teams, making Sudan the largest displacement crisis in the world.
WHO project will continue focuses on ensuring and maintaining access to essential life-saving health, Nutrition (SAM) services and vital public health interventions for refugees and their closest host communities. Direct health service delivery through operationalization of 35 health facilities in 7 states of Sudan (White Nile, East Darfur, South and West Kordofan, Khartoum, Kassala and Gedarif), to cover around 850,000 refugees (South Sudanese, Eritrean and Ethiopian). The plan will be mainly focusing to address the pressing needs for high rates of communicable diseases, lack of basic services delivery and a collapse of basic infrastructure due to impact of the long-standing camps for several years.
The functionality of the clinics through provision of free-of charge health services will be supported in areas (not covered through partners), with medical supplies mainly from country pooled fund, and partially from WHO other sources. The essential public health interventions for the protection of the life, health, and well-being of targeted humanitarian caseload will directly be implemented by WHO, NGOs and states MOH through the trainings on case management and treatment protocols, Rapid Response Teams in charge of assessment and initial response to public health events such as alert investigation and initial response implemented by WHO. By using a comprehensive public health approach that allows the timely identification of imminent public health threats and vulnerabilities will guide evidence-based of effective control measures and weekly EWARS (early warning) data will be collected and analyzed.
The expansion and management of emergency health information system and early warning, alert response systems (EWARS), will be established/strengthened to facilitate the prompt identification and timely response to emergencies, that are critical for preventing the avoidable mortality and morbidity amongst the refugees and affected communities, as well as health and well-being of targeted humanitarian case-load in these areas.
Support water quality monitoring missions at refugee and other affected areas to assess free residual chlorine levels and identify source of contamination in sources of drinking waters and respond accordingly. The project support required supplies and equipment as required.
The project support integrated vector control campaigns, the activities will include vector breeding source reduction through environmental management and chemical control of aquatic stages of mosquito vector, adult control though indoor and outdoor space spraying in areas of high transmission and high adult vector density. House inspection and destruction and modification of actual and potential breeding sites for aedes mosquito will be applied in addition to the monitoring of the suppressed adults’ mosquitoes.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHO ensures the delivery of health, Nutrition and WASH services being established and made available at camps and residential areas of the refugees to reduce the exposure of risks to violence? All WHO medical kits include barrier protection equipment, and disinfectants that are biodegradable. In addition, the WHO/MOH monitoring, and supervision check list includes indicators for Universal Precaution.The mitigation procedures will be in line with the concepts and understanding of the beneficiaries in the way that will keep their dignity especially in the indoor vector breeding sites treatment.Reducing the burden of diseases for the vulnerable household, would reduce the expenditure for health protecting the families entering damaging coping strategies that will make them more vulnerable to abuses and exploitation. safe disposal of medical waste will be supported with means and training of staff on infection prevention and control

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Capacity development of the communities will be done. Clear guidelines in consultation with the communities will be provided, community feedback mechanisms with clear coordination structures will be established, and they will be provided with reporting tools based on the context. There are call centers being ran by UNICEF, which can be harnessed while the Ministry of Health is partnership with the MHPSS team are also establishing integrated call centre with free toll hotlines. Already, WHO has trained 30 PSS staff, 10 of whom will be running this community based hotlines. The organization has its internal policy to protect staff and beneficiaries from abuse. The project will participate in inter-agency initiatives and measures to promote and Implement approaches that foster accountability or feedback to affected population. The project will use standard inter-agency tools designed to improve accountability to affected population. WHO will monitor core supplies availability on regular basis and provide update to the sector

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WHO has a zero tolerance SEAH policy, a three-year strategy, and accountability framework. The organization learnt form the lessons of the Ebola response in DRC where so much was done to help the affected population, but the reputation of the Organization was damaged due to cases of SEAH allegedly perpetuated by either the health workforce (off contract types) and partners. Sudan is keen on preventing and mitigating such situation, particularly during this ongoing multiple humanitarian situations (conflicts and outbreaks). Dedicated human resources is in place through a PRSEAH Coordinator, focal points in the field offices, who will ensure that PRSEAH is center to the health deliverables of WHO. WHO is part of the SEAH Inter agency network, and currently the interim chair of the network. WHO is also part of other clusters such as the protection cluster, and the GBV sub-cluster. The leadership role of WHO particularly at the inter-agency PSEA network provided a greater opportunity to better position health emergency response in its prioritize.
