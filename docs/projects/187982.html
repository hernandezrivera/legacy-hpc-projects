Project Summary

The project aims to reach the mostly vulnerable group of people including Pregnant and Lactating Women and Children Under the age of Five with rapid nutrition and nutrition sensitive lifesaving interventions of early detection, referral, and treatment of malnourished throughout preventative and curative activities of Community Management of Acute Malnutrition, Infant and Young Children Feeding in Emergencies, Integrated Management of Childhood illnesses, in a close linkage to the nearby Health and Nutrition centers providing Targeted Supplementary Food Program, Outpatient Therapeutic Program, and Inpatient Care services to the Acutely Malnourished cases. Moreover, a tightened involvement of awareness raising of maternal and children nutrition and health will be scaled up by the dissemination of integrated key messages of WaSH, Health education, COVID-19, and optimal feeding during the first 1000 days of child's life throughout the deployed Nutrition Rapid Response Teams that will be operational in the Districts of Idleb, Harim, A'zaz, Jarablus, Al Bab. BINAA Organization for Development will support the Nutrition Programs throughout the deployment of 31 Rapid Response Teams in 5 Districts, 10 Sub-Districts, 71 Communities, and 272 Camps from the high-level severity areas. Additionally, 5 referral teams to follow the MAM and SAM cases and coordinate the treatment or transportation related to this cases.
781,628 individuals are targeted to be reached through the response (Direct and indirect population), 188,685 of them are including the pregnant, lactating women, and 592,943 of them are children under 5.( the source is HNO 2022 – nutrition sector severity and PIN)
the direct target is : 132000 CU5 and 68000 PLWs .

4 - Needs

Chronic malnutrition remains one of the major public health and development concerns in Syria, where annually, more than 0.6 million Syrian children and one out of every three displaced children lose their future development and learning potentials because of stunting. there is an obvious deterioration in nutrition status in 2021 compared to last SMART survey conducted April 2019 in Idleb and Rural Aleppo, there was an increasing rate of wasting by MUAC from 0.9% to 4.7% while the wasting levels by Weight for Height (WHZ) increased from 0.73%) to 2.5%, both differences by MUAC and by WHZ were statistically significant (p=0.004 for MUAC) (P=0.034 for WHZ).Maternal Malnutrition continues to be a major public health problem in NWS especially in areas affected by displacements where 10.8% of Women of Reproductive Age (WRA) are malnourished, this rate was higher among pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) 12.4%. The prevalence of anemia found to be 46% among children 6 – 59 months, while the prevalence of anemia among WRA was 53.85%. In line with Nutrition Sector Humanitarian Response Plan, this partnership will address and provide lifesaving and life sustaining nutrition needs in Northwest Syria among children under the age of five years and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) Idlib and Aleppo in 2 districts and 5 sub-districts hosting the largest number of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) including new IDPs.

BINAA Organization through RRTs will provide nutrition services for PLW and CU-5 as follow: Direct population: 170,000 individuals (PLWs and CU5) Children under five: 110,000 (50,000 boys, and 60,000 girls) Pregnant and lactating women: 60,000. Disabilities: 25500 individualsThat will be by implementing the following activities: 1-Establishment of (RRTs) in the targeted communities and conduct MUAC screening, distribution of Supplies, distribution of awareness brochures, referral of malnutrition CU5 and PLWs, and providing key messages about feeding and hygiene promotion. 2- Distribute of Nutrition Supplements and micronutrient powder to children 6-59 months, Supplements, and micronutrient tabs to PLWs for preventing anemia and malnutrition, hygiene kits to children 12-59 months. 3- Conduct awareness-raising sessions. These sessions will include well-being activities for children and their caregivers, in addition, to provide one to one counseling to mothers through these sessions.As a part to COVID 19 response, Binaa is going to apply adjustments of the nutrition activities to mitigate the risk of infection by integrate the (IPC) measures.All data will be registered (sex, age, and disabilities).Binaa will comply with WFP and NC reporting requirements, including: (4Ws matrix, RRTs reports, and other reports).M&E team will conduct monitoring visit to a sample of beneficiaries to evaluate the process and collect their satisfaction on the quality of services.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

During the implementation of project activities, BINAA staff will ensure compliance with ve key messages on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Feedback mechanisms will also be established and announced to the targeted populations. Gender and age disaggregated data from project coverage and impact will be collected, analyzed and routinely reported on, while project activities are monitored for improvements in self-reliance as well as beneficiaries satisfaction and resilience of both women and men. The targeted population will be involved in different stages of the project management cycle, either by contacting the Key informative persons like Local council or camp management or by direct communication with beneficiaries through the ME questionnaires during baseline, end line, PDM, PIM, and the implementation and distribution monitoring activities, and through the focused meeting which will discuss the reaching consensus about the proposed activities and laying the foundations for active participation and involvement during implementation of the project and to prevent any harm may the intervention cause.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

BINAA set out the requirements for Safety and Security and ensure the awareness of safety issues, joint operating procedures (JOPs), Security policy and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) among the staff when working in Syria along with preventing armed and sanctioned actors from interference in the projects that violates the JOPs and humanitarian principles. Moreover, BINAA has a record of all incidents related to its AoOs which assist in managing risks, update risk assessments properly and balancing the need to provide humanitarian assistance with the need to protect the health and safety of BINAA’s staff. Safety and security issues are being carefully and fully considered in the planning and implementation of all programs, projects, and operations by Security Management Team (SMT).orthwest Syria for more than six years of continued programs.The risk management has been compiled in such a way that the standard operating procedures (SOPs), policies, joint operating procedures (JOPs) and contingency plans (CPs) can be used as standalone documents; this also provides for ease of revision. BINAA tries to clearly define the risks, reducing them. It does not offer any guarantee that incidents will not occur during program implementation. BINAA works toward a comprehensive model of security risk management based on how we enable the programs. Moving from a structure of when to leave in a security situation to how can we stay and continue programming methodology.
