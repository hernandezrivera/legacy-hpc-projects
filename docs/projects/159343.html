Project Summary

The project goal is to provide community based and protection-oriented assistance to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and conflict-affected population, including returnees and persons at risk of arbitrary displacement living in the areas along or in close proximity to Line of Contact (LoC) in the Government Controlled Areas (GCA) of Ukraine, to affected persons who suffered injuries or other damage to health from mines and explosive remnants of war hazards and to elderly people living alone, the most vulnerable elderly people and people with disabilities living in the settlements with limited access along the ‘contact line’ in GCA, most of potential beneficiaries are women, also through 2 private care homes for elderly people evacuated from the territories of hostilities live on a permanent basis.
The project will be implemented through seven offices at Svitlodarsk, Toretsk, Chasiv Yar, Avdiivka, Mariinka, Maiorsk and Volnovakha in Donetska oblast and three offices in Luhanska oblast established in Shchastia, Stanytsia Luhanska and Zolote. Also, social workers from among local residents in communities with limited access along the “contact line” will provide assistance (daily care) to elderly people living alone. Interventions will include:
● advocacy for access to rights at national and regional levels and to remove any discrimination;
● protection monitoring including individual case management, protection counselling, information sharing, referrals, and assistance for implementation of UNHCR cash programme;
● mobilization and empowerment of local communities through community support initiatives;
● promotion of peaceful coexistence by small-scale infrastructure support for communities with procurement of certain services, materials, and other supplies;
● social help to residents of line of contact settlements, where the Ukrainian government does not have the capability to fully provide its services;
● psychological support and counselling for residents of line of contact settlements
● permanent housing for those who live in danger or whose houses are destroyed
● mine victims assistance

1 - Needs

At national level, Ukrainian policy, practice and law continue to be in contradiction with international standards. The government’s efforts to address the protection needs of IDPs and the conflict affected persons are of poor quality and often violate the human rights of beneficiaries. On regional level, there are often discrepancies in implementation of regulations affecting IDPs in different regions. In some of line of contact settlements Ukrainian government does not provide its services, so the need of social help is high. Forced to leave their homes or living along the line of contact, IDPs and conflict affected people in Ukraine face protection risks. Main concerns are denial of registration, denial of payments, limited access to services, arbitrary deprivation of land, homes and other property, and direct exposure to the effects of armed conflict. Elderly persons are especially affected by those risks, and affected population in some 70% are women and nearly half of them are older women. Some elderly people living alone and people with disabilities do not want to leave their homes and move to state care homes, or this is not possible due to various reasons, but they cannot take proper care of themselves either. Others can`t get to state care homes as the legislative framework of Ukraine is structured in such a way that not all categories of elderly citizens can exercise this right. Assessment of the needs of the population living in settlements with limited access was carried out during 2014-2018. According to estimates of Proliska, about 550 elderly people (most of them are women and may have disability or serious medical condition) in the settlements with limited access need assistive devices, diapers, social accompaniment, daily home-based care and hot meals. On many locations along the line of contact families are still suffering from regular shelling and are in need of urgent shelter assistance or relocation to safe areas. Localised flare-ups in the conflict, or other related emergencies, incidents damage housing and infrastructure cause large scale shelter and basic needs, especially for most vulnerable and persons with specific needs. Communities living close to the line of contact are in very specific situation, marked with security concerns, isolation, lack of the access to basic services, and under constant threat of displacement. As a demographic consequence of the conflict most of community members are women (some 70%), elderly (some 40%) and people with disability (some 10%). Their capacities to organize themselves and to use their own strengths are significantly weakened. Many of community infrastructures in the areas where Proliska is operating are in bad condition. There was no opportunity to the authorities to rebuild the infrastructure due to frequent conflict escalations. Those living in remote locations with imposed restriction of movement are not properly covered by the government provided services. Due to the protracted nature of the crisis, people’s ability to cope decreased drastically. The ability to access services deteriorated and livelihood opportunities become more limited which most of all affects elderly people and single parenting women. Thus, humanitarian actors are still the main source of the protection and assistance provided to conflict-affected population residing in areas close to the contact line. Settlements in this zone are often composed of people of different background: while many are long-term residence, certain number of IDPs and returnees also live there. In some cases, host communities have raised concerns that IDP presence is making additional pressure on already limited community resources, services and infrastructure. As long as the problem of mine contamination is relevant, the civilian population will be exposed to systematic risks to life and health. The state lacks programmes to help civilians affected by ERW.

2 - Activities & outcomes

It is critical to continue advocating for the rights of beneficiaries vis-à-vis the Ukrainian government. Efforts will be made to promote administrative practices that are consistent with applicable standards relating to internal displacement and to remove any discrimination. Similar approach will be taken with regional and local administration. Expected impact: With these interventions it is expected that Ukrainian policy, practice and law will be brought to an international standard and reflect the current needs of IDPs and people at risk of displacement. Proliska's staff conducts individual case management for vulnerable men and women with protection risks including elderly persons and persons with disabilities also to those who live in private care homes and provides assistance for displaced and other conflict-affected people of concern with the emphasis on people with specific needs. Single elderly persons in need who can`t take care about themselves will also be provided with diapers as living in isolated settlements they have no access to social services. Being present in the communities allows Proliska to trace the population movement dynamics and to identify current protection trends and concerns. Proliska will carry out the protection monitoring and will collect, verify, and analyse information obtain through various sources (field monitoring visits, interviews with PoCs, contacts with local authorities, etc.) in order to identify human rights violations, protection risks encountered by IDPs and other affected populations. They will also explore coping mechanisms for effective integration in host communities. Immediate response will include individual protection counselling, sharing of information, as well as referrals to appropriate service providers. Expected impact: Both UNHCR and Proliska will identify and measure trends and changes in the protection situation over time, and will have the information necessary to inform specific protection interventions. IDP access to appropriate services will be increased. The number of households that will be able to repair their dwellings to the adequate standards of living will increase, the number of families who will get access to adequate housing and whose right to life and security be ensured will increase. In the cases of emergency (direct damages of houses caused by shelling, forced displacement, etc.), Proliska will assist affected populations with the UNHCR provided non-food items and shelter materials for acute emergency repair. In addition, purchasing houses in safe locations will assist vulnerable persons to voluntarily relocate from isolated villages close to the contact line. Expected impact: The number of households that will be able to repair their dwellings to the adequate standards of living and to meet their basic needs will increase, the number of families who will get access to adequate housing and whose right to life and security be ensured will increase. Through the activities conducted by Proliska, within the broader concept of community-based protection, communities living in the areas close to the line of contact will be supported to develop knowledge and capacity to identify their concerns and design response to these concerns. Through implementation of peaceful co-existence projects in selected communities, where community support initiatives are already established, Proliska aims to improve community life. These projects will support communities who established self-management structures, who are able to properly articulate their needs and who can establish connections with local authorities and other relevant organization in their area. Expected Impact: the quality and availability of local social/ community infrastructure will be improved. Promotion of access, support to medical, physical and psychological rehabilitation services, will improve it for person affected from mines and ERW hazards after trauma.

3 - Indicators & targets

Advocacy interventions will be conducted on two levels: national and regional. Proliska will conduct some 20 advocacy interventions on national level and some 35 advocacy interventions on regional level. 1000 protection monitoring missions will be conducted and recorded. 15000 PoCs will be provided with consultations of a general nature by social consultants and outreach workers (Male 4600: under 18 - 1350, 18-59 – 2050, 60+ - 1200; Female 10400: under 18 - 1500, 18-59 - 4100, 60+ - 2800). Individual case management will be in place, long-term assistance will be provided to 3730 PoCs, residing on or in immediate proximity to the line of contact (Male 1140: under 18 – 245, 18-59 – 160, 60+ - 735; Female 2590: under 18 - 360, 18-59 – 1020, 60+ - 1210. 100 persons will receive targeting services (in-kind). 1000 PoCs will be reached by individual psychosocial support and counselling. Proliska staff will conduct at least 300 group consultations for at least 3000 PoCs with aim to empower affected population to defend their rights and access to services (Male 1020: under 18 - 470, 18-59 - 150, 60+ - 400; Female 1880: under 18 - 580, 18-59 - 600, 60+ - 800). Transportation support will be provided to some 57000 PoCs from vulnerable categories of persons at “Stanytsia Luhanska” EECP between “zero” checkpoints for the first time and to some 228000 PoC in total during the year. 160 elderly persons will benefit from the provision of hygiene items – adult diapers. (Male 60+ - 60; Female 60+ - 100.) Besides, acute emergency shelter support, general NFIs will be provided to 960 PoCs in acute need of assistance, who reside along the line of contact. (Male 422: under 18 – 86, 18-59 - 259, 60+ - 77; Female 538: under 18 – 96, 18-59 - 327, 60+ - 115). 20 households (48PoCs) will be supported with permanent housing. (Male 18: under 18 – 8, 18-59 - 8, 60+ - 2; Female 30: under 18 – 12, 18-59 - 16, 60+ - 2). 45 peaceful coexistence, peace-building or social cohesion projects or community support initiatives are going to be implemented/facilitated during the 1-year project period, benefitting at least 3000 conflict affected PoCs. (Male 1300: under 18 - 260, 18-59 - 600, 60+ - 440; Female 1700: under 18 - 340, 18-59 - 780, 60+ - 580) 50 mine victims will receive assistance. Proliska is committed to the accountability to affected population framework and Age, Gender and Diversity Policy by accomplishing the following: establishing effective feedback mechanisms for persons of concern and assigning focal points for processing such feedback. As AAP mechanisms Proliska will use complaint boxes situated in each field office, the keys of which will have only the head of the organization who will regularly personally deal with the complaints, also Proliska will have “hot-line”. Proliska has information about this mechanism available to all persons of concern in accessible ways, in a manner and language understandable to them (Proliska`s informational bulletin, web page, page on Facebook). Correspondingly, every feedback will be handled by Proliska`s office or referred for further processing in effective, confidential and timely manner and response will be given to the person of concern.
