Project Summary

The proposed WASH emergency interventions will improve sustainable access to safe and sufficient quantity of drinking water per (SPHERE standards) at household and community level for displaced and host community populations in the areas with highest malnutrition rates (GAM) through rehabilitation of existing water sources as WASH services remains in adequate in Bor South and Twic East counties. With the recurrent flooding, most of the water sources become contaminated and given the poor sanitation settings posing public health risk to water borne diseases and other worm’s related infection.
The action to improved existing water sources and sanitation in Health facilities for more especially facilities with nutrition Programme, schools and promotes good hygiene behaviours among IDPs, host communities, returnees and children with a special focus to mitigate risks of Gender Based Violence (GBV) for women, young and adolescent girls in Both IDPS and Host communities.
Bor South and Twic East counties are areas prone to cholera and this project will aim at preventing Cholera and other Water borne diseases occurrence and reduction of the disease burden to women and children through participation in Hygiene promotion activities targeting women of child bearing age (WCBA) in host community, Returnees and health facilities with safe access to water supply as integrated in the WASH programming.
The Project will encourage involvement and participation of the local community including women and children in all the Payams in the proposed Counties during implementation that will form strong transparency and accountability platform through engaging local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the Bomas within the Bor South & Twic East counties based on the identified needs. Hygiene promoters and hand pump technicians will include both IDPs and host community men, women, boys and girls in the targeted payams.
The local authorities, host community men, women, girls and boys will also be involved in the selection of the community and institutional water supply facilities locations putting into consideration the need for safe access for females to water supply facilities.
The Health Support Organisation has been providing basic WASH services in Bor South & Twic East counties since 2015 to date with funding from Peace Winds Japan and SSHF thus well articulates with the context and needs for WASH services.

Needs

Bor South and Twic East counties have ever been experiencing natural disaster and man made like floods, armed inter clans/tribal conflicts, and the conflict between opposition forces and government which have been interrupting the planning and provision of services to the community. It’s estimated that less than 32% of the population can access basic WASH services. Less than 27% of the existing community /institutional hand pumps are functional. The flow of returnees has made the few existing water points being over stressed and populations to get inadequate safe water supply hence people sharing dirty, distant surface water points with animals especially in dry seasons. According to the local authorities in the areas, cases of fighting at the few functional water points especially among women have been reported. Poor and bad hygiene practices among the host community in the counties is one of the biggest challenges as little is being done to equip the host community with basic knowledge of good hygiene practices and household levels in the counties. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low.During rainy season Bor South and Twic East Counties are one of the counties in Jonglei that always experience heavy floods, which always displace hundreds of populations from their homes and this also exposed the populations to high level of vulnerability of contracting water related sicknesses. The proposed WASH interventions aim at delivering integrated live saving WASH services at household and community levels targeting returnees and host community in the areas with highest malnutrition rates in Bor South and Twic East counties. The WASH Project will prevent Gender Based Violence (GBV) frequent risks occurrence for women, young and adolescent girls at few functional water sources whilst reduced WASH related GBV in Bor South and Twic East through improvement of availability to WASH infrastructures. The project prioritizes hard-to-reach areas in the targeted payams of Bor South and Twic East especially due to flood and insecurity including also poor roads conditions.This action will also prioritize hot spot areas prone to cholera within the Counties with main aim of preventing waterborne disease prevalence and reduction of the disease burden among women and children through participatory hygiene promotion activities targeting women of child-bearing age with access to safe water supply, access to safe defecation practices as integrated in WASH planning through rehabilitation of the existing WASH facilities in Health facilities, Schools in both Bor South and Twic East Counties of Jongeli State.

Activities

- 60 existing non-functional institutional and community water points rehabilitated and properly being maintained and used well in Twic East and Bor South counties.- 5 new boreholes drilled/installed being used and maintained properly by the users in the target community and institutions in targeted payams in Twic East and Bor South counties Jonglei.- 2 non functional community water yards comprehensively rehabilitated and upgraded to a complete water yard ( installed with over -head tanks and solar system), being operated and well maintained by community representatives in Twic East and South counties. - 20 water storage points upgraded to permanent structures with the tank base constructed with concrete, replacement old water tanks, fencing the water points with chain link.- 67 Water User Committees (WUC with 67x7 =467 members) formed and trained for each of the rehabilitated hand pumps and rehabilitated water yards - 40 community hygiene promoters formed and trained (20 in Bor South and 20 in Twic East county) - 8 school health clubs formed, trained and promoting hygiene promotion in their respective primary schools ( 4 in Bor and 4 in Twic East counties) - 20000 populations reached with key good hygiene practice messages ( 10000 persons in Bor and 10000 in Twic East counties)

With all stakeholders, THESOs objective is to build credibility and trust, develop supporters and champions of our project, and make sure that the needs and wishes of our stakeholders are taken into account in decision-making. Community engagement is part of a larger stakeholder engagement strategy that begins at every outset of our projects and continues well after the implementation. THESO’s approach to community consultations is to ensure that the process is ongoing and cyclical. Conducting a community consultation process will allow you to form relationships with community stakeholders and to leverage existing community assets and resources for your project. At the same time, it allows you to identify potential threats, such as individuals or organizations that may have concerns about your project or other external issues that may hinder the success of your project. It is particularly important to engage local community stakeholders in the vision development process to ensure that your project’s vision is understood, shared and supported in the community. Community involvement in the building design process is also important, especially if the project will have a significant physical impact in the community, either in its size or architectural form, or in increased traffic, noise, etc
