Project Summary

Following the Humanitarian Needs Assessments and joint other crisis and location specific assessments conducted in the 2021/2022, 2.7 million people in South Sudan are still in need of humanitarian assistance with Kapoeta South, Akobo and Bor South rating with severe and high-risk child protection concerns; child labor, forced early marriage, unaccompanied and separated children, and distress are some of the issues that have also been exacerbated by the multiple crises of floods and protracted displacement that comes with it, food insecurity and inter-communal violence/conflict which put children at risks of abuse, violence and exploitation; the HNO emphasizes the growing child protection concerns with current services not able to cover the gap. In Kapoeta South for instance, there is less coverage of child protection agencies with a high levels of child marriage and child labor attributed to their cattle-keeping lifestyle and passion for livestock. Scenarios in Akobo which is complex after the floods in 2021 and inter-communal conflict, cattle-raiding, abductions and protracted forced displacements causing distress among children and their families.
A comprehensive Child-focused, community-based Child Protection project targeting these three less-served counties; Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatoria and Akobo (East and West) and Bor South in Jonglei states is quite imperative.
The project will reach 35,000 individuals including persons with disabilities, with standard child protection interventions implemented through needs and evidence-based strategies of non-focused and focused level - 1 MHPSS activities for children and their families in a structured program; The Healing and Education through the Arts (HEART) program, is an arts-based approach to providing psychosocial support for children using expressive arts to help children – in schools, child friendly spaces, youth centers and other structured settings, individual support to extremely vulnerable children and children survivors of abuse, neglect and exploitation through comprehensive child protection case management to, strengthening community-based and community-led child protection systems by establishing and strengthening CBCPNs (Community-Based Child Protection Networks), mass awareness-raising on pertinent child protection concerns and services, community dialogues and establishment of child help desks for monitoring and reporting key child protection concerns as well as information-sharing, as prevention interventions against abuse, neglect and exploitation of crises-affected or extremely vulnerable children and their families in the target locations. The project will also strive at improving quality of child protection services through capacity building of front-line staff from both child protection on CPIE concepts comprehensive case management, Alternative care for UASC, CPIMS+, roll-out of minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian action (CPMS 2019), centrality of protection and MHPSS/PFA as well as other other sector staff to enhance their participation and in child protection creating opportunities for integration for a more holistic response to children and their families affected by multi-faceted crises
Cognizant of coordination which is the first standard of minimum standards of child protection in humanitarian contexts, the project will support the gaps of child protection coordination at the state level through strengthening effectiveness for activities of the child protection working groups and CPAoR-specific action plans. The project will support advocacy initiatives through the members of CMTF, CPWG and UASC WG. Learning will be incorporated through a case management satisfactory survey to inform our case management system in South Sudan and re-contextualize the standard operation procedures. An advocacy initiative to trigger government accountability will be implemented.

1 - Needs

The Child Protection needs to be addressed through this project include unaccompanied and separated children including released formerly abducted children especially in Akobo and Bor South (in 2021, 47 girls and 45 boys were reunified with their families after being released by their abductors in Jonglei. Reports from UNMISS/HRD indicate that 502 abductions of women and children have been reported since 2018 to date, meaning that more women and children are still in captivity and their needs of psychosocial support, appropriate interim care including basic needs and medical support, family tracing and reunification as well as reintegration, still have gaps), survivors of early marriage in Kapoeta South where early marriage is prevalent because communities are pastoralist and girls/women are exchanged for more livestock which is a source of wealth and pride - the need to engage community to end these negative practices needs to be supported coupled with comprehensive case management, CAAFAGs (children associated by armed forces and groups) in Akobo due to active conflict which triggers abduction and forced recruitment. The fact that South Sudan has not endorsed the Paris Principles that should protect children from unlawful recruitment or use by armed forces and groups leaves a gap in efforts of duty bearers in protection of children which calls for advocacy initiatives which this project will initiate. Communities in Akobo were displaced by floods in 2021 and are still unable to return to original villages as the water has not yet receded; they lost property, livelihoods, livestock, in the displacement process and food in their farms was all destroyed. The same communities also faces incidents of inter-tribal clashes which are a trigger of psychosocial distress in children and their families that could lead to negative coping mechanisms such child labor, forced early marriage and other forms of violence and neglect (Akobo IA - flood vulnerability assessment report 2021)The Child Protection Working Group for Kapoeta in Eastern Equatoria is inconsistent, does not meet coordination objectives because there are no Child Protectio actors enungh to respond to all the child protection needs in the community, leaving girls and boys exposed to risks of abuse, neglect and exploitation of which child labor and forced early marriage are prevalent according to a report on a joint assessment conducted December 2021 led by the county office in charge of children's welfare and humanitarian actors. This project will cover that gap with both implementation of appropriate and standard child protection interventions; strengthen the community based structures and case management systems and also coordinate the response - creating a safer environment for children and their families.

2 - Project activities

2. 1 Reach 515 children including children with disabilities with individual support through comprehensive case management. 2.2 Support children, parents and caregivers with focused and non-focused level-1 MHPSS interventions. These will be done through establishment of two Child Friendly Spaces for locations with displaced persons in Akobo, HEART programs sessions for children, sessions on positive parenting for parents and caregivers and inclusion of MHPSS training for CBCPNs2.3 Strengthening community-based and community-led child protection systems through establishment of 6 community-based child protection networks (CBCPNs) in target locations in Kapoeta South, Akobo and Bor South, 2.4 Setting up functional child help desks at service points such as schools, health centers and distribution points, child protection messaging as well as community awareness raising campaigns and dialogues to discuss prevalent child protection concerns in the communities2.5 Establish and strengthen 4 county-specific child protection referral pathways2.6 Training of 200 individuals on basic CPIE, comprehensive case management, CPIMS+, alternative care, PFA, and caregivers sessions.2.7 Facilitate monthly caregiver sessions for 150 parents and caregivers (87 females and 63 males) on positive discipline using the Parenting without Violence approach.2.8 Conduct a case management satisfaction survey to establish how effective our case management interventions are to inform program implementation within the case management SoPs and minimum standards.2.9 Support child-led initiatives to commemorate, Day of the African Child, International Children's day and International Day of the girl child2.10 Support advocacy initiatives on government endorsement of Paris Principles for the protection of unlawful recruitment of children by armed forces or groups.2.11 Support interventions to strengthen coordination at the state and national level. These will include Capacity Building for partner agencies including local organizations, field monitoring by the CPAoR, mid-term review, and participation of global meetings2.12 Establish two Child Friendly Spaces for children in displaced communities in Akobo West

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Several opportunities will be availed during the course of the project; at the inception, meetings will be organized with different stakeholders with representation of women, men, girls, boys and the elderly as well as youth to share the details of the project and its objectives and activities to be done, with details of the target and mode of implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Save the Children will proactively through engagement of community structures seek out those who are the most marginalized including girls, adolescent wives and mothers, children out of school, children living without appropriate care, and children with disability, to ensure they can access our program. Community and beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be established through an Accountability to Affected Persons Committee (AAP committee) that will be established, trained on ways they can monitor and report complaints with scheduled engagements with SC community mobilizers. The AAP focal persons with representation of women, men, persons with disabilities, elderly, boys and girls, will be selected from the Community-Based Child Protection Network Members (CBCPNs) to avoid formation of parallel structures and strengthen their functionality. They will be oriented on how to receive complaints and agree on how to deliver appropriate feedback in time.The AAP committee will also oversee/manage the operations at the child help desks (CHDs) that will be set up at schools and service points like health centers, nutrition outreaches or General food distribution points; they will not only be for child protection information-sharing monitoring but also general feedback and complaints on all project interventions including other sectors of concern serving the population.Monthly meetings will be held in the communities before, during and after implementation of activities. The community at inception will be encouraged to reach out to SC staff through the established platforms of child help desk and AAP committees to request for information which will be readily available.The project will also use mass media for both child protection messaging which is part of the activities and incorporate COVID-19 SOPs and any details about project interventions to ensure information is widely shared to all stakeholders including the elderly, persons with disability, religious leaders, men, women, youths, boys and girls have access to timely project information through their focal points and public places such as distribution points, health centers and schools.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will ensure all staff, volunteers and consultants upon recruitment, are oriented on child safeguarding, safe programming and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), information shared and acknowledge understanding the policies, sign and adhere to them. Clear reporting and response systems will also be put in place at the field level from community to entire field operations which is part of SC standards of operating. SC has PSEA and CSG focal persons at the field offices in Bor, Akobo and Kapoeta, and they will oversee the monitoring and reporting of incidents, conduct refresher information-sharing during the course of the project. They will also establish reporting systems that is accessible, easy to use by all staff and stakeholders to maintain its functionality.Once the staff and operations have PSEA incorporated in the work system, information sharing will be enhanced and available to all beneficiaries at all times. At the community level, PSEA messaging will be shared at all meetings and gatherings from the time of inception of the projects and during implementation. The established community-based Child Protection Committees will all be oriented on PSEA at the start of their establishment and during the life of the project, this will ensure that within their roles and responsibilities and within the monitoring and reporting of child protection incidents, structure members will also be able to report any PSEA incidents and also share the information and reporting at the child help desks which will be access points for the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries will be collected through different means including verbally and written. Community mobilisers will ensure that all beneficiaries are fully aware of their right to provide feedback and how they can do so. Information will be shared with them on beneficiary selection and targeting at the inception and during the project implementation as well as during monitoring visits and at the end of the project cycle.Beneficiaries will be deliberately asked to participate in meetings to gather their feedback and also encouraged to provide feedback directly to staff or through the selected AAP focal persons who would have been introduced to them at the start of the project or at the meetings.Feedback gathered will be compiled and detailed in monitoring reports and applied when planning continuing activities if it is established before the project ends but also used to inform project re-design in next phases by SC or other stakeholders targeting the same.SC will share all feedback through the coordination platforms for Child Protection which are the CPAoR, the Case Management Task Force (CMTF) and the Unaccompanied and Separated Children Working Group (UASC WG) to inform progress on child protection interventions and make changes in implementation and strategic focus if and where need be especially if feedback is in relation to changes in the context.All feedback will be analyses through our MEAL staff and applied to our programs not only for child protection, but other sectors as well.Feedback will also be adapted to strengthen relationships/rapport with beneficiaries, encourage community engagement and participation for ownership and sustainability of interventions.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project location is in flood-affected communities of Akobo and Bor South and the interventions reaching to persons affected by effects of floods will be done. The protection concerns and risks to children and their families as a result of loss of property during floods and protracted displacement that lead to negative coping mechanisms that expose children to risks.During awareness raising campaign and dialogues for child protection concerns in the communities of Akobo, Bor South and Kapoeta South, some resilience messaging will be included to prepare the community for flood eventualities. All child protection messaging on disaster preparedness will be included so that the community is aware of flood possibilities and make decisions for their families before the occurrence to reduce the incidents of displacement and its effects.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Through community-based engagements and interventions, GBV messaging will be incorporated because the issues that are discussed and messaged during awareness raising are aligned with the concerns that are existent and prevalent in the community of which GBV will be part of the narrative. There is a link between child protection case management and GBV case management during prevention and also response which covers aspects of referral pathways for GBV services. SC will ensure that all case workers and SC staff implementing the child protection project do have information of GBV status in the community and how to respond through referral. Linkages and coordination will be done with GBV partners under the Protection Cluster umbrella at the national and state level for Jonglei, and Eastern Equatoria state working groups. It will be ensure that all gender and age groups participate in different interventions that include dialogue meetings and selection of members to be part of community based child protection networks or the accountability to affected persons committees. All monitoring and reporting of child protection interventions will ensure that the standards of mainstreaming GBV are included especially the being part of monitoring and referrals and this will contribute to the mitigation of risk and response to GBV.During monitoring and the child protection case management satisfaction survey that is to be conducted for in the target locations of Bor South, Kapoeta east and Akobo, questions on GBV will be included and shared with stakeholders and partners on different coordination platforms to influence decisions. The project has been designed to ensure that gender norms are observed during project delivery and through project monitoring to ensure that there are no triggers of GBV in the community through the activities of the project.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

By targeting and reaching to vulnerable children with children exposed to risks of multiple child protection concerns through responsive interventions, such as case management programming, MHPSS activities, the severity and occurrence of the risks would be reduced by more than 50% and prevention interventions that focus on community-based child protection programming through strengthening systems by the establishment and support to local structures (CBCPNs) to monitor and report incidents and of child abuse, neglect and exploitation in the community, via the child help desks, holding community dialogues and campaigns on child protection concerns and negative practices that are affecting children's well being and mass messaging through media. Responsive and preventive interventions would reduce the effects of the negative impact of crises and distress faced by the people in IPC 4 locations through the planned psychosocial support interventions
