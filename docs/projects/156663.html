Project Summary

Provision of school meals for primary school children in regular schools and Cash Based Transfers to out-of-school children enrolled in informal education or alternative learning opportunities. The project target areas with high concentration of Internally displaced persons, and those with poor food security and education indicators.

Needs

According to the needs highlighted in the Needs Humanitarian Overview for 2018, 6.1 million children in Syria are in need including 1.75 million out of school children while 1.35 million are at risk of dropping out. Furthermore, more than one of three schools are damaged with more than 4,000 schools across the country remain either damaged, destroyed, used as IDP shelters or occupied by parties to the conflict resulting in a 32 percent decrease in functional schools since the onset of the conflict. Many of the remaining schools are either overpopulated or suffering from inadequate facilities or materials and a large influx of IDPs have resulted in a severe overburdening of schools. Additionally, many children in hard to reach areas have access related challenges to benefit from education. Household food insecurity leads to negative coping measures which result in children being kept out of school which eventually contributes to intergenerational poverty. Additionally child hunger and poor micronutrient intake leads to poor learning outcomes and is an impediment to physical growth and future well being of children. Findings of WFP's Food Security Assessment in Syria show that 32% of the population is extremely food insecure whereas the prevalence of food insecurity is most critical in the governorates of Hassakeh (52%), Aleppo (49.4%), Hama (46.6%), Quneitra (46.4%) and Daraa (44.7%). Around one-third of the population is 15 years of age or younger which forms a significant proportion of Syrians in need of food assistance and are also within WFP's school feeding programmes target group. Households across Syria are suffering from the effects of high food insecurity whereas the growing and large number of children out of school depicts a dismal situation where children are either out of school due to physical insecurity or economic hardship which forces them to supplement household income levels.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1: Provision of in-school snacks to primary school children enrolled in regular schools The activity objective is to provide nutritional and educational benefits and foster stability in the lives of targeted students. Implementation process:• On school days, distribution of fortified nutritious snacks will be provided to students in targeted areas to encourage regular attendance, meet half of their daily micronutrient needs, eliminate short-term hunger and improve concentration.• Capacity development and enhancement support for improved implementation by partners• Ensure that implementation of the programme takes into account the safety of children and staff• Coordination with ILO for complementary child labour sessions as part of the school’s capacity development programme Activity 2: Provision of Cash Based Transfers to out-of-school children enrolled in UNICEF curriculum B learning sessionsThe activity aims toward supporting families in returning their dropout children back to school in UNICEF supported accelerated curriculum-B programme, providing their families with voucher values that enable households to buy nutritious fresh foods from the local market. Implementation process:• Monthly disbursement of conditional food vouchers for the families based on 80% attendance of the students as an incentive to regularly attend the learning sessions.• Coordinate with UNICEF the list of schools hosting Curriculum B and children enrolled• Capacity development and enhancement support for improved implementation by partners

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

If some schools in the same geographical area are not part of the projects, that may cause inter community tension. It is also possible that schools which are not implementing the project might then experience high drop out rate. Both risks have medium likelihood and impact. Selectively assisting students in the same school (via the voucher modality) can lead to tensions between those students that receive assistance and those who do not. As mitigation measures: In kind school feeding support provides blanket coverage to all targeted children in a geographic or administrative zone to prevent tensions and/or pulling children from unassisted schools. The criteria for voucher assisted children is clearly communicated to the school authorities and also depicted through sensitization materials made available at the school level. Additionally, WFP implements its activities in the same schools as UNICEF and/or other education sector partners who are able to extend protection services to targeted schools.
