Project Summary

DRC aims to prevent and respond to the various protection threats identified in the camps 6, 10, 11,12 and in the host communities of Palong Khali and Rajapalong. DRC will provide General Protection services ( case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring, awareness raising, capacity building and community engagement in camp 6, 10, 11 and 12 aiming to avoid the overlaps in services. This will be done through the project by focusing on bolstering synergies and integrating with other protection and non-protection actors, direct case management services will be made available (wherever possible and appropriate). DRC plans to build on its success by increasing its funding for community-based safety and security initiatives. DRC will provide community-led awareness and sensitization, case management, follow-up and referrals, protection monitoring, and community-led peacebuilding and conflict resolution initiatives are just some ways in which existing community groups will be bolstered to detect better, prevent, and respond better to protection risks.
DRC will conduct disaggregated age, gender, and diversity analysis to inform its programming response (AGD). Alongside, DRC will increase its protection monitoring initiatives to better inform its protection monitoring and advocacy efforts. In order to ensure that data is systematically collected on specific vulnerabilities like PwDs, single-headed households, and sexual minorities, DRC will improve the sampling methodology and expand the staff and volunteer base's capacity to collect data on a wide variety of risks, including protection risks. Further, more women will be included in the community's volunteer force. In order to prevent unnecessary duplication of effort, DRC caseworkers will maintain close communication with the existing complaint and feedback procedures. This involves collaborating and interacting with the CFM and CFP on behalf of each case management client to ascertain that he or she is not already receiving assistance and forwarding any appropriate non-protection situations to the CFM and CFP. Besides, DRC will target beneficiaries who previously has very limited access to protection services and will also conduct outreach sessions in this area.
DRC will be providing PSEA training to its whole employees and volunteers will frequently gives the network SEA data. Additionally, DRC will participate actively in the PWG and its related sub-sectors. Going forward, DRC plans to increase the frequency with which protection monitoring data is shared with the PWG and sub-sectors in the form of summarized overviews of risks observed in the camps while also continuing to comply with routine reporting requirements by the sector and ad hoc requests on information that may arise. To ensure that refugees have access to assistance, that humanitarian aid and protection activities do not cause harm, DRC will also continue providing protection mainstreaming training to non-protection actors in accordance with the resources and guidance provided by the sector.
In the end, DRC will keep working with RRRC and CiCs to coordinate efforts to ensure timely access is prioritized, and resource gaps and duplications are avoided through coordination. Briefings on DRC's operations in the camps and efforts to increase awareness about the significance of protective measures are shared routinely in advocacy briefings with the CiCs to ensure alignment and continuing the project interventions with sustainable strategies together with other actors and partners in an integrated approach.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Based on the recent assessments and findings, an alarming rate of Protection challenges have been observed by DRC protection monitors and DRC case workers in the camps. As of the protection Monitoring report June 2022, A total of 462 protection incidents documented in this quarter (July to September) where 433 protection incidents have been recorded in camps (camps 6, 8E, 8W, 10, 11 and 12) and 29 protection incidents recorded in host communities (Rajapalong and Palongkhali). The majority of the incidents documented this quarter were safety and security incidents of the violent type (214 incidents out of 433) in camps, which represented more than half of the total incidents for this reporting period. Concerns involving violations of housing, land, and property (HLP) rights (55 out of 433) and restrictions to accessing basic services (55 out of 433) have been identified as a common trend in camps. Furthermore, concerns about sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) (42 out of 433) and population movements (52 out of 433) have been constantly growing throughout all camps. The existence of criminal and armed groups in the camps has worsened security, increasing the dangers women and girls face while not at home. Volunteers and armed group members frequently try to break into the shelters of widows and women-headed households where male family members are absent, and women and girls often report being physically or sexually assaulted by male members of the community, particularly while visiting the latrines at night or during night patrolling (Source: DRC protection monitoring, July 2022). Anonymized data from DRC's case management suggests an increase in reports of child physical abuse, child marriage, child labour, and neglect and findings from the Combined MSNA support this. The Child Protection Sub-Sector in Cox's Bazar has also reported an uptick in minors' likelihood of being trafficked or smuggled. There are numerous anecdotal allegations, particularly in Cox's Bazar and other districts, of young boys being recruited for domestic labour. Due to increased stress, decreased resources, and a lack of support services, DRC case management has documented increased instances of Violence against children in the camps (DRC Case management, GBVIMs reports). Protection monitoring in DRC ensures systematic data collection on a range of dangers, including SGBV, Population movements, HLP violations, Violence against children and access to humanitarian/protection assistance, allowing for the identification of protection requirements. PWG, GBV, and CP Sub-Sectors contribute to the needs assessment by sharing reports. According to DRC protection monitoring reports, recent months have seen a decline in the protection environment in the camps. Increases in physical abuse, forced night patrolling, forced relocation, killing, torture, and enforced disappearances committed by criminal gangs against the camp population. Increases in forced recruitment by armed organizations. The monitoring results show that many people living in the camp are experiencing high levels of anxiety, mistrust, and uncertainty. Using Violence and coercion, armed players are not only expanding their influence and control in the camps but are also progressively filling the responsibilities previously played by humanitarian organizations. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms in line with the social cohesion mandate and following Do No Harm Principle. At the same time, DRC will address the needs of both refugee and host communities. DRC will also seek to improve the inter-sectoral camp-based coordination in a coordinated manner through CCCM and integrating appropriate service delivery to refugees and host communities while maintaining a collaborative and complementary relations with the Government/CiCs and other sector agencies.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is aligned with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) plan and recognizes the importance of focusing on critical protection needs and targeted protection activities for people at heightened protection risks. In line with the rationalisation Principle #3 for promoting integrated approach, DRC will aim to fill the gaps in protection services for women, men, adolescent girls/boys and persons with disabilities with the components of protection monitoring, case management, community-based protection and gender-based violence following the integrated approach. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms in line with the social cohesion mandate and following Do No Harm Principle. At the same time, DRC will address the needs of both refugee and host communities in order to ensure Considering the Principle #4 for ensuring predicability and continutiy, DRC will ensure its continutity and stabiity of the programme together with other donor funded projects by DANIDA, ECHO, etc. Additionally, DRC has a livelihoods and economic recovery component through which it will ensure integration with protection and livelihood/economic recovery and continue integration of the interventions in both camp and host community to improve relations with refugees, the Government,and other partners in the camps. Besides, DRC will regularly interact with the District Commissioner, RRRC, sector and other government agencies and host community to ensure alignment with govt. rules. The project will aim to build synergy with the current legal protection and community based protection initiative by UNHCR and other partners to complement General Protection case management and protection Monitoring initiatives avoiding all duplication. Simultaneously, the project will ensure safety and dignity, and meaningful access and participation principles.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

DRC protection project will be mainstreamed into other programs of DRC such as CCCM and Livelihoods. Also, Gender Based Violence with protection’s other components such as case management, community-based protection, protection monitoring and livelihoods. According to ISCG Gender Profile for Rohingya Refugee Response, women and adolescent girls, who comprise 52% of the refugee population, are vulnerable to risks of abuse, exploitation, domestic violence and gender-based violence (GBV). DRC protection team will ensure mainstreaming to all sectors mentioned above and at the camp level where DRC exists through inputting need assessments and providing training to protection and no-protection staff. Additionally, producing protection sector information management products and building the capacity of the camp level sectors by the protection focal receive ToT through trained protection focal.

3.2 - Gender

While designing the project, the unique needs of men and women, boys and girls, and the population's gender diversity were considered. Data that has been segregated by gender, demographic location and will be gathered/utilized to track the indicator's improvement. Sexual assault and exploitation are entirely unacceptable in DRC and DRC has a PSEA policy and a way for employees to report SEA issues internally and initiate preventive and curative actions to consider gender sensitivity issues. The AGDM policy will be put into practice by DRC Bangladesh while the project is being carried out. A general framework for mainstreaming gender components in projects is provided by the Age, Gender, and Diversity Mainstreaming Policy, which exists at DRC and will be utilized for the project.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

DRC is communited to ensuring age including safeguarding approach and principle (best interest of children, confidentiality, do no harm) accorss its programming cycle. DRC's LLH staff and vulunteers capacity will be strengthen through the DRC's Child protection team when necessary in particular the new onbaord staff. All DRC employees and volunteers have received training on safeguarding concerns. In addition, the reporting system is in place. For the purpose of data collection tools and sampling approaches are being revised to ensure data collection can be disaggregated by AGD profile. In addition, the reporting system is in place. Besides, all aged group will be covered where adult women, men and children under 18 people will be the targeted beneficiaries and all data collected will have sex and age disaggregation, and including disability markers.

3.4 - Diversity

DRC is working towards making its services more accessible and ensure diversity for people with disabilities and minority groups by receiving technical support from HI. HI, is doing regular assessment in DRC centers and providing constructive feedback on which DRDC will take further action. Also, an action point has been developed on disability inclusion from DRC Bangladesh. While implementing its projects, DRC will apply the AGDM policy. DRC will seek to increase the accessibility of services for all age and gender categories. DRC will continue to involve the beneficiaries in each stage of planning and implementation for identifying the needs, vulnerabilities, priorities, and coping mechanisms.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

DRC has established complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms (CFRM) to receive, refer and respond to beneficiary complaints and feedback. This project will utilize CFRM mechanisms to receive and address beneficiary complaints. DRC will also seek to increase the representation of diverse groups in its community structures to increase the effective participation of more marginalized groups like PwDs, women, and adolescent, utilize its strengthen its commitment to AAP and ensure a right based and AGD approach is implemented across its program cycle. Satisfaction surveys and IPA assessments will help in understanding the client's feedback on the case management services. Regular feedback sessions will be held to monitor the quality of the participants. AAP mechanisms with GBV and culture sensitivity in all stages of project life cycles. At the same time, DRC will keep collaborating with PSEA networking Group for investigation of PSEA cases.

Yes, DRC community-based protection team received training on DRR and provide/will provide awareness sessions to the community groups so that they can contribute at the camp level while any climate and hazards occur in the camps.
