Project Summary

The deteriorating nutrition situation in the Whole of Syria (WOS) depicted a worsening trend in 2022, with the 2023 HNO projecting an even worse trend in 2023. Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition, increasing levels of acute malnutrition and emergency levels of anaemia among children and women of reproductive age. Aggravating factors promoting malnutrition include food insecurity, elevated food prices, devaluation of the Syrian pound, scarce and unsafe water use, disease spread and loss of household income. This situation calls for the need for a robust coordination mechanism at all levels that will enhance provision of an equitable, inclusive, harmonized and high-quality response at scale. The nutrition sector estimates that 5.9 million vulnerable people, including 3.7 million and approximately 0.9 million people with disabilities will require life-saving nutrition services in 2023. UNICEF as the cluster lead agency (CLA) for nutrition will undertake the below coordination functions:
Coordination and Information management: CLA will lead the coordination of the collective response planning process to clearly articulate the needs analysis, critical priorities, response strategy and funding requirements. In collaboration with other sector leads, CLA will advocate for availability of required human and financial resources as well as other strategic issues that will provide an enabling environment for a quality response at scale. The sector coordination team will strengthen the reporting systems among nutrition sector partners through use of nutrition information systems and 4Ws to enable generation of information products for an evidence- driven response. To the best extent possible, this project will support the decentralization of coordination mechanisms to lower levels to ensure a harmonized, coordinated, and inclusive response. The coordination from Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) Hub will be undertaken at national and sub-national levels through 6 sub-national sector coordinators covering Government of Syria (GOS) controlled areas.
Strengthening nutrition resilience: From a vantage point of leading the WASH and Education Sectors as well as child protection Area of Responsibility (AoR), CLA- will provide the required inter-sector support to partners in coordination with Ministry of Health to develop and adopt evidence-based strategies to address the root causes of malnutrition from a multi-sectorial approach.
Strengthening localization: In line with humanitarian principles and ‘Do No Harm’ approach, CLA will strengthen the technical and operational capacities of local actors to coordinate, plan and implement a high-quality integrated nutrition response as guided by the nutrition sector response plan. CLA, will support the development of standards and guidelines across all hub to empower communities to prevent, detect and refer to treatment all forms of malnutrition. CLA will continue to advocate for improved funding channels directly to local organizations.
Capacity building and technical support: Through a robust capacity mapping exercise, CLA will work in coordination with the Ministry of Health and sector partners across all hubs to address response capacity and coverage gaps in order to deliver a high quality response.
Contingency planning: In collaboration with other sectors, the nutrition sector will conduct risk analysis and develop scenario- based contingency plan to strengthen the collective sector’s preparedness capacity. The plan will be updated with on-gong nutrition sensitive surveillance system
Accountability to affected population (AAP) and considerations of gender and specific groups/children with disability: The sector lead will support and coordinate the mainstreaming of AAP in the nutrition response through ensuring inclusive consultation and engagement of the affected population in the entire project cycle.

3 - Needs

Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition depicted by high prevalence of stunting, increasing levels of acute malnutrition and emergency levels of anaemia among children and women of reproductive age. Aggravating factors promoting malnutrition include food insecurity, elevated food prices, devaluation of the Syrian pound, scarce and unsafe water use, disease spread and loss of household income. According to the latest HNO, 609,979 children below 5 years are stunted in Syria. About 363,556 children aged below 5 years suffer from acute malnutrition. Of which, about 287,830 will suffer from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) in 2023- a similar trend depicted in 2022. To accelerate gains in the nutrition situation, it is imperative to ensure the availability of a robust nutrition response coordination mechanism that safeguards and improve the nutritional status of the most nationally vulnerable through ensuring an appropriate response that is predictable, timely, effective and at scale. Specifically, the coordination mechanism should be able to support service delivery, Inform HC/HCT’s strategic decision-making, plan and implement cluster strategies, monitor and evaluate performance, build national capacities in preparedness and contingency planning and support robust advocacy for an enabling environment whilst ensuring accountability to affected population as a cross-cutting theme.

4 - Implementation of activities

The sector lead will provide technical support to MOH and sector partners to plan and design interventions that target the most nutritionally vulnerable people. Continuous advocacy and intersectoral coordination will be undertaken with key sectors such as WASH, Health, Food security, Education and Child Protection to address the root causes of malnutrition and strengthen nutrition resilience among nutritionally vulnerable individuals, households and communities. The sector lead will coordinate the reporting process among sector members through use of 4W tools, Activity Info, Early Recovery Reporting, RRT Kobo Tools, sitreps and other suitable channels as will be collectively agreed. The coordination team will develop systems to ensure submitted data is verified, complete and of good quality. This data will be collated, segregated by gender, age and disability and shared to OCHA and the Global Nutrition Cluster (GNC) to feed into the broader humanitarian response reporting system. Information products will be developed to inform response progress against PIN and targets to ensure an evidence- driven response. The sector lead will strengthen the AAP participatory process among sector partners to ensure communities are fully consulted, engaged, listed to and adequately supported in the entire project cycle. Coordination performance monitoring surveys in the three hubs will be undertaken as part of the collective nutrition sector

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The sector lead will coordinate and support implementation of strategies to ensure centrality of protection is mainstreamed across the collective nutrition response through the below strategies. – Prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: Work towards ensuring the prevention and minimizing as much as possible any unintended negative effects of sector's intervention which can increase children and women vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. – Meaningful Access: Work collaboratively with sector partners to promote equitable access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Geographic prioritization to inform service delivery using both static and rapid response mechanisms will be carefully analyzed and adopted as appropriate. Special attention will be given to people with disabilities. – Accountability: Promote, strengthen and expand appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities among sector partners to empower individual.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The Nutrition Sector will share relevant inter-sectorial guidance on mainstreaming PSEA in nutrition projects to all sector partners. Relevant documentation and guidance on PSEA mainstreaming including the checklist to Protect from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse during COVID-19 and IASC Interim Technical Note on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) during COVIID-19 Response will be shared to sector partners and uploaded on the online information sharing platform for quick reference. The sector coordination team will support sharing of lessons learnt among partners to strengthen systems and capacities on PSEA screening, reporting and referral to appropriate service delivery sites. The sector will incorporate relevant PSEA indicators in routine reporting for partners to support monitoring progress on agreed indicators and support as will be necessary any identified gaps, in close collaboration with the PSEA focal point.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

 Delay in obtaining the required approvals by IPs to implement projects: UNICEF will continue to advocate with WFP to develop and formalize required partnerships to implement programmes for the prevention of moderate wasting at scale.  Suspension of sector partners/operation: UNICEF will provide required technical support and capacity building to partners on adherence to humanitarian principles and financial risk management. Identified risks may be internal and external ones. CLA through different Hubs would support with the development of contingency planning and prepositioning of supplies when needed.
