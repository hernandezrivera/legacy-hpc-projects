Project Summary

Given the growing humanitarian needs in Lebanon which have been further compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic, Beirut Port explosion and continuing political and economic instability, NCA continues to build on its existing gender-based violence (GBV) program intervention, to target women and girls from all community groups, including both refugee and host communities and to ensure vulnerable Lebanese women and girls are reached specifically. The current crisis in Lebanon has had significant impact on all communities, increasing household’s vulnerabilities, limiting access to basic items and services, with women and girls being disproportionately affected in terms of reduced protections and increased risk of GBV. Global studies, current programming and NCA’s recent context analysis study on Lebanon continue to confirm that women and girls are disproportionately affected in terms of increased risk to GBV. Data has shown that women and girls are at greater risk of violence because of COVID-19 and subsequent lockdowns, are carrying additional burdens in relation to managing their households and mental health and are at risk of exploitation and abuse due to an increased reliance on negative coping strategies.
Despite the availability of safe spaces/GBV services in refugee and host communities, findings show that these are not necessarily reaching vulnerable Lebanese women and girls or that they are unaware of such services. GBV service providers including ABAAD reported increases in calls to their GBV hotlines, reporting a 9% increase of Lebanese GBV survivors accessing case management since 2020. Given the further deterioration of the situation in Lebanon, it is likely that this figure will continue to increase as needs grow. In response to these changing needs in Lebanon, NCA and its partners will ensure that community outreach targets vulnerable Lebanese women and girls in key locations, and will provide them with access to lifesaving GBV services and psychosocial and emotional support focusing on healing, empowerment, and recovery. To complement GBV response interventions, GBV risk-reduction and prevention strategies which aim to address harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality will be implemented particularly amongst vulnerable Lebanese communities with a focus on raising awareness about GBV, information dissemination about available services and referral pathways, GBV/PSS key messages, and protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA).
Through NCA’s partnership with the Middle East Council of Churches (MECC), Lebanese women and girls alongside other community groups can visit MECC’s Our Lady Dispensary (OLD) centre in Mount Lebanon to access PSS/specialized PSS services, referrals and case management. Awareness activities aim to build the capacity of women and girls to protect themselves and their families from GBV and establish relationships and networks with other women in a safe environment.
As for the GBV response, NCA and partner will provide cash assistance to families at risk following a thorough need assessment taking into consideration specific protection needs. The cash assistance will help the families afford their basic needs and reduce negative coping mechanisms in light of the socio-economic crisis in the country.

Implementing Partners

NCA is partnerning with MECC, being NCA GBV partner in Lebanon since 2015, for the implementation of this project.
The preservation of Human Dignity has been and will remain a strategic objective of MECC since its founding in 1974, through social, humanitarian and development services programs. This program has evolved throughout the MECC historical process to include relief services for the forcibly displaced people and those uprooted, in all regions of the Middle East that have witnessed civil wars, armed conflicts, humanitarian disasters and occupations. The program encompass several activities aimed at empowering people directly or indirectly affected by humanitarian crisis, ensuring their protection, advocating for their rights, providing them with basic assistance to enable them adapting to changing living conditions and reduce negative coping mechanism. MECC in Lebanon has been responding to the needs of Palestinians, Iraqi and Syrian refugees while in parallel working closely with the Lebanese vulnerable population around the country.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

NCA’s GBV interventions will aim to target women and girls survivors or at risk of GBV through response and prevention activities, by providing focussed and structured PSS and awareness sessions, case management and outreach activities in Beirut and Mount Lebanon districts. After thorough needs assessment and follow up, most vulnerable individuals will be provided with cash assistance services to help them afford their basic needs, and respond to the socio economic crisis affecting all families in Lebanon. Monitoring and evaluation tools will be conducted throughout the project to measure the impact of services provided and feedback collected from beneficiaries. NCA and partners will ensure to coordinate all aspects related to the project with other GBV actors, will participate actively in the GBV Working groups, task force meetings , and will report relevant achievements in the Activity Info Platform.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

NCA ensures that its PSHEA policy and key principles are integrated into all programmes. NCA has also developed cross-cutting checklists and capacity-builds partners on these in order to ensure key issues around accountability, do no harm and gender/age/diversity are integrated and reported on throughout the project cycle. To mitigate and respond to SEA, all NCA projects have complaint response mechanisms in place for both NCA and partners, include regular capacity-building of staff on CRMs, and community engagement to mitigate the risk of SEA and to ensure reporting channels are developed in line with the cultural/local context. NCA’s GBV projects, integrate PSEA through strong community outreach and sensitization on PSEA and reporting mechanisms as part of wider awareness-raising on GBV and available services. NCA’s GBV projects also respond to SEA directly, through the provision of survivor centered GBV services including case management.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

NCA is Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) certified; NCA’ complaints and response mechanism in line with its commitment to the CHS and aims to ensure feedback, transparency and learning for strengthening transparency and accountability. Beneficiary participation is integrated during programme design, implementation, and M&E stages of the project. NCA supports partners to strengthen the integration of cross-cutting issues such as accountability, do no harm, gender/age/diversity and the UNSCR 1325 in all projects. NCA also supports partners to develop M&E tools including feedback and satisfaction surveys to measure the quality of services and conducts assessments to ensure that NCA and partners are able to understand, respond and learn from the safety and protection concerns of women and integrate them into programming. Finally, all awareness-raising materials are developed in collaboration with the community to ensure key messages are culturally and contextually relevant and appropriate.
