Project Summary

The goal of LABDI’s project is to provide education assistance to 20,000 children directly across the BAY States thereby further reducing the rate of out of school children in the North-East by 30th December, 2022. Direct Project participants are to include 1,400 girls and 1,600 boys and provide support to 200 teachers. To this end, LABDI has planned to:
a. Construct WASH facilities in schools: the absence of functional latrines and WASH facilities has been a large deterrent to quality and access to education while raising protection concerns. Also, with the global pandemic poses threat to handwashing as a major COVID-19 preventive measure. Therefore, LABDI will construct 5 gender-segregated toilets alongside hand washing stations with soap and provide hygiene promotion messaging as well as Menstrual Hygiene sensitization within sites of our intervention.
b. Rehabilitate 10 existing classrooms within 5 schools each in Mubi North and Hong of Adamawa State. During a recent rapid assessment to these areas a remarkable need has been seen in some selected school where the condition of existing structures is not safe for learning and raises concerns. LABDI will select 5 most vulnerable schools in each of these LGAs to rehabilitate worst hit classrooms.
c. Rehabilitate existing WASH facilities: In targeted schools LABDI will rehabilitate existing latrines ensuring that gender markers are clearly placed on such and protection concerns are addressed. LABDI also will ensure that slabs for disabled persons are constructed into existing WASH structures and Menstrual Health Management is taken into account for girls.
d. Establish 3 Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and Child-to-Child Education in Mubi North and Hong. LABDI will fill in this gap by establishing a 1 in each LGA. Each CFS will be equipped with PSS materials and toys for the children. There will be adult facilitators while older children will be equipped to provide Child-to-Child Education within the safe spaces.
e. Provide training as well as mentorship to teachers in the targeted schools: the quality of education is tremendously poor within the targeted communities of Hong and Mubi North. To increase the capacity of teachers to deliver quality, learner-focused teaching, positive discipline, protection and psychosocial support to learners that come to their school, LABDI will provide mentorship training for 200 teachers of the which 120 are males and 80 are females in targeted schools. LABDI will also provide psycho social materials in all schools selected for intervention including footballs, nets, jerseys, bats board games among others. g. Provide basic classroom furniture to the schools in Hong. LABDI will work in collaboration with the SUBEB in the Adamawa state to provide teaching government approved teaching textbooks to teachers in schools of intervention. Also, other job aid will be provided to improve the quality of lessons delivered.
f. LABDI will distribute learning materials and school uniforms to the children and provide basic remuneration to volunteer teachers and CFS facilitators.
g. Conduct advocacy, sensitization and Campaigns geared towards the enrollment and retention of more learners in schools and learning centres. The project will be executed in close collaboration with the Education in Emergencies Cluster, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Women’s Affair and the State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB) in Adamawa state as well as other local structures within our communities of intervention.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020, with 935 schools closed, over 1.4million girls and nearly 1.3 million boys out of school are facing an uncertain future without access to education. The global pandemic and lockdown in country in from May to July greatly affected Education in the North-East. It is feared that a lot of children may not be able to return to their classrooms due to the effects of this lockdown. Also, in a recent SitRep shared by OCHA the number of persons targeted for Education intervention reaches 3.1 million. Additionally, in Hong, formal school attendance is 55% and non-formal school attendance is 22%. In Mubi North attendance of formal and non-formal schools were 40%. In north-eastern Nigeria, 2.8 million children are in need of education-in-emergencies support in three conflict-affected States (Borno, Yobe, Adamawa). In these States, at least 802 schools remain closed and 497 classrooms are listed as destroyed, with another 1,392 damaged but repairable. (UNICEF.org/Nigeria/Education). In a recent assessment to Hong, the female teachers in the Hong, the Government School interviewed stated the need of the provision of girls’ dignity kits as it will further encourage girls to continue in learning and strength the hygiene promotion messaging and menstrual hygiene management in the schools. Discussion with government Stakeholders revealed that learning furniture is not sufficient in some of the classrooms and there is need for recreational materials. While the government have been able to intervene to rehabilitate and construct boreholes in some of these schools a lot are still in need of rehabilitation to make learning conducive for the children in a protective environment. FGDs were conducted with parents and SBMC committee members and it was discovered that lack of adequate latrines in the schools constituted a large barrier to continuous attendance of the learners in school especially the girls. The idea of constructing gender segregated latrines was a welcomed idea. A sample of parents in an assessment conducted by REACH specified that they will want Education support to be provided to them so that their children can continue learning.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project strongly carries protection mainstreaming and safe guarding by incorporating the basic humanitarian principles, non-discrimination based on tribe, gender, age and disability. Learning facilities and Sanitation facilities will be constructed to protect learners from more harm or violence. Persons with disabilities will be taken into account while constructing learning spaces so that their access is not in any way limited. Part of the school rehabilitation program will involve fencing of the schools and CFS. Community engagement and participation will be employed to ensure the school siting was done in secure sites. LABDI as an organization has a strong PSEA, Protection and Child Safeguarding Policy guiding its operational environment. Such policy will be translated to all volunteers, partners and vendors operating on the project. The learners as well will be trained on the Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy and what to expect during the project as well as safe channels to report protection concerns. LABDI will network with protection actors to provide other services not covered by the project.
