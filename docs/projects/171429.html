Project Summary

The rehabilitation of Abu Nasser valley, which is located in Bani Suhaila, is still a priority for the local municipality and in line with its strategic urgent plan. This was conﬁrmed by WASH cluster focal point in Khan Younis and Coastal Municipal Water Utilities (CMWU), which qualiﬁed it as “in bad situation” and considered it as high priority. The valley is located in an area of AL Zana, which is at high risk of ﬂooding that might affect approximately 2,600 people. In addition, in Bani Suhaila, ﬂoods affect AL Eman and Al Thelal communities, which notably include market streets, shops, passers-by, children, etc. The ﬂooding in this area continues to damage adjacent housing units, and render public access more diﬃcult, especially for pupils in UNRWA schools, both girls and boys, as stagnant water pools are formed. AL Fukhari community is also affected by ﬂoods specially the south-east and south-west areas. In total, the action will benefit 8,911 individuals.
With the outbreak of the COVID 19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip, the situation is more critical for older people and people with chronic diseases, who are more exposed to health issues. It is also a burden for women, including female headed-households, as they are under further pressure to protect their children by engaging in more cleaning and washing tasks.
In close coordination with all key stakeholders in the WASH sector, PUI proposes to rehabilitate Abu Nasser valley in Bani Suhaila and mitigate ﬂoods in Al Fukhari community in order to protect people and their assets, including small-scale farmers’ lands from ﬂoods and harsh weather conditions, and collect rain water, which considered as environmental wealth, and beneﬁt from it for other uses.
The proposed action is to build 1 km of drainage system with rain-water harvesting gullies, and to transfer the collected water to Khan Younis lagoon. It will also support municipalities to evacuate the stagnant storm water from the low level/ﬂood-prone areas in the targeted area, through the provision of heavy machines and needed materials.

The planned action will be implemented with the Palestinian Environmental Friends association (PEF) and PUI’s local partnered community based organizations (CBO) in the targeted areas. Local partners have showed vast experience in emergency preparedness and urgent interventions in areas at risk of ﬂoods through their staff and volunteers’ network.

1 - Needs

In September 2017, PUI updated its assessment conducted in 2016 on Abu Naseer valley in Bani Suhaila. In collaboration with PUI, the municipality of Bani Suhaila held a consultation meeting with a number of people living and working in the areas surrounding Abu Naseer valley to discuss the issue of the frequent flooding, identify the needs, and propose solutions. The meeting took place in the meeting room of the municipality and was attended by local people living in the area, community leaders, men and women, head teachers of the affected schools, mayor of the municipality, municipality's engineers and PUI's representative. In August 2020, PUI contacted again with the municipality, Khan Younis WASH focal point and CMWU who confirmed that the needs are still unmet, urgent and valid. The main ﬁndings of PUI’s assessment for the Abu Nasser valley are as follows: - The change of the course of the valley due to the last wars is the main reason behind the ﬂooding and the forming pools of stagnant water. The pools of stagnant water lead to the spread of insects like mosquitoes as well as infectious diseases; - The ﬂooding of the valley threatens the passers-by, especially children, older persons and people with disabilities; - The recurrent ﬂoods hinder the movement of people and vehicles in and out of the affected areas; - The limited access to the schools caused by the ﬂooding of the valley leads to the disruption of the educational process in the surrounding schools; - The ﬂooding causes severe damages to the houses, properties, schools, and other public facilities; - The main facilities affected by the ﬂooding include 3 public schools, 2 UN schools, kindergartens, mosques, poultry farms, car repairing workshops and a primary health care center. The municipalities in Bani Suhaila and Al Fukhari indicated that main challenges faced during rainy season are: ﬂooding of rain water in the low-level areas; ﬂooding of sewage maintenance holes; ﬂooding of the main streets during the heavy raining storms due to the blockage of drainage system caused by throwing solid wastes/garbage into it; ﬂooding of agricultural, not paved and muddy roads during winter which makes it diﬃcult for farmers to reach their lands; and leakage of rain-water into houses covered by metal/asbestos sheets. Furthermore, the lack of fuel needed to operate drainage pumps and water wells especially during emergencies and the lack of the human resources to carry out the routine maintenance works and/or the emergency tasks related to ﬂoods were raised by all the municipalities. 8,911 individuals are affected by floods in five localities in Al Fukhari (south east and south west areas) and Bani Suhaila ( Al Zana, Al Eman and Al Thelal areas). In April and August 2020, PUI conducted an assessment on the impact of pandemic outbreak on most vulnerable households in the area of intervention. Some of the ﬁndings are as follows: - 87% of the surveyed population reported an increase in spending on food items, 81% on hygiene materials and 8% on non- food items. They explained that the increase in the expenditure of hygiene materials is linked to the safety and precaution measures they are following to maintain a hygienic and safe environment for their families - Vulnerable families face heightened tensions, ﬁnancial uncertainties and other pressures. Women and girls face intensiﬁed vulnerabilities; the vast majority of the surveyed households mentioned that the home duties on women and girls in general, have increased as a result of the pandemic in terms of cleaning work, and washing of clothes to avoid infection. Stay-at-home lockdowns in response to the C19 pandemic have brought an increase in women’s domestic burdens especially with schools closing; - Children are more anxious, nervous and stressed due tothe fear of being infected, closing of schools, and concerns of losing the second semester of the year; - Only 16% of the surveyed groups could afford to buy C19 protection materials (e.g. masks, gloves). It is important to note that gaps and risks in terms of shelter, further exacerbated by the C19 pandemic, disproportionately affects groups of people with speciﬁc needs and capacities, like people with disabilities (PwD), older persons, people with mental health (MH) issues, vulnerable women and girls, etc. While such groups are affected more severely, they also face increased diﬃculties in terms of accessing and receiving humanitarian aid in an equitable manner. PUI will seek to consult with varied groups of people in terms of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD), to gain a greater understanding on how the outlined issues affect them, and the ways they would like to be supported to prevent and mitigate those risks appropriately. As such, planned activities will be tailored, to the extent that it is feasible, to their suggestions. To ensure this level of diverse community engagement is maintained throughout service provisions, PUI will focus on ensuring adequate protection, age, gender, diversity and PSEA mainstreaming by mapping possible barriers, risks and opportunities for different types of people in terms of accessing humanitarian assistance and preventing, mitigating or strengthening them appropriately. PUI maintains a larger protection mainstreaming framework that is contextualized with project-specific activities and mitigation measures guided by community consultations. Considering the immense toll instances of SEA can have on the survivor, the community and the humanitarian agencies as a whole, PUI and partners recognize the ever-present need of preventing, or addressing SEA in confidential, appropriate and survivor centered manner.

2 - Activities

PUI will work closely with its local partner NGO and CBO volunteers in the ﬁve targeted localities: SE and SW localities in Al Fukhari; AL Eman, Al Zana, Al Thelal localities in Bani Suhaila. The action will benefit to 8,911 individuals ( including approximately 4000 women and girls) among the populations in the ﬁve localities: AL Fukhari, south-east area: 1100 individuals AL Fukhari, south- west area: 1250 individuals, Bani Suhaila, AL Zana: 2600 individuals, Bani Suhila, AL Eman: 1,826 individuals, Bani Suhila, Al Thelal: 2,135 individuals. PUI will implement the following activities: Activity 1: Upgrading of Abu Naseer Valley in Bani Suhila through the construction of a rain-water harvesting system in collaboration with CMWU and the municipality, which should beneﬁtto all the population in AL Zana (487 HHs). PUI in close coordination with the municipality of Bani Suhila will conduct a deep technical assessment of the needs in the targeted area in order to prepare the ﬁnal designs and BoQs for the required construction works (rain- water harvesting and drainage system). The proposed system will be constructed through carrying out the following sub-activities: - Levelling works for the valley with spreading of Kor-Kar gravels to reach the design level; - Supply and install 1km of 500 mm (20 Inch) UPVC pipes, maintenance holes, and concrete gullies (catching water basins); - Supply and casting concrete B300 for culvert rooms, ground beams and retaining walls to reinforce the valley sides in order to protect residence homes and assets; - Supply and casting reinforced concrete 15 cm thick ground slab in the beginning of the valley in order to protect the valley’s entrance; - Connecting the proposed rain-water drainage system toAl Qrara rain-water drainage system which delivers the collected rain-water to Khan Younis Al Amal lagoon. - Activity 2: Support to two local municipalities in targeted areas to enhance their emergency response capacity through the following activities: - Rent of heavy machines for emergency interventions; a contractor will be hired to support the targeted municipalities with front loader machines to evacuate the stagnant water from the low- level areas and muddy streets, make trenches, and build sand dams to prevent storm water from reaching home and assets; - Provision of Kor-kar gravels in the targeted muddy areas in order to reduce the possibility of water stagnation and to ensure the smooth movement of people, especially children ,PwD and older persons, to their workplaces, schools, and primary health centers. - Provision of fuels to operate the rain-water suction pumps and the tools needed for emergency response activities related to ﬂoods. Throughout project activities protection, gender, age and diversity will be taken into consideration in a manner that reflects the unique needs and capacities of the target community. Specifically, all planned activities will be followed by safety audits (including GBV safety audit) to prevent and mitigate possible risks associated with service provision, including SEA. As part of the larger PUI Protection Mainstreaming framework, activities that respect gender equitable norms, by promoting for example female employment in a traditionally male dominated sector (construction work) where possible, will be adopted. To further ensure minimizing and address the risks of SEA, accessible awareness-raising will be ensured with people from different groups, that might be more vulnerable to the threat of SEA (e.g. girls and women with disability, unaccompanied children, etc.). Following the necessary awareness-raising, PUI and partners have a robust reporting mechanism where people can voice their concerns regarding SEA in a safe and confidential manner. SOPs on handlining SEA complaint and dealing with SEA investigations guide the whole process and put the wellbeing of the survivors at the center of any action.

Several activities will be carried out by MEAL team in close collaboration with different project partners to maintain a regular and a close follow up at all stages of project implementation. - A comprehensive project management tool including implementation and MEAL plan will be developed at the beginning of the project in full coordination and collaboration with PUI’s operational team, PEF’s team and local partners’ teams. Monitoring visit reports will be produced regularly where the progress of project activities and the feedback collected from project targeted groups will be recorded - Throughout the project life cycle, the MEAL team will be responsible for the capitalization process, collecting and documenting sources of veriﬁcation on a regular basis. - PUI is committed to ensuring that its interventions remain accountable toward the targeted communities. Lessons learnt will be presented to PEF's civil engineers and CBOs HPs. All monitoring exercises, tools and consultations with the communities will include culturally appropriate questions to reflect on the level of protection, age, gender, diversity and PSEA mainstreaming within the project. All data collected will be disaggregated by age, gender and diversity, whereas the inclusion of different AGD groups will be prioritized. All modalities of monitoring and community consultations will be accessible to people with different characteristics, like people with disabilities or people who cannot read or write. When feedback and /or complaints draw the attention of management team about any potential improvement, a meeting is held between operation teams and management team to make the intervention more eﬃcient and effective.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is crucial in humanitarian aid delivery. PUI is dedicated in ensuring do no harm and dignity to all people accessing services regardless of their personal characteristics. PUI is currently developing a Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy to reﬂect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming strategy and on the planned community consultations, a project speciﬁc Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed Action onset. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • Action onset a protection and gender risks and mitigation analysis will be drafted and further reﬁned and triangulated with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of GBV and challenges for PwDs. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project speciﬁc and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identiﬁed risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEA is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects for protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEA, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEA reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneﬁciaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEA training will be provided to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEA will also be disseminated to beneﬁciaries. • Beneficiaries with or without speciﬁc vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or PSS services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed or drafted. At the same time, staff members will be trained on “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “ Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures” . • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reﬂect on the needs identiﬁed during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneﬁciaries. Some of the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, AGD mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, PFA, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of PwDs. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected ﬁeld staff members will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with speciﬁc protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneﬁciaries on protection related issues. While the above outline the PUI protection and gender mainstreaming framework, specific project related mainstreaming activities will be planned and implemented together with the target community, to further ensure community involvement. Successful protection mainstreaming efforts should put beneficiaries at the center of service provision, while also ensuring they are adequately involved in their planning and delivery.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PUI ﬁrst concern is to protect target groups via tailored action based on identiﬁed needs, gaps, gender analysis and priorities for all households’ members. In the context of COVID 19, this concern is higher for all stakeholders including PUI & local partner staff. Before starting the rehabilitation works in the Abu Nasser valley, all target groups will be invited for an information meeting where they will obtain information about the different planned activities (rehabilitation works) to take place. They will also receive information about safety and security practices during the implementation phase, as well as briefed on the danger of COVID-19 outbreak and precautionary measures to be taken. As described above, PUI is very vigilant in acknowledging and addressing the intersectionality of different characteristics with different needs and capacities. To ensure people with different characteristics and coping mechanisms are able to access assistance equally, speciﬁc mitigation measures will be adopted. These mitigation measures will derive from community consultations, with people from diverse groups and different vulnerabilities. The protection and gender risk and mitigation analysis that will be further enriched and complimented throughout the project duration, identiﬁes the potential risks and systematizes the provided responses.. A specific vulnerability mapping will also take place, to compliment the protection and gender analysis. The vulnerability mapping exercise will be participatory and inclusive to all diverse community groups. The outcome of the exercise will be a clearer picture of the kinds of groups within the community that might be experiencing added vulnerability, including prevalence of disability and types, prevalence of other protection concerns (e.g. unaccompanied children), age and gender groups with specific needs, etc. The community consultations, assessments and information dissemination methods and modes will be tailored to ensure people with different characteristics are included equally. The accessibility of the project consultations, assessment, FRM and PSEA reporting mechanism people belonging in different age and gender groups, as well as to people with different diversities including people with disability, will be ensured and monitored throughout project duration. PUI has developed SoPs to be applied by all due to the outbreak of COVID 19 pandemic. The SoPs are part of PUI’s contingency plan and updated on a monthly basis taking into consideration the local authorities’ recommendations notably the Ministry of health. PUI’s staff and local partners’ staff are well-aware of the SoPs and speciﬁc training sessions took place so all involved personnel is trained and aware of the importance of applying the safety and prevention measures. The SoPs details all phase of the action and necessary precautionary and preventive measures to be taken into consideration such as the use of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), social distancing, reduction of gathering to a minimum number in an open space, aid distribution measures, alternative options for monitoring and evaluation etc. At the same time, the protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reﬂect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality with also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people. Accessible information in different modalities for different groups of people regarding COVID-19 will be provided. Lastly, the continuation of the FRM, regardless of COVID-19 related changes in programming will be ensured.
