Project Summary

The IRC Child Protection (CP) intervention will focus on Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), adolescents and their caregivers by providing them case management services and linking them to specialized services that will cater for their peculiar and immediate needs arising from the effect of the conflict. This will include Health, Nutrition, Gender Based Violence (GBV) services and support from other IRC sectors while the CP team offers specialized case management services to the categories of children mentioned and their caregivers. The IRC tool kits for Socio-Emotional Learning (SEL) and the parenting skills, with 24 weeks sessions each of lessons, games and activities will be used to provide the support they require. These tool kits aim at providing children and their caregivers social and emotional support which will in turn help them become resilient and take control of their lives that have been shattered by the conflict. The intervention will also provide them with the opportunity to develop positive coping strategies through acquisition of skills that will empower them economically thereby providing them with proper means of survival. Also the intervention will provide trainings for IRC staff, volunteers and partners on the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and the Organization's Child Safe Guarding Policy (CSP) and Anti Sexual Harassment Policy (ASHP). This is as a preventive measure aimed at protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse.
The IRC will continue to be a part of the Child Protection sub Working Group (CPsWG) and contribute to achieving the overall set objectives of the CPsWG while it supports the building the capacity of local partners.

Needs

Adolescents have been affected negatively in the over nine years of the conflict in NE Nigeria with the conflict disrupting their formal education and opportunity to acquire skills that will become a source of income generation. The conflict also destroyed social structures within their communities which supported them emotionally and psychologically. The conflict has increased their level of vulnerability and left them with no choices but to engage in negative coping strategies and open to varying degrees of abuse and exploitation. They are unable to get the support they require from their caregivers and communities who are also dealing with the effects of the conflict. All this has continued to leave adolescent boys at risk of being recruited by Armed Opposition Groups (AOGs) and exposing adolescent girls to sexual abuse and other forms of GBV including early/forced marriage. The conflict has not only disrupted their learning and access to formal education and literacy/numeracy skills, it has also prevented them from access to skills acquisition. This has increased their level of vulnerability and leaves them with negative survival strategies which are detrimental to their very existence. The conflict has also left the caregivers of these children unable to live up to their responsibility of meeting the needs of the children. This failure on the parts of the parents has resulted in poor relationships between parents and children and has led to poor parenting on the part of the parents. The result of this is seen and felt in the larger community and the society at large with adolescents involving themselves in criminal acts.

Activities

This project will provide case management to UASC/other children and adolescents who have suffered or are at risk of suffering from abuse including GBV. It will include conducting assessments of individual cases identified, registering, working with children and caregivers to develop case plans and follow up. It will also afford adolescents the opportunity of developing life skills and other skills which they can use to empower themselves financially. Age and gender sensitive Children's clubs will be formed and will be a means through which children will develop social skills and develop action plans on community based activities they will engage themselves in while it also helps them identify and build positive coping strategies. Parents will benefit from one on one sessions with case workers and group parenting skills sessions that will afford them peer support from other caregivers and spouses. The female caregivers will also be placed in groups for Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLAs) based on interest shown. They will be supported with the expertise of the IRC Women Protection and Empowerment (WPE) on starting income generating activities through VSLAs.
