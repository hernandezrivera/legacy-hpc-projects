Project Summary

The project seeks to expand access to sustainable fuels and energy in Northeast Nigeria through an integrated humanitarian and market based approach.
On the humanitarian front, the project seeks to solve the immediate sustainable cooking technology and fuel needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities in Jere and Konduga LGAs in Borno State and Damaturu and Gujba LGAs in Yobe State. At least 5,000 households in these LGAs will be provided with fuel efficient woodstoves. The stove to be disseminated was developed by ICEED and has been adopted by FAO, WFP, IOM and other humanitarian actors. Each benefitting household will be provided with non-carbonized biomass briquettes, that work optimally with the ICEED stove, for at least 12 months. In addition to distribution of stoves and briquettes, the project will provide DC solar electricity home systems for at least 300 households across the target LGAs. In 2020, ICEED working with Mercy Corps piloted the provision of DC solar home systems for 650 households in Bama and Gwoza LGAs in Borno State with outstanding success. This success will be replicated by the project so the target households can enjoy the co-benefits of sustainable household lighting solutions.
On the developmental front, ICEED will seek to build livelihoods and create sustainable business for vulnerable and at-risk persons. ICEED, working with FAO in 2018, established fuel efficient woodstoves production centres in Jere and Konduga LGAs and trained 200 women and youths on the production and sale of the ICEED stoves. The project will leverage on the existing capacity to expand the skills to reach at least 200 more women and youths across the target LGAs in Borno and Yobe States. Four new and additional stove production centres will be built in the target LGAs and expanded to be able produce briquettes. The project will select a few persons with existing capacity who will support ICEED technical staff to train the 400 women and youths on fuel efficient woodstoves design, production and marketing. ICEED technical team will also train the women and youth on production and sales of non-carbonized briquettes. To further boost their business capacity, the beneficiaries will be formed into cooperatives with legal registration with appropriate authorities and provide them with starter tools and cash grants. Further, they will be engaged and mentored by the project to produce and supply the 14,000 stoves and briquettes being targeted by the project. Finally, the project will explore opportunities to link them to other humanitarian actors, private sector and the market.
In addition to the capacity development on stoves and briquettes, the project will build the capacity of 200 girl and boy youths across the four target LGAs on the design, installation, maintenance and market of solar PV electricity systems. These youth will form the core of technicians that will install the 1,200 solar home systems to be provided by the project as part of a cash-for-work scheme. Beyond this engagement, the project will form the beneficiaries into cooperatives with legal registration and provide them with starter tools and cash grants so they can establish viable solar PV businesses. A further support to the youth will be placement in apprenticeship programmes that enables them to acquire more market skills. Finally, the project will explore opportunities to link the trained youths to job opportunities with the various state governments, other humanitarian actors and the private sector. The aim is to demonstrate that crisis affected persons can be groomed to be wealth creators.

1 - Needs

Nigeria experiences remarkable household energy poverty. Access to the basic household energy requirements including lighting and cooking energy solutions is rapidly declining. According to the National Bureau of Statistics, 55% of all Nigerian households are not connected to the national electricity grid. For households connected to the grid, supply is inadequate. The Bureau also estimates that 72% of Nigerian households rely primarily on firewood for cooking using traditional 3-stone and metal tripod stoves. While the household energy poverty situation across Nigeria is worrisome, it is critical for households affected by the insurgency in northeast Nigeria. Worst hit by the insurgency and its attendant myriad of crisis are Borno and Yobe States. Access to modern lighting and cooking energy solutions which was hitherto low in the past decade has further declined across these states. In 2017, ICEED and Mercy Corps conducted a rapid assessment of household access to lighting and cooking energy across Biu, Hawul, Kwaya Kussa, Mafa and Jere LGAs of Borno State. The findings showed that outside of the core urban areas of Biu and Maiduguri, access to sources of energy including basic lighting and cooking is extremely limited. The conflict has increased already rapid rates of deforestation, resulting in degradation of watersheds. Increased fuelwood collection by IDPs and the absence of government support for agroforestry activities mean that tree cover has been decreasing dramatically. The survey further showed that grid electricity is currently only available in host community areas of Biu, Maiduguri and Jere while supply is grossly inadequate. Small generators, rechargeable or battery operated torches and kerosene lanterns are the main source of lighting for most households. Fuelwood is the main fuel for cooking but access to this fuel is becoming increasingly very limited and exacerbated by insecurity as fetching it involves taking high levels of risk, particularly for women and children. Firewood is the only affordable cooking fuel and access to this resource is significantly declining. A stick of firewood goes for about N20. An average household needs about 10 such sticks to cook 3 times daily though it will be much higher for larger families. On average a family spends about N5,500 monthly on firewood which is quite high when compared to 12.5kg LPG which sells for about N4,000 in Maiduguri and can last more than one month. Majority of families are unable to switch to LPG as a result of unavailability (only available in some parts of Maiduguri) and initial cost of purchasing the fuel. Most families are unable to afford the initial cost of cooking gas appliances while only a few families are able to afford one-off payment for the fuel. Majority of families can only be able to purchase the fuel at subsistence level (less than N300 per purchase) but the LPG market does not offer such flexibilities. Moreover, ability to pay for energy services is very limited due to reduced economic activities as a result of the insurgency. This continued prohibitive expenditure on firewood puts financial stress on already overburdened households. There have been recorded instances where households have to sell part of their food rations to be able to purchase firewood.

2 - Activities

1. Technical and business capacity development programme for 200 girl and boy youths on design, installation and marketing of solar PV solutions – This project seeks to establish a pool of 200 young technicians and marketers who will service the installations to be provided by the project. They will also be able to service the local market for households non-benefitting who want to have solar PV solutions. Proposed activities for this objective include:1.1 Technical and business capacity building for 20 youths on establishment of solar PV businesses – The youths will be trained on system design, installation and maintenance. To further enhance the capacity of the youths, ICEED will explore the use of Video Instructions for Solar PV System Installation, Maintenance and Troubleshooting. In addition they will be trained on effective ways of establishing successful solar businesses. Beyond the capacity building, ICEED will explore opportunities for mentorship and apprenticeship programmes including linkages with successful solar PV business.1.2 Provide startup kits and business linkages for the trainees – The project will provide startup kits and conditional cash grants for the trainees. In addition, ICEED will explore opportunities where these youths perform solar PV installations for government and donor-funded projects. This is in addition to linkages with established solar vendors and companies for mentorships and apprenticeships. 2. Technical and business capacity development for 200 women and youths on the production and marketing of fuel efficient woodstoves and briquettes – 2.1 Technical and business training for 200 women and youths on fuel efficient woodstoves and briquettes marketing – This training will be impact-oriented and tailored to the peculiar business needs of the of the environment. An identification and capacity needs of potential trainees will be undertaken by the project. Based on set criteria, the final 200 trainees will be selected. 2.2 Conditional cash grants for trained women and girl youths –The trainees will be provided an advance grant with the condition that further grant will be disbursed based on successful utilization of the first grant. This is in addition to linkages to the market .3. Delivery of solar lighting solutions to 300 vulnerable households – The project will procure and install solar DC lighting kits in 300 households. This will be a pilot project that will serve as the basis for further advocacy for replication. To achieve this objective, the following activities will be carried out:3.1 Selection of households – The project will liaise with community leaders and the management of IDP camps in the target LGAs to conduct an assessment on willingness and ability to pay for solar power. Based on the results of the assessment, 300 households will be selected for the installation. 3.2 Procurement of solar kits – The kits to be procured will come as single units with each unit consisting of solar panel, a box housing the charge controller and battery, 3 DC lights and a phone charging port. 3.3 Installation of solar kits by trained youths – The solar kits will be installed by the trained youth under the supervision of ICEED engineers. The aim is to ensure that with repeated practice, they will become hands-on on the job. 4. Delivery of fuel efficient woodstoves and briquettes to 5,000 vulnerable and at-risk households using a market based approach – Selection of beneficiary households will be based strictly on the vulnerability criteria. The following activities will be carried out to achieve this objective:4.1 Selection of beneficiary households – The project will rely mostly on community leaders and management of IDP camps selection will be done by the project after desk review of the selected list and onsite verification. 4.2 Procure and distribute stoves and briquettes

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

As earlier stated, women will form a very significant percentage of the trainees. ICEED experience overtime shows that women in northern Nigeria are most times restricted to certain activities. However, to ensure gender inclusion, one of the criteria for selection of trainees will be affiliation with an established farmer / potter group and association. Such criteria will expressly specify the ratio of men and women-led groups to be selected. This has especially worked for ICEED similar project in Katsina State. Where there are no established groups, ICEED will work with development partners in the selection process. Experience overtime has shown that local government and community leaders are favorably disposed to accepting what development partners already working in their communities tell them. Gender disaggregated capacity building programmes will be carried out by the project. This will reduce possible clashes because of the cultural distinctions between male and female folks in northern Nigeria. Finally, women are the primary users of the stove technologies. They are also mostly responsible for firewood gathering. Moreover, the activities of the insurgents has led to most households being women-led. Stove distribution will focus more on women-led and at-risk households. Development partners and religious organizations such as JNI and CAN will be part of the organizations to be consulted in selection of households and distribution. Though not fail proof, the idea of involving these potential partners is to ensure that, as much as possible, the most vulnerable households will be reached. ICEED experience overtime has shown that when government or community leaders are entrusted with distribution of items, most of the critical target audience are often not reached.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will set up a down/up feedback mechanism in addition to horizontal communications. Field officers will be engaged by the project who will visit each of the project communities at least once a week. Progress, complaints and feedbacks will be related through them. In addition, the project will conduct periodic village level meetings and FGDs to understand the feelings of the communities on implementation of the project and opportunities for improvement
