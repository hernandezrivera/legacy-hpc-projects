Project Summary

In the Eastern regions, the insecurity caused by the socio-political crisis in CAR since March 2013 has forced many Central Africans to seek refuge in neighbouring countries. Cameroon has been the main destination of this displaced population because of the 800 km of border it shares with CAR and also the cross-border ethnic affinities between the two countries. According to UNHCR (November, 2019), 292,863 CAR refugees from different arrival waves (2003, 2008 and 2014) are settled in the East, Adamawa, and North regions of Cameroon, mainly in the rural areas. 30% of these refugees live in 7 refugee sites while the remaining 70% live in more than 100 localities across 23 communes in these three regions. This refugee population is largely made up of children, young people and women. This demographic pressure has resulted in increased vulnerabilities, and a trio-crisis of humanitarian-peace-development challenges. The findings of JAM conducted from November 2018 to May 2019 revealed that 81% of refugees are highly vulnerable, about 58.9% of refugees are in need for immediate food assistance, out of which 38.9% need livelihood support. Overall food consumption is acceptable among refugees (63 %) and host communities (79 %), with distributed food consumption patterns across regions. The consumption rate of inadequate diets was reported by 35.7 % , 33.8% and 31.2 % of households respectively for Adamawa, North and East. Despite this stability, the refugee households rely on unstable food sources, mainly food assistance, humanitarian cash transfers and gifts. The presence of CAR refugees has greatly increased pressures on natural resources and basic social infrastructure that were already precarious. Competition over scarce resources and the consequent tensions between refugees and host communities, farmers and herders have led to an increase in food needs, as well as a deterioration in the provision of basic social services and severe environmental degradation. The multifaceted assistance provided by the Government of Cameroon and humanitarian actors is still far from optimal to ensure sustainable food security and basic needs for this population. This is due to several factors, including the persistence of insecurity in CAR (and the worldwide multiple crises which have shifted donors’ interest from the situation in CAR. This has led to a decline in resources for CAR refugees in Cameroon. A UNHCR survey (June 2018) of 4,000 people in refugee sites and host villages in the East and Adamawa regions indicates that 74% of CAR refugees (70%) prefer local integration in Cameroon as a durable solution rather than returning to their home country, mainly due to insecurity in CAR. This situation is especially of concern since, according to the 2019 JAM for CAR refugees, these population groups are worse off than host communities. Assessments also report that vulnerable local populations have had to resort to negative food-related coping strategies, reducing the number of meals (to one meal a day) and quantity of food consumed by adults in order to feed the children. A survey carried out by UNHCR-WFP in Eastern Cameroon in September 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic negatively affected income-generating activities of CAR refugees, causing supply difficulties, reducing economic activities, limiting access to fields, reducing access to seeds and other agricultural inputs, due to restrictions on movement. Due to resource shortfalls, WFP has been providing 50% food rations which has negatively impacted the food consumption of CBT beneficiaries and significantly increased the chances of households moving from the acceptable to the poor or borderline group (PDM, November 2020). Through this project, WFP is planning to provide unconditional cash transfers to the CAR refugees to ensure they can cover their food and nutrition needs and, therefore prevent them from resorting to negative coping mechanisms which would deepen their vulnerability.

1 - Besoins / Needs

WFP is proposing to provide immediate food assistance, unconditional cash-based transfers for general food assistance to 48,800 beneficiaries, and host communities/local vulnerable populations affected by shocks to prevent food insecurity, accompanying social and behavioral change communication for vulnerable households affected by the CAR crisis. The rollout of CBT in the East and Adamaoua for CAR refugees was informed by multi-sector assessments and by Transfer Modality Selection (TMS) analysis of in-kind food assistance, voucher and CBT . Both the assessments and TMS analysis recommended CBT for urban and semi-urban areas in the East and Adamaoua, with mobile money as the preferred delivery mechanisms. The choice of transfer modality of food assistance is based on solid programmatic and operational analyses, including the functionality of markets, beneficiary choice and dignity, cost effectiveness and efficiency, mitigation of unwanted effects, lead time deployment and multiplier effects on the economy. WFP will provide a food ration at 50% of the household food needs as reflected by the gap analysis of the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) constructed for the Eastern region. General food distributions will provide cash for food to households to make up for shortfalls in household food access, i.e. the difference between their food consumption requirements and what they are able to provide for themselves without adopting distress (damaging coping) strategies. WFP will explore the opportunities for economies of scale to ensure efficiency and reaching the maximum number of beneficiaries. Community leaders will be sensitized to social and behavioural change messaging, which will be accompanied by awareness-raising on food and nutrition security and will focus on diversified food consumption, sanitation, hygiene and gender equality. Additional refugees currently provided with in-kind food assistance will transition to cash-based transfers in the first quarter of 2021. To reduce the impact of inclusion and exclusion errors, in the absence of biometric verification due Covid-19, WFP and partners will be conducting a physical verification of refugees who will switch to CBT as well as those who will transition from restricted e-vouchers to unrestricted mobile money. WFP will continue to use SCOPE, the corporate beneficiary information and transfer management platform, to optimize its operations. SCOPE helps to enhance the impact of WFP operations by ensuring the right person receives the intended benefit, at the right time, in the best way. In Cameroon, WFP rolled up the SCOPE in 2019 and in 2020, and it will carry on registering beneficiaries and monitoring operations in the system in 2021. In line with WFP’s commitment to protection, vulnerable groups will be identified through participatory gender and protection analyses. Besides the mandatory corporate monitoring activities, WFP will leverage on market price and supply chain monitoring to reduce inefficiencies and to ensure that the beneficiaries get most value from their cash assistance (price, quality, assortment and service). Accountability and feedback mechanisms continue to be implemented to enable beneficiaries to express their views and provide feedback on the food assistance which will be integrated in operational and further subsequent food assistance programme.
