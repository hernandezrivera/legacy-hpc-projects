Project Summary

As of December 2023, 4.8 million IDPs reside in NWS, and more than 4.1M People in need in northwest Syria (NWS). Over 6.7 million Syrians are internally displaced, with 2.8 million in the northwest region alone, living in dire conditions. Access to essentials like safe water, food, medicine, healthcare, and livelihoods is limited, particularly in NWS. The humanitarian situation is expected to deteriorate further in 2024, with threats to access and a stalled political resolution. Livelihoods emerged as the second most common unmet need in NWS, cited by 47% of households. This is despite a higher average income per person per day (0.39 USD in NWS - below the global absolute poverty line of 2.15 USD) (HNO -2023). According to REACH Needs Assessment 2023, food and livelihood were reported as the top unmet needs by internally displaced persons (IDPs) and resident/host community members, with 43% of households lacking income and work opportunities.
Given the significant levels of displacement in Syria, IOM continues to promote durable solutions, where possible, for affected populations residing in IDP sites and planned camps, and will:
1) Conduct comprehensive assessments to identify the market sectoral gaps and potential sectors for livelihood activities.
2) Establish Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and train the CET members on how to conduct volunteer work, improve their self-reliance, draft community initiatives concept notes, and engage with the community.
3) Identify activities for CVA (CfW, BDS) through community consultations to foster social cohesion and engagement.
4) Register, verify, and select IDP and HC beneficiaries for IOM’s livelihood activities (VT, BDS and CFW).
5) Refer vulnerable households to external livelihood opportunities through IOM’s Information Counselling and Referral Service (ICRS).
6) Conduct VT labour market characteristics as identified by the market assessment or assessments in camps, followed by business grants.
7) Conduct training and provide financial or in-kind support in communities and camps able and willing to create a new business or expand a new one.
8) Provide CFW opportunities to the selected beneficiaries and related to public services and/or initiatives in camps and communities. In camps, BDS and CFW support would support beneficiaries of food assistance in moving towards more self-reliance.
9) Implement Community Support Projects (CSPs) to improve access to basic services and markets in the communities and camps through light construction/rehabilitation of infrastructures and/or provision of specialized equipment.
IOM will clearly communicate the selection and vulnerability criteria to the communities prior to selection, as well as the project’s limited resources; train implmenting partner (IP) staff and CETs on conducting effective and respectful discussions with the communities and how to manage any tensions; raise awareness with IPs and CETs on principled humanitarian assistance, work with CETs to mitigate undue influence from local actors, and train IPs on reporting any incidents or interference to IOM’s Programme and Access teams as early as possible to ensure the safety of the field teams and the integrity of the project; and conduct PSEA awareness raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities during the activities and introduce PSEAH reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. All field activities will be supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks. Community consultations play a crucial role in the implementation of CVA projects for early recovery and livelihood interventions. By adopting a community-based approach, social cohesion and engagement are fostered, allowing for the identification of market sectoral gaps, potential livelihood sectors, and the establishment of CETs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Through town hall meetings, CET and focus group discussions, IOM ensures the engagement and consultation of women and PWDs in the design and implementation of projects, such as tailoring activities to their needs. This process helps to ensure that the needs and priorities of the entire community, including women and PWDs, are taken into account in the design and implementation of the projects. PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse)-related risks are following: 1) Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by IP staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes; 2) Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA; 3) Lack of availability of and information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries; and 4) Lack of female IP staff in the field. PSEAH-related mitigation/prevention measures: 1) Code of conduct that includes PSEA language to be signed by the project partners; 2) All IP staff are trained on PSEA; 3) Porject teams and partners are well-informed about internal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity; 4) Conduct PSEA awareness raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available; and 5) Ensure that all field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks. Assumptions: 1) Security situation in NWS allows IOM to implement response activities; 2) Activities can be implemented in coordination with local councils; 3) Beneficiaries start their business after receiving cash or in-kind support; 4) Vendors in targeted areas have sufficient capacity; and 5) Community actively participates in project activities, as well as its design. Risks: 1) Security situation in NWS does not allow IOM response activities to be undertaken; 2) Local authorities are not in favor of IOM intervention or influence on beneficiary selection/aid diversion; 3) Support packages are sold by beneficiaries instead of being used as planned; 4) Vendors lack the capacity requested to provide required items; 5) Disengagement of communities during the assessment, planning and implementation stages; and 6) Social tension between the different groups resulted from the partial inclusion of community members. Mitigation measures: 1) Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, including a contingency plan to target alternative locations; 2) IPs ensure local authorities are engaged from initial stage; 3) Support packages are designed and cash assistance are provided based on beneficiaries’ business requirements; 4) Well-designed market assessment to make sure of items' availability for local procurement; and 5) IPs work closely with the communities to prioritize main sectors to be targeted.
