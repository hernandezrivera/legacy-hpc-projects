Project Summary

HRO aims to provide comprehensive support to communities affected by crises, facilitating their transition from emergency response to early recovery and sustainable development. Through a multi-sectoral approach, the project addresses the immediate needs of vulnerable populations while laying the groundwork for long-term resilience and recovery. As reported in HNO 2024, 49 % of households in NW Syria lost their jobs or are unemployed resulting in a barrier to meeting basic needs, and they are facing significant income expenditure gaps, the household incomes are at $85 and expenditures are at $183 monthly, resulting in a 115 % shortfall, based on MSNA data. HRO will support 400 individuals with a small business grant through start-up and recovery Micro, Small, and Medium-sized Non-agricultural Businesses (MSMEs) USD, based on specific selection criteria, and referrals from protection sectors ensuring support reaches the most vulnerable populations in addition to providing grants for 100 BNFs from those who already have MSME. HRO will prioritize businesses based on market demand identified during the designing phase to maximize impact and sustainability and will train 400 households (HHs) on soft skills or transferable skills.
700 BNFs will be targeted through vocational or business training, 500 BNFs in home-based businesses, and 100 individuals will enroll in an apprenticeship or benefit from on-the-job training business. HRO will conduct 40 workshops bringing together beneficiaries and private-sector employers and will create 200 short-term job opportunities.
1,000 children with severe disabilities will be provided with cash transfers and case management to improve their livelihoods and access to essential services, and 200 vulnerable households (Female-headed households, older people, etc.) will receive market-based modalities assistance. HRO will engage 400 community members and representatives in capacity-building activities for ERL needs and conduct 40 initiatives to promote social cohesion within the targeted communities.
According to HNO 2024, the lack of school furniture/ supplies and operational WASH facilities makes Learning environments unconducive. HRO will rehabilitate 10 classrooms/ schools to enhance learning conditions environments to ensure gender-responsive facilities and promote education as a key driver of recovery and empowerment. In addition, HRO will rehabilitate 5 KM of roads to enhance accessibility, connectivity, and transportation efficiency within the community, and foster economic growth and resilience. HRO will also increase access to health services through rehabilitating 5 clinics.
HRO plans to provide 200 equipment and tools to enhance public service delivery, support community development, and promote safety and sustainability within the areas it serves. As per HNO 2024, in north-west Syria, there is a notable shift towards renewable energy, with 54 % of households using solar panels, managing an average of 15 hours of electricity per day. HRO will distribute and install 10 solar systems to support public facilities and 50 to households, further advancing the adoption of sustainable energy practices and contributing to the resilience of local infrastructure.
To include and fit the PwD needs, we adapted our centers (By installing rails, ramps, seated toilets, etc..), in addition to adapting the materials and activities and training our staff on including PwD. Thus, we plan to support children with disabilities with cash transfers and case management to improve their livelihoods and access to essential services, also we will provide market-based modalities assistance for vulnerable households (Female-headed households, elderlies, PwD, etc.).
HRO has a zero-tolerance policy for exploitation, sexual exploitation, and abuse, the received complaints are seriously handled and investigated. PSEA complaints and feedback mechanisms are in place and communicated clearly to beneficiaries, including PwDs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Assumptions: The availability of skilled labor for project implementation. The security situation will not deteriorate. The HCs will have a sense of ownership over the rehabilitated facilities. Economic stability in the operating environment.The risks: Security threats such as conflict escalation. Skilled labor may face job insecurity or seek better opportunities elsewhere, leading to high turnover rates within the project. The local economy may be vulnerable to fluctuations caused by factors such as political unrest, conflict, inflation, or currency devaluation. Over-reliance on external support or funding for the rehabilitation project may undermine the community's sense of ownership and responsibility for the facilities, leading to a lack of sustained commitment.Mitigations: Conduct scenario analysis and contingency planning to anticipate potential economic shocks and develop strategies to mitigate their impact. Continuous monitoring of the security situation and provision of services where accessible. Set a response plan and design the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation to achieve the objective. Conduct regular employee engagement surveys to identify factors contributing to turnover and address them proactively. Launch advocacy and awareness campaigns to educate the community about the importance of taking ownership and responsibility for the rehabilitated facilities.HRO is an active member of the PSEA network and has safeguarding policies, including PSEA. HRO also has a strict recruitment policy for new employees, who have to sign the HLG-approved code of conduct and are provided with special orientation and guidance in this regard.Improve access to livelihood opportunities and business recovery through supporting 60 HHs with non-agricultural businesses (including those complementing and/or interdependent with agricultural value chains). - Improve access to basic services through rehabilitation infrastructure (by rehabilitating or lighting roads, rehabilitating school walls, and rehabilitating public parks).To ensure the effectiveness of the intervention, a robust MEAL framework will be implemented throughout the project lifecycle. Regular monitoring activities will track the progress of key project components, including livelihood support, infrastructure rehabilitation, and capacity building. Quantitative and qualitative data will be collected to assess the impact of interventions on targeted households and communities, with indicators such as business growth, infrastructure utilization, and knowledge acquisition being monitored closely.
