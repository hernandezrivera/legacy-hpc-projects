Project Summary

In line with the prominent sectoral needs detected in Blue Nile State target areas, this Action envisages a comprehensive intervention in WASH sector which aims at improving to access to water and sanitation infrastructures, as well as to hygiene services, while building local capacities through the existing social bodies and WASH Committees. Overall, the Action prioritizes a community-based participatory approach in all its phases.
This proposed project aligns with the three Sudan WASH Cluster objectives:
Objective I: 1.87 million crisis-affected population will access basic water services.
Objective II: 1.79 million crisis-affected populations will access adequate, protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services.
Objective III: 4.71 million crisis-affected populations will have access to hygiene promotion and other environmental health interventions.
ACTED will embed anti-COVID-19 measures in all the proposed activities. ACTED teams will receive trainings on COVID-19 preventive measures and a full set of PPE stock to be used during project activities. During activities, ACTED will ensure the beneficiaries and ACTED staff follow social distancing measures. Moreover, ACTED will install handwashing stations and provide soaps at training and distributions sites for beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

The proposal aims at increasing crisis-affected children and families’ access to equitable and sustainable WASH services through an integrated response which scales up access to sanitation, improves hygiene and water access. In Sudan, nearly one-third of HHs (over 10 million) practise open defecation (OD), which is in turn linked to poverty level and exposes especially girls/women to SGBV risks. Access to improved sanitation significantly varies between urban (39%) and rural areas (28%); 13% have access to basic handwashing; one fourth have no access to basic drinking water. At institutional level, 40% of health centres and 65% of schools are devoid of latrines; only 10% of schools have access to handwashing, due to lack of soap and running water. In Blue Nile (BN), 11.4% of population practises OD, which is widely spread in Bau and Geissan because of unsafe, few/collapsed latrines. High prevalence of poor hygiene, including OD and inadequate treatment/storage of drinking water, contributes to high incidence of water-borne diseases such as cholera, diarrhoea, typhoid. Diarrhoea kills one in ten Sudanese children and makes them more vulnerable to infectious diseases, the risk of dying for is higher for stunted children, who represent one third of the total in Sudan. 50% of acute malnutrition cases, which affects 19% of total population only in BN, are associated with diarrhoea/worm infections related in turn to OD and poor WASH. Even acute respiratory infections – another major cause of children’s mortality- are closely linked to WASH gaps. Sanitation and handwashing represent crucial barriers to faecal-oral contamination, and OD elimination can reduce diarrhoeal morbidity by 36%. COVID-19 has further highlighted WASH gaps, and exacerbated the need for improved access to clean water and WASH services as communities need to ensure regular sanitary practices such as hand washing to limit the spread of disease transmission. Furthermore, Blue Nile is one of the prone-to-floods states of Sudan. Only in 2021, over 1,834 people were affected by floods in El Damazine and Al Rusayris. Floods have impacted on access to food, safe shelters, WASH services. To date, the vast majority of the localities that are flood-affected remain in high need with minimum level of support provided. As known, Blue Nile has a long history with AWD/cholera outbreaks, which started in 2007 and repeated every year till 2010. Then, in September 2019 the Federal MoH declared a cholera outbreak, which was declared officially over on 23rd January 2020, after 202 cases and 8 deaths. In this scenario, also to lay the groundwork to prevent new outbreaks, it is crucial to rehabilitate local water pumps to ensure the usage of clean drinking water and promote improved hygiene practices among local communities, and ensure their access to hygiene items, whose shortage were recorded by ACTED during a recent assessment conducted in 6 villages around Damazine and in Al Rusayris, especially in terms of soap, jerry cans and dignity kits. Combination of above hazards, coupled with the ongoing macro-economic crisis, high inflation, increased prices of basic commodities and consequent food insecurity that reaches its peak during lean season, could have a significant impact on people, and in particular on the most vulnerable categories including children, (pregnant and lactating) women, people with disabilities. The exposure of these groups to protection risks, psychological distress, abuse, exploitation, neglect, GBV and violence in general is likely to increase. In particular, gender inequality is compounded by the destruction of essential services and economic structures. For these reasons, ACTED shall inform this WASH proposal with a solid gender- and disability-sensitive approach, which considers safety of people in charge of fetching water or using the sanitation facility, accessibility of the latrines for PwDs, and adapt its design to fit the particular need per HHs.

Local resources and expertise as well as a greening approach in procurements of all the Items will be favoured. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The Program envisages the specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups direct and encourages gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedding the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before each intervention, ACTED conducts an assessment of the security context and protection risks, followed by a regular monitoring of the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the scope and the activities of the project and fully informed on the selection criteria, which have been designed to address the protection risks and cover the sectoral needs of the most vulnerable (single women, PLWS, children, elders, PwDs, HHs with no source of income, HHs with PwDs, HHs with more children under 5 years old). Program Implementation Phase: - ACTED mainstreams gender-adapted and key protection messages during its community sensitization and trainings. - ACTED will ensure easy-to-access and gender-segregated latrines, with lockable doors and solar lamps. Sanitation facilities will be suitable for elders and PwDs. - MHM component included in the proposal. - Distances to fetch water, waiting time, safety during traveling will be addressed through rehabilitations and ad hoc protection measures. - ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment, improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusionary mechanisms. In all activities, ACTED will promote an equal gender participation. - First aid kits and protective wear are also provided to ACTED’s staff, beneficiaries and daily workers, in line with the type of activities. Accountability: - ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with experience in setting up Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM at Area office and Field office levels, where beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Human Resources - ACTED has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. ACTED also complies with The Code of Conduct for The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and the UNHCR Code of Conduct. This CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediately reported to the coordination through a standardized communication pattern and actions will be taken by the coordination. - Finally, ACTED will set-up a gender-balanced Program team to ensure a safer environment and inclusion mechanisms.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, ACTED will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly trough the safety audit tools, the CRM and the inclusion of PSEA training modules into capacity development action plans for community governance structures. ACTED has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment - ACTED prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening processes. All people employed by ACTED already have the obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. - Administration and employee cycle: - Presentation and signature of the Code of Conduct for staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected, - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. Training program related to safeguarding issues. - ACTED has an in-house training program related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at headquarters for 7000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. Iii) Specific ad-hoc training on SEA & Child Protection in countries of intervention. ACTED Complaints and response mechanisms. - As part of its on-going efforts to improve accountability to beneficiaries, ACTED will establish a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive a response in a timely manner. Complaint’s investigation & disciplinary measures - ACTED has the capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and the alleged perpetrator (ACTED SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; ACTED Child protection policy, Articles 20 to 22). In any case, ACTED must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. ACTED also endeavours to protect victims of prohibited acts (ACTED SEA policy, article 23). Proactive approach - ACTED aims at improving its gender, age and disability representativeness in its team, to the extent possible. Because sexual exploitation and abuse cases disproportionally affect women and girls, ACTED intends on implementing protection and gender mainstreaming throughout the project implementation.
