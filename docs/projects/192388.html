Project Summary

The project will identify and select food-insecure migrants and provide them with food assistance over a 12-month period. The provision of food assistance will also enable wider vulnerability screening and onward referral where necessary to cover other needs, including health, nutrition, protection services and voluntary humanitarian return, as needed. Via the food assistance outreach and distribution modality, beneficiaries will also be provided with multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA). The project will be implemented by the International Organization for Migration (IOM) in partnership with a network of outreach volunteers from civil society organizations (CSO) and community-based initiatives, whose capacity regarding protection and safe referrals will be strengthened.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Sector Objective 1: Address the unmet immediate basic needs, including food needs, of the most vulnerable Lebanese and migrant populations IOM through this project will provide food assistance to 15,000 migrants facing food insecurity. Activities will include: Coordination with the Food Security Sector Working Group to consult partners on logistical good practices for distribution of food assistance, re in-kind or cash-based, money transfer or pre-loaded cards. Targeting and outreach to potential beneficiaries via IOM community outreach workers and partner networks, registration, screening, and referral of prospective beneficiaries, through a gender-sensitive approach to assess multisector vulnerabilities. Selection of food assistance beneficiaries. Distribution of in-kind and/or cash-based food assistance on a 3 monthly rolling basis, with possibility of 3-month extension pending revised screening. Distribution of MPCA also on a 3-month rolling basis with possible extension to 6-month period. Post distribution monitoring and ongoing targeting of prospective beneficiaries to ensure continuing caseload. Case-management follow-up to refer respondents with non-food needs to appropriate support services.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As part of its implementation strategy, IOM will focus on promoting resilience and sustainability as well as ensuring IOM’s programming are safe, dignified, and accessible to all as follow: Ensure women’s and girls’ voices as well as participation of migrants led initiatives are promoted during all phases of the response. Enhance community-led initiatives by engagement of outreach volunteers to increase outreach, awareness, and referrals to available services. Train partners and outreach volunteers on protection, GBV core concepts, PFA, humanitarian principles, risk identification and mitigation measures, data protection, PSEA, AAP and code of conduct. Support evidence-based response and learning between partners using data collected through direct assistance.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IOM’s approach toward meeting AAP standards, will be guided by a range of frameworks and tools developed by IOM HQ, and designed to see key principals put into working practice. These frameworks cover a range of themes and issues, from operating in crisis contexts, gender equality, mainstreaming protection, and humanitarian principles. IOM’s AAP approach, will see a critical attention paid to upholding a number of core operational principles, including do no harm, non-discrimination, zero tolerance for sexual abuse and exploitation, data protection, principled humanitarian action, and protection.
