Project Summary

This project builds on the Strategy of the Prevention and Counteraction of Conflict Related Sexual Violence in Ukraine, which was developed with support of the United Nations Organization of Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women (UN Women) in Ukraine and the United Nations Monitoring Mission to Ukraine, and coordinated with the Office of the Vice Prime Minister for European and Euro-Atlantic integration, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine (https://www.ohc hr.org/Documents/Countries/UA/ReportCRSV_UA.pdf). The project also builds on and establishes the most important and critical goals for improving the humanitarian situation in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, which are provided by the Multi-Year-Multi-Partner-Protection-and-Solutions-Strategy-2018-2022, the Sustainable Development Goals, the Long-term Strategy of the Protection Cluster, UNHCR’s Age, Gender and Diversity Strategy, UNHCR’s SGBV Strategy and the Humanitarian Response Plan for 2020. At least 80% of all planned activities will focus on Zone 1a of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts. The other 20% of the activities will be focused on Zone 3 of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, where the response will be held in coordination with UNHCR.
The main goal of the project is to raise awareness on protection from SGBV and reducing risks of SGBV, prevention of SGBV and response to individual SGBV cases reported by women, men, boys and girls of concern to UNHCR. In addition, the project is aimed at strengthening capacity of local communities and government agencies, which in the long term will lead to improved human rights protection, gender equality at the local level, and creation of appropriately functioning and effective referral systems on protection from SGBV along the contact line between the Government-controlled areas (GCA) and NGCA.

1 - Needs

Military conflict, daily shelling, destruction of civilian housing, damaged infrastructure, human rights violations, forced displacement, economic difficulties, lack of livelihoods, high mine risks including in the agricultural lands, psychological trauma due to protracted military conflict and presence of military in civilian areas increase risks of SGBV among IDPs and persons living in areas along the contact line. The issue of SGBV remains a taboo. Existing gender norms and stereotypes normalize domestic violence in Ukraine. During the Participatory Assessment focus groups discussion on women's safety, participants expressed concerns that by majority of the community domestic violence is taken as an everyday norm and people are reluctant to report or act against domestic or gender-based violence. There are several reported cases of conflict related sexual violence, and this may be due to lack of trust in the state protection. Traditional support mechanisms, such as family, friends and neighbors have been violated during the conflict. Communities along the contact line (Zone 1a, Zone 3 for people with disabilities) suffer from a continuous military conflict that continues to threaten life and property and violate provision of basic services. Community Support Initiatives (CSI) include a series of activities aimed at mobilizing and empowering communities strengthening their ability to respond to emergencies, protect themselves and take solutions. In addition, since decision-making powers are being decentralized in Ukraine, it is important that IDPs and those affected by the conflict are included in the decision-making process at the local level and could have an impact on policy and resource. Ongoing conflict negatively affects social cohesion. In the last few years, various strategies have contributed to dissociation of community ties. This is disastrous, since maintaining such relationships will serve as a basis of a sustainable peace. In the framework of the Peaceful Coexistence (PCP) Projects in 2020, an atmosphere of peaceful coexistence between IDPs and host communities as well as conflict-affected communities along the contact line will be supported. The projects (PCP) in Zone 1a will be a component of small and medium-sized repair of social infrastructure, using a methodology that takes into account age, gender and diversity, and focuses on the needs of the local community. IDPs in Ukraine are constantly faced with numerous risks, discrimination and violation of human rights, including denial of IDP registration, permanent blockage and impossibility of obtaining social and pension benefits, limited access to services, including legal, groundless deprivation of land, houses and other property, impossibility to issue documents and solve problems in the government. Often, because of the low financial level, IDPs cannot use the Internet to communicate with their relatives or use electronic government services, including the electronic personal account of the Pension Fund of Ukraine, which is especially important for people with disabilities and the elderly. Due to lack of money and free PSS receiving psychosocial support remains an important issue for IDPs. Over the past year, more than 450 people appealed to the Slavic Heart in Sviatohirsk with a request for psychosocial rehabilitation. In the city of Sviatohirsk the number of IDPs from NGCA exceeds the number of local population. Eighteen (18) percent of IDPs are people with disabilities, 78% of the town's population are people of retirement age.The planned activities are based on the results of an assessment of the needs of boys and girls of different age groups and adult men and women of different age groups. In total, since February 2019, the project team has used a variety of needs assessment methods, such as surveys, questionnaires, focus groups and a joint needs assessment method, to ensure maximum involvement in the decision making of the affected population.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Activities & outcomes/ Indicators & targets 1) Objective 1-Prevention and protection of gender-based violence at the community level. • Slavic Heart will hold 150 information sessions on measures to prevent SGBV. Activities will be held in communities in 0-5 and 5-20 km GCA of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts. Quantitative indicator covers 2,250 people, of which at least 70% are female and 30% are male. • Slavic Heart will organize 50 information sessions on community-level on prevention measures for the affected population in Zone +20 km of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts at the request of Donetsk and Luhansk Regional State Administrations. Quantitative indicator covers 750 people, of which at least 70% are female and 30% are male. • Slavic Heart will conduct 25 information trainings for officials of public authorities and public organizations in Donetsk and Lugansk Oblasts (government officials, medical workers and education specialists), staff of international NGOs, UNHCR partners. Quantitative indicator covers 500 people, of which 50% are female and 50% are male. • Slavic Heart will initiate creation, organization and coordination of meetings of the Coordinating Councils of multi-sectoral response to domestic and gender-based violence in local communities. Particular attention will be paid to village councils along the contact line. Quantitative indicator - at least 10 rural councils have coordinating councils and work successfully. Is is especially important in order to increase the capacity of government officials and self-government bodies. • In order to enhance effectiveness of SGBV prevention activities brochures and booklets containing information on actors, both governmental and non-governmental and international organizations providing SGBV services will be produced, as well as each participant in the training session will receive a road map for responding to SGBV cases. In addition, information materials will be provided by UNHCR partners and government agencies. Quantitative indicator - at least 50,000 brochures Identification of SGBV cases provides for the following activities: • Ensuring operation of the SGBV Hotlne. • Registration of SGBV cases and monitoring of the work of state institutions in cases identified during the implementation of the project and upon informed consent from the survivor. Slavic Heart will register all identified cases of SGBV and transfer this information to the police with the consent of the survivor, followed by monitoring the consideration and response of such bodies to registered cases. All received information will be analyzed and entered in the corresponding reports. Quantitative indicator of minimum 200 cases of SGBV have been identified, of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts, • Continue to coordinate with all actors in the field of protection against SGBV to increase the possibility of identification of SGBV cases - National Police of Ukraine, UNFPA, authorities, social services, NGOs and so on. In order to strengthen coordination and awareness in the field of prevention and identification of SGBV, it is planned to organize a minimum of 4 round tables with all actors in this area. Quantitative indicator is 3 round tables in Donetsk Oblast and 1 round table in Luhansk Oblast. Following round tables, recommendations will be made to improve the work on prevention and identification of SGBV cases. Response to SGBV cases and rehabilitation of survivors of SGBV • Organization of work of Community centers. In order to increase the coverage of emergency response services, the project team is planning to work with 3 community centers, from which mobile visits will also take place in SGBV cases (Centers in Sviatohirsk, Krasnohorivka (FO Mariupol), Nyzhnie (FO Severodonetsk), on the basis of which it will be possible to receive services directly on the spot.

3 - Indicators & targets

Activities & outcomes/ Indicators & targets (continuation) Quantitative indicator of 3 community centers are created and maintained. • Organization of work of mobile brigades. Slavic Heart will organize six (6) mobile brigades that will consist of a psychologist, social worker, a lawyer (1 lawyer for 2 brigades) and a driver for emergency response to identified cases of gender violence in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts. The mobile brigades will go daily to the places of residing of SGBV survivors. Necessary comprehensive assistance will be provided on-site to minimize the impact of SGBV on the survivor. 2 brigades will be based and work in Luhansk Oblast, 4 brigades will be based and work in Donetsk Oblast. Preliminary locations of the mobile brigades are Nyzhnie (Popasnianskyi district), Stanytsia Luhanska, Sviatohirsk and Krasnohorivka. Quantitative indicator is responded and supported by 200 registered SGBV cases. • Provided legal assistance to SGBV survivors. • Inclusive Interregional Shelterfor SGBV survivors with a round-the-clock stay will be created in Kostiantynivka, Donetsk Oblast. There is a round-the-clock duty of 4 social workers of the Foundation will be organized, which will provide social support and phycological assistance to survivors for quality stay and rehabilitation at the Shelter. All the people will undergo a medical examination and receive medical assistance if necessary. Quantitative indicator - 1 Inclusive Interregional Shelter was established, at least 120 people received protection and services for round-the-clock living and rehabilitation at the Center. 2) Objective 2 Strengthening protection against the effects of armed conflict Slavic Heart will conduct and document about 100 monitoring visits related to SGBV in Sviatohirsk and Sloviansk district. During missions, Slavic Heart will monitor the situation of internally displaced persons by collecting, checking and analyzing information in order to identify human rights violations and risks associated with SGBV. Slavic Heart will ensure that individual cases are properly managed for identified security risk cases, by providing individual protection advice or referring them to other organizations, as well as tracking such cases. Access to reliable and timely information will be provided in the form of individual and group counseling in the community center. 3) Objective 3 Community self-government is supported and strengthened: This component of the project will be implemented in several Collective Centers of Sviatohirsk and partly in Zone 1a (Marinskyi and Yasynuvatskyi district of Donetsk Oblast, Popasnianskyi and Aidarskyi districts of Luhansk Oblast). Slavic Heart will be responsible for informing and support of potential communities that could benefit from community mobilization activities. In implementing community-based activities, Slavic Heart will use the community-based protection model developed by UNHCR. This will be done through: 1. trainings on mobilizing and empowering communities, on (self) management, and 2. by supporting the creation of various structures / mechanisms in the communities. In this process, Slavic Heart will hold 20 trainings for about 100 community representatives. 4) Objective 4 Promoting peaceful coexistence with local communities Projects in the interests of host and displaced communities will be implemented primarily to activate the women's movement in Zone 1a of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts (Krasnohorivka, Nyzhnie): • 10 peaceful coexistence projects identified and implemented related to Community support initiatives for building community groups' capacity to support the project; • Support to 2 community centers, special attention is paid to those centers whose activities are aimed at activating the women's movement.
