Project Summary

Over 2.6 million children and adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the north-east remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict. This project will be providing live saving emergency child protection services to vulnerable children and their caregivers households. Activities to be implemented are;
Training and building capacity of child protection actors to identify and support children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for children who have suffered grave child rights violations.
Provision of timely reliable and safe alternative care arrangement for children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is assessed as inadequate.
Identification and training of foster parents for the implementation of foster care plan including monitoring/supervision.
Provision of NFIs and dignity kits for children and caregivers in extreme vulnerable conditions.
Identification, training and supporting of caregiver and adolescent target group with Life skills opportunities and livelihood initiatives directly.
Establishment of Child Friendly spaces
Establish and monitor community-based child protection mechanism to provide appropriate mentoring and supporting mentors.
Establishment of Children Case Management Task Force
Conduct master and step-down training on psychosocial support/counseling.
Conduct family tracing and unification as well as referral to government agencies for further support.

Needs

Over 2.6 million children and adolescents (1.4 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in the north-east remains seriously at risk mainly due to the complex and protracted conflict. This project will be providing live saving emergency child protection services to vulnerable children and their caregivers householdsAt least 39,500 children in need of care and protection including unaccompanied and separated children are in need of integrated case management services. Additionally, 1,405 child-headed households will also require support alternative care and access to basic services. These children are more vulnerable to abuse, violence and exploitation if not provided with appropriate and timely protection services that include family reunification and alternative care. The conflict in North-East Nigeria has exposed threats to the mental health and well-being of children. More than 2 million girls, boys and caregivers require psychosocial support services due to the severe distress, hardship and displacement. In addition, adolescent boys and girls require basic life skills support to help them cope better with the threats and challenges occasioned by the conflict. At least 6,000 girls and boys in North East Nigeria are estimated to have been recruited, abducted or held by non-state armed groups; approximately 50% of these children are survivors of sexual and gender-based violence. Looking at the trend of release of children from administrative custody in the past two years , nearly 3,000 boys and girls are expected to be released in 2019 and will require socio-economic reintegration assistance. Sustained follow-up and community-based approaches to reintegration for children formerly associated with armed groups will be required to mitigate risks such as stigmatization and rejection within communities. More than 4,000 children have been abducted, killed, recruited or have been victims of violent attacks in the ongoing crisis. Such vulnerable girls and boys and their caregivers require socio-economic and psychosocial assistance to recover from their experiences and to reintegrate back into society. Threats to the physical security of children has been cited as a barrier to children’s access to learning. A multi-sectoral approach will be required to address the protection risks affecting children’s access to basic services.Based on the analysis of incidents reported in security assessment reports, it is estimated that 770,000 children and caregivers are at risk of being injured or killed by unexploded devices and remnants of war. Children and their families need critical knowledge on how to mitigate the risk of injury by such devices. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but it has also negatively affected the ability of parents to appropriately care for their children At least 150,000 caregivers of vulnerable children, particularly female-headed households, foster carers and single caregivers with several children, will require psychosocial or socio-economic support to assist them to better cater for children in their careAssessments identified the following main triggers of movements: voluntary relocation (24%), poor living conditions (17%), military operations (15%), improved security (14%), ongoing conflict (12%), fear of attacks (11%), farming activities (3%), flooding (3%) and involuntary relocation (1%). Dikwa is next to Bama in the receipt of new arrivals and congested camps in Borno state. The majority of new arrivals are children and most of them while in transit or captivity of the insurgent were exposed to high risk of exploitation, abuse etc and are not able to reach opportunity for positive socialization, safe and protective space for recreational and playing activities. Adolescents particularly lack opportunities to develop skills and positive role within the community. Some of the children has loss link with parent or family members. Activities

In the 12 months of implementation, GREENCODE will be implementing the following activities as a contribution to the CP-WG/Cluster Objectives.1). Training and building capacity of child protection actors to identify and support children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for children who have suffered grave child rights violations.2). Provision of timely reliable and safe alternative care arrangement for children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is assessed as inadequate.3). Identification and training of foster parents for the implementation of foster care plan including monitoring/supervision.4). Provision of NFIs and dignity kits for children and caregivers in extreme vulnerable conditions.5). Identification, training and supporting of caregiver and adolescent target group with Life skills opportunities and livelihood initiatives directly.6). Establishment of Child Friendly spaces7). Establish and monitor community-based child protection mechanism to provide appropriate mentoring and supporting mentors.8). Establishment of Children Case Management Task Force9). Conduct master and step-down training on psychosocial support/counseling.10). Conduct family tracing and unification as well as referral to government agencies for further support.11). Project routine monitoring, evaluation and reporting.12). Conduce GBV and Mine Awareness Session monthly in the community.
