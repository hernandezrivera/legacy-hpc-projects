1 - Needs

Israeli Security Forces continue to use excessive and lethal force against protesters, including children, partaking in the Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations despite the condemning findings of the HRC CoI published earlier this year. Al Mezan’s documentation shows that since the beginning of 2019, Israeli forces have killed 33 Palestinians in the context of the GMR protests, including 11 boys, 1 woman, 1 paramedic, and 1 person with disability. Another 4,934 persons received treatment at local hospitals for injuries sustained during the protests, including 1,827 boys, 43 girls and 231 women. 1,840 of the wounded were injured by live fire, including 639 boys, 4 girls and 36 women. Further, targeting of medical personnel and journalists left over a hundred injured, with some targeted more than once. Some severe injuries resulted in amputation of extremities for 19 victims: 9 men and 10 boys, leaving them with life-long disabilities and urgent need for rehabilitation and MHPSS. Despite the relatively lower turn out to the demonstrations in 2019 compared to 2018, thousands of Palestinians continue to participate every week in the demonstration. With no political solution on the horizon and no tangible ease of restrictions emanating from the blockade, the protests are expected to continue with the same intensity in 2020, with a turnout of over five thousand participants every week and higher turnout (over 10,000) in weeks commemorating land mark occasions. This will require human rights organizations working on monitoring and documentation of IHL/IHRL violations to maintain increased presence in the field to ensure prompt monitoring and documentation of incidents. The collected data and analysis of trends will feed into reporting and advocacy activities of human rights organizations and will help humanitarian actors to design and prioritize their actions. Israel continue to impose strict measures on freedom of movement as the blockade enters its 13th year. Although restrictions affect the entire population of the Gaza Strip, they are particularly detrimental to the life of patients referred to medical facilities outside Gaza to receive treatment unavailable in the coastal enclave. According to WHO monthly reports covering the period from 1 Jan. to 31 Aug. 2019, referral patients submitted 16,565 applications for exit permits to cross Erez to the Israeli Coordination and Liaison Administration (CLA), less than 70% of these applications were approved. Although the average approval rate for patients’ exit permits in 2019 is slightly higher (by 6%) than that of 2018, the number of delayed/rejected applications in the first eight months of 2019 exceeded five thousand applications (3,333 for male patients and 2,172 for female patients —including 1,100 for child patients). Should the same approval rate continue in 2020, an average of 688 applicants are projected to be denied/delayed access to medical care every month. Further, in the past 18 months, new restrictive policies hampering movement of referral patients were uncovered, including: (1) a blanket policy depriving causalities of GMR protests from exiting Gaza for treatment; (2) rejecting patient applicants on grounds of their familial relations with Hamas operatives; and (3) rejecting Gaza patients whose relatives overstayed their permits in the WB or Israel. Human rights organizations (Adalah, Gisha, PHR-I, and Al-Mezan) petitioned these forms of collective punishment before the Israeli High Court of Justice (HCJ) and successfully overturned the first policy and, to a lesser extent, undermined the other two. Similar but uncovered restrictive policies may come to light in 2020 which will require the same legal approach to be taken by human rights organizations.

2 - Activities

Activities under PC objectives #1:(1) Al Mezan field workers will continue to monitor and document IHL/IHRL violations committed against Palestinians in the Gaza Strip, with focus on violations against protestors and grave violations against children. Field workers will fully document, i.e. collected personal info, testimonies, and supporting evidence on all fatalities and cases of injuries affecting children and women, and will monitor (i.e. compile data) on the rest of cases that were treated at local hospital. Victims will be referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to receive legal counseling and intervention. PwDs in need of legal services will be visited at home by the lawyer to provide legal counseling and sign documents relevant to legal intervention.(2) Documentation data will feed into the database for analysis of violation trends and will be utilized to draft press releases, reports, factsheets, monthly statistical updates on IHL/IHRL violations and violations against children. The produced materials will be disseminated to over 700 professionals, media organizations, and NGOs through existing mailing list, and will also be shared via Al Mezan’s website and social media platforms. Data will also be used to answers requests for sharing information received on weekly basis from humanitarian actors working in oPt. (3) Locally, representatives of Al Mezan will meet in Gaza with members of the international community to brief them on violation trends and protection concerns. Field visits will also be arranged when possible. Activities under PC objective #2: (5) Al Mezan lawyers will provide legal representation before Israeli administrative authorities for patients delayed/denied exit permits to access treatment outside Gaza. In case of child patients, intervention will also seek to secure exit permits for the child’s companion.(6) Al Mezan will join forces with human rights organization in Israel to petition restrictive measures on patients’ freedom of movement to the Israeli HCJ similar to petitions made in the last 18 months. HRP and FTS:Al Mezan will report on relevant indicators to HRP periodic reporting and will also disclose financial contribution towards the proposed intervention on the FTS. Fieldwork Methodology:(1) Monitoring phase: On the days of the protests, typically every Friday, fieldworkers will be deployed to the protests’ gathering sites to monitor developments and collect preliminary information; while others will be deployed at local hospitals to collect preliminary data on fatalities and injuries.(2) Documentation phase: Fieldworkers carry out documentation work during the weekdays whereby visits are conducted to meet and interview victims and eyewitness, take testimonials, and collect supporting evidence. During interviews with victims/eyewitnesses, fieldworkers use questionnaire to which incorporates a large set of questions – 95% of which are closed questions to facilitate data analysis and the rest are open ended to capture qualitative information such as descriptions of incidents. (3) Verification and data entry: Data from questionnaires is corroborated with other sources such physical evidence and medical reports before entry to the database. Under the proposed action, 2 additional fieldworkers will be deployed in each district of the Gaza Strip to expedite the time-consuming process of locating and contacting victims during the documentation phase. Experience over the past year showed that preliminary data acquired from local hospitals during the monitoring phase contains discrepancies on victims who are treated and discharged shortly thereafter; on some occasions, it lacked any means of communication and had non-specific location of residence, which would require more effort and time to be spent during the documentation phase to locate the victims. By deploying 10 additional fieldworkers, Al Mezan will ensure all documentation are concluded in timely fashion.

3 - Indicators

Legal interventions methodology:(1) lawyers will represent referral patients before the CLA at Erez: official communication with relevant information and supporting documents is sent prior to the patient’s hospital appointment; further communication is made by email and phone in the following days leading up to the hospital appointment date. In some cases, when multiple requests for the same patient fail to secure an exit permit, lawyers can express intent to resort to court by sending a preliminary petition to the Israeli authorities which may resolve the issue to avoid going to court. (2) Regarding joint petition before the Israeli HCJ: this intervention will be initiated once a suitable case with several victims is identified and can potentially benefit victims beyond those directly represented and thus can reduce the number of needed responsive interventions (complaints to CLA) if the court ruled in favor of the plaintiffs. (55 women, 40 men, and 30 children); Monitoring and Evaluation:Monitoring:Fieldwork coordinator will be in constant contact with (senior) fieldworkers out in the field throughout the day. The coordinator heads a weekly meeting (typically every Thursday) attended by senior fieldworkers, database administrator and data entry officer where they go over data gathered during the week and identify any gaps that require additional collection of data, evidence, or testimonials. they also discuss their deployment plan for the coming days based on trends of violations identified over the course of the previous weeks. The database entry records will serve to gauge progress made and any pitfalls during implementation and inform management decisions, the data will be presented by the database administrator and/or the data entry officer to the fieldwork coordinator during the above-mentioned meeting. Evaluation: Beneficiary of legal counseling and representation are solicited to provide feedback and suggestions using a questionnaire specifically designed to measure satisfaction of legal aid recipients. Al Mezan organizes at least one evaluation session annually (in January) whereby a gender balanced group of beneficiaries is invited to participate in focus group discussions to elicit their feedback for Al Mezan’s annual evaluation process. Al Mezan’s training staff is responsible for collection of data with support from internal focal points, including the fundraising officer and project coordinator. Data is analyzed and used for learning, planning and reporting.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Given the fact that affected communities are the main source of data for Monitoring and Documentation, their involvement starts at an early stage when fieldworkers obtain testimonies from victims and eyewitnesses, and in the process, victims are provided with explanation of the work and its expected outcome. Further, victims of violations are referred to Al Mezan’s Legal Aid Unit to discuss their legal options. Legal services are implemented in a manner that ensures respect of protection mainstreaming principles, most importantly ‘do no harm’ principle. The recipients of legal services are informed of possible ramifications of the legal actions, and it will be made clear that the final decisions to initiate any legal action is theirs to take. Al Mezan's code of conduct, read and signed by all staff members, sets clear guidelines for service providers on dealing with beneficiaries, particularly children who in case of being the precipitant of any service must be accompanied by their legal guardian at all times.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Monitoring of IHL/IHRL violations will encompass all affected communities in the Gaza Strip (children, women, men) while documentation work will cover all cases of killings and priorities documenting cases of injuries for these groups in the following order: children; women; PwDs; medical personnel; and journalists (during documentation of injuries, cases with severe to moderate injuries will take precedence over cases with below-moderate injuries). Al Mezan will ensure recruitment of at least one female fieldworker to accommodate female victims of human rights violations who due to their social background will prefer to be interviewed by a female fieldworker. During monitoring and documentation action, victims who are identified by fieldworkers as potential beneficiaries of legal intervention are refereed to lawyers. In circumstances were victim's condition/injures hinder his/her ability to reach Al Mezan's offices, lawyers will arrange a home visit to provide legal aid, take affidavits, and sign power of attorney.
