Project Summary

The populations living in returning areas and areas that have been newly liberated from ISIS occupation face serious protection risks in Hamdaniya, Mosul and Telafar districts of Ninewa Governorate. Situation with returnees in some parts of Hamdaniya and Telafar (Zummar) deteriorated after disputes between Bagdad government and KRG after Kurdish referendum in September 2017.
Conflict, displacement, family separation, and other factors have exacerbated gender-based violence (GBV). Societal norms, attitudes and beliefs became more pronounced in this context, leading to increase in forced&early marriage, and other forms of sexual violence and exploitation, particularly against women and girls.
GBV has become a widespread epidemic in this conflict. Sexual violence was used as a tactic by the ISIS against women and girls, as well as men and boys, of a particular ethnic and religious minority. Those suffering under ISIS were often victims of sexual slavery, abduction, torture and abuse. Families tend to rely on negative coping mechanisms due to lack of resources , which .can limit livelihood opportunities, interfere with children from seeking an education and further undermine women and girls’ already weak social position.
There is a need for increased access to mental health and psychosocial support services (PSS) for children and youth that create a sense of normalcy and routine crucial to the healing process and to preventing psychological and other long term implications of a protracted emergency. The scaling up of PSS for survivors of GBV is a key aspect of combating GBV. The proposed project will address GBV through static and mobile modalities including case management, legal support, awareness raising activities and trainings for service providers on GBV.
The proposed activities will focus on creating protective environment for conflict-affected populations by connecting them with community-based psychosocial services and resources strengthening their coping mechanisms, while providing guidance on understanding and managing their own psychological wellbeing. Social activities work to build resilience among individual families and communities not only to improve the current well-being of conflict-affected community, but also to better position them to engage constructively in the recovery phase. The programme’s training activities for local community members will ensure that capacity of community is built in a way that will continue to support them in the future. WRO will establish adolescent girls group in each center and use Adolescent Girls Toolkit, Iraq to strengthen girl focused programming

Needs

WRO has been working in Zummar since 2016, in Hamdaniya since 2017, in Mosul since 2018 running community centres focusing mostly on GBV response and prevention. The proposed activities will cover needs of women, girls, boys and men.Women and girls are suffering from psychological distress due to ISIS, displacement and living in camp. Many of them were exposed to violence during living under ISIS or during displacement. In addition, gender -based violence is wide spread and is not caused only due to conflict and displacement. Early marriage is still practiced in some communities and many women and girls were forced to marry before conflict. Economy situation of the returnee families is very often dire. There is high number of female-headed households where women are the sole breadwinner. GBV survivors and those at risk are missing legal assistance if necessary. Adolescent girls: Special category are adolescent girls as those at risk of gender-based violence. Besides, adolescent girls are essential agents of change for the future. To support them to pass through adolescence to maturity, WRO will implement program based on Adolescent Girls Toolkit for Iraq. Boys and men: unfortunately, men and boys are still main perpetrators of gender-based violence and especially boys contribute to harassment in the camp. WRO will focus on men and boys through awareness raising activities and capacity building.

Activities

Key activities: Conducting GBV Safety audits which examine safety issues in target areas, as well as barriers to accessing existing GBV response services, gaps in service provision, etc. Safety audit findings will be used for risk mitigation with local authorities and service providers in other sectors, strengthening of referral pathways, as well as for general advocacy purposes. Providing targeted psychosocial support (PSS) to women, girls and boys including case management and referrals. PSS will be individual or group counselling and psychoeducation sessions (e.g. dealing with stress, anger management, etc.). PSS and GBV case management will provided by WRO's case and social workers, cases which need special services will be referred to specialized service providers (health, legal, psychologists, livelihood, etc.). Part of PSS is also adolescent girls program based on Adolescent Girls Toolkit. Providing psychosocial support to women and girls through recreational activities to women and girls. Recreational activities will be decided based on focus group discussions with women and girls. Providing livelihood opportunities to women and girls, GBV survivors and those at risk. Besides PSS, WRO will in cooperation with local authorities and based on the capacity and market assessment, provide livelihood opportunities including material assistance and temporary income-earning activities. Providing legal assistance and counselling to women, girls, boys, mostly GBV survivors and those at risk. The legal assistance include ID documents, PDS cards, divorce, marriage certificates, etc. Providing awareness raising activities to women, girls, boys and men living in the camp to prevent gender-based violence. Awareness raising activities include awareness sessions, outreach activities and celebration of international events related such as International Women's Day, 16 Days of Activism, etc. The topics will be chosen based on actual need. Capacity building for non-GBV service providers. WRO will conduct training mostly on GBV Basic concepts and referral pathways.

Mitigation Measures

1. WRO is local NGO, registered both in KRI and Baghdad. WRO will follow all necessary procedures to get access and permission to operate. WRO will also maintain a network of relations with local authorities. 2.The program has been designed and developed from a ground up approach ensuring local ownership and accountability of all activities.3. The mobile teams’ efforts and close coordination with the existing communities, as well as other actors mobile teams, will mitigate project beneficiary attrition through beneficiary movement.4. WRO will go through transparent recruitment process with clear job description of particular positions. If there are no personnel with appropriate skills on the local market, WRO will invest to the capacity building and intensive training of the staff. 5.Although the targeted communities of Mosul and Zummar are considered more conservative and the proposed activities are stigmatized, WRO from its current and previous interventions in these targeted locations does not see a significant risk resulting in the lack of attendance due to biases. WROs program approach of a comprehensive support community center will significantly reduce stigmas and will further be mitigated through the community based components and awareness raising. 6. Mitigated through proactive recruitment of staff representing diverse populations and sensitization of project staff through on-going project monitoring and staff coaching, and messaging towards beneficiaries through trust-building activities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

WRO will meet the IASC Commitments to Affected Population through all five areas:1) Leadership and Governance (by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management and will be highlighted in reporting); 2) Transparency; 3) Feedback and complaints (CRM will be mainstreaming since the beginning of the project); 4) Participation (affected population will be engaged); and 5) Design, monitoring and evaluation (affected population will be engaged to monitoring and evaluation of the outputs of the project; project design is based on needs assessment with affected population participation). Through WRO's previous and existing work in the target locations beneficiary involvement has been paramount and ensuring community consultation has been crucial. Main tools how to engage affected populations to the different stages of project cycle are through FGDs, Client Feedback Surveys, and Key Informant Interviews. WRO will have its Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) in place. To raise a complaint will be possible through complaint box in the static centers, through email or phone call. WRO will also check relevance of the offered services through FGDs and KIIs.WRO management and M&E staff will monitor and evaluate the activities regularly.WRO has always developed programming and design in direct coordination with the local communities, including coordination with local authorities, religious leaders, and all ethnic and vulnerability groups to ensure accountability. WRO senior, project and support staff has been trained in Protection Mainstreaming, PSEA, IASC principles and guidelines including protection, MHPSS, and gender in emergencies, IEC materials, Do No Harm, CP SoPs, Caring for Survivors of Sexual Violence in Emergencies and was actively involved in revising the SGBV SOPs for 2018. WROs capacity ensures the community, most vulnerable, and those reached by programming are protected and involved with the utmost dignity and care. All WROs staff are required to sign and commit to a through Code of Conduct outlining performance and conduct expectations including confidentiality and service delivery with dignity, WRO internal employee manual states instruction on how to use WRO mechanisms to handle incidents and reports of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) against its staff.
