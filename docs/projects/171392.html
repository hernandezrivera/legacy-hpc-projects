Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2021 HRP speciﬁc objective 2 of WASH Sector: “Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank have access to essential services” and specific objective 3 “Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank, to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.”
The action will focus on three different interventions:
1 - households intervention
Based on recent WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map 2020, CESVI assessment (August 2020) and extensive consultations with key stakeholders the project will target 500 vulnerable households (2500 individuals) in Jericho, Jerusalem, Ramallah, Nablus and Tubas governorates. The support aims to improve the quality drinking water for those who are not connected to a pipeline network and relied on unsafe water source, through a treatment system at the point of use (in accordance with PWA regulation). This approach is complementary to the intervention of other WASH Cluster partners who are aiming to improve the water access and distribution (WW-GVC and AAH through water trucking and infrastructures installation). Moreover, the provision of latrines and hygiene awareness promotion (innovative behavioural change approach) will reduce the risk of cross contamination. This component will be delivered by the Rural Centre for Sustainable Development (RCSD) who has great experience in previous interventions in Area C. The project will be supported by a protection mainstreaming during the preliminary activities through an individual vulnerability-based assessment and participation in defining the needs and the responsive solutions. The proposed intervention will be implemented considering the WASH protection principles and minimum standards. A project Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be designed to address any issue and suggestion and to allow the beneficiaries to participate in the decision process.
2 - Technical assessment to model the disease incidence of water borne diseases caused by contaminated drinking water
The WASH Vulnerability Map address the three main component of the drinking water service, according to the SDGs: accessibility, availability and free of contamination. At the national level, there are not enough data for assessing the water quality; thus a specific approach is needed.
A formal, quantitative risk assessment approach that combines scientific knowledge about the presence and nature of pathogens, their potential fate and transport in the water cycle, the routes of exposure of humans and the health effects that may result from this exposure. All this knowledge is combined into a single assessment that allows evidence-based, proportionate, transparent and coherent management of the risk of waterborne infectious disease transmission. This model will allow the decision makers (PWA, WASH Cluster) to identify the priority actions towards the drinking of safe water at the household level.
Moreover, a specific training will deliver to the key stakeholders to be able to use the model and include it in the Water Safety Plan.
3 - support medical waste management
Based on Cesvi assessments in Solid Waste Management (SWM - 2019) and in medical waste management (July 2020) the project will support the Joint Service Council in improving the collection and transportation of infectious medical waste. The pandemic overloads the capacity of the existing system. In the period April-July 2020 Cesvi already supported the north of the West Bank, but the middle area is still uncovered and the south area is facing challenge in providing the service to all the facilities (screening, quarantine and isolation centre). An additional dedicated vehicle is necessary to reduce the health impact on the environment and the population living close to the mentioned centred, where waste accumulation is a common risk.

1 - Needs

1 – household WASH interventionIt is estimated that over 1.6 million Palestinians across the oPt are suffering from insufficient access to WASH services and will be in need of related humanitarian assistance in 2021. (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021)In the West Bank, the restrictive planning regime enforced by the Israeli authorities in Area C and East Jerusalem continued to prevent the connection of Palestinian communities to the water and sewage networks, as well as the deployment of rain-harvesting cisterns and mobile latrines (HNO 2021). The COVID -19 outbreak and its associated lockdowns, compounded by the growing PA budget deficit and suspension of salaries for government employees, led to the erosion of WASH coping mechanism by vulnerable households, alongside the adoption of negative practices, such as reducing water consumption, consuming water from unsafe sources. (HNO 2021)The WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map 2020 shows that nearly 187,000 Palestinians, 85 per cent in the West Bank, reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied. For their water supply, the HHs in these communities mainly relies on rainwater harvesting (runoff) or/and trucked water from filling. A recent CESVI WASH assessment (August 2019) shows that drinking water quality in those communities is contaminated in 30% of the households at the point of collection and almost in 50% of the cases at the point of use. The data shows that the contamination is due to poor hygiene practices, and exacerbated by inadequate hand-washing and sanitation facilities. Those communities are facing a high rate of open defecation practice due to lack of toilets. These conditions are harmful to the dignity of the women and girls as they have to put in place risky coping mechanisms. The assessment took in consideration protection principles to evaluate related risk to WASH facilities access and use. In the 47% of the households, the basic WASH needs are not met and it includes access to safe toilets and basic hygiene items. Moreover, in the targeted locations, almost 2% of the population have disabilities and those are the most affected by the unsafe sanitation facilities. 2 –the incidence of water born diseasesAccording to the SDGs, the drinking water should be free of contamination. In oPt, the analysis of water quality data is not generally available. The existing and updated dataset are available thanks to surveillance carried out by organisations like Cesvi for the specific area only. The local authorities do not have available and useful data for evaluating the situation in the country at the household level. This situation affects the effort of the WASH Cluster to assess the most vulnerable areas and to prioritise the intervention. Furthermore, in order to assess the health impact of drinking water quality, the sample analysis result dataset is necessary, but not sufficient. A deeper analysis in terms of risk of waterborne infectious disease transmission is needed. 3 – medical waste managementIn the last year, Cesvi assessed through different actions the situation of medical waste in oPt (July 2019: Assessment of UNRWA’s Health-Care Waste Management System in the North of the West Bank; Dec 2019: Solid Waste Management in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, West Bank including East Jerusalem & Gaza Strip). Moreover, between April and July 2020, through a project funded by HF supporting the medical waste management in the North of the West Bank, Cesvi highlights the challenges of the sector affected and overwhelmed by the response to the pandemic (July 2020: Assessment report – Covid-19 Medical waste management in Palestine, West Bank). The most critical situation is in the middle area of the West Bank, where there is not a proper collection and transportation system for medical waste. Thus, infectious waste is mixed with municipal waste and often disposed of in uncontrolled dumpsites. In the south, the system is in a critical situation, due to the increased amount of Covid-19 facilities where infectious waste is generated. In addition, the newly Covid-19 isolation centres are only partially covered by a proper infectious waste collection service.

2 - Activities

Activity 0 – The preliminary activity provides a refresh training to the project staff, in terms of WASH and protection standard. Moreover, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be designed with the beneficiaries and it will include the culturally and gender-sensitive PSEA complaint handling mechanism. PSEA mandatory training will be provided for the staff. A1.1 Baseline assessment that will evaluate access to water, sanitation and hygiene practices in HHs in unconnected communities in Area C, to identify women, men, girls and boys at risk of open defecation (OD) and poor hygiene. The collection of sex-, age- disaggregated data allows the team and the beneficiaries to identify the most vulnerable families, considering WASH and protection principles. Moreover, the presence of the elderly and people with disabilities will take into consideration as a priority. The beneficiaries will be informed on project activities and mechanism for feedback and direct participation. A1.2 Installation of HWTS and training in 377 HHs. The innovative technology will be selected in accordance with the PWA indications and based on a data-driven approach. Cesvi since 2018 is implementing different treatment systems and has developed a specific user-centred methodology to evaluate the user acceptance, considering age, gender and protection-related risk elements. A tailored system will be prepared for people with disabilities, according to the specific needs, to allow everyone to safely use the HWTS (e.g. adjusting the height of the stand) A1.3 Collection and analysis of water samples in HHs covered Water samples will be regularly collected from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use, and analysed to assess water quality. At the same time, feedbacks from women, men and children will be collected to adapt the design of the treatment system. A1.4 A behaviour change approach will be carried out in a culturally appropriate manner to systematically analys and tackle OD, practices contaminating water and enhance positive hygiene practice. The behavior analisys will be conducted with an age and gender disaggregation, also considering PWD. Specific sessions will target mothers and children to alleviate the risk of deteriorating their health and dignity. A1.5 173 latrines will be built in the most vulnerable HHs in the targeted communities. The action aims to reduce the practice of OD and to ensure privacy and safety, especially for women and girls who are more harmed by the practice of open defecation. The construction of the latrines will be implanted through a beneficiary-driven construction modality, through cash for work. Based on experience from RCSD, in line with the WASH Cluster approach, the final design will be defined using a participatory methodology (co-design workshop and FGD), taking in consideration inputs from women, men, children and PWD. Moreover, the latrine design will follow the standards for accessible sanitation for people with disabilities.A2.1 A steering committee(SC) will be established including PWA,WASH Cluster,UNICEF,MoH,PCBS. The body will manage the process to adapt the Quantitative Microbial Risk Assessment to the Palestinian context and the local governance, providing information and feedback. A2.2. International experts will be hired to model the presence and nature of pathogens, their potential fate and transport in the water cycle, the routes of exposure of humans and the health effects that may result from this exposure, as well as the effect of natural and engineered barriers and hygiene measures. The analysis will be based on the data collected in A.1 and available data(MICS6). Cesvi is implementing a QMRA pilot in Gaza and the lesson learnt will be used in West Bank, including complaint and feedback received. A2.3. Through training provided by external experts will allow key stakeholders to include the tools in the data-driven decision process: identification of vulnerable areas and intervention priority.A3.1A SC is established including MoH, MoLG, EQA for a comprehensive understanding of the medical waste management (collection and transportation) and the possible solution provided by the project. Feedback and information will be used to adapt the support to the medical waste collection system. A3.2 The dedicated vehicle will be purchased and the existing Standard Operational Procedure will be adapted to the needs and through the SC monitoring activities will be adapted and finalised. A3.3.Training for the sanitation workers will be provided enhancing the management capacity, in line with Cesvi previous experience.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneﬁciaries and communities will be ensured through the project Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM), implemented in all CESVI projects, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard. It is harmonised with PSEA complaints and whistle-blower procedure. The system is overseen by MEAL officer. All beneﬁciaries of the action will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in conﬁdentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will provide with information material summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CFM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, ﬁeld paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback to the complainants. This CFM system will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms feature several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by staff to ensure that feedback is received from beneﬁciaries, and, when preferred, conﬁdentiality is ensured and respected. The final communication channels will be identified with the support of the community, considering all the age and gender groups. Engagement and acceptance of community members is a key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of projects. Beneﬁciaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored through regular visits and interviews to beneﬁciaries regarding ongoing activities. The MEAL officer will report any issues brought to its attention.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention mainstreams protection following indications provided by the Protection Cluster and relevant guidelines and tools (e.g. Protection Mainstreaming toolkit, Gender & WASH Toolkit). Both activities and implementation modalities are designed in order to prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm. Possible risks - security, safety, physical, and psychosocial - were considered, including unintended negative effects as much as possible. Accountability is ensured by a dedicated tool - project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) - specifically tailored on the project as per Cesvi procedure. It will also support - along with other specific activities (e.g. workshops, regular monitoring with a questionnaire) -participation of beneficiaries and their empowerment. The action improves the resilience of the communities, due to improved access to WASH services, and a better-coping capacity. In particular, water filters are not at risk of confiscation, thus can improve the quality of water consumed even in the case of forcible transfer. They do not need any electricity, consumable nor chemical, thus they will work with no additional cost for the household. Cesvi commits to: - Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. For instance, Cesvi will ensure that the location of EH facilities and routes to them are away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence (GBV); use discussion groups and observation to detect/discuss this and ask the community to come up with solutions; Latrines and HWTS design will preserve the safety and dignity of its users, facilities design must preserve the safety and dignity of its users - Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Cases of violations should be referred promptly and in accordance with standard operating procedures established in the area. Eventually, Cesvi staff will be trained on protection procedures for referral of eviction and demolition orders. Based on the safeguarding policy Cesvi commits to doing all the needful for preventing programmes having any negative effect on children. Cesvi takes all the complaints and concerns about violence against children seriously and it initiates a rigorous investigation of complaints indicating a possible violation of this Policy as well as of Cesvi Code of Conduct and of Cesvi Ethical Code. The safeguarding policy complements, without replacing: Cesvi Ethical Code, Cesvi Staff Code of Conduct, Cesvi PSEA Policy, Cesvi Policy on the Use of Images of Children and Young People and Human Resources Policy. RCSD agreed to follow the CESVI procedure for having an aligned approach to the communities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposal is based on the need assessment and context analysis carried out by Cesvi and other stakeholders. The action proposed met the recognize that different groups and individuals have different needs and provide an appropriate answer in proportion to need. Needs were identified household per household before the submission of the proposal but will be confirmed during the implementation, in order to crosscheck again conditions of beneficiaries and confirm the selection based on vulnerabilities. Water treatment, latrines and awareness will be adapted and introduced in order to guarantee meaningful access for all the beneficiaries, removing any barrier (e.g. latrine will include adequate lighting, internal lock and privacy fencing in order to ensure women safety, security and privacy inside; awareness session will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices). The above-mentioned project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will support the project staff to adapt the response to the emerging needs or to correct the ongoing actions. Furthermore, it will be provided information, in accessible formats and languages, about entitlements and the complaint-feedback mechanism in place. Cesvi commits to: - Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Cesvi will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, for instance, the installation of ramps and railings to EH facilities -Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.
