Project Summary

Taking into consideration existing need of returnees, IDPs and host communities population affected by conflicts, the project aims at benefitting displaced and host community girls and women living in prioritized districts of Quissanga and Mocimboa da Praia through provision of quality life-saving, prevention actions and protection mainstreaming actions in General Protection, Child Protection and GBV.
The approach will have the people at its centre, in line with AVSI vision and mission, where the person is helped to be aware of his or her value and dignity, is a protagonist in his or her own and his or her community's integral development, even in contexts of crisis and emergency. The intervention are conceived in a multisectorial and integrated way, as a tool to promote this awareness in all those involved, has in itself a need for communication and sharing, and exerts an impact capable of generating positive change. While for GP and CP the project will work strongly on case management and first response/service provision, GBV is mainstreamed to all the protection interventions with special focus given to prevention and response to child marriages. This project will target 17.926 beneficiaries: 12.634 for Mocimboa Da Praia and 5.292 for Quissanga.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project is fully aligned with the Protection and HRP goals and aim to contribute to reach the following objectives: - Ensure that the most vulnerable girls and boys at risk of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress, along with their caregivers, receive timely, well-coordinated, and high-quality child protection services tailored to meet their specific needs, including considerations for disability, age, and gender responsiveness. - Strengthen the capacity of responders, communities and stakeholders to identify child protection risks and needs, implement effective mitigation measures, and deliver high quality, accessible child protection services to internally displaced populations, returnees, and host communities. - Promote a child-rights-centered approach by enhancing monitoring and reporting mechanisms for child rights violations, ensuring accountability, and integrating advocacy efforts to uphold and protect the rights of affected children and adolescents.This project is aimed to implement and improve prevention, case management and referral system to identify and manage cases of vulnerability among vulnerable population, with a special focus on children and their caregivers, as well as women and girls in Quissanga and Mocimboa da Praia districts. The main objective is to strengthen the case identification system, community participation and capacity to respond, as well as coordination between the response services, involving line local authorities and creating a close link with the case workers. They will be also able to provide protection and psycho-social support services for children, adolescent, caregivers and vulnerable individuals in the targeted communities. AVSI will rely on its long experience to support the displaced population (being them IDPs or returnees) and is working closely with DPGCAS and SDMAS, using the same identification and reference forms, involving staff in training and holding monthly meetings to coordinate the referral system. This approach is aimed at sharing the work with the local authorities, in order to support and allow them to carry on after the end of the project. Services will also be specifically provided to UASC and CAAFAG. At the end of the project the trained activists shall continue supporting their communities and might be supported by the district departments of Acçao Social and Education. While for GP and CP the project will work strongly on case management and first response/service provision, GBV is mainstreamed to all the protection interventions with special focus given to prevention and response to child marriages. This project will target 17.926 beneficiaries: 12.634 for Mocimboa Da Praia and 5.292 for Quissanga.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

AVSI will have an approach of continuous involvement of the beneficiaries and members of the target communities, inclusion of different social status in its interventions in order to contribute to promote ownership reinforcing sustainability. Persons of concerns well-being is central to the intervention and adaptation of the response and services provided shall be pivotal in the response modality, taking into consideration the needs and situation of each community and beneficiaries. Its areas of operation will be the districts of Quissanga and Mocimboa da Praia.Key activities are summarized below.- Identification and support for family and community reintegration of CAAFAGs: case management in response to their needs, including vocational training to contribute to their social reintegration.- Training of Caseworkers on child protection, case management for the care of CAAFAG, carry out community-based psychosocial support activities in a structured way, providing them with support and well-being- Case management, with particular focus on children in situations of vulnerability: starting with identification by AVSI volunteers, CCPC members, community leaders and other community members, follow-up plan, provision of services until the case is closed.- Support for access to primary services inside and outside the district by beneficiaries in case management (health, education, legal support, psychological care, etc) through direct provision or referral to various services and partners providing services in the target communities.- Identification and screening, reunification and family reintegration for UASCs: screening will be done in coordination with the action sector at every step of the process.- Structured Psychosocial Support activities with children and their carers: beneficiaries will be divided into groups and age groups (0 - 5 years, 6 - 11 years, 12 - 17 years and carers 18+) in 3-month cycles.- Community awareness-raising on GBV, PSEA for prevention, mitigation and response, including early unions: the target groups will be adolescents (12 - 17 years) and carers (18+) who will benefit from periodic awareness-raising on the two topics, as well as some awareness-raising campaigns for other members of the community in general.- Intergenerational dialogues between adolescents (12 - 17 years) and caregivers (18+) on conflict prevention and child recruitment to armed forces and groups: the approach will include beneficiaries and other community members to analyse potential situations that can and do lead to conflicts in the community as well as management and possible solutions.- Mental health and psychosocial support (Psychological first aid, individual counselling, group activities) levels 2 and 3: In coordination with community leaders, collective psychotherapy activities with games and recreational activities will be carried out, with a greater focus on the elderly, people with disabilities, identification of mental health needs, individual and group counselling sessions, promoting the care of beneficiaries through direct services and referrals to other service providers.- Promoting cohesion and peaceful coexistence: through awareness-raising sessions on human rights, preventing and combating violent extremism (in groups as well as door-to-door), collective play and recreational activities with teenagers and young people, carried out by young community engagement volunteers identified in the target communities and trained for this purpose.- Protection referrals and information on available services: through the focal points who identify cases of protection risks in coordination with the Protection Officers and Psychologists who follow up, refer and refer to both internal AVSI services and external services to assist beneficiaries in the target communities, based on the activities being carried out in these communities as well as through referrals by local leaders and other community members and partners.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

AVSI has internal risk control policies, in addition to training and inductions (ToT’s and Workshops) focused on collaborators and stakeholders, as per IASC Standards on Humanitarian Actions on Child safeguarding policy, Code of Ethics, conduct and PSEAH policy.To mitigate external risks, factors from the societal context (e.g. political development, cultural aspects, natural and human disasters and corruption), AVSI takes as a priority the regular carrying out of a Gender safety audit, involving discussion groups with girls, boys, caregivers and key informants as Leaders and actors from complementary sectors (i.e. wash, health…) . The Socioecological model will be implemented, which promotes the principle of doing no harm, an approach aimed at individuals and peers, families, schools, communities, civil society and state authorities who share responsibilities for community development, following the principle of their greatest interestAVSI will be engaged in creating a protected and safe environment, in particular in an emergency context, focusing on the improvement of most vulnerable categories well-being and family support through case management and provide access to basic services. Animators/facilitators groups are aimed at creating a protected family environment: providing psychosocial support in emergency and case management are two key actions to be planned in all the interventions. AVSI will conduct regular surveys and safety audits to monitor, identify and mitigate potential risks, and will regularly coordinate with partners through participation to Cluster meetings and district coordination system. A big effort will be put to mainstream protection across each project component and to have regular multisectorial assessment performed in the implementation areas, to ensure that possible route causes for additional protection risks are assessed. Embedded AAP and CFM system ensures to limit risks for beneficiaries. Case Management activities allow the identification and management of the most vulnerable cases. Through a mapping of the districts, AVSI is able to have a clear picture of households and their residences.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

AVSI will have an intersectional approach where assistance will be guaranteed to the most vulnerable groups. To this end, information will be gathered on the needs of the affected communities through the voices of women, girls, men, boys, elderly children, people with disabilities and other excluded and marginalised groups.Since the aim is to provide multi-sectoral assistance and contribute to the well-being of the affected community, the community will be actively involved in decision-making processes and the consensus reached will be taken into account and included in the project's intervention. This will be done through community meetings, public consultations, discussion groups or digital participation platforms, ensuring that the real needs of the population are heard and met. During the process AVSI staff will collect information, complaints, suggestions and feedback in an appropriate manner (e.g. groups are separated by sex, gender and age, confidentiality where necessary) and make sure that the feedback is in line with the gender analysis assumptions.Complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner, prioritising the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages, and will refer complaints that fall outside the scope of the organisation to a relevant party in a manner consistent with good practice. AVSI will at all times assess the application of the core humanitarian standard (CHS) and other collective standards, including the IASC AAP commitments, gender equality programming, the PSEA minimum operating standards and the good practice guide for setting up community-based inter-agency grievance mechanisms (CBCM) and the accompanying standard operating procedures, through self-assessments, peer review, third-party verification or certification.In this project, AVSI aims to ensure greater participation of excluded groups - women, girls, the elderly, children, people with disabilities - in project activities in an equitable manner, empowering my groups with technical knowledge to seek immediate and lasting solutions in their lives and in the community. In this way, groups of people in the project will be trained in the key themes of the project, to identify incidents and risks of protection, child protection, GBV, in the community and so we can gather ideas, experiences of how together we can prevent some cases in our communities and in the resettlement centres.Our expectation is that most members of the community will be aware of and take ownership of the project's activities and feel part of the process, that they will be willing to be actively involved in the changes, that AVSI will implement the activities in an integrated way so that the interventions complement each other, taking into account local cultural norms and involving all members of society. This community engagement in GBV protection activities will be done by raising awareness, continuing education, creating support networks, and strengthening collaboration between community leaders, local organisations and community authorities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Inclusive activities are integrated into all AVSI projects. We also understand that Persons with disabilities include those who have long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory disabilities which, in interaction with various barriers, may make it difficult for them to participate fully and effectively in society on an equal basis with others, the project will also be interested in identifying whether practices are adapted to the diversity of users, particularly Girls and Boys with disabilities. It allows to evaluate at which level the practices are inclusive at the beginning of the project and at which level these practices are being promoted during our implementation Our approach will include two (2) pillars:1. The institutional component corresponds to the internal practices of the structure (level of accessibility to infrastructure, movement with autonomy and safety); 2. The operational component corresponds to the practices and approaches implemented in the provision of services to users, including Monitor access (disaggregate disability data); Inclusive and protective committees and WASH; Inclusive recruitment of staff and volunteers.According of that, people with disabilities will be identified using the child function tools. It will include awareness campaigns. And, with the people with disabilities, we will find the barriers they experiment in their daily lives that harm their full participation as example: Physical or architectural barriers; Information (and communication) barriers; Attitudinal barriers. And The next step will be to remove all types of barriers and create synergies with teams specialized in functional rehabilitation and adaptation of resources, including inclusive auxiliary devices. It will be in our interest to see small adaptations in infrastructure so that they are accessible to girls and boys with disabilities. In this regard, we already have experience working with Khandlelo partner technicians who provide specialized occupational therapy and life skills services. AVSI will also be interested in the economic strengthening of people with disabilities, as a way of removing barriers.Inclusive activities are mainstreamed in all AVSI projects. Animators and Case workers are trained on safe identification and referral and according to the needs, specific topics are tackled concerning physical or mental disability. In some cases, special educators are hired to provide continuous support and mentoring to teachers on the inclusion of children, from needs assessment to progress monitoring, as well ascoordinate the work of specialized service providers. Activities can be implemented in different context (non-formal, formal, or informal). In different projects we engaged also specialized services – speech therapy, psychology, psychomotor therapy, mobility assistant for the visually impaired children, etc both for the assessment of child’s needs and for the provision of support services. Referral pathways are always mapped and shared. The engagement of parents for these activities, in the parental meetings to discuss the child’s progress and problems is fundamental.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed activities will adopt an environment-friendly approach by showing a particular attention to the materials used for the activities and by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. Specific attention regarding the site selection and sourcing of materials, favoring the use of local resources, like the locally sourced building materials, sun-cured soil bricks produced in affected communities and the limiting of the use of timber wherever feasible, for all rehabilitation and construction. Instead of timber, the use of bamboo will be preferred as a renewable resource with no significant environmental impact. Synthetic plastics use will be avoided as much as possible. With a strong engagement and participation in rehabilitation activities, attention will be given to community sensitization involvement on environment and waste management, as well as possible measures of climatic DRR, particularly in peri-urban settings. Main vector to promote environmental good practices will be children and youths, through peer clubs and mainstreaming environmental concepts in PSS and recreational activities. Finally, health and hygiene education will have a component of key messages regarding the environment.
