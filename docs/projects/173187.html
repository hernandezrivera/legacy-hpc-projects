Project Summary

UNHCR and partners (BRAC, CARITAS, DRC) will continue shelter interventions to improve the living conditions of refugees households taking into account AGDM and individual specific needs across all camps under UNHCR's AoR. Shelter actors are planning to assist 1,500 refugee households with double storey shelter in 14 camps and 3,000 refugee households with mezzanine shelter in registered camps. During the identification of beneficiaries for planned Shelter assistance, PwSN and EVIs are prioritized based on the list provided by UNHCR.
UNHCR and BDRCS will ensure the provision of NFI for new arrivals and households who need items to be replaced in all UNHCR managed camps. Distribution of LPG sets and refills as well as associated approved cooking equipment will continue in camps and host communities’ areas.
In addition, UNHCR will ensure the prepositioning of emergency shelter materials and NFI as a contingency for households affected by various natural disasters and protection incidents. Shelter / NFI focal points in disaster management committees are enhancing capacity and involvement of refugee volunteers as part of the overall camp level emergency preparedness and response, and DRR activities.
UNHCR interventions will focus on community-based approach and gender mainstreaming, engaging more with community groups, thus increasing community ownership and improving support for vulnerable households. UNHCR will increase its capacity for more effective coordination and participation with CiCs as part of the capacity sharing initiative.
In relation to AAP, complaints and feedbacks related shelter and NFI related activities are mainly channeled through Complaints, Feedbacks and Referral Mechanisms (CFRM) as well through protection hotline, Community Outreach Members (COM) volunteers, SMS partners referrals and bloc leaders. Mandatory online and in-person trainings on CoC and PSEA are facilitated by UNHCR and dedicated partners focal points. Partners need to comply with the Checklist for the assessment of the PSEA capacity of partners.
Each distribution exercise take place in a distribution point that is gender and age sensitive: segregated waiting lines and WASH facilities, covered / shaded waiting areas, porter services as needed, emergency exit, security provided. PDM are also conducted so distribution processed are improved and more inclusive.

1 - Identified Needs

Decongestion remains a priority activity to provide safe and more durable shelters for refugees in the camps. With the recent approval of steel frame shelters, including double-storey shelter solutions, and the RRRC’s new priority of double-storey shelters as a means to free up space on the ground. UNHCR plans on moving ahead with the construction. The settlement density will remain high and increase in the coming years, but by building upwards, some more space on the ground can be freed up, allowing for improvements at the neighbourhood and community levels and contributing to the general quality of life in the settlements. Ongoing use of temporary materials such as untreated bamboo and tarpaulin requires regular material replacement and structural shelter reinforcement. Reduction of shelter activities due to COVID-19 containment measures in 2020 resulted in shelters not being upgraded for almost one year and, without assistance, shelter conditions deteriorated. The Shelter Sector’s Rapid Damage Verification recorded over 100% increase in damages compared to the damages reported in 2019. More specifically, a total of 29,434 verified damaged shelters were recorded between May and September 2020, compared to 12,299 units during the same timeframe last year. The urgent need to provide shelter assistance remains, as well as more durable materials to improve the lifespan of the shelters. 38% of households reported facing mobility challenges inside and/or outside their shelters. The response shall be addressed with shelter-level site improvement with a focus on the accessibility of PwD (persons with disabilities). 58% also reported not have enough light. Shelter assistance also include the provision of padlocks and chains in case of damages or loss. Improved shelter access is provided for disabled persons. Community outreach is conducted at bloc level prior to distribution to ensure that everyone are aware of it. According to 2020 PDM for Shelter & NFI, 98.2% of Rohingya refugee households (HH) cook with LPG. The target is to reach 100% of new arrival HH and ensure continuous refills for all the refugee households. The 2020 J-MSNA shows that 88% of refugee HHs are using exclusively LPG. In light of LPG stoves lifespan of 2 years, replacement of stoves and other related items will be also needed. In coordination with local authorities, the distribution of LPG sets and refills will be expanded to host communities to enhance social cohesion. As compared to 2019 JMSNA results, the proportion of households using exclusively LPG increased from 15% in 2019 to 26% in 2020, while the proportion of households using purchased firewood decreased from 63% to 45%. UNHCR will continue to distribute CRI kits to new arrivals, with essential domestic items such as blankets, sleeping mats, plastic sheeting, kitchen set, plastic bucket, and a portable solar lamp with mobile charging station. In addition, NFI assistance is required for vulnerable households in-home care/quarantine as well as targeted individuals (i.e. elderly, EVIs, women/girls, etc.). Precarious land tenure arrangements and land disputes also impede the provision of shelter assistance, give rise to protection challenges and weaken peaceful co-existence between the refugee and host community. In addition, the increased cases of eviction, requests for a relocation due to rent, safety and security issues in the camp, fencing, and camp consolidation measures have increased the demand for scarce suitable land and coordination among HLP, Shelter/NFI, SMSD, Protection and WASH sectors for provision of shelter and services.
