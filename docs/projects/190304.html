Project Summary

IOM is leading WASH activities in many IDP sites and host communities across Ethiopia, including distribution of hygiene kits, water trucking, construction and rehabilitation of water points, emergency latrines, and handwashing stations, establishment of community-based water or sanitation management committees, and hygiene promotion activities through contextualized campaigns and mobilization of gender-balanced hygiene promoters.
In Ethiopia currently the internal displacement and humanitarian needs are mainly derived by the ongoing conflicts and droughts in some parts of the country. The displacement level in Ethiopia doubled in 2021, with over 2 million additional people displaced during 2021. According to IOM’s latest Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) National Displacement Report released in December 2021, a total of 4.2 million people were internally displaced across Ethiopia. This includes around 2.11 million IDPs identified in Tigray and neighbouring zones in Afar and Amhara regions as a result of the Northern Ethiopia Crisis. The ongoing displacements continue to pose serious protection concerns for the affected population. The majority of the displaced population live-in collective sites or in spontaneous settlements where they have limited access to essential services, dignified and safe living conditions to sustain their families and communities. A large number of the displaced population are women and children under 18.

IOM works under the framework of the 2021 HRP and 2022 draft HRP to enhance understanding of the crisis and assist joint, strategic response planning. This is particularly relevant to address new shocks and the confluence of needs. All interventions occur based on assessed needs, inter-cluster Initial Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA) and cluster-specific assessments. All assessments integrate specific gender analyses. IOM further considers key protection needs including the risk of exposure to harm, exploitation, harassment, deprivation and abuse, including forms of violence that are gender-based; the ability to meet basic needs; access to basic services; and coping mechanisms. IOM uses both qualitative and quantitative assessment methodologies, including IOM's DTM; questionnaires; household and key informant interviews (including with local authorities and key community leaders); focus group discussions with women and youth; direct observation and review of secondary information.
IOM has existing WASH programming in Tigray, Amhara, Afar, Oromia (East Hararghe and Borena), Somali (Dawa and Fafan zone), and SNNP regions (Konso zone) covering IDPs, returnees and host communities. Limited access to WASH NFIs, inadequate sources of safe water, inadequate sanitation facilities and prevalence of poor hygiene practices can render the populations living in collective centers/sites at risk of preventable diseases and overcrowded and congested IDP sites also pose a high level of risk to waterborne diseases, such as cholera. Assessments indicated that integrated WASH services are critical to ensure that facilities are maintained at a good standard and can be scaled up in case of population influxes, both in terms of the provision of safe water and increased hygiene promotion to prevent disease outbreaks.
In 2022, IOM appeals for USD 23,5 million to reach 1,6 million individuals through its WASH activities in IDP sites and host communities across Ethiopia. Interventions will comprise water trucking, construction and rehabilitation of water points, emergency latrines including distribution of hygiene kits, and handwashing stations, establishment of community-based water or sanitation management committees, and hygiene promotion activities through contextualized campaigns and mobilization of gender-balanced hygiene promoters. IOM will also support implementing partners, particularly national NGOs to provide access to safe water, WASH NFIs and hygiene promotion.

1. - Projects activities

IOM’s 2022 WASH strategy focuses on the expansion of emergency response capacities coupled with recovery activities in areas that are stabilizing from conflict and natural disaster. In close coordination with local government authorities and the WASH cluster, IOM will implement WASH programming directly and through sub-granting to national NGOs. Activities will include: • Rehabilitation and expansion of water supply systems and water trucking as a temporary solution prior to the completion of work on water point rehabilitation. • Provision of water supply to basic institution (Schools and Health facilities) through Expansion, rehabilitation, and maintenance of water schemes. • Construction of emergency sanitation facilities and upgrade and maintenance of existing facilities in IDP sites (gender segregated). • Construction or rehabilitation of communal sanitation facilities, including handwashing facilities in health posts/facilities, schools based on community consultations, and considering gender-specific needs.• Hygiene promotion and awareness-raising including on COVID-19 and including capacity-building of relevant local actors. • Provision of basic hygiene kits including menstrual hygiene management (MHM) components.• Provision of operation and maintenance tools and capacity-building of WASH committees on water supply system management in collaboration with local government. • Construction of household latrines for returnees through in-kind and cash-based interventions. • Provision of water supply to returnees’ areas and key community institutions (schools and health facilities) through new construction, expansion, rehabilitation, and maintenance of water schemes • Provision of WASH and infection prevention and control (IPC) services at the health facilities supported by IOM.• Production and distribution of IEC/BCC materials that supports participatory water, sanitation, and hygiene promotion approach such as CLTSH, PHAST and SLTSH. • Provision of Capacity building support (training, equipment’s, spare parts, key supplies, WASH MIS, water quality, etc. ) for local government and Humanitarian partners . • Participation in all levels of WASH cluster coordination meetings and engagement in integrated WASH program implementation.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Based on experience and to encourage community participation, this project considers using a mix of cash or voucher and in-kind approaches. In WASH facilities particularly, the sanitation component requires behavior change and creating demand by the community to invest in it. Cash approach in WASH projects is a recent phenomenon and less used compared to other sectors, it is advisable to use a mixed approach as part of encouraging community participation and ownership and progressing slowly towards cash only approaches, where this can be relevant and appropriate to apply based on learning.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

IOM participates in all levels of WASH cluster coordination meetings and engages with other local, national and international partners on integrated WASH program implementation. IOM maintains a participatory approach with direct beneficiaries, host communities and local authorities at all project levels, from design stage to monitoring and evaluation. In the previous years working within IDPs and host communities, IOM has consulted extensively with the local community including women, men, youth, religious leaders, influential men and women, minority groups, people with disabilities and local administration to better understand their needs and to help in designing interventions. The design of this project reflects cumulative learning and engagement with the population coupled with the Government recent assessment results on the target population. Information about the project, target beneficiaries and beneficiary selection criteria will be shared through various channels with beneficiaries, community leaders, suppliers, and contractors.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

IOM’s integrated programming assures that the WASH and CCCM teams work together closely in camp like settings for community-based feedback mechanisms to receive and follow up on complaints. During programming and particularly in areas without camp management actors, IOM and Implementing Partners will organize a committee or work with existing committees to design a CFM that meets the needs of beneficiaries. IOM also maintains a hotline for anonymous submissions of complaints and feedback. The committee will also receive complaints during targeting of activities to assure that the most vulnerable receive support and communities understand the criteria of the response. During WASH NFI distributions, IOM and implementing partners have help desks to receive complaints and provide feedback. The Mission’s feedbacks and complaints mechanism considers potential dangers and risks to all parties and incorporate ways to prevent injury and harm. This includes ensuring confidentiality and addressing the possibility of retaliation against witnesses. It also means protecting victims from continuing to suffer from the abuse about which they filed the complaint and providing other help such as medical and psychological assistance.
