Project Summary

Norwegian Church Aid will provide integrated multisectoral, emergency lifesaving GBV responses to five prioritized counties in four states, including Malakal, Malakal TC, Mawit, Nasir and Renk counties (Upper Nile); Aweil East County (Northern Bahr El-Ghazal); Pibor, Duk, Uror, Ayod counties (Jonglei State).
The project will work to achieve Protection Cluster/GBVAOR objectives PCO 1 & 3, in which NCA aims to support 4,738 individuals including internally displaced people, returnees, refugees and vulnerable affected people from the host community using both modalities of service provisions through GBV Mobile Response Team ( MRT) and Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS). These efforts will ensure that survivors of sexual and GBV have access to a range of services and information through the following key activities: 1) provision of GBV case management, 2) specialized individual psychosocial support through Integrated Therapeutic Techniques, 3) cash for protection under case management, 4) distribution of dignity kits, 5) support for survivors to access multisectoral services through safe referrals, 6) updating and development of GBV referral pathways and service mapping in operational areas, 7) awareness-raising sessions targeting women, men, boys, and girls on GBV risks, PSEA, and MHM together with WASH teams, available response services, and timely reporting, 8) deployment of the GBV Mobile Response Team (MRT), 9) prevention initiatives through the Enhancing Girls’ Agency and Gender Equality (ENGAGE) program, 10) safe motherhood programming, 11) capacity building for GBV service providers and government social welfare staff, and 12) implementation of community feedback mechanisms.
In addition, according to the Humanitarian Response Plan 2025, there are (2,751, 842) people are in need of the GBV response, from which (412,020) in acute need in prioritized counties. This project will also address the specific needs of adolescent girls and people with disabilities by ensuring they have access to GBV information and case management services. Direct beneficiaries will include women and girls most at risk of GBV. Community men and boys will also benefit from the project through GBV awareness and capacity-building initiatives, serving as allies to promote positive social norms against GBV in their communities, supporting survivors in accessing GBV services.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains challenging due to access constraints, inter-communal violence, armed group activity, cattle raiding, political instability, economic fragility, and poor infrastructure, further worsened by flooding and Sudan war. The long-lasting effects of ongoing instability and recurring environmental crises, coupled with major physical barriers to access, have greatly hindered people's ability to reach essential services and contributed to a growing demand for humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. With that, GBV remains widespread in South Sudan, due to entrenched harmful social norms and practices, systematic gender inequality as well as conflict-related sexual violence due to the prolonged nature of the conflict are severely impacting the safety and protection of women and girls rights. According to UNFPA- 2023 study the Sudd Institute indicated that 50-70% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months, while the GBVIMS 2024 half-year report noted physical violence as the most reported GBV incident, mainly connected with intimate partner violence which counting 36% of incidents, followed by sexual violence ( rape 24% and sexual assault 6% which constituting 30% of all reported incidents in the first half of 2024 were mainly reported in prioritized states. IOM’s 2023 Intersectoral Need Assessment findings suggested women and girls are at an increased risk of GBV, including rape with a significant number of respondents avoiding water points and firewood collection as a result. Children face significant risks of violence, exploitation and abuse due to conflict, forced recruitment, displacement, family separation and limited access to education, nutrition and healthcare. The same 2023 UNFPA study documented a high prevalence of child marriage (34.6%) with Lakes and Jonglei states accounting for some of the highest rates in the country (60.7% and 40.9%, respectively), while the Intersectoral Needs Assessment indicated Child, Early, Forced Marriage as the biggest concern families have for girls. Lack of adequate healthcare in hard-to-reach areas leads to South Sudan having one of the highest maternal mortality rates globally, with over 1223 deaths per 100,000 live births, primarily due infections, haemorrhage and obstructed labour, as well as social norms that prevents mothers utilising skilled birth approaches (WHO, 2023). According to Protection Cluster’s Protection Monitoring Spotlight (September 2024) it indicated that in (Western Bahr El-Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile, and Central Equatoria) 42% identified armed actors as the main perpetrators of sexual violence, with 28% business owners, 28% to community members. GBV remain underreported and 67% KII report that fear of stigma, 43% cite fear of retaliation, and 43% mention lack of knowledge are key barriers to reporting GBV. The Humanitarian Response Plan 2025, severity classification highlights the urgent response actions, with (2,751, 842) people are in need of the GBV response, from which (412,020) in acute need in prioritised counties. Women and girls in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile endure harsh conditions, with displaced individuals, returnees, and those living in poverty most affected by severe physical, sexual, and psychological abuse. This situation demands immediate GBV prevention, risk reduction, and enhanced response services.

2. Project activities

With consideration to age, gender, rights-based and survivor-centred approaches that supports survivors’ recovery, NCA will ensure that project activities are inclusive, participatory, culturally appropriate, confidential, grounded in the principles of empowerment and active participation, thus, each beneficiary is recognized and treated as an individual that is fully capable of determining their own goals and recovery plan. In line with the prioritized Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HNRP) indicators, NCA will focus on deploying mobile teams, establishing women and girls' safe spaces, and collaborating with the Ministry of Gender, Children, and Social Welfare (MGCSW) facilities. These efforts will provide comprehensive, age-appropriate, and culturally sensitive case management services, psychosocial support, and integration of therapeutic techniques for traumatized women and girls. Tailored one-off cash assistance (CVA) will also be provided to highly vulnerable survivors, prioritizing displaced women, refugees, returnees, and conflict-affected women and girls who have experienced or are at risk of S/GBV, this assistance will be integrated into case management services at project sites, with NCA’s internal GBV-CVA SOPs in place to identify vulnerable survivors. Additionally, in coordination with GBV AORs at field level, NCA will distribute dignity kits, update and develop GBV referral pathways, and create service maps in selected counties. Efforts will also be made to ensure survivors can access multisectoral services through safe referrals. The central focus of the project is to enhance community-based self-reliance and self-protection through outreach teams, Men and Boys engagement through NCA’s Enhancing Girls' Agency and Gender Equality (ENGAGE) program, and awareness-raising on GBV risks, alongside the widespread dissemination of PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) information. In 2025, NCA will integrate SRH into the GBViE programming, with a specific emphasis on safe motherhood and family planning awareness sessions by midwives. Furthermore, strengthening the capacity of GBV service providers and government social welfare staff to ensure sustainable, effective support for survivors. NCA will regularly report to 5w.
