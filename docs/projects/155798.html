Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to assist local Syrian CBO/NGOs to increase their institutional and technical capacity to respond to the needs of affected population in an efficient and principled manner.
This will be done through different activities including:
1. Organizational capacity assessment, identifying areas of strength and areas which require improvement.
2. Development of Organizational Capacity Development Plan with specific targets set for areas of improvement
3. Systems administration, programme design and programme operations (including but not limited to Grant Development, Project Management, Financial Management, Logistics Management, HR and Donor Compliance, MEAL), integration, inclusion, Child Safe-guarding, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Protection Monitoring, and Abuse (PSEA), Core Humanitarian Standards, Needs Assessments trainings are provided to CBO/NGOs personnel as required and appropriate through OCA identification and through the OCD planning process.
4. On-the-job coaching and mentoring are provided to both systems and programme staff to ensure the application of gained knowledge
The proposed intervention will be built upon the experience of Orange in the delivery of capacity building training workshop for over 24,235 personnel or civil society actor working either for civil society local organizations or and international non-governmental organization personnel, volunteers and community members. In addition, Orange carried out several organizational capacity development assessment for several local organizations and based on these developed and implemented capacity development plans. This project intervention will target mainly gross root organizations to enable them to design and implement programming in Syria.
In terms of coordination, Orange will coordinate these activities with relevant actors implementing similar programming. This will be done through sharing information with relevant stakeholders including UN-OCHA, UNDP and other actors on the project activities, civil society organizations involved in the capacity building project, type of capacity building provided, and location of provided activities.

Needs

Prior to carrying out the capacity building programming, Orange is planning to carry out organization capacity assessment with grassroots organizations to identify capacities, skills, gaps and type of proposed intervention of capacity building. By funding and partnering with local civil society, donors can spend development dollars twice by providing direct relief while building the capacity of local actors where needed, jumpstarting economic recovery and investing in a sustainable response. Syrian CSOs possess unique skills, expertise, and are on the frontlines delivering an estimated 75% of aid, yet they only receive roughly a very small share of direct funding. This is largely because INGOs continue to receive the the biggest share of grants due to their ability to meet strict donor requirements and humanitarian principles, even when they are largely unable to cross the border and operate inside Syria. While the nature of the Syrian crisis response is extraordinarily complex and donor policies are rarely supportive of contracting local organizations, challenges with localization in this context also stem from basic barriers, including a lack of reliable data on active CSOs, their capabilities, and what is required for improved delivery. With regard to communication and advocacy, a high percentage of grassroots CSOs reported the need for capacity building on communication policy and procedures, adherence to humanitarian principles; and communications and advocacy work. This was represented through the lack of dedicated personnel for communication and advocacy and the lack of written policy and procedures for communication and advocacy. Furthermore, in terms of governance, these grass-root CSOs lacked the presence of a proper governance structure, including the lack of strategic planning, resource mobilization, and real board of directors. In terms of project management, the majority of the CSOs highlighted that they have good capacity in implementing projects, with some lagging in project development, related to project proposals and funding application development, monitoring, and evaluation and the lack of quality standards to measure their programming as well as sustainability mechanisms for their programming. On the part of coordination, CSOs indicated that they have a high level of coordination among each other as CSOs but they highlighted that they have weakness in coordination with other relevant stakeholders. A good percentage of CSOs reported having safety and security policies and procedures. However, they reported the need to train their staff on safety and security protocols. Related to procurement, there is a need to properly document and proper identification of suppliers through selection and specification criteria as well as the need for spot checking to ensure transparent systems are present. Staff inside Syria reported the need for more training on organizations' procurement policy and procedures. Finance wise, CSOs stated they do not have an operational chart of accounts that assigns a unique code to each expenditure. As organizations grow, and take on larger grants, this weakness could lead to incorrect allocation of expenses to budget line items, potentially causing disallowed costs during financial audits. Over one-third of CSOs reported that they only reconcile expenses at the beginning and/or end of the project, which could lead to mismanagement of funds and overspending on project implementation, underspending, or disallowed costs. 36% of organizations have no such policy. This could suggest a possible gap in considering and targeting qualified female candidates during the recruitment process. 71% of Syrian CSOs described their organizations as expanding, it is critical they have robust recruitment processes that allow for the sourcing and selection of high quality candidates, including women.

Implementation of activities

The main activities of the project will be: Carrying out an Organizational Capacity Assessment (OCA) with selected organizations to identify needs and gaps at the organizational level focusing on Development of Organizational Capacity Development Plan (OCP)Implementation of Organization Capacity Development Plan (OCP), including trainings, policy and procedures review, follow up and coaching.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Security situation deteriorates and authority changes hands with the area of operation which might cause delay implementing the activities.2. Training fatigue

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Staff are trained in and adhere to security protocols, developed on the basis of ongoing monitoring and analysis of the changing security situation. Orange employs program and monitoring staff from the area so they can move more freely and negotiate movement. 2. targeted capacity development initiatives will be provided based on needs and tailored based on needs trainings will be provided.
