Project Summary

To improve the protection environment and support early recovery through increased access to protection services and economic opportunities for vulnerable conflict- and displacement-affected populations. In order to ensure that the DRC programme response is relevant and appropriate it will be required to understand and address the needs of the different primary groups who are displaced and otherwise conflict-affected: - IDPs in host communities and camps (official and spontaneous) - Returnees (from internal displacement or voluntary/forced repatriation from mainly Cameroon) - Host Communities (hosting IDPs or receiving returnees). DRC strives to address tailored protection and assistance needs of people affected by displacement.

Needs

DRC through protection monitoring activities reported a continuation of the Lafiya Dole and the initiation of the “Last Hold” military operations. There has been a marked increase in the number of Armed Opposition Groups initiated attacks against civilians and Security Forces (SF) in Northeast Nigeria especially Borno and Adamawa states. Military operations continued to trigger the displacement of civilian populations seeking safety in camps and towns on the Maiduguri Ngala axis, and in Bama IDP Camp in Borno state.This in return has caused an influx of IDPs in the camps. In addition to the effectiveness of the response to IDP needs, there is a noted weakness in planning and coordination amongst service providers, humanitarian actors and the government agencies, which continues to raise important concerns, particularly over the access to food, health, and education provisions for individuals in need. The displaced population has extensive WASH and Shelter needs which were further exacerbated by heavy rains and floodingduring the last few months. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview for the Early Recovery Sector; In 2018, one million IDPs remain in host communities while 1.6 million have returned to their LGA of origin, primarily to areas no longer directly affected by the conflict but also to severely affected LGAs in Borno (Damboa, Konduga, Monguno) Adamawa (Madagali, Mubi North, Hong) and Yobe (Gujba, Potiskum, Fika) states. Large numbers of IDPs (1,8 million; DTM XXIII) as well as of returning IDPs continue to place a heavy strain on already vulnerable communities with pre-existing inadequate levels of access to basic infrastructure and services. Data from recent multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments inadequate levels of access to basic services and public infrastructure relative to the size of the population, limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands, leading to societal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, and the elderly, against future vulnerabilities and shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs (whether they choose to return, locally integrate in their place of displacement, or relocate elsewhere in the country) and restoring social cohesion. Without those pre- conditions, a premature return of IDPs (or refugees) could lead to secondary displacement, deepen IDPs’ vulnerability, and exacerbate tension and social polarization between IDPs, host communities and returnees. According to the IOM DTM data on displacement, as of 30th of June 2018, it is estimated that 1,918,508 individuals are currently internally displaced in Nigeria. This is an increase of 37,310 individuals compared to the month of May (1,881,198 IDPs). The vast majority of the total Nigerian internally displaced population is displaced within Borno State, which counts 1,439,953 IDPs (75% of the total Nigeria IDP population). In Borno State, 100% of displaced individuals have reported the on-going conflict to be the reason for their displacement.Displacement routes used by fleeing individuals remain particularly dangerous due to the AOG presence and harsh environment factors, including high temperatures, heavy rains, and contaminated lands. This project will aim at supporting Livelihood Stabilization and Income Recovery through promoting micro and small enterprise recovery through short- cycle business-management training, cash grants, access to micro-finance schemes and coaching and other income-generating activities targeting particularly women and youths. This will complement to addressing the sector priorities.

Activities

Activity 2.2.1: Small business management training for SME benefiries: A total of 250 households (representing 1250 individuals - women and youth) will be supported with small business management trainings and business grants to improve their skills and increase their access to safer livelihood options for income opportunities and future recovery. Beneficiaries will engage in the viable small businesses identified by the 2017 DRC Livelihood Market Assessment for Adamawa and Borno States. Activity 2.2.2: Business planning: Business Management Trainings will support with disseminating information on financial planning that clearly justifies businesses having different start-up capital requirements. Beneficiaries will design business proposals that will describe business operational and financial plans. Activity 2.2.3: Business grants disbursement to beneficiaries: The DRC Livelihood Market Assessment (LHMA) identfied marketable IGAs that are appropriate to the local needs of beneficiaries and their communities and where there are market gaps that can be exploited in Borno State. Existing viable income generating activities identified for Borno includ: tailoring, groundnut oil extraction, petty trade, selling of fish, water melon, meat, dry pepper, groundnut, bed sheets and curtains, beans, bean cakes and goats. Beneficiaries will receive business grants in tranches. Activity 2.2.4: Follow-up and business advisory services: DRC will provide business advisory and technical support to the beneficiaries operating businesses (cash- ow, cost benefit analysis and performance ratios such as gross pro t ratio, return on equity and return on investments). DRC will also support beneficiaries to ensure that basic record keeping is in place.
