Project Summary

As per HNO, HRP 2024: In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023 .
At least 12.9 million people across Syria need food assistance. By October 2023, the cost of the food basket had doubled compared to January and had quadrupled in two years.
Almost 50 per cent of the residents in Syria are estimated to be food insecure
Evolution of People in Need (food sector) is expected to be 15.4 millions people, (in increase about 400 thousands people from last year) 3,191,946 in aleppo , 2,090,304 in Rural Damascus, 898,968 in Damascus, 895,518 in Homs, 752,768 in Lattakia.
post-July 2021 onward, the situation of food insecurity worsened due to an increase in the price of subsidized bread, a rise in the price of subsidized diesel, import restrictions, and a fall in wheat production.
High inflation, currency depreciation, and increases in commodity prices continued to drive humanitarian needs in Syria. This resulted in an increase in poverty, reliance on humanitarian assistance.
After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs.
Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, resulting in millions of people unable to meet their basic needs. Recovery from the earthquakes will necessitate long-term funding and improved emergency preparedness.
As from Tamayoz work in Aleppo, Damascus and RD the increased need for food assistance are highly reported especially after WFP decision to stop food baskets distribution to Syrian people, this has exacerbated the food insecurity and made a high pressure on NGOs including Altamayoz to look for alternative funds to continue support vulnerable people who, if not supported, will be at risk of malnutrition, illness, and even death, in addition to affecting the mental health well-being of most people. Adults are more severely impacted as they frequently engage in adverse coping mechanisms to ensure that their children have enough food to eat.
This project aims at supporting food-insecure families in Aleppo, Homs, Latakia, Damascus and 10 areas in Rural Damascus (Kisweh, BAbella, Maliha, Kafrbatna, Arbin, Duma, Harasta, Alzabadani, Einelfijeh,and Darayya). those locations have been discussed and selected in consultation with food sector. selected locations are of severity scale 3 and 4 where the severity label is extreme in 6 locations.
the project will support 8750 families (43750 individuals) in 2024 with food baskets on monthly basis for at least 8 months and will support rehabilitation of 1 bakery in Rural Damascus to support about 1000 individuals.
AlTamayoz has a very good experience in GFA through a partnership with WFP since 2014 that proves its high-quality level of service provision. It has also implemented GFA project under SHF fund in Harasta that has ended by 30 November 2022. And another projects under SHF in Aleppo and Rural Damascus that will end in March 2024. AlTamayoz has offices in Aleppo, Homs, and Latakia through which Altamyoz started providing services, and has a strong registration and distribution system in Damascus that will be used in other governorates for registration, validation, and monitoring of activities as its system has proved its efficacy and accuracy during the last years of GFA activities implementation.
