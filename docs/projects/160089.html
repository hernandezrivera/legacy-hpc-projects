Project Summary

Somalia has been almost continuously in a food security and nutrition crisis, with rates of acute and chronic malnutrition exceeding emergency thresholds in most areas. The situation is worsened by low coverage of primary health care facilities, poor child care practices (especially those associated with infants and pregnant and lactating women), poor access to hygiene and sanitation facilities and vulnerable livelihoods. Across Somalia, current estimates indicate that 1 in 10 children under the age of five is acutely malnourished with the median National GAM of 13.8 percent at Serious level, translating to 1.08M boys and girls of which 178,000 are affected by life threatening severely malnutrition while 830,000 are moderately malnourished. A further 832,000 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of nutrition assistance. Furthermore, the 2019 Post-Gu report highlights the continuation of a very critical nutritional situation in Lower Juba, the middle Jubas and with Gedo alone having 5.4% of the malnutrition burden according to the survey. Considering the high caseload and high risk of death associated with un-treated acute malnutrition, treatment of acute malnutrition remains a significant priority for Somalia ministry of health, Somali Aid and partners.
Due to the dry and harsh Jilaal (January March) season and a significant delay in the start of the 2019 Gu season rainfall, Moderate to Severe drought conditions prevailed in most parts of Somalia between March and mid-May. Accordingly, the 2019 FSNAU Post Gu nutrition assessment findings showed that lower Juba and Gedo regions are under emergency IPC4 and are considered hotspots in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions.
Immunization remains very low with measles’ coverage among 1 year olds being only 30% overall and 15% in inaccessible parts of South Central Zone. The situation thus remains of high concern and sustained focus on a holistic treatment, promotion and prevention program focused at the community level is required in order to build the resilience of the vulnerable populations. Acutely malnourished children will require life-saving treatment, while their mothers learn improved feeding practices to reduce risks of malnutrition through nutritional education and IYCF programs.
The Nutrition Cluster estimates caseloads of about 750 SAM and 3,900 MAM annually in Badhaadhe district alone while Gedo, middle juba and lower juba alone have combined SAM burden of 23633 (16954 non IDP cases and 6679 SAM cases in the IDP population).
Somali Aid due to its presence in the region takes first response in emergencies & have been carrying out routine health services through the only health facilities in the area. Somali Aid also implements a Polio intervention program through core group in Gedo and has a fairly good network of community workers and volunteers which we intend to continue working with in the nutrition program. In addition to the poor rainfall and deteriorating nutritional status, there has been a funding gap and minimal or no nutritional programs leading to untreated cases and consequently upsurge in the monthly admission of acutely malnourished children as reported from the nutrition cluster and facilities in Badhadhe district. Somali Aid has also been responding to the recent up surge in acute watery diarrhoea working closely with the ministry of health and stake holders like UNICEF and WHO and has confirmed cholera case therefore may further lead to deterioration of nutrition status in the children under five.

Project Activities

Activity 1. Diagnosis and treatment of acute malnutrition in children and pregnant lactating women. activity 1.1. identification and treatment of 1,897 severely malnourished (968 boys and 929 girls) under 5 children through the provision of quality treatment and prevention services as defined in the BNSP. Activity 1.2. identification and treatment of 3,306 moderately malnourished (1686 boys and 1,619 girls) under 5 children through the provision of quality treatment and prevention services as defined in the BNSP Activity 1.3. treatment of 2,130 pregnant and lactating women through MAM program. Activity 1.4. distribution of multiple micro-nutrients to 2,130 pregnant and lactating women. Activity 2: integration of Immunization services within the nutrition programs Activity 2.1. Immunization of 5203 children (2,654 boys 2549 girls) visiting the nutrition program using the National vaccination schedule. Activity 2.2. Vaccination of 1065 pregnant women in the nutrition program with Tetenus Toxoid vaccine as scheduled. Activity 3: Vitamin A supplementation and deworming of 5203 children (2,654 boys 2549 girls) as national schedule. Activity 4. undertake community education and individual counselling on infant and young child feeding and identification of malnutrition. Activity 4.1. Individual IYCF counselling for 1704 PLWs and caretakers of children under 24 months Activity 4.2. Training of 400 Caregivers on family led MUAC. (25 from each static facility)Activity 5. Training of health workers and community on management of acute malnutrition Activity 5.1. Training of 53 health workers (30 Male, 23 female) on IMAM and IYCF. Activity 5.2. Training of 150 (110 female, 40 male) community owned resource persons on health, hygiene and nutrition package.

Mitigation Measures

Volatile risks in Somalia context with service delivery has been very fragile. Somali Aid will focus of the humanitarian response and provision of protection services has been accessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance or in drought impacted communities in accessible, less conflict prone areas. Somali Aid has in place policies to mitigate corruption, digital literacy, diversion of assistance, delays of providing assistance and delivery mechanisms capacity market failures, etc. Somali Aid will ensure safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics related risks. Gender-responsive measures are incorporated into the project design, and are be based on evidence about gender roles and preferences for modality. Areas with active conflict, will be benefiting from all measure of the health humanitarian response, have seen minimal protection engagement through the efforts of some highly motivated individual at the community. Somali Aid will engage further in conflict affected areas through various actions including but not limited to engaging the various gatekeepers and the local authorities and the IMAMs.
