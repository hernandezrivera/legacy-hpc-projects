Project Summary

In response to the urgent and escalating humanitarian needs in Cabo Delgado, the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) is structured around three key pillars to deliver comprehensive, timely life-saving assistance to vulnerable populations (IDPs, returnees and/or host communities) affected by a shock.
1. Humanitarian Monitoring and Rapid Needs Assessments (RNA)
The RRM enhances humanitarian surveillance through early warning systems, supported by a network of focal points and close collaboration with existing humanitarian coordination groups, such as sectoral clusters and sub-clusters, and other humanitarian actors, to verify and share alerts. Central to the initiative is the ability to conduct Rapid Needs Assessments (RNAs) to accurately identify the immediate and critical needs of vulnerable households affected by crises.
2. Emergency Response
Guided by RNA results, targeted first-line interventions, such as the provision of NFI/Shelter kits as part of life-saving, multi-purpose kits (WASH, Shelter/NFI and Food assistance) for a swift and effective emergency response. Complementary emergency WASH activities include rehabilitating and repairing water sources, establishing emergency sanitation infrastructure, providing emergency water-truck activities, and, when necessary, collaborating with health partners to implement comprehensive Cholera response measures.
3. Advocacy for Complementary Responses
The action also emphasizes advocacy to mobilize complementary responses (second line) from the humanitarian community. By leveraging technical expertise and strengthening information management systems, the initiative seeks to reinforce coordination and collaboration among stakeholders, addressing gaps and maximizing the impact of humanitarian interventions.
This initiative aligns fully with core objectives of the WaSH, Shelther/NFI and FSL Clusters:
• WASH Cluster: Provide inclusive, gender-sensitive, life-saving WaSH services, items, and facilities for vulnerable populations affected by conflict, including displaced individuals, returnees, and those in hard-to-reach areas.
• Shelter/NFI Cluster: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
• FSL Cluster: Provision of Life-saving food assistance to acutely food insecure population including displaced population, host communities and returnees.
This project sheet describes the multi-sector nature of the project whilst the activities and budget only correspond to Emergency Shelter and NFI sector.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project aims at reinforcing existing Rapid Response Mechanisms (RRM) to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to affected population (IDPs, returnees and host-families) following a shock in Nangade, Macomia, Muidumbe, Meluco, Mueda, Quissanga, and Mocimboa da Praia districts. It ensures a standardized minimum response package is consistently delivered across all targeted districts by SI under three different, but complementary, sectors (WASH, Shelter/NFI and Food Assistance).1. Humanitarian Monitoring and Rapid Needs Assessments (RNA)The RRM, through its network of focal points and collaboration with first-line actors and Clusters, will monitor the humanitarian context and raise alerts for critical events, such as conflict, violence, disease outbreaks, natural disasters (cyclones, floods, droughts), or sudden return movements, affecting more than 50 HH. Key Actions:• Humanitarian monitoring• Alert raising• Rapid Needs Assessments (RNA)• Data Oversight• Prioritization of affected areas for interventions Deactivation Triggers:The RRM will deactivate under the following conditions:• IDPs return home or relocate safely.• Local authorities can provide adequate support.• RRM partners complete the first-line response.• Security issues block access to the affected area for three consecutive weeks.• Other organizations fully cover response needs.• RNA results show reduced needs below thresholds.• Initial alerts remain unconfirmed.2. Emergency ResponseBased on RNA results, and the severity of needs assessed, SI RRM team will implement emergency responses by providing distribution of survival kits and emergency access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene to support recently affected or accessible IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by a shock. Key Actions:• Distribution of survival kits (composed by WASH, Shelter/NFI and Food items) • Improve water access• Improve sanitation conditions• Hygiene promotion• Epidemic response Survival Kit composition (for one HH): • Hygiene items: 1 20L Plastic Bucket with Lid, 1 20L Plastic Bucket with Lid and tap, 2 Female Undergarment, 3 Certeza 150ml, 3 Cloth capulanas, 4 Soap bar• NFI/Shelter items: 1 Bag, 1 Tarpaulin, 1 roll of 10m Nylon rope, 1 Kitchen set, 1 Blanket, 1 Mosquito net, 1 Plastic mat (single size), 1 Solar lamp.• Food items: 1 Carton packaging, 5 Canned beans (400g), 8 Canned sardines (125g), 10 Marie biscuit (100g), 1 bag of Rice (10kg), and 1 Oil (2L).3. Advocacy for Complementary ResponsesThis result focuses on strengthening coordination and advocacy efforts within the RRM actors, ECHO-Funded Consortia, and with other first-line responders to improve emergency response efficiency in Cabo Delgado. It will involve collaboration with external actors such as OCHA and the sectoral clusters, UN agencies, INGOs and NGOs to ensure sustainable assistance to affected population. SI will also advocate with key humanitarian and development stakeholders and local authorities to expand reach and enhance the effectiveness and speed of both first- and second-line responses. The narrative describes the integrated nature of the RRM Programme while this project sheet only outlines the indicators and activities under the Shelter and NFI component.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The proposed project activities reflect a comprehensive analysis of risks and vulnerabilities, integrating conflict, environmental, and climate considerations. The RRM is designed to support populations affected by shocks, thereby reducing potential tensions with host communities, previous IDPs, or returnees. SI applies a meticulous approach to risk assessment and mitigation, ensuring that interventions benefit vulnerable communities in Cabo Delgado without causing harm. Commitment to Do No HarmSI consistently applies a "do no harm" approach across all projects. This Action targets the most vulnerable populations while avoiding tensions between communities and beneficiaries. To achieve this, selection criteria will be clearly communicated to community members and leaders, with active community involvement in activity planning whenever possible. Environmentally Sensitive and Protection-Focused ImplementationFor WASH activities, the locations for constructing or rehabilitating latrines and water points will be carefully selected to minimize environmental impacts and protect local ecosystems and water sources. From a protection perspective, sanitation facilities will be designed to ensure safety and privacy, particularly for women and girls, mitigating risks of gender-based violence and fostering a secure living environment.SI prioritizes environmentally friendly approaches by incorporating sustainable practices such as sourcing locally made materials and carefully selecting procured items to minimize negative environmental impacts. Security-Conscious OperationsRecognizing the region's security challenges, activities will be implemented in hard-to-reach areas under strict safety protocols. These include regular security briefings, emergency response measures, and close coordination with local authorities to ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff. Health Standards and Risk MitigationTo minimize health risks, the project will align with WHO and Sphere standards in constructing and maintaining WASH facilities. This includes ensuring water quality, proper waste disposal, and hygiene promotion to prevent contamination and disease outbreaks. Monitoring, Evaluation, and Community EngagementA punctual monitoring and evaluation system will be implemented to keep the project adaptive. Community feedback will be incorporated during the interventions, allowing for necessary adjustments to improve outcomes and responsiveness. By integrating these measures, the project will provide lifesaving WASH, Shelter/NFI, and food assistance while prioritizing safety, well-being, and sustainability for all stakeholders. In light of the region’s security challenges, activities will be conducted in hard-to-reach areas with strong safety protocols, including regular security briefings, emergency response measures, and close coordination with local authorities to protect both beneficiaries and staff.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project prioritizes meaningful access to assistance by addressing gender and age disparities, promoting the active participation of women and elderly individuals in decision-making processes. Women, often primary caregivers in crisis-affected households, are prioritized as recipients of survival kits, ensuring their direct benefit and reducing economic vulnerabilities. Their perspectives, needs, and vulnerabilities are actively considered throughout the project, with targeted efforts to include them in water management committees and, where feasible, to recruit them as focal points for humanitarian monitoring. Elderly individuals, who often face mobility challenges and exclusion, are also actively involved in community engagement initiatives. Their insights and needs are incorporated into planning and implementation of activities, ensuring they have a voice in decisions that affect their access to resources like WASH facilities and distribution sites. Gender- and age-sensitive data collection further ensures vulnerabilities are identified and addressed in alignment with best practices. To amplify its impact, the project integrates these considerations into its staffing and operations. Solidarités International (SI) actively encourages the recruitment of women and advocates for the inclusion of women and elderly individuals in community committees to enhance representation and empowerment whenever is possible. These efforts not only ensure equitable assistance but also create an inclusive environment that leverages the unique perspectives of women and elderly persons to improve the effectiveness of interventions while fostering a sense of dignity and self-reliance among all beneficiaries.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will identify persons with disabilities and/or facing mobility challenges using a community-based approach, working with local leaders, families, and individuals to understand their specific needs and barriers. Whenever is possible, SI will adapt its assistance to address their unique challenges, such as mobility issues and health concerns, and will prioritize age- and culturally-sensitive practices. Accessibility assessments will ensure physical, and communication barriers are removed at distribution and service points. To facilitate access, SI will provide shaded waiting areas, prioritize distributions for those with limited mobility, and offer support to help elderly individuals and persons with disabilities transport their kits home. Inclusive measures will extend to WaSH facilities, with adaptations such as handrails, ramps, accessible latrines, and handwashing stations designed for ease of use whenever the need is identified. Hygiene promotion activities will include visual aids, audio materials, and simplified messaging to reach individuals with visual, hearing, or cognitive disabilities. SI will also ensure these groups are actively involved in decision-making through regular meetings, focus groups, and feedback mechanisms, fostering tailored solutions that promote dignity, safety, and equitable access to essential services.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Given the emergency nature of the project, the focus will be on rapid, essential live-saving interventions, that aim to minimize environmental impacts within the constraints of the situation. The distribution of survival kits, as well as basic WASH activities, will be conducted with environmental considerations in mind. To reduce waste, the project will prioritize the use of eco-friendly packaging, minimize single-use plastics, and ensure proper disposal of any waste generated during the distribution process. For the WASH activities, such as the construction of emergency latrines and water point rehabilitations or repairs, locations will be selected to avoid contamination of local water sources and minimize environmental disruption. The project will make use of locally sourced materials where possible, ensuring that the construction of facilities such as latrines is done with minimal impact on the natural landscape. While full environmental assessments may not be feasible in this emergency context, the project will incorporate basic environmental safeguards, such as monitoring water extraction levels and choosing latrine sites away from sensitive areas, to protect both the environment and the communities served.
