Project Summary

FISTA’s vision is a community where disabled people have full rights. Our main goal is to ensure that disabled people are not excluded and to encourage their social inclusion and safety. Thus, FISTA is committed to ensuring that every child is provided with quality education and that children with disability and children who dropped out from school are receiving adapted and inclusive learning support that responds to their needs and are facing less protection risks.
Since 2016, FISTA provided services to 1040 children with disabilities (of which 35% are Syrian and mostly supported by the UNICEF-funded program). 640 of 1040 children reached received mental health services and 400 received specialized services in Tripoli and Akkar. The families of the targeted children benefitted also from psychosocial support and parental engagement follow up. Although there have been some positive developments in recent years to improve access to education for children with disabilities (CWDs) in public schools in Lebanon, CWDs are still not fully accommodated in formal education and thus require specialized support to ensure that they are receiving the adapted program that fits their needs on all the levels.
Thus, under this project, FISTA will be targeting both children who dropped out of school and children with disability. Thus, when conducting the needs assessments, FISTA will identify 150 children who dropped out of school and 150 children with disability and outreach to their parents/caregivers to enroll them in the various project activities.
FISTA’s strength is its staff capacity and network of various partners, this project will be implemented in coordination with governmental and non-governmental bodies and UN agencies. In particular, FISTA will continue to be an active member of the UNICEF Education and CP Working Groups and to adopt the referral mechanism developed with all concerned stakeholders, involving UN agencies, INGO’s NGOs, municipalities, PHCs, SDCs and outreach volunteers. Fista will also be partnering with Restart Center for any Psychiatric referrals and provision of medications as well as WRF For the assistive devices.
Under the 2021 ERP appeal, FISTA aims to assist in total 600 unique beneficiaries divided between 300 children and 300 parents/caregivers identified in the needs assessment phase and based on specific set criteria. 300 children (150 children with disability and 150 who had dropped out of school) will benefit from Cash for Education and various educational sessions to support the children’s learning process face to face and or remotely throughout the academic year. Children will benefit from specialized educational session, supportive therapy sessions, and homeschooling support and will in parallel participate in PSS SEL and sessions promoting SGBV prevention and other protection concerns. 80% of FISTA beneficiaries, both parents and children will be Lebanese identified in Tripoli and Akkar and 20% will be mainly Syrian migrants residing in the same communities.
While Lebanon has recently made some progress in providing education for children with disabilities, significant work is needed to implement relevant laws and bring Lebanon into line with international standards. This project will take practical steps to protect the rights of children with disabilities as enshrined in the CRPD and prepare them to access quality inclusive education.
The program will also apply a participatory approach involving children and youth, specialists, caregivers/parents in the implementation and evaluation of the children specialized educational services and/or treatment plans.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Sector Obj 1: Under this project, FISTA will undertake an assessment in the locations it will operate in. The assessment will be focused on identifying CWD and those who are out of school but will also look deeper into the challenges faced by children. Based on the findings, FISTA will conduct awareness sessions with emphasis on the approaches that can be adopted to support CWD and to ensure their future inclusion in the formal education. Children identified by FISTA for specialized services will undergo a more in-depth assessment to identify the children eligible for the Cash for education support over 8 months. Through its homeschooling, PSS SEL program, promoting and applying SGBV risk reduction in adapted learning programs, FISTA will tailor the sessions to the needs and challenges highlighted by parents. Sector Objective 2: Under this objective, FISTA is specifically targeting children with disabilities and children who are out of school In its outreach and assessment, FISTA will identify 200 children who are in need of inclusive and adapted catch up learning support. FISTA’s specialized team is capable of accompanying children and supporting them to ensure that they are prepared to reenter schools. FISTA will provide group educational sessions of max 8 children. will also provide individual sessions when needed. 300 children enrolled in its program will also receive age appropriate and needed stationary , books and other specific education material.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

This project will ensure increased access to protection and response services for children with disabilities through an integrated inclusive package of education, services and prevention awareness activities targeting both parents and children. In particular, capacity building programs on the inclusion and integration of children with disabilities and their protection from all kinds of exploitation, abuse and violence, through monitoring and reporting, will be provided to school communities, sport instructors, municipalities, university students and active youths from social movements. Moreover, FISTA will make sure that all the employees, partners, engaged consultants and caregivers are in line with FISTA’s safeguarding and protection policy.FISTA’s PSEA focal points will be implementing refreshing sessions with all the team and the consultants as well as bus driver. A draft PSEA action plan for the year 2021-2022 is developed and will be reviewed with the UNICEF focal point.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

In order to ensure proper information provision to parents and allow opportunity for parents to share their views and consult them about the needs of their children, FISTA will conduct a meeting with the parents of the children identified to benefit from the specialized support at the onset of the program as well throughout the duration of the program to gather their observations and feedback about the progress of their children. During these meetings, Parents will be informed about the services that FISTA will be providing and they will be invited to give their feedback and to highlight the challenges that they face with their children.Mid way through the program, phone consultations will be conducted with caregivers by staff members in FISTA that are not directly working on the project to gather any complaints and feedback from the parents. All beneficiaries will be informed about the complaints and feedback mechanism and will participate in the evaluation of the project.
