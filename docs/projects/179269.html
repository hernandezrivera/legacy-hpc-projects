Project Summary

SARD is committed to assist affected people most in need in northwest Syria, despite unpredictability and the risks of working in areas with security threats. The response plan with a budget of 1 130 000 USD will address the needs of more than 10000 people over the course of 12 months, providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance.
The action contributes to improve community services and access to livelihoods for selected most vulnerable persons. This project is closely interlinked with the protection and the Shelter project implemented by SARD in the same locations.
Implemented with a protection lens, and addressing gender and disabled-specific needs, the income generating project is aiming at providing an exit strategy to protection project beneficiaries, providing livelihoods solution to foster families and contribute to reduce the risk of separation of the children. As per identified needs the intervention will integrate an early recovery and livelihood component aimed at strengthening the resilience of foster families and families vulnerable to separation. This component will follow SARD’s livelihood approach, successfully implemented with most vulnerable families in the last three years, comprising of cash grant support to restore and/or create income-generating activities.
In the mean time, closely integrated with its SNFI project, SARD is committed to promoting social cohesion including shelter programming based on a community approach which includes community projects of common interest. these community projects are implemented by contractors and managed by the local community/authorities as part of developing their knowledge and capacity to administrate and manage community service contracts.

4 - Needs

In SARD’s recent needs assessment, 95% of households interviewed reported a significant impediment in their access to food. In order to cope with the lack of food / money, households tend to resort to stress and crisis coping strategies such as decreasing spending on non-food essential items and on productive assets, purchasing food on credit, borrowing / sharing food or selling assets. Additional barriers of access were reported for women or persons with disabilities were cited, as the security and safety in the markets remains a concern. With regard to income generation and restoration, lack of job opportunities in the local market, lack of financial means to start a business along with lack of technical qualifications were reported as the main challenges faced by affected households. On average, 68% of households interviewed relied on income-generating activities for sustenance while 16% had no cash sources available. As described in the HNO 2021, reliable access to electricity remains low across the country while 74 per cent of the available basic infrastructures facilities are reported damaged, and the poor conditions of access ways and roads affected by ten years of conflict, in addition to the harm caused becauseof natural events such as heavy rains, negatively affect the possibility to boost the speed of local economic recovery.Furthermore, social cohesion and and community security is also at stake with a tremendous increase number of IDPs in the area, pressure worsened by the COVID-19 pandemic’s also impacting on social cohesion.

5 - Implementation of activities

With a similar approach as in the FSL project, and as the project is integrated with the protection project, SARD aims to support the sustainability of the family unit as a whole through livelihood recovery support (ERL sector). In situations where children are vulnerable to separation from their families, SARD's Mobile Case Management team intervenes through provision of case management services. Based on the case, an integrated program is developed to strengthen the family unit which includes livelihood support for the caregivers. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria to gauge the family’s capacity. A BoQ (Bill of Quantities) will be signed with selected families and followed by cash grant dispersion. Following the action, review and satisfaction survey are conducted by the M&E team to verify implementation and measure the impact of the action.At community level, the project will support 10-15 communities with 15 different community projects, benefiting 6000 families (30000 individuals) in restoring their basic infrastructure through public infrastructure repairs improve community safety and economic activity (such as, access ways to markets, access ways to cultivable lands, water channeling to reduce risk of flooding, light rehabilitation of water, sewage, drainage and electricity systems). Selection of the project will be made by the local population through consultation, group discussions and common agreement. In both type of actions, selection criteria are transparent, and communities at the center of the process through sensitization sessions. Community members are encouraged to report any complaints - including on selection, to SARD's CRM.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Women and girls being excluded of the program design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: As per our protection mainstreaming policy needs of women and girl are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff, questions and action/response targeting the most vulnerable and the gender sensitivity. Risk: Boys and girls (under 18 years) are excluded of the design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Needs of boys and girl are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff (part of SARD child protection mobile team) with adapted assessment tools and action/response targeting the young and the gender sensitivity. The CRM is adapted for under 18 years, ensuring the feasibility of a safe and secure feedback mechanism, as well as adapted follow up to address the issues. Risk: Older persons and persons with a disability (PWD) part of the design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Program staff is regularly trained inclusion and adequate program response tailored made for PWD. Risk: Increased sensitivity between host communities and IDPs. Mitigation: Based on a community approach, social activities are conducted based on communities’ initiatives, ensuring an inclusive participation of all groups. SARD ensure the involvement between the host community and internally displaced persons once the project (specially in Shelter and infrastructure project) starts in order to mitigate any tension within the community. Risk: Program staff unintentionally perpetuate harm or increase stigma of data confidentiality is not respected due to lack of training. For more details, SARD makes a protection risk analysis update twice a year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

I. Safety and Security Risks The volatile and often unpredictable nature of the Syrian conflict continues to impose significant risk on humanitarian staff and beneficiaries' safety. Risks could include but not limited to a sudden escalation in hostilities, airstrikes, armed clashes, and kidnapping. The likelihood of increased conflict hostilities is considered moderate in northern Aleppo compared to opposition-controlled areas of Idleb and western Aleppo. The impact of this risk is assessed as high in both areas. SARD has developed and adopted tools to ensure a sufficient level of standard of care to safeguard employees and beneficiaries from a reasonably foreseeable risk of harm is maintained.II. Operations and Program-related Risks The high-risk environment of northwest Syria continues to impose significant risk on the continuation of humanitarian activities. Risks to humanitarian programs implementation in the context of northwest Syria include but not limited to partial or complete closure of border crossings with Turkey; disruption of the supply chain; fluctuating exchange rate; and community acceptance. The likelihood and the impact of operations and program-related risks in areas of northern Aleppo, Idleb, and western Aleppo assessed as high. SARD has developed and adopted tools to ensure its readiness to cope and adjust with sudden unexpected risk as they arise. III. Financial and Reputation Risks The absence of a fully functioning banking system in northwest Syria is a key challenge is how to facilitate fund to northwest Syria with the at most due diligence approach. SARD has developed and adopted tools to minimize the risk and ensure the incorporation of due diligence in its financial transactions.
