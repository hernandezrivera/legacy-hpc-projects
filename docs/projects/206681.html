Project Summary

The project targets 3012(2000 boys and 1012 girls) children, adolescents, and youths (3-17 years old) who will have access to educational programming and attend schools in Leer County, Unity State. About 4 temporary learning spaces will be established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and 4 sex-segregated WASH facilities will be established. About 2000((1200 girls and 800 boys) will have access to new gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls). Another 2000(1200 boys and 800 girls) children will benefit from rehabilitated gender-sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards (unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls. About 12 teachers (4 males and 8 females) will be supported with teachers' incentives for 12 months.
Furthermore, about 1500 (800 girls and 700 boys) children and youth (6-17 years) will benefit from recreational material supplies, and children (3-5 years) will benefit from Early Childhood and Development (ECD) kits, and another 2500(1500 boys and 800 girls) children (5-17) and teachers provided with teaching and learning materials.
The adult direct beneficiaries are teachers who will be trained on EiEs, basic pedagogy, and learner-centered methodologies, targeting 12 teachers (8 males and 4 females), and they will also be trained on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms. Other adult beneficiaries are community education committee members and local education officials who will be trained in school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS), and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) targeting 28 people (16 males and 12 Females)
Active mobilization for school campaigns will be organized to launch back-to-school/learning campaigns at least once per term (Three times a year)

1. Needs

Protracted periods of violent conflict in South Sudan have negatively impacted the pace of development and the government’s capacity to deliver basic services remains low. South Sudanese continue to return from Sudan in large numbers, requiring significant emergency support. Rising food insecurity, disease outbreaks, and seasonal flooding continue to impact humanitarian conditions.According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), Feb 2022/ about 8.9 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 compared to the year 2021. The country remained in a protection crisis due to a lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services, which are holding people back from stability and sustainable development mostly due to the triple shock of intensified inter-communal conflict, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. The Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) projections for July 2021 estimated that Leer is expected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4+). The majority of people children, boys, girls, adolescents, and youths in Leer County lack basic primary education or drop out of school at an early primary level due to culturally related factors such as early marriages, early pregnancies, and demand for bride wealth. Pupils attending schools in Leer County are showing poor educational performance that has been necessitated by the factors mentioned above, especially the girls who are disproportionately most affected; other factors include insufficient school feeding programs for hungry children, inadequate teaching, and learning materials, inconsistent teacher payment, inadequate teacher training, limited sanitation facilities at schools, low enrolment and attendance, and high drop-out rates, especially among girls. Existing community infrastructures such as schools serve as a unique entry point for protecting children and other lifesaving services, including access to safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, and protection mechanisms reaching one of the most vulnerable groups - children. So far, the government-supported education services are extremely inadequate and the children are left in the villages doing nothing while others look after cattle. United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs also highlighted a similar situation in regard to the context situation and impacts in South Sudan as below; It is worth noting that, when the war broke out in Sudan in April this year, many South Sudanese returnees and refugees crossed the borders into South Sudan, especially from Renk, Poluch, and other entry points. This has increased the burdens on already meager resources and services to the suffering populations of South Sudan in terms of health, education, and food security among others. Coupled with the floods that had destroyed livelihood, swept away education infrastructures, and endangered the possibility of enrolling more returnee pupils in schools.J4CO with vast experiences in education programming in Leer and other parts of Unity state plans to implement education in Emergencies to respond to the education needs of the most vulnerable children both of the host, displaced, returnees, and refugees children by establishing semi-permanent learning spaces, support teachers, training and incentivize them, establish WASH facilities to promote a protective environment for education in Leer county.

2. Project activities

undertaken to achieve the overall objectives project/ cluster objectives and humanitarian cluster objectives;The activities are the construction of 6 new Temporary Learning Spaces and 4 segregated latrines of 2 stances each. renovate 2 already existing TLS will be rehabilitated also with 2 sex-segregated latrines of 2 stances each. About 1500 pupils (800 boys, 700 girls) 3-5 years old will be enrolled in ECD, and 2000 (1200 girls, 800) will be enrolled in basic primary school. The project will ensure that of EiE supplies including, ECD, teaching, and learning materials are provided to 2000 pupils (1,200 boys girls 800). The project will also support the training of 12 teachers (8 males, 4 Females) of Pedagogy, ethics, and classroom management (3 each from the 4 schools) on psychosocial support, and referral mechanisms for Child Protection/nutrition/health and 28 PTA members on school governance. All the 12 teachers will be provided with incentives for motivation for 12 months. Out of the 12 teachers, 8 of them (2 teachers from each school) will be trained in ECD animations and programming. Other activities will include training teachers on PSS and establishment of referral pathways, training adolescent girls on MHM and provision of dignity kits, Conducting Back to learning Campaigns, Community Engagement Meetings.
