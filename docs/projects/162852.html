Project Summary

This is a food security and livelihoods (FSL) project planned to be implemented in four states including West and central Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan. WRS is currently providing integrated humanitarian emergency response, recovery and development programs in health, nutrition, education, protection, FSL, peace building and WASH activities funded bilateral and multilateral donors including OFDA, DFID, SIDA, SHF, UNICEF, WFP, MoFA of Netherlands and private donors.
This FSL project is designed with the objective of saving lives and building resilience of the most vulnerable HHs and reducing acute food insecurity and malnutrition through ensuring availability of nutritious food to 69035 HHs in the four states including El Kurmuk, Geissan, Dilling, Kadugli, Talodi, Geneina, Habila, Jebel Moon, kerenik, Kulbus and Sirba localities. In allignment with the FSL sector objectives, WR FSL project also focuses on two major areas. The first focus area is that ensuring conflict and other disaster affected people in the four states including IPDs, returnees, host communities and nomads receive timely, adequate and required assistance during and in the aftermath of a shock. The other focus area is improving availability and accessibility to sufficient, quality and nutritious food to individuals impacted by protracted crisis including IDPs, returnees, host communities and nomads in the four states. To achieve these objectives, WR conducted a comprehensive assessment in Blue Nile and South Kordofan. The assessment includes community level discussion, field visit, discussion with government line ministries, UN agenies, NGOs operating the area and review of various data. In West and Central Darfur WR conducted community based participatory planning and project plan has been prepared with active involvement of the community and activities are prioritized for implementation based on community priorities and triangulated with data from different sources. IDPs, returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities are the central focus of the project. Provision of agricultural inputs, improving livestock veterinary services & water schemes, income generating activities, various capacity buildings, saving and loan schemes, restocking, promotion of FFA activities and gender & environment are the main focus areas.
Even though women are heads for almost half of the households, they are often hindered by many traditional norms from making real progress which make it difficult for them to access new agricultural technologies as well as improved livelihood opportunities. This project will mainly target women in order to build their capacities thus allowing them to sufficiently increase their productivity beyond consumption. Farming activities, which have been dominated by traditional and low yielding varieties, will be improved to enhance production that will ultimately improve the income for women. To this end, the project will organize vulnerable women into groups, provide motorized pumps & build their capacity to grow vegetables and other crops by providing training and required inputs.
The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. While implementing this project, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders and beneficiaries themselves. As described further below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls and children while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability.

1 - Needs

In order to save, protect and support the affected population to have a better future, in collaboration with community and other stakeholders, World Relief identified and prioritized the most pressing needs. In FY 2019, WR conducted comprehensive assessment in Blue Nile and South Kordofan and Community Based Participator planning in West and Central Darfur. The findings of the assessments and other sources of information reveal the following needs:  Erratic rainfall to sustain sufficient production of food crops: trends show unpredictability of rainfall patterns in the localities with adverse effect on crop production, pasture development for animals, and market prices which undermine efforts of vulnerable people (especially women);  Shortage of water for livestock: 96% of households covered by the study indicated shortage of water for livestock which gets pronounced during dry season forcing households to travel more than 10km to get water for livestock.  Incidence of seasonal pests: pests often destroy crops and negatively affect annual harvests. Poor harvest, in turn, adversely affect food security and nutritional status of women, children and men. Poor harvests adversely impact on income generation of vulnerable households. Provision of appropriate agricultural supports and training will be important;  Environmental degradation: environmental degradation is worsening overtime. Firewood need for bricks making requires cutting of trees. Over grazing continue to adverse effect the environment. These resulted in dwindling vegetation cover exposing soil to various erosive agents. Poor or non existent agro forestry practices contribute to the environmental degradation. Raising environmental awareness of communities with practical actions is important; Inadequate agricultural/livestock extension services: lack of access to government extension services is a bottleneck. None of the women covered in the studies had access to extension services of MoA; Female headed households constitute 44% of HHs in Darfur; 85.1% of them are actively involved in agricultural activities. However, women earn less than men. About 92.3% of these women farm with rudimentary traditional tools; they do not have access to appropriate agricultural inputs. Gap in training on appropriate agricultural skills: No sufficient training is provided on modern food crop production, post-harvest management, conservation and marketing; and Food commodities are available in the markets, but the prices are high. • The HH food consumption is only sorghum without diversification, could lead malnutrition especially among young children • IDPs access to land is constrained by insecurity, due to the protracted conflict, the IDPs live in fear of land mines and UXOs• Lack of improved varieties resulted in limited productivity of cash • Mono-cropping, lack of crop rotation contributed to expansion of the noxious weed (Striga) and reduced the soil fertility; • Lack of improved farm tools • The farmers’ low knowledge and skills about improved agronomic practices • Lack of extension system, farmers’ training centers, • Poor post-harvest handling practices• Absence of organic fertilizers usage to improve the chemical (nutritional) and physical properties of the soil. • Shortage of vet clinics and poor facilities• Lack of vet drugs, supplies to serve livestock holders• Limited number of CAHWs and low level of skill and capacity to provide primary level service to livestock owners• Lack of productive skill to lead market oriented business• Lack of skill and technology on modern honey production• Lack of capital/credit for women to benefit from small scale business development• Lack of market outlets to link the producers with customers

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project provides the maximum care not to cause damage to the environment. For activities having an impact on the environment, the project design required mitigation measures, which will be implemented parallel to project activities implementation. Environmental markers guideline was reviewed to ensure that the project is designed sensitive to the environment and necessary mitigation measures taken as per the guideline. To ensure environmental compliance and adhere to no-do-harm principles WR will provide training on integrated water resources management and create awareness to the community on environment. The project work hand in hand with the MOA so that the different types of inputs provided to the community pass through quality check based government standards and other quality check measures. Through other FSL programs, WR is supporting several tree nurseries in the project proposed localities and the seedlings produced in the nurseries will be distributed to individual households, planed in public places, and provided to different institutions including health facilities, schools and social centers.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Sector coordinators ensure that all activities contribute to improving the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women access to and control over assets created by the project. Women and girls are overburdened in domestic and agricultural activities and are sole responsible for child care in the household. Besides, women have almost no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and develop strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. Women and women headed households will get priority in addressing the needs identified by the project. More than 60% of the beneficiary households will be women. Improved technologies that reduces the burden of women are included in the project so that women get enough time for domestic and other reproductive activities. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls and minority groups. While implementing this project, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations that expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Mobile veterinary services will be conducted, reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances to project sites. The organization will also be sure to promote peace sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. In addition, the consortium M&E system segregates data by groups to clearly measure and analyze how the project is affecting different groups. To this end, the organization will focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding of the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity. In addition, the organization ensure that all staff, volunteers and contractors working with the organization sign code of conduct.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The actions proposed by WR are prepared based on the needs and priorities of the communities, which has been collected and compiled through needs assessment, field visit and discussion with community leaders and line ministries. In addition, the intended communities have also confirmed their commitment to fully participate through the entire project life cycle. • Overall the community will be involved in the project as depicted below. - Through their representatives the community had been involved in needs assessment and formulation of this project; - WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers working on health and nutrition. The volunteers involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level health & nutrition committees. This committee closely works with health facilities & oversee WR engagement in health and nutrition. The third layer is working with community-level projects management committees. These committees established to oversee the work of WR in their respective areas or locality. In addition, Community Animal Health Workers and Village Extension Agents, who contribute to the strengthening of livestock and agriculture-based livelihoods of the communities, and provide feedback through various reporting and communication mechanisms.• The community level engagement materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting ( monthly) at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. The monthly meeting feedback discussed at management level, which is conveyed, on monthly bases. In different WR facilities ( health, nutrition, WASH, vet post etc) level, WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. The information obtained from the hotline report will be verified by WRS’s team and for serious allegations, a committee will be established to carry out a detailed investigation.Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box to get the feedback from beneficiary communities. The beneficiary community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR coordinators will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with all concerned including higher management. Also, the actions taken to the community findings will be shared back to the community.
