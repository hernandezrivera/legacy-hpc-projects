Project Summary


* All the sub-districts that we are targeting is within the severity 3,4,5 by that the percentage will be 100% with the mentioned severity.
* The estimated cost per beneficiary is 5$.
* According to RSRP field assessment that the proposed project will cover 15% of total people with disabilities within the targeted areas.
* As for 2020 RSRP received fund of $ 280,000 for implementing Education services.
* For 2021 RSRP has received fund of $ 835,783 for implementing Education services.
* During 2020 RSRP through implementation of projects have reached 2024 of affected people in need fo Education support.
Considering the complicated impact of the 2020 situation in the conflict-related displacement area of intervention in early 2020 and the longer-term impact of the COVID-19 pandemic - 78% drop in value of the Syrian currency in just one year (to SYP 3,030/US$1 on informal market (WFP, December 2020)). This generated additional needs for IDPs and results 2.4 million children out of school. The key reasons why children were not able to receive proper education was that schools were not accessible in their area, schooling has not being affordable, children labor is common to support the family household and schools are either damaged, non operative or used for non-education purposes. In addition, teenager and children with disabilities have usually no access to educationservices due to limited availability of education facilities and specific services needed.
After consultation with the education cluster in regards to the known education gaps in the area of intervention. RSRP deployed an assessment team to conduct a deeper assessment in regards to the needs and needed time of possible implementation. The team considered all vulnerability groups, and gender aspects, The fact finding mission identified in addition students at risk of dropping out, children out of school, persons with disabilities. The mission was done knowing the general sector assessment and conducted in coordination with the local direatorate of education (DOE).
The project’s aim and task is to scale up safe and equitable access to education for 728,170 children in 35 sub-districts, youth and adults of displaced people, returnees and overburdened host community within the highly neglected sub-districts of:
Farqalas , Tadmor/ Homs Governorate
Ziyara, Madiq Castle, As-Salamiyeh, Muhradah / Hama Governoarate
Jebel Saman, Atareb, Haritan, Nabul / Aleppo
Deir-ez-Zor, Muhasan, Tabni, Khasham, Abu Kamal, Jalaa, Al Mayadin, Ashara / Deir Ezzor Governorate.
Sabkha, Madaan / Ar-raqqa Governorate
Dar'a, Busra Esh-Sham, Kherbet Ghazala, Da'el, Mzeireb, Ghabagheb, Izra', Jasim, Hrak, Nawa, Mseifra, As-Sanamayn, Shaqa / Dar’a Governorate
As-Sweida, Salkhad, Qarayya, Shahba / As-Sweida Governorate.
The total of 728,170 targeted beneficiaries, will receive education assistance as following:
Total of (3-17yrs) students 710,731
Total of (18-24yrs) youth 7789
Total of (25-59) teaching personnel 9650
Total number of persons with disabilities 116,507
Worth to mention is that the project is in line with HNO 2021 and the Education sector strategic objectives (SO2 and SO3) and priorities:
Strategic Objective 2: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by promoting international law, IHL, IHRL and though quality, principled assistance
Strategic Objective 3: Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.
RSRP will coordinate with the WASH sector as well as the education sector to rehabilitate 10 schools / learning spaces which will include WASH facilities, and to implement soft component activities in schools.

4 - Needs

According to the conducted assessment and HNO 2021, Deir Ezzor, Aleppo, Dar’a, Ar-raqqa, Hama, Homs and Sweida score level 3 to 5 on the sectoral severity scale and estimated that nearly 3,506,856 children, teenagers and children with disabilities face challenges accessing learning services of children, while 74% aren’t attending classes due to the poor condition of educational facilities where about 8230 of schools are damaged, used as shelter or inaccessible. Limited availability of functioning ECE centers and vocational training opportunities. Furthermore, the displacement of educational staff has led to severe lack of qualified educational personnel capable of teaching where over 116,896 teachers need education assistance. 5 - Implementation of activities

RSRP is implementing by direct implementation only. RSRP by their hub office in DZ as sustained presence will serve north and northeastern regions and is now implementing the ECW for 2021-22 with UNICEF’s, as have been done in2018 and implementing the exam project since2019 until current date with provided remedial lessons in accommodation centres managed by RSRP. RSRP has implemented projects located in Raqqa and Dar’a. According to assessment, RSRP planning to expand the services to the geographic areas which are accessible to RSRP, the activities will be in line with the Education sector’s response strategy adhering to the needs-based approach, identification of children aged 3-17,youth aged18-24 and adults PIN within the framework of 2021HNO to sub-district level and adhering to HRP2021 strategic objective SO2andSO3.RSRP MEAL has crucial points to ensure the quality of the output:-All activities carried out are monitored by the field staff/teachers pre, during and post-implementation to monitor/evaluate and fill the gaps.-RSRP’s quality assessment, the tools are structured around (conceptual framing, openness and transparency, cultural appropriateness, robustness of methodology, validity, reliability, and cogency) aspects.-CMS tool will be used to register and monitor the children. MEAL department identifies cases to refer it either to protection services, another education service, or to public school.-Creating different channels of communication for beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints.-Consider measures of COVID-19. All activities will be implemented in coordination with the sectors, MoE, local authorities and other stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

PRA1:Learning facilities unable to put into place and maintain Covid-19 safety measures including positive behavioral changes.Likelihood: Medium Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: 1-RSRP will ensure that learning facilities should have Covid-19 safety protocols in place and protocols should include identification, prevention measures, awareness raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health center. These protocols include no-touch thermometers, hand washing and hand-sanitizer stations, face masks reminded of good practices and encouraged to follow themPRA2:Insufficient water and sanitation facilities result in an unwillingness or inability to use WASH facilities or a mixing of sexes and ages.Likelihood: High Impact: High.Mitigation Measures:1-Sufficient water and sanitation facilities should be provided and maintained.2-WASH facilities should be sex and age segregated and accessible to people with disabilities.PRA3:Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. Likelihood: Low Impact: High.Mitigation Measures:- RSRP learning facilities to have an enforced code of conduct covering school; staff, aid workers or contractorsPRA4:Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection due to damaged infrastructure.Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures:1-Learning facilities should be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected including stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electric, water,2-RSRP to ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start
