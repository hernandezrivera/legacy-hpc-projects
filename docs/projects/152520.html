Project Summary

South Sudan health indicators are among the worst in the world, high disease burden and < 5 Mortality at 93/1000 live births and maternal mortality ratio is at 789/100,000 births, a young girl in South Sudan is three times more likely to die in childbirth than to finish primary school (according to South Sudan national human development index 2016). Following the renewed conflict in 2016 and the resultant economic downward spiral, violent behaviour towards civilians, particularly against the women and children increased, with most of the population been affected and the gender implications has been dire. Roles have changed due to absence of men; women and children have taken up the role of breadwinner in the family by moving in search of food, firewood, and petty jobs, these somehow has exposed them to exploitation and sexual violence. Additionally, school attendance has been affected as most children are forced out of school to support their parents and HMIS includes girls engaging in transactional sex that exposes them to negative reproductive health consequences.
Together, the level of humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains precarious as communities are still continuously being forced out of their respective areas due to active conflict between the warring factions. In June 2018, thousands were forced to flee their homes after fighting broke in different locations in greater Baggari-Wau County, mainly women, children and elderly . During the fighting the existing functional health facilities supported by partners including Islamic Relief in (Wadalel, Ngodakala, Ngoku, Ngisa and Mboro) were either vandalized and/or properties and supplies looted. For the last 5 years active conflict in Wau resulted into health facilities destruction, attacks on civilians, and few functional health facilities face challenges of delivering the complete basic package of health-care services because of pipeline breakage of drug supplies and shortages of skilled health personnel. It has been challenging for humanitarian organizations to access the areas because of denial or uncertain security situation in greater Baggari in to provide services, however the need to provide basic health services is high to the population including children <5 who are susceptible to Vaccine preventable diseases (VPD), malaria endemicity, acute malnutrition, war related injuries, and disease outbreaks risk within the county.
On that backdrop, Islamic Relief South Sudan (IRSS) intends to continue to provide lifesaving humanitarian health assistance to approximately 12,855 (50.4% Male and 49.6% Female) individuals, with focus on women, children and elderly as the most vulnerable population in Wau (Baggari and Bazia). The interventions will respond to the immediate needs and contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality in the conflict affected host communities and IDPs in greater Baggari and Bazia of Wau County, Western Bhar El Gazhal.
Guided by MOH and WHO relevant health guidelines with presence of technical capacity; through qualified health team, Islamic Relief South Sudan will implement the project at which very person will be entitled to the highest attainable level of medical services regardless of age, gender, religion and social diversity.
The Purpose of Interventions is life saving and contribution to reduction to mortality and morbidity in the area of operations through;
1. Implementing essential primary healthcare emergency clinical packages targeting the most vulnerable displaced population in Baggari and Bazia (Wau)
2. Immunize the most vulnerable displaced groups (Children U5, PLW)
3. Implement outbreak investigation and response.
4. Procurement of emergency health pipeline
5. Mainstream Accountability to Affected People.

Needs

Greater Baggari and Bazia area (South-West) of Wau have been known to have humanitarian needs and poor access for last 2 years. This region is settled by many IDPs displaced from Wau town and surrounding suburbs, though there is no reliable data for the population size and the number of people displaced into the Baggari area, the displaced people integrated into the host community and no typical IDPs camps. During the same period the government has not been able to offer any social services in Baggari area Because it is under opposition control, that has also not been able to provide adequate services due to inadequate capacity. It is through humanitarian agencies that Baggari and Bazia people have been getting basic assistance. Mboro, Farajalla and Ngisa used to be functional PHCUs before the conflict begun in 2013, most of the facilities were closed after then due to lack of supplies and human resources. From 2017 Humanitarian access has been intermittent, MSF provided services in Mboro, Farajalla and neighbouring Ngohalima until April 2017 and ICRC re-opened Ngodakala and neighbouring Ngoku and so they were the only functional facilities in the area. It was followed later by UNICEF and partners from August 2018, and they re-opened Ngisa and Mboro health facilities. Health, nutrition, education, WASH and Protection services and facilities were fully established in Baggari area early 2018, including Islamic Relief been providing health services in Gede, Mboro and Ngisa PHCC. But all these were ruined again following the recent June 24th 2018 resumed fighting between government and the opposition forces in the area and NGOs vacated and the community is displaced in forested areas and away from the main centres/roads During RRM visit led by UNICEF in September 2018, all the five health facilities in Baggari (Ngisa, Farajallah, Ngodakallah, Ngoku, and Mboro) were closed for about two and half months. Health supplies were looted, 8 tents that provided stabilisation centre and admission area in both Ngisa and Mboro were burnt. KI with some health workers and Community leaders reported 11 death (8 Children < years). Major causes of morbidity are Malaria, ARI and Diarrhoea and mothers continue to deliver not attended by skilled cadre. With all these the need to provide basic health services is high to the population including children <5 who are susceptible to Vaccine preventable diseases (VPD), high malaria endemic, acute malnutrition and disease outbreaks risk within the county. The main specific needs this intervention is attempting to address is; to improve access to preventive and curative health care for services disadvantaged population of greater Baggari and Bazia of Wau County.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

IRSS believes in participatory approach and local communities are engaged right from project planning, implementation and management interventions. information regarding project activities are shared with the community during field level project orientation and also shared through the community health committees, community health workers and community leaders who speak the local language.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

All the messages related to health and hygiene promotion are made contextualized or tailored to the local needs especially needs of women, girls, boys and men are considered and focused. Banners and other printing materials are also made in local language so that targeted communities (men, women, boys and girls) understand it easily. For example, diseases prevention, health promotion and education activities will involve women and men.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS intends to create awareness and train our health staff on handling cases related to sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) /HIV/TB and Mental health cases by linking with existing facilities, facilitate and support them. IRSS will support SGBV cases according to the facility health capacity and level of care while cases within this output will be referred according to the referral pathway that will have been established.All new IR staff complete a mandatory induction which includes the Code of Conduct (reviewed and signed), and where a session (1:1 or group) is provided by both Protection and Gender staff. IR also has a policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, child protection and a Safeguarding focal points to address any allegations of sexual exploitation or abuse and all staff are trained on these policies. In addition, programme staff participate in training sessions on protection and gender (general principles of gender mainstreaming and safe programming and specific themes).

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The project follows a strong community based approach at all steps from targeting, to consultation on design and implementation to accountability and monitoring. Before planning these intervention, besides consulting community leaders, IRSS also consulted with patients coming to the health facility. moreover, IRSS is closely working with local communities through community health workers and social mobilizers who get feedback after the health education sessions or mobilization are done. Separate discussions were held with different groups including PLW through the community health workers to identify their needs and concerns.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

It is indeed one of the core values of Islamic Relief Worldwide South Sudan that every person must be accountable. Complaints and feedback are valuable source of information that senior management use to fill up the loopholes. In this context, a complaint and feedback handling mechanism has been introduced by IRSS that consists of a complaints and feedback focal point who is available at the health facility and also complaint registers and boxes are placed at health facility level. IRSS has also a telephone numbers (where network is available), which is disseminated during the project interventions. Separate banner are printed in local language, which includes the telephone number and email ID of the focal point. The health facility management committee members are trained to collect community complaints and feedback during their consultations and interactions with community members and pass the information to the focal point.Based on the complaints and feedback received a monthly report will be generated by the focal point and shared with management for necessary actions. Through the feedback and complaints mechanisms that include whistle blowing on corruption by beneficiaries at the community level through hotlines. The whistle blowers are protected by the organization during investigation process. IR globally is a certified member of CHS alliance which obligates it to design and apply accountability mechanism.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Besides engagement of local communities in project orientation, implementation and management, IRSS also engages committees in monitoring and evaluation of the project interventions. the health facility management community members are responsible for regular monitoring of our health interventions. Beside providing feedback and suggestion they also assist our team during health interventions especially organizing health outreach programme. IRSS values the suggestions and feedback of members of health facility management committee. Members of these committees are representatives of different communities/tribes/groups living the catchment of our target area.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS gender and protection analysis shows that women and girls have been disproportionately affected by this crisis. In the target areas women have an important role in providing economic support to their families amid these hard times and yet lack decision making power at both the HH and community levels hence are more exposed to protection threats. Women and girls are particularly affected by a lack of basic health services, IRSS will address these needs through active participation of health facility management committee members in the project activities, stress on engaging volunteers from community especially women and equal opportunities for recruitment for males and females. The staff and community committee members will be train on how to provide awareness about equal opportunities for all vulnerable groups specially women and girls

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IRSS has a gender and protection policies in place that guide staff conduct and equal employment opportunities. The participation of women and girls in community health management committee, address inequality and protection threats in the target community as members of the committee are being train and aware on handling the challenges in the community. During service intervention are delivered to every person regardless of age, gender, religion and social diversity. Understanding that women, men, boys and girls are affected differently, IRSS will build local capacity on protection threats both gender.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will measure access of beneficiaries through regular monitoring and evaluation of equitable access to health services in this projects through consultations with communities particularly women and girls, disabled and elderly regarding their satisfaction, access and perceptions of safety in relation to health interventions. Data collected will be sex and age dis-aggregated. Complaints and feedback mechanism will be fully functional and will be used to measure beneficiary access through the concerns raised and views provided

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of beneficiaries especially protection cases are been carefully handled. Privacy, dignity and confidentiality of patients information is taken care of by IRSS project team as well as monitoring staff. The project monitors protection from SEA and SGBV through the placement of feedback mechanisms; the training of staff and CHWs, particularly on being able to detect indicators of violence and ask the right questions. Regular monitoring of the project will also include consultations with communities particularly women and girls regarding their perceptions of safety in relation to health interventions.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Post health services delivery in facilities will include consultations through focus group discussions with communities particularly women, girls and special needs group such as the disabled and elderly regarding their perceptions to their dignity in relation to health interventions and confidentiality of information of the clients. IRSS health staff will ensure access to patient consent for health procedures and information sharing.
