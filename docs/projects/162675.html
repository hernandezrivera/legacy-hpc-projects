Project Summary

The proposed project aims to respond to the HRP 2020 objectives by providing emergency and life-saving humanitarian assistance for South Sudanese refugees in West Kordofan, South Kordofan states
The project will link to the objectives of the Refugee Response Strategy in Sudan by providing basic services for new arrivals, addressing ongoing needs for the refugees already settled, and their host community population. The intervention aims at responding in health, nutrition, WASH and FSL sector through an integrated approach, moreover contributing toward self-resilience among refugees and their host communities.

1 - Needs

According to HNO draft report, there are 1.1. million of refugees and asylum-seekers in Sudan (source: UNHCR and the Government of Sudan Commission of Refugees (COR) report as of 30 Sept 2019). Sudan hosts the largest population of South Sudanese refugees in Africa, with nearly 859,000 people as of 31 August 2019. According to UNHCR October 31 data, West Kordofan state has 62,468 refugees (24% are females in reproductive age, and 49% women headed HHs). In South Kordofan state there are 36,644 refugees (25% are females in reproductive age, and 66% women headed HHs). Concern WW has been a leading partner of UNHCR and SHF in the refugee response in both states by providing integrated humanitarian assistance (health, nutrition, WASH and FSL sector) for refugee children u5, PLW/Gs, and in 2020 we would like to continue working for refugees and their host community population for the following reasons: 1) While operating in the region, Concern experienced significant gap in the health services e.g. lack of health staff in rural health facilities, old and dysfunctional infrastructure, lack of health supplies, consumables and medicines, and PHC services. Weak life-saving curative and preventative services for pregnant and lactating mothers, children u5 is a constant struggle. However there is a health safety net (medical insurance coverage) exists in the region, refugees are not part of this safety net, and even their host population don't have access to all the required coverage. Due to the weak health service, the referral system is not working effectively, and although Concern has provided assistance in the health system strengthening, the current economic situation does not support the self-management of the system without additional support from the humanitarian community. Provision of medical supplies, medicines, infrastructure development, capacity building is needed to strengthen the weak health system. 2) During the 2019 operation, Concern provided integrated nutrition services within the catchment of the health facilities, and experienced critical malnutrition among SS refugees (children u5, PLW/Gs), and some of the host population females and children as well. An average of 80-100 admission cases/month registered from which an average of 5 required stabilization in the health centers. More than half of the refugees are females in reproductive age given a high number of pregnant and lactating mothers and children u5 are in critical nutrition condition requires nutrition support, and vitamins. Therefore Concern proposes to continue nutrition services for the SS refugees, and their host families in critical needs, particularly in isolated locations. 3) In 2019 Concern managed to narrow the gap of both community and institution WASH needs, however, there are so much need in both states that the support of WASH intervention, particularly in health and nutrition facilities, is critical. E.g. renovation of water yards and connect them to health/nutrition facilities would serve not only the refugees but their host community population as well. Besides durable solutions, Concern would like to continue improving knowledge, attitude and behavior change around the health/nutrition facilities among the SS refugees and their host communities to increase the level of resilience against outbreaks and epidemics. 4) SS refugee population needs a critical food security support, due to their vulnerable position in both states. They live together with host communities, yet they cannot have land, and they cannot own property that reduces their change for stable income and sustainable survival. According to FEWSNET IPC forecast, by the end of April 2020, there will be serious hunger gap (level4) in Kordofan, because of inadequate harvest. Concern would like to continue serving the most in need refugees, in their host communities through an integrated food security and livelihoods support, to improve their nutrition and health conditions

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the project design and implementation climate change adaptation approaches will be considered particularly for the renovation of health/nutrition facilities, and the water source renovation and management. During the technical assessment and technical design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of the important elements to consider. During the renovation of health/nutrition centres, solar system and related innovative solutions will be considered. Nutrition and health activities will also consider the environment by using environmentally manageable materials for building renovation, and waste management (debris) will be organized during and after the renovation. However the project is not directly impacted by the environment, during the health and nutrition capacity building training environment protection particularly related to medical waste management (storage and incinerating medical waste) will be considered, and a daily waste disposal will be organized around the health and nutrition facilities.Other aspects related to designing and constructing HH latrines will use locally available, natural resources to reduce the negative impact on ecological footprint.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups( IDPs, refugees) within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. - Newly arrived SS refugees will be oriented about registration procedures and they will be referred to the nearest registration point. Concern /national NGO partner will coordinate this procedure with UNHCR- Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be identified and they will be responsible for dealing with sensitive issues e.g. GBV cases and related protection issues. - Mobile services (nutrition/health) will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly female) for dealing with sensitive issues - Children will be in high focus during the field activities by the field staff and they will also report any protection related case through the child protection reporting mechanisms. Although in some areas this referral system is weak, Concern will look for adequate focal point/partner to implement child protection case management. - Other issues e.g. youth (children) and refugee youth migrating from the targeted localities to other locations due to livelihoods opportunities will be a central focus for the IGA and related activities to encourage them to stay in the region. - In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.- activities where the participation of women usually higher e.g. health/nutrition awareness raising, males (boys, husbands) will be also encouraged to participate where culturally appropriate and accepted to increase the gender balance - Each health facility will map the nearest referral point for GBV clinical response (nearest hospital)- During renovation of health/nutrition facilities, project staff will inform beneficiaries, particularly children about the "no-go-zones" for safety reasons. This will be also be marked, and signed clearly.- All data in the health and nutrition centers will be collected and analysed based on gender segregation

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

This project will contribute to the HRP 2020 health/nutrition WASH and FSL project with a special focus on refugee and their host communities, therefore, the Accountability Mechanisms will be developed as a joint exercise with the beneficiaries. The intervention will build on MoH strategic partnership in South/West Kordofan, to ensure that Concern meets PHC minimum standards. Concern is a member of HAP, hence, the principles and values of humanitarian accountability will be applied over the course of the project. Given the project is developed to fill the gap in the services provided previous interventions, and it will extensively focus on the refugees needs. This will be clearly communicated to all segments of the targeted population. The selection of the beneficiaries for particular activities will be carried out by the community management committees representing the different segments of the targeted communities based on set criteria that will be mentored by CWW and its national partner NGO. Applying the "'Do No Harm"" concept, services between the different kind of groups e.g., refugees, and host communities, will be carefully distributed as per their population proportion in the community and level of vulnerability. Concern has an updated Community Response Mechanism (CRM) guidelines which has been developed in consultation with community members. There will be various CRM tools applied for the intervention e.g. feedback boxes, focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions/meetings and record on feedback will be documented and responded. Concern, the affected populations, MoH, the national partner NGO, MSGs and community health and MSG, will select two community members, including one female, to represent their community and to be part of CRM committee to participate in the opening of the CRM boxes, complaint analysis and giving feedback to the community for the relevant complaints. During the project implementation, focus group discussion, informal/formal meetings and discussion will be held with various groups with a special focus on refugee groups, and their host communities, MSGs, youth group to have their feedback about the intervention. As much as possible Concern field team will use the feedback to build on the next step in the project implementation, and where it is not possible, it will be clearly communicated to the local community.
