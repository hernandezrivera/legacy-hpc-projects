Project Summary

Child protection issues are exacerbated during disaster, requiring immediate protection services to bring normalcy back in the lives of girls, boys and women. Measures to prevent abuse and violence and respond to cases of survivors of violence and exploitation need to be strengthened by providing safe access to protective services. Likewise, psychosocial distress experienced by people at the onset of any emergency requires professional care and support. Hence, the project provides mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) and coordination as a core area of response. An increase of CP risks is being observed in the current COVID-19 pandemic as well. Some of these may include: Physical and emotional maltreatment, Gender-based violence (GBV), mental health and psychosocial distress, child labour, unaccompanied and separated children and social exclusion. Following are some of the interventions:
1. Establishment of protective spaces to cater the needs of vulnerable girls, boys and women including disable. The protective spaces will be run by trained facilitators and provided with recreational and learning materials. It will offer opportunities for girls and boys to build resilience and recover from their experiences, provide a daily routine and a sense of normalcy.
2. Provision of Psychosocial Support (PSS) is primarily aimed at those individuals or group of people who require care and support including those in isolation or quarantine in case of the current COVID-19 situation. Digital platforms will be used for delivering PSS while there are movement restrictions in place. The PSS will be provided at three levels tailored to the needs of target population:
• Within facilities receiving suspected/infected people for care and support including isolation and quarantine facilities.
• Community based support in areas identified with suspected/infected people or receiving people exiting isolation/quarantine facilities and going through the social reintegration phase. Families, relatives and caregivers of such people will also be able to avail such services and support.
• Referral pathways in collaboration of case management for accessing social protection services/packages.
3. People demonstrating exacerbated distress and signs of mental health concerns or adverse reactions, would be referred to specialized mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Digital platforms will be used for delivery of MHPSS while movement restrictions are in place.
4. Provide CP monitoring and outreach social mobilization through dedicated and trained CP monitors and facilitators in coordination with community-based CP structures and CP Working Groups and Govt. counterparts;
5. Establish and strengthen coordination amongst key stakeholders and community-based CP structures for the identification of vulnerable girls, boys and women, including missing, unaccompanied, separated and disabled children, and promote their access to protection, social and psychosocial services through an effective and efficient case management and referral system;
6. Capacity building of relevant service providers, Govt. officials including DMAs, CSOs, rights-holders and duty-bearers in Child Protection in Emergency (CPiE), psychosocial support and strengthening advocacy and support for girls, boys and women affected by disaster to achieve enhanced protection for them;
7. Dissemination of critical information for prevention of violence against children (VAC) using different communication channels. In the current context, service provision will prioritize prevention of stigma, labelling and discrimination of people who are perceived to be infected or affected by COVID-19. The promotion of correct information and addressing emerging myths and rumors including positive understanding and behaviours about ‘social distancing’ should therefore include actions that support people to adopt and maintain healthy and rationale lifestyles.
