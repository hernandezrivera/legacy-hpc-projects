Project Summary

To continue supporting the Government of Mali in the management of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) database, in order to provide timely and accurate displacement data and help form the basis for rapid and effective humanitarian response and policy –making.

Needs

From 2012 to 2014, IOM Mali, was leading the Commission on Population Movements (CMP, a humanitarian coordination body comprised of the Government of Mali, NGOs and UN agencies), As part of a capacity building effort, IOM and the Government of Mali started co leading the CMP at the end of 2014. The CMP has been compiling and analyzing data gathered through its Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and from other sources for sharing with all humanitarian actors and helping a better planning of activities benefiting the most vulnerable. Results of the DTM show key information on specific displacement locations, areas of origin, age and sex disaggregation, assistance needed or received, and information on host families. Following the same capacity building process, IOM transferred its DTM program to the Government of Mali (GoM) through the DNDS on the 13th of November 2014. The leadership of the management of this database is now in the hands of the Government of Mali and IOM continues to actively support the GoM in this important activity in terms of data collection, analysis, and capacity building. The sustainability of displacement tracking activities through DTM in Mali is crucial. The conflict in 2012 led to the displacement of roughly 500 000 people. Of these, only 77,046 remained displaced while 526,505 had already returned to their place of origin by September 2018 (CMP Report of October 18, 2018). Despite a gradual stabilization recorded in 2013 and in 2015 following the peace agreements in Ouagadougou and Algeria respectively, the political, security and humanitarian situation deteriorated in May 2014 following incidents in Kidal and in April May 2015 following the incidents in Gao and Timbuktu. During this year, 2018, clashes between armed groups in Gao and Menaka, and social conflicts in Mopti, Segou, Gao and Menaka regions led to renewed internal displacements. The need for closely tracking and monitoring trends and patterns of displacement and return movements is essential, just as much as those who have remained in conflict affected areas. There is a need to keep an early warning system to further monitor sudden displacements of population that might be caused by a deterioration of the security situation in the Center and North of Mali. This will enable the provision of targeted lifesaving assistance and contingency planning through reliable data collection and analysis on figures, modes of transportation, routes, and areas of settlement and origin that are crucial for relevant humanitarian actors and stakeholders to build strong responses. In addition, the data collected will enable the Malian government to develop more informed and targeted public policy for the populations affected by the conflict. The data collected will be desegregated by gender considering the needs of the most vulnerable populations including women, children and elderly. IOM’s technical support to the government for 2019 will be essential to allow the continuation of this program. The project will permit to reinforce the capacity of the Malian authorities regarding data collection, data processing and analysis. In 2015, IOM introduced the use of tablets and Open Data Kit (ODK) in the data gathering process. This new technology has the merit to provide timely (daily or real time if needed) information on displacement. In 2016, IOM launched a series of training for technical staffs of the GoM on mobile data collection form design and management. In 2018, IOM assisted the GoM to build an online reporting system on population movements that will help all stakeholders to access dashboards, statistics and reports in real time. The capacity of the Government of Mali will continue to be built on displacement data collection, analysis and reporting in 2019.The project will ensure that gender balance is respected through the hiring and knowledge building processes.

Activities

- Strengthen the capacity of governmental institutions in charge of matters related to population displacement with a focus on women working in the targeted institutions:- Assist the DNDS in data collection for the DTM on the ground, through capacity building of the GoM personnel in the field in Gao, Timbuktu, Menaka, Kidal, Segou and Mopti regions, and filling gaps when needed:- Assist and strengthen DNDS in data analysis to provide better and regular updates of returnees and IDPs statistics reports.- Provide technical support to DNDS to assess needs in the main regions of return and among the IDPs with a focus on the specific needs of girls, boys, women and men. - Provide technical support and training to DNDS to conduct profiling exercises of IDPs and Returnees to better understand and address their needs and return intentions through surveys among IDPs in in Gao, Timbuktu, Menaka, Kidal, Segou and Mopti regions.- Support the DNDS in the management of DTM database through capacity building of its personnel in Bamako and maintenance of the database center- Assist the DNDS in developing and distributing material and DTM publications through an online reporting system.
