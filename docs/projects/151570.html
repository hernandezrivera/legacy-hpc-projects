Project Summary

The projects seeks to improve food security access and accelerate livelihood recovery for households affected by 2017 drought and perennial conflicts. The intervention will cover regions of Bari, Nugal, Mudug, Sanaag, Sool, Togdheer, Benadir, Bay, Bako0l and Lower Juba. The project will target mainly Internally displaced households, refugee returnees and very poor hos community households. The project objective covers the following
Objective 1: Improve immediate food security status for households affected by drought and conflict emergencies in Somalia
Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihood sources for drought and conflict destabilized households to realized enhanced food and income sources.
The specific activities will largely include cash based interventions that will target emergency crisis affected households of IPC 3 &4. The Cash which will include both conditional and unconditional is meant to enhance households immediate access to food through improving their purchasing power. Integrated in to this, will be a livelihood protection based activities covering restocking, emergency livestock support and diversification of livelihood source to enhance the recovery initiatives of households just emerging from the drought and classified to be in IPC 2&3. As part of its effort to address the long term food security needs, CARE will link this relief and recovery based project to its ongoing multi-year funded resilience base intervention such as the Som-rep resilience initiative. The project is designed to mainstream protection principles and to promote access, safety and dignity for all beneficiaries. This will include promoting safety and dignity and avoiding to cause harm and ensure impartial and neutral services delivery all the targeted beneficiaries based purely on needs. The project will equally ensure gender mainstreaming approaches remains its connectome in its design and implementation. Women, girls, boys will widely be consulted in every phase of the project. Wide consultation will be made with the community leadership to develop an all accepted selection criteria that will prioritize the most households as beneficiaries including women, households with elderly and disable individual and large size poor families. About 60% of the registered households that will receive the cash distribution will be women headed households. The project will partner with local CBOs, national NGOs and government line ministers i fulfillment of localization approach to aid delivery as advocated in the HRP 2018/19.

Needs

CARE need assessment conducted in the region of Sool, Sanaag, Nutgall, Bari, Galgadud, Mudug and Lower Juba indicated that most rural pastoral families lost between 60% of their livestock herds to recent drought. These families are still forced to continuously sale part of the remaining few herds for their daily food needs, thus further depleting the herd sizes of the productive assets. This undermines their immediate livelihood recover. The low livestock sizes has equally reduced the households income levels. The very poor category households that have no livestock to sale are still faced with acute food shortage. Their meagre income of about USD 1.5 / day t is not enough to cover both their food and nonfood needs. The price of basic commodities has risen by 30 % in the past one year. Two-thirds of IDPs especially the new groups are equally faced with acute food shortage. Their average income of US$20-30 is all spent on food The new IDPs are destitute and have no source of income and mainly rely on donations from relatives and well-wishers and/or support from humanitarian agencies. The poor households including the new IDPs currently meet around 40 % of their monthly food needs through market purchases and own production combined. This gives food deficit level of 60%. On average, they access one meal per day without the consideration of ongoing humanitarian assistances. While food is generally available in the local markets, almost all the poor households are faced with severe food deficit due to lack of access. The most urgent unmet needs of these food insecure families include immediate food access through sustained cash injection combined with enhance livelihood recovery assistances to build their capacity to fend for themselves. CARE intends to provide emergency food security assistances integrated with livelihood recovery support 36000 HH ( 216000 Persons) with immediate food access and livelihood recovery support in Somalia

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Durable solution strategies call for long terms sustainable support to livelihood diversification and strengthening to build self reliance’s among the huge population of vulnerable population including the protracted IDPs, returnees and structurally vulnerable persons. The Somalia Recovery and Resilience Framework (RRF) launched in 2018 calls for a collective vision around a strategy of addressing recurring drought and perennial conflicts which are the main drivers of food insecurity. Sustainable access to food security is best delivered where there is a link in short and long-term planning and financing of the sector through a coordinated engagement between actors involved in the sector. CARE t will put a great consideration linking this response to various pillars of durable solution as outlined in the Somalia durable solution framework. This will include strengthening partnership with government line ministries including ministry of Humanitarian and Disaster management , Ministry Agriculture, Fisheries and Livestock and Ministry of water both at state and federal level to steer and provide sound sectoral management . project will align its activities and strategies with Somali National Disaster Management Policy that provides the legislative framework for disaster management within relevant government institutions, and improves disaster risk governance at federal and regional state levelsThe project will equally coordinate with the government humanitarian actors in the country including the Food security cluster, the UN Humanitarian coordination forums. The project will equally build strong partnership with local stakeholders and national organization to ensure services reaches the most vulnerable and hard to reach women, girls, boys and , are not left behind in the education The project will equally advocate for long term funding of livelihood sectors through multi-year resilience and reconstruction funding in the country alongside sustaining short term measures to address needs arising during the emergencies. CARE has already secured a number of multi-year resilience based development program from different sectors such as, WASH and Food security from key donors EU, DFID, GAC USAID and ADB. Several of households that will be targeted in this intervention will in the long run be absorbed in CARE multi-year resilience and development program. Under multi-sector approaches, CARE will make use this intervention as launching pad to provide complimentary support to other nutrition, basic healthcare, WASH and protection. Already the areas targeted in this project are located in location where CARE is conducting other resilience based programme covering the sectors of WASH, Nutrition, Health, Food security and Protection. This linkage would certainly go along way addressing their diverse and complex needs in humanitarian and development responses
