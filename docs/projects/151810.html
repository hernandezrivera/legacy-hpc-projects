Project Summary

The proposed project aims to respond to water, sanitation and hygiene needs of internally displaced people in Baidoa , Bay Region, Jowhar Middle Shabelle region, Galkacyo south Mudug region, Abudwaaq Galgaduud region, Mogadishu/Kahda Banadir regions of Somalia. International medical Corps(IMC) will target IDPs and poor Host communities in the above mentioned geographical areas where it has been providing an integrated health, nutrition, protection and WASH intervention. IMC is proposing interventions intended to mitigate the impact of WASH related diseases in these regions.

Needs

International Medical Corps has been undertaking hygiene promotion and providing environmental sanitation activities at the IDP camps as part of an integrated package of humanitarian services that includes Health centers, primary health care units (PHUs), mobile health outreach and protection, to complement services provided by other partners, IMC has noted critical gaps in adequate latrine coverage, water sources within safe accesssible distances of households and clean water storage containers, particularly in the wake of the heavy Gu 2018 rainfall. . UNOCHA estimates that half a million (500,000) individuals have been affected by riverine flooding and a further 272,000 by flash floods across Somalia. FEWSNET reports indicate that an estimated 794,761 people have been affected by the flooding in southern and central states. A needs assessment conducted by IMC in September 2018 in Baidoa, Mogadishu/Kahda, Jowhar, Abudwaaq & Galkacyo South districts, revealed that most of the IDP camps experience shortage of safe water and inadequate sanitation services. Key informants interviewed reported that on average 40% of the HHs visited received less than 15 liters per day, and on average 30% of the IDPs take more than 1 hour round trip to access water source, which is not necessarily safe water. Almost all the assessed village IDPs had no strategic permanent water source, relying on water trucked from privately owned boreholes. Where rain-fed shallow wells exist within the host community settlements the recent Gu rain flash floods destroyed most of the shallow wells, leaving many un-protected and susceptible to contamination. In Baidoa,majority of IDPs are using unsafe water, while 70 percent of latrines are either damaged or already at capacity. Though there are organizations responding to the needs at the IDPs to various degrees, on average 30% of individual households visited have no water collection and storage containers (20 liters’ jerry cans or buckets).

Activities

1. Ensure Safe water supply to 6 IDP settlements: through Borehole pipeline connection.2. Conduct regular chlorination of the water in the storage tanks to maintain free residual chlorine of at least 0.2mg/l-0.5mg/l (In cholera hotspots) IMC will ensure FRC is maintained at> 0.5mg/l). 3. Establishment and training of 5 WASH committees (each comprising 3 males and 2 females) in the areas targeted with water supply systems. 4. Establishment and training of 60 solid waste management volunteers (male and female) for 6 IDP camps (10 each for Baidoa, Kahda & Galkacyo South districts). 5. Recruit and train 11 community hygiene promoters (both male and female.6. Procure lockable solid waste disposal pins for 6 IDP settlements. 7. Construction of 80 communal latrines in targeted IDP settlements and Jowhar riverine villages. 8. Distribute hygiene kits to vulnerable households including women headed households, elderly, disabled and new arrival IDPs.9. Rehabilitation of 6 solar powered shallow wells (in Baidoa & Jowhar 3 each.10. Rehabilitate 8 Rain-fed retention structures (Berkards)IMC has been working with communities to implement both hardware and software WASH interventions across Somalia for more than two decades during conditions of drought and flooding among other humanitarian needs. IMC has technical and operational capacity to undertake the activities listed in the humanitarian needs overview. Apart from the in-country technical team there are HQ technical specialists that backstop the work of the field teams. Activities are closely monitored and that any deviations are promptly addressed. IMC employs participatory approach to accountability to the affected population through WASH committees and community outreach sessions. Information gathered from these interactions is fed back into project design to ensure community ownership at all levels.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: GBVLikelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures: - Ensure latrines are lockable from the inside.- Ensure gender segregated latrines form men and women.- Locate WASH infrastructure within safe and visible access of the community.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk:Likelihood: High - Impact: HighMitigation measures:1. IMC program staff are trained on gender equity considerations and these are mainstreamed at all phases of the project cycle.2. Ensure project data generated is disaggregated by sex and age in order to provide the necessary gender and age specific information needed for future program improvement as well as day to day impact. - Ensure gender segregated latrines form men and women (marked in pictoral form). Women's latrines will be equipped with menstrual hygiene management facilities.- Incorporate gender sensitivity into community messaging to ensure important hygiene messages are disseminated to the women who are shy to attend sessions organized by men. - Ensure gender balanced composition of community structures such as WASH committees and Solid Waste Management committees.- Coordinate with Protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities (eg provision of solar powered torches).
