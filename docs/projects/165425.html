Project Summary

SECD during this program in the northwest of Syria aim increasing resilience and affected
people’s access to basic Education Based on the recent assessment conducted, the project
defines the priority areas and priority schools, maintaining the following two main
objectives: enhancing the quality of education services and scaling up equitable access
to education and WASH services; within the proposed project SECD will support 20
schools at Idleb and Aleppo governorate , about 15,412 Child (7239 girls and 7861
boys)and 319 teachers will receive education assistance. SECD will conduct the
following activities during the program:
- Provide children and youth with formal education
- Provide children and youth with non-formal education programs.
- Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns.
- Establish, expand and rehabilitate new classrooms.
- Rehabilitate, improve or construct gender-sensitive WASH facilities
- Provide students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks), school bags and
school in a Carton kit in formal and nonformal settings
- Provide professional development to teaching staff (child-centered and protective
pedagogy, active learning, self-learning, life-skills, Curriculum B, ECCE, New Curriculum,
psychosocial support and referral mechanisms.
- Provide teachers and education personnel with incentives
- Provide children with school-based psychosocial support programs in formal and
nonformal settings
- Provide children with life skills and citizenship education programs in formal and
nonformal settings
- Provide children with supplementary learning materials for children attending formal
and non-formal education

4 - Needs

Regarding OCHA reports: Violence in northwest Syria continues unabated since November 2019 in southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates impacting civilians and impeding the delivery of assistance in many areas. As the fighting continues, the humanitarian situation further deteriorates reaching unprecedented levels of needs;  The escalation of hostilities from 1 December 2019 to 31 January 2020 resulted in the displacement of nearly 520, 000 people mainly from southern Idleb and western Aleppo governorates, 80% of displaced are women and children. Many of the people displaced have been displaced several times, intensifying existing vulnerabilities. More people have moved in the interim period; Heavy aerial bombardment and artillery shelling has been affecting civilian infrastructure, including IDP camps, schools, health centers, and hospitals. Winter conditions are compounding the vulnerability of displaced people. New displaced people are in urgent needs of humanitarian support, particularly shelter, food, health, non-food items, wash, and winterization assistance;According to Syria HRP 2019, The crisis has forced an estimated 2.1 million – more than one-third of Syria’s children –out of school. A further 1.3 million children are at risk of dropping out. More than 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestinian refugee children) and 120,000 teachers need education assistance inside Syria, of whom 61 percent are in acute and immediate need.According: HNAP (October 2019). Disability: Prevalence and Impact, Over 150,000 school-aged children are in need of education services, and approximately an additional $30 , million is required to fund these services; it is important to note that when school-aged persons have a disability, 82 percent of school-aged persons, (aged 12-23) with disabilities such as mobility and self-care difficulties are not attending school at all1

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat (1) : Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, a direct attack on the teacher or student, intimidation of school staff/students or military use of the school. Mitigating Measures: Learning facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA. De-confliction does not guarantee that a learning facility will not be attacked. An organization should undertake an informed process before it decides to de-conflict or not de-conflict. Please contact your Coordinator or OCHA if you need help in this process. All attacks should be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. The Education Sector, Cluster and Working Group should use this information to advocate for safety education. Please contact your coordinator if you need more information or need help in this process. Preparedness training such as self-care, first aid, fire drills, evacuation drills, and stay-and-shelter drills should be regularly practiced. These processes and procedures should be appropriate for the age of the involved students. Where relevant, students and education personnel should be trained on unexploded ordinance risk awareness. Students, their caregivers, teachers, and other education personnel should be provided psychosocial support services. Those services should have the capacity, ability, and resources to address basic needs. For needs that are beyond what should be reasonably expected from learning services, there should be a referral system to specialized protection services. If the organization is not clear what to do it should contact the Child Protection Sub-Cluster for help.

Thefollowing table shows SECD's risk management plan for the project: Risk 1: Outbreak ofconflict prevents access to project locations. Likelihood of Risk Occurring: low. Effect onExpected Results: Very High-Risk Management Strategy: Experience from 2013operations found that while conflict occurred, access could be restored within a few days.A cautious approach that plans for these type of delays is expressed in the project workplan. Risk 2: Armed groups ban specific types of activities such as information-sharing.Likelihood of Risk Occurring: Low Effect on Expected Results: Very High-RiskManagement Strategy: In 2014, ISIS banned NGOs from activities related to education,information messages, and any activities with children. As such, the proposed projectlocations are in non-ISIS controlled areas. Thus far, the armed groups in the project targetlocations have not expressed an issue with these type of interventions; SECD willcontinue to, liaise with these groups’ representatives through the NGO Forum and localpartners. The project design considers this limit to be a possibility and is as suchdesigned with built-in alternatives to direct engagement or messaging so that projectresults may still be achieved. Risk 3: Airplane shelling Likelihood of Risk Occurring:medium Effect on Expected Results: Very High Risk Management Strategy: The risk ofSecurity situation (only heavy shelling) and the risk of the local council buildings beingtargeted by airstrikes are a very low probability according to the historical information for1 year ago, also SECD will be linked with the de-conflict mechanism which provided byOCHA
