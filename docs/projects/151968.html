Project Summary

As a result of the crisis in Libya, many public health facilities providing reproductive health (RH) care services, including emergency obstetric care are either damaged, difficult to access or suffer from severe shortages in material and human resources leading to compromised access to lifesaving care and consequently increased maternal mortality. Maternal Mortality ratio reached up to 90 deaths per 100,000 live births in some hospitals in Libya.
The overall objective of this intervention is to avert excess Maternal and Neonatal Mortality through implementation of Minimum Initial Services Package (MISP) for Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) in crisis.
The intervention will focus on providing lifesaving care for women and newborns throughout the continuum of care from community to primary healthcare facilities and to hospitals for all people in target mantiqas (Tripoli, Benghazi, Sirt, Al Jufrah, Ghat, Sabha, Ejdabia, Kufrah, Murzuq and Ubari).
Major activities are: deployment of Emergency Medical Teams (EMT) in facilities and through mobile clinics, provision of essential lifesaving medicines, Emergency Reproductive Health Kits and medical equipment, health education on danger signs during pregnancy, delivery and post-delivery care, including safe referral to facilities, support the establishment of a maternal and newborn death surveillance and response (MNDSR) system and effective reproductive health coordination. With significant investment in supporting the capacity of health system and practitioners, this projects offers and opportunity to support system resilience and sustainability.

Needs

As a result of the crisis in Libya, many public health facilities providing reproductive health (RH) care services, including emergency obstetric care are either damaged or difficult to access. In addition, according to WHO SARA (2017), out of the 1,151 health facilities, only 69 (6%) are providing delivery services, amongst which only 20% of the PHCs meet the basic readiness criteria for BEmONC. The MoH has requested UNFPA support to maintain Reproductive Health service provision, ensure quality of services through training existing human resources and providing essential, lifesaving medicines and medical equipment. Lack of coordination at national and sub-national levels on RH issues led to several overlapping and gaps which resulted in increased maternal deaths during the last months. This is particularly important for migrants and refugees as they fall often under human trafficking and smuggling networks which prevent them from access to basic services. In the same context, reproductive health services continue to face alarming accessibility, availability and quality challenges. Only 12% of health facilities in the South provide antenatal care with total absence of trained staff. As a result of the crisis, many public health facilities providing reproductive health (RH) care services, including emergency obstetric care are either damaged or difficult to access. The barriers women experience in accessing RH services include lack of information about where and when to seek care, unavailability of transport to health facilities, and high cost of services, where available and accessible; all of which jeopardize timely interventions, particularly for safe delivery. In addition, obstetric care services are limited to 8,5% of the health facilities in the South. Sabha Medical Center (SMC) for example, remains the only secondary health facility with neonatal care that offers specialized delivery services to all people in the South of Libya. Without continuous support, the already overwhelmed system of care has produced a remarkable increase in maternal mortality ratio reaching up to 90 deaths per 100,000 live births in some UNFPA project locations.

Activities

1- Establish, train and deploy 5 Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC) Emergency medical teams to existing secondary health facilities where maternal and newborn health staff is in severe shortage (Emergency, EmONC teams consist of a gynecologist, anesthesiology, scrub nurse and midwife). UNFPA will deploy 5 Emergency Medical mobile teams in referral hospitals in Al Jufrah, Ghat, Sabha, Kufrah and Ubari. In addition to the role of teams o provide services, they will offer capacity building to local staff enhancing their capacity in the long term. 2- Provision of essential lifesaving medicines, Emergency Reproductive Health Kits and medical equipment to 10 hospitals and 10 primary healthcare facilities in order to mitigate the long lasting shortages in supplies and ensure functionality and safety of medical equipment. 3- Contribute to the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) activation through: predisposition and distribution of urgent and most important emergency supplies to lifesaving medicine including RH kits and MAMA kits for newly displaced families, in hard-to-reach areas or for those stranded near the front lines. 4- Danger-sign oriented health education and awareness raising at community level: through community health volunteers, young people (Including Y-PEER and Scouts) and midwives, awareness raising will focus on key messages related to identification of danger signs during pregnancy, delivery, and post-delivery care, including planning for safe birth at referral facilities. 5- Operationalize EmONC guidelines and Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) protocols with focus on management of obstetric and newborn complications, basic and advanced life support. 6- Establish and operate a maternal and newborn death surveillance and response system (MNDSR) as a contribution to achieving third HRP objective (disease surveillance). 7- Scale up Reproductive Health coordination in emergencies, through strengthening the emergency RH sub working group. 8- Integrating GBV medical case management into basic package of health services. With significant investment provided under this intervention to support material and human resources in health system, provision of equipment, capacity building and training and development of MNDSR system will increase system resilience and is linked to a long-term and sustainable vision. As migrants are hard to reach, and in order to avoid paraller system of care for migrants, UNFA will build on its coordination and joint work with organizations on the ground such as IOM, MSF, IMC, LRC and others to ensure aspects of SRH are covered for the migrant population within and outside detention centers.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

While ensuring access of women to basic SRH services across the continuum of care, special measures are put in place to measure access, quality and survival of women and their infants, in a safe and dignified environment. UNFPA has a zero tolerance policy on PSEA. Its mandatory for newly recruited staff to receive the PSEA training prior to deployment. In addition, UNFPA ensures that implementing partners staff are trained on PSEA and supported to establish and sustain internal procedures for reporting and investigations. At the interagency level, UNFPA is leading the roll out of SEA prevention in Libya including the establishment of the in-country PSEA network and community based complaints mechanism.
