Project Summary

Our Education in Emergencies (EiE) Programme's ambition is to increase equitable access to culturally and age appropriate care, learning and development through early childhood education and parenting. Plan International also aims to provide primary education to children aged 3-14 and work with out-of-school and drop out children (boys and girls) to increase their access to safe protective and inclusive educational opportunities. We also work towards building and strengthening the capacity of teachers and other education personnel for improved quality education for all children.
Plan International Bangladesh is implementing a one-year education in emergency response in support of the Rohingya refugees’ education program through compendium of community-based and mobile approaches in reaching the target beneficiaries in Cox’s Bazar refugee camps.
Plan International will continue to implement EiE activities based on ambitious and locally contextualized innovative approaches that have been able to witness increased enrolment of children into learning centres. To date, 13,520 refugee and host community children of age 3-17 years as well as 500 Youth and Adolescent targeted for support. 50% of the beneficiaries reached are female. This coming year we plan to expand our programming to provide increased access and opportunity for children in the highest-need camps and surrounding host community.
Our innovative approaches are a combination of home-based and community-based learning centers. We will rollout three models for basic education (ages 7-14) and a model of home-based pre-primary (ages 3-6). These have been successfully piloted during the 2018 year and as part of the previous 2018 Joint Response Plan and will build on this success and lessons learnt to provide better access and better quality education to a target of 31836 refugee and host community children and adolescents.
Each learning center is clustered into learning communities which have a focal point and an Alternative Learning School Management Committee to help oversee, monitor and provide feedback on the learning centers and the project at large.

Needs

Despite progress made over the past year in providing immediate access to learning opportunities for Rohingya refugees, there is still a significant number of children and youth not accessing any education, including over 39 percent of children 3-14 years and 97 percent of adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years. Over 2,000 learning facilities are needed to meet the existing needs of pre-primary and primary aged children 3-14 (Sector Gap Analysis/UNHCR MSNA). Over 26 camps (out of 34) have less than half of the learning facilities required to cover out-of-school children. Limited space in the camps and the need to provide for more urgent life-saving provisions such as health and WASH, contributed to the insufficient allocation of spaces for learning facilities. Twenty percent of the refugee and host community population are adolescents and youth between 15 and 24.The provision of learning and skills opportunities for adolescents and youth requires urgent focus in 2019. To date, 97 percent of refugee adolescents and youth do not have access to any learning opportunities. This absence of positive engagement activities increases the risk of early marriage for adolescent girls, human trafficking, child labor for adolescent boys, delinquency of male youth, and adoption or engagement in other negative coping practices. Socio-cultural beliefs and practices, along with protection concerns have impacted on participation, in particular for female adolescents. Forty percent of parents of adolescent girls and 33% of parents of adolescent boys reported that education is not appropriate for their children, revealing a gap in community sensitization on education and child and youth rights. This is partly linked to social norms that restrict mobility for girls after the onset of puberty. Perceived safety threats in learning facilities is a concern, particularly for young learners aged 6-14 years (32% and 25% for girls and boys, respectively) more so than for ages 15-18 years (32% and 18% for girls and boys, respectively). The recent refugee influx has increased fragility in the district of Cox’s Bazar. Even before the refugee influx, Cox’s Bazar was one of the lowest performing districts in Bangladesh against many of its education indicators. Cox’s Bazar net intake rate for the first grade of primary school is the lowest in the country at 72.6% for boys and 69.1% for girls, compared to the national average of 98%. Similarly, the dropout rate in primary education is the highest in the country—39.6% for boys and 22.8% for girls, compared to the national average of 22.3% and 16.1% respectively. Host community school infrastructure is underprepared for environmental risks including monsoons and cyclones, and is urgently in need of upgrading. The influx has increased the cost of living, contributing to students dropping out of primary and secondary school to work. Bangladeshi para-teachers have left host community schools for higher paid work in the learning facilities, increasing student-teacher ratios in the schools, and leading to tensions over the impacts of the Rohingya influx (USAID Rapid Education and Risk Analysis, preliminary results, Oct 2018)

Activities or outputs

Plan and Selected Partner will provide the following outputs: Output 1: Immediate access to equitable, inclusive learning opportunities in a safe and protective environment for crisis affected refugee and host community (HC) children and youth (3-24 years) is increased: 1.1 Delivery of centre based /mobile learning programme for children aged 7-14.1.2 Delivery of Early Childhood Development package, Shishu Bikash Kendra (SBK) for children aged 3-6 including parenting (camp and HC).1.3 Delivery of Outreach tutor/Big Brother/Big Sister Mentorship (BB/BS) programme for children aged 7-14. 1.4 Delivery of life skills literacy and numeracy for adolescents/youth (camp and HC).1.5 Distribution of teaching and learning material including mobile learning kits, adolescent youth mobilization kits, in kind support to SBK homes and Centres (to ensure safety and security of children). 1.6.1 Establishment of Alternative Learning School Management Committees (ALSMC), stakeholder meetings, extra-curricular activities for children and youth, roll out of parenting sessions and delivery of training/s for ALSMC.1.7 Ensuring incentives for all facilitators for continued service.1.8 Ensuring salaries and benefits for all staff.Output 2: Improved quality of teaching and learning for refugee and host community children and youth, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunities.2.1. Review of Education in Emergencies (EIE) package (Review of training and learning material, development of new materials) and delivery of trainings (refresher) for Outreach tutors, BB/BS mentors, Adolescent/Youth facilitators, Centre facilitators and SBK facilitators working in refugee sites.2.2 Delivery of Training of Trainers (TOT) for Plan International Staff (Technical Officers and Coordinators).2.3 Delivery of training/s for HC mentors.2.4 Impact study on teachers’ capacity and students learning outcomes in alignment with the Education Sector’s Learning Competencies Framework Approach (LCFA) Core Competencies.2.5 Delivery of Disaster Risk Management (DRM) and safe schools intiatives to students, teachers, parents. (camp and HC). 2.6 Train Technical Officers, teachers, mentors on working with children with disabilities and other vulnerabilities.Output 3: Refugee and host community participation and engagement in the education of children and youth is increased.3.1 Training and supporting of Community Education Focal Points who will promote positive messages to local religious leaders, Majis and other community leaders, who hold influence over communities to access non-Madrassa education.3.2 Forming and orientation of Alternative Learning School Management Committees (ALSMC) in each learning community to closely monitor all Alternative Learning activities happening within the community. 3.3 Hosting of community outreach activities managed and budgeted by ALSMC and the Education Community Mobilizers and will include sports, adolescent mobilisation, drama and literacy events. 3.4 Targetting of children with disabilities and other children with vulnerabilities for enrolment and accomodation. Additional Activities: Plan will continue its active and regular participation in Education Sector meetings and working groups, including intersectoral Youth Working Group and Child Protection Sub-Sector. Plan’s programming will continue to link to LCFA guidance, sector materials and training opportunities. Plan’s camp-based learning programming will be supported by Plan Child Protection Case Management Workers to help identify and refer children as needed.Plan staff will coordinate and communicate as per the standards with relevant government officials, such as the Office of Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commission (RRRC), Camp in Charge officials (CiCs) and other agencies.Plan will provide associated Water, Sanitation and Hygeine (WASH) and hygiene support to students and facilities.
