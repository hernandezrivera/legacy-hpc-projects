Project Summary

Years of armed conflict in Somalia has still resulted in widespread contamination of explosive ordnance including explosive remnants of war (ERW), landmines, improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and the proliferation of small arms and light weapons which continue to injure and kill civilians. The presence of unexploded ordnance - does not only endanger the lives of the population, have a negative impact on peacebuilding and state building activities, but they also provide a readily available source of explosives to manufacture IEDs, which aggravates the threat to communities and are the weapon of choice employed by armed opposition groups against security forces and affect civilian population collaterally constituting more than fifty percent of resulting casualties. Within the last decade, 1,380 entries of civilians injured and killed by Explosive Ordnance were registered. The incidents resulted in 21% fatalities in urban areas and 32% fatalities in rural areas. Many of those injured in explosive ordnance accidents succumb due to the time it takes to reach a medical facility where the catastrophic bleeding could be stopped, and the victim stabilized. The data above indicates a difference of 11% fatalities in urban and rural areas. At a webinar during the 24th International Meeting of Mine Action National Directors and United Nations Advisers (UN-NMD), it was mentioned that by increasing basic medical knowledge, the fatalities can decline by 40%, which could reduce the fatality from 21% fatalities in urban areas to 12.6% and 32% fatalities in rural areas to 19.2%, which is significant.
This proposed project intends to provide community based Improvised First Aid Training (IFAT) to midwives, traditional healers, and voluntary community health workers in hard-to-reach areas which are lacking medical facilities and IDPs who originated from areas where conflict is still ongoing and unexploded ordnance likely to be found in their original settlements upon return.
