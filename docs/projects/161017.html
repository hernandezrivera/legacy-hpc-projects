Project Summary

Since the situation in South Sudan as created a high demand of health care service, this project is aiming at enhancing access and strengthening the provision of emergency lifesaving health care services in Cueibet, Jur River, Mayendit and Leer counties through the provision of 2PHCCs, 2PHCUs and 4mobile clinic response. Although the current security situation is relatively calmed in Tonj north, there are still pockets of IDPs in Cueibet county that were displaced from Tonj north due to inter-clan fighting and that is still raising the need of health care services in the county. Despite the ongoing health care response in Jur River county, there is still a wider gap in health service delivery because HPF3 is only supporting 31 health facilities leaving out 20 HFs. The communities that were served by the 20 HFs are now moving to seek health care from those payams that have functional health facilities. Given the fact that security situation is currently stabled in Leer and Mayendit counties, so many returnees are reported coming back from Sudan and IDPs camps in Bentiu and Juba on daily basis and this has created a high demands on health care service in the two counties. This project proposal will be targeting a total of 81,024 beneficiaries of which 20,422 individuals in Jur River, 25,552 in Cueibet, 11,886 in Leer and 23,164 in Mayendit counties respectively. This project will benefit all gender groups including, 31,761 men, 33,059 women, 7,940 boys and 8,264 girls across the four project catchment Counties. Projections from IPC of August 2019 to April 2020 as estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 1.7 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 10,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). The Aug 2019 to April 2020 IPC projection has shown Cueibet and Jur River falling under IPC Phase3 or worse acute food insecurity. In Leer, Medair conducted a SMART survey in April 2019 with GAM rate of 14.3 %( 10.8 - 18.6 95% C.I.) While SAM stood at 3.2 %( 1.8 - 5.5 95% C.I.). The Aug 2019-April 2020 IPC projection shows that the food security situation remains a concern in the four counties of Mayendit, Guit, Koch and Panyijiar due to persisted pockets of insecurity, cattle raiding, high food prices, flood related destruction of crops and disruption to market access. The prevalence of major illnesses reported among the surveyed children include: fever (49 percent), Cough/ARI (26 percent) and Diarrhea (22 percent). The prevalence of fever/malaria in all ten former States is consistently high. The provision of this primary health care service shall include OPD curative consultations and IPD services at the 2PHCCs of Rocrocdong and Leer TPA, 2PHCUs of Dablual and Duony in Mayendit and Cueibet counties and 4mobile clinics of Pandiet in Cueibet, Dhorlek in Mayendit, Payak in Leer and Rocrocdong in Jur River counties. We will also support the psycho social and basic mental health services through case identification, first aid counseling, treatment and referral of the clients to nearby health county or state hospitals.

1 - Needs

There are a big gaps in health care services across the four project targeted counties of Cueibet, Jur River, Mayendit and Leer. Twenty health facilities are not functional in Jur River county because no partner is supporting them; this has also created a high demand and causing a big congestion of patients at the few functioning health facilities in the county, making the drugs and other medical supplies insufficient. As a result of security stability in the Country, so many IDPs and refugees are returning back home, especially in Mayendit and Leer counties, raising the demands of health care services in the areas. Displacement and destruction of livelihoods due to floods in Leer and Mayendit has been reported to have created a negative impact health care services through cutting some communities from accessing health facilities, causing more water born diseases especially Malaria, AWD and skin infections. A pocket of IDPs from Tonj north are still living in Cueibet county due to fear of inter-clan fighting in Tonj north county, their presence in the area has significant increased the demand of health care services in Cueibet county.Most health facilities needs a major renovation because the buildings were partly or completely destroyed during the conflict and those that were grass thatched tukuls were also burned down to ashes. Hence there are no adequate spaces in all HFs for provision of health care services. 2 - Activities

1. UNIDOR will improve access and strengthening the emergency lifesaving response by establishing 4 Mobile clinics, Support 2 PHCUs and 2 PHCCs. The 2PHCCs will provide normal clinical packages and at the same time support referrals of SGBV survivors, CEmONC or surgical emergency cases to county or state hospitals. 2. More than 52,332 (20,514 A5 male, 21,352 A5 Female, 5,128 U5 male, 5,338 U5 female) will benefit from OPD curative consultations.3. UNIDOR will target 450 pregnant mothers for safe deliveries at health facility by SBA. 4. CHWs will be trained on IMNCI, Infection prevention and control, CMR and Quality Of Care (QOC). 5. About 3,200 males under 1 year and 4,300 females under 1 year will be targeted for routine EPI service. UNIDOR will strive to reach 75% coverage with Penta3 in Jur River and Leer Counties.6. UNIDOR will target 20,552 (M,10,070 and F, 10,482) above 6 years and adults to be reached with health education awareness sessions on hand washing with clean water, safe waste disposal, timely health seeking behavior, 6 months exclusive breast feeding, benefits of routine vaccination vaccinations, environmental and personal hygiene etc) during outreach and in the health facilities. 7. Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases and enhance weekly IDSR and EWARS reporting. 8. Prepare to respond to epidemic outbreaks (Malaria, Measles, Meningitis and Cholera) integrate WASH in the 2PHCUs and 2PHCCs supported.9. Improve resilience among vulnerable populations including disabled groups and mentally ill persons through psycho-social support services.10. Integrated response for health/Wash/Nutrition/ food security and protection including Management of SAM with medical complications 11. Fora for information sharing on services gaps and achievements for planning and accountability to affected population12. Quarterly joint assessments and monitoring of services through the RRM team.13. Advocating for development emergency funding readiness to support unforeseen emergencies through other development donors like DFID/HPF.
