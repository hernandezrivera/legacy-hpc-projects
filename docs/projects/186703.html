Project Summary

The proposed project for 2022 will contribute to the WASH sector strategy. Project will be achieved by increasing sustainable use of gender sensitive emergency WASH services such as water supply and sanitation and hygiene promotion sessions, and ensuring that conflict affected populations receive immediate assistance and operation and maintainace support. The project will provide WASH assistance at IDP in camps and community level as well as in health facilities and schools. It will enable male, female, boys and girls to have improved access to safe drinking water in secure places, enhance their knowledge on WASH practices, prevent COVID 19, reduce prevalence of diarrhea and ultimately also lead towards improved nutrition security. This will be achieved through a combination of interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of water points, Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), formation and training of WASH COMs with 40-50% female and building capacity of government authorities to effectively provide lifesaving assistance and have improved capacity to meet the immediate needs of newly displaced or returning populations. Throughout the course of the project, the active participation of women in the design, implementation and sustainability of interventions will be ensured and protection needs of women and girls will be identified and integrated into programming.

1 - Needs

in 2021, Nigeria is faced the worst outbreak of cholera in the past decade. According to Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) sitrep (week 47), a total of 106,348 cholera cases are reported with the death of 3579 individuals and case fatality rate (CFR) of 3.4 %6. Children aged 5-14 years are the most affected age group, while males and females are affected equally (50%). The rainy season (June to Sept 2021) has exacerbated the risk of cholera, and other water-related diseases, while Flash floods in Borno and Yobe State left 25,000 people at-risk of food shortages. Access to safe water has been constrained as a result of new and protracted displacement and returnees movements. Host communities are registering a reduced resilience due to high pressure on the existing and already overburdened water points and sanitation facilities. In BAY states, a colossal of 1.02 M people receive less than 15 liters per person per day while about 1.06 M people have intermittent water access, with over 500 meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queuing time at water points8. Furthermore, access to hygiene and sanitation for an estimated 2.8 million is hindered as the households have no latrine and practice open defecation or share a single latrine with more than 20 others, with limited access to handwashing with soap and water and no personal hygiene materials. The fecal sludge management in IDP camps is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desludging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for the yearly outbreak of cholera. Open defecation and flash floods are further contaminating the water sources.

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved access to safe drinking water supply for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno states.Mode of implementation: In close coordination with the Government and the WASH sector-working group, the response will be deployed, integrating protection concerns and adopting a gender sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) that prioritizes first distribution to vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality testing including free residual chlorine will be tested for all water supply to ensure the quality of the water. The IDP camps will be supported with operation and maintenance of water points to ensure continues safe water supply. Where every possible WASHCOM will be formed and trained with 40-50% of female in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation. Activity 02: Improved access to sanitation service for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Mode of Implementation:Multi-unit communal gender separated latrines will be constructed in IDP camps in safe places with light to ensure the safe access. Male and female Hygiene and sanitation volunteers will be trained and supported to monitor the cleaning and maintainace of latrines. Solid waste management will be conducted through distribution of waste bins, trainings and coordinating with BOSEPA for waste collection. In communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on household latrines. Following a transect walk focused on the negative aspects of open defecation, the Community Lead Total Sanitation approach will be launched. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their own resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that pour flush/VIP latrines for health facilities and schools are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child-friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved access to hygiene promotion and gender sensitive hygiene kits for affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno stateMode of Implementation:The interventions are targeting hard to access areas with new arrivals in locations with limited coverage of critical lifesaving WASH services, emergency hygiene and sanitation sessions conducted to demonstrate measures such a handwashing, good hygiene practices and the safe disposal of waste, with the aim to mitigate against the spread of water borne disease. An experienced consultant and trained ACF staff will undertake a study on the barriers communities face to carry out hygiene practices. This will inform the selection of messages highlighted in the Behavioural Change Communication strategy that will be launched in partnership with the Rural Water and Sanitation Management Agency (RUWASA). Handwashing stations will be established next to constructed latrines and utilized during emergency hygiene sessions covering personal hygiene, domestic hygiene, the proper use of hygiene kits and WASH infrastructure. The project will also commemorate world days of significance

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The safety of beneficiaries will be prioritised in the delivery of WASH infrastructure such as water points, latrines and showers. With open defecation being practiced of both women and men in target area. Following an environmental analysis, AAH will prioritise the construction of latrines that are proximate to households and with adequate lighting. As 87% of women and girls are partially or totally responsible for collecting water, water points will be located in places convenient for women and girls. AAH will work to increase women’s voice and leadership by working with the state and community structures. We would ensure women are active participants in project decision-making and monitoring by conducting household surveys.
