Project Summary

In the North-West, South-West regions, IOM plans interventions for 2022 to respond to the most urgent needs of displaced populations through a multisectoral approach. The last Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) exercise conducted in West and Littoral regions that the North-West (NW)-South-West (SW) crisis-related displacements and related needs are spilling over to these neighbouring regions, IOM plans to expand its programmes accordingly.
IOM Cameroon will also mainstream protection and recovery through the development of local capacities, in all its proposed activities to support displaced populations in preparing and achieving durable solutions.
IOM’s multisectoral response in the NW and SW regions will be composed of the following activities:
Coordination Sector
IOM plans to rely on its existing network of enumerators and capacity to:
- Conduct regular MNSAs in NW, SW, Littoral and West regions in close coordination with humanitarian clusters and OCHA to collect information on the key sectoral needs.
- Produce and distribute regular sudden displacements alerts through its emergency tracking tool (ETT) dashboards, that present reliable and up-to-date information on the size, trends and triggers of displacements, needs and living conditions, to be shared with humanitarian actors including the RRM and OCHA.
- Collect information on livelihoods and stability, through the roll out of a tailored conflict tracker index for the NW and SW regions in order to help facilitate geographic targeting and strategic planning for HDPN interventions.
Shelter/ Non-Food Item (NFI) sector
IOM plans to work closely with the shelter/NFI cluster and coordinate its interventions with the RRM to provide a range of different shelter solutions. Kits will be designed in line with cluster guidelines, and undergo periodic review using beneficiary feedback and complain mechanisms, including:
- Distribution of 1,000 emergency shelter kits to populations who have been recently displaced and are in need of quick assistance to meet their basic needs.
- Distribution of 500 transitional shelter kits to displaced populations that have settled in camp-like informal settings, notably in rural areas, as well as technical assistance to community members for their construction.
- Cash-based modalities to respond to the needs of displaced populations (1,000 households) who are based in urban and peri-urban areas that are renting shelters with a financial service provider based on the findings of a rental market baseline assessment and market assessment, in coordination with the Cash working group.
- Distribution of 1,500 NFI kits including basic household and cooking items, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) items including soap, sanitary towels, and water purification tablets.
Health Cluster
Through Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services, IOM aims to bolster the well-being of crisis-affected populations using people-centred and culturally sensitive approaches, in coordination with the MHPSS working group for NWSW. Actions will be tailored to the age and gender of beneficiaries, in line with the IOM Manual on Community-based MHPSS and IASC Guidelines, including:
- Needs assessment to understand the key challenges, needs and opportunities for MHPSS services in crisis affected areas.
- Capacity building for existing MHPSS structures, including local authorities, civil society and NGOs, traditional leaders and women and youth groups, including on Psychological First Aid (PFA), communication skills, stress management skills, or (lay) counselling.
- The strengthening or establishing of safe spaces, local structures and committees by promoting communities’ ownership and active participation.
- Provide direct MHPSS interventions through the deployment of psychosocial mobile teams (PMT), providing counselling and sensitization to vulnerable groups facing protection concerns such as GBV.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Humanitarian needs in Cameroon remain high. In the North-West, South-West regions, the socio-political crisis that erupted in 2017 after peaceful protests by lawyers and teachers against a perceived encroachment of the regions’ cultural practices and autonomy turned into an armed conflict between the government defense forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). The conflict has since continued to trigger very dynamic and massive internal displacements and related humanitarian needs. Throughout the year 2021 the landscape of NSAGs has become increasingly fragmented following a further breakdown between the diaspora and the NSAGs on the ground, blurring command and control structures and making humanitarian access more complex. This and the sustained rise in the use of improvised explosive devices points towards the increasingly protracted nature of the conflict, with unaddressed grievances of local populations in NWSW, the continued trafficking of small arms and light weapons, and low amounts of socioeconomic opportunities for youth who are also vulnerable to exploitation and recruitment by armed groups. An estimated 166,255 displaced populations are presently displaced to the neighboring regions of West and Littoral due to the spillover of the conflict (MSNA September 2021). Since the beginning of the year 2021, IOM’s ETT has identified more than 80 new forced displacements and around 20,000 return movements within the NWSW. IOM’s ETT dashboards also highlight the increasing trends of recurring short-term displacements in NWSW, or ‘pendular displacements’ characterized by individuals seeking temporary refuge in makeshift shelters or agricultural structures in the surrounding bushes. Priority needs, as indicated through Multi-Sector Needs Assessments include Food, WASH, Protection, Shelter and NFI. Assessments in the NWSW region, such as previous MSNA findings and shelter assessments, also recognize the rising number of urban and peri-urban displaced populations and the identification of cash as the preferred modality of assistance, as these persons have more access to functional. Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility of the NWSW crisis at an international level. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need in the NW, SW, Littoral and West for the reinforcement of response capacity, including a Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the North-West, South-West regions (August 2021) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors. For the Shelter/NFI sector for example, 52 per cent of internally displaced persons (IDPs) reported living in self-settled, makeshift shelters or rented homes, with the majority of makeshift shelters being constructed using fewer durable materials such as tarpaulin (55%), wood (55%), and straw (54%). Key difficulties reported in terms of access to NFIs included them being too expensive (54%) or difficult to access in the local market (18%). For the health sector, 67 per cent of respondents noted that they had difficulty accessing health services due to health services being too far away, and less as them being too expensive (17%). As a result, displaced populations are to a large extent currently relying on alternative sources for healthcare, including traditional healers (68%), community health workers (49%), pharmacies (26%) and mobile clinics (14%).
