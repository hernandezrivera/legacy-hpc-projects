Project Summary

The project of "Comprehensive Protection Response through provision of psychosocial, mental health, case management and transitional shelter assistance for Female Headed of households (neglected women, wives of mental health patients, addicts and women with cancer), GBV survivor’s victim of violence in the Gaza strip" aims to contribute to mitigate the impact of conflict on the psychosocial, mental health and wellbeing of affected women, men, boys and girls in the Gaza Strip. The main outcomes of the project are: 1) provision of GBV & mental health services for women affected by conflict in Gaza. 2) build the capacities and awareness of duty bearers (a) Men and Women in the community and family of neglected women (b) community leaders and service providers through raising their awareness on GBV, and Women legal rights. The outputs are: 1.1) Mental health services provided for GBV survivors. 1.2) Case management for GBV including transitional sheltering and economic empowerment. 2.1) Improved the copying mechanisms and resilience for the families of targeted women in the Gaza strip. 2.2) The capacities of service providers build on Hawa elzahra and case management.
Aisha will work to achieve these outcomes through various activities starting from coordination with main stakeholder to outreach the affected women, and networking with partner CBOs receive GBV survivors to host the project activities. Then, providing structured psycho-therapy and specialized mental health, integrated security, and empowerment training activities for Female Headed of households (neglected women, wives of mental health patients, addicts), GBV survivor’s victim of violence, and detect the severely affected person in need for advanced psychological, mental health service to Aisha mobile clinics.
On other hand, Aisha and the protection cluster partner will apply the principle of do no harm in all activities especially during the period of providing transitional shelter assistance for six months where it will provide these services as transitional phase until the targeted women will be able to manage their own issues which will be through the case management services that provided to them. As well as, AISHA and the protection cluster partner will adopt the inclusion in this project through targeting different groups of women, harmonize the sessions for targets groups and WWDs.
Moreover, AISHA will apply the previously developed manual Hawwa Zahra(attached), developed according to IASC guidelines, that was revised and mainstreamed among the protection cluster and it sub- working group member. In addition, in 2017 Aisha established Aisha community center for mental health, substance abuse, and GBV that was license from the Ministry of Interior and the Ministry of Health. With UNFPA support Aisha developed the protocol of Aisha community center including 5 parts, management of common mental health disorders, case management for GBV, Case management for substance abuse, and 32 of related NGOs and INGOs and ministries staff trained on this protocol for 20 days. The clinic starts working officially in February 2018 with full time staff (psychiatrist, 2 psychologists, nurse, and others) where during this project, cases in need for specialized mental health services, medication and case management will be referred internally to Aisha Community Center. Also, AISHA has already a referral mechanism in which all referrals and needed information are followed up and traced. It is worth mentioning that AISHA is already a part of the SOPs' manual managed by UNICEF and includes all referral pathways.
It is worth mention that AISHA with the protection cluster partner designed the project in partner with the community through conducting focus groups and data base and will engage them in the whole project cycle through conducting regular follow up for the project implementation, complaint system, field visits, interviews and focus groups, in order to assess the needs of the beneficiaries.
Aisha in partnership with the protection cluster partner will be responsible to provide the psychosocial, mental health, case
management activities, and transitional shelter assistance by provision of rental fees to most vulnerable families in close coordination with shelter cluster

Needs

Due to challenging humanitarian situation in GS, GBV is increasing. OCHA allocation strategy (2017) stated that violence experience can significantly impact the mental health and psychosocial well-being of a person both immediately & over long term. MHPSS is a priority response of protection cluster in the HRP 2018-2020. UN OCHA (2017) in its HNO report presents a number of gender-based vulnerabilities, concerns and associated needs where gender dynamics in the Palestinian society is key in shaping realities, opportunities & status of men, women, boys and girls, and in shaping their experiences during & post conflict, humanitarian situations and coping strategies. Also, the lack of specialized & structured PSS services, social stigma toward women approaching MHPSS services or centers, & the deteriorated economic situation & lack of quality and comprehensive services prevent women and girls from seeking for MHPSS or other fundamental services. Findings from analysis of data base at Aisha and protection cluster partners updated early June 2018, included urgent protection and shelter needs for FHH and WWDs to alleviate their suffering, save their lives and maintain their human dignity. According to MOSA sources (2018), there are 250-300 evicted families who reflected their need for safe, accessible and healthy shelter. There are 576 GBV survivors who need MHPSS, 158 cases who need psycho-therapy and 85 cases who need transitional shelter assistance (TSA). Furthermore, highlights from the FGDs under the ongoing Global Affairs Canada- funded project, WWDs needs psycho-social support, MHPSS, legal services, psycho-therapy, and dignity kits following SOPs including case management. Also, AISHA's services are mostly demanded by the wives of governmental employees (PNA and HAMAS). That is due to the deterioration of humanitarian situation, salaries cut, legal status and individuals with financial liabilities and loans upon employees which get them locked up based on financial debts, makes women looking for income sources to depend on it as they became the breadwinner. The data base clarified there are specific humanitarian needs under the current crisis in Gaza including psychosocial support session, equal access to services, transportation fees to facilitate their access to medication and other services, recreational activities, and financial support where most of them were the breadwinner for their families and now there is no income resource for food security or shelter or basic health services. Aisha with the protection cluster partner conducted 5 focus groups with 86 women, men, boys, & girls aged of 15 to 58, they assured for their needs for the services offered by AISHA & the implemented partner through the integrated work approach & the provision of comprehensive services within them, which is specialized psychological services, economic empowerment and it is for the first time were provided by women's institution take into consideration the sensitivity, the privacy of each case & trusted by the family, and provide treatment and medicines, MHPSS, social, legal, economic and protection services for them and their children. In Focus groups there were a large gap, high and special need were women & men asserted on TSA while they living in poverty. Also, women asserted on this need especially with the absence of the head of the household or the inability to meet their needs. Also, the service providers assured in another focus group on the lack of specialized institutions that provide services in a clear methodology that meets the real needs for families. Accordingly, AISHA with protection cluster partner designed these interventions to respond to above needs where it facilitates & promotes equal access to quality and deep mental health services considering differentiated gender needs, it offers psychosocial, mental health, case management and TSA, dignity kits, referral for specialized services and empowerment for GBV survivors.

Activities

The main activities under protection cluster:1. Provide group psycho-social support sessions for a total of 1000 women including (500) Female Headed households – FHH (neglected women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, women survivor of cancer) and 500 WWDs survivors of GBV (50 groups, each group 12 group sessions).2. Conduct awareness sessions for (500) of the families of neglected women and WWDs on gender, stress management, and women rights (25 group, each group 2 sessions).3. Provide (4) structured legal awareness sessions for a total of 1000 women including (500) FHH (neglected women, wives of mental health patients, addicts, women survivor of cancer) and 500 WWDs survivors of GBV (50 groups)4. Conduct family therapy for (30) family of FHH and WWDs .5. Provide Case management for (50) women sever cases ( out of them 20 WWDs) the case management includes (psycho-social services, legal consultation, social services, and economic empowerment also, Conduct psycho-therapy and medication intervention for a total of 100 women including (50) FHH and (50) WWDs based on women needs.6. Provide dignity kits for (400) FHH (out of them 200 WWDs)7. Refer (150) cases FHH to receive additional legal support services ( out of them 70).8. Conduct (2) ToT training on Hawa elzahra and case management approach with total (60) hours for (2) groups of service providers. 9. Organize (10) open days and recreational activities for a total of (1000) women including (500) FHH and (500) WWDs.The main activities under shelter cluster will be implemented by Palestinian housing council – PHC: Provide transitional shelter assistance for women GBV survivor’s victim of violence, internally displaced, suspended women particularly wives of mental health patient and addicted until to be able to open and manage their own project. (during the period of psycho-social support and vocational training). This will be performed through provision of rental fees for 50 families who are evicted or at risk of eviction and cannot afford paying rental fees for a period of up to 6 months. All selected cases will be cross checked with shelter cluster, Ministry of public works and housing, and Ministry of social development. The fees will be provided in 2 to 3 instalments while after each instalment the team will verify that it is used for the shelter purpose and the humanitarian needs are still there before paying the next instalment. Any other shelter needs will be assessed and referred to shelter cluster using the agreed upon referral system.
