Project Summary

This projects aims to reach 118,711 beneficiaries, targeting 72,4503 children<5yrs and 46,261 above 5yrs in 6 Counties of Juba, Budi, Ikwoto, Lopa-lafon, Baliet and Panyikang. The beneficiaries comprise of children and adults, both male and female inclusive of persons with disability. The proposed target accounts for 26% of nutrition cluster targets for these locations.
Health Link intends to implement varies activities to achieve reduction in malnutrition related morbidity and mortality in South sudan through achievement of nutrition cluster objectives as below:
Sectoral objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2021.
To achieve the above objective, HLSS intends to:
 Prevent micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns.
 Behaviour change communication campaigns through awareness campaigns, radio talk shows, FGD and community engagement meetings.
 Conduct individual MIYCN counselling for pregnant and mothers and caregivers of children
 Train Health &Nutrition workers on the MIYCN integrated prevention package.
 Conduct nutrition screening targeting children under five years as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and strengthen the family MUAC screening approach.
Sectoral objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2024.
 Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months.
 Identification and referral of children with severe acute malnutrition with complications.
 Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months and PLWs.
 Training of health and nutrition and nutrition workers on CMAM and MIYCN with major focusing on case detection and referral for community health workers.
Sectoral objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2024. HLSS will work to attain the above objective through:
 Participation in national nutrition surveys.
 Routine submission of NIS reports on to the national database.
 Routine surveillance and reporting of diseases of epidemiological importance.
 Training of Health and Nutrition workers on Emergency response.
 Support national nutrition surveys in locations of implementation.
Sectoral objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2024.
HLSS intends to implement the following to achieve the above objective:
 Screening and testing for malaria for all children with SAM.
 Training of Health and Nutrition workers on Malaria testing and treatment.
 Support existing facilities to establish kitchen gardens at nutrition sites for demonstration.
 Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities.
 Training of Health and nutrition workers on GBV, case identification and referral.
 Provision of WASH kits to children admitted diagnosed with SAM
 Provision of LLITN for all children admitted with SAM for malaria prevention.
 Training of CNV and Nutrition workers on provision of ECD
 Establishment of ECD activities at static nutrition sites to support child stimulation.
This proposed project is mean to integrate to the existing health, nutrition, and protection project run by HLSS in these locations and activities will be implemented in collaboration with County health officers and SMOH and implementing partners of Health, WASH, Protection, FLS and Education among others in accordance to MOH-SS guidelines.

1. Needs

The Malnutrition situation in South Sudan remains Serious with Overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels at 11.6%, 3.4% points short of the 15% emergency threshold. In Central equatorial, GAM levels have consistently on the rise from 8.2% in 2018, to 9.6% in 2019 and then to 15.3% 2020, 19.0% (FSNMS r24 ,r25, r27, SMART SURVEY –JUBA IDP-CWW) although proxy GAM rates are estimated at 8.9% in Juba (Nutrition Cluster Nov2023). Juba has remained fairly security wise except mangalla Payam facing a complex emergency as vulnerable populations are forced to seek refuge following persistent flooding in Jongole state and later conflict rose between their supposed host communities where the population has thought refuge. In EES, Proxy GAM rates are estimated at 18.4%, 18.9% and 17.5% in Ikwoto, Lopa-lafon and Budi respectively (SMART SURVEY June 2022 by ACF, Nutrition Cluster October 2023), all classified at phase 4-critical malnutrition levels. This area anciently known as a food basket for the country changed following 2013 insecurity as communities fled to neighbouring countries for refuge, this led to reduction of food supplies. Upper Nile State on the other hand has consistently reported critical GAM rates fluctuating between 18.2% in Dec 2018 and 16.4% in Dec 2019 and 17.3% in 2021. (FSNMS Rd 27). GAM rates have been observed rising from 10.5% to 20.5% in Baliet respectively, (FSNMS r24 Dec 2019, FSNMS r25¬ Feb 2020, FSNMS r27). With the wasting levels reported at 17.3 % and stunting levels at 11.42 %. (FSNMS Survey 2021), these statistics are worrisome considering IYCF indicators remain poor with 22.4% and 9.6% Minimum meal frequency and Minimum Acceptable Diet respectively and other underlying factors still continue to prevail. Based on the Consolidated Approach to Reporting Indicators of Food Security, 76% of households across South Sudan were food insecure; contributing factors to this statistical finding includes high levels of food insecurity which are driven by climatic shocks (floods and droughts), local insecurity, persistent annual cereal deficits, diseases and pests, the economic crisis (high prices of food commodities and sustained depreciation of the SSP against the US dollar) that have eroded households’ coping capacities, and the loss of livelihoods (IPC, FSNMS r27). This has suffocated livelihoods in families and children under5 are hit hardest by this catastrophic situation. Similarly, these same situations greatly affected local food production, access to clean water and has triggered high morbidity as evidenced by recurrent measles outbreaks and/or upsurge of infectious diseases with increase in communicable diseases such as Acute Watery Diarrhea, Malaria, hepatitis among others, increasing susceptibility of children to Malnutrition related morbidity and mortality (DHIS-2 2022). HLSS therefore intends to scale up its strategic nutrition interventions in line with the nutrition Cluster strategic Objective by strengthening provision of quality nutrition prevention and treatment services at 51 sites at the functional health facilities with 01 stabilization centres in locations of Ikwoto, lopa-Lafon, Budi, Baliet, panyikang and Juba counties. HLSS will also conduct mobile outreach services in specific target locations of -Ibaahure, Lodomiok and Lohirang in Lopa/Lafon County, Romula, Mohina, Madwany and Ifere in ikwoto county, Ryangnum, Benythiang, Baychan in Baliet County, Pakwa and nyibol in Panyikang and some other selected sites in Budi County. These locations present seasonal difficulty to access nutrition services and beneficiaries walk over 2 hours to the nearest OTP/TSFP site. This proposed Nutrition Project shall strengthen the existing and ongoing Health, Nutrition, Protection and BHI interventions currently supported by HLSS in the locations.
