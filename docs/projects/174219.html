Project Summary

Through this project, NRC intends to contribute to improving the living conditions of the populations most affected by new displacements due to conflicts and natural disasters in the northern and central regions of Mali. Through this project, the NRC aims to provide the most vulnerable people with rapid and life-saving assistance in the non-food goods (NFI) sectors. The project will also provide targeted households with hand washing kits and hygiene awareness on preventive measures to help prevent the spread of Covid-19.
To respond effectively to the urgent needs of affected people, the intervention strategy will be based on the four pillars of emergency intervention, in particular the pre-positioning of human and financial resources, humanitarian watch (alerts and assessment of multisectoral needs), multisectoral response and coordination. This strategy will enable the NRC to be informed and act quickly in the event of a sudden crisis by providing a rapid multisectoral, coordinated and integrated response. To do this, needs will be systematically analyzed based on the results of rapid multisectoral assessments in coordination with other NRC CCs and other humanitarian actors on the ground.
Finally, the selection of beneficiaries will be done with a strong participation of community representatives and based on vulnerability criteria which will be defined by their support. Interventions will be carried out by means of cash transfer (CASH / Voucher) or through a distribution in kind if only the first options are not feasible due to security or protection constraints or linked to the market.

1 - Contexte

The humanitarian context in Mali remains dominated by an escalation of violence, recurring insecurity and the constraints of access for peasant populations to basic social services and forced displacement caused by inter-community conflicts, military operations and clashes between the forces regular support by international and regional forces against radical armed groups and sometimes between different armed groups active in the North and Center of the country as well as natural disasters including mainly floods that destroy fields, agricultural granaries, animals and houses, thus impoverishing populations. All these elements are believed to be at the root of the deterioration in the living conditions of civilian populations, especially in the northern and central regions of the country, the epicenter of conflicts and military operations. Rapid multisectoral assessments carried out by RRM partners in the Center (Mopti and Ségou) and North (Gao, Kidal, Ménaka and Tombouctou) regions of Mali from January to October 2020 estimate the number of newly displaced people at over 245,000, including more than 183,000 received humanitarian aid from RRM partners headed by NRC which assisted more than half (60%). In addition, the reports of the regional directorates of social, economic and solidarity development of the regions on the floods show that the rainy season of this year caused floods which hit several villages in the northern and central regions of the country, this which have exacerbated the already fragile humanitarian situation of thousands of vulnerable people and indicate a total of around 92,000 people (15,000 households) affected and in need of humanitarian assistance, only half of whom were assisted due to lack of humanitarian response capacity , and this despite the existence of the national flood contingency plan. For the year 2021, there are no signs that the situation will improve.
