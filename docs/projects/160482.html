Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to contribute to the reduction in morbidity and mortality resulting from waterborne diseases, by increasing access to safe water, sanitation and better hygiene practices among displaced populations, vulnerable communities, and host communities in South Sudan. Rates of SAM and MAM still remains high in various counties in South Sudan, particularly Renk and Aweil. Additionally, aaccording to RSS EVD contingency plan, the general health situation across South Sudan remains fragile and the ongoing crisis has seriously affected health service delivery. The risk of EVD importation to South Sudan has been assessed to be very high. South Sudan is one of the countries prioritised to enhance operational readiness and preparedness based on the WHO EVD Preparedness Checklist. Water, sanitation and hygiene needs still remain critical for many counties in South Sudan, with almost all assessed counties registering critical need for latrines and safe drinking water, and reports of mortality due to lack of proper WASH. Medair will continue to work in Renk, Leer, Aweil and any other Emergency Response sites, to support interventions aimed at combating malnutrition providing integrated WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level through multiple integrated approaches and activities. Medair will assess existing water points and repair water supply infrastructure as necessary, carry out hygiene and sanitation activities through care group model, and support latrine constructions in emergency and disease outbreak contexts. These activities will contribute to the following WASH cluster strategic objectives. CO1: Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package. CO2: Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level. CO3: Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.

1 - Needs

Malnutrition is a huge risk in various locations: A SMART survey in June 2019 in Renk County revealed GAM and SAM rates above the emergency threshold, GAM prevalence was 32.1% among the host communities and 24.4% among IDPs and 26.9% and 6.4% among the host community, respectively. Preliminary SMART survey results for a SMART survey carried out in Aweil showed GAM prevalence of 19.3%, whereas the GAM rates in Leer County in April 2019 showed a GAM rate of 16,8%. All these point to a strong need for an integrated approach in order to improve the malnutrition rates not only in these locations where Medair is intervening, but also in other areas across the country that have shown high prevalence in malnutrition. According to RSS Cholera prevention and response plan, acute watery diarrhoea remains the second highest cause for morbidity. Cholera is endemic in South Sudan and its neighbouring countries where recurrent outbreaks continue to be reported with both local and cross-border spread. Displaced persons in POCs, refugee camps and other areas with high concentrations of people have a higher risk of suffering cholera outbreaks. According to RSS EVD contingency plan, the general health situation across South Sudan remains fragile and the ongoing crisis has seriously affected health service delivery. The risk of EVD importation to South Sudan has been assessed to be very high and South Sudan is one of the countries prioritised to enhance operational readiness and preparedness based on the WHO EVD Preparedness Checklist. There is currently high risk of cholera outbreak in Upper Nile region, after the recent cases in Blue Nile region in September 2019. Medair is one of few NGOs working in the WASH sector in Renk County and is currently supporting WASH services for an estimated 12,410 people, including 3,490 displaced people living in three sites: Abayok, Payuer and Wunthou. Due to multiple shocks and instances of displacement, the populations of these settlements fluctuate and comprise a mix of IDPs and returnees, although host community members also access the services. These settlements now have a more or less semi-permanent core population yet still absorb transitory populations, including IDPs. Since 2014, conflict in Leer County has left many people displaced and with limited access to WASH services. The majority of the population practices open defecation within all communal areas, including near water sources and homes. Lack of sanitation facilities and safe excreta disposal mechanisms continue to represent a significant risk of disease transmission. According to the Assessment carried out by REACH, in August 2019, water, sanitation and hygiene needs still remain critical for many counties in South Sudan, with almost all assessed counties registering critical need for latrines and safe drinking water, and reports of mortality due to lack of proper WASH. Medair has also been supporting health and nutrition projects with WASH services that benefit both SAM and MAM patients, as well as their communities through provision of safe drinking water and improved sanitation and hygiene behaviours through care group model. Needs for WASH services in Aweil still remain high, with few WASH (INGO/NGO) partners active.The ongoing humanitarian crisis comes on top of pre-existing large-scale humanitarian needs and against a backdrop of some of the worst human development indicators in the world. South Sudan ranked first on the 2017 Fragile States Index (compared to second in 2016) of the Fund for Peace. Although there is hope that security and economic situation on South Sudan will improve after the recent signing of the revitalized peace agreement, the situation still remains precarious and uncertain. Necessity for capacity to respond to disasters such as ongoing conflicts, displacements and returning communities is still high, especially with lifesaving water and sanitation services.

2 - Activities

1. WASH in Nutrition/Nutrition and Health Centres: Medair will continue to work in Renk, Leer, Aweil and any other Emergency Response sites, to support interventions aimed at combating malnutrition. Within the context of extreme food insecurity, it is critical to ensure that households have access to safe drinking water for malnourished children, and proper sanitation and hygiene, which are critical to recovery of malnourished child, especially as this contributes to reduction occurrence and episodes of diarrhoea. Medair will provide safe drinking water or PoUWT (such as PURTM and AquatabsTM) to MAM and SAM patients as well as ways to purify drinking water at nutrition centres for appetite test and general drinking use for patients and staff. Medair will also provide latrines for patients and staff to prevent open defecation and to dispose of faecal matter safely. Provide hand-washing stations to promote hand washing amongst patients and staff. Distribute hygiene kits to SAM and MAM patients and provide hygiene education to caregivers of SAM and MAM patients. Carry out follow-ups on correct use of PoUWT items. Construct/rehabilitate waste pits at Medair health and nutrition facilities; install burning pits / simple drum incinerators or other appropriate combustion facilities at Medair health and nutrition facilities. Train clinic staff on proper use of solid waste management facilities and conduct clean up activity at each site to remove existing debris and utilise proper solid waste management facilities. 2. Integrated WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level:This will be achieved through multiple integrated approaches and activities. Medair will assess existing water points and repair water supply infrastructure. Distribute point of use water filter kits or PURTM/ AquatabsTM alongside relevant training and monitoring. Conduct bacteriological water quality testing of all water sources installed or repaired. Provide training to pump mechanics drawn from the community members. Provide training of proper management and maintenance of water points. Provide safe water supply and appropriate chlorination in nutrition/health facilities, including WASH in Acute Watery Diarrhoea, IPC required by cholera or EVD. Construct/ rehabilitate hand washing stations and in emergencies where latrines are not possible or appropriate, Medair will promote alternative sanitation solutions. Train households on cleaning and maintaining latrines. Conduct hygiene promotion campaigns with relevant IEC materials. Distribute WASH NFIs and/or household water treatment products to IDPs, vulnerable host HHs. Conduct clean-up and bucket washing campaigns whenever relevant, to reinforce hygiene promotion messages. 3. WASH interventions in communities: Medair will assess existing water points and repair water supply infrastructure as necessary, such as hand pumps, motorised boreholes and hand dug wells. Additionally Medair will distribute point of use water filter kits, PURTM/AquatabsTM alongside relevant training and monitoring. Conduct bacteriological water quality testing of all water sources installed or repaired. Provide training to pump mechanics drawn from the community members. Provide training of proper management and maintenance of water points. Construct/ rehabilitate stances of adequate, appropriate sanitation facilities with hand washing stations and in any emergencies where latrines are not possible or appropriate. Promote alternative sanitations solutions. Train households on cleaning and maintaining latrines. Conduct hygiene promotion campaigns with relevant IEC materials. Distribute WASH NFIs and/or household water treatment products to IDPs and, vulnerable host HHs. Conduct clean-up campaign and bucket washing campaign whenever relevant, to reinforce hygiene promotion messages.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The situation in Aweil Centre is quite stable. It is mostly host population with few IDPs. Although there was some recent conflict, this has not persisted. Access remains good.In Renk, the security situation is stable. The border with Sudan was closed from March 2018 but has become more porous and may open. The numbers of Sudanese crossing the border for healthcare has increased in recent months and the opening of the border may result in increasing numbers of Sudanese who come for health services and return across the border. Insecurity in Sudan would drive Sudanese and South Sudanese refugees into Northern Upper Nile.Leer County remains stable, there has been significant movement from islands to the mainland and some limited returns.All needs assessments include protection components. Building or promoting availability of WASH services closer home-such as water points and toilets, and segregating the latrines, reduce the protection risks faced by women and children when accessing WASH facilities, especially at night. Medair seeks to enable different population groups to access services safely and equitably. Additionally, sensitization on importance of sharing facilities without resorting to violence, and understanding they are for the common good of everyone is emphasized during the planning and implementation phase, and during handovers.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, Medair establishes trains water user committees and pump mechanics for all water points developed or rehabilitated. Medair also uses sustainable behaviour change communication model-the care group model, that looks to communicate various WASH, nutrition and health messages in a sustainable manner. Lastly, Medair always hands over WASH infrastructures established and activities that require follow ups to longer term development partners in the project area(s)

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Before project implementation, Medair will carry out assessments that will inform the type of assistance the beneficiaries need, and the modalities they would want to receive it. Barrier analysis will be carried out to engage community participation in overcoming obstacles to accessing services and practicing proper hygiene and sanitation behaviours. During implementation, MEDAIR will undertake regular meetings with communities throughout interventions and feedback mechanisms including a beneficiary telephone hotline, community focal point person to receive the feedback and post distribution monitoring will be used to receive feedback on the affected persons concerns, perceptions, and interests. As mentioned under the gender and disability inclusion sections, gender and disability inclusions issues will be factored in various methodologies to be accountable to affected populations, where appropriate during both program design and project implementation. Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities, and feedback is gathered through the Care Groups. In addition, Medair will carry out regular monitoring of use of PoUWT, and WASH nonfood items through house-to-house visits, discussing challenges, successes and opportunities with beneficiaries. Additionally, Medair will conduct regular ‘knowledge, practice and coverage’ evaluations, using local staff to conduct these exercises which elicit information from communities regarding hygiene, and sanitation needs and behaviours. Medair will carry out ‘exit interviews’ and post distribution monitoring, as part of programming proactively and consistently, to elicit feedback from beneficiaries.
