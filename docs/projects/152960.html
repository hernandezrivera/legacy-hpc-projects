Project Summary

REVISIONS 11/14/18: All revisions requested have been completed: (1) Budget reduced to $800K & cost/beneficiary improved; (2) Explanation of avoiding overlap with other CP partners explained in Summary; (3) CoP, Acct,Comm sections strengthened.
This project seeks to provide life-saving CP prevention, mitigation & response in alignment with the HRP Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3, Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives 1 & 2, and the priority strategic objectives/actions identified by the CPSC.
The project prioritizes Tier 1 Yambio and Nagero; Tier 2 in Aburoc, Juba; Tier 3 in Tonj East, Twic, Gogrial West, Renk, Nzara & Tambura (prioritizing Tier 1 locations but recognizing the limited resourcing of CP response services in Tier 2, 3 locations for 2019 and World Vision’s operational presence with capacity to respond or integrate with other sectors of programming such as Education and GBV Prevention & Response).
The project will target a total of 350,000 individuals (W: 90,000; G: 100,000 (Adolescent: 50,000); M: 60,000; B: 100,000 (Adolescent: 50,000)), with life-saving child protection assistance to 8,000 girls and boys through case management (incl FTR, CAAFAG) and community based PSS interventions (Girls: 4,000 (Adolescents: 3,000) Boys: 4,000 (Adolescents: 2,000)). Given World Vision South Sudan is also both a GBV and Education partner, specific emphasis on strengthening life-saving assistance meeting the unique and unmet needs of child survivors, specifically adolescent girls, and linking/referring girls and boys in need of child protection services to education opportunities.
The project will include the following key interventions in all locations, based on specific assessed need, disaggregated by sex and age: Comprehensive CP case management (Identification & documentation of children with protection concerns, case management capacity building, family tracing & reunification (FTR) of UASC and placement in alternative care within framework of comprehensive case management, referrals to specialized services); specific case management and provision of focused psychosocial support (PSS) for CAAFAG and other vulnerable children with protection concerns; Capacity building of CP staff, frontline workers, teachers, government/local authorities in CPIE; Strengthening of community based CP structures (CBCPCs); Provision of structured community based PSS through Child Friendly Spaces, peer support for adolescents and other relevant modalities; Community awareness raising on CPIE/children’s rights, incl engagement of adolescents and youth, positive parenting, messaging and campaigns.

The project reflects the feedback of beneficiary consultations and includes accountability mechanisms, and considers the unique needs and adapts its design based on gender and age considerations/vulnerabilities (Code 4M). The project approach seeks to place communities at the centre of interventions and contribute to localization through capacity building in all community-based initiatives (i.e. strengthening CBCPCs in monitoring, reporting, CBPSS, referral). The project design prioritizes a Do No Harm approach with an explicit intention to address protection issues experienced by girls and boys, recognizing that respect for children’s rights is an enabler of the South Sudan humanitarian response strategy.
WV will avoid overlap with other CP actors by continuing to prioritize CP prevention/response in existing areas only, which have already been locally coordinated to avoid overlap (i.e. WV & MCC in Renk, WV & CINA in Tambura) or where no other CP actor works in the payam & this has been agreed through state level coordination (i.e. WV will only work in Kodok, Fashoda, only Rajaf in Juba). WV aims to coordinate closely through the CPSC & with CP actors to ensure all avoidance of duplication and reach only those locations with no CP assistance other than that provided by WV.

Needs

In 2018, the humanitarian crisis continued in South Sudan despite the signing of the peace agreement in August, and continues to be defined by widespread protection concerns, gross violations of children’s rights and widespread failure to realize children’s rights as found in the CRC and Child Act 2008. The 2019 HNO (draft) highlights how women, girls and boys continue to bear the brunt of the crisis, as an estimated 62% of the 1.9M IDPs are children. Children continue to be affected by a wide range of protection concerns and denial of their rights as children, with the majority of violations for girls specifically linked to GBV as well. An estimated 19,000 children have been recruited, associated with armed forces/groups (CAAFAG); World Vision South Sudan currently supports the largest caseload of CAAFAG in Yambio with reintegration and comprehensive case management, over 700 girls and boys. According to MRM data, 252 verified grave violations of children’s rights have occurred, affecting 4,385 children in 2018 to date. Other child protection concerns include the following: sexual violence against children, with 22% of all reported GBV incidents involving survivors under the age of 18; over 16,000 unaccompanied & separated minors (UASCs) in need of family tracing & reunification (FTR); and attacks / occupation of schools interrupting children’s access to education (GBVIMS; Child Protection Trends). Though the peace agreement may bring an end to the conflict, its impact on children of all ages is severe. UNICEF estimates that 1M children are in need of psychosocial support (PSS), while WHO findings estimate the experience of conflict can lead to a 10% increase in mental health needs (Protection Trends Report). These child protection concerns demonstrate a need for life-saving assistance to affected children, through comprehensive CP case management, which includes identification and assessment of individual needs, provision of PSS, and referral to services necessary for a child’s recovery and healthy development. In many locations across the country there is an absence of a CP actor with capacity to provide these services, or these services are significantly under-resourced in a manner that prevents life-saving CP assistance from reaching all children with serious protection concerns. Attitudes, behaviours and practices result in or compound CP concerns. World Vision baseline data and KAP Survey findings indicate many parents and teachers believe corporal punishment is appropriate/necessary and practice this form of discipline; both mothers and fathers reported the importance of marrying their girls to receive dowry, particularly in light of economic deterioration. Increasing community awareness of children’s rights and safe, healthy development, prevention/mitigation of recruitment and separation, and capacity to monitor, report and refer child protection concerns is essential to lessen the scope/impact of child protection concerns and reduce the overall need for life-saving CP assistance in the future. Girls and boys face unique child protection concerns, which require specific interventions and approaches to address these. The majority of CP incidents affecting girls are sexual violence, denial of education, drop-out due to early/forced marriage arrangements, and stigma/hygiene concerns around menstruation. These concerns can occur concurrently, with a girl survivor forced to marry their perpetrator as “settlement,” which also results in denial of continuing with education and often leads to the challenges of early pregnancy. Boys are at high risk of recruitment (as are girls), and the PSS distress that can come from combat and/or spying; boys are also denied education out of expectation to manage herds (cattle camps). Interventions are designed to specifically prevent and respond to the needs of girl survivors of GBV, and help promote positive opportunities for both girl/boy adolescents through peer support.

Activities

Objective 1 – Provision of life-saving assistance to children with protection concerns.(CP Strategy Obj 1 & 4)Key Activities- Identification & documentation of children with protection concerns- Support vulnerable girls & boys with protection concerns through comprehensive case management & referrals to specialized services- Support UASCs with FTR incl identification, reunification and placement in alternative care within SOPs of comprehensive case management- Support CAAFAG reintegration within SOPs of comprehensive case management, incl identification of children, case management, reunification, placement in alternative care- Support child survivors within the scope of comprehensive CP case management, and strengthening linkages to GBV response systems and services. - Provision of community based psychosocial support (PSS) through child friendly spaces & PSS provided by social workers - Capacity building of frontline workers, CP staff, relevant government in comprehensive CP case management, CPIE, children’s rights Life-saving assistance for children with protection concerns will focus on provision of comprehensive CP case management per the newly launch SOPs for South Sudan, with a specific focus on FTR for UASCs and reintegration/FTR for CAAFAG in Yambio. Assistance is provided by a CP Team consisting of case supervisor and case/social workers; case/social workers are responsible for providing case management services inclusive also of reunification, placement in alternative care & direct provision of focused PSS. Special attention will be accorded to child survivors, recognizing WVSS expertise in GBV response, and integrated GBV-CP approach. All case management, safe referrals will be documented per SOPs incl use of hardcopy forms & CPIMS to ensure high quality, confidential CP information management. All assistance will consider the unique needs of girls with protection concerns in comparison to boys at various age/development periods. OBJ 2 – Prevent, mitigate and respond to Child Protection issues through enhanced preparedness and resilience(CP Strategy Obj 2 & 3)Key Activities- Provide training/mentoring to child protection volunteers & other actors of CPIE, CP approaches- Capacity building of CP and non-CP actors, government/local authorities in CPIE focused on prevention of CP concerns, separation, recruitment, etc. - Provision of structured community based PSS interventions through child friendly spaces- Strengthening capacity of CBCPCs through capacity building, coaching & supervision; building capacity in CPIE, PFA for children, CBPSS, etc. and strengthening of community based CP structures on CPIE- Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members- Support adolescent girls & boys with lifeskills and resilience programming, and link to peer support and mentorship initiatives- Community based awareness raising through various, contextualized mediums promoting children’s rights (focus on 6 grave violations, GBV issues affecting children, attitude/behaviour change to promote greater respect for children’s rights, strengthen community resilience and preparedness to mitigate future CP concerns)- Mainstream CP and basic PSS in schools; Strengthen CPIE capacity of Education actors, structures in schools & for out of school children.Community engagement in CP prevention, mitigation & first response is a priority to ensure CP systems/services in hard to reach locations & create local ownership of CP. Formation of & continued capacity building of CBCPCs is a key priority, supporting CBCPCs in monitoring, reporting, referring CP concerns, facilitating dialogues on CP/children’s rights in their communities to promote positive attitude/behaviour change & parenting, & leading community awareness raising. Engagement of adolescents and youth will be a key priority to promote prevention of CP issues adolescents can be uniquely vulnerable to (ex. girls – marriage; boys – recruitment).

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Yes. Community consultations are broad, recognizing that leaders may be elder and male. Efforts are thus made to consult with women’s group leaders, existing CPCBCs (male/female all ages), children (at CFS, thru school clubs), vulnerable children participating in CP services being provided by WV.Examples: Use of Household survey and Focus Group Discussions – Mixed and sex/age disaggregated groups – methodologies targeted gender and age specific community members using sampling methodology design for a representative sample. Child participation techniques are explicitly used to garner feedback from children from age groups 0-6, 7-10, 11-14, 15-17 years, in formats that seek feedback separately from girls and boys, as well as together. While Key Informant Interviews and feedback is collected from local authorities, community leaders and faith leaders, the majority of influencing data and feedback is sourced from women, men, girls and boys within the community broadly.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes. Feedback/complaint mechanisms are in place through Child Help Desks. Where mobile phone network is available, WV operates a hotline. Community sensitization/engagement always includes awareness-raising about the hotline or other feedback/complaint mechanisms in place, how to access these, and what action will be taken next.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Communities are engaged thru CBCPCs trained in CPIE, supported and responsible for monitoring, reporting and referring child protection issues; CBCPCs coached & supervised to lead CP awareness raising at community level; Community based PSS interventions (CFS) involving local staff and community volunteers; Training of community leaders/local authorities in CPIE; Establishment of peer support groups for adolescent girls/boys. WV uses monitoring and evaluation approaches with inclusion of participatory tools and exercises to engage women, girls, men and boys, with specific use of child-friendly age-appropriate tools to prioritize feedback from girls and boys. Information collected by CBCPCs informs reporting and evaluation. Close-out meetings at local level shares findings from M&E with diverse group of community stakeholders (w,m,b,g; all age groups/abilities).

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project is based on protection risk analysis, such as through Do No Harm analysis which is conducted at each project location & identifies the specific needs and risks for girls and boys, as well as different groups (women, men; specifically vulnerable age groups i.e. adolescent girls; PSNs; older persons) to inform project implementation methodology. Planned intervention locations, activities or approaches are adapted to mitigate safety/displacement risks and promote a rights-based approach in consideration of DNH & other protection risk analysis findings; action plans for risk mitigation (especially GBV RM) are developed & rolled out.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. Physical barriers to access are assessed in each location of operation. Efforts to mitigate access barriers are made, such as moving services closer to boma/household level to avoid long distance travel and providing services in the home (Ex. Case management, PSS, CP prevention and children’s rights dialogues). Other barriers can include those that are security related, in which case WVSS works closely with local authorities, community leaders and relevant UN Access Negotiation teams. Implicit barriers resulting from gender inequality and social norms specifically affect girls from accessing services (ex. Denial of education, drop out due to early/forced marriage); efforts are made to address this type of barrier through community awareness raising and engagement of youth in promoting women’s and girl’s equal rights and gender equality.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes. The project is designed to specifically address protection threats experienced by girls and boys under the age of 18 years, which includes within its definition all forms of coercion, exploitation, deprivation and neglect. Life-saving CP assistance for girls and boys with protection concerns will be supported with comprehensive case management, safe referrals to specialized services, and provision of focused PSS (among other activities to address these threats). This project will also address inequalities and protection threats that affect girls and boys through prevention activities to change attitudes and behaviours, and through mitigation seeking to reduce or avoid CP risks that contribute to child protection concerns for girls and boys.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes. The project monitoring measures access of beneficiaries through Child Friendly Space participation registers, CPIMS data, and project activity participation tracking. In addition, routine Protection monitoring (Safety Audit, Safety Perception mapping) assesses and consults community members on access and risks experienced therein. Feedback mechanisms such as comment boxes/Help Desks provide an opportunity for community members to share access issues.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes.The project conducts quarterly (or ad hoc as necessary safety audits/safety perception mapping); these are compared over time in consultation with community members to measure the real & perceived safety of beneficiaries, identify actions/mitigating measures, adapt intervention approach.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes. Dignity of beneficiaries, especially girls and boys, is reviewed in overall project methodology, as well as specifically for each activity implemented or service provided, with an emphasis on promoting beneficiary dignity.
