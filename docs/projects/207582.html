Project Summary

"In 2024, Plan International Bangladesh will focus on implementing integrated CP program in camps 17,20E, 21,24,25 and host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf for prevention, mitigation and response to abuse, neglect, violence and exploitation of children. This proposed action will follow the socio-ecological model and will include outreach activities, CP Systems strengthening & capacity building, CP case management (specialized services), community-based child protection and CP Structured and Sustained Activities.
These interventions will ensure inclusion of children/persons with disability, other children with specific needs and elderly people. Special attention will be paid to child survivors of sexual abuse, early/forced/child marriage, child labor, trafficking as well as unaccompanied and separated children. Through this project Plan International Bangladesh will put special focus on addressing child marriage issue as it is a raising concern and fall under severity level 05 according to CPIMS and expert judgement.
The project will reach 96821 people (Female 49292 male 47529 with approximately 11619 children and persons with disability), while closely engaging with children and their caregivers from both Rohingya and host communities. They will be consulted throughout the project cycle from designing to evaluation stage.
The project will, through a holistic approach, build the capacity of community structures, including duty bearers such as community leaders (Majhis), religious leaders, camp authorities, and associated government officials and train specialized case workers in quality case management and referral pathways, and work with parents and caregivers to adopt positive parenting practices, together creating a more enabling environment.
CBCP activities will be structured through feedback from community consultation and participation to ensure inclusion and ownership by both the host and the refugee communities. Community-level interventions will include promoting social cohesion and strengthening self-protection of children.
The project will offer gender responsive comprehensive case management services including referral, integrated menstrual hygiene management (MHM) services, MHPSS covering structured, non-structured PSS and specialized services. LGBTQI population will be kept in special consideration.
As part of system strengthening, Plan International Bangladesh will host workshop with national child protection government structure actors on child protection approaches that includes Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and child safeguarding policy.
PIan International Bangladesh is committed to coordinating the response through active participation in different coordination platform such as Child Protection Sub-Sector, CMTWG, MHPSS Working Group, CP focal etc. to ensure meaningful access, prioritization of safety and integration of services. The services offered will be in line with CPMS, interagency tools and SOPs, CPSS strategy and will be delivered through structured training enhancing competencies in providing quality gender-responsive and timely services. All the Services will be provided through Child Friendly spaces (CFS), Multipurpose centre (MPC), child club, adolescent club and block and community level spaces.
As the project will be implemented through partner national NGOs, Plan will undertake various capacity strengthening initiatives for them and community volunteer as well at every stage of the project. A participatory M&E framework will be developed and used for keeping the actions in track and achieving project goal with quality.
This proposed project will extend it’s intervention additionally in camp 1W where a critical gap of resources exists as per CPSS analysis. The project will have no cash distribution intervention rather than cash incentive of volunteer."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Entering the 7th year of the Rohingya humanitarian situation in Bangladesh the needs remains dreadful. According to the UNHCR population factsheet (as of September 2023), 52% are children among the total population 965,467 in 32 camps. CPSS have targeted 480947 children who are in need. According to the child protection information management system (CPIMS+) factsheet (January- December 2022), a total of 13,422 children have experienced or are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse or neglect which made the total of children in protection concern 55,768 since 2017. CPIMS+ data shows increased current need of child protection than the previous year.The factsheet (January-December 2022) demonstrates neglect, psychological distress, child labor, serious medical condition, emotional and psychological abuse, physical abuse, separated, child marriage, physical disability or impairment and mental disability as the top protection concerns chronologically.According to the CPIMS+ neglect has frequently raised protection concerns, commonly associated with other protection concerns such as psychological distress, serious medical condition. Child labor and child marriage remain major child protection issues affecting boys and girls differently- 98% boys make up the 15% child labor cases whereas 99% girls make up the 8% child marriage cases. There is a seasonal pattern in child protection cases as it has been identified that child marriage, physical disability or impairment, mental disability, teen pregnancy, serious medical condition, sexual abuse or sexual exploitation and child in conflict with the law are the frequently identified protection concern during monsoon. Besides, child trafficking cases are more likely to occur during winter as the identification is higher during beginning and end of the year. CPIMS data has also reflected the quality of care arrangement for the vulnerable children. 69% of the children are living with their parents or primary caregivers,25% of children are with a relative or family member,6% of children are with other care arrangements such as with a neighbor from Myanmar or temporary caregiver where 53% caregivers are male and 47% caregivers are female. JMSNA 2023 also indicates a crucial perspective of children’s separation from their household. It shows reason behind separation are (a) married and left the house 43.4% in Camp and 4.5% in HC (b) to seek employment 32.1% in camp, 17.1% in HC and (c) Missing (left and no news) 3.7% in camp. This data indicates that he children are still at high risks of child marriage and child labor. Baseline study (2023) of PRACTICE project under Plan International shows that children are facing multi-faceted crisis like Physical punishment by parents/caregivers, Child Early Forced Marriage and Union, child labor, Child pregnancy, Gender Based Violence, Trafficking in person, Psychological distress, Illiteracy/lack of education, Food security/malnutrition etc.Alongside the recent unrest situations like fire incidents, landslides, conflict among criminal groups/Rohingya extremist groups make the situation unsafe and life threatening. Overall need analysis data indicates the need of emergency and consistent support of safety, health, psychosocial support, positive parenting and awareness on Alternative care arrangement, addressing child marriage, Child labor, child trafficking, empowering children, strengthening community-based child protection mechanism and strengthening CP system in both camps and host communities.The proposed action will address all these crucial needs the children are in through it’s prevention, mitigation and response intervention like awareness raising, life skill training, strengthening community based protection mechanism, CP system strengthening and case management services."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

"PIB ensures Protection is mainstreamed throughout all its projects, and endeavors to uphold the Sphere Protection principles and mitigate potential protection risks related to activities. The project will apply the following principles of Community, Engagement and Accountability (CEA) mainstreaming in all of its activities:• Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: It will strongly prevent and minimize any adverse effects of its intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability and risks. For example, girl’s enrollment in its intervention will be in special consideration from cultural point of view.• Meaningful Access: It will ensure access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without discrimination. It will pay special attention to individuals who have difficulty accessing assistance. For instance inclusive intervention will be designed and implemented with participation of different age, gender including LGBTQI community.• Accountability: It will set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can be involved in all participatory aspects of project cycle and establish measures for affected populations to measure the appropriateness of interventions and organizations can address concerns and complaints. • Participation and Empowerment: It will support the development of communities’ and individual capacities and assist people to claim their rights."

3.3 - Age & Disability

"Plan International Bangladesh (PIB) always designs project/s covering diversity in terms of disability, gender, ethnicity and religion. The project will cover 12% person/children with disability of total reach. Special measures will be taken for children with disability to ensure their participation in project intervention and access to required services as well. For instance, home based support will be provided for the children having movement difficulties, facilities like CFS/MPC will be made accessible for them etc.PIB will ensure that communication is inclusive and transmitted through multiple media options including radio, visual guides, and community mobilization as well as in a diversity of languages like Rohingya and accessible formats when using technologies. Participation, Community engagements and consultation of most affected groups that includes women and youth groups, adolescent girls and boys groups, and those having age and disability-related concerns will be kept in account to ensure age and disability inclusive program."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"Children friendly complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be established at the beginning of the project. CFM includes three channels of data flow that are a hotline number, opinion box and community reflection session with children, youth, parents, and caregivers and other community members. PIB recognizes that Child-Friendly Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFRM) play a key role in strengthening reporting mechanisms for PSEA and in safeguarding children and young people.Feedback received from different channels contributes in program adaptations in many ways including changing implementation strategies. Community members will be encouraged to raise their issues by engaging in project activities, phone call and feedback and information centers.Plan International Bangladesh will make the community people, volunteer, community structures to strengthen the capacity and resilience that will reduce the dependency on aid.Plan International will follow the Tip sheet for AAP made by CPSS at every stage of the project. "

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"Plan International Bangladesh has a PSHEA policy for children and young people and is committed to the 6 IASC principles related to PSHEA. All staff and volunteers will sign the associated Code of Conduct, Whistleblowing and Bullying and harassment policy and will get orientation on same that will prevent them causing any PSEA incident. Capacity building sessions for internal staff and partners will be conducted on PSEA as a means of maintaining the do-no-harm approach.PIB also has a clear reporting and responding procedure with regards to safeguarding communities. Plan and it’s partner will maintain close communication with PSEA network as an active member of this and will follow all the advisory of the network in implementing the project.In order to ensure that the community is aware on and able to report any PSEA incident community mobilization will be conducted and Child-Friendly Community Feedback Mechanisms (CFRM) will be put in place which will include a hotline number, opinion box and community reflection session with children, youth, parents, and caregivers and other community members. CFRM will play a key role in strengthening reporting mechanisms for PSEA and in safeguarding children and young people. Feedback and/or issues/concerns raised by children and community people will be addressed continuously throughout the project. It will also contribute to programme adaptation and improvements as well. "
