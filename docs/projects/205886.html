Project Summary

Through multisectoral mobile response approach and static team based in the field area, DRC will improve access on Shelter and Non-Food Items Services to the most vulnerable people affected by displacement across South Sudan. The project targets to mitigate exposure to weather elements and disease-causing vectors through; provision of cash for shelter assistance, Provision of essential in-kind shelter and Non-Food items for new displacements, rehabilitation of communal and individual emergency shelter and Support in construction of transitional shelters to vulnerable IDP's/returnees/residents with secure land tenure: The project will manage to safeguard the right to an adequate standard of living to promote safety and dignity through addressing specific needs of people of concern. DRC will continue with upper Nile SNFI cluster coordination lead role as well as participate in joint missions to avoid duplication and improve on resource sharing and timely response. DRC will seek additional support from SNFI cluster Core pipeline for SNFIs items to support the affected populations. DRC will focus on assessment- based responses and deployment monitoring in compliance with SNFI cluster guidelines and priorities, providing feedback and learnings to improve projects and responses.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire; More than 8.5 million people (more than two-thirds of the population) need humanitarian assistance. The ongoing crisis in Sudan and presidential elections scheduled for 2024 may affect the displacement pattern. Of all people in need of humanitarian assistance, 2.1 million people need emergency shelter and NFI assistance. (HNO 2024). The current floods in some states, social violence, natural disasters, and food insecurity have increased Shelter and non-food item needs across the country with Jonglei state, Unity State and Upper Nile State being the most affected. The continued displacement combined with sustained flooding and economic crisis, have further depleted natural resources such as timber and grass, leading to competition for the limited available resources thus increasing the conflict within the communities. The effects of flooding have cut off access to markets challenging the supply of shelter materials, thus leading to functioning markets lacking essential shelter materials and NFIs. In addition, the ongoing conflict in neighboring countries (Sudan and Ethiopia) has led to increased number of returnees further increasing the needs for shelter and NFI. The displaced people, returnees, and residents require the top three shelter/NFI-related needs: access to appropriate shelter, NFIs, and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure tenure security. Access to shelters and NFIs will alleviate the deterioration of the affected population's living conditions and resilience from recurring hazards. Shelter is the foundation and basis for engagement in livelihoods and economic activities, and it further provides people with protection, identity, peace, stability, and security. Inadequate or lack of shelter can affect people's well-being, dignity, and health, generating increased protection risks, particularly for women and children. Shelter and NFIs should be provided promptly to the vulnerable, the pregnant mothers, children, the elderly persons with specific needs to protect their living conditions, thus mitigating exposure to weather elements and disease-causing vectors.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Provision of cash for emergency shelter upgrades: DRC SNFIs sector using a multi sectoral approach and, through a community-based approach, targets to implement shelter activities using cash for shelter modality for the most vulnerable IDP’s, returnees and residents. The implementation will be conducted jointly by protection and shelter sector, where protection and shelter vulnerability assessment will be conducted using the protection criteria. The response will mainly be to support vulnerable IDPs/Returnees/residents to upgrade their emergency shelters. The cash intervention rates will be determined by market analysis. The protection risk analysis will determine whether cash is a safe /appropriate modality for the response. The project targets 12000 beneficiaries (2000HH) with cash for shelter Activity 2: Provision of essential in-kind shelter and Non-Food items for new displacements: DRC will provide emergency SNFI assistance through mobile response modality based on multi-sectoral need’s assessment findings. DRC will focus on responding in locations where there are no or limited capacity to respond or to cover the needs and will apply selection criteria targeting individuals who have need for lifesaving or life-sustaining core relief SNFIs. Targeting and selection of beneficiaries will be done through participatory approaches involving community members to promote accountability to the affected population. In this response DRC will use NFI stocks procured and prepositioned as well SNFI core pipeline. Where needs maybe high, DRC will advocate for support from other partners to fill any remaining gaps. The project targets 34,800 beneficiaries (5800 HH) Activity 3: Rehabilitation of communal and individual emergency shelter: Using a multi-sectoral approach and community engagement DRC will continue supporting IDPs within the Malakal POC to rehabilitate their damaged emergency shelter. The rehabilitation will be conducted by engaging the IDPs trained artisan from the previous years and with direct supervision from the shelter technical team. DRC will identify and verify beneficiaries referred through the protection, GBV, or CCCM referral path and complaint and feedback mechanisms (CRM) to receive shelter assistance. The SNFI cluster will provide materials for rehabilitating the emergency shelters from the pipeline upon request. The project targets 1,800 beneficiaries (300HH) through rehabilitation of emergency shelter. Activity 4: Provision of transitional shelters to vulnerable IDP's/returnees with secure land tenure: In support to durable solutions, DRC shall provide transitional shelters to vulnerable IDP’s/Returnees with secure land tenure. Land verification process shall be carried out with support of available HLP structures and relevant ministry within the government. DRC will provide cash for transitional shelter to the most vulnerable community members based on findings of the need’s assessment carried out in key return areas and new areas of displacements. Cash provided shall be used to source locally available materials and will be complemented with provision of labor services for wall mudding of the transitional shelters. DRC will provide hardware materials which is not locally available which can include iron sheets, timbers, nails, rubber washers, doors and windows. Technical guidance and supervision shall be provided to the community during harvesting of local materials to minimize negative effect on the environment. In Locations where there is no available stock in the markets, DRC shall procure all materials and engage artisans within the community for construction. Casual contractors will then be hired from the local communities to provide labor for construction of transitional shelters. DRC shelter team will provide technical support during construction to ensure quality. The project shall target 1200 persons of concern (200 HH) through provision of transitional shelters.
