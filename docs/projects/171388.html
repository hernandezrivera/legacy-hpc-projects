Project Summary

Due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. This project will improve food access of most affected populations in Somaliland and Puntland. The most vulnerable households with low Food Consumption Scores and high rates of negative coping strategies will have access to unconditional and conditional cash transfer to address food insecurity during the period of the project. In addition, households who lost their harvest due to locust invasion and have limited purchasing power to access food in the local market will be the prime target of the intervention. The other aim is to improve seasonal livelihood inputs through provision of agricultural inputs and protection, and restoration of livelihood asset including provision of seasonal inputs and livestock treatment drugs. The project will give high emphasis on women and person with disability. 60% of project beneficiaries will be women and person with disability. On the other hand, the project will also take in to account mitigating the risks of COVID 19 through community awareness and provision of protection measures. This intervention will mainstream protection measures including the installation of street lights so as to improve the safety of women during nights. In addition, Staff will be trained safe-guarding policies and protection policies to increase their knowledge and also rights of the beneficiaries Feedback Mechanism (using both project-specific and centralized feedback processes helped issues be addressed at all levels). Islamic Relief will make sure that all staff follow general COVID-19 guidance carefully - including hand washing, equipment cleaning, and proximity to program participants, stakeholders, and other members of staff. The project also includes a child protection component. This component will safeguard children from taking up labor intensive works to draw income for their families. The project will provide an awareness sessions to mothers on child feeding and balanced diet. This will allow mothers to improve the health of their children and eventually keep the child in schools with out engaging outdoor labor activities.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The experience of IRW shows that cash-based modality in this project is rated to be less costly and better adjusted to people’s needs and preferences and it is more timely. Also, the beneficiaries will spend the cash on both food and non-food items and it will easily be invested in livelihood security. Moreover, cash transfers is opted by the fact that it has a potential of stimulating the economic recovery by encouraging traders to move supplies from areas of food surplus to areas of food deficit. This helps to maintain prices (and production) in areas of surplus. Apart from this, the recent outbreak of COVID_19 pandemic, it is wise to avoid crowds of beneficiaries while making the services efficient and affordable. Therefore having a wide coverage of mobile money transfer in somalia plus the prevention of the pandemic cash is considered to be the best option.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

While developing this project IRW has evaluated whether markets are functioning or likely to recover quickly following a disaster, and whether the basic items that people need are available in the markets. According to the market assessment did by the agency the markets in the target areas are rated as stable and have the capacity to supply the commodities to the consumers. This data will also be updated quickly in the first few days following a rapid-onset emergency, by visiting markets and interviewing traders. When time permits, a more detailed market assessment will be conducted, as part of an in-depth livelihoods analysis.

Mitigation Measures

To reduce the risk of marginalized groups not involving the project. The project will make sure the beneficiary selection criteria includes marginalized groups. The project will also set up complaint and feedback mechanism facilitating every beneficiary to easily access. The project will use different feedback mechanisms including suggestion box, telephone lines and person to person. In order to avoid external stakeholders interfering the project implementation, the project will share the plan, and documents of the project with concerned stakeholders and hold an inception workshop inviting all relevant stakeholders and vividly explaining the approaches, targets and criteria of beneficiary selection and intended objectives. This approach will reduce the rise of unusual confusion from external stakeholders. Furthermore, As COVID 19 pandemic is still in the country, there is a apparent requirement to make sure the wellness and safety of beneficiaries and staffs involved in the project. For instance, registration of beneficiaries will consider issues of preventing crowding, queuing with sufficient distance between each person and alternative methods. Hand sanitizers or hand washing facilities will be made available before any contact between a person registering and a beneficiary; devices will also be cleaned each time (e.g. fingerprint collector device). In addition, Islamic Relief will make sure that all staff follow general COVID-19 guidance carefully - including hand washing, equipment cleaning, and proximity to program participants, stakeholders, and other members of staff . The following specific activities will be implemented during the project period; • Avoiding crowding and “ensure adequate space is available to keep a 1.5-meter distance between beneficiaries and between beneficiaries and staff from organization/partner organization/service provider etc. • Sensitize households receiving cash about COVID19 prevention/mitigation measures, especially in relation to going out to spend the assistance. • As people with symptoms pose the highest risk for transmission, beneficiaries coming to the distribution will be screened for increased temperatures (using infrared thermometers). • Hand washing facilities will be installed in project sites and sanitizers, masks and gloves will be provided to beneficiaries
