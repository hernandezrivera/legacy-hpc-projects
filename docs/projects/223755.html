Project Summary

This project aims to enhance access to quality, life-saving Integrated Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) services for conflict-affected populations across seven Local Government Areas (LGAs). It specifically targets internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities.
The primary intervention involves implementing a comprehensive integrated service package through UNFPA-supported One Stop Centres (OSC) and integrated Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS), focusing on life-saving SRH and GBV services. A team of trained professionals—including midwives, nurses, doctors, family planning counselors, GBV case workers, psychosocial counselors, and mobile outreach teams—will provide a wide range of services, including clinical management of rape, antenatal and postnatal care, safe deliveries, family planning, sexually transmitted infection (STI) treatment, and GBV case management (including referrals to psychosocial support services, legal aid, and safe shelters). Special attention will be given to men and boys survivors of GBV.
Expected outcomes include increased access to integrated SRH and GBV services within the seven conflict-affected LGAs. This will be achieved through, an expansion of Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, the procurement and distribution of essential reproductive health kits (such as post-rape kits and clean delivery kits), and cash assistance-based interventions, in favor of vulnerable women and girls, including pregnant women and survivors of GBV. Other initiatives will include procurement & distribution of dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age, to serve as an entry point for accessing life-saving SRH/GBV information and services, integration of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), including Psychological First Aid (PFA), integration of PSEA risk mitigation measures across all sectors and UNFPA implementing partners (IPs).

1 - Needs

Access & Availability Gaps; The available GBV services were noted (UNICEF/UNFPA assessment) to be inadequate to meet the demand, particularly in case management. There is a shortage of social workers and para-social workers with the necessary skills and experience to support child survivors of GBV. There remains a significant gap in access to justice for survivors of GBV. Over the years, obtaining legal services has consistently been a challenge in GBV service provision across the BAY states. Analysis of GBVIMS data from the past four years reveals that legal services are often unavailable or have limited access. In 2024, 61% of those needing legal services reported that they were unavailable at the time of reporting, compared to 39% in 2023, 36% in 2022, and 48% in 2021. Additionally, other essential services identified as unavailable or limited in the BAY states through the case management process include safety and security (59%) and livelihood interventions (56%). This lack of access exposes women and girls to increased risks of GBV, including incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. PSEA; Findings from a joint CCCM multi-sectoral needs assessment in Yola North LGA of Adamawa highlight the need to improve reporting mechanisms for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) among affected populations. The report reveals that 56.6% of respondents reported a lack of a confidential, safe, and accessible PSEA reporting system. Protection sector monitoring in the BAY states indicates that affected populations prefer reporting mechanisms facilitated by community leaders. This feedback offers valuable insights for developing and enhancing PSEA strategies, ensuring they are culturally appropriate, trusted, and accessible to the communities they serve.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

This project seeks to put in place protective measures to prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV affecting women and girls including men and boys. This project plans to mainstream and integrate GBV risk mitigation analysis and measures in the HNO and HRP to ensure that humanitarian interventions across all sector do not cause or increase the likelihood of gender-based violence and that they Proactively seek to identify and act to mitigate GBV risks in the environment and in programme design and implementation. It also aims to facilitate and monitor vulnerable groups’ safe access to services. This project also aims to promote an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach to the protection of all IDP women, girls, men and boyz irrespective of their race, religion, political views, sexual orientation, social status, or place of residence. They will benefit from the project as persons of concern regardless of their gender or sexual orientation.
