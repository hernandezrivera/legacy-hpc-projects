Project Summary

CRS will implement the GFA, FFA and nutrition interventions to address the issues and problems most vulnerable people are facing in the targeted four localities of West Darfur as described below.
General food assistance GFA: CRS will use the GFA intervention to distribute food, using in-kind and unconditional CBT modalities, to most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host residents, selected using wealth ranking tool and community validation process. Food items will be selected based on the local food basket to meet the basic food need of highly vulnerable households, including those households with women heads, pregnant & lactating women (PLW/G), elderly and people with disability (PWD). GFA will use in-kind and CBT modalities. CRS will use CBT modality to ensure that provision of food to targeted food insecure households does not undermine but stimulate local market processes in Central Darfur. The GFA intervention will provide food that will enable most vulnerable food insecure households to engage in developing and maintaining their livelihoods and generate income to meet their basic needs, reduce hunger, and contribute to achieving the SO1 on People affected by shocks in targeted areas have access to food, nutrition and livelihoods during and after crises.
Nutrition: Under SO1 People affected by shocks in targeted areas have access to food, nutrition and livelihoods during and after crises, CRS will implement emergency blanket supplementary feeding (e-BSFP) in tandem with GFA as a response to shocks which occur during the life of the project. In parallel, CRS will contribute to SO2 Food insecure residents in targeted areas have sustainably improved nutrition by 2024 through the provision of a wider package of nutrition interventions, using in-kind modality. Services for CU5 and pregnant & lactating women/girls (PLW/G/G) will include community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and Social and Behavior Change (SBC) to provide nutrition knowledge and change practices.

1 - Needs

In July 2020, the Central Bureau of Statistics for Sudan produced the Sudan Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) snapshot for the June-Dec 2020 period, which revealed a record breaking 9.6 million people in Sudan (21% of the population) currently experiencing emergency and crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3 and above), including 2.2 million people in emergency acute food insecurity (IPC 4). Additionally, 15.9 million people are in stress acute food insecurity (IPC 2). FEWS Net estimates that all four states in which CRS operates in Sudan (Red Sea and Central, West, and East Darfur) are facing crisis (IPC Phase 3) and worse food security outcomes through October 2020. Several factors contributed to the dire food insecurity situation in Sudan, including: the prevailing economic crisis characterized by shortages of foreign currency and essential fuel and medicines, high inflation and soaring food and productive input prices that reduce both household food access and availability; COVID-19 pandemic that led to a loss of household livelihoods and reduced food access and availability; conflict and inter-tribal clashes; and climate-induced heavy rains and flooding. The UN reports that approximately 522,000 children under five (CU5) require treatment for sevWest Darfur’s context is characterized by chronic food and nutrition insecurity, economic crisis, socio-political uncertainty, and conflict and protracted displacement. To alleviate these humanitarian challenges being faced by people in West Darfur, CRS proposes to implement for 12 months, with assistance from WFP, food assistance programs on GFA, nutrition and FFA interventions in West Darfur. The interventions will directly contribute towards WFP’s goals of attaining “Zero Hunger”, ending malnutrition and building resilience in Sudan. CRS has extensive experience, since 2004, collaborating with WFP in implementing GFA, nutrition, post-harvest losses (PHL) reduction, school meals (SM), food assistance for education (FFE), FFA and productive safety nets (PSN) food assistance programs in Central and West Darfur states. It will draw on its experience of working with community leaders, communities, women, youth, Food Relief Committees (FRCs), community volunteers that include community nutrition volunteers (CNVs), community health promoters (CHPs), community extension workers (CEWs), community animal health workers (CAHWs) and savings & internal lending communities field agents (SILC FAs), and state and federal Ministry of Production & Economic Resources (MoP&ER), Ministry of Health (MoH), Ministry of Social Services (MoSS) and Humanitarian Affairs Commission (HAC) of the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs to successfully implement the food assistance interventions, while complying with the WHO/MoH COVID-19 guidelines. By implementing GFA to highly-vulnerable beneficiaries, FFA for vulnerable households with able bodied members, women heads, PLW/G, elderly and PWD, and nutrition interventions for moderately to severely malnourished CU5 and PLW/G, CRS will build resilience of targeted communities in West Darfur to sustainably transition them from a prolonged emergency to recovery and development. The cross-cutting issues of gender, protection, nutrition-sensitive programming, and natural resource management (NRM) will ensure that the path forward is socially equitable and valuable resources are sustainably used and preserved for social justice and future generations. The food assistance interventions will directly contribute towards the WFP’s country strategic goals of attaining “Zero Hunger”, ending malnutrition and building resilience in Sudan. This will enable WFP to contribute towards its SDG 2 that seeks to end hunger and ensure access to food, end all forms of malnutrition, double agricultural productivity and incomes of small-scale food producers, and ensure sustainable food systems and resilient agricultural practices in the next 15 years.
