Project Summary

This project is designed to address the development problems in Tonji North county of Warrap state associated to lack of clean water access and low level awareness of the people on improved methods of sanitation and hygiene practices. It will specifically targets communities in the payams of Aliek, Marial-Lou ,Rualbet ,Pagol, Akob and Alabek. The total number of population inhabited in this county is 81,240 (County RRC, 2022). The 2021 IPC nationwide report classified the county at the level of five as a result of communal conflict and the devastating impact of seasonal flood. Similarly, the 2022 IPC report (Nov. 2022) depicts that the county is at the level of IPC Phase 4 which requires emergency intervention. The situation in 2021 resulted in displacement of people from six payams and also various infrastructures were damaged including boreholes, houses, roads and institutions. The people displaced were hosted by neighboring communities which had a significant impact on the livelihood and various types of infrastructures. The coming year (2023) IPC project also shows that the county will remain in the same level and addressing the people that require assistance will be a priority. Hence, this project is aimed at mitigating the challenges related to safe water access in the selected six payams of the county. Besides, it will endeavor to enhance knowledge and technical skills of the target households on improved practices of sanitation and hygiene. A total of 28,200 people will be reached with various types of WASH interventions along with the FSL activities that ZOA Dorcas and its partners are implementing in the area. This sector includes crop production, horticulture, income generation, animal husbandry, disaster risk reduction and humanitarian aid. Furthermore, to improve nutrition status of the target community the project will work with World Vision in building water sources in nutrition facilities, conducting distribution of standard kits to SAM children and PLW, sanitation facilities and enhancing the knowledge of the communities on hygienic practices. The integration of different sectors is expected to bring tangible and sustainable outcome among the target households. Major undertakings of the project will be new drilling of boreholes, Rehabilitation and repairs of boreholes, hygiene and sanitation promotion campaigns, training of water management committee (WMCs), training of Community hygiene promoters (CHVs), training of hand pump mechanics, supply of bicycle for hand pump mechanics, provision of repairs tool kits for boreholes, provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls, conduct water testing for chemical and bacteriological tests, and setting up of hand washing stations in public markets. Furthermore, the project will increase the awareness of the community on sanitation through the application of CLTS approach which will be triggered in each village. ZOA Dorcas has applying this method in Wau and Jur River counties and successful results were achieved by teaching and encouraging people to construct latrines at household level using locally available materials. In addition, in collaboration with nutrition partners provision of standard kit for hygiene promotion will be done to SAM children/caretakers and PLW in the selected locations. Besides, along with hygiene awareness soap will be distributed to the needy households. Following the need at nutrition facilities various activities that will improve access to safe water will be undertaken giving high priority.

1 - Needs

According to IRNA Report (April 2021) at least 54% of the population in Tonj North (165,000) are each year affected by recurrent subnational violence and seasonal flood. In 2021, a total of 89,100 people were displaced within the county due to subnational violence triggered by cattle raid and revenge attacks. Among the displaced, 25,000 people encountered emergence and crises situation (IPC4 and IPC5) between January and June 2021. The situation was mitigated through massive humanitarian assistance from partners in operating Warrap State. This year, the IPC projection indicated similar situation like last year. But some humanitarian partners predict that it could be worse if subnational violence are not addressed. In June 2022, armed confrontation between armed youth and SSPDF in Rualbet triggered massive displacement of people from Rualbet to other payams in Tonj North and other counties. This has disrupted livelihood and deteriorated the vulnerability of hundreds of people in the area. The humanitarian needs of the vulnerable people in Tonj North has been further deteriorated by limited access, and difficulties in delivering humanitarian supplies.While security situation remains fragile, torrential rain and flood has severely affected many payams in Tonj North this year. Many families have been displaced from their villages to higher grounds. The flood submerged crops, affected public facilities, and forced many cattle to leave. Partners described the current situation as crises. The situation has prompted an IRNA in August 2022 and ZOA Dorcas was part of it. According to the findings, an estimated 15,700 HHs or 94,200 people have been affected by the flood in 2022. This is equivalent to 54% of the total population living in the county. Therefore, this project is designed to mitigate the impact of the disasters in the selected payams that requires immediate intervention. As per the assessment report of IRNA conducted by Partners in September 2022 there are 381 broken boreholes and 13 water yard damaged by flood from 30 existing water yard in the County. Besides, lack of sanitation facilities, poor hygiene practices, lack of clean drinking water that may put the population at risks of contracting diseases related to WASH needs interventions to reduce the negative impact in the communities of six Payams. The majority of the people lack access to safe and sufficient drinking water imperative for the healthy life. The activities implemented through this project is expected to reduce prevalence of water borne diseases and mitigate risks of targeted households particularly women and children and person with disabilities. Zoa-ZOA Dorcas is planning to support vulnerable people with WASH interventions to alleviate the communities from suffering and provide clean drinking water. According to inter-sectoral Clusters Coordination Group (ICCG) report of 2020-2021 about 75% of South Sudan population practices open defecations. Particularly in Tonj North the practice of open defecation is estimated at 90% which indicates the high need of intervention. The general WASH problems in the selected locations are: 1. Lack of safe drinking and people walk far distance for fetching 2. Non-functional water sources3. Poor hygiene and sanitation practices which put children and women at risk. 4. Lack of sanitation facilities at household level 5. Limited knowledge on the water resources managements7. Lack of access to hygiene and sanitation itemsThe organization years of experience in the area of WASH and carried out various activities in Warrap state. Some of the major undertakings are: Drilling of new hand pumps; Repairs and rehabilitation of broken/non-fictional hand pumps; Hygiene and sanitation promotion through CLTS ,CHAST & PHAST approaches; Formation and trainings of volunteers (WMCs, CHVs and VSCs); Training of hand pump mechanics and equipping them with tools and bicycles and others.

2 - Project activities

In the prioritized project locations various WASH activities will be undertaken to achieve the planned objectives. The interventions will target households that necessitate access to basic WASH services. These includes availability of clean water and improved sanitation & Hygiene awareness and practices. In the project area as a result of shortage of safe water the majority of the people fetch polluted water from unprotected sources. Moreover, there is a lack of knowledge on improved hygiene and sanitation practices which increased vulnerability of the people to different types of diseases. Therefore, the project will target those community members deprived of clean water access by bringing permanent solution by developing new water facilities and repair/rehabilitation of non-functional water sources. The interventions will strive to make sure the sustainable use of the facilities by establishing and capacitating water management committees in collaboration with respective government experts. The organization has good experience towards local resources mobilization through enhancing the awareness level of the communities on sustainable use of water facilities which brought significant changes in other operation areas where households began to contribute money on regular basis. The contributions have been collected by selected water management committee members in the objective of maintaining broken boreholes or nonfunctional water sources. Therefore, applying similar approach this project will upscale the good practices and learnings to Tonji North in the objective of ensure long term use of the clear water sources. In connection to improving hygiene practices the same target groups will be reached through various approaches to enhance communities awareness and skills. This will be done through organizing and training of village Community Hygiene Promoters. The awareness creation will be conducted through facilitating campaigns and door to door education on sanitation and hygiene. These approach are expected to reduce morbidity and mortality rate and level of severity in the county. The planned intervention of the project will also be integrated with other components which ZOA ZOA Dorcashas been implementing in the same locations. In the sector of FSL the organization has planned to carry out various activities associated to crop production, horticulture, income generation, animal husbandry, and disaster risk reduction which will be funded by other Donors. Furthermore, the designed project will work hand in hand with nutrition partners of the areas through intervention approaches that link WASH and nutrition such as hand washing celebration, construction of clean water sources like borehole drilling, distributions of various NFIs like soap and others. Hence, the organization has been working to reach this groups of people as they are vulnerable and impacted by different kinds of crisis. High probability to disaster risk, negative attitude, illiteracy, dependency on others, low participation in community affairs, lack of skills, and low income are the major challenges of individual with disabilities. In the last couple of years Zoa-Dorcas has given attention to people with impairments and revised the beneficiary registration template to include information related to this group. This will assist the staffs to collect the relevant data and ensure that during the implementation process all the registered beneficiaries including those with disabilities have received equal support from the project. Secondly, the PMC will be informed to make sure that PWDs are all given the first priority when they conduct beneficiaries, selection. In the registration forms, there is a column for PWDs which will be used to track number of beneficiaries with disabilities. During beneficiaries’ verification, the general community meeting shall be held and the participants will be asked to confirm if anyone with disability have been missed out and why not included. It is clearly understood that, in every disaster such as conflicts, floods and draughts, people with disabilities suffered more because of their inabilities to move far or to listen or to see the magnitude the disaster. They are often left behind by relatives who cannot afford to take them along to long distances. As such it has often been emphasized by the consortium to make sure the people with disabilities are either supported by the projects or provided jobs if they have they the required qualifications. It has become and internal policy of the Dorcas to make sure all the vulnerable groups are represented in the project as well as in the communities structures established by the project. Additional methods used to ensure that PLWD are included in the response are as follows:- During the initial assessment households with PLWD’s were identified, though not yet registered for the response (this happens after the response starts and after the reassessment and inception meetings).- In the inception meetings, Zoa-Dorcas will discuss the selection criteria with community representatives, consisting of women, men, youth, PLWD and other groups.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Accountability measures allow ZOA Dorcas to monitor the implementation of policies and procedures on PSEA and make improvements as needed. Accountability also entails sharing relevant information and feedback with beneficiaries and local communities. This helps build trust in the organization and encourages them to report any serious concerns in the future. The organizations will take the following measures to ensure accountability: monitor the implementation and effectiveness of strategies on PSEA; include regular opportunities for community-level monitoring (Ask questions about awareness, experiences or observations related to programming); regularly communicate to personnel, beneficiaries, and others regarding the organization’s efforts and respond to their feedback on programming, including SEA-related issues; review and learn from actual incidents within or outside the organization and adapt PSEA policies and procedures periodically to reflect lessons learned; and document and share progress, opportunities and challenges in prevention and response with senior management and other key stakeholders. Training and awareness-raising of personnel play a central role in building the organizational culture that does not tolerate abuse. These efforts can help address knowledge gaps and create a more open discourse on SEA, which remains a sensitive issue in many cultures. Therefore, ZOA Dorcas will first organize a training in collaborating with its partner Help a Child to enhance knowledge and capacitate the staff to effectively apply the strategies. The second step will be to share relevant information to target beneficiaries. The organizations will conduct activities to raise awareness among the beneficiaries and local communities, including children, and others on PSEA and relevant policies and procedures. It is very crucial because some beneficiaries and community members may not understand what SEA is, why it is dangerous, what their rights are, and what they can do to prevent and respond to incidents. Awareness-raising efforts will include developing core PSEA messages for beneficiaries and community members covering: 1) beneficiary rights (i.e. assistance is free; the right to information about the project/program; the right to be treated with respect, the right to report inappropriate behavior, etc.); 2) prohibited behaviour of personnel; 3) how to report complaints (e.g. hotlines, contact of PSEA focal points). The message will also include personnel covering: 1) definition and prohibition of SEA; 2) relevant roles and responsibilities; 3) the organization’s reporting and referral procedures, including relevant contact details. Besides, the project will adapt communication messages, materials and channels to the various target audiences, including children, using relevant languages and a communication style that is appropriate and accessible to the audience, especially to groups considered at higher risk of abuses. At the same time the project will widely publicize PSEA-related information by using a variety of communication channels (e.g. staff/community meetings, posters, radio). The project will closely work with protection partner like Help a Child on awareness-raising activities, including as part of GBV and child protection campaigns. ZOA Dorcas will highlight beneficiary rights in project/program awareness materials before and during implementationPresently, ZOA Dorcas and Help a Child are jointly implementing South Sudan Joint response project in the same location. In areas where this project will be implemented Help a Child will continue to be a partner by jointly providing beneficiaries information about protection against sexual exploitations and abuse. In this way the WASH and Protection components will be integrated to reach the community members that have challenges related to the sectors.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

ZOA Dorcas has protection policy that all the employees should sing before beginning to work in the organization. In order to identify and mitigate or avoid risks of SEA through programming, ZOA Dorcas will take the various action. The organization jointly with partners will conduct thorough and inclusive risk assessments on SEA. This may include site safety mapping, focus group discussions with intended beneficiaries and other stakeholders (including women, children, local authorities/communities, etc.) and other relevant research methods. As part of this assessment, the project will identify groups that are most marginalized and at heightened risk of SEA (e.g. children with disabilities). The project will consult with beneficiaries and local communities, including at-risk groups (e.g. adolescent girls, people with disabilities, as part of the initial needs assessment and routine monitoring to identify locally relevant risks and protection measures (that are in line with international laws and standards). Consultations will be safe and culturally appropriate.To minimize risks the organization will take the following measures.- Ensure that safe recruitment practices are followed for hiring programme personnel, including local volunteers, day laborers, etc.; this includes using a recruitment panel that includes at least one woman. - Ensure that all personnel are PSEA-trained and have signed the code of conduct, and reporting and response procedures are set up before the start of the programme. Include female personnel in programme implementation teams, where possible specifically if their roles require direct interactions with beneficiaries.- Ensure that there are shared responsibilities of processes during programme intervention so that more than one person is involved (“segregation of duties”).- Engage women and at-risk groups in the planning, design, implementation and monitoring of activities to the extent possible and if safe for those involved- Design programmes in a manner that limits one-on-one interactions between beneficiaries, particularly children, wherever possible.- Proactively communicate information about aid distribution procedures to the community (“aid is free”; beneficiary s; administrative requirements; how to report concerns; etc.) and take advise from the community to engage effectively.Furthermore, during activity implementation close monitoring will be made in collaboration with community representatives through establishing feedback mechanize by creating awareness among target groups. Hence, HaC will partner in the county of Tonji North to work on protection related activities and facilitate referral system. The improvement in clean water access and improved practices of sanitation and hygiene will enhance food utilization of the targeted households which is imperative to improve food security. In Tonji North County Dorcas has planned and secured fund to implement FSL project which will be integrated to activities of this project. Target farmers will be trained on improved crop production techniques and various agricultural inputs like assorted seeds and tools will be distributed. In addition, Dorcas will grant a national NGO to carryout income generation activities by providing vulnerable households focusing on women business management training and delivery of startup grant. The beneficiaries are expected to engage in different small business to fetch income and improve their livelihood. In 2021 the organization implemented similar activities addressing vulnerable members of the community. Furthermore, in order to improve income source households and their nutrition status people were trained honey production techniques and supplied with improved type of beehives. This integrated approach is expected to reduce vulnerability of affected people and reduce proportion of people facing recurrent food insecurity.
