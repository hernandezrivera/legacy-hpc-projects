Project Summary

Needs,
The needs assessment survey was conducted over three days in December 2023 by IHR field staff. It encompassed two tools: The Child Protection Needs Assessment and Protection Needs Assessments. In December 2023, six data collectors, comprising of 2 males and 4 females, were trained on the tools and the objectives of the assessment by the ISHA team. Throughout December, they underwent training on data collection methodologies.
According to the findings of the Child Protection Needs Assessment, all participants emphasized that education stands out as the most crucial need for ensuring the safety of children in the area. Additionally, 79% of participants highlighted the importance of specialized services for child protection and gender-based violence, while 74% emphasized basic needs such as clothing and heating. More than half of the participants (53%) reported that while some children in the community have access to humanitarian aids, others face barriers such as disability, lack of transportation, poverty, and displacement, preventing them from accessing these aids. This underscores the necessity for humanitarian interventions to directly target these vulnerable children.
All participants in the interviews emphasized the necessity of having activities for children in earthquake-affected areas such as Jandairis, Sharan, and Afrin, such as awareness-raising activities and educational activities. Additionally, there should be activities tailored for parents and caregivers to help them deal with their children and build their capacities to create a safe environment for their children within their homes, especially since the majority (68%) of parents expressed their inability to cope with the new displacement situation and their children's fear resulting from earthquakes.
Protection risk:
- Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers have not adequately trained/ services are of low quality
Likelihood: High.
Impact: High
Mitigating measures:
-ensure training packages are in line with child protection minimum standards and consider the remote managed context.
-Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules. include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place. Child Protection messaging to non-protection actors
- Protection risk: Risk to the wellbeing of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma)
Likelihood: High.
Impact: High
Mitigating measures: Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organisational HR policies

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project is in line with the 2021 Syria Humanitarian Response Plan Objective 5: (SO 5) " Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action. By addressing these objectives, IHR will respond to the strategic needs of the targeted beneficiaries, especially UASC, child laborers, CAAFAG, children deprived of their liberty, early marriage, survivors of violence, and sexual violence, including children with disabilities (CwD). These most vulnerable children will be provided quality child protection services through case management and referrals. Will provide life-saving specialized child protection services, per CPMS standards, through case management and individual protection assistance to address the individual protection needs of the child and their caregivers. In addition, the intervention will establish child protection community awareness . IHR will be implemented through a tactic center in addition to community outreach activities. Identification of cases will be conducted through the centers with existing caseloads, group activities, visitations to collective centers and camps, and referrals sanitizing when conducting the sessions.
