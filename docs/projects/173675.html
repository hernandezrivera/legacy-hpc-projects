Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring that SGBV survivors have access to essential services, including medical, psychosocial and livelihood support. The project includes an explicit focus on strengthening capacity within civil society - including but not limited to CBOs, community-based committees, schools, youth and women groups, religious groups/associations – and governmental counterparts to mitigate risk factors and coordinate a survivor-centred response which includes early identification, timely and well-coordinated referral to specialized services. The project seeks to provide survivors and people at risk - with particular attention to women, girls and boys – with life skills and access to income generating activities to help establish support networks and livelihood opportunities.

1 - Needs

Limited availability of specialized services for SGBV survivors such as psychosocial, medical, safety, security and legal services remains a challenge in Yobe State despite the efforts developed through the Protection Sector Working Group (PSWG) and the SGBV sub sector to build adequate prevention and response programmes. According to assessments conducted by UNHCR and other protection actors, internally displaced women and children are disproportionately vulnerable to SGBV. Unaccompanied girls and adolescents, single heads of households, child mothers, child spouses, and women and girls living with disability are most at risk. The existing culture of impunity coupled with weak justice system hampers prevention of and response to violations against women and children. Most survivors do not report SGBV incidents for fear of reprisals by perpetrators and stigmatization by their own communities, as well as having no confidence that the authorities will hold perpetrators accountable.North East Nigeria still faces immense humanitarian and protection challenges. An estimated 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities. COOPI has been addressing the needs of unaccompanied and separated children in Yobe since 2015 witnessing substantial gaps in SGBV prevention and response services and coordination mechanisms for effective service delivery. Based on the identified gaps, the project seeks to increase access to comprehensive and well-coordinated SGBV services to survivors in six LGAs of Yobe State - Damaturu, Geidam, Bade and Potiskum – and MMC in Borno State where COOPI holds strong relationship with key community actors and the State Ministry of Women Affairs (SMWA).Vulnerable girls, women and boys are lacking the fundamental protection of a caregiver, whether parents, extended family members or care professionals and are exposed to heightened risk of sexual violence, physical abuse and exploitation. Moreover, they have been subjected to grave violations with resulting psychosocial distress, including from abduction, exposure to sexual and gender-based violence, separation from or loss of family members, destruction of property, looting of food and livestock and forced displacement. As a result, the project’s community strengthening initiatives will directly engage community leaders and stakeholders to create a conducive environment for reintegration. The analysis of data showed that women and children under 18 continued to bear the brunt of this crisis. Child and forced marriage, physical assault, rape, survival sex, sexual assault and denial of resources were the major incidents (UNHCR 2019 Annual Report on Sexual and Gender-Based Violence Northeast Nigeria).

2 - Activities

1. Conduct a SGBV assessment through focus groups and key informant interviews with women, men, girls, boys and relevant community actors and groups;2. Establish and maintain a confidential individual file management system to register and track SGBV survivors, and record the assistance they require and receive;3. Training of SGBV focal points, community leaders, community groups on how to identify SGBV survivors and referral pathways;4. Provision of immediate support to survivors (information, counselling, psychosocial support and referral to emergency care especially for cases reporting within 72 hours and other specialised medical services);5. Provision of medium-term support to survivors and people at risk (PSS, assistance to access legal services, skill trainings and income generating activities);6. Mapping of existing SGBV prevention and response programmes and services in each target location and existing functional SGBV coordination mechanisms/structures;7. Monitoring the effectiveness of the referral mechanism and the care provided by the designated services to SGBV survivors reported in the target locations to ensure needs are met and accountability is strengthened;8. Training sessions and field coaching for Social Workers of the SMWA;9. Training of CBOs on SGBV response programmes and livelihood support;10. Training of teachers, as well as headmasters and school administrators, on SGBV prevention strategies in schools and the importance of engaging boys in prevention activities;11. Sensitization sessions with Imams/religious leaders and existing Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) around SGBV;12. Sensitization activities at community level, including focus group discussions with men, women, youth, boys and girls, teachers and educators within the existing Child Friendly spaces (CFS);13. Distribution of NFI Kits.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

COOPI staff will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. CSOs, caregivers and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistleblower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM will coordinate with each other in order to ensure that protection is mainstreamed at programming level.COOPI will put into place a protection system to guarantee children’ safety and security at the community level and at reunification sites. This includes: extensive sensitization which should favor community acceptance; family’s preparation ahead of reunification; community safety plans developed jointly with the communities of origin; providing children with skills and job placement opportunities to divert them from wanting to re-join armed groups. Generally, the close collaboration with Government authorities will enable the program to better manage external risks and to design more informed solutions to protect children.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will build on previous experience of monitoring and evaluation of protection, emergency and early recovery programs to ensure that the proposed project's strategy is appropriate to respond to context-specific needs as well as to demonstrate changes in outcomes to which the programme contributes. The monitoring system will include regular site visits, supervisory visits by the management team and/or technical staff, and community feedback and accountability mechanisms. It will be used to inform periodic management meetings in which the team will review data, identify trends, and propose changes to interventions when necessary.COOPI will establish a community feedback mechanism and Project Management Committees in alignment with guidance provided by the Protection Sector, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sectors, UNFPA, UNICEF and other donors. COOPI will ensure regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to capture their preferences, their respective priority needs and document barriers they face in accessing to safe and meaningful assistance and information. Dedicate protection and AAP officers will be recruited that speak the local languages and are gender balanced to facilitate communicating with communities. Dedicated Community Feedback assistants will be recruited for the help and feedback desks. They will be equipped with computers/tablets to reduce the risk of data mismanagement/breaches in data protection.
