Project Summary

Based on the success and learns learnt from the first year of the Emergency School Feeding
Programmes in Libya in 2019 [HLBY19‐EDU‐151894‐1], WFP will expand the complementary school meals programme as means to ensure children’s access to food in times of crisis and enhance diets through increased micronutrient intake. Assistance will take into account protection and safety, and where possible, contribute to their broader protection. In 2019, the school rations including nutritionally‐fortified date bars, which are culturally‐acceptable, programmatically‐feasible and can contribute to enhanced micronutrient intake among children, were actually provided to over 18,000 school-aged children against the original plan of 20,000 school‐aged children.
In 2020, WFP plans to reach 40,000 school-aged children in need for Education Sector's assistance, out of which 30,000 school-aged children will be prioritized for the humanitarian-oriented assistance under the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020. This programme could also provide food incentives to promote and encourage the enrolment of the children of the migrant populations in urban settings. The programme would review implementation arrangements to strengthen synergies with education and livelihood sectors and increase nutrition sensitivity, gender sensitivity, local procurement and community ownership. This would promote community infrastructure, rehabilitation, and reconstruction ﴾including schools and health centres﴿ as well as promote the reintegration of displaced populations and returnees, with special attention paid to the different needs of women and men.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2020, 122,970 school‐aged children are in need of education. School feeding remains one of the key priorities set under Libya Education Sector in order to establish a resumption of normalcy in children’s lives, especially for schoolchildren in the South and the areas hosting IDPs and returnees. The Libyan Ministry of Education has highlighted the problem of the triple burden of malnutrition which schoolchildren suffer from, i.e. undernutrition, micronutrients deficiency andoverweight/obesity. The situation surrounding schoolchildren has gotten more challenging in 2019. The crisis in Libya has negatively impacted public education services, causing challenges for schoolchildren to enjoy safe access to fully functional schools and quality education. WFP signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Ministry of Education for the partnership in implementing the emergency School Feeding programme for the targeted group of 20,000 schoolchildren in four districts in the South of Libya. WFP and the Ministry of Education geographically targeted four districts based on the criteria of high concentration of IDPs, food security, school infrastructures and access, and all of primary and secondary schools within the targeted districts were reached through the Emergency School Feeding Programme. WFP managed to assist some 18,000 schoolchildren at 58 schools with vitamin-/mineral-fortified date bars.

4 - Protection risk analysis

SAFETY - Child safety at risk during their travelling to/from schools; SAFETY - Poor storage of school meals could affect food safety; SAFETY - Widespread use of corporal punishment in schools;DIGNITY - The provided food commodity is not living up to children's expectations from what the term of "school feeding programme" means in local language; INTEGRITY - Children without legal documents are excluded from the education system;INTEGRITY - Emergency School Feedinng programme is limited to certain geographical areas, leading to concerns about impartiality.

5 - Mitigation measures

Measures mitigating SAFETY risks - Suspend school feeding during periods of high insecurity; Ensure that proper storage arrangements are in place at schools; Engage with teachers to ensure that corporal punishment is not used during the distribution. Measures mitigating DIGNITY risks - Adapt the terminology that is used to describe the programme. Measures mitigating INTEGRITY risks - Extend school feeding to informal education programmes for child migrants through coordination with communities, mayors, education sector members; Continue to engage with the Ministry of Education regarding the national school feeding strategy.
