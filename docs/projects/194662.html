Project Summary

Action Against Hunger has been operational in what is now South Sudan since 1985, responding to both chronic and acute humanitarian needs. AAH strategy is to addresses the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in children U5 by linking nutrition with WASH and FSL interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon communities’ resilience as well as prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Currently Action Against Hunger is implementing a WASH program under SIDA, GFFO, GAC, and ECHO activities in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap, and Jonglei states with established field offices in Malual-kon, Alek, Old Fangak, and Paguir Payam. In 2023, WASH projects will be implemented to ensure that host communities, IDPs and returnees have safe access to clean water and sanitation facilities with improved hygiene behavior. The proposed activities will improve the nutritional status of children U5 and pregnant and lactating women through an integrated WASH and Nutrition response aimed at breaking diarrhea – malnutrition vicious cycle. This proposed project will ensure the effectiveness of addressing malnutrition in the proposed target area by reducing disease burden in communities as well as availing safe, potable water for use in appetite tests at nutrition sites including monthly Water quality tests which will be carried out by our WASH team in both locations using our established laboratories.
To achieve the overall project objective and objectives of the HRP-2022/3, we will provide critical services to 41,320 individuals with gender segregation of 21,478 males, 19, 842 females. among them 16,471 under 5 children at both Nutrition/Health facilities and at the village level with 4 main activities which include:
1) Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition.
2) Integrate Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties).
3) Community member’s exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure.
4) Increasing knowledge of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. ACF will implement the above in the catchment areas of its nutrition facilities in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap and Jonglei states. Baseline KAP surveys will be conducted in all target areas to identify the WASH-related knowledge, attitude, and practices of the communities.
Detailed needs assessments will be conducted to assess community access to safe water sources and sanitation facilities. The implementation strategy will align with the needs identified in assessments and KAP surveys at the project start. Additionally, the ACF multi-sector emergency response throughout the country will include a WASH component to complement its nutrition work.
The proposed program strategy takes into account specific gender-related needs during surveys and activity implementation to ensure the equitable participation of all gender and age groups in the locations mentioned above.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continued to experience historical levels of severe and extreme humanitarian conditions mainly driven by climatic shocks (i.e., recurrent flooding) and sub-national violence in Jonglei and Warrap States. This severe humanitarian situation has culminated in mass displacement and high multi-sectoral needs across the states, which has grossly aggravated the humanitarian needs in the affected areas. As a result, an estimated 8.3 million people in South Sudan were in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. In addition, this has negatively impacted living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 6 million people due to inadequate access to clean water, sanitation and poor hygiene practices in 2021 and a raise of 6 million people per year is anticipated for the coming 2022 and 2023. Consequently, the decreased level of access to resources, logistical constraints due to rainy season road blockage, limited access and the uncertainty in the availability and prices of key WASH items in the local markets has impacted the ability for humanitarian agencies to respond to humanitarian needs in an efficient, effective and timely way. Rains are becoming less predictable, perennial rivers are becoming seasonal, and more water is being lost to evapotranspiration. These changes are increasing stress on existing limited water infrastructure and supplies. Support for WASH programs is necessary to reduce water-borne diseases, cut off the vicious cycle between diarrhea and malnutrition, and ensure that communities have safe and equitable access to water and sanitation facilities and practice good hygiene behavior. This is equally important in emergency response by Action Against Hunger (AAH)’s Multi-sectoral Emergency Teams in hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan like Jonglei State. Also, with the threat of COVID-19 in addition to common WASH-related diseases, WASH will play an important part in infection protection and control in Nutrition and Health facilities and communities' level in Warrap, Jonglei and NBeG States. Unusually severe flooding in 2021 exacerbates this situation in some parts of the country and is likely to have a negative short-term as well as medium-term impact. Finally, despite persistent needs, improved security and economic activity because of the peace agreement have warranted opportunities for transitional as well as emergency assistance.

2 - Project activities

The project has four aims. 1) Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected women, men, girls and boys. 2) Ensure that SAM and MAM children and their caregivers have access to WaSH services at the static and emergency nutrition sites, including safe water for drinking and conducting the appetite test. 3) Increase knowledge and practice of good WaSH behaviors among vulnerable and affected populations. 4) Address emergency WaSH needs through multi-sectoral emergency team (MET). First, in partnership with respective state water ministries, ACF assess and map existing functional and non-functional water points in the target areas, and update relevant databases. We will also analyze catchment communities’ practices for safe water sources and compliance with Sphere Standards for water access. Data will be used to strategize the rehabilitation of existing non-functional water sources and tinstallation of new ones, including boreholes, at targeted nutrition sites and catchment villages. In partnership with local state ministry and State Cluster Pump Mechanics Associations, pump mechanics will be identified and trained on borehole rehabilitation to ensure sustainability and ownership of the rehabilitated hand pumps. However, to ensure WaSH access at nutrition facilities, gender-segregated latrines will be constructed for caregiver families. The project will also provide a WaSH minimum package, adapted to the specificity of any related outbreaks, at targeted ACF nutrition facilities (safe sources of water, gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, and soap-equipped handwashing stations). Access to hygiene items and their proper usage is key to breaking the diarrhea-malnutrition cycle. As such, AAH will distribute hygiene kits to caregivers of SAM and MAM children at Nutrition facility level. Female sanitary items will be included in the kits for pregnant and lactating women and including adolescent girls. Caregiver families in nutrition facilities will receive regular hygiene promotion sessions, including instruction on the proper use of kits. The project aims to enable vulnerable and affected populations to maintain safe, sanitary and hygienic living, and increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices. As part of its sanitation strategy, ACF will launch CLTS/anti-ODF campaigns in target areas of Aweil East, Gogrial West and Fangak Counties to provide secure, dignified and gender-appropriate sanitation services. CLTS is the primary component of South Sudan’s Environment and Sanitation Strategy for SDG 6. Household self-construction of latrines ensures equitable access of women, which will further help in addressing GBV and protection issues at community level. Through PHAST and CHAST approaches, ACF will train and build the capacity of local communities and schoolchildren for improved hygiene behavior. Student Hygiene Clubs will be formed in targeted schools where children will be trained to practice and promote good hygiene behavior at school and community level. Children will work as Ambassadors of change, while their elders will be included through the PHAST approach. Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) tools will be used to motivate communities for proper handwashing at key times, safe water handling and drinking, environmental hygiene and household hygiene practices. ACF will also respond to COVID-19 and Wash-related disease outbreaks with appropriate WASH infection prevention and control, in coordination with other partners. Therefore, our countrywide multi-sector emergency team (MET) provides response to recurring acute malnutrition crises. As part of their mandate, the MET undertakes borehole rehabilitation, delivery of a minimum WASH package to nutrition facilities, hygiene promotion, hygiene kit distribution, and building capacity of local pump mechanics by training them on the techniques of proper handling and use of boreholes by the users. 5% will be through CVA.
