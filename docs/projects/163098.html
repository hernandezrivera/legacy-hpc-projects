Project Summary


The project aims to respond in a multi-sectorial approach to needs of people that have been affected by the humanitarian situation through different hazards (cyclone, cholera and drought) by ensuring that they have access to safe water, improved hygiene and sanitation practices and a means to practicing good hygiene in Chipinge urban and Harare City. In Chipinge urban and Harare City 58.3% and 66.7% respectively of households do not have access to water. Chipinge urban’s water challenges which had part of its water infrastructure damaged under Cyclone IDAI , 15 boreholes will be rehabilitated, 5 boreholes will be drilled, equipped with inline chlorinators in areas currently not being serviced by the towns reticulation system. The supply will be further augmented through rehabilitattation of 5 springs that have been previously protected in Gaza. Harare City which a fresh cholera outbreak in 2019 will also target the drilling of 5new boreholes with inline chlorination targeting Mbare, Highfileds and Glen Norah residence. At least 5 schools in Harare will have their water storage capacity increased or constructed in order to meet their water needs.
In both towns provision of water is expected to improve the sanitation situation by ensuring that flush toilets function. The good operation of sanitation facilities will be supported through provision of regular cleaning detergents and equipment to public and school toilets. The CHC approach will be used to as a way of improving hygiene practices by either refreshing or recruiting community health clubs at residence and schools level. Clubs will encompass everyone including women, men, the disabled, youth and the elderly. As a sustainability measure several trainings which will include water point management, water quality, and operators training will conducted. Disaster Risk Reduction structures from residents level to the Provincial level will be formed or resuscitated for enhancing preparedness in humanitarian situations.Distrubition of NFIs will be conducted with key cholera and hygiene messages
