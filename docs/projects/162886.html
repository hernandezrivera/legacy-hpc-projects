Project Summary

Prolonged conflict and instability in Darfur and the surrounding regions has caused mass displacement and necessitated the establishment of Internally Displace People (IDP) camps. The proposed project aims to intervene in Child protection sector which will focus on improving existing child protection networks, respond to the immediate needs of vulnerable children and promoting provision of protective environment for IDPs, returnees and host communities’ children in the four targeted localities (Darasalam, Tawila, Mellit and Kutum), who are placed on continuous insecurity and vulnerability due to Darfur conflict and emergency situation. Through community awareness raising about gender roles and relationships will be conducted to promote (aspirations for) gender equality by providing opportunities for women and men in community committees to discuss gender relations and to set their own a agenda for change. Plan has been working in Darfur since 2005 and since March 2011 Plan has been responsible for Zamzam camp coordination and management. This role includes, coordination, and mobilization of the camp residents ensuring that common policy frameworks, and humanitarian standards are applied.

1 - Needs

- In North Darfur, approximately 14,410 male and female children among the total 33,876 vulnerable people in the four targeted localities (Tawila, Kutum, Mellit and Darasalam), have experienced different types of violations, neglect, abuse and exposing to continuous vulnerability and insecurity in the past ten years. - The lengthy emergency and protracted displacement in the region has contributed in worsening the humanitarian situation particularly in areas of most presence of IDPs and returnees. Humanitarian agencies have been providing periodic emergency support to people of most need to save lives. However, some INGs, UN agencies and donors have already shifted some of their programs to recovery and early development interventions. This has resulted out from the improvement of security situation that will contribute positively in voluntary return, normalization of living methods towards self-production, self-resilience and dependency. In addition to encouragement of fund raising, most particularly from the donors who feel fatigue of supporting humanitarian programs without concrete lasting impact on the targeting population. - Project activities will be expanded and more focused on areas of voluntary return in addition to IDPs camps and host communities, where children are experiencing abuses, violations, arm recruitment, neglect and harassment

2 - Activities / Outputs

• Training of 300 CBCPNs members and community leaders on CR/CP, reporting and case management in Kutum, Malliet Tawilla and Darasalam localities. • Establishment and training of 16 community-based children clubs (160 girls and 160 boys) on CR/CP, leadership and decision making• Provision of equipment (seats, tables, cupboards.) and recreational materials for the newly established and trained community-based children clubs, as one in each of the targeted localities. • Conduct of 160 awareness sessions, as 20 per each of the targeted localities on CP issues, focusing on FGM and early child marriage• Provision of financial support for 4 CBCPNs (20 networks in total) monthly action plan in four targeted localities• Organization of 32 children competitions/ open cultural days, as 2 per each of the targeted location• Training for 20 Plan International Sudan and partners staff on safeguarding policy, code of conduct and do-no-harm approach• Conduct 20 sessions with communities on child labour and related risks• Establishment of 12 community-based multi-purpose centres (including child, child protection, women and youth activities) 72000 in total (20,880 girl, 18,000 boy, 18,000 women and 15,120 men).• Provide cash voucher program including NFIs and food support for 75 juveniles and children associated with their mothers

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Base interventions on gendered analysis of the causes and impacts of crises, the different need of young women and men, and design programs to meet those different needs, including collecting and having a regular analysis on SADDD, in order to target assistance towards those most at risk. • Look for opportunities to support young women and men in ways that contribute to the transformation of gendered power relations: lay the groundwork for gender equality and greater resilience to conflict and crises, by actively supporting women’s leadership and by challenging attitudes and beliefs that undermine women’s rights, including engaging with men and boys. • Promote the full participation and leadership of young women and men in all aspects of programming – from planning to evaluation. • Have gender-sensitive M&E system by developing gender sensitive indicators (quantitative and qualitative), as well as conducting regular tracking on the impact of the emergency response towards young women and men.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with group consultations with aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Above can only select one but will have multiple channels including face-to-face with community leaders, billboards, complaints box, and help desk during distributions. For Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
