Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to contribute to the emergency response to the conflict and drought-affected IDPs, returnees, and host communities. With the current conflict and drought situation in Amhara, Oromia and Afar all basic services, including health care are extremely affected and struggling to provide basic health services. It is also implied that health facilities are fully or partially affected due to conflict, which may prevent them from providing the full package of primary health care services. There are 926,416 IDPs and 181,735 affected host communities are looking for lifesaving health service access. The status of COVID-19 cases is also increasing because of weak prevention practices in the community. It is expected that with the current emergency transmission of COVID-19 is also exacerbated due to the possible interruption of COVID-19 prevention and control activities which is another humanitarian concern. To respond to this crisis and to provide lifesaving activities, IMC has selected priority activities that would respond to the emergency health needs in selected regions and corresponding woredas.
The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality as a result of poor access to basic Primary Health Care services through integrating COVID-19 prevention in the Amhara, Afar, and Oromia regions. To realize the required support MHNT will be deployed for the outreach service to have an important role to play to improve access to essential life-saving health care services for vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host communities. The existing PHCs services are also supported by deploying additional staff as a surge team. This can result in improved access to health and nutrition services and a reduction in morbidity and mortality. The project will plan to provide comprehensive priority services like basic life-saving primary health care services, strengthening surveillance and prevention services through various awareness creation modalities.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involved those concerned local and national actors (including government sectors office at all levels, community representatives, and crisis-affected communities) to gather information related to needs, gaps, and priorities of problems faced by the affected population during the project designing. During project implementation, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been also involving those actors since Grant Opening Meetings (GOM) and provided information (on the objectives, target groups, budget, and beneficiary selection criteria) to the crisis-affected community throughout the project implementation. In addition, the International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involving Zonal and Woreda Government sector offices (including Planning and Economic Development Commission (PEDCO), Women, Children and Youth Affair (WCYA), Health Bureau (HB), Water, Mineral and Energy Bureau (WMEB), and Disaster Risk Management Commission (ODRMC)) in joint project monitoring and supervision to monitor the status of the project implementation, achievement, and its relevance. Community representatives from crisis-affected communities also participated in the joint project monitoring and supervision and provided their feedback regarding the quality of the project as well as IMC Ethiopian staff behaviors. Furthermore, International Medical Corps has been organizing a final project evaluation to examine the effectiveness, targeting, relevance, and sustainability of the project through targeted, feasible, and useful performance evaluation. This final project evaluation has been carried out by regional government bodies, which signed a project agreement with International Medical Corps Ethiopia. These Signatories include the Regionals Plan and Economic Development Commission (PEC), Regional Women, Children and Youth Affair (RWYCA), Regional Health Bureau (RHB), Regional Disaster Risk Management Commission (RDRMC). Thus, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been involving concerned local and national actors throughout the project cycle management.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

International Medical Corps Ethiopia conducted community consultation with various groups of crisis-affected communities (including women, men, girls, boys, and people with disability) crisis-affected communities regarding how the beneficiaries view complaints mechanisms; how complaints are currently dealt with; how they would like to submit complaints to the organization; what might potentially prevent them from complaining, and how they wish to receive feedback about complaints. As a result, International Medical Corps Ethiopia identified appropriate feedback channels, and set up safe, accessible, and preferred CBFRM that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages in new proposed intervention areas. The feedback and complaints channels to be accessible for the most vulnerable group of the affected population, International Medical Corps Ethiopia has been set up multiple channels including a helpdesk, dedicated telephone, community volunteers, face-to-face meetings, community events, and complaints boxes, which increased the coverage of CBFRM in each service delivery siteFeedback and complaints have been collected on a weekly basis from various established CBFRM channels, and all complaints are reviewed by the Standing and Review Committee (SRC), with details and course of action are determined, and resolution of all feedback and complaints completed within 30 days from receipt. The decision and course of action taken for each complaint have been communicated to the complainant to close the communication loop. In addition, IMC Ethiopia has been incorporated the feedback and complaints received from crisis-affected communities and taken corrective action throughout the implementation of the project. The CBFRM data are monitored and evaluated on monthly basis, with realistic and agreed action plans developed to ensure beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback are incorporated and correction has been made throughout the implementation period of the project
