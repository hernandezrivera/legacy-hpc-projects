Project Summary

"Considering the crisis is multifaceted and protracted, FIVDB shall undertake a holistic approach to enhanced economic opportunities; improved provision of and access to nutritious food; NRM, and disaster risk reduction (DRR) that will synergistically contribute to the well-being of people living in Rohingya and Host communities. The project promotes women's empowerment through context-specific interventions driven by gender-inclusive agricultural interventions. For Rohingya camps, the project includes interventions including climate-smart homestead gardening and mushroom production, and composting training with input support. The activities shall aim to enhance food security by enabling the targeted participants’ access to diversified vegetables and improve their nutrition needs. The project shall encourage the beneficiaries to engage in producing value-added products including mushrooms, pickles, and fruit jams with the yielding from their gardens to promote economic empowerment, and self-reliance and increase their purchasing power through selling the products to camp markets and among other community members. In host communities, the major interventions shall include homestead gardening, composting, fisheries, livestock, and poultry rearing interventions with input support or start-up capital. The overall interventions shall target to improve access to diversified food of the targeted host community participants. The participants will be encouraged to undertake high-value crop production which they can sell in the market for economic profit. In coordination with the Department of Agricultural Extension, Department of Fisheries, and Department of Livestock Services extension staff, the project will provide intensive training and equip lead farmers and community vaccinators to disseminate skills and knowledge on improved agricultural practices to other community members. The project will focus on connecting the targeted participants with private sectors, traders, or WFP aggregation centers depending on the availability of the service providers and the convenience of the participants. The project shall adopt an eco-friendly agricultural approach by engaging participants in producing organic manure and pesticides for homestead gardening. The project shall undertake tree plantation activities in host communities as a natural resource management initiative. As a disaster risk reduction initiative, the project shall train the participants on reducing disaster risks i.e. cyclones, landslides, and floods, and introduce early warning systems. Alongside participants will be linked with Union Disaster Management Committees and Ward Disaster Management Committees to support disaster risk management.
It will work in close coordination and collaboration with all the relevant sectors including food security, nutrition, energy and environment, etc., NGO platform, local government, and all other relevant stakeholders. Community engagement has always remained the key priority of FIVDB and for this project also community participation will be ensured from design to the implementation phase. The overall program will aim to address gender inequality by increasing women’s participation in agricultural intervention and purchasing power. The project shall enable response and humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable individuals from female-headed households, households with persons with disabilities, marginalized groups, 3% persons with disabilities and households with LGBTQI+. The project participant’s data will be segregated maintaining SAAD criteria and the outcome and impact of the project will be assessed following OECD criteria. The project will ensure rigorous coordination with existing actors in the project intervention areas to avoid any duplication in project participant selection and response. FIVDB aims to include 60% of women as project participants and promote youth engagement in the interventions. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"More than 1 million Rohingya are currently living in 33 Rohingya camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf having a high dependency on humanitarian assistance for survival which is often unable to meet the basic demands of such a large population. (UNHCR, 2024). 80% of Rohingya households identified the need for better access to fresh food and the continuation of food assistance as critical to their survival (REVA-7 Report, 2024). 94% of households depend on food assistance, with 17% expressing dissatisfaction due to insufficient aid. 53% of female-headed households face barriers to income opportunities, further heightening their vulnerability (JMSNA,2023).The World Food Programme (WFP) has reduced food vouchers from 12 USD to 8 USD in 2023, further straining food security levels. Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) indicates that 58% of Rohingya households are food insecure, up from 50% the previous year, necessitating urgent interventions for access to nutritious food. Women bear a disproportionate share of the burden, with 93% of households employing consumption-based coping strategies. Many households also rely on in-kind transfers, with 46% borrowing food, highlighting a lack of sustainable food sources. The report also reveals that 93% of households have adopted coping mechanisms like asset selling or reducing meal sizes due to food scarcity. (RIMA, 2024). This restricts access to diverse and nutritious food sources, further perpetuating nutrition gaps, and potentially leading to malnutrition and related health issues including stunting, wasting, anemia, and impaired cognitive development. These health problems can have long-term consequences for the well-being of Rohingya refugees. The most vulnerable groups are identified as children, pregnant women and lactating mothers.Economic vulnerability is pronounced, with 65% of households in Cox’s Bazar spending below the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB), a significant rise from 38% in 2022. Female-headed households experience higher rates of inadequate food consumption (49%) compared to their male counterparts (41%), reflecting the broader systemic issues of access to resources and income-generating activities. Lastly, 80% of households have expressed a critical need for greater access to fresh food and continued aid, with over 80% of women of reproductive age suffering from micronutrient deficiencies. (REVA 7, 2024). In host communities of Cox's Bazar, food security remains a pressing issue, with 88% of households reporting an acceptable Food Consumption Score (FCS), while 12% face borderline food security, particularly in Palong Khali (29%) and Ratna Palong (27%) unions. To cope with food shortages, 55% of households resort to less preferred food options, and 40% borrow food from friends or relatives. Gender disparities are evident; 63% of female-headed households struggle to afford sufficient food compared to their male counterparts. 81% females face difficulties accessing food, and crisis coping strategies are more prevalent, with 14% selling assets and 17% cutting non-food expenses. Economic pressures are significant, as 62% of households report reduced purchasing power, affecting food access for 81%. Additionally, households with Persons with disabilities experience higher food insecurity, with 51% reporting access difficulties among Marginalized groups, which may include ethnic minorities, and the economically disadvantaged, typically have lower income levels, limiting their access to sufficient and nutritious food. (JMSNA, 2023). This underscores the urgent need for targeted nutritional interventions and support to enhance food security in the Rohingya and host communities.Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (J-MSNA) - Cox's Bazar, December 2023, FAO. (2024). FAO Resilience Index Measurement and Analysis II: RIMA II Report. WFP. Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA-7) Report, July 2024."

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project shall prioritize the safety and dignity of the affected population by conducting a thorough analysis of the community's protection context. This includes identifying vulnerabilities and risks faced by different groups, particularly those most at risk, such as women, children, and individuals with disabilities. The project will gather data from affected populations to understand their specific needs and contexts by utilizing participatory assessments. The analysis shall guide the project intervention strategies to minimize harm and enhance the well-being of the targeted participants. The project shall tailor outreach strategies to reach the most disadvantaged individuals including female-headed households, Vulnerable women and youth groups, persons with disabilities, gender-diverse groups, and marginalized populations. The project shall ensure that information about available services is widely disseminated and accessible in easier and more appropriate languages and mediums. Community engagement will be integral to the approach, as the project will involve local stakeholders in decision-making processes to foster trust and ensure that the interventions are relevant and effective. A safe, accessible, and functional CFRM will be established where 10% of randomly selected beneficiaries who have given consent will be surveyed to identify their overall satisfaction with the input provided. The project shall regularly monitor and evaluate activities, incorporating feedback to adapt and improve the services. Training on protection and safeguarding principles adhering to humanitarian standards ensured for all staff. The project shall rigorously collaborate with relevant actors to enhance protection, avoid duplication, and address risks. Additionally, all staff shall be equipped with the necessary knowledge of existing services within project intervention services to enhance coordination and overall response.

2.3 - Age & Disability

"The project will prioritize the inclusion of older persons and persons with disabilities by adhering to the Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older People and Persons with Disabilities, as well as the IASC Guidelines on Inclusion. FIVDB will implement targeted programming that aims to engage at least 3% of Persons with disabilities, ensuring their specific needs are addressed. The project will incorporate the Washington Group Questions (WGQ) enhanced set for effective identification and referral of persons with disabilities and will conduct regular accessibility audits to identify barriers in facilities and service points. Additionally, the project will provide training for staff and volunteers on inclusive programming, ensuring that all information regarding the project is accessible and that Persons with disabilities are actively involved in project activities. The project shall ensure necessary adjustments in the intervention modalities based on the specific needs of the identified persons with disabilities to ensure an inclusive program. Where possible the project shall provide porter services to people with specialized needs including older persons for assistance, as needed, and provide door-to-door service for women and girls with disabilities when necessary. Alongside the project will ensure accessible complaints and feedback mechanisms in all facilities. By integrating these elements, the project aims to empower Persons with disabilities and older persons, facilitating their meaningful participation in all project phases. The project shall collect and provide gender, Gender, Age, and Disability segregated data of all project participants and extremely vulnerable individuals as identified by each sector. The project shall ensure accessible information during awareness-raising sessions."

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is designed based on the findings from REVA 7, RIMA 3, JMSNA 2023, and other relevant assessments to accurately reflect the needs and priorities of the targeted communities. The project shall ensure regular consultations with affected populations to explain the beneficiary selection criteria, ensuring transparency and inclusivity. By involving community members in evaluations and sharing results in accessible formats, the project aims to foster a sense of ownership and trust between the project and the communities. All the assistance will be tailored based on the priorities and needs of the targeted participants followed by relevant assessments engaging affected communities. Implementing effective Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanisms (CFRMs) to gather feedback from beneficiaries regarding the adequacy and quality of the humanitarian response is a strategy of the project. Regular monitoring will be conducted to assess community perceptions, utilizing existing CFRM and relevant assessments. These insights will guide project adjustments and improvements, ensuring that the voices of marginalized groups—especially women, youth, gender diverse groups, and persons with disabilities—are addressed. This approach will not only enhance the project's responsiveness but also promote community empowerment. Furthermore, the project will ensure that all demographic groups are included in AAP planning and implementation prioritizing the specific needs of vulnerable women and youth, older persons, persons with disabilities, gender-diverse groups, and marginalized populations to ensure their representation in decision-making processes. By providing information and updates about changes in services or assistance in clear and understandable formats, the project will maintain transparency and encourage ongoing dialogue with the communities. All IEC will be available in English, Bangla, and Burmese transcription in accessible formats .

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The program is designed to equip communities with knowledge and skills for natural disasters and monsoon seasons, available DRR services by disseminating messages, and involving community members in response efforts. It also facilitates staff and outreach workers' effective participation in disaster response by providing information on damage and needs assessments, as well as social mobilization interventions, disaster risk reduction (DRR), and emergency preparedness. Moreover, the project adopts an integrated, cross-sectoral approach that recognizes the links between WASH (water, sanitation, and hygiene), site management, and DRR committee in natural resource management, all of which contribute to overall community resilience. FIVDB will ensure effective coordination among relevant government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP), ISCG’s operational partners, and sectors, along with the Energy & Environment Network (EEN). Alongside this, the project will coordinate with other humanitarian and development actors including camp DRR committees to ensure that climate adaptation and mitigation strategies are effectively integrated into all interventions. The project will refrain from any construction that could harm the environment and will discourage the use of plastic. All project staff will receive orientation on proper waste disposal and waste management practices. The project will conduct a disaster vulnerability assessment during facility selection and shall equip facilities with safety materials including fire extinguishers. FIDVB’s own DRR contingency plan aligned with ISCG’s guidelines for DRR will be adopted in the project. The project training modules will include Early warning system , DRR and environmental issues for project participants to improve their knowledge regarding DRR and other environmental issues

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes. The project emphasizes improving the ability of host and impacted communities to mitigate, adapt to, and recover from shocks. This includes establishing community DRR plans, which typically encompass strategies for responding to emergencies. The project includes components for monitoring and learning, which are crucial for adapting strategies based on evolving risks and the effectiveness of response measures. The EPR plans will be aligned with ISCG’s Mult Hazard Response Plan 2022, Standard Operating Procedures (e.g. cyclones and Fire SOP), National Anticipatory action protocols, and National Plans for Disaster Management (2021-2025). The project addresses the importance of coordination with humanitarian organizations to leverage resources and expertise in emergencies. During the project implementation, FIVDB will adhere to Sector-specific contingency and business continuity plans, EPR plans, and the EPR WG work plan to ensure effective coordination with Sectors, EPR WG members, and ISCG’s EPR focal point to address potential issues. FIVDB will ensure membership in the EPR WG to harmonize approaches and build synergy with overall EPR mechanisms.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

"Yes. The project emphasizes providing evidence-based training to youth aged 18-24 on climate-smart agricultural practices. This training aims to enhance their knowledge and skills in producing high-value, nutritious crops. Youth will be encouraged to take on leadership roles within their communities, promoting their active participation in gardening activities and decision-making processes. By supporting youth in establishing and managing homestead gardens, the project shall aim to create income-generating opportunities. The initiatives shall foster collaboration with local youth groups to strengthen social capital and build networks that can facilitate access to resources and services. The project will integrate gender-sensitive approaches to ensure that both young men and women have equal opportunities to participate in homestead gardening activities. Linkages to broader market systems in the project include connecting youth with local market and agricultural input suppliers to ensure they have access to necessary resources and support for sustainable gardening practices. The project shall apply IASC Youth Guidelines to ensure that the project is youth-responsive. Overall, the project shall create participatory approaches that actively engage youth in program design, implementation, and monitoring, ensuring their voices are heard. The project shall emphasize age and culture-appropriate youth engagement strategies to ensure youth empowerment and effective response."

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

"Yes. The project shall promote self-reliance and resilience by sustainable climate-smart gardening including composting and organic farming. The project shall supply seeds, tools, and fertilizers to enable refugees to start farming quickly. Also, the project shall create performs and linkages to connect project participants with local service providers and private sectors based on availability and participant’s convenience. The project shall also encourage the participants to produce value-added products including pickles, mushroom to enhance income opportunities. 4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project’s homestead gardening and agricultural activities in the host community align with the development initiatives and priorities outlined in the District Development and Growth Priorities of Cox’s Bazar (December 2022).The project addresses food security, a critical priority in Cox’s Bazar, by promoting homestead gardening, which increases local food production and nutritional status among households. By supporting sustainable agricultural practices, the project contributes to local economic growth, fostering self-employment and entrepreneurship within the host community. .Training and capacity-building initiatives for local farmers support the district's goals of enhancing agricultural productivity and empowering local communities. The project enhances community resilience against shocks by diversifying income sources and improving livelihoods, crucial in a region facing challenges like the Rohingya refugee crisis.
