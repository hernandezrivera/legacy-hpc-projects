Project Summary

The Lotus Flower is collaborating with Days for Girls, an international NGO that advocates and promotes sustainable solutions for female menstrual health, and directorate of Health in Kurdistan Region of Iraq, to implement a menstrual health education program to girls between 12-16 years old and women of child-bearing age who are currently living in the internally-displaced camps within the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. The Lotus Flower will conduct girls’ health educational sessions at local schools within the camp and upon completion, distribute DfG’s discreet menstrual kits to all attendees. We aim to empower young girls and women through health education and, as a result, mitigate social stigmas so the girls can continue their education.
A 2015 UNICEF/WHO assessment reported that at least 500 million girls and women lack adequate facilities for managing their periods. Menstrual health is a topic that affects women and girls in all corners of the globe; it has a long-term impact on education, economics, public health and societal norms. However, social taboos, inadequate facilities, poverty, and limited health education continue to mitigate what resources are readily available. It is even harder for women and girls who are fleeing violent conflict and currently live in IDP or refugee camps. Women and girls miss out on educational and economic opportunities when they do not have the resources they need on a monthly basis. The Lotus Flower is partnering with Days for Girls (DfG), an international NGO that is committed to providing girls with sustainable menstrual care solutions and health education in order to evolve how women and girls see themselves and how they are seen by their community. Effective hygiene solutions empower women and girls to thrive in society by restoring dignity and enabling mobility and opportunity.
Target Area & Beneficiaries
Currently, the only DfG program in the Middle East is located in Lebanon. We want to expand this program to be the first one in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. Many girls are from conservative areas where discussing a girl’s monthly period is considered taboo and subsequently, are surprised when it happens in their early teens. Because of this, they are not sure how to deal with it and are ashamed to ask or purchase supplies. Our pilot program will be in the Rwanga Community camp, an internally displaced camp located in Duhok Governorate in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. There is a total of 6,905 women and girls living within the Rwanga Community camp, including 769 girls between 12-16 years of age and within this demographic, 64% of these girls are attending 3 local schools within the camp without adequate menstrual health resources. Following the completion of the pilot program, we will apply to implement this program in other nearby camps.
Activities
Our Regional Project Manager has completed DfG’s online training and 10 female members of the Lotus Flower Centre. Training will approximately take 1-2 weeks and once complete, the newly trained members will build the DfG kits and do outreach to the local schools to educate the girls regarding menstrual health and provide DfG Kits for all attendees.
The Goal of the Project:
We aim to provide menstrual health education and distribute DfG kits to all of the 500 girls attending school in the Rwanga Community camp. Following this, we aim to reach 1,000 women and girls in each camp, reaching 1500 women and girls in total. By doing this, young girls will have the tools they need to continue their education without physical restrictions.
Outcomes
• Purchase or produce 1500 DfG kits
• Conduct menstrual health awareness sessions in the school
• Distribute all 1500 kits to girls between the ages 12-16 current attending the local schools within the Rwanga Community camp and to women of child-bearing age

Activities

Our Regional Project Manager has completed DfG’s online training and will recruit 10 volunteer female members of the Lotus Flower Centre. Training will approximately take 1-4 weeks and once complete, the newly trained volunteers will build the DfG kits and do outreach to the local schools to educate the girls regarding menstrual health and provide DfG Kits for all attendees. The staff of Lotus Flower will conduct a camp assessment to collect the data required and see the number of young girls in each school and explain the project for them to see the number of interested girls who would like to take part in this program. This is the first phase of the program and will take two months for training, procurement of the hygiene kits, camp assessment, and mobilization within the camp. This phase will run from early January 2019 through early March 2019. By conducting outreach through our existing women’s center and door-to-door, we will aim to target 40% of beneficiaries to be women of child-bearing age (WCBA) to reach the necessary population. The second phase of the program will be the hygiene training sessions where each girl will receive their own hygiene kit at the end of the class. We will host 3 sessions per week with 25 participants each. We will reach 300 young girls every month and the training will continue for 5 months in total. This phase will run from March 2019 through July 2019 in order to reach 1,500 women and girls. The program will continue to follow up with additional awareness sessions for these young girls on Health to make sure they have items they need and give them opportunity to ask questions they need the answers for. This can be done with the rest of the community even those who didn't participate to ensure everyone has access to information about Period and slowly removing the shame and stigmas of talking about it and will include women and girls of all child bearing age. By the completion of the 7-month-long program, we aim to reach 700 girls between the ages of 12-16 and 700 women of child-bearing age.
