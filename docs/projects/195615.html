Project Summary

The project is one of the yearly main projects CARE implements from Turkey as a cross-border response in NW Syria. The project aims to provide emergency response activities and seasonal winter assistance in NW Syria for the most vulnerable people amongboth the IDPs and host communities. The project covers three main activities as follows:
1. Providing seasonal winter assistance through 2 modalities; 1) cash for winter and 2) winter commodity vouchers
2. Providing NFIs kits for the IDPs in NW Syria camps and conducting an emergency response for new emergencies like new displacement waves or natural disasters.
3. Providing multi-purpose cash (MPCs) for the IDPs and host communities under new emergencies.

3 - Needs

The Syrian conflict, now in its twelfth year, continues to drive new displacement and creates an ongoing humanitarian emergency. NWS, with a population of 4.4M people, hosts 2.8M IDPs - the highest number of IDPs in Syria - many of whom have experienced repeated and protracted displacement, and require immediate humanitarian assistance. Extreme weather and near economic collapse, coupled with COVID-19, compound this displacement and have created huge multi-sectoral challenges for households (HH). The rapid depreciation of the Syrian Pound (SYP) and accompanying inflation has eroded HH's purchasing power.Furthermore, during winter tents in IDP camps are insufficient to protect families from the poor weather conditions and fall apart or are damaged, and CARE’s 2021-22 winterization needs assessments found winter clothes, stoves, and fuel for heating as priority shelter needs.Moreover, with the growing need for essential items among IDPs and limited support being provided, it is critical to continue supporting IDPs with NFI items, particularly as they settle in areas with market access challenges.

4 - Implementation of activities

1. Winter assistance: Due to the increased winter season needs, CARE will support 100,000 individuals (60,0000 F / 40,000 M) most vulnerable IDP and host community individuals (20,000 HHs, targeting elderly, female-headed, and child-headed HHs) with additional winter cash assistance. Each HH will receive $150 USD, which will be sufficient for purchasing winterization essential items. The winter assistance will be provided through 2 modalities one is cash for winter and the second is winter commodity vouchers.2. NFIs kits: CARE will continue to distribute in-kind emergency NFI Kits (15,000 NFI kits), reaching 15,000 newly displaced HHs (75,000 individuals) and vulnerable IDPs and returnees, wherever they are located – informal camps, communal facilities, or residential buildings (Cluster guidance). To ensure cost-effectiveness and the flexibility and speed of the response. 3. MPC: Provide multipurpose cash assistance (MPCA): CARE will provide a one-off MPCA distribution of $150 per HHs (cash in hand) to 50,000 individuals (35,000 female and 25,000 male with10% PWSNs) new and current displaced individuals (10,000 HHs), across Aleppo and Idleb, providing these beneficiaries with the flexibility to choose the items that meet their basic emergency food and non-food (NFI, shelter, and WASH) needs with dignity from local markets.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

While cash interventions can increase women’s empowerment and decision-making, they can also shift HH power dynamics potentially causing retaliation from men who are concerned about losing their traditional role as breadwinners/heads of HH. This can lead to an increase in various forms of GBV, particularly intimate partner violence (IPV). Cash distribution points may lack gender-sensitive planning in terms of locations, routes, and times or maybe without the presence of female staff. Women with cash may also be more vulnerable to attack.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Renewal of cross-border resolution: The current UN Security Council Resolution (UNSCR) 2585 expires on 10th Jan 2023. Non-renewal would have a significant impact on our activities and other essential services being run by UN agencies. CARE has developed a draft UNSCR 2585 non-renewal contingency plan and action plan which provides details on CARE’s planning efforts, scenario forecasting, and options to immediately adapt and scale-up programming to address related gaps and needs.2. Security monitoring: Security levels do not prevent implementation. CARE contiuously monitors the NWS security situation through Cluster partners and the UN Access WG and has proposed a flexible approach to shift locations, activities, and modalities, as required.3. Humanitarian access: CARE and its IPs will be able to operate in the targeted areas. CARE’s coordination with stakeholders, participation in NWS Clusters, and its neutrality, will enable CARE to monitor any context changes and ensure to continue to reach these communities.4. Population movements: Displacement may continue during the program. Activities will therefore have maximum flexibility across sectors and modalities to ensure vulnerable individuals are reached and that our response takes these conditions into account.5. Local markets and liquidity: Local markets remain open, functional, and accessible, with sufficient availability and liquidity. CARE will use alternative modalities, if necessary.
