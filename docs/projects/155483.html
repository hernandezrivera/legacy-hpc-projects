Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of risks and to respond to protection needs including Gender Based Violence (GBV) through the provision of quality principled assistance for 14,050 (6,463 females; 7,587 males) refugees in Tongogara Refugee Camp. The project recognizes that many refugee families with the anticipated El Nino drought conditions which threatens their food security status might end up resorting to harmful coping strategies that particularly impact women and children as well as the elderly and persons with disabilities. The project intends to strengthen inter-sectoral cooperation to improve protection monitoring working with Refugee Community Protection Committees (Child Protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence committees) and community based committees. The project will aim to mitigate GBV which becomes more acute during the drought crisis. Underpinning the response are key protection principles prioritized by the IASC in all humanitarian activities: 1) ‘do no harm’: preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities that can increase people’s vulnerability to physical and psychosocial risks; 2) equality: ensuring affected refugees have meaningful access to impartial assistance and services in proportion to need and without any barriers or discrimination, paying special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services; 3) feedback and participation by the population of concern in a manner consistent with IASC guidelines on “Accountability to Affected Populations”: ensuring there are appropriate mechanisms through which affected people can provide feedback on the adequacy of humanitarian initiatives and channel any concerns and complaints; 4) participation and empowerment: supporting the development of protection and resilience capacities and assisting affected civilians to access basic services and goods- including food, shelter, water and sanitation, education and healthcare in cooperation with the government. TDH and UNHCR working with the Department of Social Welfare will ensure Gender Based Violence (GBV) risk mitigation measures and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and complaint mechanisms are systematically mainstreamed into all sectors of the humanitarian response in Tongogara Refugee Camp. GBV interventions will provide survivor care and support.

Needs

GBV and other protection risks may become more acute in the wake of the anticipated El Nino drought in Tongogara Refugee Camp. GBV has a greater impact on women and girls than on men and boys, linked to their subordinate status in society and their greater vulnerability to violence, though it is important to note that men and boys may also be victims of gender-based violence, especially sexual violence. The project will aim to prevent and respond to the different forms of GBV which become more acute during a crisis and these include;• Sexual violence, including sexual exploitation/abuse and forced prostitution. • Domestic violence. • Trafficking. • Forced/early marriage. • Harmful traditional practices such as female genital mutilation (FGM). Difficulties involved in accessing services especially for new arrivals will need to be addressed to reduce the risk of GBV.

Activities

Activities will include;• Training and awareness raising of humanitarian staff and refugee communities to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)• Establishment of identification and complaint mechanisms and setting up community child safe guarding systems in Tongogara Refugee Camp• Protection, human rights, reporting and referral pathway for GBV survivors capacity building workshops. These workshops will specifically target Community Protection Committees (Child Protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence committees) and community based committees (Disability, Wise Councils, Shelter Committee, Water Point Committees, irrigation scheme committee (16),); religious and country leaders (42), teachers (75 from ECD, primary and secondary schools), Fosters parents, Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), Health workers• Community mobilisation for the right to protection, improving relationships with authorities, expanding access to referral services and advocacy. Sensitisation tools (posters, radio broadcasts) are used to encourage dialogue within communities.• Training of medical staff in screening SGBV cases and provision of Psychosocial support • Medical Care for SGBV survivors including targeted psychosocial support • Provision of Post Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) kits to the Refugee Clinic • Train 21 Psychosocial promoters/peer educators on psychosocial helping skills for refugees• GBV case management, psychosocial support and other activities to care for survivors of GBV• Provide and promote the utilisation of GBV response services, increasing the capacity of partner staff to understand, coordinate and refer GBV survivors to relevant services and strengthen confidentiality and cultural sensitivity in the delivery of services.• Community workshops teaching couples practical skills to strengthen healthy family relationships, manage stress and anger and resolve conflicts peacefully• Separate women and men’s action group discussions and awareness-raising efforts, including poster discussions, dramas and campaigns• Producing PSS IEC material i.e flyers , audio visual materials on PSS counselling and information dissemination • Setting up Adult learning Spaces (languages school, legal systems, small business startups) • Facilitate protection coordination meetings in Tongogara Refugee Camp f on for Children and women survivors • Facilitate the provision of safe shelter to reduce GBV
