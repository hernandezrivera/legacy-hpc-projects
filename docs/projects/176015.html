Project Summary

The protracted armed conflict since December 2013 has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population in South Sudan, further stretching their coping capacity. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. The people of South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. Because of the weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks, it is estimated that in 2021 4.9 million South Sudanese will have their health and wellbeing impacted. The health facilities which are operational face challenges of delivering the complete basic package of health-care services. The absence of services means that cases needing emergency obstetric care, as well as tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS and mental health issues go largely untreated, causing increased morbidity and mortality. Through the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan, World Vision International (WVI) South Sudan will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Additionally, the emergency health intervention WVI intends to implement will focus on sexual gender based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Owing to the high mortality rate of under 5 children (90.7/1000), maternal mortality rate (789 deaths/100,000 live births) in South Sudan, World Vision will provide also maternal and child health interventions, mainly skilled birth attendance, prevention of post -partum hemorrhage, ante natal care, post-natal care, basic and comprehensive emergency neonatal and obstetrical care (EMNOC), Sexual and Reproductive Health/ family planning service and Expanded Programme Immunization (EPI) and quality management of Severe Acute Malnutrition with medical complication.
World Vision will ensure that there is continuum of case for under 5 with complicated SAM so that they have access to OTP and TSF facilities run by nutrition partners, including World Vision who is a provider of Community Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practice. World Vision will ensure that people with disabilities and mental disorders have priority access to emergency health services and are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks, malaria seasonality. The emergency lifesaving response World Vision will provide will be done in line with the recommended health cluster emergency clinical services package for primary healthcare units, primary healthcare centers, county hospital, mobile medical team, and Integrated Community Case Management (ICCM). These interventions will be in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health’s policies and guidelines, namely basic package of health and nutrition services, Boma Health Initiative, sexual and reproductive health, immunization guidelines and health policy of the Republic of South Sudan.
This project plans to reach 90,250 people with a budget of USD 3,000,000 over one year.

1 - Needs

The protracted armed conflict since December 2013 has resulted in multiple, acute, and chronic needs among the conflict affected population in South Sudan, further stretching their coping capacity. Two years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people. The country is characterized by lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services, which are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. The people of South Sudan have limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding and an underdeveloped health care system. Because of the weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks, it is estimated that in 2021 4.9 million South Sudanese will have their health and wellbeing impacted. This is a 36 per cent increase from the 3.6 million people in 2020. Only 7.5 percent of the 1,763 reporting health facilities provide full package of basic health services. Vast inequities in geographical spread and coverage of health services due to inadequate health infrastructure, human resources, funding and essential commodities for health implementation have resulted in poor health outcome. Health situation has further worsened due to last year’s intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. According to South Sudan Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response System, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64 per cent) and mortality (45 per cent). The absence of basic health care including vaccination services has resulted in low immunization coverage throughout the country, increasing the risk of vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks. According to WHO and UNICEF estimates of immunization coverage from 2019, more than half of children are not fully immunized and the transmission of vaccine-preventable diseases such as measles continues unabated. Through the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan, World Vision International South Sudan will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL activities. Additionally, the emergency health intervention World Vision intends to implement will focus on sexual gender based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR) and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Through this proposed project, WVSS will aim to achieve the following Humanitarian Response Plan Specific Objectives:- Reduce morbidity and mortality, as well as suffering from protection threats and incidents, of the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5.- Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level 4 and 5.
