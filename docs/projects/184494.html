Project Summary

Boys and girls continue to endure multiple protection risks such as family separation, sexual and gender-based violence (GBV), child recruitment, mental and psychosocial distress.
Preliminary findings of a UNICEF study on drivers of child recruitment show that Al-Shabaab explicitly targets children through forced recruitment and abduction. Rape and sexual violence remain a threat to children’s survival. Sexual violence increased by 35% when compared to the same period last year.
This project would like to respond first to the problems of sexual exploitation and abuse present in all emergency interventions. UNICEF has carried out an in-country assessment on SEA, and the biggest gap is the establishment of safe complaints mechanisms available to communities.
Secondly the project will address the root causes of GBV in Emergencies through evidence based social norm change programmes; mitigates the risk of GBV occurring in camp and IDP concentration points; and provides multi-sectoral services to survivors of GBV. The three core components of the programme will be implemented in key accessible areas of Somalia in all Federal Member states and engages UNICEF's network of civil society partners who will continue to benefit from capacity building support delivered through UNICEF's local, regional, international team of GBV experts. The purpose of the project is to ensure continued expansion of lifesaving services whilst at the same time undermining the structural causes of GBV. Women, girls and boys accessing GBV risk mitigation, prevention through targeted norm change activities and response ; Men, women, boys and girls can access a safe complaints mechanism; we will improve the safety of IDP camps and concentration points decreasing the likelihood of SGBV for more than 1 million displaced people;

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1 Train and strengthen the capacities of service providers on the management of SEA cases Activity 1.2 Set up a safe SEA complaint and response mechanisms for the benefit of communities Activity 1.3 Disseminate SEA complaint mechanisms to communities Activity 2.1 Provision of age, sex and culturally sensitive protection services. Activity 2.2: Training of key service providers in provision of survivor friendly GBV services (include training on the Clinical Management of Rape protocols, provision of psychological first aid, training in case management and improved policing through training of police officers) Activity 2.3: Strengthen coordinated protection responses through GBV Working Group and information sharing mechanisms among humanitarian, child protection and GBV actors Activity 2.4 : Broad based dissemination of protection messages via multimedia channels Activity 2.5: Conduct a safety audit in key IDP hosting sites to assess improvements, lessons learnt and recommendations from previous years Activity 2.6: Provide integrated GBV in Emergency guidelines training to humanitarian workers from WASH, Shelter, Camp Layout, Education, Nutrition and Health clusters. Activity 3.1: Scaling up of community-based protection system through Communities Cares to increase community-based referrals of survivors of abuse, decreases stigmatization and reduces the vulnerability of women and children. Activity 3.2: Conduct community mobilization and sensitization campaigns that reach and engage community members through the Communities Cares Community Discussions and Action Plans community dialogues, house to house visits, interactive radio and SMS campaigns, public events and trainings are undertaken by community leaders to change harmful norms

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

As in all GBV programmes, the risk to survivors of further stigmatization, abuse or isolation is high if they access Professional support services. In order to mitigate against this risk we ensure partners apply fundamental principles in the delivery of services ensuring absolute confidentiality (in both consultation as well as record keeping); ensure survivors are given full power and control through the case management and care process through the application of informed consentprocedures. This is monitored by UNICEF and capacity support is provided on a rolling basis to all partners. Regarding SEA cases, the complaints mechanisms will be confidential to avoid reprisals on the beneficiaries. Free hotlines will be set up, complaints mechanisms addressing directly to UNICEF or the interagency complaints mechanism will also be set up. Professional United Nations investigation teams (UNICEF and other agencies) will be used. All partner organizations will be assessed on their capacity to set up complaint mechanisms as well as on the support offered to beneficiaries. Finally, all partner staff will be trained on SEA and consequences and will be subject to binding contractual conditions.
