Project Summary

The project is designed to build the resilience and coping abilities of farmers from 8 villages of Jerusalem and Salfit governorates, affected by restrictions linked to the coercive environment in accessing and cultivating their lands. It will increase their production capacity through land rehabilitation activities and through specific support to olive farmers whose lands are located in areas requiring prior coordination with Israeli authorities during both ploughing and harvesting period. The project will stay in strict contact with MoA for planning and implementation. Additionally, the project will include targeted advocacy activities in order to advocate for the rights of farmers affected by settler violence and access restrictions.
It will therefore contribute to the Country Strategic Objective 2, “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL” and 3 "Strengthen the ability of acutely-vulnerable Palestinian households to cope with protracted threats and shocks ". The project will also specifically contribute to the FSS objective 1 “The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected” and address the FSS main activity 1 “Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and essential infrastructure for farmers, herders breeders, fishers, and small scale food processors including promotion of technical capacities and small scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience.

Needs

The cluster partner conducted a field study on the socio-economic situation of WB farmers from 09/16 to 01/17, collecting qualitative data through FGD and quantitative data through questionnaires. In 2018, PUI conducted an impact survey on 5 years of support to farmers during the olive harvest.Based on OCHA data (2017), more than 10 million olive trees are cultivated on approximately 86,000 hectares out of 183,000 hectares of land for agriculture in the oPt. Access restrictions imposed on lands located in the vicinity of settlements, closed military zones or behind the Barrier hinder essential agricultural activities to be implemented throughout the agricultural calendar. They affect women headed households even more strongly, as land rehabilitation activities require extra manual labour and hiring daily workers amounts to additional expenses. Villages targeted in the NE part of Jerusalem are facing a protracted humanitarian crisis due to Israeli restrictions and the construction of the barrier wall that separated them from Jerusalem and restricted their access to over 32,500 dunums of land. In Salfit, Deir Istya, Kifl Hares, Hares and Qarawa Bani Hasan are facing similar constraints due to the strong settlement enterprise in the area, which directly affected around 49900 dunums. These conditions are worsened by the system of Israeli administration over land as over 90% of the land designated for these villages is under full Israeli civil and security control. Lack of construction and maintenance of agricultural assets including agricultural roads, water cisterns, retaining walls and the planting of new seedlings impedes the utilization of these lands. Settler violence (SV) including harassment, trespassing, property damage and casualties further deter farmers from accessing their lands. Impact of Israeli occupation and deterioration of the economic situation in the WB has contributed to the high rates of unemployment especially among the youth. According to PCBS, these villages face a 35% unemployment rate among its labour force (54% among the youth). Women labour force participation rate in these communities does not exceed 19%. Villages’ proximity to settlements make them highly vulnerable to SV and military operations. Based on OCHA PoC database, Israeli SV against Palestinians has been on the rise since the beginning of 2017. The rise has even worsened since the beginning of 2018, with 186 incidents resulting in Palestinian casualties or property damage were reported till August, representing a 86% increase compared to the 2017 monthly average. This situation is even more alarming in a context of impunity for settler crimes and misdemeanors (only 8% of investigation files lead to prosecution, Yesh Din). Risks of SV and IF operations further intimidates from accessing and cultivating their lands. Land cultivation not only is a source of income but can also contribute to preventing land take-over. In Palestine, between 80,000 and 100,000 families are said to rely on olives and olive oil for primary or secondary sources of income, and the sector employs large numbers of unskilled labourers and more than 15% of working women. The presence of settlements restricts access to land for cultivation purposes. Approx. 90 communities own land within or in the vicinity of 56 Israeli settlements and settlement outposts (OCHA 2017) Incidents linked to SV escalate just before and during the OH season. This crucial time of the agricultural calendar is particularly challenging for farmers owners of lands located in ARAs requiring prior coordination with the Israeli authorities. Most farmers supported by PUI in 2017 were granted 2 to 5 days by the ICA and challenged by changes in the days, late granting of permits, limitations on tools and olive transportation allowed from and to the fields. 100% reported the following type of support as priority goals: provision of daily workers, provision of agricultural tools and protective presence.

Activities

Activity implementation will be carried out in close collaboration with the registered cluster partner's network of farmers committees. Past work of PUI and the registered cluster partner in targeted villages will ease with Village Councils and Community Eyes Watch implemented to monitor and alert on incidents related to settler violence. 1. Land rehabilitation in 8 targeted communities Land rehabilitation will be carried out for 500 dunums (200 in Salfit / 300 in Jerusalem), including building of retaining walls, rehabilitation of existing cisterns and construction of new small cisterns if needed, ploughing, cleaning and planting both seedlings and seeds. Rehabilitation of agricultural roads for 10 km in Salfit / 15 in Jerusalem during the project cycle. All rehabilitation works will be implemented directly by the farmers, with whom MoUs will be signed. The project will ensure women’s participation; women land owners will be actively called upon to apply for land rehabilitation activities and their specific needs will be taken into account. Women will be specifically targeted through the scoring system used for beneficiary selection, therefore encouraging women's land ownership (Palestinian women own less than 7% of private lands) and activity in the agricultural sector). Farmers will be introduced to intercropping techniques that will help them generate food and revenue from their lands; this will be ensured through the supply of local seeds to address the issue related to dependency on imported seeds. The project will foster the use of local seeds adapted to the area, ensuring better productivity and tolerance against drought and pests. 2. Support during both olive ploughing (spring) and harvest (autumn) seasons to farmers whose lands are located in access restricted areas, that needs prior coordination granted by the ICA (inside or close to settlement boundaries) or in dangerous areas exposed to settler attacks.a. Human resources support to farmers to accelerate ploughing and harvesting activities within the granted number of coordination days. The human resources will be either provided through cash for work for daily workers or through volunteers when possible. Daily workers will be identified among most vulnerable women and men in need of work. Selection criteria for supported farmers will include vulnerability to protection threats, size of land requiring coordination and own capacities to carry out the needed agricultural activities within the coordination period.b. Protective presence ensured by project team and volunteers to deter settlers from approaching and attacking farmers during agricultural work, and providing safer working conditions.c. Provision of agricultural inputs to most in needs farmers PUI and partner will coordinate with PDCO to ensure security and information on coordination dates.3. Advocacy activities aiming to address protection concernsAdvocacy initiatives will be carried out to complement operational activities with actions aiming at addressing the protection concerns. In coordination with other members of the Protection Cluster and the Settler Violence Core Group, the two organisations will articulate a narrative linking the specific vulnerabilities of these communities with the ongoing violations of IHRL and IHL in these areas. The settler violence related incidents will be presented in a larger frame of settlement enterprise and linked to the periods of the agricultural calendar that increase the exposure to such attacks. This narrative will be used to raise awareness of such issues and to engage with the concerned duty bearers and third States in order to stop such violations and reduce further risks. Outputs will include factsheet, photo story and case studies of affected households.The project will last beyond its first year of implementation in order to provide similar assistance to other communities facing similar threats and vulnerabilities.

Indicators

PUI and the registered cluster partner M&E teams will develop a monitoring and evaluation plan at the beginning of the project to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organizations and between them.• Monthly monitoring visits will be conducted to follow up the progress of project activities and to ensure that the activities are being implemented in accordance with the work plan developed at the beginning of the activity. Written report summarizing the main findings will be issued after each monitoring visit.• Introductory and information meetings with targeted population and community representatives will be arranged to provide accessible and timely information on organizational procedures, types of project activities, beneficiary selection process, criteria of selection, etc.• A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up to make sure that the opinions of target population is heard and taken into consideration. Complaints regarding the beneficiary selection process or suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to.• Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be carried out to assess the impact of project activities against baseline survey results.PUI indicators:80% of farmers in the targeted villages are able to finalize the agricultural work within the coordination periodsBy the end of the project, 80% of farmers benefiting from land rehabilitation activities are satisfied regarding the quality of the in-kind assistance provided75% of farmers benefiting from protective presence report an improved feeling of safety during coordination days.Please see section below for cluster specific indicators.Adoption of further indicators will be investigated under the guidance of the FSS, upon availability of resources and consolidation of good practices.
