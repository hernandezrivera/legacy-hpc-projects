Project Summary

This project aims to support the SNFI response to the immediate needs of the most vulnerable displaced populations in northwest Syria through the (1) distribution of essential non – food items (NFIs) and (2) improve the basic infrastructure and shelter conditions in and out of camps.
The distribution of NFI activities include:
i. Provision of essential NFI to at least 30,500 households (at least 183,600 individuals) in Northern Syria under the IOM direct distribution and rapid response mechanisms (RRM) in coordination with SNFI and CCCM Clusters (Sub activity 1.1.1).
ii. Providing procurement, storage and logistics support to the humanitarian partners operating inside Syria under the single source procurement pipeline (SSP) targeting around 40,000 kits to reach around 240,000 individuals (Sub activity 1.1.1)
iii. Provision of at least 17,000 winter NFI kits and 102,000 winter clothing kits as complementary to the NFI package through in – kind and voucher modality reaching at least 102,000 individuals (Winterization) (Sub activity 1.2.1)
iv. Conduct pilot activities of multi-purpose cash assistance to the newly displaced families targeting around 1,800 households (at least 10,800 individuals) in the IDP locations where the needs are high and MPC is feasible considering the security, safety and other associated risks (Sub activity 1.1.1)
The basic infrastructure and shelter response will be done through:
i. Replacement of 5,000 damaged family tents with new ones (Sub activity 1.3.2)
ii. Ensuring 15,000 family tents are available to members of the CCCM and SNFI clusters for the contingency stock under the rapid response mechanisms (RRM) (Sub activity 1.3.2)
iii. Provision of 1,000 Refugee Housing Units in IOM managed planned camp (Sub activity 2.1.2)
iv. Rehabilitation of 2,000 unfinished shelter units (Sub activity 1.4.2)
v. Repair of 3,000 damaged shelter units (Sub activity 2.1.1)
vi. Repair of 1,200 damaged shelter units in collective centres (Sub activity 1.4.1)
vii. Improving site infrastructure and facilities of 30 informal settlements. Infrastructure works include graveling of roads, graveling of tents ground as well as construction of drainage canals and culverts. (Sub activity 1.3.2)
viii. Winterization of 12,000 family tents through internal thermal insulation, external weatherproofing and ground raising and levelling (Sub Activity 1.2.2)
For the basic infrastructure and shelter component, the project will target 256,000 individuals living in Idleb and Aleppo governorates, northwest Syria with shelter solutions.
Overall, the project intends to reach 792,400 individuals targeting most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls and prioritize family members with high vulnerabilities according to the beneficiary selection criteria (IDP status, no source of income and support, persons with disabilities, elderly, female/single/child headed households) and identified priority geographic locations)

4 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), there are over 4.7 million people across Syria in need of shelter assistance. The continued movement of IDPs towards northern Idleb and northern Aleppo, particularly since the escalation of military operations in April created an additional burden on already over-crowded IDP sites and host communities. The overall shelter absorption capacity in northern Syria has been dramatically depleted by the continued displacements in 2019. Displacement since December - up to 800,000 IDPs - has further exacerbated shelter and NFI needs in northern Syria. 20% of the newly displaced are residing in camps, with 16% in unfinished houses or buildings and 12% in family tents. The two most urgent needs and challenges reported by the newly displaced populations are non-food items (NFI) and shelter (CCCM Cluster Snapshot 9 Feb 2020). For those displaced residing in IDP sites, there are high needs for family tents. NFIs are critical priorities because many IDPs lack basic household items. Additionally, many IDPs sites have experienced serious flooding during winter seasons, which often causes damages to household items and to family tents. For those IDPs who are able to find a space to live in unfinished buildings or collective centres, there are often large numbers of adequacy issues (privacy, safety and security and protection from weather conditions). Targeted shelter interventions can address these adequacy issues and decrease the vulnerability and protection risks for households. According to recent needs assessment conducted by IOM in November 2019, 840 tents in a planned camp are in urgent need of replacement. IOM will pilot a new project by replacing tents with refugee housing units (RHU). The lifespan of RHUs is expected to be between 3 and 5 years, which will save yearly efforts of replacing tents (which have an average lifespan of one year).

5 - Implementation of activities

For NFI distributions, IOM will engage implementing partners and will closely coordinate with SNFI and CCCM clusters through the different implementation modalities including: 1. Direct distribution (Emergency NFI, Cash and Voucher and Winter Response); 2. Rapid Response Mechanisms; and 3. Single Source Procurement. Building on its existing partnerships with Syrian NGOs, IOM and partners will continue to respond to the pressing NFI needs, focusing on newly displaced households and other IDP households that did not receive NFI assistance in the last 6 months. Through direct and continuous coordination and partnerships with Implementing Partners (IPs), SNFI Cluster and other stakeholders, IOM will implement shelter rehabilitation of unfinished buildings, repair of occupied damaged houses, repair of occupied collective centers, replacement of tents in targeted locations with Rapid Housing Units, and well as tent winterization activities. Repairs and upgrades will also improve WASH and other critical infrastructure needs. For shelter activities, IOM will aim to support: 1) People living in hard-to reach areas, or in newly accessible areas, 2) IDPs living in last resort sites, collective centres, and/or living in open areas, 3) Newly-displaced populations, 4) Spontaneous/ self-organized returnees, 5) Over-burdened communities. Shelter repair teams will conduct technical safety assessments on targeted shelters, verify HLP due diligence requirements, and profile beneficiaries in cooperation with protection unit to determine priority and eligibility for the assistance based on the predetermined vulnerability. Projects will be monitored by IOM's M&E unit and contracted engineers. SOPs will be agreed with implementing partners and validation will be conducted at each project stage including location selection, selection of buildings for repair/rehabilitation, beneficiary selection, vendor selection, and during works.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

For tent distribution and insulation activities, risk of community tension due to insufficient/different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions. Also, risk of eviction from IDP sites/camps due to lack of approval from property owner or HLP issues. Mitigation measures include: standard assistance package followed by all partners, ensuring common beneficiary selection criteria and clear communication within target communities. Feedback/complaint mechanisms will be established and publicised. Work in IDP sites should only begin after written approval is obtained from owners. For shelter rehabilitation and repair activities, crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increased risk of GBV. Physical safety of beneficiaries/communities can be an issue due to structural condition of the building and/or the presence of ERWs in or in close proximity to potential work sites. Mitigation measures include: ensuring structural integrity and safety of the buildings targeted for repair/rehabilitation work; inclusion of electrification works. Selection criteria may be influenced by local authorities which may risk most vulnerable being left out, lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights including civil documentation. Mitigation measures include: ensure partners are aware of prioritization based on assessed needs and vulnerability criteria; ensure partners know how to recognise, report and resolve any attempted interference; ensure that there is a written approval from owner for specific period to use the property for IDP accommodation. For NFI distributions, IOM will provide and ensure adherence into the NFI guidance note and beneficiary selection that outlines important protection consideration in the implementation. IOM will also ensure implementation of the commodity tracking system (CTS) and roll out of its feedback/AAP mechanism.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

For Shelter activities: i) Risks include loss of access to project areas due to armed conflict. These factors are beyond the project's control. As above, the assumption is that access remains possible throughout project areas. Mitigation measures include regular communication with implementing partners to report any security incidents of attempted interference by groups. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will conduct prioritization exercises to determine alternative locations in coordination with the SNFI cluster and project donors. ii) Landlords may request extra rehabilitation works beyond project scope, and may lack of valid proof of ownership of targeted unfinished buildings. Mitigation measures include approaching community representatives and local councils to verify building ownership, Signatures of community representatives as witnesses to the issued document by LC, a publication will be put in public locations containing names of claiming owners to targeted buildings and planned activities, requesting any who wish to dispute the ownership to approach the IP within 15 days and provide supporting evidence. iii) For NFI and tent distributions, there are risks that beneficiaries sell the assistance received, HLP issues with landlords, and lack of proper site planning by implementing partners and not following Sphere minimum standards in tent installation. Mitigation measures includes technical training on site planning and sphere standards for implementing partners, enhance targeting and vulnerability criteria when selecting beneficiaries for distribution of tents and NFI kits for new arrivals.
