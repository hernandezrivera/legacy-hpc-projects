Project Summary

Médecins du Monde France (MdM) responds to the emergency situation resulting from the violent armed conflict in the North-eastern part of Nigeria, in order to improve access to free comprehensive Primary Health Care (PHC) services including Nutrition, Mental Health and Gender Based Violence (GBV) services for the most vulnerable conflict affected populations in Borno State. In terms of operational approach, MdM first considers direct support to MoH and SEMA, wherever a health facility is still available or functional, to reinforce the health management capacities and maintain quality of care. Indeed, MdM is supporting two MoH HFs in Azir and Gumsuri, in Damboa LGA, with provision of drugs and medical equipment, basic rehabilitation, and capacity building and incentives to MoH staff.
Where there is no health facility, MdM set up a temporary clinic to cover the essential and most urgent healthcare needs of the affected population in IDP settlements and informal camps in Maiduguri, Jere and Damboa LGAs. During the calendar year 2020, MdM intends to run five clinics: two in Maiduguri LGA (Kawar Maila and Garba Buzu IDP camps), one in Jere LGA (El-Miskin IDP camp) and two in Damboa LGA (Hausari and GTS IDP camps). Also, MdM has an Emergency Response Team that has complete mobility to deploy to respond to emergencies (epidemics outbreaks and influx of populations).
Each clinic provides a comprehensive package of PHC services which include:
- PHC: medical curative consultations, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and immunization.
- Nutrition services: MdM runs an OTP in Garba Buzu and Kawar Maila clinics. In all clinics, MdM screens children under five and refers SAM cases to nutrition partners for their admission in OTP or SC.
- Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services: individual counselling, group support sessions and referral to secondary healthcare facilities.
- GBV services: medical care for GBV survivors (Clinical Management of Rape), MHPSS counselling, and case management by social workers.

1 - Needs

It is 10 years since the beginning of violent attacks from Jama‘atu Ahlis Sunna Lidda’awatiwal-Jihad (JAS), commonly known as Boko Haram (BH), in the North-eastern part of Nigeria. This violent and chronic armed conflict has resulted in widespread displacement, violation of humanitarian and human rights law, high protection risks and a worsening humanitarian crisis. In Borno State, the conflict shows no sign of abating and is adding to a long history of marginalization and chronic under-development as well as a higher rate of poverty, illiteracy, unemployment and human suffering on a massive scale. Health The destruction or damage of health facilities (around 15% of HFs non-functional and 30% damaged by the conflict in Borno State as per the Nigeria HNO 2019), lack of trained health staff and medical supplies result in an urgent need for integrated PHC services. The stock of essential drugs and supplies in some health facilities is low or inexistent. Poor access to emergency integrated basic PHC services like immunization, integrated management of childhood illness, maternal and child health, lack referral services, HIV services and management of non-communicable disease have been reported for IDPs and host communities. Nutrition Nutrition remains very fragile in Borno State. Many households depend on humanitarian assistance. Pregnant or lactating women and children under five are the most vulnerable to malnutrition. As per the Nigeria HNO 2019, an estimated 368,00 children suffer from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and 727,000 from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). The main causes of malnutrition are linked to inadequate food intake, unsafe water, insufficient hygiene and sanitation, diseases and forms of psycho-social deprivation resulting from the conflict. Global acute malnutrition rates in many Borno and Yobe State LGAs were high, and ranged from 10% to 15%, as per the 2019 Nigeria CH-analytical framework. According to this same source, MdM three areas of intervention, Maiduguri, Jere and Damboa LGAs, are considered in Phase 3 of Food and Nutrition Insecurity from June to August 2019. Mental Health The ongoing conflict has resulted in grave violations of human rights, including brutal attacks resulting in death, injuries, sexual violence, abductions, early and/or forced marriages, arbitrary and extended detentions without trial, disappearances, deprivations, denial of services, family separations and forced recruitments by armed groups. Conflict-related psychological trauma is widespread, including for survivors of GBV meanwhile mental health services are inadequate. Despite the magnitude of the problem, the region has a weak mental health system with few mental health professionals compared to the rest of the country. MdM is one of the few health actors implementing mental health activities in addition to psychosocial support. A strong collaboration with WHO was set to refer patients from Maiduguri or Damboa. Gender-Based Violence The crisis in North-East Nigeria has significantly affected the dignity of women, and this further entrenches pre-existing gender disparities. According to the Nigeria HNO 2019, approximately 700.000 women and adolescent girls of reproductive health age are at risk of sexual violence and exploitation, and three million people are in need of GBV services in Borno State. Women tend to become mothers at 17/19 years of age, and 63% of vulnerable households are comprised of female-headed households. Early and/or forced marriage, forced prostitution and survival sex are common coping strategies for economically depressed households. Women and adolescent girls are also attacked while engaging in everyday activities such as fetching water and going to the latrines. Despite increased rates of GBV, services to support survivors are still very limited, and only 18% of the functioning health centres in Borno State can reportedly provide integrated clinical management services.

2 - Activities

PHC services will include Integrated management of childhood Illnesses, an expanded program of immunization, SRH, management and prevention of communicable diseases (malaria, diarrhea, lower respiratory infection and skin diseases) and management of non-communicable diseases. MdM will ensure the provision of drugs and medical equipment to all supported clinics. MdM will implement referral and counter referral systems for emergency cases, including life threatening conditions, complicated pregnancies and deliveries, and MHPSS cases. Also, a network of community mobilisers will provide key health messages and behavioural change communication sessions to the targeted communities. MdM will ensure the early warning, alert and response system (EWARS) is functional at health facilities level to enable early detection and notification of any change in the disease trends in targeted communities. MdM will maintain emergency stock and an Emergency Response Team to enable rapid response for any outbreak or in case of massive displacement. Indeed, in May 2019, MdM ERT deployed to GSS camp, in Damboa, LGA, to respond to the urgent needs of approximately 10,000 newly arrived IDPs with the provision of MdM comprehensive package of healthcare and protection services. MdM plans to support management of severe acute malnutrition through strengthening and/or establishing OTP in the health facilities. MdM will ensure integration of OTP into primary health care system wherever possible. Under this project, MdM proposes to support two fixed OTPs, through providing essential medicines, consumables, equipment, cleaning materials, stationeries as well as provision of training to health and nutrition workers and to community mobilisers. MdM also plans to support IYCF corners in Kawar Maila and Garba Buzu clinics. These IYCF corners will provide counselling services to lactating mothers and caregivers of children under 2 years of age. All clinics have nutrition screeners who screen all children under five coming to MdM clinics. SAM cases are referred to OTP or SC when necessary. MdM MHPSS interventions are an integral part of the comprehensive PHC care package. There is no specific focus on a particular age or gender. Additionally, there is a great collaboration with the other services in the PHC specifically GBV related needs and nutrition programmes. GBV survivors are provided with individual counselling, follow-up and referral in addition to recreational group sessions. Care givers/mothers of children benefiting from the nutrition programme interventions receive groups support sessions given that in situations of displacement the psychological wellbeing of the care giver highly affects the level of improvement of the child. MdM provides the following types of interventions: individual counselling sessions, group support sessions, psychoeducation and sensitization on mental health issues and GBV concerns. MdM supports GBV survivors with medical care, principally focused on Clinical Management of Rape, and with MHPSS support provided by the counsellors. The GBV department in close coordination with MHPSS will organize capacity building trainings for MHPSS counsellors so they are able to consider the specific needs of GBV survivors. Every supported clinic has two social workers who will be in charge of referring and accompanying GBV survivors to service providers according to their specific needs. MdM social workers will ensure GBV survivors receive a comprehensive response. Constant awareness raising is conducted at community level by community mobilisers and the GBV team in coordination with community religious and traditional leaders to fight gender cultural norms and stigma about GBV survivors. MdM provides regular training to the medical team, nutrition workers, MHPSS counsellors and social workers on their respective areas to build their capacity and ensure the quality of MdM service provision.
