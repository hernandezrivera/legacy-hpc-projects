Project Summary

Armadilla has been working in Syria within the health sector since the year 2005 with a specific focus on social inclusion for persons with disabilities. The Syrian crisis in March 2011, has dramatically worsened the situation, and since then the Organisation developed and implemented projects in the Damascus Area, in strict cooperation with different local actors (both NGOs and health specialised centres), in coordination with the Ministry of Health of Syria.
According to the World Bank, over one billion people, or 15 percent of the world’s population, live with disabilities; in Syria, 3.7 million or 27 percent of the total population (aged 12+) have a disability, confirming that prevalence and negative impacts of living with a disability are more pervasive in developing and crisis-affected countries. According to the “HNAP, Disability: Prevalence and Impact. A Nationwide Household Survey Using Washington Group Methodology”, 2019, 26% of resident population in Syria suffers one or more form of disability, while the percentage among IDPs raises up to 29% and around 25% among the returnees. Distribution of people with disability among the national territory is somehow consistent, having a peak in the Rural Damascus Area (32%) and the less prevalence in As-Sweida (19%), even if the difference among the different territories may have different explanation, including difficulties in certain areas in collecting useful and fair data, more or less presence of International Humanitarian Aid, etc.
What is certain is that disability in the whole of Syria is one of the first concerns that humanitarian actors should considered, especially since the capacity of Syrian actors (health, education, social welfare) in assisting and supporting the needs of them has been hugely reduced by the conflict, and it is a condition that shall impact the capacity of families and communities along the time.
In this framework, Armadilla developed a twin track approach to respond the crisis, with projects and actions aim to:
a) Deliver immediate assistance to people with disability (support to families in various forms, therapy pathways for children with disabilities, psychosocial support for people with disability or parents/relatives of children with disability); and
b) Develop and implement actions with the aim to increase the capacity of Syrian actors working on the disability topics.
Moreover, in collaboration with the Syrian association Zahret Al Madan, which among the different activities implemented in the Damascus and Rural Damascus area manages a clinic in the Yarmouk Camp, Armadilla will establish a service of Reproductive Health, providing the necessary resources for the clinic, and delivering awareness sessions for women and the community on RH topics. This service, according to one feasibility study developed in the Autumn 2020, is totally absent in the Yarmouk area. The medical service will be provided by a doctor who will be present in the clinic two days a week for the entire period of the project (spaces, equipment and medicines will be made available by the clinic).
Furthermore, the project here presented is integrated with the activities proposed by Armadilla under the umbrella of Protection sector, which include assistance to children with disability, PSA for women, services for women and girls victims of violence, vocational trainings for women.

4 - Needs

In the whole of Syria health care systems for trauma, suffers for lack of equipment and, above all, health workers are insuﬃciently trained. The Prosthetic and Rehabilitation Centre (CPR) of the Ibn Al Naﬁs public hospital is the only public hospital in Syria where prosthetics and orthosis are built and physiotherapy sessions for people with born or acquired (amputations) disabilities are extensively implemented, which bring uncountable of patients in requesting assistance and health cares. The CPR receives a huge number of requests for diagnosis and treatment, which can be processed only minimally. Treatment for minors is particularly complex, due to the low number of technicians specialized in working with children and youngsters, both in prosthetics and rehabilitation paths. Two huge gaps hinder the capacity of the Syrian actors in delivering quality services to final beneficiaries in the field of disability. 1) techniques taught to students of physical rehabilitation and prosthesis are old and do not fit with the present problems deriving (also) by the consequence of the war, and b) a chronic lack of equipment and consumables. Regarding the Occupational Therapy (OT), has been observed that the practice is totally absent on the Syrian territory, while professionals trained on the subject will be able to immediately give the possibility to People with Disability to perform daily personal and work tasks, allow them to quickly recover abilities which support their social inclusion. Needs assessment are continuously performed by the I-NGO, through bi-weekly meetings with local actors and partners , in order to determine needs and possible activities to be developed and implemented.

Project activities will be implemented by the Organisation in strict co-ordination with local partners (CPR of Damascus, Zahret Al Madan, and Aamal/University Manara of Latakia). At this aim, Armadilla already established general Memorandum of Understanding with the partners, which are willing to cooperate to the project. Armadilla since the year 2006 has signed a protocol of cooperation with the Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Social Affairs of Syria. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to the Area of the activities: Locations where the project will be implemented, Centre for Prosthesis and Rehabilitation (CPR) of Damascus, University Al Manara of Latakia and Yarmouk clinic, are areas so far considered totally secure and accessible. However, the Organisation will be in continuous communication with the relevant security actors in order to organize activities and movements only after consultation with them. Availability of goods: Goods will be purchased on the local market, looking for the best vendor in terms of economy and capacity of guarantee the provision of service. A back-up list of potential suppliers guarantees the possibility of ﬁnding the goods in case of problems will be drafted. Lack of permission to enter in Syria for expatriate staff: Armadilla is not experienced problems in obtaining by authorities visas for expatriate staff/experts, however to avoid any delay and obstacle in implementing the activities, monitoring and logistics activities will be realized through Syrian staff of the Organisation, supported by remote by international experts. Experts and professors: until the end of the year, training delivered by international experts should be done through remote teaching, therefore no risks are detected.
