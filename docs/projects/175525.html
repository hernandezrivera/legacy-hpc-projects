Project Summary

The cumulative effects of the civil war in South Sudan that started in December 2013 and reoccurred in July 2016, has left about two-thirds of its population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and impaired the government’s ability to provide basic services for its people (OCHA, November 2019). While the situation is no longer escalating due to the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict, food insecurity remains the main driver behind the number of people in need. According to September 2020 IPC, about 5.82 million people were projected to face severe acute food insecurity and it is expected to worsen in the coming months of April to July 2021 to 7.4 million people. This is linked to flooding, low food production, effects of COVID-19 and insecurity.
Therefore, AFSS seeks to avert the situation through the implementation of the project “Improving maternal infant and young child nutrition among vulnerable households with children 0-59 months and women of childbearing age in Kapoeta East County (KEC) Eastern Equatoria state” This is aimed at improving the Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices among the vulnerable households in KEC. This will be achieved through conducting individual and group counseling sessions on the WHO/UNICEF recommended MIYCN practices to mothers/ caregivers of children receiving treatment for SAM (3298) and MAM (8980) as well as the pregnant and lactating women (9888). Additionally, AFSS will use Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches, such as; music and drama shows to disseminate the key messages on optimal MIYCN; establish model vegetable gardens (24); dissemination of IEC materials (20,000) with the key messages on optimal MIYCN, COVID-19; conducting food and cooking demonstrations (288), training on formulation of Sorghum Peanut blend (SPB) (METU-1) (288) using locally available food materials to increase food literacy; empowering of community structures including mother support groups (48) and CNVs (106) to provide MIYCN services for sustainability; and community dialogues (28) to encourage male involvement in key family care practices in line with COVID-19 guidelines. Knowledge Attitude and Practice assessments (2) to strengthen documentation and evidence generation. Smallholder poultry farming and Guinea pig husbandry will be strengthened to promote nutrition sensitive livelihood. This project is in line with 2021 HRP needs and strategic plan and AFSS will implement the proposed project in KEC in the 7 payams.

1 - Needs

According to September 2020 IPC, about 5.82 million people were projected to face severe acute food insecurity and it is expected to worsen in the coming months of April to July 2021 to 7.4 million people. This is linked to flooding, low food production, effects of COVID-19 and insecurity. In addition, the Acute Malnutrition Analysis (IPC-AMN) report (September 2020 IPC) shows that the Food consumption among children is generally poor. Although county level data on child food consumption is unavailable, FSNMS round 25-26 State level data shows Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) and Minimum Meal Frequency (MMF) among children 2-23 months are 17.2% and 25.8% respectively. Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) among children 6-23 months is 3.7%. Although there are no big changes in the MDD and MAD between the FSNMS in rounds 25 and 26, the MMF has dropped from 33.7% FSNMS in round 25 to 25.8% in round 26, Child caring practices are also of concern in the area with State level FSNMS estimates of exclusive breastfeeding at 32.3%. The prevalence of acute malnutrition in EES has remained serious (GAM >10%) with a GAM of 13.6% reported in the December 2019 FSNMS. According to the same report, more than 18.5% of the children 0-59 months are stunted and more than 17% of the women of reproductive age are wasted.This is attributed to heightened food prices, inter-communal conflicts, and cattle-raiding, as well as flood-and-pest related crop losses in addition, the Covid 19 pandemic and locust invasion has made the situation worst. To, therefore, address this challenge, the government of South Sudan, United Nations and Non-Governmental Organizations including AFSS have been implementing nutrition programmes such as the; Out-Patient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) for treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) without medical complication, Stabilization Centre for treatment of SAM with medical complication and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) for treatment of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Covid 19 awareness. In 2020, AFSS enrolled and treated 2,764 SAM and 7858 MAM cases (children 6-59month), 8791 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in OTP and TSFP respectively. Much as these programmes have been effective in treating acute malnutrition, i.e. more than 94% of the enrolled cases getting discharged as cured (AFSS NIS .2020), they have not sustainably reduced the prevalence of acute malnutrition. Instead, the prevalence of acute malnutrition has remained above the serious (10-14.9%) levels (FSNMS rounds 25-26) and is expected to remain so as projected by the September 2020 IPC report. This is because, by design, the current nutrition programmes are mostly nutrition-specific where active case finding is done by CNVs and health workers and the identified MAM and SAM case are enrolled and treated using specialized nutrition supplies. Even though maternal infant and young child nutrition has a bearing on the nutrition status of vulnerable individuals, especially children 0-59 months and women of childbearing age, the existing MIYCN interventions are implemented as complementary activities alongside the mainstream nutrition programmes (OTP and TSFP) and as a result, there is very little or no change in the MIYCN indicators. Despite the intervention by several development partners the health and nutrition situation has been poor since most of the nutrition interventions have largely been nutrition specific. Therefore, to address malnutrition sustainably, scientific evidence supports a need for combined approach of nutrition specific and sensitive programmes, and integration of available livelihood interventions. It is against this background, AFSS proposes to implement the “Provision of life saving interventions among vulnerable children under five years of age, PLW in KEC” with the objective of improving MIYCN practices, immunization coverage, the use of therapeutic foods, WASH practices and COVID-19 awareness sessions.

2 - Project activities

Conduct Baseline assessment and end line assessment (2)- Findings from this assessment will be used to inform programme design and to monitor the project progress. Conduct Orientation of stakeholder (30,2 per Payam and the other two from the county); orientation meetings with the county Health Department, health workers of the targeted health facilities and CNVs will be conducted; Conduct training of AFSS staff; (30, 20 male and 10 female) A ﬁve-day training will be conducted for the AFSS project staff on the project implementation modality, gender and protection and on the MIYCN and WASH; Provision of care and treatment with specialized nutritious foods for severe (3298) and moderately (8980) malnourished children under five years; Provision of care and treatment with specialized nutritious foods to PLWs (9888); Provision of Vitamin A supplements in semester 1 for girls and boys aged 6-59 months (30981); Conduct deworming exercise through provision of Albendozale in semester 1 for girls and boys aged 6-59 months (27883); Conduct Malaria testing of children with SAM on the OTP (3298); Provision of LLITN for malaria prevention to children admitted in the nutrition sites (13,630); Provision of WASH kits to 3208 caregivers of children admitted in the OTP (buckets, soap, jerrycan); Conduct MIYCN counsellings (12,222 caregivers reached); Conduct training of MtMSGs on MIYCN and WASH, adopting and contextualizing the existing ministry of health training manuals for optimal WASH and MIYCN practices, AFSS will train at least two MtMSGs per health facility; Conduct Food and cooking demonstrations using all the locally available food in each of the targeted health facilities; Establishment of vegetable gardens; AFSS will establish at least one vegetable garden at each nutrition center. The purpose will be to promote the production and consumption of micronutrient rich vegetables; Conduct Trainings on the formulation of Sorghum-Peanut-Blend (SPB) (METU-1); caregivers of SAM patients will be trained on the formulation of METU-1 using locally available materials (sorghum ﬂour, peanut powder, ghee and honey). This is a highly nutritious therapeutic blend that can be used to manage and prevent acute malnutrition at the household level. AFSS will therefore empower the local communities in kapoeta East County with this skill as a sustainability strategy to prevent relapsing of already cured SAM patients (1 training session per site per month). The training sessions will be integrated with the MIYCN and WASH training sessions; Support startup of Nutrition sensitive livelihood activities like: Smallholder poultry farming and guinea pig husbandry; to be able to transfer knowledge and skill in poultry farming, guine pig rearing and ultimately contribute to increased dietary diversify as well as generate income from the sale of poultry products and guinea pigs; Conduct Community mobilization and sensitization; this activity will be conducted by the CNVs at the community level and health workers at the health facility level so as to create awareness and increase programme coverage; Conduct Community dialogues;(28, 1per Payam per quarter) for community involvement and complaints and feedback, AFSS will hold community dialogues in each of the targeted health facility catchment areas on a quarterly basis. This will ensure that the views of the targeted populations are taken into consideration; conduct Knowledge Attitude and Practice assessments; to strengthen documentation and evidence generation AFSS will integrated KAP assessments into routine programme activities; Formation 48 MTMSGs constituting of 15 mothers who will be trained and provided with 322 local birds (5 hens and 2 cocks) per group; Identification and provision of referrals of GBV survivors for response (10 cases); Conduct 240 Covid-19 awareness and sensitizations (1*24*10) by use of mega phones, IEC materials (10000) example flyers/banners/posters.

3 - Project indicators

Project reporting: AFSS will develop and implement the project communication monitoring plan (CMP) whose overall objective will be to promote the success and sustainability of the project by meeting the information needs of the project stakeholders. The CMP will define the project’s structure, methods of information collection, screening, recording, formatting and distribution. It shall also outline understanding among the project teams regarding the actions and processes necessary to facilitate the critical links among key project stakeholders, ideas, and information that are necessary for project success. In this project, AFSS will essentially prepare and submit the following project reports; baseline and end line evaluation survey reports, project orientation and training reports, weekly, Monthly, Quarterly and annual activity reports. All the collected data will be dis-aggregated into gender and age. Project Monitoring arrangements will involve participatory systematic and routine collection of data for the purpose of establishing whether the project intervention is moving towards the set objectives and project goal. The data collection tools will be embedded into the project activities in order to ensure that the process is seamless with focus on the project’s inputs, activities, outputs and outcomes. Process Monitoring will be done monthly so as to identify and share project implementation gaps as well as inform the project management team and funding agency about the actual implementation of project activities in the field. Process monitoring will be conducted using designed checklists and guidelines. A monthly process reports will be prepared and shared. Progress Tracking: A tracking sheet (Indicator Tracking Table (ITT)) will be used to outline all output/outcome indicators for key activities along with monthly/quarterly target values for those output/outcome indicators. On a monthly basis, the project progress results will be entered into the ITT against those targets and the excel ITT will automatically calculate deviation against the targets. Progress Validation: Progress of key project activities will be reported by the field staff on a weekly basis. In order to validate the reported progress on outputs/outcomes the M&E Officer will collect the output/outcome tracker and identify output indicators to be validated. Validation/verification will be initiated by collecting of Means of Verification (MoV) from the Nutrition assistants and MIYCN officer. Once MoVs are collected, the M&E Officer will take a sample out of those and shall physically verify the activities, outputs and outcomes reported. Outputs and outcomes will be validated in the field using the same tool and guidelines used for process monitoring. Performance Monitoring: Baseline evaluation survey will be conducted and its results will be used to regularly assess progress regarding the realization of the project performance indicators. The project Performance Monitoring Plan (PMP) will be developed to serve as a reference document that contains the targets, contextual definition of each indicator, the methods of data collection and frequency of data collection for each indicator, who will be responsible for collecting the data and how will the collected data be used. A management information system that ensures timely access to up-to-date and accurate project and financial data will be developed and used throughout the project cycle. Results Oriented Joint Performance Monitoring will be conducted on a quarterly basis involving the key project stakeholders namely; UNOCHA, AFSS, CHD, and other Nutrition implementing partners. Data collected will be analyzed and report on project results, gaps identified and suggested solutions to inform subsequent joint monitoring activities of the project and for mutual accountability.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Gender equality will be considered at all stages of programme implementation following the AFSS gender policy 2017. During staff recruitment, right from top managers to participating beneficiaries’ gender equity will be observed. During trainings, equal participation of both men/boys and women/girls will be given priority. All stakeholders/ gender acters will be contacted at all times for proper coordination and to avoid conflict of interests. Community dialogues will be conducted quarterly and any gender related issues raised will be managed by AFSS gender and protection personnel together with County gender personnel in Kapoeta East. Beneficiaries and communities will also be given toll free numbers where they can report any issue of gender inequality at any time without physical contact with AFSS/County personnel in cases of fear. The selection of the meeting sites will be done jointly with the local leaders and community members. AFSS will also ensure that the selected sites are close to the communities. AFSS will ensure that the beneficiaries do not walk a distance longer than 5kms by taking services closer to them through outreach programs. The selection criteria will ensure that there are no protection risks. The entire implementation team from AFSS staff, community leaders and the beneficiaries will be oriented on Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (SEA) and status discrimination. Clear penalties for culprits will be stipulated. Also, the participants will always be informed about their rights and how to go about it when violated. All cases reported will be treated with integrity and confidentiality. This strategy will be under the mandate of gender and protection officer employed by AFSS. All the community interactions shall be conducted in observance of the Covid-19 guidelines.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM); help desks will be established at all the nutrition sites (24 sites) in which beneﬁciaries or community can report any complaint in relation to Nutrition services. These help desks will be headed by one member from the community. The complaints will then be shared with AFSS on a weekly basis. Reports on these complaints/feedback will be given to the government and other relevant stakeholders on a weekly and monthly basis. Community sensitization on PSEA and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse toll free numbers; sensitization and awareness creation of the beneﬁciaries on the toll free numbers will be integrated into the onsite nutrition and health education sessions and will also be printed on the IEC materials including AFSS visibility T-shirts. AFSS will integrate knowledge assessment of the numbers into routine data collection tools. AFSS will ensure that people with special needs are taken care of despite of their disabilities during the implementation period. For those unable to read or write, posters that carry pictures on PSEA, where and how to report will be displayed at each site. All beneﬁciaries will be enlightened on the modalities of the project, where to report cases of abuse and how they will get their feedback.The beneﬁciaries will be encouraged to select one member from their community who will be responsible for reporting any form of PSEA imposed to them by AFSS staff to the reverent authorities such that measures are taken against such staff.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Complaints and Feedback Mechanism - Help desks will be established at all the nutrition sites in which beneficiaries or community can report any complaint in relation to the project. These help desks will be headed by one member from the Community within the catchment area. The complaints will then be shared with AFSS on a weekly basis. Reports on these complaints/feedback and action will be reported to the relevant stakeholders on a weekly and monthly basis. All interactions will adhere to Covid-19 Standard Operating procedures (SOPS).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established at every project site. AFSS will develop complaints and feedback journals. These will be used to document and track complaints. Other platforms including community dialogues and home visits will be used to collect feedback and complaints. Joint monitoring exercise will also be used as avenues through which the project beneficiaries can share their feedback regarding the project with their local leaders. Data from the complaints and feedback journals as well as routine assessments will be used to redesign the project. Additionally, all Cluster coordination meetings at both Payam and cluster levels will be used to obtain feedback and recommendations that will be used to redesign the program. During all activity days, AFSS will share feedback with the project beneficiaries as well. All counseling sessions will interactive and in full observance of the Covid-19 guidelines.

Community dialogues focusing on gender roles in relation to food security and nutrition at household and community level will be held. 12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Health workers have a very huge role to play in promoting good MIYCN practices in every locality. AFSS will conduct mentor ship and coaching of community health workers and Mother to mother support groups in the different health facilities in Kapoeta East on MIYCN practices and guidelines. Mentor ship and coaching will specifically target health workers working in maternity, antenatal and postnatal departments. 24 sessions per quarter will be conducted (1session per facility per quarter). Dissemination of IEC materials on MIYCN; to encourage community members to challenge the beliefs and practices that sustain inadequate MIYCN, IEC materials will be distributed to both SAM and MAM beneficiaries, health workers, CNVs and other community structures. IEC materials will be in the form of brochures, flyers, counseling cards and posters printed and provided to the community. These materials will be distributed during programme activity days. 1,150 materials will be disseminated. Food literacy and Food/cooking demonstrations; an assessment will be conducted to identify and classify all the locally available (including wild) and accessible food materials. This will be done together with all beneficiaries. Food and cooking demonstrations will then be conducted using these food materials. The sessions will be participatory with mothers demonstrating how they have been using these foods to feed their infants and young children. Good cooking practices will be promoted and those ones affecting nutrition quality of food discouraged. The food demonstrations will equip mothers with the knowledge and skill to classify foods according to the three major categories (energy giving, body building and protective). After which mothers will be encouraged to balance the diets of their infants and young children. Output will be improved food knowledge and menu planning on budget and cooking skills which preserve nutrients.
