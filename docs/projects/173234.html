Project Summary

UNHCR will ensure the centrality of protection and protection mainstreaming through inclusive coordination and leadership of the humanitarian response. UNHCR will work with the Government to give Rohingya refugees a legal identity through registration and documentation, which will serve as the foundation for protection, program delivery and solutions, while pursuing community-based protection responses and providing essential services in the areas of MPHSS, GBV prevention and response, child protection, and legal assistance. UNHCR will remain a central player within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for the monsoon and cyclone seasons and in addressing the challenges brought on by COVID-19. UNHCR will bolster inclusion through support to host communities, addressing the broader challenges that undermine protection space while promoting peaceful coexistence and social cohesion. Reinforcing accountable government leadership of the response will be an important component of inclusion, and UNHCR will continue to support capacity development across the operation. UNHCR will empower Rohingya refugees by building community capacities for leadership, self-management and engagement with the Government and the humanitarian community, including through the election of refugee representatives and involving refugees from all parts of the community in a range of community-based protection programs. Within the current government policy framework, UNHCR will advocate for greater access to education and economic inclusion, so that young refugees retain hope and become a constructive force in the community. UNHCR’s solutions strategy necessarily takes into account the Government’s policy that voluntary repatriation is the only feasible solution for Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh. While promoting conditions needed for voluntary, safe, dignified and sustainable return in Myanmar, large scale returns are not likely to take place in 2021. UNHCR will work to improve protection conditions and quality of life for refugees in Bangladesh pending solutions, building upon the framework for responsibility sharing provided by the Global Compact on Refugees.

1 - Identified Needs

Despite reduced arrivals, alleged push-backs and tightened surveillance at border points by Bangladeshi authorities were reported in 2020, reinforcing the need for continued border monitoring and interventions. The joint GOB-UNHCR registration of the Rohingya was completed in 2019. Continuous registration is important to ensure refugees can establish legal status and have access to assistance and services. There are still major gaps in the civil registration and documentation of births, deaths, marriage and divorces in the camps. It is estimated that less than 40% of children are born in health facilities where they are issued birth notifications. Marriages and divorces are still largely conducted unofficially with limited safeguards for children and women. Security in the camps remains a top protection concern, with organized criminal activity and violent incidents involving both refugees and host communities reported. Refugees, especially women and girls, face increased vulnerability both within the home and in the community. In 2019, women reported violence in the home (26%) and violence in the community (25%) as their top two perceived risks. Children, especially girls, face various risks, especially with closure of learning facilities, including corporeal punishment and sexual abuse. For persons living with disabilities, access to services and protection continues to be a significant challenge. Recent efforts have therefore focused on improving independent access to information and services, and meaningful community involvement in decision making through community representation and service groups. Gender diverse populations, religious minority groups and women volunteers are marginalized. Strategic intervention is necessary to address these concerns, especially with the presence of law enforcement authorities being inadequate in most camps. Refugees have developed a range of negative coping mechanisms to deal with the challenges they face. Freedom of movement remains severely limited, often restricting access to essential services. Refugees’ unrecognized legal status renders them unable to access civil registration and hinders their access to justice, putting them at risk of unlawful detention and leaving them vulnerable to exploitation and abuse. UNHCR and its partners provide legal assistance to refugees each year, but refugees are often reluctant to seek formal legal redress due to lengthy processes of obtaining justice. Some 31% of refugee families have been identified with at least one protection vulnerability, and barriers to accessing services for PSNs, especially the elderly and the disabled along with women and children, are further compounded during monsoon and cyclone seasons. The COVID-19 pandemic has worsened access problems for many among the refugee population. Unemployment and lack of opportunities among youth places them at heightened risk of involvement in criminal activity or (perceived) radicalization. Despite the generosity of Bangladesh in hosting Rohingya refugees, the sheer numbers coupled with strains on public services, limited economic opportunities and the depletion of natural resources, has resulted in shrinking protection space and tensions with host communities. Conflicts over the destruction of the environment, felling of trees for shelter space and cooking needs, and unilateral hikes in land rent for refugee shelters by host communities, have already been reported. Access to information and accountability mechanisms remains an ongoing gap. The 2020 MSNA indicated that while the majority of households reported receiving sufficient information on most types of assistance, information relating to non-food items and livelihood assistance remains limited. While most households reported not facing problems providing feedback or complaints, almost half of all households rarely or never felt consulted about their needs, preferences and the delivery of humanitarian assistance.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1.1: Border monitoring and capacity building workshops with border guard authorities will be conducted. Activity 1.2: UNHCR will lead protection monitoring in 24 camps and provide support in 10 other camps. Individual case management will identify PSN. Support will be provided to PERU teams for COVID-19 / emergency preparedness. Activity 1.3: Capacity of legal aid providers in 34 camps will be strengthened. Community-based dispute resolution mechanisms will be made accessible. Legal representation, legal counselling, mediation, jail visits will be conducted. Refugees will be sensitized on legal rights and procedures. Expansion of legal partners will take place in 10 additional camps. Activity 1.4: UNHCR will co-chair the Anti-trafficking Working Group and harmonize anti-trafficking activities. Activity 1.5: UNHCR will maintain 3 registration sites covering 32 camps, and 2 sites at the registered camps for a joint verification exercise for refugees in the registered camps. Activity 1.6: UNHCR will lead issuance of civil registration and documentation in 34 camps with RRRC/CiC officials and strengthen alignment of refugee civil documentation with national systems. Standardization of the issuance of birth notifications to children will be supported. Activity 1.7: UNHCR will redouble efforts to pursue comprehensive approach to solutions, based on the GCR framework. Activity 2.1: COMs in 27 camps will work with communities to respond to protection and assistance needs of the community, including on protection and emergency responses. Activity 2.2. COMS will work on protection issues aimed at behavioral change within the community through specific projects designed and delivered by them. Activity 2.3: The Saturday Solidarity Initiative will conduct community development activities following steps of project cycle management. Activity 2.4: Community groups will expand to 144 groups in 31 camps comprising of 3,600 refugees, including women, girls and other vulnerable groups. Activity 2.5: Support will be provided to 150 elected refugee leaders, including 70 women, in 4 camps. Contingent on GoB approval, elections will be conducted within 4 other camps. Activity 2.6: Engagement will continue with 709 imams in 25 camps, and 328 refugee women religious teachers in 14 camps to support protection, COVID - 19 and emergency preparedness related awareness raising. Activity 2.7: 33 community centres will be operational with 5 new ones constructed in 2021. 23 Information Service Centers in 17 camps will be operational. Interactive Voice Response (IVR) system will be used for messaging on COVID – 19 and emergency preparedness. Activity 2.8: Support will be provided to 15 grassroot women and youth organizations. Activity 2.9: Support will be provided to PSN (PWD and elderly) to implement individual assistance to refugees; conduct accessibility work; provide educational support to 185 children with disabilities; and stock assistive devices. Activity 2.10: Cross-cutting support to sectors will be provided to strengthen AGD mainstreaming and inclusion. In 2021 CBP activities will be expanded and implemented in Camps 8E, 8W, 9, 18, 19, and 20. PSEA training will be conducted for staff/ partners, SEA assistance and tracking mechanisms will be strengthened, and PSEA focal points will be present in UNCHR/partners. Activity 3.1: Support will be provided to law enforcement authorities, including infrastructure development. Human right standards will be incorporated in all training to ensure Do no harm principle is applied. Activity 3.2: UNHCR will expand on improved reporting and police response to safety and security issues, through formation of community safety forums and social cohesion/ conflict resolution mechanisms between host and refugee communities. Activity 3.3: Capacity building of judicial/ law enforcement authorities on international refugee protection, human rights, gender and child sensitive approaches to policing will continue.
