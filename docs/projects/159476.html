Project Summary

Food Security and nutrition situation remains critical in South Sudan with an estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the total population) facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and in need dire of urgent humanitarian action. (IPC August 2019). Relief Corps Organization seeks viable actions that will enhance the provision of timely life saving livelihood services to 10,000HH (60,000 men, women, boys and girls) conflict affected vulnerable populations including IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Unity, Warrap and Jonglei states where RCO enjoys a five-year static presence.
With the hope of a hunger free South Sudan, RCO through this project will provide Agricultural support to populations affected by crisis in Koch, Leer, Gogrial East and Bor South Counties by increasing their Agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption and help them evade future shocks. The support will be delivered through provision of main and seasonal crop kits and agricultural tools to food insecure and displaced households and with an additional input of either vegetable or ﬁshing kits depending on the suitability of the additional inputs to speciﬁc locations, formation of farmers’ producer groups and formation of fisher folk groups. This will be coupled by capacity training on improved agronomic practices and ﬁsh preservation/handling techniques and training of farmers on land –harvest, storage and market linkages that will develop communities’ knowledge and skills that will improve food access, increase and build their resilience and future self-reliance by creating and expanding delivery models that support community empowerment and accountability.
The project will synergize multi sector response by coordinating with counterpart agencies implementing Protection, Health, WASH, Nutrition, WFP GFDs to ensure effectiveness and referrals of unmet objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of AAP principles, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.
Through this project Agricultural support will be provided to 10,000 HHs (60,000) vulnerable populations including IDPS, Returness and the host communities affected by crisis in Unity, Jonglei and Warrap states, coupled by capacity training that will improve their Agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption, mitigate irreversible coping mechanisms and build their resilience to withstand future shocks and meet their long term needs.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to spread and became more complex as a result persisted insecurity, cattle-raiding, high food prices, ﬂood related destruction of crops and disruptions to market access. (IPC August 2019). The persistent communal ﬁghting and surges of violence in Jonglei and Unity states have forced people to ﬂee their homes, many of them multiple times. The compounding effects of widespread violence and sustained economic decline have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their health, safety and livelihoods. With 6.35M people facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and clearly identiﬁed as likely be at least one phase worse without current humanitarian support therefore require urgent Humanitarian action. (IPC Aug 2019, South-Sudan/food-security-outlook/June-2019). In Unity state the insecurity due to sporadic political conflict, intra and inter communal conflict and cattle raiding witnessed in Koch, Leer counties continue to uproot and displace people resulting to households abandoning their traditional sources of income and preventing the affected communities from cultivating their lands. This frequent displacement witnessed in these areas has undermined already compromised agricultural production destroying the livelihood of the already vulnerable communities and causing food shortages. The affected populations are increasingly adopting negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestocks. These results to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. (Situation-overview-unity-state-july-september-2018). Jonglei state has not been spared either, in Bor South the situation there has been compounded by a soaring inﬂation, communal conﬂicts, prevalent cattle raiding incidents, below average harvests due to recent Fall Army Worm invasion and ﬂooding in Bor; prevalent and fresh outbreaks of livestock diseases such as Foot and Mouth Disease. (FSNMS Round 22, HNO 2019, Greater Jonglei annual humanitarian report 2018, Situation-overview-jonglei-state-jan- march-2019). These events continue to negatively impact food security and livelihood options among the most vulnerable populations in Bor South with a signiﬁcant proportion of populations at risk of sliding into IPC Phase 4 and beyond (June 2019 FEWSNET Food Security Outlook). This has subsequent led to a signiﬁcant deterioration in GAM in Jonglei (21.8%), with a national average of 19.1% (FSNMS Round 22). The aggregate cereal harvested in Gogrial East in 2018 and 2019 was about 9.1 percent below the previous year’s level. The contraction in the harvested area is reported in the key-producing areas, which includes WBeG where a 28 percent reduction was observed. As a result, Gogrial East will have aCereal deﬁcit of -3,597 tones (FAO South_Sudan_Crop_Watch_March-July_2019). Conﬂict that is speciﬁc to Gogrial East is the inter-communal ﬁghting between Aguok and Apuk communities, this conﬂict intensiﬁed at the onset of the cropping season leading to displacement and fear of attacks disrupting the farming activities. (NGO Forum Security update 2019, REACH situation-overview-greater-bahr-el-ghazal - march-2019). Needless to say the prices are expected to rise in Jan/Feb 2020 and persist at extremely high levels, above those observed in 2019. Concern is highest for the many HH in need who do not have access to assistance. It is expected some HH will exhaust the capacity to cope during the outlook period and be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). Hunger and malnutrition have escalated on an unrelenting course with about 1.5 M children under age 5 estimated to be acutely malnourished. Food insecurity is expected to be more severe during 2020 as conﬂict persists & macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate. This calls for immediate and unhindered humanitarian support and close monitoring.

2 - Activities

1. Beneﬁciary Identiﬁcation registration and distributing of staple crop kits, nutritious dense vegetable kits and tools to 10,000HH in Bor south, Leer, Koch and Gogrial East Counties. 2. Distribution of fishing kits to 9,000 Households in Bor south, Leer, Koch and Gogrial East Counties. 3. Identification and training of 600 extension farmers on improved agronomic practices post-harvest management, pest control, preservation/storage, sales and market linkages. This training is aimed at improving their Agricultural production and productivity hence strengthening their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption, mitigate irreversible coping mechanisms and build their resilience to withstand future shocks.4. Training of 600 extension farmers on pest control including scouting and Fall Army Worm surveillance, detection, prevention and mitigation.5. Establishment and maintenance of 30 demonstration plots and kitchen gardens at nutrition facilities in the target counties. 6. Formation and training of 30 farmers’ producer groups of 20 members each on to oversee post-harvest, storage and sales and marketing in the target Counties. 7. Capacity development and training of 150 CAHWs on livestock handling, treatment and vaccinations and safeguarding basic animal health. 8. With the aid of the trained CAHWs, distribute 10 solar powered cold chain facilities in the targeted locations and vaccinate 60,000 livestock in the target locations . 9. Formation and training of 30 fisher folk groups of 25 members each on fish handling and preservation methods and sales and marketing in the target counties. 10. 20 schools in the target counties children trained on good farming techniques, preservation and storage through demonstration plots. 11. Training of RCO's FSL staffs and other staff and consultants on gender mainstreaming. 12. Training on 1500 fishermen in the target counties on fish handling procedures processing and preservation methods. 13. Provide unconditional cash transfers/ Voucher system for seed procurement targeting 2000 households.14. Post Distribution Monitoring and Evaluation of the Project.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

As with all the other past projects implemented by the organization, RCO has always used its expertise and experience with effective and quality programming that recognizes the community’s rights including dignity, capacity, and ability for self-determination. RCO will adopt the same through a consultative approach in the implementation of this project; by including ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project cycle. Beneficiaries will be dis-aggregated and involved -equal representation - taking into account the different roles and needs of boys, girls, women and men); local leaders among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring a meaningful participation in implementation, monitoring and especially evaluating the project. All project activities will be aimed at obtaining full dignity and respect for the rights of the beneficiaries and communities. For example, RCO staff will consult with women and girls particularly, on best locations for distributing livelihood kits.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

RCO understands that the proposed project will be implemented in an emergency context where there are predominant cases of displacements and will therefore mainstream protection principles including GBV into project activities. The Do-No Harm principle will be maintained to ensure that project activities do not expose the displaced populations to further harm. Safety and dignity of beneficiaries including the IDPs will be a key priority of the project in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’. RCO will ensure nondiscrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly, the disabled and the marginalize people with special needs. This we be achieved through establishing a complaint mechanism, consultative problem identification and solving through dialogue

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

First the project will have an in-built capacity strengthening component through coordinating, collaborating and networking with other partners working in the same locations these partners will include government, the community, INGOs, NGOs, CBOs among others. The Government counterparts like RRC, Ministry of Agriculture and local authorities will also provide us with technical advice on the terrain of the locations and will be working closely with our locally recruited staff to be mentored and trained on FSL needs as a sustainability strategy. The project will not create new or parallel structures which are unsustainable in the long term, it will endeavor to work with government and community systems to support sustainable and catalytic interventions strengthening the supply and demand side of FSL needs, thereby stimulating renewed commitment.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is designed to respond to acute FSL acute needs at both household and community and will mainly target displaced population/host/returnee populations in the areas with highest risk of protections issues and areas at highest risk/rate of malnutrition risks. The project will also be in line with the HRP 2020 and FSL Cluster Strategy document that will prioritize frontline activities entailing FSL interventions to offer food assistance as well as provide timely life-saving livelihood support (cropping/ veg/ ﬁsh/ livestock) coupled with intensive capacity to building resilience of household and affected populations to equip them recover and absorb future shocks. Needless to say, this project and proposed activities clearly align with the national objectives.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project environment is relatively accessible and calm at the moment with a few pockets of insecurity incidents however, RCO through this project will prioritize safety and security of both its staff and beneficiaries, through close coordination with relevant stakeholders including the local government, RRC, UNMISS on the ground. In addition, RCO will also manage security issues by disseminating peace messages during trainings among other activities and invite the local authorities to provide motivational talks during some of the training and sensitization sessions. RCO is a partner and user of UNHASservices and member of the NGO Forum that provides coordination and support service for staff relocation and evacuation services in case of insecurity. Moreover, RCO will adopt a participatory approach in all the planned initiatives. Should the security situation deteriorate, RCO will adopt a mobile response team through hit/run approaches including operation nearby counties. The project will also prioritize the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries including children and vulnerable adults in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’ and ensure non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and people with special needs. Finally, the project will focus on mainstreaming crosscutting issues and also fully engage with the community among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities. This will be in addition to continuously identification of key protection concerns affecting children, boys, girls, men and women in the course of implementation including reporting to various Protection/Child Protection/GBV/FTR desks; and referrals to counterpart agencies implementing protection programs.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

First the project will have an in-built capacity strengthening component through coordinating, collaborating and networking with other partners working in the same locations these partners will include INGOs, NGOs, CBOs among others. The Government counterparts like RRC, Ministry of Agriculture and local authorities will also provide us with technical advice and will be working closely with our locally recruited staff to be mentored and trained on FSL needs as a sustainability strategy. All these trainings will ensure the beneﬁciaries (taking part in the trainings) are be selected from the local communities and will intern remain in the communities after the project to ensure continue providing FSL services and thus sustainability. RCO will also ensure the project is implemented with involvement of all beneficiaries and key stakeholders who will remain in place after the end of the project. All the trainings and other project activities will ensure community participation which will in turn build a sense of ownership and ensure there is knowledge and skills transfer in FSL interventions and direct community participation being emphasized throughout the project life cycle to ensure that the program responds to the needs of the target community and acceptance by the community thus contributing to sustainability of the project. Coordinating and collaboration with implementing partners in the target area will ensure the smooth transition and hand over after the FSL intervention with partners still existing taking over some of the activities to improve and build on what exists thus maintaining sustainability. In case there is availability of funding and a possibility of renewal of the project, then the project may continue.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The safety and dignity of the beneﬁciaries is very key aspect of this project. RCO is alive to the fact that protection and safety issues are a big challenge for many affected populations in South Sudan. RCO will involve all the affected populations in the response and their views will be taken into considerations too. The choice of distribution sites, access to those sites and distance will be taken into account to ensure safety of the affected populations. Some distributions if possible will be done around households to enable female double up in routine domestic chores and to ensure their protection from risk of sexual abuse is taken care of. Violations against civilians have been widely been reported in many locations including Gender based violence and sexual abuse mainly for women and girls. In some locations children especially boys have been recruited as child soldiers. RCO will look out for such cases and report them to relevant protection partners in those locations or where possible will use our own protection oﬃcers to manage these cases. RCO will consult all groups including people with disabilities, women and children on the project modalities and share relevant information to ensure they are aware of their rights, can claim their rights and lodge complaints when aggrieved. RCO will also work with protection actors to collectively identify actions to reduce GBV and child protection risks and improve accessibility for all people as well as create referral pathway and train our Staff on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns safely, ethically and with confidentiality.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

RCO will ensure that all groups are consulted on their needs and capacities, together and in separate groups, on the basis of gender and age, by same sex facilitators, during the initial assessment, our program team will then ask all groups, together and in separate groups, on how they want to access and design the CFRM as well as how they want to communicate with Us. RCO will then establish a CFBM that include formal and informal mechanism to respond to the different literacy levels of the community. The informal mechanism will include community members who have easy access to RCO staffs’ contact numbers for sharing any complaints related to the project. The formal complains shall be gathered through setting up information centers, complaint boxes and mobile teams seeking feedback. RCO will also share the project information with the beneficiaries to ensure they give feedback and make complaints whilst ensuring our staff have suﬃcient knowledge on how to receive, refer and report protection concerns confidentially, safely and ethically and adhering to our policies to ensure Protection from SEA including SOPs, investigation and response mechanisms as well as training the community on what SEA is and how to report it. The organization through our existing SOPs that includes PSEA will then analyze all concerns/complaints from the CFBM raised and respond to them according to the SOPs including consulting with the GBV and Protection actors in the field site on the referral pathway to investigate and respond to the claims raised. During Monitoring the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on their rights to launch complaints, express grievances, and give other forms of feedback about the implementation process and the quality of services to measure their overall satisfaction. Project management will reflect on the information generated to enable on-going program improvement and create evidence base and platform for providing feedback to the project beneficiaries.
