Project Summary

The proposed projects aims to support 5,000 vulnerable households (30,000 individuals) in the recovery of their livelihoods through agricultural inputs and training in better agricultural practices in crop and vegetable production. In addition, the project will target 1,000 fisher folk households (6,000 individuals) to be trained in better fishing practices in addition to receiving fishing kits. Therefore, 6,000 households (36,000 people) will be targeted under this project. Beneficiary selection criteria will include the food consumption points (FCS) and coping strategy index (CSI) in addition to other vulnerability criteria agreed upon with selection committees. WFP approved tools will be used to ensure that the correct information is collected. This will also ensure uniformity when it comes to analysis and report sharing.
The activities are adapted to the seasonal calendar and are strictly based on the subsistence activities of the target communities in the target area, i.e. agriculture and fishing. Vegetable cultivation will be carried out during the dry season as well as during the lean season, while crop farming will be carried out during the rainy season. Fishing activities will complement to crop and vegetable farming to cover for any gaps in food supply. The combination of these measures is also intended to protect crop seeds from consumption and serves as a protective strategy against negative coping strategies as they await harvest. The expected outcome for the target group includes rebuilding of livelihood assets as well as improving dietary diversity. This will also contribute to improving the resilience of the intended beneficiaries. The project is needs-based and will therefore target the most vulnerable.
To avoid duplication, activities will be coordinated through the food security and livelihood cluster at county, state and national levels. 5W reporting matrix will be used to provide regular and timely reporting to promote coordination.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC classification and projection for October 2020 to July 2021, there is an estimated 24,000 people classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, in Pibor County and the Greater Pibor Administrative area. Pibor County is the only county under Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) which demonstrates the severity of the humanitarian situation there.A combination of factors have added onto the precarious situation in this area. Inter and intra communal fighting reported in 2020 affected parts of the county. Above average rainfall during the rainy season, coupled with overflow of River Pibor caused widespread flooding in the area, leading to loss of harvests and entire agricultural seasons.

2 - Project activities

The proposed project seeks to address the following cluster objectives;CLFSC/CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5).CLFSC/CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties).Under cluster objective CO2: Complementary emergency cropping, livelihood & livestock support (cropping/ veg/ fish/ livestock): Pibor falls under IPC 5 (Catastrophe). An agricultural kit as well as a fishing kit will be provided. This will include the following: a). Vegetable seeds: Amaranth (20g), Onion (20g), Tomato (20g), Eggplant (20g), Collard (20g) and Okra (50g). b). Crop seeds: Maize (5kg), Sorghum (3 kg) and Beans (2kg). c). Fishing kit: Fishing twine (2 spools), Monofilament (1 coil) and Hooks (2 boxes). d). Farming tools: Hoe (1pc), Maloda (1pc), and Axe (1pc).Under cluster objective CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties)Pibor is the only county, out of 78, falling under IPC 5, Catastrophe. Training will be conducted to accompany all distribution. This will primarily be based on better agronomic technics including but not limited to land preparation, use of farm tools, vegetable production, cereal production, water for production & irrigation, post-harvest control, pest & disease control. Training will also be conducted to accompany distribution of fishing kits and to complement traditional ﬁshing methods so as to increasing productivity. Cross cutting issues: (1) Gender; Beneficiary committees, comprising of women, will play key role in design, implementation, and monitoring of project activities. Through them, the response will introduce and promote PSEA, gender equality, and prevention of COVID-19 and SGBV among project beneficiaries. They will also act as local level feedback and complaint handling mechanism. Female-headed households will be purposefully targeted for this intervention as a way of empowering them towards contributing positively towards the food security needs in their families. Gender related roles and responsibilities at household level will be taken into account to avoid conflict within the household. Strategic needs will be responded to, by supporting and increasing women’s equal contribution in households’ coping strategies and capacities and to promote equity within the household. This will also be achieved by ensuring access to means of production as well as unconditional cash, which will, in the short and medium term, strengthen the position of women positively towards improving their own situation and equal position at household and community level. (2) Environment; FCA has in place guidelines and policies such as the FCA Climate Tool (2020) and the FCA Climate Action and environmental protection guidance package (2020) which will guide the environmental analysis of the project. Farming groups will be influential in this project. The project will use lead farmers and farming groups to promote environmentally sustainable agriculture practices by emphasizing farming techniques such as mulching that conserve the soil moisture, enhance the nutrients status of soil and control soil erosion; promoting the use of compost or animal manure as a fertilizer to improve soil structure, etc. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to engage in dry season farming through the use of locally available water sources such as perennial streams and rivers so as to ensure food availability throughout the year and not just rely on rain fed agriculture. (3) HIV/AIDS and COVID 19: 1. Awareness raising activities will be conducted on COVID 19 at the same time as with HIV/AIDS. With so much attention directed towards the fight against COVID 19 at the moment, other diseases such as HIV/AIDs risk taking a back seat.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

8.5.) Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: This risk will be mitigated by including representatives of persons with disabilities, older persons and the youth in beneficiary committees to ensure that their voices are heard and to ensure that they contribute positively to the project activities.(8.6.) Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: To mitigate against this risk, beneficiaries will be sensitized on the need and use of the complaint feedback mechanism. Potential misunderstandings or misconceptions will be addressed and ironed out to ensure uptake.(8.8.) Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: Pibor and GPAA in general is very volatile both politically and socially. Project activities will therefore be closely linked to ongoing peace building initiatives in the area to ensure that tensions are reduced and social cohesion is promoted.(8.9.) Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area: Given the presence of IDPs, this is a likely risk. To mitigate against this, advocacy activities will be undertaken to ensure that vulnerable households, especially women, can access land. Local authorities will be involved in such discussions as the legal duty bearers.
