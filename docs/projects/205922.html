Project Summary

According to the FSNMS Round 28 report, SMART nutrition surveys for 2022, an estimated 2 million people will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2023, including 353,600 children (180,340 girls and 173,270 boys) suffering from SAM, 1 million children (552,900 girls and 531,220 boys) suffering from MAM and 737,810 acutely malnourished PLW. An estimated 2.1 million children (1.1 million girls and 1 million boys) will require Vitamin a Supplementation (VAS), including 1million children (517,800 girls and 497,500 boys) between 0 and 23 months and 507,650 pregnant women who will require Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN), 1.7 million children (902,330 girls and 866,950 boys) between 12 and 59 months who will require deworming and 1.8 million children and women who will require BSFP. IPC’s acute malnutrition projections for the 2023 lean season indicate a worsening situation. A total of 59 counties are projected to be in Phase 4, representing the highest classification in the IPC for the past three similar seasons and contributing to an increase in the number of people in need.
ADA intends to use funds under this project to support the implementation of a wide range of nutrition activities to achieve nutrition cluster objective 1. These interventions include; a) Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years in collaboration with the County Health Team .b) Conducting MIYCN counselling for pregnant and mothers and caregivers of children 0-<23 months c) Conducting nutrition screening targeting children under five years as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and strengthen the family MUAC screening approach. To achieve nutrition cluster objective 2, ADA will: a) Conduct nutritional screening for all children under5 seeking medical attention at health facility entry points and screening. b) Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months at all functional health facilities. c)Treatment of severe acute malnutrition with medical complication among children 6-59 months at all functional health facilities. d) Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months and PLW at all service delivery points. e) Training health and nutrition care providers on inpatient and community management of acute malnutrition (IMAM and CMAM). To achieve nutrition cluster objective 3: a) Support FSNMS survey to integrate nutrition indicators at all counties of operation. To achieve nutrition cluster objective 4: ADA will conduct; Screening / testing and treat malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months b) Establishment of child friendly spaces at the service delivery points so as initiate child stimulation and early child development during care of children with SAM. c) Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM d)Sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs including hygiene and sanitation messaging. e) Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites or in the vicinity f) Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM. g) Mainstreaming of GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites and availing information on GBV services/referral pathways is available. h) Establish a complaint and feedback mechanism to allow accountability to the affected population (AAP).

1. Needs

According to 2023 HNO, an estimated 2.2 million people, including 1.5 million host community, 315,500 returnees and 390,000 IDPs, will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023. Out of 1.4 million children under age 5, 353,600 children will suffer from SAM, 1 million will suffer from MAM and 737,800 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) will have acute malnutrition. An estimated increase of 172,900 children and PLW suffering from acute malnutrition is expected in 2023. Furthermore, 26,560 (7.9 per cent) out of 337,120 refugees residing in 11 counties in South Sudan will need treatment for acute malnutrition, including 5,140 SAM children, 14,350 MAM children, 4,337 acutely malnourished PLW and 2,730 patients with HIV or tuberculosis. Based on last years’ seasonal historical data of food security and nutritional monitoring data system and admission trends for 2022, South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2023 indicate that up to 2 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute nutritional of which 302, 078 are children, 575,548 are pregnant and lactating mothers, this is triggered by food insecurity, disruption of services by either intercommunal fights or rainy flash flooding amongst others. This need scaling up of the services to alleviate the suffering of children and women affected across the country with immediate provision of CSB, SAM, MAM and TSPF supply to the affected populations in Canal/Pigi and Fangak of Jonglei state. As mentioned above, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15% was reported in people in twenty-six counties in six states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Eastern Equatoria). A continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Jonglei and will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in the following year. Furthermore, the FSNMS Round 28 report, SMART nutrition surveys for 2022, estimated that 2 million people will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2023, including 353,600 children (180,340 girls and 173,270 boys) suffering from SAM, 1 million children (552,900 girls and 531,220 boys) suffering from MAM and 737,810 acutely malnourished PLW. IPC’s acute malnutrition projections for the 2023 lean season indicate a worsening situation. A total of 59 counties are projected to be in Phase 4, representing the highest classification in the IPC for the past three similar seasons and contributing to an increase in the number of people in need.

2. Project activities

ADA intends to use funds under this project to support the implementation of a wide range of nutrition activities to achieve nutrition cluster objective 1. These interventions include; a) Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years in collaboration with the County Health Team .b) Conducting MIYCN counselling for pregnant and mothers and caregivers of children 0-<23 months c) Conducting nutrition screening targeting children under five years as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and strengthen the family MUAC screening approach. To achieve nutrition cluster objective 2, ADA will: a) Conduct nutritional screening for all children under5 seeking medical attention at health facility entry points and screening. b) Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months at all functional health facilities. c)Treatment of severe acute malnutrition with medical complication among children 6-59 months at all functional health facilities. d) Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months and PLW at all service delivery points. e) Training health and nutrition care providers on inpatient and community management of acute malnutrition (IMAM and CMAM). To achieve nutrition cluster objective 3: a) Support FSNMS survey to integrate nutrition indicators at all counties of operation. To achieve nutrition cluster objective 4: ADA will conduct; Screening / testing and treat malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months b) Establishment of child friendly spaces at the service delivery points so as initiate child stimulation and early child development during care of children with SAM. c) Provision of WASH kits to children with SAM d)Sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs including hygiene and sanitation messaging. e) Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites or in the vicinity f) Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM. g) Mainstreaming of GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites and availing information on GBV services/referral pathways is available. h) Establish a complaint and feedback mechanism to allow accountability to the affected population (AAP). Summary• Screening of children age 6-59 and PLW with SAM and MAM. • Admitting and treating of children age 6-59 months and PLW with SAM and MAM• Establishing complaint desk for beneficiaries. • Conducting monthly community meetings to evaluate project progress• Monthly coordination meetings with County health department and other actors to strengthen services • Conducting On-job trainings of staff by program managing team. • Training beneficiaries on kitchen gardens establishment for dietary diversity• Conducting Vitamin A and deworming campaigns.
