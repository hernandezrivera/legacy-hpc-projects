Project Summary

This project will focus on livelihood training efforts to improve the livelihoods of disadvantaged women from host communities and Rohingya refugee Women and girls. The host community women and Rohingya refugee women and girls will benefits from livelihood and learning activities. It will equipped them with skills that will enable them to start income generating activities. The programme is design to bring together Rohingya and host community women and girls during livelihood activities, specifically will pay attention to joint awareness raising activities on women empowerment. The purpose of the project is community cohesion through promoting Rohingya Refugees and Host Community women’s and girls’ empowerment through learning and livelihood skills. The key interventions are includes:
1.Study on potential areas for livelihood and vocational skills development;
2. Design appropriate training programs for Rohingya women and girls and Implement blended learning and livelihood program.
3. Grants/seed funds program for Rohingya women and girls to start income generations activities and Cash for Work options
4. Three new MPWC are constructed, beside 5 available ones will be used for learning and livelihood skills, as well as other life-saving activities
5. Build capacity of Women from Host Community with skills and enabled to provide Livelihood training to Rohingya women and girls
6. Develop livelihood skills of Women from Host community and provide seed funds to start or expand their livelihood activities.
7. Develop capacities of women on mediation.
8. Joint activities of Rohingya and Host community women on protection, security life and livelihood skills

Needs

In the 2018 Joint Response Plan (JRP) for Rohingya humanitarian crisis, “lack of income-generating opportunities and transferable skills development has catalysed the exploitation of adolescent girls and women in the form of forced marriage, survival sex, trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation, drug smuggling and forced labour.” Human trafficking for sex work and labour is reported to be on the increase. There is a very high level of illiteracy among Rohingya women, especially adolescents who have missed out on education in Myanmar, repressive gender roles and customs being reinforced because of insecurity, and harmful societal norms and practices like child marriage emerging. Currently there is huge gap on addressing Rohingya women' and girls’ adolescents’ learning and livelihood needs. There is need to offer opportunities to learn, develop skills and access services through a context specific learning program of life, women’s empowerment, and livelihood skills combined with income generation activities, tailored not only to women’s and girls’ needs as learners, but also to their future as earners, that will promote better life choices, so they can be forces for change in their families and communities. Based on UNDP and UN Women a joint mission in January 2018 that assessed the state of the crisis in the region, including host communities in the affected districts, the results clearly demonstrated that increased support to host communities and local government institutions is necessary to stabilize livelihoods, restore damaged and stressed infrastructure and ecosystems, and increase local understanding of the humanitarian response. In the host community among the poor and very poor, women are being disproportionately affected by the influx, because of limited access to economic opportunities. Research for the ACAPS Host Community Review found that, as of December 2017, 45% of female-headed households in the host community were vulnerable or very vulnerable, compared with 35% of male-headed households. Women from host communities have reported being exposed to sexual harassment from checkpoints and are thus facing mobility restrictions, and are facing an increase in domestic violence and abandonment by their husbands. UN women has been implementing an economic empowerment project at Cox’s Bazar for the two years period working with host community women. That gave us a good insight into the perceptions of the host community women especially with regards to the growing tension, mistrust, and apprehensions about the Rohingya women. Rohingya and vulnerable host communities, including women should be provided with tangible and sustainable economic options, and a better understanding of peacebuilding, leadership, mediation and social cohesion concepts, prioritizing women headed households, women survivors of S/GBV, adolescent girls, and women with disabilities, and in host community, landless and asset-less women. By dedicating space to the safety, well-being, and leadership of women and girls, this project puts protection as the core of its activities and will lead to the stabilization of the refugee and host community relationship.
