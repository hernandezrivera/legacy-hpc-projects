Project Summary

The project seeks to address the immediate and ongoing health gaps in information regarding COVID-19, diarrhoeal diseases (cholera and dysentery), communicable and non communicable diseases to women of child bearing age (WCBA). It also will be inclusive of disabled and elderly persons in the project area.The project will target a total of 52,200 women of child bearing age (40,000 in Gwanda Rural district and 12,200 in Beitbridge town as well as 15,000 elderly + disabled in the 2 districts. The total beneficiaries will be 67,000. Beneficiaries will be identified by Community Health Workers who will be trained on data collection as well as imparting vital Health Information to them. The activities will include Health awareness Campaigns, trainings and meetings, visits, referral to health facilities and reports to the local health facilities and the District hospitals. (Gwanda DMO’s Office and Beitbridge District Hospital). The health campaigns will be organized by MORITI staff and the local clinic staff with the involvement of the local leadership, influential Community members and Community volunteers. The awareness will focus on Covid 19, cholera, dysentery, common diarrhea and non communicable diseases. GBV of various forms will be addressed as a cross cutting issue of importance as it has been exercabated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Due to limited income sources caused by the pandemic, there is increased violence on men, women and youths and this has been stated through various research. The proposed intervention aims at improving timely presentation by expectant women to health facilities so as to avoid pregnancy complications. The intervention will also reduce stress caused by GBV on pregnant women as well as promote positive hygiene practices (personal and environment). It will also make way for the betterment of the elderly and disabled persons’ health. To further strengthen the sustainability of the positive healthy behavior and appropriate hygiene practices, MORITI will train 150 Community Health Workers (100 in Gwanda and 50 in Beitbridge town).
In order to promote the adoption of good health seeking behaviour and hygiene practices amongst communities, the project will conduct community engagement through capacitation of local leadership, Village Health Workers (VHWs) and other stakeholders. The project will train 30 councillors, 145 Village Heads and 5 Chairpersons of the Residents Associations. Door to door campaigns on COVID-19, health awareness and Hygiene promotion will be carried out in addition Mass media approach such as radio-talk show, roadshow as well as SMS blasting. Radio jingles shall be used to promote Risk Communication and Community Engagement.
Result 1: Improved access to appropriate quality health service for an estimated 67,000 beneficiaries comprising of WCBA, disabled & the elderly in Gwanda Rural and Beitbridge town
MORITI will seek to be allocated operational wards by the Gwanda Rural and Beitbridge Town Council where the Organisation will identify, with the help of the District Medical Officers offices in both Gwanda and Beitbridge all existing Village Health Workers and other Community Workers to add up to the required number. MORITI will then train enumerators to be able to prepare a data base of women of child bearing age, disabled and elderly in their respective areas. The data will be used during programming.
Result 2: Improve awareness of safe pregnancies and positive health seeking behavior with a focus on community engagement and input .Throughout the project Accountability to Affected People will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the centre of our work

Women of child bearing age will make up at least 60% of the decision-making structures within various Village and Ward committees. However, the project will also deliberately mobilize men and boys in proportional measure to ensure that men are engaged in seeking and anticipating improvements in health, water, sanitation and hygiene that will directly impact on the health of pregnant and lactating women and that of the community at large. Communities will also be involved in Health Facilities operations through inclusion in the Health Facilities committees. 3. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the partner while working in their community.
