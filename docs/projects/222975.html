Project Summary

The Strengthening perspectives for the future and the resilience of refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar Project"" is a comprehensive initiative designed to address the multifaceted challenges faced by Rohingya refugees/FDMNs and the vulnerable host community in Cox’s Bazar. With three objectives, the project aims to create a significant impact by supporting skills and capacity building through non-formal technical training, and healthy manage livelihood options. Addressing the two objectives of LSDS within the FDMN, the project aims to be implemented at Camp-17 and Camp-19. The project will assist in developing a small-scale livelihood opportunity through non-formal technical training, focusing on tailoring, Repairing of gas stoves and solar panels, and Carpenters (wooden/bamboo handy craft). Approximately 240 FDMN individuals will be targeted, with a specific emphasis on achieving at least 50% women participation. With the support and concern of camp management, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will use the existing CB manage facilities of WASH and SHELTER facilities at Camp-17 and 19 as a center for non-formal technical training. During the project participant selection process, CB will follow to the LSDS guidelines, conduct community consultations, and seek input from camp management. Following the LSDS guidelines for CBT, approximately 20 individuals will be enrolled in each batch, with two instructors deployed for each batch. Each batch will continue for 120 hours (around 3-4 hours per day). Trainees will receive a stipend of 150 BDT per day. The project will further promote skill development through follow-up and referral activities, potentially providing equipment or in-kind support to trained beneficiaries. This support aims to empower them to follow self-employment opportunities, encouragement self-reliance. By offering resources for self-employment with MSME.
In addressing Objective-3, three types of activities will be implemented in the host community of 5 unions under the Ukhiya Upazila. As per the activities groups, the proposed activities as Investment Grant, Handicraft/Off-farm opportunities, Cash Stipend, and MPCG for Income Generating Activities. Approximately 125 vulnerable households will have the opportunity to participate, while majority percent will be women. To reduce household level vulnerabilities through enhancing household capacity is also a more important component of the project. Due to the Rohingya influx, the surrounding host communities were facing a problem of reducing their livelihood options and it was a key cause of increasing dissatisfaction and tension. So the livelihood activities directly contribute to increasing their regular income and that contributes to reducing the tension between FDMN and Bangladeshi people. CB will conduct the area/cluster-based community consultation meetings and conduct an assessment in the targeted area to select the most vulnerable households in need of livelihood support, particularly for vulnerable women’s headed families or single women/female youth, will be prioritized.
In both working areas, CB will address cross-cutting issues, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV), other inclusion-related factors such as disabilities, DRR, and relevant issues. In order to ensure a dignified working environment, CB will deploy both women and men staff and manage separate and accessible toilets for women, men, and people with disabilities. In applicable cases, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will set-up a child corner for the children of project participants who have no alternative option for care. Project participants will also be made aware of these issues. The project will be implemented in close coordination with LSDS, camp management, and other relevant sectors, sub-sectors, and networks, including Protection, GBV, PSEA, and associated agencies etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

In 2023, overall vulnerability continued to rise in both Rohingya and Bangladeshi host communities, with levels significantly higher than pre-pandemic years. Among Rohingya households, 97% showed moderate to high vulnerability, far surpassing the 69% in the Bangladeshi community. Notably, the percentage of highly vulnerable Rohingya households rose by 3 points from 2022 to 86%, largely due to ration cuts and economic shocks like inflation, which began affecting the community in mid-2022 (REVA-7)The ISNA findings on livelihood and skill development reveal significant needs and gaps in both FDMN and Bangladeshi host communities. Key challenges include income source vulnerability, low-income levels, limited household income generation, and a growing reliance on casual labor. Specific findings include: Around 73% of households have at least one income source, with 79% relying solely on one. This dependence on a single income source heightens vulnerability to disruptions, emphasizing the need for income diversification to bolster resilience. On the other hand, Over half (55%) of households earn below 5,000 BDT per month, with a median income of 3,000 BDT (MSNA 2023). These low earnings often fail to meet basic needs, indicating the urgent need for skill development programs that foster sustainable income-generation. Nearly half of households (47%) now depend on casual labor, up from 38% in 2023. This trend reflects a shortage of stable employment and highlights the importance of developing long-term, secure income sources.Regarding the women engagement in economic activities, only 8% of the total engagement in income-generating activities comes from women, pointing to significant gender disparities. Empowering women through skill development and creating economic opportunities for them can not only increase household incomes but also promote gender equality and community well-being. In addition, only 35% of individuals in these households actively contribute to household income, and there are stark gender and age disparities in these contributions. Men aged 25-60 serve as primary earners, contributing 23% of household income, while women in the same age group contribute only 2%. Among youth aged 18-24, men contribute 9% to household income, but women contribute merely 1%. This reveals an urgent need for livelihood and skill development programs that promote women’s economic participation, while also engaging youth as part of the workforce.Based the above data of ISNA and REVE-7, to address these challenges, a comprehensive strategy should be devised, encompassing three key objectives. Firstly, there is a need to support the skills and capacity building of FDMNs to facilitate their sustainable reintegration into Myanmar. Secondly, efforts should be directed at supporting the utilization of gained transferable vocational skills, non-formal technical training within existing camps and aligning them with humanitarian sector response needs. Lastly, there is a crucial need to support vulnerable host communities in diversifying vocational skills development including non-formal technical training and creating a livelihood options. By targeting these objectives, a holistic approach can be taken to address the multifaceted challenges faced by these communities and pave the way for sustainable and dignified living conditions.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming is a fundamental approach in Caritas Bangladesh (CB) for the humanitarian efforts. It ensures that all project activities prioritize the safety, dignity, and rights of project participants, especially the most vulnerable groups. . This includes creating safe and inclusive environments, promoting gender sensitivity, safeguarding children, and ensuring accountability to affected populations.By integrating protection principles into every aspect of the project, CB aims to create an environment where everyone—particularly women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities—can engage safely and with dignity. To achieve this, CB implements regular training, capacity-building initiatives, and monitoring mechanisms to identify and address protection risks proactively. This commitment ensures that the project remains responsive to the diverse needs and rights of all community members.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Caritas Bangladesh will strongly address the disabilities issues for the project. In a necessary case, CB will receive technical support from CBM, CDD, HI (Humanity and Inclusion) or and other relevant organization.. CB will actively screen and identify the people with disabilities, using the Washington Group Questions (WGQ). This comprehensive approach ensures that we are attuned to the diverse needs within our target population. A key aspect of our commitment is to target around 12% of persons with disabilities in the Camp and 7% in HC within the total target for the project. In order to ensure a dignified working environment, CB will deploy both women and men staff and manage separate and accessible toilets for women, men, and people with disabilities. In applicable cases, Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will set-up a child corner for the children of project participants who have no alternative option for care. Also, CB will allocate fund for the relevant activities and will ensure linkage with relevant organization and network as much as possible. This intentional focus aims to promote equitable access and participation, fostering a more inclusive project environment. Recognizing the significance of inclusive programming, we are dedicated to providing sector-specific training on inclusive practices for our staff and volunteers. This training will enhance their understanding of the unique challenges faced by individuals with disabilities and empower them to contribute effectively to our inclusive initiatives.Through these commitments, our project not only aims to meet the standards of inclusivity but also strives to go beyond, actively contributing to the empowerment and well-being of individuals of all ages and abilities within our target communities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project is directly aligned with the needs and perspectives of the communities to serve, promoting transparency and responsiveness. CB will follow the community led approach to identifying the proper project participants as a selected dignified criterion. CB is dedicated to fostering community participation in the evaluation process. To achieve this, we will develop a comprehensive plan that actively involves community members in shaping evaluation methodologies. Moreover, the results of these evaluations will be shared with the communities in an accessible format, ensuring transparency and accountability in our project outcomes.CB will organize minimum bi-yearly consultation meetings to evaluate the project's progress. These sessions will serve as valuable opportunities for community feedback and engagement. The insights gathered during these consultations will be integral in shaping the lessons learned workshop at the working level, creating a continuous feedback loop that informs and enhances our project implementation.For AAP Effective complain and feedback mechanism is in place through setting Opinion & Complaint box in all offices, camp facilities, distribution points and majhi blocks, dedicated Hotline and e-mail account. CB conducts PDM and beneficiaries feedback survey regularly. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding committee and MEAL team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

CB also follows a comprehensive PSEA & Safeguarding Policy to this humanitarian context. In the unwavering commitment to the principles of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), our project places paramount importance on the safety and dignity of all individuals involved. To achieve this, CB pledge to conduct mandatory PSEA training for 100% of our staff, including volunteers, ensuring they are equipped with the necessary knowledge to prevent and respond to instances of exploitation and abuse. Furthermore, our project is dedicated to fostering awareness among beneficiaries through the prominent display of PSEA materials, such as posters, fliers, and videos, incorporating key messages and information about accessible Complaint Feedback Mechanisms. These mechanisms, designed to be confidential, culturally appropriate, and accessible, will provide a secure channel for reporting concerns and feedback, thereby creating an environment where the well-being of all individuals is prioritized and safeguarded. As same previous, CB will allocate sufficient budget in 2025 for PSEA activities, including capacity building, monitoring, and the development or and contextualization of relevant materials as well.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The programme will not have environmental issues but will ensure special attention to upholding the environmental sustainability. The project participants will get three types of awareness sessions on Household preparedness to reduce disaster risk, Early Warning Systems & Community’s responsibilities and How to protect productive livelihood assets during emergencies. The community people will also be motivated to protect the soil erosion through roadside plantation, keep their surrounding clean with waste management and reduce use of single use plastic for their daily chores. They will also be made knowledgeable and motivated not to cut trees indiscriminately and to plant trees at their homesteads. CB also closely coordinates with Energy and Environment Technical Working Group (EETWG) under ISCG in order to address all the issues related to environment and receive guidance on current and new approaches. CB will give priority to preventing environmental pollution in the camp and HC.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project incorporates a strong Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures tailored for the Rohingya camps, where natural hazards such as cyclones, fire, heavy rainfall, landslide and flash floods pose significant risks. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) employs a community-based approach, actively involving Rohingya refugees in decision-making. This ownership enhances resilience and preparedness. CB has established an Emergency Team trained for rapid disaster response, supported by an Emergency Stockpile of essential materials. A detailed contingency plan outlines standard operating procedures for emergencies, ensuring coordinated response efforts with clear roles and communication protocols along with LSD sector and Area Focal Agency. Training is provided to staff, volunteers, and local stakeholders in disaster preparedness and response, empowering them to effectively manage emergency initiatives within their communities. The key messages of emergency preparedness will be shared with respective FDMNs through trained volunteers and staff as well. In the host community, CB organizes emergency preparedness simulations to raise community awareness and demarcate Emergency Team responsibilities, enabling a rapid and effective response during disasters.Through these EPR measures, CB aims to strengthen the resilience of Rohingya communities in the camps and HC, ensuring they can withstand and recover from natural disasters while meeting their basic needs.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will actively engage around 50% of youth aged 15-24, providing them with opportunities for non-formal technical training within the FDMN camps. Caritas Bangladesh (CB) will prioritize enrolling youth participants in various skill development activities, equipping them with practical skills to enhance their economic resilience and self-reliance. This approach aims to empower young individuals in the community, fostering greater livelihood opportunities and contributing to their long-term development, especially in case of repatriation.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project actively promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees. Through ongoing skill development, follow-up, and referral activities, beneficiaries receive essential training and support. This may include providing equipment or in-kind resources to enable self-employment opportunities, encouraging self-reliance within their communities.Additionally, by promoting MSME (Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises), the project facilitates access to resources that help refugees apply their skills in ways that generate sustainable income. This approach empowers refugees to build long-term resilience and reduces their dependency on external assistance.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community support development goals through Objective-3 of the LSDS. Targeted activities in five unions within Ukhiya Upazila include Investment Grants, Handicraft/Off-farm initiatives, Cash Stipends, and Multi-Purpose Cash Grants (MPCG) for income generation. Approximately 300 vulnerable households, primarily led by women, will participate to boost economic resilience. By expanding regular income opportunities, the project reduces household vulnerabilities and eases tensions between FDMN and local communities. Vulnerable households are also encouraged to diversify income through various IGAs and save, fostering sustainable self-reliance and economic stability in the host community.
