Project Summary

UNICEF response will serve children and teachers with essential interventions that will ensure continuation of education in the time of protracted conflict and address its immediate and cumulative impacts. 43,500 boys and girls will benefit from a range of activities that ensure their access to safe, inclusive and enabling learning environment and improve quality of teaching and learning. Over three fourths of these boys and girls live and learn in NGCA where humanitarian needs are most severe. 700 education professionals in NGCA and GCA will be trained on identified priority topics that enable them to deliver conflict-sensitive, risk-informed and life-skills-based education. UNICEF will work with the Ukraine Education Cluster partners to contribute to the policy and advocacy efforts aimed at the Government’s signature and implementation of the ‘Safe Schools Declaration’, a political commitment to protect schools during armed conflict.

1 - Needs

With the conflict in its sixth-year hundreds of thousands of boys and girls living on both sides of the contact line experience both immediate and cumulative impacts of the conflict and are in need of various types of assistance. In close proximity to the contact line shelling and shooting around schools is frequent with 32 recorded incidents that resulted in damages to school infrastructure during first nine months of 2019 which is two times more than in the whole of 2018 (Ukraine Education Cluster Situation Report as of 01 October 2019). Apart from endangering physical safety of children, teachers and parents’ continuous impact their psychological wellbeing. According to the draft HNO 2020 some 65 per cent of the education facilities within 20km of the “contact line” in GCA have reported that the conflict has impacted their students’ ability to learn as well as their well-being.Further away from the contact line boys, girls as well as teachers experience cumulative adverse effects of the conflict. According to UNICEF research results (UNICEF, February 2019, An evidence-based analysis of the psychosocial adaptability of conflict-exposed adolescents), 22.5 percent of adolescents aged 13-17 in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts (GCA) are currently at risk of developing mental health problems. It also found that adolescents in areas near the contact line tend to have lower overall life skills and are far more likely to develop mental health and behavioural problems when exposed to bullying in schools. Young adolescents aged 13-15 who live within 15km of the contact line were found to be one of the most at-risk groups. On the one hand they have the lowest life skills of any adolescent group within the country while, on the other they do not benefit as much as girls from the protective nature of their families, school and peers.The conflict continues to have an adverse impact on income of many households in conflict affected areas and their ability to meet education related expenses. According to the Ukraine Education Cluster School Survey, 48% of education facilities reported that at least 1 out of 4 children in their education facility needs support with education materials due to displacement or economic decline in families. Lack of materials impacts quality of learning and often demotivates boys and girls to continue education and may negatively impact their attendance.Need for conflict related repairs and rehabilitation of education facilities is still present in close proximity to the ‘hot spots’ along the contact line where new damages are happening regularly and also further away from the contact line in NGCA. According to the Education Cluster Monitoring Tool most schools in 20+ km area in NGCA received little or no assistance to date but many of them were damaged at the initial phase of the conflict and as reported by partners are in need of humanitarian assistance. The conflict also puts its burden on local budgets preventing authorities from doing proper maintenance of schools and replacing destroyed or worn out school furniture, sports equipment and other required supplies. According to the draft HNO 2020 some 84 percent, or a total of 771 education facilities in GCA of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, required some form of repair, primarily to the roof, windows, WASH facilities and heating systems.Each year the conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues, a larger number of students complete their secondary education in Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) without widely recognized certificates. This puts them at risk of diminishing opportunities to continue higher education outside NGCA and enter the job market. According to the Ukraine Education Cluster Strategy the number of children and youth who received education certificates since the start of the conflict has reached over 120,000 and continues to increase by around 20,000 each year.

2 - Activities & outcomes

UNICEF will conduct a range of activities to respond to the humanitarian needs that are highlighted in the respective section. The response activities will be focused on NGCA and in 0-20 km area along the contact line in GCA.Implementation of the capacity building program that consists of Life Skills Education (LSE), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Conflict Sensitive Education (CSE) will continue and will enable teachers and community workers to deliver quality conflict-sensitive, risk-informed and life-skills-based education. In GCA the program will target teachers that were not reached in the previous years of response and in NGCA will compliment rehabilitation of education facilities and delivery of supplies whenever appropriate. Capacity building activities will be implemented in line with the Guiding Principles on Integrating Conflict Sensitivity in Education Programming that promote gender mainstreaming at all levels including support to the policies which promote protection from abuse of girls and boys, and young women and men, equitable distribution of resources, and curriculum which is free of gender and social prejudices.Rehabilitation of education facilities and delivery of supplies will be conducted primarily in NGCA were the needs are most severe while pockets of needs that exist in GCA will be also addressed. 7,500 boys and girls in NGCA and 750 in GCA will have access to safe, child-friendly, protective and well-equipped learning environments. The rehabilitation/construction of water and sanitation facilities in schools will include gender-segregated toilets to ensure girls have access to safe toilets and water for drinking and hands washing.To support families that experience economic hardship in covering expenses associated with education that are often deprioritized in conflict affected areas UNICEF will distribute individual education kits and Early Childhood Development (ECD) kits. In GCA UNICEF will consider using Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality for this intervention that’s in many contexts proven to be preferred by recipients .To address the protection risks children, teachers and schools face during the armed conflict UNICEF will continue working with the Ukraine Education Cluster ant its partners to contribute to the policy and advocacy efforts aimed at the Government’s signature and implementation of the ‘Safe Schools Declaration’. As soon as the Declaration will be endorsed technical support will be provided to the Government on its implementation. UNICEF will also continue monitoring the right of children and youth in NGCA to continue higher education in their preferred geographical location and advocate on this with the relevant stakeholders on both sides of the contact line.
