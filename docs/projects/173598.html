Project Summary

The aim is to reduce mortality, morbidity and undernutrition by providing life-saving nutrition, health and WaSH services in facility and communities.
• Improved access to healthcare services in the target areas to reduce mortality and morbidity.
• To ensure that affected populations have access to nutrition treatment and preventive services through strengthening the capacity of health facilities.
Based on the needs identified, nature and living conditions of the affected population, local capacity available, the feasibility analysis of services provision, the program will be focusing on; support government to one PHC per Ward approach in health system for provision of primary health care services that includes basic management and prevention of communicable for all age groups from children to adults, antenatal and postnatal care services, support to uncomplicated vaginal delivery services and minor perinatal complications, support to referral services. As well as support to Routine Childhood Immunization in collaboration with SMOH and WHO, support to provision of clinical management of minor physical injury, and prepositioning for Cholera and Measles outbreak in the targeted LGAs and finally provision of nutrition services. Providing medical equipment, supplies and pharmaceuticals and nutritional supplies to the health facilities through the award and in coordination with SMOH and LGA authorities. Reinforcement of technical capacity through sector/sub-sector specific technical trainings for quality service delivery. Building institutional and community capacity for promotion and provision of appropriate IYCF-E integrated with priority health and hygiene practices.
Target:
1. Children: 0-59months of age for malnutrition treatment and other children for provision of primary health services. Prevalence of malnutrition, communicable and water-borne diseases are high. The project will significantly change the condition of children in the area by providing health care, CMAM treatment or access to improved water and sanitation, and by triggering behaviours change in both caretakers (e.g. mothers’ feeding practices) and children themselves (e.g. school sensitization).
2. Women: Women and children are over-represented among the most vulnerable categories of those affected by the crisis and compose more than half of the IDP population. At least 30% of IDP households are headed by women and 40% of IDP women are pregnant and lactating, requiring special nutrition support to meet their food needs. Women-headed households, with no adult males and high dependent ratios, are vulnerable to seeking negative coping strategies. Despite being producers and income earners for their families, women’s traditional gender roles mean they face reproductive burdens, limits on their mobility, decision making power, access to and ownership of land, capital, information and technology, and limited representation in community management committees. The conflict, insecurity and over-crowdedness of IDP camps has also increased GBV significantly. Women will therefore be a key target of the project, both in its direct activities (e.g. Care Groups), as well as with the women empowerment agenda applied across the project.
3. Men: Also, men in the communities are provided with primary health care services for the management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) and communicable disease.

1 - Needs

The conflict in North-east Nigeria is in its tenth year and continues to cause untold suffering for millions of Nigerians, particularly women and children. Ten years of conflict and violence perpetrated by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) have devastated communities in the three states (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe – BAY) of North-east Nigeria. Based on the round 33 Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) report conducted (August 2020 ), more than 2,118,550 individuals mainly women (53%) and children less than 6 years of age (28%) were recorded as displaced. Nearly 7.9 million people (8% increment from 2019), particularly women and children in the three crisis-affected states, require humanitarian assistance in 2020 Humanitarian Need Overview [HNO] 2020). Conflict continued in Yobe State, including Non-State Armed Group (NSAG) attacks and clashes with government forces along with the Local Government Area (LGAs) of Geidam and Gujba, bordering Borno State. The security situation in these LGAs remains unpredictable and volatile. Government forces continue to carry out operations and maintain a high level of alertness following NSAG activities across border LGAs. The 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) and related analysis contained in the HNO revealed an increase in needs across the BAY states compared to 2019.as the lack of preparedness for and prioritization of SRH services.The current nutrition situation in Yobe State is of concern, with a sharp rise in acute malnutrition rates being reported across LGAs. Yobe State Humanitarian Situation Analysis (2020) points out that Nguru LGA has the highest acute malnutrition rates among three targeted LGAs. The Nutrition Sector plans to extend the scale-up of nutrition interventions to Bade and the other two (Geidam and Yunsari) LGAs in the state to increase the coverage. According to the IPC Acute Malnutrition analysis (2020) classification, Northern Central (Bade included), and Southern Yobe are in Phase 3 (Serious). Similarly, the overall acute malnutrition prevalence at the state level is 11.5% in Yobe compared to 8.1% in Borno, and 7.2% Adamawa . The overall GAM rate for 0-59 months children in Yobe was 12.8% as reported by Nigeria Cadre Harmonisé 2020. The level of acute malnutrition (with GAM based on BMI prevalence) among women between 15 and 49 years is 24.7% in Yobe. Only 35.1% of children less than 6 months get exclusive breastfeeding in Yobe state. One in ten children receives minimum meal frequency, whereas less than 5% of children received the minimum acceptable diets for their age (IPC Acute Malnutrition Analysis, 2020). The nutrition imbalance is driven not only by food insecurity but also by poor child feeding practices, disease, and lack of clean water, poor sanitation, and hygiene.The incidence of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) in children has increased due to poor access and unavailability of food - fewer children and women have a minimum acceptable diet. Levels of acute malnutrition have significantly increased; from Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) acute malnutrition classification Phase 2 (Alert) to Phase 3 (Serious), in Northern, Central and Southern regions of Borno State. Levels of acute malnutrition levels are predicted to remain at IPC Level 3 (serious) in Yobe, while MMC/Jere domains in Borno State are at level 2 (Alert) (IPC Acute Malnutrition Analysis, 2020). Health facilities are in a deplorable state due to conflict, out of 1,529 facilities, (58.1%) of the health facilities are functional, 268 (10.2%) are nonfunctional, 300 (11.4%) are partially functional and 326 (12.4%) damaged. (Health Sector Nigeria, 2020).

2 - Activities

The project will be integrated to produce greater impact, improve outcomes and complement other projects being implemented in the same areas. The proposed interventions will be contributing to achieve the target under Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Specific Objective 1, Nutrition Sector Objectives (1, 2 and 3), Health Sector Objectives (1 & 2)Activity 1: Effective management of communicable diseases among children under five and adults for reduction of morbidity and mortality in supported facilities:Treatment and consultation services will be provided with community-based detection and referrals of childhood illnesses . The project will preposition itself for possible cholera and measles outbreaks within the supported LGAs through the procurement and supply of cholera investigation kits and logistic support to measles vaccination campaign. In the absence of any outbreak, the kits shall be handed over to SMOH at the end of the project. Surveillance data will be shared weekly with the existing surveillance unit through the established reporting mechanism for analysis and compilation of the sector surveillance report. AAH staff will provide technical supervision and follow up to ensure that supported health facilities are providing the integrated disease surveillance reports on a timely manner, any disease outbreak will be communicated to surveillance unit of the State Ministry of Health. Activity 2: Support the training of LGA Healthcare Workers and CHIPS on COVID-19 Infection, Prevention and Control :This activity is intended to ensure continual, updated, evidence-based preventive and treatment protocols are made available to the frontline health workers. This is meant to contribute effectively to improving the prevention of the transmission of COVID-19 and other infectious diseases among patients, patients to health workers and vice versa. The objective is also to ensure early detection, promote Risk communication and community engagement (RCCE), and to participate in health facilities’ surveillance of diseases for better treatment outcomes. Activity 3: Provision of reproductive health services to vulnerable women:AAH will provide support to antenatal (ANC), uncomplicated vaginal delivery, referral supports for complicated and post-natal care (PNC) services. High-risk pregnancy will be referred to the identified higher centre . ANC visits will include Clinical examination, Blood testing to detect syphilis and severe anaemia, malaria and HIV, gestational age estimation, uterine height, blood pressure, maternal weight and height, MUAC measurement, test for sexually transmitted infections (STIs), urine test, request blood type and Rh, Tetanus toxoid administration, Iron/folic acid supplementation, and recommendations for emergencies. All services will be provided along with counseling at the facility level to encourage mothers to visit at least 4 times during the pregnancy, attending post-natal care services immediately after birth, and linking with Action Against Hunger community health activities like mothers’ support groups for optimal IYCF practices. Mosquito nets shall be provided for women who registered for ANC. Activity 5: Provision of life-saving nutrition rehabilitation services to severely malnourished children 0 – 59 months including early identification, detection and prompt referral of SAM children: AAH will implement the management of Severe Acute Malnutrition in line humanitarian SPHERE Standard and National Guideline through: (i) the provision of quality outpatient therapeutic feeding (OTP) services for children aged 6-59 months with SAM without medical complications (ii) the provision of quality inpatient management/Stabilization Centre (SC) services for children of 0-59 months with medically complicated SAM. Activity 6 Promotion of an integrated Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), health and hygiene promotion practices: AAH will implement IYCF programme both at community and health facility levels

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Across all components of the programme, data collection and reporting would be done in a gender and socially inclusive manner. Thus, data would be disaggregated by age, gender, disability and other vulnerability markers such as head or member of single-parent household and unaccompanied/orphaned child. AAH will engage a full time Social Protection and Gender ￼Advisor and team to ensure mainstreaming throughout the lifecycle of the project. This role would coordinate a state-level team with gender focal persons at the LGA level. Due to the social dynamics characterised by the lower participation of women and girls in most communities, emphasis shall be given to the constitution of project teams with up to 70% female staff to ensure service access to the target beneficiaries. The team would conduct a baseline gender analysis/assessment in the first quarter of program implementation, an interim progress assessment at the end of the first year and an end of project assessment at project close out to measure the gender and inclusion gaps of the project and how they are met in the course of implementation

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participatory monitoring will ensure to get feedback from beneficiaries. This will be done through a feedback and complaint response mechanism established for all projects as a way of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints. These are embedded in the monitoring system and beneficiaries and stakeholders are sensitized on this feedback mechanism and toll-free number so that they understand how to express concerns relating to the project. Feedback on ACF activities will also be solicited during PDM and meetings with communities. Discussions with beneficiaries/ community leaders will be done regularly to address emerging issues and to ensure community obligations and participation in project implementation. This approach has helped to establish a relationship of trust between staff and communities in various projects and to improve the impact of the response.
