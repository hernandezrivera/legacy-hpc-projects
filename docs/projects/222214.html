Project Summary

Enhancing access to education in emergencies is an essential component of interventions for resumption and continuity of schooling for children in humanitarian situations. To this effect, the proposed project will contribute to the three Education Cluster strategic objectives of 1) Enabling emergency-affected children and youth to access age-appropriate, safe, protective, and inclusive learning; 2) Providing emergency affected girls and boys with minimum quality education for improved learning outcomes and to attain the minimum standard stipulated in the national learning competencies; and 3) Building capacity of government and other partners for effective and accountable coordination in preparedness, timely response, recovery and resilience programming. As reflected in the strategic objectives, besides its immediately protective impact, education is a critical input for South Sudan’s future, providing the next generation with the knowledge and skills for recovery and to forge more resilient, peaceful and prosperous communities (South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023-2025) in line with the triple nexus framework that emphases collaboration between humanitarian, development, and peace efforts.
Specifically, the project will target a total of 270,691 emergency affected boys, girls, youth, teachers, and PTA members in the priority 1, 2 and 3 counties of the South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy. It will contribute to enhancing access to safe and protective quality education services for all children (boys and girls), particularly the most vulnerable, including children with disabilities. Furthermore, the project will strengthen the coordination and crisis management capacity of the authorities and communities including education authorities, SMCs, and PTAs to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response.
To enhance access to education, the project will implement the following key activities: 1) Establish 38 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards; 2) Establish 52 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards; 3) Mobilize children and youth (3-17 years) to enroll in pre-primary, primary, and secondary education through Back to learning campaigns and girls' education dialogues; 4) Procure and distribute teaching and learning materials to 120,103 children and teachers, including dignity kits to adolescent girls in schools; 5)Train 1,272 teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies; and 6) Conduct training for 81 SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face the most challenging humanitarian crises. It is witnessing the impacts of conflict and climate-induced crises such as floods, drought, and heatwaves and worsening economic situation, which disproportionately affect children, including their access to education. The education system, in particular, faces important challenges in providing access to safe, protective, and inclusive quality education for boys, girls, and youth in the country. Multiple crisis drivers continue to have a negative impact on education services, disrupting access to education and learning (South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy 2023 - 2025). Risk of conflict remains high causing extensive displacements, exposure to gender-based violence including early pregnancies and forced marriages. This situation is happening against a backdrop of an overstretched education system with approximately 37 percent unqualified teachers, only 20 percent female teachers, an estimated 12,870 classrooms operating under trees or in the open, and 1,281 non-operational schools, mainly due to conflict. Due to many hardships, many children start school very late, the net enrolment rate in 2023 was 38 percent (AEC 2023). The education needs of children living in emergency affected areas and those in mobile/displaced communities or cattle camps remain grossly underserved. In 2024, according to the humanitarian needs overview, approximately 2 million children and adolescents between 6 and 17 years old (53% girls), along with 1 million children between 3 and 5 years old, residing in conflict and emergency-affected areas are in critical need of education services. This includes over 311,000 IDPs, 1,535,000 resident communities, 154,104 refugees and 189,000 returnees. The Sudan Crisis has exacerbated the status of education, of the over 700,000 people that have fled into South Sudan since April 2023, over 30 percent are school-aged children. Most of these children fleeing the Sudan war have lost over a year of schooling. Education in emergencies has consistently been underfunded and not always prioritized like other sectors. For example, in 2023, out of the $52 million required for humanitarian response, only 33 percent was funded affecting education of over 480,000 children. In the same year, the education received no allocation from the country-based pool funds.

2. Project activities

To address the main educational challenges described in the needs analysis and focus on the Education Cluster priorities identified in the strategy, the project will implement the following key activities: 1) Establish 38 learning spaces in line with INEE's Minimum Standards. 2) Establish 52 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE and sphere standards. 3) Mobilize children and youth (3-17 years) to enroll in pre-primary, primary, and secondary education through Back to learning campaigns and girls' education dialogues. 4) Procure and distribute teaching and learning materials to 120,103 (47% girls) children and teachers, including dignity kits to adolescent girls in schools. 5)Train 1,272 teachers (20% females) on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies. 6) Conduct training for 81 SMC/PTA members and education authorities (30% females) on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
