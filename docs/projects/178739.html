Project Summary

Fire incidents continue to be a prevalent concern in IDP sites in north-west Syria (NWS) where already vulnerable displaced populations face regular risks to themselves, their shelters and belongings as a result of these incidents. Notably, the majority of IDP sites in NWS are self-settled (over 90% according to the sites listed in the CCCM database) and often lack proper infrastructure, site planning and camp management systems; which increases potential fire hazards and creates particular challenges in responding to incidents, such as access in an emergency situation. According to CCCM’s multi-sectoral service monitoring factsheet (published in May 2020) fire points were needed in 96% of the reported IDP sites
To improve living conditions of vulnerable conflict-affected households, to support them with winterization assistance during the coldest months, MSO is planning to respond to 3430 HHs, 20150 IDPs in 14 camps (Kansafra, Abn Taymeah, Umhat Almomene, Alfardan, Dalama, Al Wafaa, Reef Aleppo Al Janobi, Jabal Al-Hos, Ghetaa Al Rahma2, Nour Al Houda, Al Forkan, Al Sufara'a. Al Amal and Al Khan) to ensure covering critical gaps in most urgent winterization needs in camps.
MSO will establish fire points every 15 tents where a fire extinguisher will be provided for each fire point. Fire points will be clear and easy to access. training will be provided for camp management on fire prevention, mitigation and response measures. Establish fire response committees in camps Its responsibility is to develop a system to contain fires and mitigate their severity and risks. Committee will be responsible for training staff, including camp guards and people involved in the use of fire extinguishers, and how to deal with fires if they occur.
Conducting awareness campaigns and sessions for all camp residents about the dangers of fire, taking all safety and security measures, especially during winter season, and lighting the heating in a safe way within tents.
Principles of Do No Harm will be applied in all activities through several market visits, distribution and we will coordinate with NGOs to avoid any overlap.
in case of underfunding, priority will be given to the most essential activities in the proposed project which includes the provision of fire fighting equipment and establishing of fire points. provision of fire prevention training and development of contingency plan. the selected communities are the ones with no existing fire equipment or previous experience with fire prevention measures or skills.

4 - Needs

According to a recent assessment conducted by MSO in the region and secondary sources of information, there is a need to maintain the provision of adequate services. In conjunction with the start of the winter season, operating the heating within the tents have been started, this resulted in many and great dangers due to the great possibility of ignition of fires, due to the harsh living conditions and lack of awareness among the camp's residents. During the assessment, it is been noted that there are no preventive or protection measures were taken during the use of the heaters and the absence of fire breaks between the tents or any equipment for extinguishing the fire or people trained to use it. Contingency plans also not existed, as the targeted camps are devoid of fire extinguishing equipment and there are no committees responsible for that, and there is no evacuation plan in the event of an accident.To confirm these needs, MSO assessed the emergency situation of in-need people in 14 camps of Harim district in Idleb governorates. The assessment was held in first-March 2021, and it was conducted through household interviews and Key Informant Interviews. The assessment concluded the following key findings: - 100% of the interviewees confirmed that many people are facing shortages in NFI. Where the most urgent NFI items needed are fuel for heating, children's clothing, stove, or heater, 94% of the interviewees confirmed there was no support in terms of NFI items in the last period. - All respondents confirmed that all of the NFI items are available in the markets in most locations, but still there are few items that are not- In all the targeted camps, there is no equipment or staff specialized in preventing fire.

5 - Implementation of activities

the proposed project will respond to the needs with a comprehensive response approach according to the CCCM cluster fire prevention guideline which includes community awareness-raising, capacity building and training. establishing fire response committee, Establish fire warden systems and contingency plan, and create fire points and distribute fire fighting equipment. 1- Create Fire Points and Distribute Fire Fighting EquipmentFire in camps is the most deadly and destructive risk. Whilst they cannot stop smaller fires, fire breaks can prevent fires from spreading and becoming catastrophic, and can save lives. Mitigating actions should include leaving sufficient spaces between tents in addition to the provision of fire points (one for every 15 tents) where a fire extinguisher will be provided for each fire point. Fire points will be clear and easy to access.2-Establish fire response committees in camps with a view to empower people to be able to preventand respond to possible fire incidents where they live. 3- Establishment of contingency planning with firefighting system and evacuation plan.4- Organize on the ground effective fire prevention, mitigation and response training/workshops for camp management staff, including Organize training through developing a fire warden system at IDP sites, including identifying and training wardens and backup wardens on roles and responsibilities.Through training and sessions, raise awareness of the fire prevention and response and IDPs communities on protection mainstreaming imitative and risks related. identify protection risks in the camps Pathway rehab.Ensure reporting and monitoring the provided services in the camps to identify gaps

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

MSO will work with staff, stakeholders, and other actors to ensure gender, as well as protection considerations, are mainstreamed into all activities by prioritizing consideration of the specific and often sensitive issues related to gender, disability, age, and protection among the beneficiary populations. This will be emphasized in the collection and analysis of data disaggregated by age and sex and key informant interviews conducted by relevant staff throughout the project cycle with both mixed and single-sex groups. This information will help in adjusting the project’s emergency program design to meet gender-specific needs, disparities, and vulnerabilities affecting targeted populations. MSO take all aspects - gender, diversity, and age - into account in its project cycle management:• Assessment: M&E carries out consulting meetings with women and girls on water and sanitation needs to ensure program strategies that mitigate exposure to risk.• Implementation: (1) Women and girls are consulted to ensure they feel and are safe, especially when receiving hygiene kits, winterization assistance. (2) Adequate numbers of women are involved in focus group discussions. (3) The female staff is recruited for WASH interventions and NFI assistance in order to mobilize women and girls from diverse groups and to facilitate feedback from these groups.• M&E: Extra feedback from children and women is also specifically collected through FGDs• Include women and girls in user-satisfaction surveys, as both respondents and interviewersThe field teams will visit the locations days by day to ensure that clean water is provided for women, girls, boys, and men.
