Project Summary

Integration and institutionalization of C4D/Communication with Communities (CwC) approaches and interventions into health, hyegine promotion and GBV for sustained long-term and at-scale efforts to prevent, mitigate and response of Coronovirus Disease-COVID-19. Strengthening the role of religious leaders towards increasing community ownership and participation in host and camps. Continued support to strengthen community accountability through information feedback centres and closing the community feedback loop. Intensified and innovative multi-media messaging with effective, tailor-made, contextualized and language appropriate radio programmes and visuals involving national celebraties and brand ambassdors.

Needs

The World Health Organization (WHO) declared COVID-19 a ‘Public Health Emergency of International Concern’ on 30 January 2020 and it was declared a pandemic on 11 March 2020. As of 28 April 2020, there were 2 ,954, 222 cases of COVID–19 reported globally, and 202 597 deaths reported according to the WHO COVID-19 Situation Report. This includes 6462 confirmed cases of COVID-19 registered in Bangladesh.The first laboratory confirmed case was identified in Cox’s Bazar on 23 March. As of 28 April, Cox's Bazar district has registered 21 positive cases and no confirmed cases of COVID-19 have been reported to date in the Rohingya camps. The UNICEF 2018 Knowledge, Attitude, Practice and Behaviour (KABP) baseline survey confirmed the importance of face-to-face communication. Eighty-eight per cent of refugees described Mahjis as a source of information. Community mobilizers were the second most common source of information (28 per cent) while 20 per cent of refugees get information from religious leaders. Eighty-five per cent of participants in a recent survey did not feel safe having Rohingya refugees nearby. They perceive that the influx has led to an increase in the cost of living (79 per cent), traffic accidents (74 per cent), and crime (24 per cent) . To address the information needs and gaps in responding to COVID-19 in Cox's Bazar, UNICEF as a key member of the Risk Communication Technical Working Group (RCTWG) at Cox’s Bazar level, and supports technical assistance, production, dissemination and distribution of communication materials and community engagement and community engagement interventions in Cox's Bazar. Through the project, UNICEFand partners will leverage to maintain and sustain the community engagement interventions in the camp and host communities level through community mobilization and religious leaders volunteers networks and Islamic Foundation . Language-appropriate radio and offline audio programmes and pictorial IEC materials have addressed some of the communication gaps in information provision, but continued to be produced for 2019 . UNICEF will try strategize and devise mechanisms and actions to address the needs of the most vulnerable and high risk population such as boys, girls, women, elderly and People with Disability with tailor-made and age and language appropriate community engagement packages and interventions. Adosclent boys and girls will be mobilized and engaged as agent of change in building awaress across communities. In close collaboration with CSO, NGO, government and UN agencies, the project will engage and mobilize communities in DRR and natural disaster preparations and responses. Accountability will remain a key UNICEF priority in the years to come as well. UNICEF partners to collect feedback, grievances or complaints on COVID-19 and respond to them with effective and timely data sharing and evidence generation with other agencies/sectors. UNICEF will collect and compile potential rumors and will develop key messages to address the rumors.

Activities or outputs

House-to-house IPC sessions and dialogue on key lifesaving behaviors and referrals to services with a particular focus on health, WASH , GBV and DRR in the Rohingya camps and host communities.Engaging religious, community leaders and institutions to ensure community participation and ownership through community consultations, meeting and loudspeaker messaging at strategic locations with a particular focus to address the need of boys, girls, elderly and PWD. Recording, reporting of rumors and recording as well as redressal of complaints, feedback and queries for voicing needs/concerns, including feedback and complaint mechanisms through 18 Information Feedback Centres (IFCs) in the Rohingya camps and host communities. Capacity development of humanitarian workers and volunteers of UNICEF partners on key COVID-19 RCCE messages, tools and techniques
