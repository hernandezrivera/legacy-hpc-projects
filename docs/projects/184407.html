Project Summary

Humanitarian needs continue to rise in Somalia due to the protracted political crisis, armed conflict, climate change implications, desert locust infestation, and COVID-19 pandemic. Based on FSNAU Gu Assessment carried out in June and July2021, an estimated 3.5 million people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) between October and December 2021 due to multiple risk factors. The post Gu 2021 nutrition assessment has
indicated critical malnutrition situation among the IDPs in Banadir, Baidoa, Bossaso, Hargeisa and
Galkayo. The post Gu 2021 nutrition assessment findings also showed marginal decrease in the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among children aged 6-59 months of 11.1 % down from 11.8% of post Gu 2020 and 13.8% of post Gu 2019. Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence among children aged 6-59 months is estimated at 1.5% in 2021 down from 1.8% in 2020 post Gu. Despite improvements in prevalence of acute malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months, still an estimated 1.28 million children aged 6-59 months are expected to face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 295,515 severely acute malnourished children. Urgent nutrition and health support are required to address their needs. Under 2022 HRP, UNICEF and implementing partners will target an estimated 236,222 SAM children (120,474 girls and115,748 boys) i.e. 80% of the SAM burden and 1,142,002 pregnant and lactating women ie. 75% of the total needs. UNICEF will ensure provision of nutrition services through life cycle approach. Target nutrition sites will function as a platform for the
delivery of integrated multi sector services particularly Nutrition, Health, WASH, C4D, CP/GBV and referral for other services in collaboration with other sectors/organizations. UNICEF recognizes that girls, boys, men, women and people with disabilities have different nutritional needs at different life stages with different capacities and risks in accessing nutrition services. UNICEF and implementing partners will support preventive and lifesaving nutrition interventions through provision of maternal, infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN), treatment of children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), capacity building and robust information systems nationwide. UNICEF as sole provider for SAM nutrition supplies, will ensure to procure nutrition supplies to nutrition cluster partners including SHF recipients, SHINE and BRICSconsortia. The estimated total cost of delivering the UNICEF nutrition HRP project is estimated at USD 40,627,208 for the year 2022.

1. - Projects activities

Various interventions and activities will be implemented by UNICEF and partners to respond to the needs. The planned activities are: 1. Community mobilization and active screening for early case detection of children with SAM and referral. 2. Provide individual targeted and group counselling on MIYCN to caregivers of children aged below 24 months, including men. 3. Provide integrated treatment to children (girls, boys) with SAM at static facilities and through community outreach. 4. Provide integrated care for children with SAM in OTP/Stabilization Centers (SC) in health facilities, including WASH services and ECD at nutrition sites. 5. Procurement and distribution of lifesaving nutrition supplies including ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) to nutrition center/implementing partners.6. Conduct training on the revised IMAM guideline for health/nutrition workers national, state and regional level. 7. Train health and nutrition workers on MIYCN at national, state, regional and community level. 8. Monitor the progress and quality of nutrition interventions and services at the static nutrition facilities and outreach sites. 9. support nutrition cluster/sectoral coordination at national and state level.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

To prevent sexual violence and sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance, UNICEF continue to organize training and capacity building of implementing partners on prevention and response to sexual exploitation. UNICEF continues to stand firm on zero tolerance for (sexual) misconduct. Therefore, all UNICEF implementing partners, are obliged to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. Managers at all levels have a responsibility to support and develop systems that maintain this environment, are committed to the protection of vulnerable populations in humanitarian crisis, including from sexual exploitation and abuse. By entering into an agreement with UNICEF and implementing partner undertakes to adhere to: (i) the standards set out in the Secretary-General’s Bulletin “Special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and sexual abuse” (ST/SGB/2003/13); (ii) any minimum operating standards adopted because of the Statement of Commitment on Eliminating Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. (iii) any other Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy or guideline. Accountability to affected population and need based principle: UNICEF and implementing partner will ensure affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence. Therefore, UNICEF and partnerswill adhere the principles of humanitarian principles including "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project effaced population and beneficiaries. women, men, girls and boys will be engaged in decision-making and enhanced their resilience / capacity to prevent and response to severe acute malnutrition
