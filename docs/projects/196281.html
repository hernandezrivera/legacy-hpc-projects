Project Summary

SARD is committed to assisting the most affected people in northwest Syria, despite unpredictability and the risks of working in areas with security threats. The response plan will address the needs for the people over the course of 12 months, providing life-saving and life-sustaining assistance. The action contributes to improving shelters for selected most vulnerable persons including IDPs who have been recently displaced or displaced multiple times.
Implemented with a protection lens, and addressing gender and disabled-specific needs, the shelter response is aimed at providing housing options in emergency and life-sustaining situations. The activities include emergency shelter in camp settings, temporary shelter solutions in open fields, life-saving shelter upgrades, and life-sustaining house rehabilitation through a self-help approach. The modality of intervention will be adapted to the urgency, situation, needs, and HLP situation of the beneficiaries. SARD is committed to promoting social cohesion including shelter programming based on community approach. The shelter activities are detailed as follows:
-Upgrade of IDP sites/camps including infrastructure improvements, Road graveling, tent insulation, and tent replacement to cope with flood season. Upgrade of IDP sites/camps will also include the shift to dignified shelters where SARD will replace the tents with better shelter solutions that will provide safe, secure, and longer-term accommodation to the beneficiaries. SARD’s team in coordination with CCCM, SNFI clusters, and local authorities proceeded with the assessment and the selection of the most vulnerable IDP sites/camps camp.
-Emergency shelter option with the distribution of shelter kit by SARD’s team.
-Collective shelter upgrade and unfinished building repair by contractors under SARD’s technical supervision.
-House rehabilitation through provision of cash grants under SARD’s supervision.
-Site improvement on both the HHs and the site level by contractors and under direct SARD’s technical supervision.

3 - Needs

The draft 2023 HNO for Shelter indicated: It is estimated that 5.5 million people in Syria will require shelter support in 2023, which is a decrease of 8 percent from 2022. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 percent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelters, of which 19 percent reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues’. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified. With some situational displacement expected to continue, and a potential increase in the rate of return (IDP and refugee), the scale and severity of need for shelter support is projected to remain through 2023, with IDPs and returnees being the worst affected by poor shelter conditions. In 2022, SARD’s conducted a YNA. Findings showed that the vast majority of household respondents reported a certain level of damage in their residence as follows: 38% Minor damages (damages that can be easily repaired), 29% Moderate damages (e.g. holes in roof and many parts of wall, partial glass for windows, and some windows/doors broken), and 14% Severe damages (e.g. many holes in roof, large holes in walls, windows/doors damaged or destroyed). On the other hand, 33% reported facing environmental hazards mainly wind damage and flooding.

4 - Implementation of activities

To address the shelter needs as described above, shelter assistance will be provided ranging from emergency interventions with shelter kits, road graveling (include tent insulation, and tent replacement), house rehabilitation, shelter upgrade, and community projects. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have access to life-saving assistance through emergency basic shelter kits in the open field and quick-fi xing at collective shelters/unfinished buildings. The action provides also life-sustaining shelter support to most vulnerable families including house rehabilitation through cash grants. In addition to this, SARD will be upgrading IDP sites/camps on both the site infrastructure level and the shelter unit level. Activities will take place as follows: assessment of the security, locations, tenders, and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash, voucher, or in-kind). Identification and targeting of beneficiaries, registration, preparation of BoQs, distributions/implementation, monitoring, and post- monitoring, and close coordination with relevant clusters, including sharing the4Ws matrix and identifying gaps. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria and scoring mechanism, taking into consideration HLP documentation. Bill of Quantities will be prepared for shelter upgrades in close consultation with the beneficiaries. For self-help modality (cash grants).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women-headed HH, PwDs, may be excluded from accessing the service due to socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD increases visibility for self-referral to ensure the inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries to categorize them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target of the project may lead, in some cases, to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitor the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a robust CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities; currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1) Risk: Beneficiaries of SARD services may be targeted for sexual exploitation/abuse by a staff member.Likelihood: low, Impact: highMitigation: Staff is aware of the PSEA policies and undergo regular refreshing training. 9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

I. Safety and Security Risk The volatile and often unpredictable nature of the Syrian conflict continues to impose significant risk on humanitarian staff and beneficiaries' safety. Risks could include but are not limited to a sudden escalation in hostilities, airstrikes, armed clashes, and kidnapping. The likelihood of increased conflict hostilities is considered moderate in northern Aleppo compared to opposition-controlled areas of Idleb and western Aleppo. The impact of this risk is assessed as high in both areas. SARD has developed and adapted tools to ensure a sufficient level of standard of care to safeguardemployees and beneficiaries from a reasonably foreseeable risk of harm is maintained. II. Operations and Program-related Risks The high-risk environment of northwest Syriacontinues to impose significant risk on the continuation of humanitarian activities. Risksto humanitarian programs implementation in the context of northwest Syria include butnot limited to partial or complete closure of border crossings with Turkey; disruption of thesupply chain; fluctuating exchange rate; and community acceptance. The likelihood andthe impact of operations and program-related risks in areas of northern Aleppo, Idleb, andwestern Aleppo were assessed as high. SARD has developed and adapted tools to ensure its readiness to cope and adjust with sudden unexpected risks as they arise. III. Financial and Reputation Risks The absence of a fully functioning banking system in NWS.
