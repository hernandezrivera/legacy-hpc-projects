Project Summary

The project aims to address the problems of high morbidity and mortality rate and common diseases: Malaria, Diarrhea, Pneumonia, Measles and cholera, a situation brought about by limited number of functional health facilities, scarcity of supplies and flooding seasons, not only that, it will also reduce excessive morbidity mortality by timely detection and coordinate response to epidemic prone diseases as well as improve equitable to life-saving essential, provision of quality comprehensive package of basic healthcare
The beneficiaries will be provided with essential primary healthcare services in static, outreaches and mobile health facilities using qualified, Clinical officers, Midwives, Nurses and other health care providers. They will also be provided with mosquito nets, blankets and water chlorination tabs that will increase drinking safe and clean water, which is favorable for human use, the staff will provide independent services and coordinate with developmental health providers and CHD to synergy and capacity build the CHD staff where necessary.
Based on latest projections, in 2024, the health needs of more than 2 million IDPs, including over 74,000 people in Malakal County of the Upper Nile State according to the latest IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), and more than 500 thousand refugees and returnees from Sudan will remain high. They will require essential life-saving health services, such as prevention, preparedness, response readiness, and response. To improve the access and quality of health care, there is a need to ensure coordination and integration between Health, Nutrition, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and food security and livelihoods (FSL) sectors. There is also a need to strengthen community health services through Basic Health Initiative (BHI) and community engagement HNO.
This project will positively contribute to health cluster objectives Objective1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations. This cluster objective is linked to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the 2024 HRP.
Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases. This cluster objective is linked to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2 of the 2024 HRP.
Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. This cluster objective is linked to Strategic Objectives 3 of the 2024 HRP.
Impact on the well-being of the affected vulnerable groups, at the area around life expectancy South Sudan is ranked to have remained in top 10 of world 58 years in 2020 and 2021 respectively according to HNO report, this has made the under-five mortality rate remained higher with 96.2 percent, this had remained driven by shortage of quality healthcare centers and sometime inadequate health services in some areas across the country, therefore this project will enhance adequate provision of quality healthcare services in the areas of Ayod and Fangak Counties.

1. Needs

Ayod and Fangak Counties have the highest disease burden in Jonglei State and are susceptible to triggers such as widespread insecurity, inaccessibility and disease outbreaks. The situation is aggravated by recurrent spates of floods that result in multiple displacement and severe damage of health infrastructure. This, coupled with shortage of qualified medical personnel, weak cold chain system (Ayod-40%, Fangak-51%) DHIS-2 and insufficient vaccines availability (hence 58% average) which continue to impede access to health services. Referral of patients in Ayod remains dysfunctional with provision of safe CEmONC absent due to cut-offs and lack of infrastructure. The available referral facilities are Old Fangak and Lankien; a distance of up to 7 and 3 days walk respectively. As per DHIS-2 report, the current health status of the population is poor and characterized by high malaria morbidity and mortality, with 50.2% of cases and 39.4% of deaths attributed to malaria; low measles vaccination coverage, with only 66% of children vaccinated and measles outbreaks reported since December 2022 in both counties; Hepatitis E in Bentiu IDP (Internally Displaced Persons). according to UNIGME 2022, the infant mortality rate is high at 84 deaths per 1000 live births, and the under-five mortality rate is even higher at 98.7 deaths per 1000 live births. Moreover, floods have caused devastating impacts on the population’s health and well-being. Initial rapid needs assessments (IRNA) conducted in 2022 indicated that over 909 thousand people were affected by torrential rains, out of which over 140 thousand were displaced from their homes in 29 counties that included Ayod and Fangak Counties.The country dependency on humanitarian aids provision had been a challenge living a lot of gaps unclose due to insufficient resources cause by the huge needs across the world since the influx of refugees and IDPs is a global concerns in almost all the world continents, As South Sudan is amongst the countries with huge humanitarian needs, health remained the great concerns, living as health cluster PiN indicate, 5, 467, 710 are seeking health services across the country, and HNO report indicate that, 5.5m, 50% representing women, 52% for children and 15% are people living with physical impairments, all are in critical needs of health services, therefore provisions of quality essential healthcare services shall be the top priority of this project, in doing so, qualify health professional shall be recruited and deployed across the healthcare units and center to reduce maternal mortality, this will be achieve through professional midwives to deliver mothers, EPI services to be intensify to help reduce childhood diseases, Episcopal Development Aid -EDA, will mobilize medicine from MoH, WHO, Health cluster and other stakeholders to be preposition at the sites, there shall also be intensive awareness on infection preventions and diseases control, facilities, coordinate with other actors to strengthen the referral pathway with GBV victims for further management, nutrition providers for nutrition cases management, we will integrate our existing health project with SSHF project and coordinate with other actors in the areas. Unpresented humanitarian needs continue to be high and brought about by the following factors:• Climatic shocks - Floods and droughts, led to displacements, destructions of health facilities, cut off road linkages due to fear on highways, destruction of homestead, wash off of roads by either flooding or rainy water. • Sub-national and inter - communal conflicts, coupled with widespread insecurity, affected livelihoods and loss of productive assets as well as reduction in ownership of livestock amongst the counties.• Macroeconomic crisis, causing continuous depreciation of the South Sudan Pound and corresponding skyrocketing of prices and thereby eroding the purchasing power of commodities who rely on markets to purchase.

2. Project activities

1. Recruitment and deployment of healthcare workers i.e. Clinical Officers, Nurses, Midwives and field highly technical team to supervise the junior clinicians. 2. Early detection of epidemic diseases, carry out intensive surveillance, IPC practices, investigation and timely reporting to the next level of care within 48 hrs. 3. Intensifying expanded activities on immunization by administering all the childhood diseases penta3 targeting 400 children below 1 year. (192 male) and (208 female) in Ayod County. 4. Provision of life-saving healthcare services to manage and prevent communicable and non-communicable diseases throughout the lifespan of the project in either static, mobile and outreaches at (Hospitals, PHCCs, and PHCUs) supplying other significant medical equipment that may include, BeMONC equipment, midwifery kits amongst other. 5. Referral of children and mothers through community health-based services, the like of Boma health initiative, this will be achieved through coordinating with other actors and existing of our health project funded by the world bank in Ayod, this will help to identify and report the children who missed their interval doses to receive full coverage. 6. Requesting medicines and other medical supplies, the like of vaccines from; WHO, mosquito nets from PSI, UNICEF, MoH, UNFPA, Health Clusters as well as finding transportation through Logistics cluster WFP for emergency transport that will support the project. 7. Health education and promotion through hygiene and keys health messages (men 1500, female 2500, boys 2700, girls 3300). 8. Training of healthcare providers through on job and special training sessions on CMR, HIV, COVID-19, IPC, and on how to report suspected outbreaks and how they can response to.
