Project Summary

Ongoing armed conflict and insecurity, as well as cyclical climatic shocks, amid compounding political and socioeconomic factors, continue to drive and impact the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. Such factors have resulted in protracted economic vulnerability and the loss of livelihoods, particularly among agro-pastoralists, rural pastoralists and riverine farmers.Violence and limited access to humanitarian assistance and basic social services in rural and hard-to-reach areas has spurred mass population movement toward urban and peri-urban areas, further straining the limited resources and absorption capacity of host communities, this has been worsened by global COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent flash floods. The combined impact increasingly exposes households to risks of violence, exploitation and abuse. As a result, in 2020 3 million Somalis in IDPs, host communities, returnees and refugees are in need of humanitarian assistance across the country of these 51% are female, however, a total of a total of 148,829 displaced and 24,747 non-displaced person in Baidoa, 16,980 displaced and 24,143 non displaced person in Beletweyne as well as 2,572, displaced ,10,119 non-displaced in Ceelbarde and 2,153 and 400 displaced and non-displaced in Rabdhure are in need of physical and mental health as well as in humanitarian crisis.

Confirmed cholera cases from the beginning of the year to epidemiological week 31 (2nd August 2020) recorded 5,341 cases, 29 deaths with a case fatality rate (CFR) of 0.5% ,this coupled with COVID-19 pandemic which continues to spread in Somalia with a total of 3,212 COVID-19 cases confirmed; including 93 associated deaths as of second August 2020. The large majority (83 %) of cases are aged between 20 and 60 years, with a Case Fatality Rate (CFR) 3% and 1,827 recoveries. Since the onset of the outbreak in Somalia, 3212 laboratory-confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported including 93 associated deaths. The large majority (83 %) of cases are aged between 20 and 60 years, with a median age of 32 years (ranging from 1 to 110 years). As in previous weeks, 73% of the laboratory-confirmed cases were amongst male. The number of samples tested during this reporting week is higher than in previous weeks, but the positivity rate is significantly lower than in previous weeks (from 6.7% the previous week to 3% during this reporting week)
Therefore, the project intend to support life-saving emergency healthcare to an estimated 12,200 crises affected boys, girls, Women, men including disable beneﬁciaries through 10 static and 8 outreach/mobile clinics in Bay, Bakool and Hiraan regions
Emphasis will be on maternal, neonatal and child health including emergency immunization to the host communities and Internally Displaced People (IDPs). This action will
focus on provision of both preventive and curative health services at primary level integrated with nutrition and WASH activities to provide holistic and integrated support. Through deployment of trained health workers including midwives and community health workers in facilities and mobile teams, essential primary care services including treatment of common childhood illnesses, as well as emergency obstetric and neonatal care for pregnant and lactating women will be provided. HIDIG an actor in the target regions will leverage on existing support & activities to ensure the gains made in the post-drought response are not rolled back. Building the capacity of health workers through training on Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmONC) will be done to equip health workers with the required skills to deliver quality health care services to the affected population. The project will ensure to provide mental health and psychosocial service support, clinical case management of rape and inclusion of gender and age appropriate gender mainstreaming to vulnerable boys,girls, women and men including disable people hence improving their health care

Mitigation Measures

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Beletweyne, therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing health facilities may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive package of health services within the IDP camps by establishing health facilities in consultation with the host communities and health authorities. Where not possible outreach and mobile teams will provide needed services. To promote communal cohesion, host communities will be provided with access to camp health facilities without any impediment. Based on the Health Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the IDPs and host communities, Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of aged people and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health infrastructure, many facilities do not have proper planning so that aged patients and those with disabilities can easily seek services. To overcome this, HIDIG will ensure that our facilities can support aged patients and those with disability. Our referral system will promote assisted delivery of services to ease their challenges. Sexual and reproductive health needs of women of child bearing age will be prioritized. Also, services will be ensured that they reach the most vulnerable through identification and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services will be accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. To support women seeking services, female workers will be recruited. To ensure equity, outreach teams will be used to reach rural communities HIDIG will also building community structures including local health committees will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through mobile and outreach teams. Where IDPs are involved, facilities that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence

Please provide further explanation

Based on the cluster objectives, the response strategy includes the need to deliver a core package of integrated services, where priority will be given to the delivery of life-saving services, while simultaneously addressing underlying causes of exposure to the risk of malnutrition, disease outbreaks and poor sanitation and hygiene. To increase community uptake of provided services, community level demand creation will be strengthened. HIDIG will strengthen facility level quality service delivery by training staff to deliver quality services. Communities will be involved in project designation and will be part of the facility level committees supporting district level health committees. Working with other actors will ensure leverage amongst themselves and providing maximum support to target communities strengthening their capacities in various aspectsThe project is however, meant to build community resilience by strengthening local healthcare infrastructure and thus provide timely action, maintaining momentum of the response, and expanding reach in underserved areas promoting equity
