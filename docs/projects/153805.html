Project Summary

Approximately 6 million South Sudanese face multiple protection threats and risks resulting from active conflicts, displacement, and breakdown of community protection systems and structures exacerbated by economic decline, lack of basic services and disease burden (Protection Cluster HRP 2019 Strategy). 184,129 persons of concerns are in need of protection assistance in Duk and Twic East (Jonglei) and Awerial (Lakes). 156,360 of these people are IDPs. 61% of the affected people are children, 2% are elderly and 50.4% are women. This project will target 35,000 persons of concern (13,000 girls, 12,000 boys, 6,000 women, 4,000 men).
Duk Payuel experienced deadly attack in late 2017 leaving 51 children missing, 42 dead and houses burned down. Similar attack happened in Duk Poktap on 30 October 2018 where 6 people died and hundreds of herds of cattle raided. Mingkaman IDP camp in Awerial hosts the largest caseload of IDP in the country. Poor protection indicators in these locations include insecurity, lack of freedom of movement, lack of access to basic services, precarious incidents of SGBV, child abduction, and increased number of people with special needs who lack services, and lack of land tenure systems.
To ensure that individuals get protection assistance from multiple threats and risks, CINA will provide case management to persons of concerns including IPA and community assistance, provision of NFIs, protection monitoring and risk assessments, and community safety audits, advocate for access to humanitarian services and promoting peaceful coexistence and youth resilience programming.
To ensure centrality of protection, this project will address risks facing children, women, elderly persons, people with special needs and survivors of violence and abuse. CINA’s staff will sign and adhere to code of conduct and CP policy to ensure safety, dignity and avoid causing harm. The team will conduct community and consultations on risk perceptions at NFIs distribution sites, etc and conduct monthly beneficiary feedback mechanisms on quality of protection services delivered by CINA as a means for Accountability to Affected Populations. To ensure synergies, the project will be coordinated with Protection Cluster, UNHCR, OCHA, RRC and other humanitarian partners in Lakes and Jonglei. CINA therefore, will require USD 400,000 to provide emergency protection assistance to persons of concerns in Lakes and Jonglei.

Needs

On 28 November 2017, Duk Payuel 12 kilometers north of Duk Poktap came under heavy armed attack and reportedly 44 people were killed, 51 children abducted, 19 injured, over 1,300 cattle raided and 567 houses burnt. Duk Payuel PHCU supported by John Dau Foundation was attacked medicines and other supplies destroyed. CRS rubhall storing WFP supplies was as well broken into and 2.3 MT of food looted. Similar attacked happened on 30 October 2018.Mingkaman in Awerial remains the largest IDP camp in the country hosting 102,413 IDPs. Inter-communal violence in Yolakot and Kalthoc in March 2018 increased the IDP burden, straining the already limited protection services for the displaced children in Mingkaman. The conflict affected people live in fear and panic in Duk Payuel. The CINA’s active FTR caseload for September 2018 in Duk, Twic East and Awerial is 453 UASC awaiting FTR case management. SGBV incidents against adolescent girls are being reported in Duk and survivors lack referral services. The protection needs of elderly women and men and persons with disabilities are usually underestimated. Most lack NFIs, wheelchairs, crutches, walking sticks, clothing, etc. Community-based protection mechanisms are wrecked and social support systems have been devastated. In Duk, there is lack of protection information on protection risks and perceptions of affected people. While humanitarian agencies are present in Duk, Awerial and Twic East, there are no well coordinated safe referral services and information on availability of services for persons of concern. Elder persons, SGBV survivors and other persons of concerns lack case management services including individual protection assistance. In Twic East East and Duk, there are severe episodes of psychosocial distress and trauma noticed among the elderly and survivors of violence. As a result of widespread poverty, violence and displacement, there are no socio-economic support activities for the elderly and other POCs. Instead of using youth as agents for change and protection risks mitigation, they are being used as fighters in inter-communal violence, especially in Awerial and Duk. There are no youth empowerment opportunities to engage them. While Revitalized Peace Agreement has been signed, there is still lack of peace dissemination among the conflicting communities and still pockets of violence in Duk and Yirol East are ongoing. With peace prospects, returnees and IDPs are expected to move back to places of origin to Twic East and Duk where their lands have already been occupied. The IDPs and returnees also lack land for cultivation. Protection preventative and response services will be therefore required for the most vulnerable including survivors of violence and great support will be required to protect HLP rights of returnees and IDPs to obtain land for settlement and farming, without causing more conflicts. There is also need to strengthen peace building, reconciliation, and trauma healing and civic engagement by empowering youth and women.

Activities

The proposed protection interventions will include: 1. Protection risks assessment and monitoring2. Comprehensive case management for persons of concerns identified3. Psychosocial support (PSS) for the elderly, survivors of violence and other persons of concern4. Individual Protection Assistance (IPA). CINA will ensure a concrete and well coordinated case management for the IPA component by first conducting protection monitoring and assessments to identify cases in need of IPA. Using a vulnerability criteria (to be agreed with PC), IPA cases (including of: SGBV, murder, disappearance/family separation, forced recruitment, underage recruitment, unlawful detention, land disputes, Documentation issues, and cases of extreme vulnerability related to illness, poverty, number of dependents, etc) will be prioritized for IPA case management services. IPA case package will include NFIs, cash assistance (for some selected trial cases); referral for socio-economic reintegration especially for youth and child survivors of violence, CMR for SGBV survivors, FTR/tracing actions for separated and unaccompanied minors, legal aid for unlawful detention cases, referral for food assistance for those in extreme need, etc. Referral pathways will be developed and strengthened within the target locations. To ensure safe referrals, CINA will train protection monitors who will ensure beneficiaries have informed consent obtained before referral. The teams will conduct follow ups and provide feedback to beneficiaries on availability of services. Some IPA cases may be accompanied by protection monitors to access referral services. The team will keep confidentiality, credibility and integrity and sensitivity when providing IPA assistance. CINA will use its existing case management capacity to provide effective IPA case management including both for database and physical case management to persons of concerns.5. Community-based protection6. Protection awareness raising & prevention messaging; Community awareness raising and sensitization on fundamental rights, etc7. Protective socio-economic or material support to vulnerable people or persons of concerns8. Youth programming (youth as agents of change for protection risks mitigation and community preparedness and resilience)9. Peacebuilding and peaceful co-existence

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project will provide awareness sessions on the availability of protection services within the community, particularly through establishment of friendly centers for children, youth and women as entry points. Awareness activities on prevention and response to protection issues by the community members will be presented on the banners, leaflets, and posters and displayed in the CFS/youth centers, schools, churches, and during community gatherings. Preventive messages will be passed through local radios (especially in Mingkaman). Community volunteers will be trained to relay specific messages in local languages to children and their caregivers and entire community.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Specific messages on prevention of family separation support to child survivors’ referral services, dangers of child recruitment, unlawful detention, land disputes, protection of the elderly, prevention of SGBV, peace and reconciliation, women and youth rights, child marriages and child abduction practices, and prevention of psychosocial distress will be produced/printed and widely disseminated to boys, girls, women and men. Messages on where women, elderly, boys and girls and OPCs can find protection assistance will be provided through the protection helpdesks. How and where to report grave rights abuses and violence against children and women occurring within the community.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CINA staff and volunteers will be trained to ensure adherence to IASC PSEA guidelines throughout the project cycle. Each will sign a Code of Conduct. All CINA staffs are subjected to enhanced reference checks up on taking job offer. A commitment statement on PSEA is found on CINA’s job adverts. CINA will ensure that staff are trained and uphold humanitarian principles including, of neutrality, impartiality and participation, and where feasible, CINA team will remain diverse as much as possible to achieve this

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CINA is currently implementing child protection activities in Awerial and Duk and Twic East. During the situation and response monitoring within these communities, community members have been consulted on their perceptions on prevailing protection issues, particularly in Duk. Women and men participated as KIIs and adolescents, youth and women groups were involved via Focus Group Discussions, as well as caregivers of UASC. In particular, child-headed households in Awerial were much involved in the FGDs.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Beneficiary Complaint mechanisms will be established at the community level and feedback will be collected using a form, complaint box or hotline number. Banners on SEA awareness and reporting will also be displayed at the CFS/ youth/women centers and in the schools. In particular, Protection help desks in the schools will be used to receive feedback from community members.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will involve community members during setting up and design of CFS/youth/women centers . Community members will be tasked to check the relevance of PSS activities to their children/youth/women and OPCs. In particular, they will interact with CFS animators on specific PSS activities, including sensitive protection issues according to context. Caregivers will be involved in PSS activities to check that they gain the necessary parenting skills and support children, elderly, survivors of violence and OPCs. Youth and women peer groups will be established to support other community members understand the protection and leverage peaceful coexistence

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will be implemented in line with IASC Protection Strategy. Specific needs of boys, girls, women and men and adolescents and youth will be considered. The project will ensure that UASC, SGBV survivors, persons detained unlawfully, elderly sick, and other persons of concerns are supported based on vulnerability criteria. Information for and about the beneficiaries will be kept confidential, and shared on need-to-know basis when password-protected. Protection needs of persons of concerns will be considered and ensure that they are included in socio-economic reintegration activities. Needs of child and women survivors of SGBV, PWDs and elderly and other harmful cultural practices will be well coordinated, to ensure that they get urgent and appropriate case management services, including IPA.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project will ensure that persons living with disabilities, HIV/AIDS, children living and working on the streets, and child-mothers and children and lactating mothers in conflict with law, etc are identified and included to benefit from protection services. In particular, awareness raising messages will be disseminated to ensure that no PWDs and other persons of concerns are not left behind from attending PSS and other protection activities. Youth and women peer group members will be targeted and included as well.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

The project will monitor and respond to any violence, coercion, exploitation, deprivation or neglect against children, women, elderly, PWDs, survivors of mines/ERWs, UASC, child survivors of SGBV, CAAFAG, child-mothers, children living with disabilities, children living and working on the streets and children in conflict with law, and to ensure that all children and survivors of landmines/ERWs are considered equal

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project will consider monthly situation and response monitoring to check whether beneficiaries have been accessing protection services including by boys, girls, women, elderly persons, PWDs, especially through protection risks monitoring, FGDs with caregivers and community based child protection committees. CFS/youth/women friendly center will be established and will be monitored to ensure that it meets minimum standards including being in locations that are safe, have shades, not near markets and within the closest open space within the community, that can be accessed by persons of concern.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Protection activities will be monitored regularly to ensure that the location is safe for children, women, elderly and PWDs in terms of security, the surrounding is clean, free from flooding, have toilets, drinking water and, shelter and a big shade. Where applicable, security will be provided during the CFS/women/youth friendly activities. Overcrowding will be avoided, and a CFS will have a maximum of 200 children per day.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project will monitor that the animators, volunteers, staff and clients respect children’s dignity by not sharing information about children or taking children’s photos without their consent. In particular, children, women, PWDs, elderly will not be exposed physically or emotionally. Toilets will be ensured in the CFS sites, marked separately for female and males. Female volunteers and staff will be recruited to ensure that sensitive concerns of girl’ child are kept confidential, when they chose to share.
