Project Activities

As the global and country level CCCM Cluster co-lead agency, UNHCR will provide assistance to the existing capacity of the local actors and humanitarian service providers in IDP settlements in case of emergencies and situation of protracted displacement. UNHCR, through current and active partnerships will enhance the outreaching and protection competences of regional and local government agencies (NCRI, Commission for Refugees and IDPS (CIR), JRIA, MoI IAD, MRR&R), and other stakeholders, providing capacity building activities to adequately coordinate IDP matters with service providers operational in the sites including UN agencies and other stakeholders responsible for IDPs protection. Building upon the existing system and strengths, UNHCR as the CCCM Cluster co-lead agency will expand its site coordination and management activities in the sites of Puntland, Somaliland and South Central. Camp Coordination and management will be strengthened in the sites of targeted locations through camp governance structure, capacity building and site infrastructure development activities. UNHCR through its partners will create coordination structures and mechanisms that will help the beneficiaries to access different services in their sites and settlements. Communication channels will be enhanced to foster information sharing in the camps with other service providers in the targeted areas. UNHCR will also ensure equitable access to services, improve the quality of life of the IDPs during displacement while advocating for solutions and preparing them for life after displacement. Camp level structures will be strengthened where they exist and established where necessary. UNHCR will continue advocating for public land with government authorities where the returnees and IDPs are willing to locally integrate. UNHCR will also continue advocating for the placement of permanent land/shelters - similar to Galkayo and Bossaso in 2019 - in locations where integration with host communities and access to basic services will be improved. Inclusion of IDPs in the planning process will be crucial for planning peaceful coexistence activities and achieving implementation that enhances integration. The following activities will be implemented throughout Somalia:• CCCM coordination mechanisms are established/reinforced at appropriate levels• Site level information is available and shared with stakeholders • Management of sites and settlements is improved• Improve community participation, living conditions and safe access to services and assistance in selected sites • Involvement and participation of affected community is ensured/ increased • Affected populations living in sites and settlements have information they need to access services • Communication/ information centers (in coordination with local authorities and protection actors)• Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites• Support the protection and restoration of livelihoods to strengthen resilience of communities in IDP sites This is underpinned by IDPs needs in the sites and informal settlements across all the regions. These needs were identified through UNHCR participatory assessment tool which uses age gender and diversity mainstreaming (AGDM).

Mitigation Measures

Displaced women, men, girls and boys in Somalia remain victims of various protection incidents and other human rights violations. Violence is occurring in places of refuge and in areas which are controlled by non-state actors and which are not accessible by humanitarian actors. Continuous displacement and lack of livelihoods has also reduced the capacities of these uprooted communities to access humanitarian and medical aid. •UNHCR will continue to monitor closely all protection incidents occurred within the context of forced displacement in Somalia. By advocating and putting in place response systems, every protection incident should get a referral or direct assistance. Additionally, the information gathered through a thorough monitoring system feeds into the wider system to better analyze main protection incidents in the country and better tailor programming to needs. •Influenced by the HCT’s Centrality of Protection strategy, UNHCR will pilot monitoring in areas which are occupied by armed non-state actors. Especially in South West State and Jubbaland State, individuals will be identified with strong connections in areas which are currently not included in humanitarian programming and assessments. Expected is that these Independent Monitors will feed protection related information to UNHCR and the wider humanitarian community on specific protection needs among these communities.

Please provide further explanation

UNHCR has been the key contributors in terms of resource allocations and coordinating the durable solution initiatives in Gaalkacyo, Baidoa, Bosasso, Kismayo and sections of Mogadishu. As an active Task force member, UNHCR will prioritize these locations further ensuring local integration is based on informed decisions with a clear focus on the protection of the displaced communities and sustainable access to basics services. UNHCR through its lead and co-lead Clusters will also work in collaboration with UN agencies, state and local government institutions and partners at the national and regional levels by ensuring regular meetings, providing reports on IDPs protection concerns in the informal sites and settlements, monitoring response to serious issues by relevant actors and advocating for promotion of IDP durable solutions in line with the National Development Plan (NDP) and Durable Solutions Initiatives (DSI) of Somalia. UNHCR will continue advocating for public land with government authorities where the returnees and IDPs are willing to locally integrate. Involvement of the authorities is key to address the high risk of evictions by landlords and to ensure sustainability of the settlements. The Ministry of Planning is a key counterpart in this regard.
