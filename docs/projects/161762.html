Project Summary

This project aims at providing quality nutrition services to 21,800 conflict-affected women, men, Boys, and girls through sensitization and education of women and men on proper infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in Damaturu, Gujba and Gulani LGAs of Yobe state within 12 months.
Through this project, GOALPrime will identify 1500 Pregnant and lactating women (PLW), women within child bearing age and men for sensitization and education on IYCF practices and support using the Mother-to-Mother Support Group (MtMSG) model. GOALPrime will identifying and use already existing women groups in the three LGAs to recruit targeted women for MtMSGs activities. This groups will run in two circles, 6 months per circle within 12 months.
Also GPON will deploy trained Community Nutrition Monitors to carry out regular nutrition monitoring and refer any SAM and MAM cases to the nearest stabilization centers, carryout regular sensitization on key nutrition massages and hygiene promotion in IDP camps and host communities to reach 20,000 children, Boys and girls, men and women in the proposed project locations within 12 months.
GOALPrime will coordinate with ACF, and MSF-S who currently operate Stabilization centers in Damaturu to ensure access to SAM and MAM treatment to the identified cases by the trained Community Nutrition Monitors across the three project locations.
GOALPrime having been engaging children in schools and at Child friendly space with child protection activities to ensure psychosocial stabilization and life-skill building among conflict-affected children for over two years now will leverage on this project to train 150 men and women in child protection and protection principles to promote protection in communities and IPD camp and sensitize 150 young women aged 15-49 on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risk mitigation measures in IDP camps and Host communities.
This project shall be implemented through coordination with the target beneficiaries and nutrition partners to ensure quality and need-driven project implementation. First GOALPrime shall start the project with a consultation with all the stakeholders both partners and beneficiaries of all age and community groups, followed by the establishment of toll-free line for feedback and complain on the project implementation process. GOALPrime shall commit to resolve issues and gaps raised on the project implementation by the beneficiaries within the capacity of the Organization and coordinate with the nutrition sector and other nutrition partners to resolve any issue, gap identify that is beyond GPON capacity.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in northeast Nigeria has steadily deteriorated throughout the crisis, with 2.7 million children and women in need of immediate nutrition services with a sizeable number of these cases recorded in Yobe state. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Assessment, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months remain consistently high, with over one million children suffering from SAM and MAM across 18 LGAs in the BAY states. This includes 367,000 children under five (179,830 boys and 187,170 girls) suffering from SAM and 727,000 (356,230 boys and 370,770 girls) with MAM. 2.8 million people, including 1.7 million children under five and 1.1 million women of reproductive age within the BAY states, need life-saving nutrition services. Of these, 534,000 are IDPs; 389,000 returnees; 242,000 in inaccessible areas; and 1.6 million in host communities. Women and children are the most affected and those arriving from inaccessible areas are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed humanitarian services before their arrival at the reception sites. (OCHA, Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2019). A survey by the nutrition sector estimates that there are approximately 79,965 women and children Under 5 in Damaturu, Gujba and Gulani LGAs in need of nutrition services. With about 14,634 PLWs, it is paramount that key nutrition services and messages on IYCF be disseminated in these locations. The locations identified for the project are characterized by the relatively high influx of new arrivals who need nutrition services. According to a National Bureau of Statistics/UNICEF, SMART survey in Bama in August 2018, screening of new arrivals at reception centers indicate a high level of nutrition and other humanitarian needs among families coming from inaccessible areas. In July 2018, out of 679 children arriving in Bama from inaccessible areas, 146 (22 percent) were classified in the red category (MUAC <115mm). These numbers, although very high, only represent a small proportion of the overall caseload of children aged 6-59 months in Bama (15,726 children), but underline the severe nutrition status of children coming from inaccessible areas.Despite a scale up of nutrition interventions in 2018, the ongoing conflict continues to have a direct impact on people’s nutrition status which is further exacerbated by weak health infrastructures, poor infant and young child feeding practices, limited access to safe water and sanitation services, poor hygiene conditions and food insecurity.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will demonstrate its commitment to being accountable to the affected population by ensuring feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into project strategies, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings and performance management, partnership agreements, and highlighted in reporting. Also, GPON will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organisation and its affected populations over information provision through intermittent workshops and meetings with stakeholders. GOALPrime will actively seek the views of affected populations through the use of suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations in the community to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. GPON will also empower and enable affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practice to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalised and affected are represented and have influence and also design, monitor and evaluate the goals and objectives of the programme with the involvement of affected populations, feeding learning back into the organisation on an ongoing basis and reporting on the results of the process.
