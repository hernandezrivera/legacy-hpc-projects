Project Summary

UNICEF is both a service provider under the partner presence rationalization in the sector and the provider of last resort (POLR). The largest response is in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states) where protracted armed conflict has continued to force displacement of population into camps and camp-like settlements for more than a decade. In other regions, the need for response a rise from sporadic conflicts, natural disasters like floods or risks of outbreaks/spread of infectious diseases such as cholera. Gaps also arise as a results of new population displacements or diminished capacity/departure of other humanitarian WASH partners. As a service provider, UNICEF is optimizing the water production and distribution, increasing attainment of the required minimum standards. UNICEF is rolling out the approach of urban type network water distribution in deep field LGAs with high yielding boreholes and high-density population of displaced persons or returnees and host communities. This approach will support decongestion in camps and expansion of WASH services to schools and health/nutrition facilities. UNICEF will also expand cash in wash programming for hygiene kits and other WASH NFIs. Through this project, UNICEF will have specific target populations in needs in IDP camps and host communities in Borno State, specifically in Maiduguri Metropolitan City and 18 LGAs (Askira Uba, Bama, Biu, Chibok, Damboa, Dikwa, Gubio, Gwoza, Jere, Kaga, Kala Balge, Konduga,Mafa, Magumeri, Mobbar, Maiduguri,Monguno, and Ngala), , whilst in Adamawa and Yobe, and possibly other states, the target population will be determined by locations where needs will arise. UNICEF will adhere to the SPHERE standards and scaleup the sustenance of the services as needed. To meet the demands of the POLR responsibility, UNICEF may need to mobilize additional resources. Through this project UNICEF will provide the initial lifesaving response to fill the gaps, prioritizing areas receiving newly displaced people or under an outbreak of cholera or other WASH-related diseases. UNICEF will strive to sustain the basic initial WASH services until further response becomes available from other sector partners. The project is also aimed at sustaining critical humanitarian water, sanitation and hygiene services to displaced population affected by protracted armed conflict in NE Nigeria (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States: target 1,060,298 people) and emergency affected population in three states

1 - Needs

The protracted insurgency and conflict in the NE BAY states and repeated sudden onset of natural disasters that impact WASH infrastructures, the safe water and sanitation coverage in NE Nigeria BAY states is at varying proportions with Adamawa and Borno 22% and 21% of HH have access to unimproved water sources with 6% in Yobe. However, these states' level aggregates mask differences in LGA access levels to safe water which is reflective of SNRA findings where 66% in Borno and Yobe and 76% in Adamawa have at least access to 15l/p/d. The overall situation for sanitation is more concerning still. Use of unimproved sanitation appears to be common across all three states; 30% of assessed households in Adamawa, 36% in Borno, and 43% in Yobe reported using unimproved sanitation facilities, with more than 20% of households using unimproved sanitation facilities in all but 6 LGAs across the BAY area. The findings from WASH Severity Classification is not far from SNRA findings which indicate 52% in Yobe,36% in Adamawa and 49% in Borno use unimproved latrines and placed majority of the assessed LGAs in the 3 BAY States between severity 3 and 4 in terms of lack of access to improved sanitation with access to sufficient quantity of water in the assessed LGAs stands at severity 3 (Severe) ,which doesn’t mean necessarily safe water free of contamination and safe excreta management, especially with such disaster occurrences as floods and regular breakdowns and ground water challenges in IDP camps especially in Borno. Sanitation across the IDP camps is a persistent challenge including challenges in fecal sludge management reducing useability and access to latrines. Of the assessed camps through the CCCM site tracker, 64 percent reported a latrine-to-person ratio above 1:50 below SPHERE standards. Camps in Bama, Dikwa, Mafa, Gwoza, Ngala, Maiduguri Metropolitan City (MMC), Jere, and Damboa have up to more than 150 people per latrine highlighting the need for a sustained desludging service. Additionally, flooding is a major concern as most of the FS worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste. Introduction of treatment and possible recycling of the sludge will render the sludge safe and incapable of becoming an environmental hazard. Additionally, the intersection of food insecurity and inadequate WASH services has contributed to heightened malnutrition levels among population groups especially children under five years. In 2024, Nutrition Sector projections indicated that 1.53 million children are at risk, including over half a million children under five who may suffer from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition (SAM). Children account for 60 per cent of the population in need and are highly impacted by poor WASH services making them susceptible to diseases and malnutrition affecting their growth and development. PLWD requires adaptive WASH infrastructures through which their dignity and hygiene are upheld.Within the different population groups overall unmet WASH needs remain high in IDPs compared to host communities and returnees across the BAY states with IDPs in Borno state having high water and sanitation needs compared to Adamawa and Yobe states. Host communities' needs remain high with 674,491 people in host communities in Adamawa compared to 131,422 IDPs in severity scales between three and four and in Yobe 818,637 people in host communities in severity scales between 3 and 4 compared to 123,239 IDPs. In Borno, the opposite is true with 1,454,048 IDPs in the severity classification between three and four compared to 595,494 people in host. Limited sanitation facilities and poor access to clean and adequate water impact menstrual hygiene management for women and girls. This is worsened by limited to no access to bathing soap to promote hygiene, especially for returnees and IDPs. Similarly, the rise in prices limiting fewer women and girls to access to these essential products on their own.

2 - Activities

UNICEF will implement seven key activities (outlined below) through various partnerships, Long Term Agreement (LTA) contracts or Project Cooperation Agreements (PCA) with selected partners and State Govt Agencies under the Basic Cooperation Agreement with Federal Government of Nigeria and Annual Work Plans with the state governments in Borno, Adamawa, Yobe. This project will consolidate UNICEF capacity as a sector partner implementing in allocated locations/camps in Maiduguri Municipality and 19 LGAs (Askira Uba, Bama, Biu, Chibok, Damboa, Dikwa, Gubio, Gwoza, Jere, Kaga, Kala Balge, Konduga,Mafa, Magumeri, Mobbar, Maiduguri,Monguno, and Ngala), where UNICEF will reach SPHERE standards. In Adamawa, 15 LGAs (Demsa, Girei, Gombi, Hong, Lamurde, Madagali, Maiha, Michika,Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Shelleng, Song, Yola North and Yola South). InYobe, 6 LGAs (Bade, Damaturu, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani and ,Potiskum) where UNICEF will support AWD/flood response. Additional resources will be needed for UNICEF to play the second role of provider of last resort (POLR) where there is no other capacity from the sector. The key activities to be implemented under this project are: 1) Water trucking for limited periods to provide temporary access to safe water to newly displaced people or people affected by water, sanitation and hygiene related disease outbreaks. UNICEF will deploy LTA contractors on water trucking to the areas in need. The same population will be provided more permanent water sources. Target: 50,000 people (also part of the target below).2) Optimizing water production, storage and distribution to provide 15 litres per person per day, increase storage and expand distribution to reduce water collection time to within30minutes round trip including queuing time. This is based on assessed needs and tested optimal water production of existing water resources. Dysfunctional facilities will be repaired or rehabilitated according to needs. Higher capacity pumps matching the tested safe water pumping will be installed. Storage tank capacities will be increased to cover two days' water demand in case of breakdowns. Priority are large population camps with high-capacity boreholes. This activity will optimize benefit for the affected population and enable possible development of water supply for host communities and other population. Construction of new facilities will be a last resort when there are no pre-existing facilities. Target: 322,686 people. 3) Daily chlorination and management of the operation and maintenance of water supply services to sustain access to safe water in IDP camps and urban areas in Maiduguri and Yola. Implementing partners will support the management of existing water systems in camps allocated to UNICEF: daily chlorination of water using automatic chlorine dossers; monitoring of the free residual chlorine levels and faecal contamination of the water at production point and at the shelters using the water; and organizing orderly queuing at the water collection points and support repairs in case of breakdown. The target is to restore service within 48 hours. Distribution of water treatment chemicals to Borno and Adamawa States for municipal water treatment and distribution in cholera affected LGAs. Target: 625,586 people. 4) Increasing access to safe sanitation. UNICEF will construct additional latrines to reach the SPHERE standard. Target 124,700 people. 5) Daily cleaning and disinfection of latrines including desludging of latrines in allocated camps and per demand from CCCM for other camps. Target: 864,261 people. 6) Distribution of hygiene kits including cash for kits in Maiduguri Target: 225,520 and 7) Risk informed hygiene promotion in camps and host communities, and cholera hotspots, using a range of communication channels. Target: 1,054,572 people.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protection is cross cutting and will involve local authorities, community leaders and civil society in coordination with the Protection, Health, and WASH Clusters. Key protection issues such as impact of and stigmatization of cholera cases, menstruation, location of water points and latrines will be addressed through consultations with community groups to ensure the services to be provided do not pose a threat to their safety, security, and dignityWomen and children shall be enabled to equally participate (as appropriate) in the coordination of the project and the management of WASH infrastructure, including the formation of WASH committees and engagement of local area mechanics and toilet business owners. Women leadership will be promoted for facilitating cholera risk awareness and hygiene education session/events with the community members with special attention on menstrual hygiene. Furthermore, efforts will be made to address existing gender stereotypes and norms relating to the role of women and girls in sanitation and hygiene. For example, hygiene promotion activities will be designed in a way to promote the equal responsibility of men and boys together with women and men. Involving them in community water and sanitation-related decisions can be an empowering experience

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The community are also reached through the "U-Report", a social platform created by UNICEF, available via SMS, Facebook and Twitter where members of the community express their opinion and play the role of positive agent of change in their communities. During this funding window, similar consultations and experiences from all were considered for the designing of this project At all stages, UNICEF separately consults with girls, boys, women, and men, including older people and people with disabilities (PwDs), to ensure that all programs enable access and reduce risks of violence. Communities will be sensitized on their right to complain and provide feedback with confidentiality. The result will reinforce accountability and improve assistance design and delivery.
