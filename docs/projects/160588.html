Project Summary

Through this project, ADRA aims to assist conflict affected communities in Eastern Ukraine to meet their basic needs & improve their basic economic security & self-sustenance. This will be achieved by:
• Providing access to food to the vulnerable residents living in isolated settlements (<5 km zone) through distribution of food electronic vouchers. This will increase the resilience of elderly people in isolated settlements to poverty.
• Providing access to basic items & services to meet the basic needs & improve social protection outcomes for the most vulnerable & marginalized communities through well coordinated MPCG programming (<20 km zone) .MPCGs are an ideal aid tool designed & tested to offer crisis-affected people a quick, effective, & flexible support while maintaining their dignity in areas where most markets are still functioning.
• Providing conditional micro-grants for agriculture & livestock for rural residents (<5 km zone)
• Implementing early recovery intervention to provide financial literacy, entrepreneurship & technical skills trainings & micro-grants to vulnerable families to set-up micro-businesses (0-20+ km. zone). The above two components will strengthen the recovery of the beneficiaries by providing a longer-term impact through stable income for several years. This approach allows beneficiaries to use & build on their existing skills & knowledge to generate income for their families.
• Supporting the conflict-affected communities (<5 km zone) to recover through participatory light rehabilitation projects from which the most vulnerable community members will generate income. This component is designed to create temporary work opportunities to vulnerable segments of the population through small-scale projects & it serves as an initial step for income stabilization for crisis-affected population. It also helps to restore their dignity feeling themselves able to contribute to improving their own community structures. Also this component will support facilities & social institutions with small repairs & renovations which will benefit the whole community on the long run. These facilities will be used also in the nearest future as the first step of rehabilitation & development of conflict-affected communities. The facilities will be identified in close cooperation with local authorities to ensure ownership by the whole community & sustainability of results.
Providing both food e-vouchers and MPCGS to communities in the <5km zone will boost the local markets, keeping in mind that isolated settlements will be connected to main cities with large retail chains through a free social transportation project.
The project will be implemented in GCA Donetsk & Luhansk Oblasts with main focus on settlements near the contact line (0-20 km), & will be targeting vulnerable groups with focus on elderly people, persons with disabilities or injury, persons with chronic illness, & women & girls. Activities will be coordinated with local authorities, local & international organizations operating in the same areas & sectors, & FSL cluster & CWG’s strategies.
The project proposal is prepared based on HRP 2019. The project will contribute to the implementation of HRP Strategic collective outcomes 1 & 2: 1.Affected people have access to adequate protection & means of livelihoods. 2.Provide emergency assistance & ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for populations in need. The project will also contribute to the FSL Cluster Objectives 1,2,3: FSL Objective 1 : Most vulnerable conﬂict-affected groups have immediate access to food. FSL Objective 2: Affected populations improve food security status through rehabilitated agriculture &/or increased agricultural production for sustainable livelihoods. FSL Objective 3: Affected populations have access to employment & income generating opportunities through rehabilitation/building sustainable livelihood assets. Additionally, it contributes to MPCs objectives 1 & 2.

1 - Needs

For almost 5 years, the continued impact of the conflict in eastern Ukraine has been severe, sharpening vulnerabilities related to poverty. The weak social protection systems are not adequate with rising prices. The continued disconnect between GCA & NGCA, disrupted access to markets, as well as high levels of unemployment & lack of livelihood opportunities have severely impacted millions of people. An estimated 5.2 million people are affected by the conﬂict in Eastern Ukraine, of whom 3.5 million people need humanitarian assistance & protection. The most vulnerable groups in need of humanitarian assistance are the elderly, single-headed households with low income, people with disabilities, & children. The elderly constitute 30 % of those in need, while households headed by people aged 40-60 are more vulnerable compared to previous years due to widespread unemployment .Elderly with disabilities in conﬂict-affected areas are often excluded from humanitarian assistance .Their main concerns are access to healthcare due to limited resources. They also often face insecurity & are unable or unwilling to move away from their homes. To survive, they often rely on their meagre income & on families or neighbors to access basic services & food. IDPs are also among the most vulnerable groups, as they have more special needs & challenges. A total of 89 % of IDPs, who were actively seeking employment, reported facing difficulties, 80 % of them women . IDPs’ coping strategies differed in their severity, from stress strategies such as borrowing money, to emergency strategies such as selling property. Lack of livelihood opportunities not only threatens food security, but also increases vulnerability, constantly forcing people to make impossible choices between food, medicine, heating or their children's education. According to the 2019 HNO, 825,000 people are food insecure in Eastern Ukraine. Savings are exhausted, & analysis indicates that people are resorting to negative coping strategies to make ends meet, such as decreasing health & education expenditures or resorting to crime & survival sex (HRP, 2019). Of the 1.1 million people in need of FSL, 69% are female (HNO 2019). FHHs face particular challenges with 39% currently unemployed compared to 25% of MHHs, as many businesses have closed & adults in GCA previously went to NGCA for work. In Donetsk oblast, the unemployment rate has doubled since 2013, while in Luhansk Oblast, it almost tripled. It is estimated that up to 406,000 unemployed working-age people, including IDPs are still in need of livelihood assistance. The REACH winter assessment data confirms the trend identified in 2017, directly linking food security & unemployment. Minimum subsistence levels, minimum salary levels, utility prices, social protection services, & economic factors have a direct impact on a household’s food security status. All state social payments (such as assistance for a household when a child is born, support to households with members with a disability, support for single parents etc.) are based on the ‘legal stationary minimum subsistence level’. However, this level does not always match the level of inflation & vulnerable groups, as people receive far less than they did in 2013 prior to the conflict . The conflict has had a significant impact on the agricultural sector, traditionally a major sector for livelihoods in Ukraine: 21,000 rural HHs in GCA require agricultural support (HNO 2019).

2 - Activities & outcomes

• Outcome 1: Most vulnerable conflict-affected groups have immediate access to food  Activity 1.1: 8,981 people in isolated settlements will receive food electronic vouchers. The transfer amount is 2,475 UAH covering 3-month (825 UAH/month). Implementation - Locations identifications through FSLC. Signing agreements with supermarkets - Beneficiary selection based on vulnerability criteria, cross-checked with other NGOs - Electronic voucher distribution; - PDM. All beneficiaries, regardless of age, gender and disabilities, will have equal access to registration, and be selected as per vulnerability and scoring matrix. All registration premises will be equipped with ramps.• Outcome 2: Increasing the purchasing power of targeted population to cover their immediate basic needs  Activity 2.1: 3,376 targeted conflict affected people will receive multipurpose cash of 970 UAH per month to cover their basic needs for 4 months. Implementation of activity (74% in <5km, 26% in 5-20km) - Locations’ selection through FSLC - Beneficiaries’ selection based on vulnerability criteria and scoring matrix, cross-checked with other NGOs – Cash disbursement through post office or banks - PDM – FGDs. Registration of beneficiaries will prioritise parents with children and pregnant women. Online or home-based registration will also be open for those who cannot make it physically, including parents whose children may be in hospital or people with disabilities. Door-to-door cash transfers through Ukrposhta.• Outcome 3: Affected households improve resilience through rehabilitated agriculture or increased agricultural production.  Activity 1: 75 HHs will receive agricultural grants up to 1,000 USD. Implementation: - Locations’ identifications through FSLC. - Beneficiaries’ selection to be cross-checked with other NGOs - Development and selection of business plans based on scoring matrix. - Disbursement of the 1st tranche of cash through post office and/or banks (75%). - Conditionality assessment. - Disbursement of the 2nd tranche of cash (25%) - Mentoring and consultation • Outcome 4: Affected populations have access to employment and income opportunities through the restoration of sustainable livelihoods & rehabilitation/building of sustainable livelihood assets  Activity 4.1.: 115 people receive agricultural grants of up to 1,000 USD for self-employment or micro-enterprise. Implementation - Locations identifications through FSLC. - Beneficiaries’ selection giving equal opportunities to men and women, to be cross-checked with other NGOs. Provide six 5-day trainings (115 participants) covering various topics - Development and selection of business plans, based on scoring matrix. - Disbursement of the 1st tranche of cash through post office and/or banks (75%). - Disbursement of the 2nd tranche of cash (25%) after conditionality assessment. - Mentoring and consultation. Activity 4.2.: 440 people participate in light rehabilitation projects. Implementation - Locations’ identifications. - Beneficiaries’ selection - Engagement with authorities and beneficiaries to define the scope of work, taking into account local skills, community needs & capacities. - Establishment of teams & trainings - Procurement of materials. - Cash for work for men and women equally. The types of work will be agreed and distributed according to gender and age. The selection of CfW and additional activities for livelihood restoration will take into account the ability of all genders and risk groups - both workload and types of work - and the flexibility to do so. The program will be specifically focused to ensure accessibility and inclusivity for female headed households and other groups with specific needs whose capacity in traditional labour activities may be limited. Where appropriate these activities will be adapted for old and less-able people to avoid possible discrimination. In certain circumstances, some beneficiaries will receive multipurpose cash grants.
