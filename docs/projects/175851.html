Project Summary

The project anticipates to provide an essential lifesaving health care to vulnerable and hard to reach localities and people with special need with Preventive and curative health services. This project is intended to reach to a total of 16,464 beneficiaries in three supported counties. Through this project we will synergize the emergency humanitarian response to focus on lifesaving activities while striving at building more durable solutions towards the developmental activities where it will be an integral part of the developmental health projects /PEHSP of the CHD from the inception of the project. Eighteen MoH Health facilities and mobile units in three counties will be targeted to be supported.
The project will ensure that access to EPI and provision of immunization is ensured in all the supported health facilities, nutritional screening and education, provision of safe motherhood to pregnant mothers (Antenatal care, skilled delivery, Post-natal care, treatment of sexually transmitted diseases, Family planning, treatment of aliments, community based awareness and prevention of COVID-19, provision of CMR services to survivors, Psychosocial support to mentally traumatized people including SGBV survivors. Strengthening health facilities infrastructure to be able to provide quality care and services, Nutritional screening to under five and WRA mothers, provision of Vitamin A supplementation and Nutritional counselling to mothers including exclusive Breast feeding treatment of under-five children with SAM with complications, strengthen basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care including the clinical management of SGBV , Intensify surveillance and support immunization of children against measles, polio and other vaccine preventable diseases and integrated capacity building refreshers (WASH, Health and Nutrition) to prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks focusing on cholera/malaria /measles and other diseases of public health concern(TB/HIV AIDS), Providing psycho-social first aid, providing F-ANC and Post Natal Care service check-ups, first aid for mass casualties and putting up ORT- Corner for immediate on site management of Diarrhoeal cases in Akobo, Pigi and Uror Counties. The community based BHI-team will be the arms of social mobilization and awareness creating team that will ensure early case detection and referral of cases to nearest mobile unit.
Disease outbreak investigation and timely response capacity will be built at the county and facility level which will ensure a timely detection and response to outbreak occurrence through IDSR systems that will be instituted at all levels.

1 - Needs

Akobo County had faced heavy flooding that had affected most of the payams in the county resulting in displacement of communities from their residence sites to be IDPs within the county. There are bouts of conflict that had affected mobility and access to service delivery units within the county. Akobo is labelled among the severely affected and most food insecure counties in the country. In Akobo County, Alali payam an estimated 30,000 people were affected by flooding in the area, this locality is hard to reach and with no access to basic services.Lack of trade channels and morbidity have remained high in the greater Akobo and accessibility is also a challenge since some routes have armed elements making it hard to move from one side to the other to provide humanitarian services. Akobo is one of the five counties of Jonglei State have so far confirmed COVID cases on 25-Feb, four cases were detected, The response activities such as risk communication and community engagement, and hand washing stations in public places are minimal. Limited funding and humanitarian organizations’ presence for COVID-19 response further continues to constrain the response, this indicates the need for COVID prevention activities both at community and facility level (SS Key Context Update Feb24-March 2). Malaria and diarhoea remains to be the leading cases of morbidity in Akobo and Uror counties, secondary facility report showed that a total of 46,238 cases in Akobo this is close to 44% increase in cases of the same report of 2019 which was 32,540 casesEPI coverage in Akobo county has reached 32% of the set annual target that is far low to reach the minimum level of coverage (85%) to ensure herd immunity which attributes to access to vaccine and service delivery in the county.Current IPC grading (April-July 2021) showed that Akobo is Akobo is at IPC-4, planned levels of HFA, 0.3% of the population (about 31,000 people) will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC-5) acute food insecure. Routine health report showed that Diarhoea, Pneumonia and Malaria are the leading causes of morbidity and mortality in the county. In Akobo health facilities are poorly equipped and there are no well-established, The WASH infrastructure in health facilities is a rudiments and in most cases none existent. In Akobo and Uror counties EPI services are limited to selected localities are mobility to provide health services including EPI remains to be a challenge hence EPI service need to be rejuvenated. Pigi County is among the most affected counties IMC is supporting one PHCC and four PHCUs in Kurwai, Pieri, Diel and Kolanyang PHCUs while the rest of the sites that are being settled by the returnees Atar and Alam doesn't have health care services. Kurwai Payam and surrounding bomas had overburdened the existing weak health system in the area. The river route had been the front line and all the payams and health facilities across the river Nile had been abandoned and left since the community also left the locality to a safer location. Recent WASH severity mapping showed that the among the counties with Counties with Lowest Sufficient Access to Safe Water showed Pigi ranked top with 100% grade (WASH Cluster 2021 Severity Mapping “Sufficient Access to Safe Water”). This denotes that the county doesn't have any source of safe water source except the river water and local swamps. April-July Projection showed that Pigi is among the 45 counties labelled as IPC-4/Emergency phase/ with an urgent need for humanitarian support. In general community in these counties had been exposed to multitude of stressors most have lost their families properties and the social protection fabric has deteriorated the economic incapability limited access to food and basic needs, loss of belongings as a result of flooding and repeated conflict had left most of the mothers and adolescent girls exposed psychological trauma and stress.

2 - Project activities

This project aims to increase access to emergency essential primary health care services through an integrated service delivery package using both the developmental and emergency service.Nile Hope believes that an enhanced coordination between humanitarian and development approaches is essential. Hence, Nile Hope will work on enhancing and synergizing the emergency humanitarian response to focus on lifesaving activities while striving at building more durable solutions from the start. The main focus of this project will be on the major causes of Morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable community (Mothers and under five children). So as to contribute to an improved resilience of crisis affected populations, With the urgent life-saving needs that exist in South Sudan at present this is a considerable challenge which requires a joint effort of intertwining and synergizing both the development and humanitarian responses together. The main project activities include; treatment of under five children with SAM with complications, strengthen basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care including the clinical management of SGBV, Awareness and community involvement on COVID-19 prevention and control activities, Provision og PEP and screening supplies to supported health faciliteis, Intensify surveillance and support immunization of children against measles, polio and other vaccine preventable diseases and integrated capacity building refreshers (WASH, Health and Nutrition) to prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks focusing on cholera/malaria /measles and other diseases of public health concern(TB/HIV AIDS), Providing psycho-social first aid, providing F-ANC and Post Natal Care service check-ups, first aid for mass casualties and putting up ORT-Corner for immediate on site management of Diarrheal cases in Akobo, Pigi and Uror Counties. The community based BHI team will be the arms of social mobilization and awareness creating team that will ensure early case detection and referral of cases to nearest mobile unit.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project coordinator will have a regular meetings and an awareness raising campaigns will also be conducted to ensure community level understanding of PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community representatives from the most vulnerable will be represented to ensure their participation to ensure that their voice and views is taken in to account. The monthly beneficiary meetings will enable the project to be geared wiht the full participation of the target beneficiaries

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During the inception of the project an inception stakeholder meeting will be held where beneficiary representatives will be invited, the workshop will provide details of the project regarding the targeted beneficiaries, the duration of the project, targeted locations, the services to be provided and other relevant information including the role of community in ensuring project ownership for service sustainability.A discussion and information sharing platform at county and payam level will be organized where respective team leaders ensure community perspectives; concern and recommendations are shared to ensure that the service provider is fully accountable to the beneficiaries and the concern and demand of beneficiaries is taken in to account. Qualified locals are given priority to work in project activities to enhance project ownership at the community level. Their view on the appropriateness and quality of the service or response is continuously sought through platforms such as monthly review meetings of county level progress update and their reflections are taken in to account. Consultations with communities/beneficiaries are also conducted to assess their views regarding the quality of services and the level of beneficiary satisfaction compared to community expectations. The project team leaders are responsible for organizing and coordinating the engagement of the targeted communities. Each monthly meeting is held at county/facility level hence community feedback is populated for management decision making. The need to deliver services in ways that reach women headed households and households hosting IDPs is clear. Gender and Social Exclusion to accessing health services affects all people Men, women, boys, and girls, however women and girls are dis-proportionally disadvantaged.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Community engagement is key in this project where by community level project ownership and full participation is ensured. Their view on quality of the service or response is continuously sought through platforms such as monthly review meetings of county level progress update and their reflections are taken in to account. Consultations with communities/beneficiaries are also conducted to ensure their involvement in supporting the project implementation in different mechanisms such as informaiton sharing, periodically share gaps assess their contributions and views on the level of beneficiary satisfaction compared to community expectations.
