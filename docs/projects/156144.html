Project Summary

In this project, Ataa is planning to target children who are in Aleppo and Idleb governorates as follows:
• Aleppo governorate, Afrin sub-district.
• Aleppo governorate, Jandairis sub-district.
• Aleppo governorate, Atareb sub-district.
• Aleppo governorate, Jebel Saman sub-district.
• Aleppo governorate, Zarbah sub-district
• Idleb governorate, Dana sub-district.
• Idleb governorate, Ehsem sub-district.
• Idleb governorate, Ariha sub-district.
• Idleb governorate, Saraqab sub-district.
• Idleb governorate, Teftnaz sub-district
The schools located in Aleppo governorate are 12 (11 in villages and 1 in camp) whereas there are 11 schools located in Idleb governorate, 2 of them are in camps while the other 9 schools are in villages. The above-mentioned communities were chosen and prioritized based on 1) its high severity on HNO severity scale, 2) the concentration of IDPs, and 3) its accessibility for Ataa.
The targeted areas in this project are economically poor suffering from high rates of severe illiteracy and dropout especially among children of basic education. The poor state of education in the targeted areas is mainly because families not sending their children to schools because they cannot afford to, damage to schools that need rehabilitation and lack of education personnel because most of the qualified teachers work in other positions. Many children are also in a state of psychological distress as a result of frequent displacement, harsh circumstances, and difficult living conditions. Therefore, providing education opportunities have become a requirement that children would like to accomplish. So, there is an urgent action to address the devastating impact of conflict on children in particular which is the main goal of this project.
In this project, Ataa will address the problems identified above, in line with the WoS goals of increasing access to education for crisis-affected children and improving the quality of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment, through the support of these schools in Idleb and Aleppo governorates ( the locations which will be provided with formal and non-formal education explained in the implementation of activities chapter). Ataa will address the most critical needs such as the rehabilitation of damaged learning spaces, including WASH facilities in line with INEE/Sphere standards; support delivering of increased pre and in-service teacher training support in key areas of Basic Teacher Training, inclusion, wellbeing, classroom management, psychosocial support, child protection, child safeguarding & referral systems.
The education in the targeted schools will be (formal and non-formal education) includes the basic and secondary education level, from the first till the twelfth grades which are through teaching the SIG curriculum as well as providing remedial, catch up classes, basic literacy and numeracy and SLP.
The project will provide education opportunities for 10715 children (Female; 5429, Male; 5286) and 537 educational personnel includes headteachers, teachers, cleaners and guards (Female; 285, Male; 251), the percentage of students (51% females and 49% males), the percentage of staff (47% females and 53% males).
Ataa will conduct FGDs with targeted communities to inform them about the goal, outcome, and, activities of the project. Brochures and banners will be used to share information with the communities. Ataa will set a community – based mechanism to make sure that community members can submit their complaints and feedback. feedback tracker will be prepared on monthly basis.
The project will support parents in their children's education and increase the awareness of targeted communities in relation to children education issue. The project also will ease the burden on other schools that can’t accommodate children in safe and quality conditions. Moreover, it will help the host communities to improve their resilience and their capacities.

Needs

Nearly eight years of brutal conflict in Syria have reversed more than a decade of progress in children’s education. Every day the crisis is prolonged, the pain endured by innocent families grows, leaving deep scars that are likely to disfigure the affected populations and beyond for years to come. Most affected of all are Syria’s children who are at risk of becoming a “lost generation”. According to the HNO 2019 by education cluster, the crisis has forced 2.1 million, over one-third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and about 121,972 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need. The provision of education services inside Syria is limited. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. So, based on the secondary data taken from HNO 2019 report and recent ACU reports along with the education need assessments conducted by Ataa Association that covered many areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorates through three types of tools (community assessment, school assessment and direct observation), Ataa was able to figure out in the targeted communities how far the education status has been affected by the current conflict, the status of school infrastructure, access of beneficiaries to education, condition of schools that are out of service, main problems the education process faces, and main sources of educational curricula, measuring and evaluating the educational levels, educational needs, schools capacity, level of representation of IDPs, the constant displacement, and main reasons for non-enrolment of children. Children at higher risk of being exposed to exploitation, abuse and rights violations. The intensification of hostilities in some areas has negatively impacted thousands of children who lost access to education opportunities. Access to education is lost when children are forced to suddenly leave their area of residence, schools close or have no teaching personnel available and do not provide certified education. Teaching capacity remains overstretched, a big number of teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts due to displacement and working as volunteers resulting in poor quality of education and also the recruitment of unskilled teachers with limited financial remuneration is impeding learning outcomes achieved by students so Ataa will equip teachers with the knowledge and skills required to plan, implement and coordinate high-quality EiE responses in the targeted locations. Ataa through this project will pay more attention to reach out to the out of school children through using life-saving protection-oriented implementation modalities. Access strategies will include small-scale rehabilitation of damaged schools and establishment of TLSs through a gender-sensitive and disability-friendly approach, formal education, as well as the implementation of non-formal education learning programs such as the certified, accelerated learning program, SLP and BLN.An advocacy plan, in line with the No Lost Generation (NLG) Initiative, will also be developed to ensure awareness raising, donor support, and to support the education process.Information for this assessment comes from:• Secondary data were obtained from the following reports: 1- HNO 2018 report, HNO 2019 education cluster report 2- ACU report, 3- UNHCR Flash Update report, 4- Bahar organization need assessment.• Primary data was obtained from interviews with KIIs in the targeted communities, KIIs were IDP representatives, members of targeted communities’ local council, schools' headteachers, NGO staff members and members of teachers’ union, all interviews were conducted by ATAA field team in face to face manner

Implementation of activities

Ataa will carry out the direct implementation of this project through its offices and staff in Syria and Turkey. The project will continue in promoting the education process in targeted areas and it will be considered an added value for the education sector in Syria as it is in line with the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Ataa will pass through coordination with all stakeholders regarding meetings with Education cluster, education directorates, local communities, and parents. Ataa follows new methodologies to develop the capacities and efficiency of its staff through providing different curricula: formal and non-formal (literacy and numeracy, life skills, SLP, remedial and catch-up classes), the needed topics will be based on a baseline ASER assessment for the students in the targeted schools. 20 of the targeted locations will be provided with formal& non-formal education through teaching the SIG curriculum and additional classes for those who have weaknesses and during summer program, while 3 locations in Aleppo (Atareb sub-district) will be TLSs and will be provided only with SLP and BLN.Conducting a complete assessment to the teachers by education supervisors who visit the schools and attend the teachers’ classes on monthly basis suggesting to provide them with the needed trainings such as EiE, INEE, PFA, PSS, CP, CSG, SLP, BLN, teaching inclusion, life skills as well as new and effective teaching methodologies for a better-quality education. Ataa will make sure that the student body is balanced as much as possible in terms of male/female representation and the same with the recruitment of teachers and management staff. Through the parent outreach sessions and support from the local councils, parents will be encouraged to send both their female and male children to the school. The rehabilitation of damaged learning spaces, including WASH facilities in line with INEE standards will take place at the beginning of the project to ensure that the schools are safe environment for all students especially for those with disabilities. Also, book banks will be established at each school provided with learning materials and furniture.Children will be enrolled in the targeted schools which will be rehabilitated and equipped according to INEE minimum standards. Ataa will provide children with school kits and school textbooks, individual support for the most vulnerable students (transportation, medical equipment, medicines and clothes).Education personnel will receive monthly incentives at the end of each month after collecting the timesheet from the schools and be filled by the data entry.Back to Learning (BTLs) campaigns will be conducted in the location of each school to increase the awareness of both students and communities about importance of education in which the rate of dropping out will decrease. Also, the community initiatives will be conducted to increase the community mobilization.Ataa will prepare for winter in the schools by distributing heating materials to provide children with a protective and safe environment.Ataa will conduct classroom observations in order to ensure that education provided to children is of high quality. Child protection activities have been integrated within education and the referral mechanism will be activated at each school to ensure CP.To ensure equity in the project, children and school staff of all ethnicities and religions will be accepted in the schools, as well as children with learning disabilities and special needs.To ensure sustainability, Ataa will invest in strengthening community relationships to the schools by operating Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and hiring and training local teachers and education personnel. And thus, a capacity building plan will be established to target the PTAs members to cope with any changes.Ataa will establish feedback and complaint mechanism in schools using the suitable methodologies for handling and receiving the complaints.

Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. • The project will benefit all members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and active involvement of the traditional leadership structure.3. Attacks on schools• Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks.• Using OCHA’s Deconfliction Mechanism as a method to hold attackers accountable for their actions4. Risk of long distance and young children walking alone. Children may get lost, be attacked or lose interest in learning• Security/safety training students and school staff5. The host community rejects the presence of Ataa or the project because the host communities feel unequally treated or threatened/stigmatized, disapprove of activities implemented, or other reasons.• Ataa will disseminate information about projects within the community and will also organize community events to build acceptance and provide services, in coordination with local authorities.• Beneficiaries participated in the assessments feeding the design.6. Risk of children dropping out of formal education to attend SLP centers • Ataa will identify specific criteria for accepting children into the program; • Train parents and children on how to use the material at home instead on regularly attending the centers7. Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same center/class• Create separate times of attendance for each age group.8. Interference of armed groups, including confiscation of sensitive materials (laptops, phones);• In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, Ataa will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.9. High fluctuations in prices of items needed (if procurement happens inside Syria)• Prior to the start of implementation, a market assessment will be conducted in order to see what is available and what is not. 10. Delay of the printing and timely delivery and some locations not accepting textbooks with MoE logos.• Print books with and without logos depending on the location served.15. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. The security situation, the schools are all located in the opposition areas, so there might be a risk of shelling by the regime and its allied forces. • Ensuring that the schools are in safe locations as much as possible • Suspension of schools during fly overs or insecurity• Training will be conducted for the teachers to instruct the students about safety issues.• Promote the establishment/support of early warning systems2. The security situation in Northern Syria may prevent project staff from travel or cause other problems for implementation• Putting in place an emergency plan• Having a field officer at each school who will be linked into local networks3. Continued risk of harm to children if negative pedagogical practices are not remedied through improved teachers and education personnel practices.• Ensure that teacher and education personnel training address assessed priority needs that focus on the well-being of the child.• Better ensure application of training content through pre-post training quizzes, follow-up and refresh training and where/if possible, in-classroom review and support.4. Insufficient stipends continue to result in teachers and education personnel absenteeism due to additional employment and/or leaving their jobs to seek higher paying work.• A standardized and harmonized stipend scale that ensures adequate remuneration should reduce turnover, absenteeism and tensions between teachers and education personnel.5. A limited number of teacher and education personnel benefiting from the project could cause inter-community tension and pressure on partners and local authorities.• At the beginning of the project, Ataa will aim to quell expectations by being as transparent as possible by clarifying the number of people who will benefit from each component of the project and the criteria for selection6. Lack of support for the project by the local community• Raise awareness of the project in the local community through regular communicate and Local Council communication.• Including local community (such PTC) in the project and thus increasing project ownership.7. Loss of project supplies or assets due to bombardment or seizure of warehouse and/or stocks.• When possible, Ataa will agree with local authorities on where safe warehouses can be established.• Ataa will sensitize local authorities on the need to keep warehouses and stocks safe and used for their intended purpose.8. Delay or inability to deliver supplies in a timely manner• Ataa will be encouraged to locally purchase project supplies/materials.• Ataa will liaise with local authorities and communities prior to distributions to ensure they are received in a safe and timely way.9. Loss of finance or other compliance documents due to bombardment, seizure/destruction of computers/files, etc.• Ataa will transfer soft copy scans and move hard copies of project and compliance data on a frequent basis.
