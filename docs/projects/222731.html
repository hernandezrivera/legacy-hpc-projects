Project Summary

In 2023, South Sudan faced outbreaks of measles, cholera, and other infectious diseases, exacerbated by the impacts of climate change, such as flooding (World Health Organization [WHO], 2023). Key health indicators lag behind global targets, with a maternal mortality ratio of 295 per 100,000 live births in 2017 (WHO, 2024) and an under-5 mortality rate ranging from 30 to 112 per 1,000 live births across different states (WHO, 2024).
New mothers and their babies face extraordinary obstacles accessing quality health care. On average, South Sudanese women give birth to seven children and only 11%of mothers give birth in a healthcare facility. Emergency obstetric care services remain limited, with services delivery being largely provided by international organizations. The poor health indicators are due to several factors, including the weak and underfunded healthcare system. The incidence of noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) in South Sudan is rising, and WHO estimates that 28% of all deaths occurring in the country are due to NCDs. Despite the rising cases and deaths, only 10% of primary health centers (PHCs) provide NCD services. Limited knowledge of the population on prevention of NCD risk factors, such as lack of physical activity, cessation of smoking and promotion of healthy diet, has contributed to the growing burden of NCDs and mental health conditions. The lack of data hinders the development of an investment case for work on noncommunicable diseases. South Sudan declared a cholera outbreak. Given the country's challenging context and suboptimal immunization coverage, there have been outbreaks of measles.108 As of May 2024, there is a measles outbreak in Northern Bahr El Ghazal. As of May 19, the cumulative total of suspected measles cases from week 1 to week 20 is 2 407 of which, 164 (6.8%) were lab-confirmed, 772 (32.1%) epi-linked, 1 350 (56.1%) clinically compatible, and 121 (5.0%) discarded.110 A total of 30 rubella-positive cases were discarded (negative measles cases). About 66% (1604 out of 2407) of all cases were in children under 5 years old, and only 23% of those cases involved children who had received at least one dose of the measles vaccine. The prevalence of mental disorders such as depression, anxiety disorders and post-traumatic stress disorder among South Sudanese is high.114 WHO estimates that in settings affected by humanitarian emergency, such as the case of South Sudan, one in five people is likely to suffer a mental health condition. Since April 2023, South Sudan has received more than 800,000 people displaced by fighting in Sudan. Although the large majority - roughly 80% - have crossed the border through a single town in Upper Nile State, intensified fighting in the neighboring Kordofan region of Sudan in 2024 has resulted in the emergence of another major entry area further west, in Northern Bahr El Ghazal (NBeG). Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 41,000 people are facing catastrophic IPC levels 5. The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. IPC AMN (October 2024 to March 2025) Aweil East, Aweil North, Aweil South are classified at PHC 4 critical which more nutritional support.

1. Needs

There is significantly increase in maternal mortality rate of 789 per 100,000 live births in South Sudan, which has been attributed by low ANC attendance, only 39% women attended four Antenatal Care Services during pregnancy. Health facility delivery and skilled birth attendance are low at 12.3% and 14.7 per cent respectively. Access to BEmONC is very low leading to maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality among the most vulnerable populations. Teenage pregnancy is estimated at 30% among girls 15-19 years, contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods is 6 per cent with modern methods at 5 per cent, with the unmet need for family planning estimated at 29.7% for 2020 (FP 2020). The majority of health facilities (47%) are operating at a moderate functional level. The poor living conditions and Inadequate health care services resulting from Sudanese armed conflict, flooding causing displacement coupled with already disrupted health system in Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil West Counties has led to the spread of common conditions such as Malaria, pneumonia and Acute Watery Diarrhea, malnutrition and other health related cases. BRIDE-SSD will focus on Primary Health Care, Maternal, Neonatal & Child Health (MNCH), Clinical Management of Rape (CMR), Psychosocial support (PSS) and emergency preparedness and response as well integrate nutrition services for OTP and TSFP in the project intervention in the targeted locations. The project will ensure the vulnerable crises-affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable, safe and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs, vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action through AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring and Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action. BRIDE-SSD intends to expand its cervical cancer project to greater Aweil Counties and incorporate cervical cancer programme into this project activities comprehensive management of cancers cases in Norther Bhar El Ghazel State. BRIDE-SSD will Promote access to adequate nutrition through both emergency food support and long-term interventions. Key Activities: Nutrition Screening and Treatment for Malnutrition: Implement community-based management of acute malnutrition (CMAM) programs, including screening and treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in children under five and pregnant or lactating women. Provide therapeutic food (e.g., Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food - RUTF) and supplementary feeding to treat malnourished individuals. Micronutrient Supplementation: Distribute vitamin A, iron, and zinc supplements to children under five, pregnant women, and other vulnerable populations to address micronutrient deficiencies. Food Assistance and Support for Food Security: Provide emergency food supplies to IDP and returnees. Nutrition Support and Food Security Priorities: Address malnutrition among children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers.

2. Project activities

BRIDE-SSD intent conducting mobile outreaches to priority locations, when needed, providing support to the SGBV survivors: clinical care, treatment for STIs, emergency contraceptives, and psychosocial support, Strengthening the existing Primary Health care units (PHCUs) to provide Basic Emergency Obstetrics and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), Providing community-based level 2 and level 3 (non-specialized) Mental Health PSS to persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services, conducting preventive or reactive vaccination campaigns for vaccine preventable diseases such as cholera and measles including malaria during outbreaks or when indicated in large-scale displacement, investigation, and response, conduct nutrition screening and treatment (OTP and TSFP) and conduct community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs, participate in response through Boma health initiative.
