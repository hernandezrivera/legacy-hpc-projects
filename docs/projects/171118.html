Project Summary

Hiraan region in South Central Somalia faces serious food scarcity and livelihoods amid violent conflicts, recurrent drought and flooding resulting into acute watery diseases and consequent deaths; diarrhea, typhoid and cholera outbreaks and abnormal dryness/drought. This negatively affects and worsens the humanitarian situation especially for children, woman and vulnerable groups. Persons among the aged, minority tribes and the Persons with Disabilities; worsened by the COVID 19 pandemic in the midst of poverty.
Based on the cumulative, combined impacts of multiple shocks, FSNAU and FEWS NET projected in May 2020 that around 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least September 2020. An additional 3 million Somalis were anticipated to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2) and engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. Analysis of household food security survey data collected in the July/August 2020 post-Gu assessment resulted in reduced estimates through December 2020.
Moderate to heavy rains cause substantial flooding in low-lying areas along the Shabelle and Juba rivers, resulting in several deaths and significant damage to infrastructure, crops, property, and livestock, affecting more than a half million people across 17 districts in ten regions in the three states of Jubaland, Hirshabelle, and Southwest, with over 400,000 people displaced from their homes. Most flooding occurred in the regions of Middle and Lower Juba, Bay, Lower and Middle Shabelle, and Hiraan. Weeks of flooding have destroyed physical, productive, and social service delivery infrastructure – with many roads turned into rivers and farmlands fully destroyed. Livestock has been lost and agricultural production has been impacted. Food reserves and food crops have been destroyed, market prices inflated and negatively impacting on the poorest of the minority clans and Persons with Disabilities.
The heavy rains and wet soils enable the uncontrolled development and spread of Desert Locusts across Somalia especially in Hiraan. The Federal Government of Somalia, the World Bank and the UN have been addressing the flood menace and its resultant destruction in Somalia.
The project adopts both conditional and unconditional monetary approach for food and livelihood resilience enhancement in Hiraan...........including rehabilitation and construction of livelihood systems. Unconditional cash transfer is meant for the elderly, sick, persons with Disabilities and some women such as Pregnant and Lactating mothers. This intervention helps the affected populations towards meeting their basic food budget and enhances resilience in post-disaster phase in a dignified manner.
We posit provision of agricultural inputs: seeds, tools, fertiliser, irrigation pumps and fuel subsidy to assist farmers for immediate improvements in food security. This is accompanied with livestock vaccination and training in veterinary service.
We include Community Disaster preparedness Committee composing of women and men (15 Females and 14 men) including Persons with Disabilities and among the non-Dominant clans in Hiraan in mitigating to disasters. CEWI proposes study and reporting on the hydro-topographical nature of the area with view to preventing disasters through afforestation, gabion construction and management, early warning sytems... CEWI targets Biyo-Qurun, Jawiil, Dhaywabar, Qoydo and Farlibax villages for intervention.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

For self-sustainability and right to choice of the Household basic need-gap context. Cash transfer programming directly reaches the targeted beneficiaries and flexible to follow up.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

All financial systems are working at low in the COVID 19 pandemic, facing reducing financial transactions , recording less borrowing , less loaning and less or reduced availability of jobs. Persons with Disabilities and segments from Minority clans are the most affected. The injection of cash through humanitarian aid shall not cause inflation or hyperinflation. Cash will activate the economy and alleviate poverty, making markets vibrant with increased purchasing power.

Mitigation Measures

Cash based transfer and non-disclosure of targeted beneficiary data , ensuring aid centres are not far from the people, ensuring they travel short distance and not late afternoon wholly reduce and prevent risks to the project. CEWI PSEA policy awareness and enforcement among the staff and the beneficiaries and all stakeholders reduce further sexual exploitation. CEWI adopts a principled humanitarian approach in the project intervention respecting principals of aid enjoining local and national authorities for safety and security, for consultation. CEWI consults all segments of beneficiaries including the elderly, persons with disabilities and women. We shall institute feedback and complaints mechanisms such as mailboxes and direct call-lines in following up on project implementation.
