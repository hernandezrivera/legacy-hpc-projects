Project Summary

This project is written to address the water, sanitation and hygiene needs of people (men, women, girls and boys) affected by the socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions. It will contribute to improving access to safe drinking water for vulnerable populations, particularly women and girls who collect water within households and are the main users and managers; At the same time, this project will improve women's, girls', men's and boys' access to basic sanitation infrastructure, thereby reducing the rate of open defecation and consequently waterborne diseases, particularly among children under 5 years of age.

Besoins / Needs

The South-West and North-West regions of Cameroon have been experiencing instability since October 2016, when tensions between the English-speaking regions and the central government increased. The security situation has continued to deteriorate. (OCHA report April 2019). The total number of internally displaced persons is estimated at 440,000. In the north-west and south-west regions, internally displaced populations living in the bush are far from basic drinking water and sanitation infrastructure. They drink from unimproved surface waters or sources, which are at high risk of contamination in the absence of basic sanitation services, and are thus more exposed to disease vectors. For displaced populations in urban and semi-urban areas whose incomes have been drastically reduced, they must also bear additional financial burdens to have access to drinking water or, failing that, turn to unimproved surface waters and springs. For example, according to the humanitarian needs overview report cmr_hno_2019 vf, 83% of the people affected by the crisis (displaced and members of host communities) surveyed said that access to water has significantly deteriorated in quantity and quality due to the deterioration of maintenance systems and the increase in users. Nearly 70% report a deterioration in the situation of basic sanitation in their living environment, with the main risk being fecal peril diseases such as cholera, which is ongoing in part of the country and already in the Bakassi health district in the south-western region, cf SITREP cholera Bakassi N°14. This situation affects differently groups of men, boys and mainly women and girls, who are most often the main users, providers and managers of water in households. The estimated number of the population in need of water, sanitation and hygiene refers to 51% of girls and boys aged 0 to 18 years, 45% of adolescents and adults aged 18 to 59 years and 4% of older men and women over 59 years. For example, the lack of water supply infrastructure close to households forces women and girls to travel long distances and spend hours in search of water to meet the needs of their families. The same is true for the location and access to sanitation facilities, which is not easy, especially for women and girls, on whom their privacy and security depend.To help meet the needs of these populations, Plan International has been working since the beginning of the crisis in the WASH sector with the integration of CPIE's activities in part of the Ngoketunjia, Boyo and Mezam divisions. However, with the situation seemingly deteriorating further, available resources can only reach one tenth of the vulnerable population in need. The project aims to improve access to safe drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities and promote good hygiene practices for women, girls, men and boys in the above age groups in selected communities hosting IDPs in the northwest and southwest where the needs are greatest
