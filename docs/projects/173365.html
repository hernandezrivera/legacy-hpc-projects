Project Summary

The project contributes to the improvement of the public health situation in the Rohingya camps and host community by addressing gaps in the access to quality service and the awareness on non-communicable diseases (NCD) and gender-based violence (GBV). The intervention entails an extensive health education and awareness programme targeting women and men at risk of NCDs, ensuring that appropriate care and support is provided by their families. It enhances community support mechanisms, linking health, protection or social services. Selected existing community health posts and clinics are supported to assist populations at risk to adopt conducive behaviours for primary and secondary prevention of NCD, including Covid-19.
Furthermore, the project acknowledges the role of primary health care facilities for reaching GBV survivors. The intervention raises awareness and addresses shortcomings at selected health posts in the provision of professional, survivor-centered GBV services, to ensure enforcement of their mandate on the Minimum Package of Essential Services (MISP) as per binding policies of the Health sector and GBV sub sector. The project will also strengthen professional medical skills, basic MHPSS and communication to ensure standardized service quality
MdM will establish 4 community resource centers and will provide technical support to the IPs who will oversee operations. The CrCs, close to heath facilities, will enhance community-based support mechanisms through GBV outreach and critical health issues including NCDs. The CrCs will increase GBV prevention and holistic assistance to survivors, and reinforcement of comprehensive referral pathways.
Training on Gender, GBV principles and referrals for all staff and outreach, including CHW -conducted by partner organizations, will ensure engagement of staffing and communities in support of GBV survivors. The network and referral coordination mechanism reinforces a comprehensive GBV response by linking community-based resources -outreach and sensitization, existing community centres with the available medical facilities of proximity in camps and host communities.

1 - Identified Needs

Beyond the previously detailed need for mainstreaming GBV and NCD reiterated in subsequent JRPs, COVID-19 has worked as a multiplier in the effect of health risk of persons vulnerable to either, each with corresponding specific impact. Although importance of prevention and response is officially recognized, those on NCD are not yet mainstreamed. 67% of mortality is from NCD all over Bangladesh (Nujhat. S, et al, April 2020, Lancet From: https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langlo/article/PIIS2214-109X(20)30158-3/fulltext#seccestitle10). While the government is strengthening measures through setting laws, policies and plans, response at health facilities is still weak for example (cf. NCD Scorecard Bangladesh). Meanwhile, needs in support for the elderly in their management of NCD are found in our current project of COVID-19 awareness raising. This includes addressing three key elements. Firstly, many older people do not properly take medicines (they get medicines from several clinics/pharmacies including overlapping ones). This is more significant in females, who generally get less education than males. Secondly, their supporting family members do not have proper knowledge, either. Most of them are females and less free to communicate with and receive support from others outside. Thirdly, there is strong agism among people and the elderly tend to be isolated. There is still much need in support in capacity building of people with (risk of) NCD, their supporting family members and health facilities with not many humanitarian/development partners. Those people are also more susceptible to and affected by COVID-19, to which much need in response still remains. In addition, mental disorders are a risk factor for NCDs, and as a result, a significant portion of people with depression and anxiety have NCDs. Globally, prevalence of depression among people with hypertension, diabetes and cancer have been shown to be as high as 29%, 27% and 33% respectively (Ngo VK, 2013). Gender-based violence (GBV) has been another prevalent public health issue in the Rohingya camps and host community and further exacerbated in COVID-19 pandemic, with significant increase in intimate partner violence, polygamy, increased violence against marginalized groups (RGA, MSNA). Baseline data for 2020 shows that only 29% of rape survivors reported within 72 hours. The quarterly GBV Information Management System reports (GBV IMS) consistently indicate mere 1% of referrals coming from health facilities despite their mandate as the first line of care and entry point for patients including GBV survivors. Furthermore, in a survey conducted by the Health Sector in 2019, 47 Health Posts operating in 21 camps admitted providing insufficient services to GBV survivors. Parallel GBV service quality assessment at PHC level supported by MDM in February 2020 revealed that 70% of facilities undertook no measures to inform the public of GBV health services, nor had their staff sufficient skills to ensure minimum-required assistance to GBV survivors. There is limited specific guidance on the “provision of survivor-centred care”, required by the MISP, including on support men and boys, and ensuring follow-up of the multitude of one-off trainings to the key personnel. In the host community the GBV service framework is focused in tertiary healthcare at municipality level, practically beyond the reach of survivors in local communities and GBV categorized under “non-communicable diseases”.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Establishment of community-based support mechanism with close-by health facilities, by means of outreach sensitization on GBV and critical health issues, inclusive of 4 community resource centers to reinforce GBV prevention and holistic assistance to survivors, and reinforcement of a comprehensive referral pathway Activity 2: Camp meetings among and support for people supporting their family members with NCD Activity 3: Training/supervising community health clinic and health post staff and monitoring quality NCD and GBV services delivery (host and Rohingya communities) Activity 4: Reinforce the sensitization and community-health promotion and information on essential available services, the referral pathway by trained gender-balanced teams, frontlines volunteers and community workers. Activity 5: Harmonize comprehensive GBV survivor-centered case support within the Health System Activity 6: Reinforce the network and coordination between institutional key actors of Health, Social Welfare departments at local level and other humanitarian service providers for holistic GBV Health response comprising MHPSS, MISP -prevention of sexual violence, HIV and STIs transmission, sexual and reproductive health-, and the other sectors and services inegrated in the referral pathway. Activity 7: Reproductive health care -MISP services-..-and first line counselling and mental health support, referral to complementary services Activity 8: Integrate psychosocial services into GBV services with structured PSS activities (awareness raising, psychoeducation, support groups) Activity 9: Specific standards for the “provision of survivor-centred care and first line support” for health posts are developed in consultation process with partners and sectors Activity 10: GBV service gap analysis based on binding standards is designed and conducted at 20 health posts/community clinics Activity 11: Tailored capacity building programme with supportive supervision at target facilities is conducted covering training on GBV awareness, reporting, professional medical and soft skills Activity 12: Communities in facility catchment areas are reached through outreach activities and local advocacy promoting GBV awareness and available entry points including for SEA
