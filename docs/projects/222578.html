Project Summary

BACKGROUND
South Sudan has been devastated by floods and conflicts leaving about 5.8 million of the Country's population in dire need of humanitarian assistance/aid (IPC, 2024). The health system has been affected negatively as shown by the poor indicators with maternal mortality rate (MMR) still among the highest in the world at 1223 per 100,000 live births and infant mortality rate stands at 63.8 death per 1,000 live birth (WHO, 2024). Access to quality health services have been severely constraint, hence, majority of the Country's population are at risk of epidemic prone diseases and common illnesses.
The Counties of Ayod and Fangak in Jonglei State have been severely affected by the conflicts and floods causing massive displacements, and on top host returnees from Sudan. The two Counties is estimated to host 139,160 people in need. Access for Humanity (AFH) aims to provide essential lifesaving healthcare services to the vulnerable communities of Ayod and Fangak. This project will target 52,094 beneficiaries as follows; Ayod, 3,084 people displaced, 1,334 returnees and 13,604 host in Kuachdeng, Pajiek, and Wau Payams, while in Fangak 21,044 IDPs, 4,054 returnees and 8,974 host in Old Fangak Payam.
PROJECT DESCRIPTION
OBJECTIVES
Overall, the project aimed to contribute to the reduction of preventable morbidity and mortality from infectious, non-communicable, and epidemic-prone diseases related to floods, acute food insecurity and other emergencies, and in specific;
1. To improve access to integrated quality primary health care services among vulnerable or high-risk populations of Ayod and Fangak Counties.
2. To reduce morbidity and mortality due to epidemic prone diseases by timely detection, reporting and coordinated responses.
3. To promote coordination among partners, stakeholders and community.
C. POJECT IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH
AFH plans to provide integrated emergency primary healthcare (PHC) services in selected Payams of the two Counties where displaced, returnees and host communities reside. The strategies to deliver integrated PHC services will include;
1) Facility-based/static emergency services with extended outreaches targeting clustered/urban or pre-urban type of settlements.
2) Mobile units targeting pockets of populations residing 5Km away from healthcare centers or units.
3) Community health care using community owned resource persons in form of Boma Health Initiative (BHI) targeting communities beyond the mobile sites.

1. Needs

Ayod and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State are particularly severely affected by floods and intercommunal conflicts worsened by influx of returnees from the ongoing crisis in Sudan. According to the IRNA report conducted in September 2024 in Fangak, a total of 44,915 internally displaced person. Out of which, 8,444 boys, 12,666 girls, 9,522 men and 14,283 living together with 109,090 host population. In Ayod, IRNA report showed 26,700 people internally displaced residing together with the host community.Many health facilities have been affected by floods and are rendered non-functional, they include; Chotbora PHCU, Pakan PHCC, Koat-Nyakoang PHCU and Kuemduok PHCU. That has resulted in high rise of malaria and diarrheal cases in the communities, partly because of no healthcare services and largely because of the direct effects of floods. Stagnant water created breeding grounds for mosquitoes, increasing the risk of diseases like malaria, dengue fever, schistosomiasis and others skin diseases. Most of the population has been cut off from main health facilities by flooding hence leading to poor access to health services. There are difficulties in referral pathway in case of critical illness breakout.There are high risks diseases outbreaks in the form of cholera, hepatitis A, cVDPV (circulating Vaccine Derived Polio Virus) and measles among others. Pregnant, lactating women, elderly people and the children under the age of five are the most vulnerable during this flooding thus increase the risk of morbidities and mortalities rate in the county.With every conflict, women have high risk of becoming victims of sexual abuse and exploitation including rape and torture, usually referred to as ‘conflict-related’ violence. Rape and unsafe abortion cases needs proper healthcare services. The availability of dignified clinical management of rape (CMR) services is limited in the two Counties, hence, survivors are left distressed in enormous and silent sufferings.Essential basic health services in form of integrated emergency primary health care services is much needed, readiness to respond to emergency and to be situated within the vulnerable population for easy accessibility. Integration of health services with nutrition, GBV and WASH services will be the best approach to alleviate suffering and restore hope in the affected populations.

2. Project activities

Objective 1: To improve access to integrated essential primary health care services;1- Set up and equip 6 health facilities (2 PHCCS and 4 PHCUs) with IPC materials, water supply and proper waste management.2- Training of frontline health workers on IPC guidelines.3- Provision of routine immunizations/EPI services, both static (health facility-based, outreach and mobile).4- Training of frontline health workers on IMNCI (integrated management of neonatal and childhood illness).5- Provision of rehabilitation services to persons with disabilities in the health facilities.6- Provision of mental health services and psychosocial support.7- Treatment of common illnesses (malaria, diarrhoea, respiratory tract infection and other injuries) in all the age groups, gender and persons with disabilities.8- Provision of maternal health services (safe motherhood/Emergency obstetric and newborn care) ANC, care of uncomplicated and complicated delivery, postnatal care (PNC), sexual and reproductive health (SRH).9- Training frontline health workers on BEmONC, CMR and GBV.10- Provision of clinic management of rape (CMR) services in health facilities for survivors.11- Provision of health education at health facilities and outreaches. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinate responses to epidemic prone diseases.1- Distribution of insecticide treated mosquito nets for under 5 and pregnant women.2- Provision of malaria prophylaxis to ANC clients and malaria vaccines to children under 1.3- Timely event-based reporting of epidemic prone diseases and events of public health importance using EWARS and IDSR.4- Support supplementary immunization activities (SIAs), measles and polio targeting under 15 and Td women of reproductive age.5- Provision of essential medicines including distribution of IEHK kits to 6 health facilities.6- Provision of IEC materials, conduct community awareness and social mobilization on prevention and control epidemic prone diseases.7- Conduct community meetings, focus group discussions and strategic placements of suggestion boxes in health facilities for appropriate feedback. Objective 3: To promote coordination among partners, stakeholders and community 1- Conduct monthly coordination meetings chaired by County health department.2- Conduct quarterly review meetings.3- Conduct community meeting.4- Map services and establish referral pathways.
