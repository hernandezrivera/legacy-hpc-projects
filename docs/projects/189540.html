Project Summary

The project is designed to improve quality, increase access, and strengthen the capacity of primary and secondary health services, integrating MHPSS/GBV and WASH, by reinforcing the national health system at State, County, Boma, and community level. The project target is 30,000, of which 1200 children under 5 (504 boys, 696 girls), and 8178 women and 5922 men, out of who 2400 (1008 males 1392 female) are elderly, 60 years and above. In close collaboration with State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and the County Health Department (CHD), MdM will support 11 static health facilities and 4 Mobile Medical Units (MMUs), across Bor South, Twic East, and Duk. The MMUs are deployed in each of the three counties (2 in Bor South), and will target 885 women and 809 children (340 boys and 469 girls), and 305 displaced persons (128 male, 177 female). MdM partners with Sudan Medical Care as local implementer, who have extensive experience in the aforementioned areas. The project includes provision of supportive supervision, monitoring, and training of 20 health and MHPSS/GBV workers and community mobilisers and it supplies gaps in medicines, medical supplies, and basic equipment. MHPSS and GBV will be integrated through counselling at hospital, PHCC, and MMU level, and through awareness raising and a referral system at community level, and will target 172 adults (998 women, 723 men) and 1941 children (815 boys, 1126 girls). WASH will be integrated by rehabilitating essential infrastructure. The project takes a holistic approach in ensuring support is given at all levels, responding to COVID-19 needs, and by integrating the MHPSS, GBV, and WASH components. The main objective is to meet the basic health, MHPSS, and GBV needs of the population in Bor South, Twic East, and Duk counties, focusing on persons with specific needs such as women of reproductive age (project target: 7200), pregnant and lactating women (project target: 288), at-risk pregnancies (project target: 43 women with high risk pregnancies) and children under 5 (project target: 1200), mental health and psychosocial support cases (project target: 3662; 1538 male, 2124 female) among displaced people, returnees and host communities.

1 - Needs

MdM, with support of SMC, conducted a Needs Assessment in November 2021 covering Bor South, Twic East, and Duk, and aligns with the findings presented in the Humanitarian Response Overview of 2022. Due to severe insecurity and climate-shocks, people’s physical and mental well-being deteriorated significantly. In fact, Duk is reported as one of the counties with the highest number of people who are in a severe state of need (pin: 114,038) and holds the lowest availability in health services. Therefore, Duk is considered a particular area of concern (HNO 2022). Flooding and/or conflict either destroyed, abandoned, or cut off health facilities from areas in need. This means the overall availability and accessibility of healthcare is extremely poor. Only 8% of the health facilities in South Sudan offer the full package of BPHNS, and 58% are relatively functional. There are structural gaps in qualified health workers, essential medicines and supplies, and adequate WASH infrastructure. The project addressing those in the activities and indicators. The Health Cluster identified 213.731 people in Bor South, 108.610 people in Twic East, and 112.038 people in Duk in need of health assistance. Compared to last year, these numbers have increased dramatically. The project will target 30,000 people in need. MdM will prioritize the following areas, in accordance with the HNO 2022 and the Needs Assessment by MdM/SMC: Neonatal and child health services: 1 out of 10 children are expected to die before turning five years old (which is one of the highest ‘under-five’ mortality rates in the world). 75% of deaths are related to preventable diseases such as malaria, diarrhea, and pneumonia. MdM will address the extremely low immunization coverage (49% Penta 3), and will screen and apply referral pathways for malnourished children (project target: 1200 (504 male,696 female). Sexual and reproductive health: the maternal mortality rate in South Sudan is one of the highest in the world (789 per 100.000 livebirths). Low Four-ANC coverage (39%), low skilled birth attendance (14.7%), low institutionalized delivery (12.3%), and poor BEmONC accessibility contribute to pregnancy and newborn related morbidity and mortality (project target: ANC4+ 50% and SBA-30%). Communicable and non-communicable diseases: Driven by displacement and loss of livelihood, especially vulnerable groups such as malnourished children and pregnant and lactating women are more prone to common and infectious diseases, for which there is a need to strengthened referral pathways for nutrition (project target: 650 women) Emergency preparedness and response: driven by flooding and a lack of WASH infrastructure, the number of water-borne diseases is expected to be high. Covid-19 remains a threat as well in 2022. Strict surveillance and timely response are therefore key in the response (project target: 80%). MHPSS: according to WHO approximately 2.5 million people in South Sudan (22% of total population) are expected to suffer mental disorders (I.e., depression, anxiety, PTSD, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia) following conflict settings. MHPSS services are not integrated into the national health system and remain inadequate. There is a need for qualified MHPSS workers, improved integration into health services, and strengthened referral pathways (project target number of MHPSS consultations: 81). GBV/CMR: Driven by protection issues, GBV continues to be a prevailed threat. Whether during daily routine activities or in armed conflict, women and girls are often subject to sexual and gender-based violence. Having said that, GBV is reported across all affected populations. Next to physical and psychological harm, this also leads to structural fear and overall reduced well-being. An increased need for CMR services in health facilities was identified. Also, here it applies improved referral pathways are needed.

2 - Project activities

Guided by the National Health Policy for South Sudan (2016-2026, the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, the WHO Pyramid Framework and the Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services for South Sudan, MdM will ensure implementation of the National Policy to address priority health needs and enhance provision of basic essential health services while ensuring protection mainstreaming and gender integration in Bor South, Duk and Twic East counties of Jonglei State. The project focusses on strengthening the capacity of the CHD/MOH and local implementing partners to deliver maternal, child and neonatal health, SRH, prevention and treatment of communicable and non-communicable diseases, immunization, health promotion and community sensitization/awareness creation on common health subjects, MHPSS by enhancing quality standards on diagnosis, treatment, timely referral and community based prevention practices and behaviors. MDM will provide technical guidance, support supervision and on-the-job training for health facility staff and community-facilitator, emergency referrals for critical cases, repairs of health and WASH infrastructure, provision of essential medicines, consumables and medical equipment, referrals and linkages to next level of care and with relevant actors for non-medical services including protection will be provided for at least 11 target health facilities including seven PHCCs, 4 PHCUs and 4 MMUs and 2 secondary health facilities I.e., Bor State Hospital and Panyagor County Hospitals in the 3 counties. At PHCU level, the intervention will focus on quality primary level assessment, diagnosis and care for common mild acute childhood illnesses using IMNCI and adult conditions, including trauma, treatment for non-communicable and communicable conditions, SRH, health education, routine immunization, and referral; at PHCC level, outpatient care and short term inpatient diagnosis and treatment for common communicable and on communicable diseases, childhood and maternal services including EPI,IMNCI, BEMoNC and referral services integrated with MHPSS/SGBV care will be provided. Sexual Reproductive Health care will be enhanced through support for CEMoNC and CMR. By supporting the WHO/MOH EWARS and in conjunction with BHI, the project will improve real time detection, reporting and response to communicable and notifiable diseases through strengthening of community-based surveillance and early-warning system. COVID-19 surveillance, risk communication and community engagement will be integrated s with enhanced IPC protocols and screening, for early detection, monitoring and referral through the RRT for case investigation, management and contact tracing. MDM will provide support to the Isolation Unit at BSH to strengthen diagnosis and case management of COVID-19 cases. Community facilitators at PHCUs will disseminate comprehensive and simplified package of SGBV/MHPSS and WASH, health prevention and promotion messages at household and community levels with referrals for health, counseling and legal services. The facilitators will also target vulnerable groups plus men and boys to be advocates and allies for in the prevention of SGBV. Referral mechanisms from the community and within the health facilities will be strengthened with particular focus on reporting and referral of SGBV cases and acute severe life-threatening illnesses. MHPSS counseling will take place in six different health facilities in Bor South and two facilities in Duk and Twic East counties. The MHPSS services are freely available for any person (voluntary walk-in or referred) who presents emotional distress, including but not limited to those linked to medical disorders, trauma, malnutrition, and SGBV. In addition to the two counselors and a clinical at Bor State, MdM has trained 8 counselors from Twic East and Duk and enhanced their capacity to deliver mhGAP-based counseling and related MHPSS services. MDM will also operate a MHPSS hotline.

3 - Project indicators

The impact of the project corresponds with the Health and Nutrition Cluster’s planned priorities and achievements with several results related to the Protection Cluster strategic objectives. 1) Reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities by supporting 11 health facilities plus 4 mobile medical units with essential drugs and medical supplies, training, and technical supervision and 7 health facilities with an MHPSS/GBV component as seen in the activities 2) Disease surveillance and response, including diagnosis and treatment will be monitored through all data submission in the DHIS tool and measured by percentage of alerts raised through national EWARs investigated within 48 hours (project target: 80%) 3) Delivery of an integrated package of high-quality essential health and MHPSS/GBV/SRH services including EPI to reduce the high maternal mortality and U5 mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine preventable, and other infectious diseases 4) Improving access to and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems. To ensure and measure the quality and life-saving aspect of the health care services, the project will undertake regular supervision and monitoring visits to all supported health facilities, documented by supervision checklists and the health facilities’ supervision records, and is measured by the number of health facilities supported with IEHK supplies 5) The number of persons receiving MHPSS/GBV services will be measured by records kept by the counsellors of all the diagnoses and the number of consultations conducted. 6) To measure access, utilization and the lifesaving aspects in child and adult health services, the coverage of PENTA3 in under-one-year old’s (project target:75%; 1252) and measles in under 5 years old (project target 36%; 432) will be recorded through the EPI records. 7) MdM also aims to increase access to services for survivors of GBV, clients with disabilities, mental health disorders and the elderly. Health care staff (10) will receive refresher training sessions on the CMR as well as PFA. The MHPSS/GBV community facilitators and counsellors will be trained on the referral pathways and inter-sectoral SOPs to strengthen the referrals from community and PHCU level to PHCC and hospital level where CMR is available. The success of the capacity building on CMR will integrate MHPSS among health care personnel will be measured by recording the number of GBV survivors receiving CMR services (project target: 10). While following the strategic objectives of the Health Cluster, the project is also committed to achieving better critical protection related and specialized services through the integrated approach of the project to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas. This will be achieved through training and deploying a team of counselors and community facilitators in basic skills in offering remote, one-on-one and group based GBV and MHPSS services and referrals/linkage to other partners/services like protection. To measure the reach of the specialized services, the number of MHPSS consultations will be recorded and reported. Additionally, MdM will make prevention and mitigation of GBV/protection risks a priority through enhanced preparedness and mitigation measures. The reach of community-based sensitization and awareness creation will be measured by recording the number of persons reached through awareness sessions conducted by the MHPSS/GBV community facilitators on topics such as stress identification and management, suicide prevention, copying mechanisms, etc. for STI, GBV and COVID-19 affected people (project target: 3659).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from beneficiaries and staff is essential to ensure accountable, safe, inclusive, and meaningful service provision. Feedback from beneficiaries will be recorded, analyzed, and presented in a systematic way. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive feedback on their case and that as far as possible, beneficiary data will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. Every three months the M&E focal point will produce an accountability report and a workshop will take place to discuss main findings and decide on action points. Moreover, MdM’s CFRM ensures follow-up of the feedback/complaints from the entry points through: - Complaints or Feedback receipt and categorization: Complaints or feedback are sent to coordination committee that categorizes the information into 1. Feedback, 2. MdM complaint, 3. External complaint, 4. Complaint out of the scope of the mechanism - Complaints and Feedback handling category: 1. Feedback/suggestions are shared in a confidential manner with team leaders and supervisors as input for programs, 2. MdM coordination committee decides how to assess and address the complaint about quality of services or staff conduct; General Coordinator is involved in serious complaints, 3. Complaints about other services are shared with the relevant organizations as appropriate; General Coordinator is involved in serious complaints, 4. Complaints out of the scope of the mechanism do not require follow-up - Response and organizational learning: Complainants should receive a response, preferably within 1 month; Complaints and actions taken are recorded in an internal MdM recording keeping tool; Monthly reporting of key complaints and feedback is sent to coordination. All collected feedback will be reviewed by the MdM team and, if appropriate, partner organizations. Then appropriate changes, communications to health staff, policies or processes will be made, with information shared to the community through one of the already established communication channels.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

MdM continues to conduct periodic need assessments, as well as regular meetings and participation in key clusters with different stakeholders, community leaders and local and international organizations. In addition, MdM works in collaboration with local partners such as SMC (Sudan Medical Care), with an extensive knowledge on community sensitization, giving us the overview and tools to adapt the interventions to the specific needs of the targeted population, particularly those from the most vulnerable members. Therefore, the messages will be adapted to the socio-cultural context, as well as to the ages and genders of the right holders. The language and means of transmission of these will also be adapted and culturally appropriate. For example, counsellors and Community Facilitators will be trained on recording oral feedback so those who are most vulnerable cannot access the health facilities, or are unable to write have an opportunity to give confidential feedback. All participation and consultation tools will be age and disability inclusive and will have a gender and anthropological approach.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

MdM conducts training to AAP focal points, who include Women’s champions and BHI workers. All PSEA allegations and subsequent follow-up will be documented in a secure and confidential database to ensure accountability. The report will be officially acknowledged within 24 hours by the General Coordinator (Head of Mission), and within 72 hours a protection team will convene a case conference to assess immediate risks and next steps. PSEA incidents are always escalated to HQ, upon which senior management will assign an investigation team, this can be either internal or external based on the level of the incidence. A qualified PSEA focal point will be appointed to assist the survivor with necessary response, information, and follow up. The first feedback should be delivered to the survivor within the first two weeks from the moment of reception of the case. The full investigation period should not exceed more than 4-5 weeks. The PSEA focal point will support coordination and communication with personnel. Upon the conclusion of the investigation report, it is the responsibility of the Human Resources department to determine the appropriate course of action. This will be without prejudice against criminal proceedings at a national level. This procedure is documented in detail in MdM’s PSEA protocol on which all staff have been trained in 2021. A refresher session in scheduled for April 2022.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

MdM commits to the inclusion of the targeted communities into the project decision making and embeds community engagement, public communication, and management of project information into its projects. MdM incorporates Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in its interventions through communication, transparency, learning, and adaptation. During the first months of the project, the M&E Officer will collect data from the affected population on their preferred way to receive information, give feedback or complaints and the channels will be adjusted as necessary. Throughout the project, beneficiaries and other stakeholders will receive information about the project activities. This will be channeled through different ways including verbal communications and others based on beneficiary preferences. A Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) ensuring confidentiality and accessible to all beneficiaries, community members and stakeholders will be implemented through: - Satisfaction Surveys will be conducted throughout to measure quality of services, giving patients opportunity to share experiences, criticism, and highlight strengths & weakness of staff & resources. - Comment boxes will be installed in supported health facilities for patients’ recommendations and share experiences. Only MdM staff will have access and responses will be collected and reported weekly. When relevant and appropriate, the feedback and/or complaints will be discussed with partners. - A phone number and email address will be established and communicated - IEC material validation workshops with relevant and key members. - Satisfaction surveys after awareness raising sessions and training sessions. - Appointment of AAP focal persons in each health facility and each community who receive training on how to receive and report complaints in a confidential manner. By opening multiple communication channels (phone, email, comment boxes, community meetings, satisfaction surveys), MdM increases accessibility and diversity in responses, thus allowing for an improved monitoring of project performance and effectiveness. Finally, MdM commits to answering non-sensitive incoming feedback and complaints within a month, and sensitive feedback and complaints in a shorter time frame depending on the level of incidence. In the case of PSEA, this is a maximum period of two weeks.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Multiple efforts will be undertaken to address the protection risks associated with the project. The affected population will participate in program design through an assessment and participatory discussions that include questions on their basic needs and preferred ways to address them, access to channels of information, as well as protection risks associated. A beneficiary feedback mechanism emphasizing on mutual communication will be in place. The priority will be to offer a variety of channels to raise feedback or complaints, so that affected populations with different needs will be able to access and use the mechanism. Beneficiaries will be consulted on their preferred mechanisms to give feedback, ensuring that people with different vulnerabilities and characteristics are part of this assessment. Furthermore, to ensure staff neutrality, the mechanism will be managed by the M&E team, who will receive on-the-job training, including training on protection, to ensure that feedback and complaints, especially any PSAE complaint will be dealt with in an appropriate, confidential and time efficient manner. At the same time, clear SOPs and referral pathways will be developed, including managing critical or sensitive cases that will be registered on a separate database with limited access and will be answered within 24 hours. Other practical measures will be incorporated in the activity design to minimize risk of exposure to violence due to distances to medical services. Mobile clinics will be prioritised, to offer quality maternal and child healthcare, SRH, and SHBV/CMR to women in a safe way, ensuring they do not need to take dangerous routes to reach these services. The health workers running the mobile clinics will receive training on SGBV and mental health topics to better address protection needs that are identified in the rural communities. Regarding data protection, MdM counts on a reference guide on the management of data related to health and life stories of persons in the field, “For Ethics in the Field”, (https://www.medecinsdumonde.org/en/actualites/publications/2012/10/08/ethics-field), which details several tools and guidelines based on Humanitarian and Do No Harm Principles. Program users must understand what we are going to do (consultation, examination, treatment, interview, film, photographs) and must give their full and free consent. Specific guidance is provided in case of child mistreatment, acts of torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatments and victims of any kind of violence. MdM has the duty to inform, to protect and to attest following a request from a victim of violence.
