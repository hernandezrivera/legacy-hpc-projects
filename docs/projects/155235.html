Project Summary

OBAT Helpers provides preventative, promotive and curative primary health services along with simple diagnostic investigations and referral services to other health facilities.
A crucial component of the OBAT Primary Health Center is the community-based health workforce ensuring a solid preventative/promotive whereby Community Health Worker’s are trained and tasked with ensuring continuum of care between health facilities and the community and conducting health.hygiene education on key health topics.

Needs

The health status of the population in need remains fragile. Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations per person per year (Sphere standards), however critical gaps remain in access to 24/7 services with each functional 24/7 primary health care centers serving on average 54,000 refugees (standard: 1:20,000). The temporary nature of facility infrastructure poses a challenge to longer-term continuity of services, as the infrastructures degrade. Marginalized and vulnerable groups (such as the elderly, people living with disabilities and chronic conditions, survivors of gender based violence, sex workers etc.) are also not accessing health services. Adherence to minimum standards for services and staffing and quality of care in health facilities is a concern and availability of Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) treatment, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services (including inpatient/psychiatric care) and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) remain limited, as identified in the health sector external review. Community outreach activities need to be improved, harmonized and monitored. Additional support is needed to strengthen host community facilities and a mechanism is needed to cover referral costs to avoid burden on Government facilities. Entering into the second year of crisis, there is a need to extend regular National health programs to cover Rohingya population and to streamline health coordination particularly at Upazila and camp level to support the Ministry of Health. Access to non-communicable diseases treatment and essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal and newborn health services remain a major concern. An estimated less than 32% of deliveries among the refugees occurred in facilities reflecting both demand and access issues. Newborn care is poor, especially at community level and referral linkages need to be improved. Family planning needs remain unaddressed, particularly for the most vulnerable populations and support is required to strengthen neonatal care. The SRH needs for high-risk and special need populations including sex workers, survivors of gender based violence and adolescents are yet to be addressed. Progress was made in routine immunization but coverage remains insufficient in some areas, demand for immunization needs to be consistently created and surveillance needs to be maintained, supported by a robust laboratory surveillance. WASH conditions continues to pose considerable risk for outbreaks. Ensuring facility-based readiness for monsoon and cyclone season, including risk of outbreaks, requires substantive investment. Existing information management systems need to be evaluated and streamlined for improved monitoring of health status of affected populations and health service delivery. Protection services and linkages with health facilities need strengthening. All health services will meet sector minimum service package for health post/phc. Continuation of service for all health services will be 2019-20
