Project Summary

Through this project, ACTED will aim to provide food assistance to 45,000 HH (270,000 beneficiaries).
ACTED will provide immediate food assistance to insecure individuals (25,000 HH/ 150,000 beneficiaries) in Greater Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Warrap, Upper Nile and Lakes states, while working with communities to invest in long term food security interventions. To provide immediate food assistance, ACTED plans to make unconditional cash transfers, conditional cash transfers, and restricted cash vouchers. Conditional cash transfers through Cash for Assets programs will not only improve purchasing power of beneficiaries, but will also contribute to creation, construction and rehabilitation of key livelihood assets such as water catchments and dykes to manage floods, community access roads to improve movement and access (particularly for producers to markets), and shallow wells to improve access to water. Restricted cash will focus on purchase of livelihood inputs and assets such as seeds and tools to enable beneficiaries invest on their lands thereby increasing amount of land planted, and addressing the challenge of seeds scarcity.
Long-term investment and capacity building on agricultural production, modern farming techniques and productive assets will also be achieved through resilience programming, targeting 20,000 HH (120,000 beneficiaries). ACTED will adopt the APFS model to facilitate learning and encourage transfer of knowledge and skills in modern farming techniques, encourage community engagement through participatory risk assessments, toward participatory planning and joint ownership of resources. Such an investment will be pegged on utilization of local capacities and knowledge, which will then be reinforced through interactive sessions toward realizing improved livelihoods, reduced gender inequality, inclusive economic and social growth, and overall transformation of communities. In addition, ACTED will also support agricultural production through seeds and tools distributions, seed multiplication farms, vegetable gardening, post-harvest management training and construction of post-harvest structures such as granaries and drying slabs. Moreover, ACTED will support pastoralists and livestock activities through training and support to community animal health workers (CAHWs) and awareness and vaccination campaigns.
Livelihood changes have been felt across the country due to constant displacement, economic downturn, pests and diseases especially the FAW, lack of quality seeds, failed or delayed rains, and poor post-harvest management. ACTED aims to alleviate extreme hunger through immediate lifesaving assistance and interventions to build on community resilience through models that address underlying causes of food insecurity, inequality, and promote transformative change in the face of man-made and natural disasters. This is in line with the FSL cluster integration model and response strategy where ACTED will focus on building sustainable livelihoods through inclusive programming.

Needs

South Sudan’s food insecurity continues to remain severe, with 6.1m people (59% of the population) facing IPC Crisis or worse (Emergency and Catastrophe) levels of food insecurity, and with 1.7m people on the brink of famine (IPC Phase 4: Emergency) in July – August 2018 (IPC September 2018). Agricultural activities and harvests are likely to be poor in many locations due to continuing and protracted displacement, insecurity, lack of freedom of movement, and lack of agricultural inputs, therefore food insecurity levels will remain severe in 2019. Acute malnutrition levels also remain high, with 31 counties in Warrap, Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei reporting ‘Critical’ levels. Immediate causes of food insecurity are identified as conflict, reduction in agricultural outputs, decreased purchasing power, reduced trade flows, and livestock disease morbidity and mortality. In Jonglei, insecurity has rendered people extremely vulnerable as they are not able to protect their assets or livelihoods, resulting in negative coping strategies being employed such as consumption of seeds meant for planting, over-reliance on humanitarian aid, skipping of meals, and migration. 62.5% are experiencing Crisis or worse levels of food insecurity. 4,000 HH in Akobo are targeted through resilience programming, as it remains classified as IPC Phase 3, and as IDP influxes strain local resources, there remains a need for longer-term sustainable investment in FSL, and a functioning market. In Wau and Jur River 13,000 HH will be targeted through in-kind food/ cash assistance, as food insecurity remains severe due to prolonged insecurity in 2018. In particular, in Baggari it is expected that populations are in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) as of September 2018, and are in critical need of immediate lifesaving food assistance. 6,000 HH will be targeted in Maridi and Ibba through resilience programing. As Greater Equatoria is considered the country’s breadbasket region, and with WES facing relatively lower levels of food insecurity compared to the rest of the country (44% in IPC Phase 3-5), there is an opportunity to expand and encourage longer-term resilience food programming in order to prevent areas in the state from deteriorating or remaining in Crisis levels. Maridi and Ibba were classified as IPC Phase 3 in September 2018, however increased insecurity in 2017-2018 coupled by below average harvests is worsening the food security across WES, with Maridi deteriorating from IPC Phase 2 in January 2018. The project is targeting 12,000 HH through immediate food/ cash assistance and 7,000 HH through resilient programming, in Yirol East, Yirol West, and Awerial which are all classified as IPC Emergency levels. Up to 20,000 people are expected to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in Yirol East and West, with 73% of the total population facing Crisis or worse food insecurity as of September 2018. 3,000 host community HH in Maban are targeted through resilience programming, as Upper Nile continues to experience insecurity that restricts farming activities, with 70% of the population in Crisis or worse levels of food insecurity, and with much of the humanitarian response focusing on refugees. Gender relations in South Sudan are shaped by the social, cultural, political and economic realities. For instance, prevailing cultural norms marginalize women from any political activity or decision-making. Protection and gender issues are interlinked; rape, beatings, psychological abuse, denial of education and economic opportunities are commonplace and seldom reported. Gender inequality leaves women more likely to eat last, or skip meals in times of hardship. A critical learning from the DfID funded BRACED programme is the role of women as agents of transformative change. ACTED aims to not only ensure adequate presentation of women and other marginalized groups in planning committees, but also encourages them to actively participate in such fora.

Activities

1. Short-term immediate food assistance (Western Equatoria, Lakes, Jonglei, Upper Nile, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap)1.1 Unconditional, restricted cash transfers.Highly vulnerable individuals will benefit from emergency distribution of seeds and tools. These will include IDPs and returnees who have lost their livelihoods and have little/no savings.1.2 Unconditional, unrestricted cash transfers.To respond to immediate, unforeseen & catastrophic crisis in areas of intervention as well as hard to reach areas. The selection criteria will prioritize extremely vulnerable HHs. SGBV survivors will be reached through a Cash for Protection programme. 1.3 Conditional, unrestricted cash transfers (CFA).Through Cash for Assets (CFA) scheme, individuals will receive conditional cash transfers to improve immediate access to food. The selection criteria will be defined with the village planning committees, & the amount of transfer harmonized with ongoing partner projects. This will not only mitigate against conflict, but also ensure inclusivity and that the most food insecure populations are reached. 1.4 Construction/rehabilitation of communal livelihood assets through a CFA schemeAiming to increase access to shared resources, thereby easing tensions. Assets will also improve both agricultural and livestock production, by serving the communities during times of droughts and floods. Assets include: haffirs; dykes; access roads; shallow wells; fencing of livelihood assets. 2. Long term resilience activities (Jonglei, Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Lakes)2.1 Formation and training of APFS, provision of inputs: In order to foster knowledge and skills in modern farming, ACTED will work with APFS groups. Beneficiaries will learn about modern farming techniques in demonstrations gardens. They will replicate what they learn in their own individual gardens and share knowledge with their neighbours. This model will promote larger-scale agricultural production & market linkages, & improve animal husbandry including management of crops & livestock diseases. Groups will receive improved seeds, tools, & ox-plough services to enhance their production.2.2 Investment in seed multiplication farms: APFS members will be encouraged to produce good quality seeds for subsequent planting seasons & also for sale in local markets. ACTED will work with farmers, linking them to local suppliers & potential customers through trade fairs.2.3 Vegetable production during the dry season: APFS modules will be nutrition-sensitive to emphasize importance of diet diversification. This will be in the effort to address nutrition of the target population. Vegetable production will be a step forward to encourage diet diversification.2.4 Identification and training of CAHWs: To address livestock diseases, CAHWs will be trained & provided with livestock kits to provide services to APFS members & the rest of the community. They will be linked to the county Ministry of Animal Resources & Fisheries for sustainability.2.5 Formation and training of VSLAs: These are informal institutions that continue to play a key role in enabling access to credit, savings in form of assets in the face of hyperinflation, and they encourage livelihood diversification to both APFS members and other interested community members. 2.6 Distribution of ruminant animals: This will target households with no or little income resources in the effort to provide them a means of livelihood, as well as a sustainable source of food.2.7 Post-harvest management trainings: These will be factored into the APFS model, and emphasized throughout the year. Post management structures such as granaries and drying slabs will be constructed to promote proper handling, drying & storage of produce.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed throughout the project to ensure that the affected population and beneficiaries are informed and included throughout all stages of the project, particularly regarding the objectives and activities of the project. In addition, the CBPP will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community in each of the project locations, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. The CBPP approach will ensure that the project is accountable, accepted, and owned by the targeted population.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific, as they take into consideration the different contexts and dynamics of each project location, as well as the type of beneficiary (e.g. new or protracted IDP, host communities). In addition, the CBPP and Project Management Committees are representative of the different gender and age groups (male, female, youth, elderly), and specific information and messages are targeted towards specific groups. Particularly vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities and persons with special needs are also prioritised and targeted during distributions.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. A focal point for protection and gender complaints will be established in each location, to address the targeted communities’ concerns and queries regarding protection and gender equality. Furthermore, during verifications, distributions and assessments/ monitoring, ACTED will ensure that the specific needs of women and girls are taken into consideration and prioritized. ACTED’s staff have all been trained on protection, gender equality and sexual exploitation, including on how to identify and address any issues or concerns, and in addition will be supported with technical guidance by ACTED’s Protection Manager based in Juba. Moreover, ACTED has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review, agree to and sign.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project locations, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Complaints can be lodged through complaints desks/ boxes set up at distribution and project sites, over the phone to the designated CRM hotline, or directly to ACTED's staff. The beneficiaries and local community through awareness sessions, as well as the CBPP process.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. Project Management Committees will be established in each location to ensure that there is ongoing communication and coordination between the beneficiaries and ACTED. The PMCs will monitor the project activities from the beneficiaries' perspective, and provide feedback and identify issues through meetings with ACTED. In addition, post distribution monitoring/ rapid assessments will also be conducted over the course of the project, and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, as well as their level of satisfaction. The PDM will consist of representative surveys, as well as key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to gather a more in depth body of information.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. ACTED will ensure that the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities are prioritized for selection, based on vulnerability criteria including household size/ number of dependents, income opportunities, and having persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, and the elderly. Similarly, ACTED will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities (PWDs), persons with special needs (PSNs), and the elderly, are prioritized and addressed during distributions, as well as the implementation of project activities. For example, ACTED will have a designated protection staff at each distribution that will identify and fast track beneficiaries that are identified as being particularly vulnerable, such as PWDs and PSNs. Moreover, ACTED will aim to, wherever possible/ feasible, conduct last mile distributions to the most vulnerable beneficiaries that are unable to reach the distribution site.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As the project is targeting IDPs and areas that have recently experienced insecurity or are in close proximity to areas of insecurity, there are a number of potential barriers to access. Firstly, ACTED may have restricted and limited access to the various locations targeted by the project due to insecurity, which could delay or prevent the implementation of the activities. To mitigate this, ACTED will monitor the security situation closely, and advocate with local authorities for access if/ when safe to do so to ensure that the project is implemented. In addition, the targeted IDPs could become displaced again over the course of the project due to further insecurity or their lack of access to basic services, which could prevent them from accessing the project sites and benefitting from the project activities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

By providing immediate food assistance to the most food insecure populations, the project will address protection threats stemming from negative coping strategies, such as eating wild foods, and lack of livelihoods opportunities forcing women and children to go to isolated and bushy areas in search of firewood. Moreover, the project targets the most vulnerable populations in areas with high food insecurity.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring aims to measure the access of beneficiaries through specific questions about their ability to access the project sites and distributions, as well as their perceived freedom of movement/ restrictions, and reported access to various services, including local markets, and their access has been affected by the project, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring measures the safety of beneficiaries through specific questions regarding their main safety concerns, experiences, and perceptions of their own safety, both in general and during the implementation of project activities, and if/ how these have changed over the course of the project, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring aims to measure the dignity of beneficiaries through specific questions regarding their feelings and perceptions of their dignity, as well as their satisfaction with the project activities and if they are satisfied that their needs were taken into consideration, and if/ how these have changed over the course of the project, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews. The questions will also focus on how the beneficiaries perceived the project approach and activities contributed or not to improving their dignity.
