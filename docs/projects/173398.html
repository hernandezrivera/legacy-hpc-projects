Project Summary

The project will implement activities as per the following description to achieve the objective of people in need having access to water and sanitation. The three subsectors of the intervention are presented below:
Water Supply: aligned with WASH Sector’s Plan 4 small scale solar-powered piped water networks will be installed in Camp 20 Extension to provide safe water to the beneficiaries. O&M of existing tube-wells in IOM’s AoR, water network in Camps 12, 13, 19, 24 and 20 Extension will be continued. Emergency stocks will be procured to prepare for cyclones or other natural disasters. A study will be conducted to assess the current water supply approach, gaps and identify potential sources to cover the gaps in the host community and refugee camps in Teknaf. Additional reservoir/sedimentation tanks will be constructed to increase the treatment and distribution capacity in the Leda surface water treatment plant. However, new Tube Wells will be installed based on the needs in four host community villages under Ukhiya and Teknaf.
Sanitation: based on need, durable latrines will be constructed, and old latrine will be upgraded to minimize desludging and health risks in both refugee and host communities. These include provisions for gender-inclusivity and usability for Persons with Disabilities (PwD). Decentralized Wastewater Treatment Systems (DEWATS) will be constructed to address coverage gaps, and few old Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) systems will be upgraded along with regular O&M of existing FSM systems. Additional bathing facilities and high platform septic tank latrines will be constructed in the developed valley. WASH Facility disinfection activities will be continued to reduce the risk of COVID-19 disease transmission. Solid waste management will be strengthened by focusing on reduction and recycling of waste produced–promoting household waste separation, small and large-scale composting, and recycling of plastics in both host and refugee community.
Hygiene Promotion and Epidemic Control: Critical supplies of hygiene items (soap, water containers, Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) materials, etc.) will be provided, together with community education and mobilization activities targeting all age and gender groups at the household and community level. Communities will be more engaged in O&M of WASH facilities, waste segregation and preventing COVID-19 disease transmission. A real time service monitoring and community feedback mechanisms will be established to ensure timely response for any WASH-related health risks, and emergency response stocks will be maintained in case of any Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) or other outbreaks.

1 - Identified Needs

Needs of refugees:Improved Water Services. 54%of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks as partners have achieved 102 water networks while 68 are still under construction over the 189 planned as part of the Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with handpumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without fecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. 88% of households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf (55% of refugees reporting enough water in Camp 26).Access to Sanitation. Despite 99% of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached (18 persons per latrine and 38 persons per 5 bathing cubicles), many challenges remain with 22% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas. Sanitation coverage remains challenging due to a lack of space in the camps with the highest density (e.g: 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 9, 10). 68% of households reported having a private space inside or attached to their shelter for bathing but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry (56%) and dishwashing places (52%) are informal and often unhygienic. One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades and improvements. With 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produced daily in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated areas, where available, and 27% report the presence of garbage near their houses.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Appropriate hygiene practices remain low despite the fact that progresses have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 with a focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the Covid-19 outbreak and 95% reported having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), 1.3 MHM kits were provided to women and girls on average between January to September instead of one every trimester. Needs of host communities:Improved Water Services. The large majority of households in the host communities (88%) access water from tube wells/handpumps, but only 77% of households reported having enough water for their domestic need (only 74% in Teknaf where groundwater is scared). Over the past year, this figure has significantly increased (from 63% in 2019) thanks to the drilling of 893 boreholes in Ukhiya and Teknaf. Despite high pumping in the aquifers below the mega-camp, groundwater availability has been proven sustainable, however the impact on surrounding communities is significant due to depression from pumping cone requiring deeper tube wells and better handpumps in host communities (especially in the north of Kutupalong’s camp). Access to Sanitation. 63% of households have at least basic access to sanitation with 14% of the HH practicing, sometimes, open defecation. Regarding waste management, 11% reported often visible having waste in the vicinity of their house. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Host’s communities have been less supported by hygiene items and campaigns, but findings show encouraging data as 96% of the HH reported having access to soap. Hygiene knowledge seems also better than in camps as 81% of HC respondents being able to mention 3 critical times for handwashing.
