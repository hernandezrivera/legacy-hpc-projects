Project Summary

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) underscore that 15.3 million individuals will require humanitarian assistance in 2024, marking an increase of 0.7 million from the preceding year. The challenging circumstances in northwest Syria have been exacerbated by the far-reaching effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and the spread of Cholera. Although there has been a gradual decline in the number of positive COVID-19 cases, there persists a crucial need to concentrate on the treatment and containment of the virus, particularly its impact on displaced individuals and other vulnerable communities, including healthcare personnel. The focus is also on supporting the expansion of the public health response.
A significant initiative in addressing these challenges is the Food Security Project, aimed at enhancing food security for the most vulnerable populations. The Cholera spread in northwest Syria remains a concern, necessitating attention to prevent further infections. Specifically, the project targets the basic and food security-related needs of 1620 vulnerable Syrian internally displaced persons (IDPs) families (12,000 beneficiaries) in Dana Sub-district and Ariha district. This assistance includes targeted food baskets and bread support.
Rahma Worldwide initiative operates at the household level, with a focus on the most vulnerable households selected based on criteria such as gender, marital status, number of children, pregnancy, lactation, widowhood, orphanhood (under 18 years old without parents), chronic illness, disability, shelter, income, livelihood, and IDP status. Local councils are informed in advance of distribution, and beneficiaries receive notifications through SMS or other available methods.
The Food Basket comprises essential food items such as rice, sugar, oil, butter, tomato sauce, pasta, tea, and an 800-gram bread bundle. This support is designed to sustain 12,000 IDPs in Idlib and Azaz Camps, with a specific focus on women, children, widows, and elderly individuals. Distribution is conducted in-kind, door-to-door, to minimize the risk of infectious diseases, and the team adheres to COVID-19 protocols. Distribution occurs monthly during the last week of the month.
Only the mentioned activities will be conducted throughout this project and no other items like blankets will be distributed, and the selection criteria will be based upon the scoring tools that meet the targeting criteria.
As of the planning phase for 2024, there is currently no secured funding for the projects outlined.
M&E Activities :Food distribution in Idlib camps is a critical humanitarian effort, often organized by various international aid agencies, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and sometimes local authorities. These distributions aim to provide essential nutrition to displaced people and refugees who have fled conflict and violence in Syria. The food baskets typically contain items such as rice, lentils, canned goods, cooking oil, sugar, and other non-perishable items.
The distribution process involves several steps:
Assessment and Planning: Rahma will assess the needs of the camp population to determine the quantity and types of food required. This assessment considers factors such as the number of people, dietary requirements, cultural preferences, and availability of food resources.
Procurement: Once the needs are determined, Rahma will procure the necessary food items either through direct purchase or donations from governments, international organizations, or private donors.
Packaging: The food items are then packaged into baskets or parcels for distribution. Packaging is often done in a way that ensures the items are easy to transport, store, and distribute efficiently.
Distribution: Distribution points are set up within the camps or in nearby areas accessible to the displaced population. Aid workers, volunteers, or local authorities oversee the distribution process to ensure fairness
