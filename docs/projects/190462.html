Project Summary

In alignment with EHF 2021 2nd round strategic allocation document, WVE designed this project to respond to the emergency needs of the people in Oromia region of East Wollega zone (Kiramu , Gida Ayana and Gutu Gida Woredas ) and West Wollega zone (Guliso, Mana Sibu and Kiltu kara Woredas).
WVE will deploy six Mobile Health and Nutrition Teams (MHNT), one per each Woreda in the two zones.. One MHNT will consist of five health workers and will include: one Health Officer, one Outpatient Therapeutic Patient/Stabilization Center (OTP/SC) Nurse, one Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Officer, one Midwife, and one GVB, Psycho social and Mental Health Officer. MHNT is expected to act as temporary and /or outreach clinic for OPD consultation like ANC, PNC, labor and delivery, screening of GBV survivable, child and neonatal illness, communicable and non-communicable infectious diseases, referral linkage and other common lines under their respective Woreda or catchment area. The project will support the transportation and distribution of Emergency Drug Kit (EDKs), cholera kits, reproductive health kits, trauma kits, and other medical supplies from SWAN /WHO/UNICEF/UNFPA warehouses to 36 targeted health facilities.
WV will work in collaboration with district health authorities and communities to support the populations that have suffered from the conflict have access to mentally/psychologically services through enhancing the referral system. Given the especially high levels of health and protection concerns for women in in West and East Wollega of Oromia region, WV expects the needs to be high over the project period. The project will work towards creating referral linkages to an estimated 180 GBV survivors out of these 72 need referral for further investigation, diagnosis and management at referral center .The GBV officer (from protection team) within each of the MHNTs will ensure a response to the health needs of survivors of gender-based violence. The officer will help the women in a way that is supportive, helps to meet her needs, and prioritizes her continued safety without intruding on her privacy. The GBV officer will immediately refer survivors with life-threatening or severe conditions for emergency treatment, otherwise, the GBV officer will screen, diagnose, and provide PSS for less critical cases on site. All survivors will be referred for further clinical care to the nearest health facilities with referral slips. WV will provide PFA and GBV basic principles training for health workers and front line workers. Additionally, WV will advocate for service availability at referral sites through protection cluster for strengthening humanitarian assistance in the area and establishment of referral pathways for protection cases.
WV will support health facilities that are highly affected by the conflict and need support to resume operations. WV will also provide capacity building training to 48 health workers on Psychological First Aid (PFA and train 48 Public Health Emergency Management Task Force and health workers on outbreak control, management, and response. Additionally, 120 health extension workers (HEWs) will be trained on active surveillance, screening, contact tracing and referrals.
In response to high instances of disease outbreaks in East and West Wollega zones\, like measles and cholera, the project will work with the local health authorities. Finally, WV will strengthen Ethiopia Public Health Emergency Management’s (PHEM) response capacity on supervision and monitoring. More than 147, 962 IDPs and host communities who directly affected from conflicts will benefit from the project.

1. - Projects activities

The project aims to enhance primary health care and referral facilities to provide equitable, high-impact and high-quality health services across the continuum of care for host, IDP and returnees with the following key Priority activities. Provide Mobile Health and Nutrition Team (MHNT) outreach services and primary healthcare (PHC) services to vulnerable populations affected by humanitarian crisis and health emergencies in West and east Wollga zone of Oromia, reaching 64,800 people.WVE will deploy six Mobile Health and Nutrition Teams (MHNT), one per each Woreda in the two zones. One MHNT will consist of five health workers and will include: one Health Officer, one Outpatient Therapeutic Patient/Stabilization Center (OTP/SC) Nurse, one Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) Officer, one Midwife, and one GVB, Psycho social and Mental Health Officer. MHNT is expected to act as temporary and /or outreach clinic for OPD consultation like ANC, PNC, labor and delivery, screening of GBV survivable, child and neonatal illness, communicable and non-communicable infectious diseases, referral linkage and other common lines under their respective Woreda or catchment area. Additionally, WV will support transportation, logistics, and per diem for 180 HWs and HEWs from RHBs, ZHDs, WorHOs and HFs to attend quarterly review meetings. These will be sector specific joint supervisory meetings using already available monitoring and supervision checklists to assess performance of all HF teams (outpatient department, laboratory, pharmacy, etc.). Oral and written feedback will be shared with the teams. During review, each team will present quarterly performance, challenges, best practices, lessons learned, support needed from the partners, and their upcoming quarter plan. After each supportive supervision and review meeting, project staff will develop an action plan for the next quarter and share it with key stakeholders for support and follow up. Moreover, the project will support procurement and distribution of basic Infection Prevention and Control supplies and equipment for 36 targeted HFs serving IDPs and host communities. WV will also support on-site training and mentorship for HWs to ensure HFs are aligned on IPC standards and guidelines. On top of that, World Vision intends to train 48 Public Health Emergency Management Task Force (health workers, on outbreak control, management, and response). Besides, the project will capacitate 48 PHEM task force who are focal points at health facility and office level. The training will be given by using the standard guidelines and modules developed by EPHI and WHO. Train 120 Health Extension Workers (HEWs) on active surveillance, screening, contact tracing and referrals. Additionally, WV will train 120 HEWs on cholera , measles and COVID-19 rapid response, active surveillance, screening, contact tracing and transport and referral linkages. In addition, World Vision will support Mass vaccination campaigns including measles, COVID 19, cholera etc (logistic support). In this regard, the project will support government and UNICEF-led vaccination campaigns with transportation and logistics support for mass vaccination campaigns, particularly for cholera and measles, have been prioritized as these diseases are highly contagious and have the potential to spread rapidly in the affected areas.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

World Vision is regularly represented in the national and regional Health Clusters, chaired by the WHO and MOH. WV will use this information to inform its program design and implementation, as well as work collaboratively with the Cluster and other partners. At the regional-level, WV also participates in Nutrition Cluster meetings, and coordinates with the regional Disaster Prevention and Preparedness Commissions (DPPCs). Participation in these platforms enhances sector knowledge with regular updates to understand the gaps in responses to plan for optimal interventions. The Cluster coordination meetings also allow for information sharing among implementing partners based on discovered needs, emerging trends, response action, and Ethiopia Operational Activities through the 4W matrix. World Vision receives funding from UNOCHA and other donors and has strong relationships and engagement with them. In addition, WV will facilitate coordination with existing systems and donors such as the UNOCHA EHF system, with local government authorities and INGO consortium members in the region and allow for optimal use of project resources. Moreover, WV has operational presence with well-organized and experienced staff in all the proposed areas. Our extensive knowledge of the local context and strong historical partnerships with the local government and communities, and strong experience in emergency and developmental implementation makes it well placed to effectively execute sound interventions for the identified gaps. WV participates in all Zonal/Woreda humanitarian coordination forums, including nutrition sector-specific, attending regular meetings of proposed Woredas which is very important to avoid resource overlaps.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project has been participatory from its start, as it is designed based on the consultation and focus group discussion with the community during the assessment to understand the community’s priorities and choice of modality. For this reason, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries are principles incorporated into all stages of implementing program activities. The project plans to share all the required information about the project to the beneficiaries in their local language, and there will be a feedback mechanism concerning the program delivery. Sensitization sessions will also be used as potential entry points for increasing awareness. Additionally, measures for accountability to affected populations in this project include locally appropriate provision of information to project participants, complaints and feedback response mechanisms, and participation and consultation during different stages of the project. Ensuring that stakeholders, beneficiaries and those in the target communities can hold WV accountable will improve the quality of the consortium. Beneficiaries and community members living in the areas where WV is operational have a right to raise complaints and to receive responses. Establishing accessible, confidential and responsive feedback mechanisms, and ensuring procedures are in place to address issues is crucial for accountability. This project’s complaint response mechanism (CRM) includes a) information sharing: community meetings/consultations, Disaster Risk Reduction committees, Community Health Workers and other sector committees designed to provide project information, and planning and participation of communities at various stages of the project. Translated posters will also be used to raise fraud and protection issues, taking into account local religion, culture and norms. b) Community Reference Groups (CRGs): The consortium will establish CRGs with community members who are accepted and recognized in the wider community. Comprised of 2-3 people in each group with gender consideration, these groups serve as a mediator between beneficiaries and the project. The CRGs gather information, feedback from the targeted beneficiaries and confidentially share the information with the consortium. After analyzing the information, the consortium will provide responses. WV will form a committee to assess the effectiveness on the matter as required. c) Suggestion boxes: The consortium will place suggestion boxes in public places such as health facilities for beneficiaries to share any feedback.
