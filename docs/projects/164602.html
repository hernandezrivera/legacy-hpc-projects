Project Summary


The proposed project contributes to strengthening the resilience of the population of Alepp and Idleb as the measures will decrease their vulnerability and invest in strengthening structures that will help the population after project end to meet their needs. In addition, the activities have the potential to reinforce each other.
The intervention will cover the WASH needs as below:
Water:
- Water station rehabilitation.
- Water networks fixing.
- Concrete water tanks constructing
- Water stations operating.
- Water safety plan applying.
- Water trucking
- Water tanks distributing
- Water in health facilities and schools
Sanitation:
- Waste collection
- Landfill rehabilitation
- Waste management operation cost.
- Sewage and drainage system rehabilitation.
- Toilets and showers constructing and fixing
- Cleaning the rivers watercourses with all needed to fix the roads on.
- Sanitation in schools and Health facilities
Hygiene:
- Hygiene kits distribution
-Hygiene awareness
-Hygiene in schools and health facilities
The project will target men, women, boys, and girls from the three governorates, both host communities, and IDPs from within cities in those governorates as well as the surrounding areas or other parts of the city who live in the targeted neighborhoods.

4 - Needs

Since the beginning of 2019, following an escalation of violence in north-western and southern Syria, the region has witnessed the further arrival of a substantial number of IDPs. Large cross-line displacements from northern Hama and southern Idleb governorate, beginning in February 2019, have led to a significant increase of the IDP population in the region and to a growing strain on resources and services in the region. While humanitarian assistance is reaching both IDP and resident populations, major gaps remain. In order to address such gaps, CARE and its partners have conducted a rapid needs assessment to inform humanitarian actors of IDP and resident populations' priority needs and to address critical information gaps on the humanitarian conditions of IDPs and residents living in communities and camps in the region. This data set provides the findings from this assessment. Findings presented in this data set are based on data collected by CARE as part of a need’s assessment focused on IDP and resident populations living in communities of Idleb governorate and surrounding areas. Regarding the WASH services in general, 36% of interviewed HHs stated that they have been receiving water services only since they have been displaced, while 53% received nothing since they arrived in terms of WASH services. Indeed, 88% of the assessed communities said that water trucking is the primary source of drinking water in their locations. In terms of water quality, only 1% from total interviewed HHs said that they have been receiving treated drinking water., often because networks hasn’t capacities to serve all newly IDPs and host communities, Lack of sustained access to electricity. Electricity production, transmission, and distribution have been heavily affected by ongoing hostilities and this lack of electricity has had a negative impact across Health and WASH sectors since all the water boosting stations are being operated relying 100% on electricity. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Syria Relief staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in also WASH projects. Measures to protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be contextualized to the political and humanitarian situation. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the location and design of WASH facilities in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the SR MEAL staff that will always be available over the targeted locations. Moreover, SR team will be coordinating closely with partners in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. For example, latrines in schools, clinics, hospitals, and GBV centers will be lit and lockable. SR will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in schools, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. SR will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. All proposed activities are in full alignment (UNSCR) 1325. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, Syria Relief will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by Syria Relief staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Turkey for a response.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Closure of Bab Al Hawa crossing border resulting zero or limited access for staff or goods inside Idleb - Lack of access to targeted areas for the team inside Idleb due to Security situation - Limited capacity for staff to transfer funds to Idleb due to Turkish regulations - Turnover of staff - Delays in the procurement process as a result of prices fluctuation - landowners refusal for signing the MOUs for rehabilitation of WaSH infrastructure due to frictions with local councils. - Delays in project implementation due to the Turkish restriction on transporting goods inside Idleb, Hama, and Aleppo - Delays in project implementation due to the interference of the armed groups in procurement/ imposing taxes /selection of the project locations. - Overlapping of project activities and double agreement by local councils as a result of a lack of coordination. - Syrian regime offensive onto Idleb governorate (airstrikes, a ground offensive, chemical attacks…) - Violent armed conflict between opposition armed groups in Idleb. - Turkey takes control of Idlib governorate. - loss of the neutrality of the organization. - Being associated with any particular conflicting group(s) (much in line with the aforementioned, loss of neutrality) - Fuel sources directly contributing towards that of the war economy - violation of humanitarian principles or code of conduct by staff/ organization loss of confidential data (Data security) - noncompliance with donor requirements in line with a signed agreement - corruption, mismanagement of funds and fraud Assumptions and Mitigating measures: -Constant Security monitoring and analysis of Security Risk Environment and Political Developments which impact on Access -
