Project Summary

The MENTOR Initiative is a non-profit international NGO that specializes in reducing deaths and suffering caused by vector-borne and neglected tropical disease. MENTOR has been leading the international response to the leishmaniasis epidemic in Syria since 2013, has a proven track record of working effectively with local authorities and achieving direct access to the homes of millions of people in conflict affected areas each year to achieve its disease control aims. MENTOR is highly respected throughout its area of operations in northern Syria and works in close collaboration with the WHO, national authorities and other health agencies to address the epidemic across the region.
MENTOR - supported by WHO Gaziantep - has come to recognize that to bring the leishmaniasis epidemic under control, that it along with the overall HA community must intensify efforts in hopes of truly impacting the spread of this disease. MENTOR has developed a goal of local controlled elimination to tackle this epidemic with a goal of 10% incident case reduction each year over the next 3-5 years. Building on efforts to date this plan can be actioned with OFDA support through 2019.
This will include the provision of medical support to one hundred twenty nine (129) medical points serving a catchment area of over 3 million people and assuring access for all in need; while conducting targeted prevention campaigns to protect 1,159,347 of the most vulnerable people from the disease vector (sandfly) that is responsible for disease transmission.
The statistical records accumulated by MENTOR demonstrate that MENTOR’s activities have had a significant controlling effect on the level and intensity of the leishmaniasis epidemic (see Section 2.a.1 ‘Needs Assessments Communicable Disease’ pages 7-9). The program will continue to use an established and extensive network of contacts and trained personnel, using disease control and treatment protocols that the organisation has already honed, and that adhere strictly to WHO guidelines

Needs

There are not one, but two epidemics of leishmaniasis underway in Syria: CL and VL.CL epidemic: Since 2013 MENTOR has been supporting health facilities inside Syria run by other international NGOs, and operating fully financed mobile leishmaniasis clinics to cover gaps in healthcare (thus leishmaniasis treatment) provision. MoUs drawn up between the NGO/health authority and MENTOR include an agreement to use the organization’s patient registration books in each health facility, designed to capture leishmaniasis data, and to allow the weekly collection of this data by MENTOR’s medical officers. This vast archive of data tracks progress and assesses needs, and provides a sound basis to the program projection for the programme areas of the 4 governorates.Having worked and collated data in Syria since 2014, MENTOR is now able to demonstrate the impact and efficacy of disease control measures used to tackle the leishmaniasis epidemic in Syria. In the governorate of Idleb, the impact of MENTOR’s disease control measures and IRS activity can be seen . Following initial intervention with medical support, the prevalence dropped dramatically in 2014. Complimentary IRS activity in 2015 succeeded in reducing incidence which can be seen by the sharp drops following successive campaigns. After accounting for an annual surge following transmission season, the incidence is sustained at a lower level than the preceding year. MENTOR’s IRS campaign in 2015 sprayed 91,390 households providing protection to 514,436 people while the following year 263,310 households were sprayed providing protection to 1,572,479 from sandflies. Both campaigns succeeded in reducing the incidence and helping to bring the epidemic under control in these areas. VL epidemic: Historically, Syria is not a country associated with VL. Between 2004 and 2008 the WHO recorded a national average of just 14 cases per annum. MENTOR however has proven otherwise and has alarming evidence that this is a major silent killer that needs immediate action In May 2016, suspicions heightened by a trickle of VL cases, MENTOR conducted a community based disease prevalence survey in the 2 villages of Betya and Tellemar, in Salqin, Idlib, using Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) to confirm cases. A 10 day awareness campaign coupled with the support of local authorities produced a turnout of 90%, with 4,226 people tested. This saturation testing improves statistical probability and removes sampling bias. MENTOR disclosed a high rate of VL infection with 8 inhabitants testing positive (1 in every 500, extrapolating to 19/10,000 population, compared to WHO’s national estimate of 0.02/10,000). In fact, MENTOR supported activities uncovered 17 cases of VL in this small locale in just 11 weeks, all of which were successfully treated but would likely otherwise have died. Over the past 3 years MENTOR has uncovered the strong presence of VL in 5 districts . VL is clearly far more widespread than previously believed and possibly responsible for thousands of misdiagnosed deaths each year. A major lesson learnt during this survey was that health professionals are poorly aware of VL, since the disease has little historical presence in Syria, mimics other general malaises, and lacks the clear diagnostic hallmarks of CL. VL is rarely considered as a differential diagnosis. However, raising awareness through running the survey and familiarizing local health professionals with the easy-to-use RDT resulted in a subsequent spate of verified VL case reports. VL undoubtedly exists in other regions. This fatal form of the disease is endemic to southern Iraq where the recent conflict has generated a large movement of IDPs from the south to the north of the country. This has led to outbreaks of leishmaniasis in northern Iraq, which had previously not been exposed to the disease, to the extent that the MoH and NGOs requested support from MENTOR in dealing with the outbreak. Implementation of activities

Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS): The IRS campaign will be strategically conducted prior to or just inside the disease transmission season. Sandflies require a minimum temperature of 16.5°C to prosper, although larva development can take place from 9°C and above. Generally, this means a transmission season lasting from March to October, with peak sandfly numbers in September. As IRS lasts 6-9 months, depending on the substrate sprayed, correct timing ensures maximum benefit. The sandfly is 1/3rd the size of the mosquito and silent, but the residents perceive the benefit of IRS because of the reduction of more conspicuous nuisance pests such as cockroaches, bed bugs, flies and mosquitos. This is also an added health benefit. MENTOR will use FICAM as the insecticide for IRSAll insecticide will be stored in the main MENTOR warehouses in Syria in a cool room and kept on palettes, 50cm from the wall in dry conditions under 30 degrees centigrade. During the campaign, the pesticides are distributed to subwarehouses for immediate usage on a weekly basis. All warehouses are guarded 24/7. All stock is used during campaigns, additional households can be targeted to allow for greater coverage. All insecticide holders (spray tanks and empty sachets) are returned to MENTOR warehouses at the end of a campaign. At the end of a campaign all empty sachets will be disposed of in the same manner as medical waste in a designated incinerator. In addition all spray tanks will be cleaned with water with waste water which will be stored and used to fill up the next tanks the following day in line with WHO guidelines. All insecticides will be used in the campaign or disposed of in a controlled manner. Spray tanks will be cleaned and stored for future campaigns.All IRS campaigns will be carried out by MENTOR trained teams employing local workers to gain the trust and confidence of the communities. Each spraying team will consist of 8 sprayers and 8 assistants with 1 supervisor. 4 supervisors have 1 manager. The managers will report to the Syrian prevention coordinators who in turn will report to the Prevention Coordinator in Turkey. Personnel will be thoroughly trained by MENTOR beforehand, abilities assessed, and teams assembled. Training will cover health and safety, efficient spraying technique, and equipment maintenance. By engendering pride in the teams’ work, MENTOR obtains exceptional results. The assistant will go ahead of the sprayer to assist with moving or covering furniture, filling in the data sheets, and issuing and explaining IEC/BCC leaflets. Awareness will be created beforehand with banners and announcements, and in the evening after spraying with megaphones in the next day’s locality. Spraying will include animal houses and outbuildings, and a minimum coverage of 80% of households will be achieved following WHO guidelines to ensure community level protection.Community resistance to IRS in a country where suspicion of chemical reagents is at a height can understandably be a problem. MENTOR has worked to overcome this issue through effective awareness campaigns; the employment of local workers, engendering the trust and confidence of the local community; the recruitment of female workers to facilitate access to homes when the male head of household is absent; printed IEC material accompanied by verbal explanation during the house to house visits, also highlighting the general benefits of eliminating a range of household pests; spraying animal houses, beneficial to both the animals and the household by the elimination of nuisance flies; and above all, a friendly and courteous manner. MENTOR IRS teams are now requested in over 90% of accessible households in northern Syria when conducting campaigns.Primary quality control is provided by management supervision. Secondary quality control is provided by the internal auditing of MENTOR’s M&E department, which conducts both monitoring of spraying, and post IRS evaluation.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Gender Analysis and MainstreamingRelevant gender characteristics and relationships in Syria: There is a cultural dominance of the male gender in the Syrian health profession. MENTOR has an equal opportunities policy and encourages the training of both genders equally. How this may affect proposed MENTOR activities: MENTOR has tailored IEC material to specifically target women. The benefits of targeting women are: (a) to encourage seeking treatment. The male head of household should also be encouraged to accompany female members into consultations (b) as a direct route to protect children, who are most vulnerable to infection and to the psycho-social effects of scarring. Reducing existing inequalities and avoiding new inequalities: Conspicuous facial scarring and disfigurement caused by CL causes stigmatization with profound psychosocial impact, marginalization in society and long term impoverishment. Women, children and adolescents are most susceptible to these effects. IEC/BCC will be designed to encourage permanent cultural change and gender equality in access to treatment. All IRS activity benefits whole households without discrimination. How MENTOR’s activities help to improve gender equality: MENTOR will try to influence through example and careful use of IEC/BCC. Mainstreaming is embedded in OCHA’s influential Humanitarian Response Plan, encompassing the activities of all health and prevention NGOs, and MENTOR will fully participate in this respect. MENTOR hires female members of paired IRS and LLIN teams, this also increases access to households where the male head of household is absent. - 2.The project cover as part of the protection risk analysis the negative impact on BNFs. The target population when access health care services through the medical component of the project will be less exposed to violence due to the close access afforded by the network of supported health facilities and mobile treatment units, which extend services directly to communities that cannot travel safely to static health facilities. People with disabilities that prevent them from physical access to more distance static health facilities also have improved access through the rotation of mobile units in some settings, and where security allows. Specific requirements for case manage for old people, people living with HIV/AIDS, people with learning difficulties and any other potentially marginalized parts of the population will be fully integrated in training of health workers, and during on site supervisions to enhance the protection mainstreaming aspect of the proposes activities.The beneficiaries of this program are equally male and female in balance, as all people are at equal risk of Leishmaniasis. However, the potential social stigma and social impacts of severe facial scarring are most felt amongst young girls and women, and every effort is made to maximize early treatment seeking and effective resolution of infection, to reduced permanent disfigurement.The very design of the prevention component of the project reduces insecurity as prevention workers are recruited locally, regardless of gender, ethnic origins and religious beliefs to provide protection within their own communities. Trained prevention workers are not imported from outside of the area, as this provokes suspicion, reducing trust and access. MENTOR ensures throughout the entire period covered by any award that safety of beneficiaries and staff recruited for the campaign is addressed in an inclusive manner, i.e. beneficiaries and recruited staff are informed and are given the opportunity to discuss safety and the reasoning behind safety decisions. MENTOR applies a mitigation strategy to ensure that neighboring sub-districts not targeted by the campaign are informed about proposed activities and the reasons for non-inclusion. MENTOR provides a thorough briefing to beneficiaries prior to the start of an IRS campaign
