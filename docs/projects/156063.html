Project Summary

This project is going to provide an emergency education assistance to 10000 beneficiaries including 9670 Syrian children(4891 boys and 4779 girls) in formal and non-formal education , 330 (190 males , 140 females) education personnel and supporting staff in non-formal education in 25 schools and 5400 (2700 caregivers & 2700 children) in 25 back to learning campaigns(BTL) in 25 camps (15 of these camps are registered in ISIMM in CCCM and 10 aren't) in Northwest Syria in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates through prevention of children from dropping out , returning children back to school and providing an immediate access to formal and non-formal education.The proposed project is going to improve the access of 9670 Syrian children(4891 boys and 4779 girls), in 25 schools to formal and non-formal education by preparing safe learning spaces ,providing school furniture, teaching/learning supplies, textbooks and curriculum B , alleviate the suffering of during winter through provision of winterization support(fuel – plastic sheets – heaters) , and provide remedial classes to the low achievers and the children at the risk of dropping out and provide catch-up classes to the children out of schools , in addition to improving the quality of education in the 25 camps by providing incentives to non-formal education teachers , provide light professional development package including training on (Child Protection – Classroom Management – Literacy & Numeracy) to 330 non-formal education teachers and supervisors and providing PSS materials and activities and recreational materials to 9670 children(4891 boys and 4779 girls) in 25 schools located in 25 camps in 9 different sub-districts in Idleb.

Needs

The response and current interventions are not meeting the actual increasing needs of education Northwest Syria and that makes absorbing new students is difficult because of the space, supplies and teachers who are under prepared to teach in a very difficult teaching environment. Multi-level and multi-aged learners, who suffer the stress of war, in the same classrooms make it difficult for teachers to teach effectively and for lower level students to grasp concepts especially those rejoined the schools after being out of schools and dropped out for years and for students at their grade level to be stimulated and progress.ACU issued a report in 22-11-2018 about the situation of schools in the camps including an assessment to 226 IDP camps distributed within eight camps clusters in Aleppo and Idleb governorates and here are some of the report key findings : 1- There are 72 schools in the assessed camps located within 62 IDP camps whereas there are no schools at all in the other 164 assessed camps ;2- The number of students in the assessed schools was 34,139 students, where 52% (17,666 students) of them were females. Furthermore, 10% of students were orphans; 3- The number of teachers at schools in camps was 1,377 teachers, where females represent 51% (699 female teachers) of the total teachers;4-The results show that 65% of schools at northern Syria camps are not equipped to receive children with special needs, and 94% of schools do not have spe¬cialists to deal with children with special needs;5-15% of students have difficulty in concentration, 14% of students suffer from low marks at school; 6-The need for textbooks topped the list of priorities for the assessed camp schools in the north by 41%. Secondly came the need for books and stationery by 22%, while the need for salaries for the school staff came by 18%, and the need for heating fuel came with 10%;Syria Relief carried out an assessment during November 2018 in 25 schools in 25 camps located in 9 sub-districts in Idleb and Aleppo 2018 and here are the key findings of the assessment :1- 9670 (4891 boys and 4779 females ))were enrolled in the 25 assessed schools at the beginning of this academic year in 25 camps , 8,204 students of them are attending the schools now (83%) and 1,466 students dropped out of schools (17%) are dropped out of schools2-898 (boys & 450 girls) are children with disabilities and orphans in the 25 schools 3- There are 330 (190 males & 140 females)teachers and education staff in the 25 schools working voluntarily since more than one year.4- There are 195 classrooms in the 25 schools5- The availability of text books of formal education is 35% and the availability of supporting curriculum is 7% in the 25 assessed schools .6- The current conditions of wash facilities in schools is ready with 51% and still need light repair to meet the needs of schools .7- The desks of children in the schools are available in the percentage 62 % and still need 38% to meet the actual needs .8- 17 schools out of 25 do not have teaching materials whereas 13 schools have insufficient supplies to support learning and demonstrations in the classroom.9- All the students in the 25 schools didn’t receive any education supplies since one year. 10- Most of the schools did not receive heating fuel and lack heaters .11- All the schools need recreational materials for the teachers and for the students .12- 55% of teachers and education staff received various training previously and 60% of them didn’t get any training's 13. All the assessed schools lack PSS activities and materials .

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct a security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education. If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; b) Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessary; c) Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not. • Communication with donors to accept flexibility Protection Risk/Threat 2: Attacks on schools Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating Measure:Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff Protection Risk/Threat 3: Risk of long distance and young children walking alone. Children may get lost, be attacked or lose interest in learning Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating Measure: Establish ECCE within the neighbourhoods whereby children will be dropped to the center by their parents. Protection Risk/Threat : Risk of children dropping out of formal education to attend SLP centres Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program Protection Risk/Threat :Protection risks of mixing different age groups in the same centre/class Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Create separate times of attendance for each age group. Protection Risk/Threat : Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. Likelihood: Low Impact: Low Mitigating Measure:The project will benefit all members of the community and will always seek prior agreement and active involvement of the traditional leadership structure. Mitigating Measure: Programme activities which engage women will be endorsed by community and religious leaders to gain support from male community members to reduce the likelihood of resistance to these activities. Protection Risk/Threat : Increased risk as some locations do not accept Curriculum B materials with MoE logos. This may pose danger to the children and the service deliverer Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Identify locations that don’t accept SLP materials with MoE logos and print the ones without logos. Liaise with local partners and use their volunteers to deliver the services of SLP in HTR (Hard to Reach) areas.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1 : Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas. Changes could increase the risks faced, displace or restrict movements of the population, local partners and others relevant to the project Mitigation Measures: 1- Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation. 2- Ensure flexible context-informed approaches and mechanisms to deliver the project outputs/outcomes, so that the projects activities remain relevant and can be implemented in a changing context3-Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the projects beneficiaries and sufficient resources to enact those plans. Commit partners to ensure a minimum duty of care to their staff including a right of refusal.4-Ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security, including mine risk education.5-As needed/possible, minimise the distance travelled to/from learning spaces and the total number of students at any given centre by diversifying the locations where learning is carried out and keeping classroom sizes small, etc.6- Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners, civil authorities and relevant security and access stakeholders.7-All critical documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Noting that at times soft copies or computers may also need to be destroyed, partners will be responsible for frequently sending copies of critical documents to a safe location for archiving.Risk 2 : The perceived or real risk of the bombardment of schools, mines, cross-fire and kidnapping, etc. could make the targeted population deprioritize sending their children to learning spaces or schoolsMitigation 1: In consultation with the community, partners will take into account risks to students, teachers and education personnel when designing their projects and work towards minimising risks.Mitigation 2: Key messages for back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns will take into account the concerns of parents and other community members.
