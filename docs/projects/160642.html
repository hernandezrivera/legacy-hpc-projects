Project Summary

Meet the lifesaving food requirements of people in South Sudan who are facing food
security crises, including those living in POCs, those otherwise affected by conflict and/or
seasonal food insecurity and refugees.
WFP introduces GFD+ as a modification to GFD as a means for improving emergency food assistance, such that the people we serve are better equipped to fight hunger and improve nutrition. GFD+ encourages the participation of beneficiaries in activities such as trainings for skills development or light public works in conjunction with the receipt of GFD. GFD+ seeks to shift beneficiary mindsets towards building their own capacity to become less dependent on external aid and more self-reliant, while increasing their individual and community resilience to shocks.

1 - Needs

Approxmately 6.35 million South Sudanese women, men and children were extimated to be food insecure during the lean season in 2019. Between January and April 2020, some 4.3 million are projected to be in IPC phase 3, 1.2 million in IPC 4 and 0 in IPC 5. In addition, some 200,000 IDPs people in PoC sites and 300,000 refugees are expected to not have enough food or access to livelihood. Between January and April 2020, 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Former Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000. However, the severity of food insecurity us expected to lessen slightly in 2020. This slight improvement is attributed to reduced insecurity after the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, which has led to better access to livelihoods and markets. Additionally, humanitarian access has improved during 2019, further contributing to the increased access to food, nutrition and livelihood support, as well as service delivery. However, acute food insecurity persists, driven by localized conflicts, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, poor macro-economic conditions and sub-optimal market functionality.

2 - Activities

WFP plans to provide nutrition-sensitive food assistance to crisis-affected populationscomprising of IDPs, returnees and host communities. It will provide food or CBTs to crisis affectedmen, women, boys and girls in counties classified by the IPC as phase 5 –catastrophe/famine; phase 4 – emergency; or phase 3 – crisis. Rations will be seasonal oryear-round depending on the severity of food insecurity. WFP will improve health andnutrition practices through messages that reinforce positive roles of men and women. Theother main activity will be the provision of food and nutrition assistance to refugees. WFPwill provide year-round nutrition-sensitive food assistance through general fooddistributions or CBTs to up to 300,000 people in refugee camps. It will ensure thatnutrition-sensitive messaging accompanies general distributions and is disseminated bycooperating partners, and that persons with disabilities and other vulnerable people areaccommodated in dignified, safe ways. WFP will provide nutrtion sensitive foodassistance to both IDPs and host communities living in hard to reach areas throughthe Integrated Rapid Response Mechanisms. This involves the WFP led United NationsHumanitarian Air Service to enable humanitarian workers to reach most areas. The serviceprovides regularly scheduled flights to 50 destinations each week, with special flights forIRRM teams, special visits and emergency evacuations.
