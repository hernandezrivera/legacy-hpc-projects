Project Summary

CAO propose to target conflict-affected and GBV survivors, Host community, IDPs and returnees living in Ayod and Pigi /Canal. The purpose of this project is to prevent protection risk and provide quality service in a timely manner to women, girls, men, and boys in the two Counties cutting across 8 params. The target population is 12,000, (7000 Women, 3000 Girls, 1000 Boys, and 1000 Men. The project will run for a period of 12 months, with a budget of USD 300,000. This is a continuation of a series of projects CAO has been implementing including GBV in emergency in Ayod and Child protection in Pigi/Canal for the last three years and is also a build-up following the lessons learned over time.CAO will strengthen community-based structures that have been developed together with other protection partners and update the referral pathway. The other activities to be conducted include: case management , WGFS programming ,creating awareness in the community on the effects of GBV to the whole community to build resilience and mitigate GBV risks and direct effects it causes on the survivors, training men and women in the community on PFA/PSS, GBV principle guidelines, training more frontline workers on case management ,PFA and PSS , directly support survivors of GBV through direct support depending on their needs, coordinate with other actors to update referral pathways and conduct appropriate integration and referrals to Health, Nutrition, WASH, Child Protection and Education sectors. In addition, CAO will Conduct comprehensive case management to the women and girls of GBV survivors, establish WGFS. Among the direct beneficiaries will be men, women, girls, and boys.
CAO maintain strong presence in canal/Pigi and Ayod has it been implementing protection project on the target counties for the last 4 years with support from UNICEF. CAO will scale up its operation especially in the hard to reach areas of the locations where the caseload is high.

1 - Needs

Protection is among the top priority concerns of the HNO 2020. According to DRC rapid needs assessment report which was conducted in July 2019, there is an alarming and acute need for protection, especially SGBV, This report indicated that over 13,222 people live in Pagil Payam and additional 18,000 are IDPs. The emergence of a large number of vulnerable women-headed households within conservative and patriarchal communities has heightened the acute exposure of women and children to protection threats, especially GBV cases such as sexual violence, SEA, Intimate partner violence, economic violence, forced marriage and harmful traditional practices like blood compensation for the girl child. In addition to S-GBV perpetrated within the community, respondents of the DRC assessment reported that women and girls had been attacked on routes from Malakal, Bentiu to Pagiel and raped, items looted and young girls abducted. IOM DTM report of September 2019 indicates that there are more returnees expected to arrive in pigi arriving from Bentiu. ideally, returnee women and girls are dependent on the community for essential resources which causes mental distress. women and girls fear to report rape cases due to the weak judicial systems. This puts them in a position of increased vulnerability in an environment where the only access to justice is via community-based justice adjudicated by the male boma chief or administrator who often does not favor women and girls. Community Action Organization (CAO) also conducted a needs assessment in Ayod in the month of June 2019 from where it has been implementing GBV project. Our current presence covers Five Payams of Jiech, Mogok, Karmun, Pajeck and Gorwai in Ayod with GBV, and Pigi/Canal with Child Protection in Jonglei State. The report further says that the entire population of women, girls, boys and men are affected by civil war, inter-clan and revenge killings and that much of the work is done by women and girls which include fetching water and collecting firewood from bushes that put the safety of these girls and women at high risk of sexual violence . Boys and male youth have been abducted in both Counties and forced to Join rebel militia groups further increasing the stress levels of families of the abducted children. According to the CITISAMM report of October 201 on cantonment sites, there is a need to mitigate GBV risks of women and girls living near these sites through proper coordination of partners to target community leaders on GBV mitigation strategies. There is a need for community GBV awareness-raising to women, girls, boys, and men on behavior change in support of their female counterparts. GBV services are covers fewer payams in pigi county, CAO will increase access to GBV services in the two locations targeting men, boys, girls and women who are returnees, IDPs and host communities. The assessment report recommends that among the items that are supposed to be distributed, would include dignity kits, hygiene materials, there is, therefore, a need to coordinate with WASH partners on ground for an integrated GBV/WASH response.

2 - Activities

CAO intend to conduct the following activities: Activity 1. Provide comprehensive case management to GBV survivors/individual PSS and follow up for women and girls GBV survivors using appropriate and ethical guidelines in women and girls’ friendly spaces in Ayod and Pigi/Canal Activity 2. CAO will conduct awareness on the availability of GBV services and use of referral pathway in coordination with other sectoral partners in WASH , FSL and Education. CIDO will plan the awareness meetings with Health, WASH, Education, Nutrition and FSL partners to integrate GBV prevention in sectoral activities using GBV guiding principles and mainstreaming guidelines in Ayod and Pigi/canal. Activity 4. Establishment of two women and Girls' friendly space in Ayod and four in Pigi/Canal to cover more beneficiaries in both project locations with GBV services such as case management, PSS livelihood referrals and provision of dignity kits. Activity 5. Strengthen the capacity of static service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in Ayod and Pigi/Canal through training on case management and PSS. Activity 6. Conducting training of male and female front-line service providers on protection risks mitigation and early warning systems. Activity 7. Conduct monthly community dialogue forums for EMAP with male community leaders, local authority and community-based protection committees to strengthen protection mechanisms and mitigate GBV risks in Ayod and Pigi/Canal. Activity 8, CAO will provide legal service and awareness assistance to the GBV survivors. In strengthening this activity, CAO will train the police and the justice authorities, including the traditional justice mechanism.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, CAO works with local community systems in the locations of Canal/Pigi and Ayod. In line ministries as well as local community structures have been instrumental in the success of our projects. These include Protection Committees and voluntary groups amongst others. CAO will build on existing structures rather than creating new ones and forging synergies with other sectors.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives and frameworks on emergency and resilience responses and is in line with the Republic of South Sudan Resilience Strategy, South Sudan operational strategy on protection and humanitarian response plan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable. CAO implements protection interventions in the locations targeted and has taken into consideration the protection needs and environment of the locations. Standard IASC guidelines on protection will be enforced, including issues related to the safety of stakeholders, do no harm, accountability to affected populations, child protection and mitigations against GBV and PSEA. Security guidelines provided by leads and coordination forums will be adhered to while ensuring project activities go on unhindered.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes sustainability/handover through the existing community structures. CAO is a community-based organization and maintains presence in the locations targeted even at project closure. Handover is planned from the onset of the project, working with community structures and inline ministries to ensure the capacity of these is built to continue follow up of activities at project closure. Self-replenishing structures for inputs such as hand pump spare parts, soap, NFIs will be strengthened to ensure continuity and sustainability.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project has created a feedback and complaints mechanism. This followed the recent meeting with the small group focused discussion, group discussions and CAO’s team realized that some of the beneficiaries did not want to be known as having complaints. This then forced us to come up with an anonymous way of reporting in such a way that the beneficiaries do not have to indicate their names. However, this only applies to people who can write. We have staged a complaints' box outside our compound for this. We are still looking into ways of having illiterate people report their complaints.
