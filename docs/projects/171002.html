Project Summary

The proposed 12-month project seeks to increase access to quality education for 4230 crisis-affected boys and girls from displaced and poor households in Galmudug, Banadir and Gedo regions of Somalia and also to ensure they are retained in their respective schools. Targeted locations and livelihoods have been adversely affected by flooding, locust, drought and currently the economic impact of COVID 19. Therefore they do not have the income to meet basic needs such as food, medication, learning materials and hygiene needs of adolescent girls, which in turn affects enrollment and retention in education. Among children living with various forms of disabilities such as learning disabilities and physical disability which require teachers with skills of handling them and facilities meeting their unique needs. Learning centres are dilapidated with no gender, age and PWD friendly sanitation and hygiene facilities exposing students to AWDS and spread of COVID 19.
The project has integrated the two Education Cluster 2021 strategic approaches of increasing access to education and enhancing retention in schools in the design of this project. Of the targeted children 2130 are boys and 2100 are girls. The project will target 423 children with various forms of disabilities and 1510 children who are displaced. To increase accessibility to quality education for the displaced and host community children, with an emphasis on girls and children living with a disability, the project will rehabilitate 3 learning spaces in 3 targeted schools in Banadir. All of these structures will be ECDs and all centres will be equipped with facilities such as ramps, for instance, to ensure that disabled children can access the classes. To ensure general and proper hygiene is maintained in 3 Banadir schools by the children and the school management for prevention of AWD and COVID 19, 3 bakards and 3 latrines fitted with handwashing facilities will be constructed. These facilities are designed to be age, disability and gender-sensitive. To ensure the proper management of these assets for longevity, 8 CECs will be trained on school safety, with topics including hygiene promotion for AWD and COVID 19 prevention and control, schools. Maintenance and management of learning spaces. NAPAD will work closely with the Ministry of Education to equip teachers with skills and information required on basic pedagogical approaches, inclusive education, protection and emotional learning psychosocial support COVID 19 prevention and control. The Ministry will also play a key role in periodic and planned monitoring and supervision of the performance of teachers for quality assurance. These visits will be vital to ensure that children with disability have equal access to education, for instance, ensuring those who have learning disabilities such as dyslexia, dyscalculia and ADHD to mention but a few are identified and supported accordingly. To ensure that teachers and headteachers are engaged and retained in delivery of quality services, they will be facilitated with monthly incentives of $90-$100for 12 months. The project will facilitate the MoE to conduct awareness sessions for 75 teachers (52M and 23F) on the teachers' code of conduct (TCOC) and ensure they sign and adhere to it. 4230 students in 8 schools in the target regions will receive monthly food vouchers worth $8 for 12 months. Foods purchased with these vouchers will contribute to household food and other basic need and encourage parents to send their children to school during the crisis period. It is also assumed that during this time parents and guardian will have an opportunity to restore their assets and improve their standards of living of their households. 4230 students will benefit from the provision of learning and teaching materials. To improve attendance and retention rates of adolescent girls the constructed latrines are designed to be lockable from inside and they are gender-sensitive.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will support children with a voucher that will provide the beneficiaries with the power of choice on the purchase of the preferred food and at what quantities at a time. The unconditional support is also safe and does not present any risks to the beneficiaries, unlike in-kind support. The redeeming of these vouchers will also help in strengthening local markets as the beneficiaries will purchase the preferred foods from local suppliers. This will not only avoid disruption of the food markets that may be occasioned by in-kind food supplies from outside but will also inject cash in the local markets. This approach was selected as it benefits the whole family and the vulnerable families will be encouraged and obliged to ensure their child attends school consistently. The voucher is also physically manageable and non-disruptive and the child will be able to deliver it to the household head as opposed to carrying in-kind support that is heavy and affects their security.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

NAPAD currently have staff working and residing in the targeted project districts who continuously provide information on the local markets during the design of the project. The staff monitored the trends in the availability of different essential foods in the local markets and the prices. Additionally, they gathered information on the quality of different foodstuffs in the market. Observations in the target district markets are that the markets have experienced minimal fluctuations despite the movement restrictions brought by COVID 19 outbreak. Additionally, NAPAD continuously read the WFP Joint Market and Supply Chain Update. The generally stable markets informed the choice of support in the project.

Please provide further explanation

NAPAD will engage Local administration, Ministry of Education, Community Education leadership and local IDP and Host community leaders who are key ensuring project ownership and security of investments of the project and staff. NAPAD will mobilize these stakeholders to explain the project objective and ensure they participate in identification of project beneficiaries, sites and resources transparently and accurately. This will reduce potential conflict among the clans, IDPs, and host communities. The local leadership is essential for the sustainability of the project beyond the implementation period as they will be tasked with the management and operation of all assets rehabilitated and established. After each project is commissioned, an MOU stipulating ownership, management and maintenance roles of each asset will be signed between all local elders, local administration and community groups. The proposed project is linked to the objectives of MOECHE ESSP 2018-2020 of building resilience and early childhood care and Education (ECCE) and primary school education in emergency. It also linked to Education Sector Strategic Plan with an emphasis on implementing the objectives of Education in Emergency response and developing the resilience of the education sector to retain and accommodate more displaced and non-displaced children of school-going age.
