Project Summary

“Bridging Horizons" is a transformative initiative designed to address the educational and vocational gaps faced by out-of-school boys and girls in conflict-affected communities in Borno and Adamawa states. Recognizing the unique challenges these youth encounter, including limited access to formal education and vocational opportunities, this project integrates life skills and vocational training with a special emphasis on increasing school enrollment for children especially girls. By combining education and practical skills development, the project seeks to empower youth to overcome the barriers posed by the Boko Haram insurgency, fostering resilience and creating a pathway towards a brighter future.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) for 2023 underscores the necessity for education services or assistance for 1.3 million individuals. Around 959,642 school-age children encounter limited access to learning, with 42 percent of displaced children not attending school, according to the DTM 2022 report. The impact of conflict, combined with cultural norms and financial challenges, disproportionately affects school-aged girls, placing them at an elevated risk of child marriage and dropout. Children with disabilities also face disadvantages, as only 15 percent of non-specialized schools report additional provisions for their needs, despite 67 percent having at least one child with special needs.The crisis is compounded by infrastructure deficiencies, as only 46 percent of schools have access to safe drinking water, lack functional handwashing facilities or soap, and accommodate an average of 264 students per latrine. Only 6 percent of schools in the BAY states have adapted their premises for children with physical disabilities. The BAY states crisis hampers displaced children's education, with 31 percent of schools struggling to enroll students. Conflict has damaged 1,400 schools, impacting access and learning materials. Furniture shortages (47 percent in Borno) and financial constraints hinder attendance.In 2022, the volatile humanitarian situation is expected to worsen unmet needs for children, youth, families, teachers, and communities. Insecurity, internal displacement, and camp closures heighten vulnerabilities and deplete coping mechanisms. Communal clashes and flooding disrupt learning, with schools repurposed for non-educational use. Severe flooding in Adamawa State during the 2022 rainy season led to 120 schools being used as shelters, preventing 40,000 students from resuming the academic year. Windstorms and flooding are anticipated in 2023, further impacting school access.Enrollment of girls tends to decrease compared to boys at all levels, while children from lower-income families often cannot afford education-related costs. Concerning the enhancement of enrollment and attendance, caretakers of out-of-school children express a preference for material and financial support, specifically seeking assistance such as uniforms, learning materials, payment of school fees, and provision of snacks or food. Additionally, survey participants emphasize a notable demand for technical and vocational training, both for themselves and their older children or youth, particularly those who may have missed formal schooling.

2 - Activities

The activities to be carried out will include but not limited to the following, carry out assessment to know the different types of vocational skills needed and available for trainees, school enrollment for out of school boys and girls including those with disabilities, provision of preferred vocational skills to selected out-of-school boy and girls that will span for a period of 6 months, provision of conditional cash to caregiver and parents of out-of-school boy and girls to facilitate their enrollment in schools, the provision of enrollment kits, the procurement and distribution of start up kits to the children enrolled for vocational training upon graduation, carry out back to school campaigns and provide capacity strengthening to school base management systems to enhance their capacities in dealing with children in conflict and post conflict situations

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, particularly female-headed households, and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for women and girls, particularly female-headed households and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program and staff behavior. Sirri Care will also make provision for a complaint booth where issues of GBV and SEA can be reported. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project.
