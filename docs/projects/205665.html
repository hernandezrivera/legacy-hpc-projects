Project Summary

In July 2023, people’s humanitarian needs in South Sudan continue to increase, driven by compounded shocks triggered by conflict, food insecurity, displacement, disease outbreaks, climate shock & economic decline (OCHA Humanitarian Snapshot July 2023). Millions have been displaced internally and externally. In July 2020, the country received large amounts of rainfall that caused severe flooding in many parts of the country leading to the destruction of farmland and crops, increased risk of water-borne diseases, flooding, and destruction of schools and other public utilities. Sub national conflicts continue to displace more people causing death, looting, and destruction of properties. The effects of flooding & inter-ethnic conflicts affected education sector harder, destroyed school infrastructure, and the collapse of livelihoods increasing the vulnerability of parents, teachers, and children. These counties have been receiving a large influx of over 20,000 returnees from the Sudan and Ethiopia (UNICEFHumanitarianSituationReport2023).
In line with 2024 HRP education cluster objectives, this project will support the resumption of learning, provide safe protective learning spaces, improve learning conditions by supporting the learners and teachers with education materials, build the capacity of teachers and the school community to support safe learning, and improve coping mechanisms and resilience. The project directly targets 6,906 learners in the 4 most-affected counties in Jonglei & Upper Nile states. The establishment of classrooms will provide a safe and protective environment for learners. This will launch resumption of learning in the project targeted areas and protect children from a harmful associations like drug abuse, early marriages, and recruitment to armed groups. The establishment of WASH facilities including hand washing will provide a safe environment from water-borne diseases. Integrated capacity building of teachers in pedagogy, PSS, protection/GBV would help them to give lifesaving skills support to learners so that they can be able to protect themselves and improve resilience by the youths. The SMC/PTAs would be trained in the management of schools in emergencies to support education. Education supplies provision will support a faster resumption of learning in schools.
This intervention intends to cover 12 primary, 4 secondary schools & 4 ALP centers in Ayod, Pigi, Longochuk, and Maiwut Counties to provide a quality emergency learning environment to the affected IDPs, Returnees, and host community children.
UNCDR proposes to target 2,728 Girls, 2,678 Boys, 800 Women & 700 Men as direct beneficiaries among the affected communities of IDPs, returnees & host communities ’while 15% of beneficiaries are children with disabilities who have limited or no access to education services.
In line with Education Cluster strategy, the project will establish temporary learning spaces(TLS) and classrooms, establish gender-segregated latrines with water points and hygiene promotion, support school feeding programs, schools supplies, and hygiene kits, recreational materials, train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills, life skills to improve teaching-learning, train teachers and learners on PSS, Train teachers and SMC/PTA on protection/GBV, health, nutrition and governance. UNCDR is committed to AAP, encourages timely sharing of information, consultation & dialogue to the affected people to improve the quality and efficacy of humanitarian response. UNCDR further will roll out strong an integrated response modality, seeking to implement jointly Education, Health, FSL and Protection projects.
UNCDR enjoys a static presence with very good experience and good relations with local education officials and other education stakeholders as well as having implemented multi-sectoral programs integrated within the education institutions in targeted counties. UNCDR has technical educationists with experience in implementing EiE programs.

1. Needs

The estimated 3.4 million girls and boys between 3-17 years of age as in need of education. The school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures in 2020 have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education. According to the HNO report released in 2023 Children and youth, aged between 3 to 17, represent 44% of South Sudan’s population. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic crisis, instability, displacement, and widespread flooding. Unfortunately, the capacity of the MoGEI to respond to the huge needs of children remains limited. Displaced people, refugees, and returnees are identified as vulnerable groups and the education needs of children in these groups are particularly dire in Upper Nile and Warrap where a high percentage of children do not have access to education. The closure of schools and all learning institutions followed by COVID-19 as well as the severe floods continues to impart devastating effects on the education of very many children including IDPs, returnees, and host community in the counties of Ayod, Pigi, Longochuk, and Maiwut has jeopardized critical gains made over the years. This was followed by the subsequent exposure to forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. Education needs remain very high in Jonglei states to spontaneous IDPs and returnees. The 3rd phase of severe flooding in 2022 significantly impacted education infrastructure, particularly in Ayod, Pigi, Longochuk and Maiwut counties which are among the priority location targeted by this intervention. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This could have affected the second phase of reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI. According to the same report, the drivers of this crisis is food insecurity due to destruction of farmland and harvests, death of livestock and lack of access to markets leading to increased food prices, communal conflicts that have led to displacements, majorly Ayod and Pigi with poor access to food which led to increased transportation of food commodities to the markets hence increased food prices and scaled-down education and food security interventions and poor WASH facilities hence unsafe water consumption by the households and inadequate water storage containers that lead to inadequate water supply and this exposes children to frequent diarrhea diseases. In line with 2023 HRP and the MoGEI’s five-Year Strategic Plan 2017-2022. Protracted conflict and climate change resulted in record challenges to education, including school closures, school occupation by displaced people and increased number of out-of-school children, estimated at 2.8 million (SS-HNO-2023). These challenges worsened the already low learning outcomes and difficulties keeping children in school, exposing them to recruitment into armed groups, early marriage and child labour. In coordination with the MoGEI and Education Cluster, UNCDR will provide essential EiE services to the children affected by the crises (IDPs, returnees and host communities) to transition back to school and reduce their risk of dropout. UNCDR is committed to address using inter-sectorial approaches to meet the needs and demands of school children, teachers and school communities, through the provision of child protection and referral mechanisms, WASH facilities in school feeding and health and nutrition intervention in schools. UNCDR will make use of its long-term operational and static presence in Jonglei and Upper Nile states with a field office in 4 counties where the organization is implementing UNICEF, WFP, UNFPA and FAO projects. In line with Education cluster objectives and strategies, UNCDR will provide access to safe and protective education services, inclusive education services and enhance teachers' capacity to deliver EiE services in project locations.

2. Project activities

UNCDR proposed activities: •Establish 15 learning spaces in line with INEE’s Minimum Standards •rehabilitate 10 learning spaces in line with INEE’s Minimum Standards •rehabilitate 12 gender sensitive latrines in line with INEE’s and sphere standards •enroll 1,000 (520 women and 480 men) in ALP learning centers in Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Longochuk, and Maiwut counties •enroll 6,906 (3,500 girls, and 3,406 boys) learners – Pre-primary, Primary and Secondary •enroll 1,200 (500 female and 700 male) learners – Life Skills for youth •distribute dignity kits to 1,250 adolescent girls •conduct awareness raising on key issues for the girl child and her transition to post –secondary education •distribute assistive devices to 750 (360 girls and 390 boys) children with disabilities •establish reporting (toll free lines, suggest boxes, designated focal-points etc.) across 4 project counties •conduct training of 120 (40 female and 80 male) teachers and staff on child safeguarding and GBV risk mitigation, referral mechanism •provide school meal to 6,906 children •train 120 teachers on PSS •provide PSS service to 7,000 learners •distribute ECD kits to school children in 4 Centres •distribute Recreational Kits to children in 12 primary and 4 secondary schools •distribute teachers Kits to teachers in 15 schools •train 120 teachers on EIE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills •support 120 volunteer teachers with financial incentives •conduct training for 160 (68 female and 92 male) SMC/PTA members and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DDR).
