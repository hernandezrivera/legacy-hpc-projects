Project Summary

The project aims at targeting the most vulnerable people in 3 camps in Idleb governorate to ensure the sustainability of service provision through a holistic approach to meet the urgent need of 12363 IDPs (2268 men, 2918 women, 3317 boys, 3860 girls).
The project will be implemented by Alseeraj for development and healthcare - SDH, through the intervention, SDH will use its resources and previous experience in the distribution of hygiene kits, hygiene promotion campaigns, rehabilitation of existing latrines, establishment of water network, sewer network and provision of clean drinking water, solid waste management, construction of latrines, and bathing facilities.
The project will include one round of distribution hygiene kits and one round of complimentary hygiene items in respond to COVID-19 for 2139 families to maintain the level of personal hygiene among IDPs living in those camps. Hygiene kits contents are designed to meet the need of the IDPs and the WASH cluster standards.
Moreover, this project will cover the solid waste management activities on daily basis in 3 camps which includes the distribution of 246 (100 L) solid waste container across targeted camps. The collected waste will be dumped in a formal dumping sites in Sarmada sub-district.
The project will provide safe water for drinking and personal hygiene to 12363 IDPs in 3 camps by water trucking for the first two months of the project, meanwhile SDH will be working on a more sustainable solution by building a water network within the camps and provide the water from a nearby water source (Public well), SDH will also build a central water tank nearby the targeted camps to ensure the continuous flow of water. SDH will ensure the water quality by chlorinating the water at source in addition to water quality testing in the main tank and at household level.
The project will ensure that each person receives 40 L/Day of safe water as per the latest WASH cluster recommendation. SDH will pay special attention to the water quality, and make sure that water is chlorinated, and it meets the WHO standards (0.2 and 0.5 mg/l FRC) before distribution and at the household level. This activity will include installing a water tabs which will be used during the water trucking phase, and will be connected to the water network later.
the project will also cover the construction of 152 latrine blocks (456 cells) to ensure IDPs have safe access to sanitation facilities while employing the latest recommendation by the WASH cluster (25 people per latrine). the latrines design will take special consideration of protection issues as it is provided with lighting system working on solar panels to ensure safe access during the dark hours. Out of the constructed latrines 4 cells will be dedicated to cover the needs of 66 people with special needs in the targeted camps. The project will also conduct maintenance for the 40 existing latrine cells in the 3 camps. The constructed latrines will be connected to the nearby main sewage system to avoid constructing of septic tanks and for safe disposal of sewage waste.
152 bathing facilities will be constructed as well taking into consideration the gender sensitivities by separating the facilities for men and women. The bathing cells will be part of the sanitation facilities, in which each block will contain 3 toilets and one shower. (detailed sketch will be attached to the proposal.
In case of any probable new displacement waves to targeted camps, SDH will report to WASH Cluster about the deviation in number of targeted beneficiaries reached in each site of implementation.

4 - Needs

SDH conducted NA and it was conducted as follow: - Technical assessment: This NA was based on the direct observation of technical staff as a result of a field visit to each of the targeted 3 camps, in addition to interviews with targeted camps’ managers. *Key findings: -26 functional public water tanks with 2 m3 capacity in the 3 camps. -40 latrine cells are available in 3 camps which need full maintenance -3 camps are close to a sewage system -A public water source (well) is approximately 3 KM far from the 3 camps. -No bathing facilities were found in the 3 camps. -No SWM service is provided in the camps. Lack of water tanks available in those camps which make the process of storage water is difficult. Camps not served through water networks are also severely strained by cost of water, families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 25% and in some cases as much as 40% of their income on water (compared to 10% on average in rest of the country according to HNO21). SDH completed a rapid sanitation assessment in targeted camps and identified a critical need for rehabilitation and installing of latrines And sewage network to ensure the sustainability of services in 3 targeted camps. Because of the limited, unavailability and high cost of HK, hygiene practices for IDPs have decreased which lead to increasing in spread the diseases (Alburj, Atshana and Hamood): (M:2268, W:2918, B: 3317, G:3860) =12363 IDPs, (Including 66 PWDs)

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk High (Impact) (High) Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. (Mitigating) Promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff. Risk Medium (Impact) (High) Distribution of expired unhealthy items. (Mitigating Measure) Establish and monitor expiry date of supplies prepositioned in warehouse, ensure relevant storage provision and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. Strengthen end user monitoring. Risk high (Impact) (Medium) Sale or exchange of NFI kits at lesser value. (Mitigating) Consult community throughout programme cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions. Risk high (Impact) (low) Movements of water trucks in congested, camp like settings (Mitigating Measure) Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse during the WASH facilitities: All SDH staff are receiving regular training on the code of conduct and PSEA policy and theysigned on these policies, The contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration with a speci¦c clause on PSEA with an emphasis on mitigation measures and sanctions in case of abuse by an employee, Beneficiaries will receive informationon the CRM and the expected behavior of SDH staff and its contractors with an emphasis on how to report any SEA incidents. A woman staff is present during all visits to women and child led-households. SDH will also put an email and phone number to take any suggestions and solve any complaints from the beneficiaries. The creation of a complaints mechanism is vital to ensuring clear accountability towards the local population as well as it allows for a safe place for beneficiaries to provide feedback on the project, in general, All feedbacks will be archived in a database andwill be analyzed in the evaluation and lessons learned stage. A complaint box will be made available in a key center in the targeted areas, as well as the provision of an email address. Once a complaint or feedback on a form is received, the issue must bedealt with within 15 days of the reception. The information above will be disseminated through the field visits conducted by the monitoring team. In SDH as an active member within PSEA Network, All of the core staff trained on PSEA
