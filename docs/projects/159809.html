Project Summary

According to Somalia response plan (2019), humanitarian situation in the last six months demonstrates the continued unpredictable and volatile context in Somalia . While the unexpectedly plentiful Gu rain season (April-June 2018) led to an overall improvement in the food security outlook country-wide, it also brought severe flooding across vast areas of southern and central Somalia. In May 2018, cyclone Sagar devastated parts of Somaliland and Puntland, compounding humanitarian needs generated by the dispute in Sool and Sanaag.The current humanitarian situation remains precarious in Somalia due to repeated climate shocks, continued conflict and violence.Without sustained humanitarian support,most of the poor households including IDPs and returnees might face very poor food security outcomes in the coming months.
Failed /poor 2019 Gu’ (April-June) rains which followed poor 2018 Deyr (October-December) rains and of harsh weather conditions during the dry Jilaal season (January-March). Water scarcity, widespread crop failure and accelerated decline in livestock productivity have seen communities slide into acute food insecurity or worse. Consequently, the number of people in acute food insecurity (IPC 3 and 4) has spiked by ten per cent, to more than 1.7 million in 2019. The number is expected to increase if the prevailing dry conditions persist and the Deyr rains perform below average.
The October-December 2019 Deyr rains, which are forecast to be average, are expected to usher in some food security improvements. In pastoral areas of greatest concern, livestock births are anticipated to increase herd sizes, enabling poor households to engage in some distressed sales of livestock. According to FSNAU and FEWSNET,An estimated 2.2 million people are expected to experience Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes through September and are urgently in need of humanitarian food assistance and interventions to prevent high levels of acute malnutrition. This number could increase as the dry season progresses. Should the onset of the forecast average Deyr rains be delayed by 20-30 days, agropastoral households in South/Central would experience a third consecutive season of below-average production due to the shortened crop growing period. This would also extend the lean season to January, increase cereal prices to above average, and reduce agriculture labor income. Herd sizes would likely be further depleted through distressed sales and low productivity and reproduction. Emergency (IPC Phase 4) would be sustained in agropastoral of Hiiraan and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) would be widespread in the country.The exception is Guban Pastoral livelihood zone, where herd sizes are lowest and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) is likely to persist.This means that the food security outcomes ,are unlikely to improve until mid 2020.
World Vision is proposing a an integrated food and livelihood security project which will cushion the poor households against sliding into acute food security and destitution while also building a stronger foundation for resilience building.

Project Activities

1.Bush and invasive plants clearing through cash for work to open up farm and grazing lands for increased production. Prosopis and other noxious weeds have invaded huge tracts of productive land choking the land and making it non productive.Bush clearing along the rivers and communal grasslands will open up the land for productive use as well as create livelihoods from the cleared plant materials.In stable seasons ,the communities can also be engaged to make charcoal and livestock feed from the cleared plant material.2.Agricultural Seeds and Tools distribution-To the farming communities , World Vision will seek to Promote climate smart agricultural initiatives such as encouraging adaptation of drought resistant , early maturing crop varieties, including nutrition sensitive agricultural practices.This will be combined with series of trainings to communities on good agricultural practices as well as providing necessary support for agricultural inputs(seeds and tools)3.Soil and water conservation structures in range and croplands-During emergency response/relief programing WV supports community with unconditional and conditional cash transfers. In the conditional cash transfer community participate in soil and water conservation structure construction, plant trees (biological conservation), construct water catchments, and re-green range land. These all investment contribute positively in the restoration of land while at the same time addressing the basic need of people for survival-food .While the main program component attempts to restore the natural environment of the area by undertaking natural resource management interventions,this intervention has three overarching objective; 1) create temporary employment, 2) address the food need of the community, and 3) restore/rehabilitate the degraded land. 4.Support land tillage with tractor hours -World Vision proposes to support farming communities with hired tractor services to till land.5.Mass vaccination and treatment for Livestock against endemic and disease outbreaks6.Rangeland reseeding and support to communal fodder production and conservation-Occasionally the rangelands in Somalia are so denuded with all the seed banks washed away. In such conditions, the rangelands left on their own will not rejuvenate. If the best forage plants on a range are destroyed, it takes a long time for recovery, even if grazing animals are completely removed. Deliberate interventions may, in such circumstance, be necessary to restart range and hasten the process by artificially seeding the range.This will be done through carefully selecting adaptable and local grass seeds,and where possible use women and vulnerable people to collect and reseed through cash for work.7. Support formation and Establishment of village savings groups-Having access to credit is vital in any community. It helps people build their businesses and provide for their children. Savings Groups are making this possible in some of the world's most remote and impoverished communities. Savings Groups are simple, transparent, autonomous and locally run. Members of savings groups save together, lend their savings to each other and share the profits. Members provide their own savings and credit services at negligible cost, while retaining earnings and investment in their own communities.World Vision has adapted the village savings model and implemented in an integrated fashion with relief programming and cash interventions with great success.Its from such success that World Vision is proposing to replicate and intensify this model which is proven to graduate people out of extreme poverty.To ensure the activities proposed are viable,World Vision will work with producer groups and link them to market.Vocational trainings targeting displaced populations will only be offered after a labour market assessment to ensure only employable skills are offered.

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate against any sources of tension, this project will ensure that the selection criteria for beneficiaries of the project is developed in a transparent manner with participation from all the people groups in the project locations. Further, the community and local authorities will vet the list of beneficiaries selected in public fora for transparency and ownership. The project will also take into consideration any cultural sensitivities by strengthening accountability mechanisms. For instance, a platform for information sharing regarding the project interventions that is accessible to all people groups will be utilized to prevent any further marginalization of minority groups due to lack of information on resource allocation. World Vision will also strengthen protection of most vulnerable groups including children who are exposed to abuse of drugs, school dropout, child labor, early marriage particularly for girls and recruitment by extremist or militia groups.The empowerment of households with livelihood support will enable them to provide basic needs for their children including food and education needs as a result of the unconditional cash-based programming. Provision of food at household level also translates directly into reduction in cases of child labour where children are forced out of school in search of food for the household. In addition, prevention of family separation as the breadwinners through migration in search of livelihoods and food through these project activities will also contribute to the reduction on the numbers of unaccompanied and separated children in the target locations. World Vision will implement a Community Response Mechanism (CRM) that will provide a way for participants to voice their feedback and complaints to WV regarding the implementation of the project.

Please provide further explanation

The project will facilitate the gradual resolution of the protracted displacement of IDPs in target locations, by enhancing local integration capacity, which remains the preferred durable solution option for most of the IDPs.This will involve : (a) strengthening relevant governance structures through capacity building and accountability mechanisms to create the relevant sustainability potential for solutions, on (b) implementing innovative, replicable and scalable solutions for basic and protective service delivery and employmentCash-based interventions will initially enable displacement-affected populations accessing housing, tenure security, livelihoods and protective services. Through the creation of gender and environmentally sensitive income generating activities, as well as long-term employment opportunities in support of systemic job- creation, target groups will be enabled to generate the income and basic services once assistance will be phased down. The integrated interventions are novel, replicable and have a potential for scalability to become effective durable solution systems that can be linked to local governance and integrate in longer-term urban development over time.
