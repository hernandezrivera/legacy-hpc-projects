Project Summary

This project will ensure 7,577 of the most vulnerable Palestinian children (5,525 boys and 2,052 girls aged 12-17 years old) exposed to conflict-related violence, and 4,775 caregivers are reached with protection, impact mitigation, rehabilitation, and prevention services in the West Bank. It will also foster increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations against children through documenting and monitoring violations to inform advocacy and increase accountability.
This project is in line and contributes the three strategic objectives of the humanitarian response plan and to the Protection Cluster's first and second objectives that aim to increase respect for IHL, IHRL, and accountability for violations as well as ensuring that protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict-related violence.
The main objective of this child protection project is for children in the West Bank to be safe, protected, and secure from violence, exploitation, and grave violations. The project will:
• support initiatives to engage vulnerable and at-risk children positively in structured recreational, educational, awareness-raising, life skills, and resilience-building activities to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms.
• support awareness-raising for vulnerable families and communities regarding children’s right to protection; preventing and responding to Violence Against Children (VAC); and the impact of stigma, social exclusion, and bullying caused by COVID-19
• support vulnerable children affected by conflict-related violence and their families with integration and rehabilitation services especially structured counselling and mental health and psychosocial support including through helpline services. (Level 3)
• support monitoring, data analysis, and reporting on grave violations trends related to child protection concerns in Israel/Occupied Palestinian Territory (the WB) to provide evidence and inform advocacy.
• support legal assistance for children affected by conflict-related violence.
Ensuring compliance with PSEA principles and procedures, implementing partners and project beneficiaries will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse and will receive information on UNICEF email, as well as other interagency channels including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA.

1 - Needs

The escalation of hostilities between Israel and Palestinian armed groups in Gaza in May 2021 coincided with a sharp rise in protests, clashes, and arrests across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. These events exacerbated already severe protection needs throughout the State of Palestine. A total of 7,948 Palestinian children were affected by conflict-related grave violations between July 2020 and June 2021. While conflict-related violence has an impact on both boys and girls, boys continue to be at a higher risk of being injured, killed, or arrested. Following a decline in conflict-related incidents during 2020 owing to COVID-19, the resurgent violence during 2021 resulted in violations against children. Between July 2020 and June 2021, 81 Palestinian children (23 girls, 58 boys) were killed, and 1,248 reported injured (287 girls, 961 boys) in the oPt, and more than 400 Palestinian children (98% boys) have been arrested in East Jerusalem in January-September 2021. Many children previously affected by violations remain in need of continuing case management support, in some cases requiring previously closed cases to be re-opened. The COVID-19 pandemic meanwhile continues, with resulting movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic pressures exacerbating vulnerabilities in the West Bank with increased reported incidents of negative coping mechanisms including domestic violence, child labour, and child marriages. 92% of boys and 88% of girls in oPt already face violent discipline, with 14% of children in West Bank subject to severe physical violence from caregivers (boys 23%, girls 17%). 10% of children (age 5-17) are engaged in child labour, with boys twice as likely as girls to be working and six times as likely to work in hazardous conditions. The 2020 NIAF study highlighted a 26% increase in services provided to children in the West Bank experiencing violence and abuse at home. There is an urgent need for quality prevention, impact mitigation, and protection services for vulnerable children. The CP-AOR estimates that 145,000 children in the West Bank are in need of CP and MHPSS services, including 25,000 in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. There is also a need for monitoring, documentation, and verification of grave violations, to ensure timely reporting and analysis to inform the humanitarian response. The coercive environment in the occupied West Bank intensifies, driven by demolitions, forced evictions, discriminatory planning, access restrictions, settlement expansion, and settler violence, generating a risk of forcible transfer for many Palestinians in Area C, East Jerusalem, and the H2 area of Hebron city, H2. Since the beginning of 2021, 13 Palestinian boys were killed by live ammunition, and 697 children were reported injured during conflict-related incidents in the West Bank including East Jerusalem. During 2020 2,585 children were affected by demolitions and 725 people, including 380 children were displaced, and 155 donor-funded structures were demolished. 87% of demolitions occurred in Area C and 13% in East Jerusalem. Of particular concern are the vulnerable communities living in Area C and East Jerusalem in proximity and around the so-called settlement blocs in light of the threat of annexation in the West Bank and the Israeli settlement expansion plans and settlers’ violence in those areas. In 2020, 853 Palestinian children were arrested in the West Bank including East Jerusalem (Source: Israeli Police data, based on NGO freedom of information requests). Detained children remain at high risk of violence, ill-treatment, and due process violations during their arrest, transfer, interrogation, and detention, affecting their psychosocial well-being even after release (NIAF 2020). Owing to prevalent gender and social norms, adolescent boys are the most affected, and when released, they are at risk of school dropout and/or mental health disorders.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims for children (boys and girls aged 12-17 years old) in oPt to be safe and protected from violence, exploitation, and grave violations. The project’s main goal is to reduce the vulnerabilities of children and families in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, and improve their resilience. It also aims to document Children affected by Armed Conflict (CAAC) incidents within the oPt/Israel as a ‘Situation of Concern’. This will be achieved through 1) Protecting children at risk of being impacted by conflict-related violence through coordinated community-level prevention programmes and protective services in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem; 2) Responding to the needs of directly affected children and their families through the provision of impact mitigation services and psychosocial support and 3) Documenting and reporting on CAAC incidents within the oPt/Israel as a “situation of concern”. To address the increasing needs resulting from increased conflict violence and COVID-19, the intervention will prioritize children at risk of domestic violence, children with disabilities, children exposed to conflict-related violence, and children living under the poverty line. Partners will ensure the provision of both face-to-face and remote services upon need. In order to prevent increasing child protection concerns, the intervention will scale up awareness-raising activities on child protection, the impact of stigma, social exclusion, and bullying caused by COVID-19. UNICEF and partners will consult children to review existing feedback mechanisms, including accessibility, and then process their feedback and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP). Acknowledging the different needs of boys and girls and thus the need to design interventions accordingly, gender will be mainstreamed throughout the programme cycle; during implementation and monitoring. The project approach is framed through a gender sensitive lens that recognizes that the vulnerabilities of girls and boys frequently manifest themselves differently; girls are more susceptible to distress and mental health disorders, violence and negative coping strategies such as child marriage especially with many confined in their households and with schools intermittently open. Boys are more likely to engage in child labour, and without structured recreational support, they are likely to come into contact with Israeli Forces and Israeli settlers . As a result, there is a strong emphasis on reaching boys at risk with preventative measures. The project will also increase opportunities for girls and women to be involved and effectively participate in the design and implementation of different activities. The project will seek to achieve the following outputs and activities: Output 1: Support vulnerable children and their families to access prevention and protection services to improve their resilience and coping mechanisms in the worst affected areas in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

This project aims to engage with diverse community members on child protection concerns both in relation to COVID-19 protection concerns and to the VAC agenda. This engagement will include community groups discussions, awareness-raising sessions, social media campaigns, etc. with the appropriate measures including personal protective equipment (PPE) put in place to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and respect current restrictions. In all of these activities, UNICEF and its partners will ensure diverse participation of community members including girls and boys, women and men, adolescents, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Participation of children and their parents/caregivers in these activities will be voluntary and based upon their informed consent/assent. Global guidance such as the Child Protection Minimum Standards (CPMS) and UNICEF’s internal policies on informed consent/assent, confidentiality, data protection, and information sharing will be applied in accordance with the best interest of the child principle. UNICEF and partners will determine whether informed consent/assent is legally required or expected of a parent/caregiver. They will also determine from whom informed consent/assent is legally required in cases of child-headed households or children in institutions. They will ensure that consent is given in a form and language that reflects the capacity of the child. Data confidentiality and data protection will be ensured through all phases of collection, transfer, storage, and communication. These procedures will guide UNICEF and its implementing partners' interaction with children and their parents/caregivers as well as between UNICEF and the implementing partners. UNICEF will also ensure that its own staff adhere to its child safeguarding policy and that the selected implementing partners have such a framework put in place.Children with disabilities (CWDs) will be included in the various phases of the project in line with the Do No Harm principle. Also, children will be engaged in ways that are appropriate to their level of capacities taking into account power imbalances that may increase the risk of vulnerability for CWDs. Children will be listened to as a first step to enhance their participation in the decision-making processes, the design and development of activities, and the implementation of the evaluation of the impact of the intervention. CWDs will have sufficient spaces to contribute to forums/discussions the same way other children are able to take part. They will also have opportunities to challenge some decisions that are made by adults in the communities where they live and which impact their access to quality child protection services. Gaps in formal services provision will be addressed by partnering with local organizations of people with disabilities or community-based programmes.All implementing partners will be requested to include a minimum percentage, at least 1.5%, of Children with Disabilities beneficiaries. Partners will also be requested to include CWDs in all planned surveys, researches and other analyses to better understand the situation of CWDs and difficulties they and their families face. UNICEF and implementing partners will continue to discuss and agree on recruitment of specialist professionals to deliver services to CWDs. Implementing partners will also receive capacity building to enhance their knowledge and skills in the field of CP service delivery to CWDs. This will be done either directly by UNICEF support or under the umbrella of the Child Protection AOR. UNICEF will follow up with implementing partners on coordinating their interventions among other child protection /MHPSS actors to avoid duplication of services and harming the affected people. The partners will also report on their interventions using the IM tools of the Protection Cluster. In addition, the East Jerusalem coordination committee will coordinate their activities with the CP AOR.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will be informed by the United Nations Response Strategies that highlight the needs of children in oPt: the findings and recommendations of the East Jerusalem Action Plan, the Evaluation of EJ Action Plan and the consultations conducted by UNICEF with key stakeholders. In addition, UNICEF and its implementing partners will involve children, adolescents and caregivers by conducting virtual and in person (when possible) focus group discussions, and community outreach activities. Children will be consulted to review existing feedback mechanisms, to express their views and provide suggestions on how to make those mechanisms more accessible. Upon collection and receipt of complaints, responses and feedbacks, UNICEF and partners will process the feedback, responses and complaints in line with UNICEF Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) developed in the oPt. UNICEF and their implementing partners will promote the active participation of the most affected children and caregivers throughout the project and will ensure complete and equal access into different opportunities offered by this project. The project will raise awareness among caregivers and the community of the importance of female participation and coordinate closely with caregivers who might express concerns over their children’s participation. To ensure the relevance of the interventions, a systematic, objective and continuous context humanitarian analysis is conducted while designing and planning the project, while keeping an open eye to the changing needs, capacities, and contexts and thus adapting the interventions accordingly whenever required. UNICEF identified some key risks and mitigation measures as per the following: Risk 1: Widespread violence resulting from prevailing frustrations and hopelessness affecting youth, disadvantaged and disenfranchised population strata. This violence could be related both to discontent with the prevailing occupation measures, or discontent within and among Palestinians. Mitigation measures 1: Ensure that there is enough flexibility to allow reprogram of activities depending on the emerging needs. Risk 2: Communities refuse to allow children and families to participate in prevention activities for various reasons.Mitigation measures 2: Ensure continued dialogue and participation of key community members on the importance of prevention initiatives and sensitize them on the impact of rights violations.
