Project Summary

Multi-sectoral approaches reflect the interconnected needs of children and emphasize all humanitarian actors’ collective responsibility to protect children and their families. Focused, specialized child protection interventions are critical for protecting children. However, no single sector that operates in a crisis has the knowledge, skills, and resources to fully prevent risks, respond to children’s protection needs and promote. GSF shall provide community-based comprehensive child protection services for conflict-affected women children and people living with disability in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, across 5 LGAs. GSF project team will reach these direct project beneficiaries with comprehensive case management and MHPSS intervention by coordinating for referral to WASH, Education, and Health Services. The overall target shall benefit from campaigns to prevent sexual exploitation & abuse and GBV prevention. We shall work closely with WaSH and health partners on the ground to provide children with access to WASH and health facilities during CFS and in schools.
The five LGAs targeted for this project include MMC, Jere, Mubi North, Mubi South, Damaturu, in BAY state respectively which are priority sector areas for the allocations.
The promotion of the well-being of the children, women, and people living with disabilities will be at the heart of this intervention. GSF will use its safeguarding policies, procedures, and practices to ensure that the intervention is safe for the inclusive participation of these targets beneficiaries. Protecting the children and adolescents from harm by creating awareness on PSEA and mitigating the risks of harm during the process of planning, implementing and monitoring will be imperative. GSF will ensure compliance with the code of conduct for its own staff and other partners to ensure the safety of the participating children and adolescents.
The caseload will be documented and updated using the IA forms and CPIMS primero for the data storage by a dedicated CPIMS officer assisted by caseworkers and designated gadgets such as laptop, tablet, and external hard drive with an iCloud backup storage facility.
GSF will promote gender equality and equity in the design, implementation, and monitoring of this project. The specific needs and priorities of children based on age and gender will be identified through focused group discussions during the identification and profiling of the children. This project will be implemented by community actors who may be both beneficiaries as well as stakeholders. GSF will take steps to work with the community in order to build in activities that will contribute to the sustainability of some aspects of the project. Throughout the project, GSF will be working to continuously build a strong partnership and collaboration with the MWASD welfare officers and other local actors such as religious and traditional leaders at the community and camp level.
GSF also has a strong institutional capacity with internal controls systems which will ensure program quality assurance and efficiency to manage this partnership. GSF has benefitted from a series of training that supports strengthening it is financial management system as well as the governance structure. This has helped GSF to greatly improve its financial reporting using Quickbook, monitoring of the project as well as risk management.
GSF has a strong field presence in Borno, Adamawa state of northeast Nigeria. GSF is also associated with a wide network of local women groups and youth in the host communities and IDP camps. GSF has strong connections among traditional, religious community leaders and key government institutions which it uses for advocacy to enhance the service promotion and accountability to the affected population.

1 - Needs

The unabated conflict in northeast Nigeria has continued to severely affect millions of people. The protracted attacks coupled with the pandemic and economic downturn have increased the vulnerability of children. The child protection sub-sector revealed that 1.7 million children (41 percent girls, 59 percent boys) are currently living in locations with limited or no core child protection services and community-based prevention and response to protection risk thereby exposing them to child marriage, family separation, physical and sexual violence, psychosocial distress, use and recruitment by non-state armed groups in the BAY states. More worrisome is the limited access to food, quality education, healthcare, and lack of adequate parental care, particularly for children with disabilities. These continue to significantly affect the physical, mental, emotional, and social well-being of children. Out of the 1.7 million vulnerable children (55% in Borno, 27% in Yobe, and 18% in Adamawa states), 19% ovulnerable parents and caregivers have inadequate or no access to other humanitarian services such as education, health, nutrition, WASH and early recovery services. As the ongoing conflict continue to be the epicenter of humanitarian needs, modalities are consistently being put in place to support victims and to find lasting remedy to the crisis. Federal, regional, and state governments have made significant progress towards responding adequately to the needs of the affected people in the BAY states. For example, Borno state through the implementation of the 25 years’ development plan and 10 years’ strategic transformation plan that are being synchronized by agency for coordination of sustainable development and humanitarian response as at May 2021 was able to relocate about 2000 displaced households taking refuge in NYSC and Mocolis camp, Maiduguri back to their communities. Currently, the process is ongoing which is why momentum is being channeled by the agency towards harmonizing the interventions of both humanitarian and development organizations in order to strengthen responses for better impact.While efforts are being made to ensure that displaced persons are returned to their communities especially in Borno and Yobe states, most of the returnees are faced with difficulties as the required services such as WASH, health, nutrition, education and protection are lacking. This is because, the security situation still remains alarming as civilians in LGA’s lack adequate freedom to move outside the community in fear of attack. This has propelled the vulnerability level of the affected people and the need for multi sectoral intervention to provide the needed support especially for children as they are extremely vulnerable. According to the child protection dashboard for August 2021, 1,000,268 people were targeted for child protection services but only 343, 364 persons have been reached so far from January to August 2021. 675, 454 persons, 31 percent girls, 33 percent boys and 75 percent women, 63 percent men are yet to be reached with child protection specific activities including Education, WaSH and Health integrated services for conflict affected boys and girls in Borno Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. In Borno (MMC and Damboa LGAs), comprehensive multi-sectoral assistance is required for children with Plan International, GOALPrime and IDS pulling out from Damboa and from other LGAs in Maiduguri LGA, leaving gaps in the LGAs. In Yobe, (Damaturu and Gujba) though partners are on ground providing Child protection services, including Plan, SCI and IRC a multi-sectoral approach by GSF in the two LGAs in Yobe would complement the Child Protection partners on ground, while for Adamawa, (Mubi North and South) only IOM and ROHI are responding to children, IOM and ROHI currently providing PSS and Case management respectively in Mubi North, while in Mubi South, Only IOM provides Case management services with a gap in PSS provision.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming throughout the project activities. The project team will conduct risk analysis exercises prior to activities and periodically thereafter to understand the vulnerabilities, capacities, threats, power holders, gender analysis and service availability in the areas to identify what risks might exist or arise. When the risk is identified, either pre-existing or potentially due to GSF activities, specific mitigation measures are assigned (time-bound with a responsible person identified), and the risk itself is measured by the community against the positives of the activity proposed.The proposed project will take into account strict adherence to gender equality and equity throughout the design and implementation phase. The specific needs of children and women survivors are based on age and gender, ensuring for instance that adolescent females are assigned a female staff or volunteer, and those young children are assigned a staff who has experience with, and can easily interact with children their age. The staff and volunteers engaged in the project will be gender-balanced to help ensure that both girls and boys are able to relate to and talk with appropriate persons. The leadership of the community structures will include females and GSF will facilitate participation from all members, irrespective of their age and gender. The use of women to contribute to awareness creation on CP and GBV directly is a context-specific opportunity to advance women’s participation. The majority of the religious and community leaders are males with only a few female representatives at the community level. During the training and meetings with the community leaders, the project will aim to engage all female leaders to ensure their representation and may if appropriate, include other well-respected women in the community who are vocal about CP and GBV protection Concerns.Supported by the Community Based mechanism, GSF will monitor the overall protection situation in camps, informal settings, and host communities on a regular basis and develop networks and contacts with relevant stakeholders to identify incidents and protection concerns, such as access to humanitarian assistance by communities in general and by persons with special needs, UASC, GBV survivors, gaps in service delivery but also information related to education, nutrition, food, healthcare, water, shelter as well as safety and security including any related incidents. Concerns on reported/alleged violations/protection issues will be documented, recorded, verified and analyzed together to support the identification of priority key protection issues to respond to either by providing direct advice or assistance or by referring beneficiaries to appropriate service providers (in the case of incidents).The methodology for protection mainstreaming will include spot observations, focus group discussions (FGDs) with key informants and monitored populations, individual/household interviews, and area assessments as well as testimonies and complaints collected through the complaint and response mechanism. In addition, as part of the overall mainstreaming strategy, GSF will conduct two-way information sharing with the target population throughout all of its activities, thus providing information to IDPs on return conditions, rights and entitlements while seeking information from IDPs on incidents and protection concerns in camps, host communities and in their areas of origin.
