Project Summary

There is an acute shortage of Emergency and NFI support for 4.7 Million out of which, 2.7 Million resides in areas of severity. A vast majority (90%) reported unaffordability to cope with SNFI needs, coupled with inadequate shelter. As informed by HNO and through our surveys, there has been an increase of 37% from 2020 to 2021.
The proposed action will provide response to emergency SNFI needs caused by conflict- related displacement, flooding and other shocks and protect the wellbeing and thermal comfort of highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in NWS, Aleppo Governorate. We plan to focus on 4 sub-districts (Afrin, Al-Bab, Mare’ and Suran).
The planned programming intends to be achieved through an integrated program of assistance focused on providing targeted, mixed modality CVA and in-kind NFI assistance to the most vulnerable host and IDP households (HHs) in areas with greatest severity of winter need.
- Winter Response planned to target 15040 HHs for the winter response component and seasonal and supplementary NFIs by implementing CVA for one time (valued at $150 each for HHs).
- Provision of Core NFIs by procuring and distributing winter items to 1000 HHs (Gift-in-Kind - valued at $185 each for HHs).
- Shelter Response (Target 350 HHs) – For Shelter Response, we intend to reach out to 350 most vulnerable HHs (3,850 beneficiaries), in need of dignified shelter. The assistance would be direct contracting assistance, which is expected to generate gainful employment for able men and women as well.
Modality – We will utilize a mix modality (CVA and In-Kind) to achieve the targets set for 2022. Primary modality will be CVA through provision of a onetime Winter Assistance valued at USD 150 per HH. Households that have received CVA assistance will not receive in-kind assistance. The CVA would be for targeted winter response items. Direct assistance will be provided for Shelter Assistance by deployment. The selection of beneficiaries will be based on our already developed vulnerability criteria considering protection issues as well.
Data Collection and Monitoring - The monitoring will be through our well defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed starting from Verification, Distribution Monitoring, Post Distribution Monitoring and Satisfaction Surveys. The data will be collected online, primarily using tablets and Kobo tools. We deploy several tools to include Excel and SPSS to analyze and report. A robust system is in place for complaints receipt and redressal, with dedicated teams available for handling and resolving complaints. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS and email. Further, the complainant can lodge a complaint via hard copy and drop it in the drop box at our office locations.
Reporting – We will ensure prompt reporting on all of our projects through regular filing of donor and 4W reports and ad hoc reporting requirements.
CVA Support Systems – A fully automated and online Cash Voucher System developed internally and utilized for last 6 years will be deployed. The system is flexible to use paper vouchers or cards and can be of single or multiple use. To complement our voucher programs, we have framework agreements with Fund Service Providers that provide cash distributions through formal arrangements. Due to this system, our response time for emergencies range from 5 – 7 days from the onset of emergency.

3 - Needs

22.1 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, of which the highest number (2.8 million) are located in Aleppo governorate. According to HNO23, almost seven out of ten people in Syria need assistance. The overall People in Need has increased from 14.6 million in 2022 to 15.3 million in2023, including 5.3 million IDPs, 9.9 million residents, and 52,700 returnees. More than 57% of households in camps reported living in tents, prefabricated units and makeshift shelters which provide limited protection against environmental elements and require frequent replacement and/or repair. More than 90 per cent of households reported a combination of shelter inadequacies. According to the ISIMM, 30 per cent of IDP sites in NWS flooded, and NES experienced 25 storm events damaging tents and site infrastructure during the past year. Nearly 85 per cent of the overall population reported a deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs, demonstrated through a significant increase in the inaccessibility of NFI items, such as clothing, mattresses, and blankets. Coupled with the deterioration of shelter integrity, increasing shelter adequacy issues.HNO 2023We have identified 4 potential sub-districts (Afrin, Al-Bab, Mare’ and Suran) as intervention areas. Identified areas have seen surged IDPs, faced with harsh winters and flooding. The planned programming intends to address the needs of approx. 4 - Implementation of activities

The proposed action will deliver life-saving winter NFI assistance and address critical winter gaps for (15,040 HHs) acutely vulnerable people in Afrin sub-district, in addition to provision of Core NFIs by procuring and distributing winter items to 1000 HHs (Gift-in-Kind). Moreover, the action will provide increased access to safe, dignified shelter solutions for (350 HHs) of the most vulnerable beneficiaries targeted for winter NFI assistance. One-off Winter Cash Top-ups of $150 per household will be distributed to total 15,040 highly vulnerable households, in addition to provide Gift-in-Kind winter items to 1,000 HHs ($185 each for HHs) during 2023 to protect thermal comfort and household wellbeing. Winter assistance will be based on needs and target the most vulnerable households based on the eligibility and vulnerability criteria recommended in the cluster.The innovative shelter component proposed will provide cash and technical assistance to support shelter self-rehabilitation resulting in dignified, safe shelter solutions for 350 of the most vulnerable households receiving winter cash. This component will contributing to local economies and livelihoods while providing maximum flexibility for beneficiaries to prioritize their most pressing and relevant shelter repairs. The cash-based approach builds on Big Hearts extensive experience with Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) programming to meet winter, non-food, shelter, food and other needs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Syria faces the most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities which resulted in massive numbers requiring assistance. With increased deteriorating situation, the coping mechanisms are failing leading to serious protection issues. This is further complexed by change of roles where females are heading the households, a role traditionally held by the male population. These insecurities comes with protection issues, particularly GBV and child labor. Coupled with increased hostilities and COVID, resulted in negative coping mechanism such as early marriages and child labor. A safe estimates informs that 42% of the affected population faces one or more protection issues. No significant risks were found during implementation of current programming. To counter any risks, we have designed and put in place a strong Protection mechanism, where protection issues can be reported and complaints handled speedily both at field and HQ level. Our staff is continuously trained in PSEA. Variety of mediums have been deployed for complaints including telephone, email and physical receipts.To counter risks, a strong awareness campaigns are designed along with a robust Complaint Redressal Mechanism to handle complaints. A strong team is in place with appropriate trainings and refreshers to handle complaints. All complaints will be duly reported to the funding agencies.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real‑time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary.The following assumptions have been applied to program design: • LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone• 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplication• Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission • Security context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. • Female beneficiaries continue to be willing to engage in culturally appropriate livelihood activities.We are continually updating contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Should breaks be experienced due to border closures, contingency plans are to be in place to adapt to the changing context in NWS. These plans detail various scenarios and required actions to mitigate risks both operational and programmatic.
