Project Summary

The project will be implemented in three locations in northwest Syria within sub-districts of severity 5. The first location is in Afrin city, where the severity scale is 5
and the people in need for education services is 25,901 children (11,752 boys, 14,149 girls). The second location is in Dana city where the severity scale is 5 and the people in need for education services is 248,424 children (120,802 boys, 127,622 girls. The third location is in Azaz city where the severity scale is 5 and the people in need for education services is 96,101 children (51,613 boys, 44,488 girls). After signing memorandums of understanding with the local councils and the Education Directorates in those locations, 3 Temporary Leaning Spaces (TLSs) will be established to accommodate 4650 out-of-school (OOSC) and dropped out children (2325 girls and 2325 boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability) to receive non-formal education including Self Learning Programme (SLP), Accelerated Learning Program (ALP), Remedial Classes and Catch-up Classes and 1550 children (775 girls and 775 boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability) provided with school-based Psycho-social support (PSS), and Life Skills. 42 education and PSS personnel (females, males, IDPs and Host Community) will be hired and trained to run the 3 Temporary Leaning Spaces including teachers providing non formal education and PSS staff providing PSS sessions. The overall Objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to non-formal education for crisis-affected children (girls and boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability) which is in line with the education sector objectives. The project aims at increasing safe an equitable access to non-formal education for crisis affected children in line with the education sector objective and HRP 2020 Objective-1, through Back To Learning campaigns (BTL) for 6000 children (5-17 years old) (3000 boys,3000 girls) 10% children with disability. Back to learning campaigns are a response and strategy to facilitate access, enrolment and attendance to protective learning. BTL campaigns will include advocacy, awareness raising, mobilization messaging and sensitization, supplies and materials, and establishment of temporary learning spaces.We will provide 12 schools or learning spaces (4 in each location) with only gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities with 2400 school students (1200 boys, 1200 girls) 10% children with disability as indirect beneficiaries from those schools that we will provide gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities in them. 4650 students (10% children with disability) will be provided with school supplies in formal and non-formal settings (Stationary, notebooks, school bags, school supply kits).
The project also aims at enhancing the quality of non-formal education for out-of-school/dropped out children ( girls and boys aged between 8 and 17 years old – 10% children with disability) within a protective environment in line with the education sector objective and HRP 2020 Objective-2, through training 600 teachers and education personnel (300 males, 300 females) on SLP, ALP, Remedial Classes, Catch-up Classes, child-centred protective pedagogy, Education in Emergency (EiE), active learning, classroom management ...etc. The total number of beneficiaries in the project is 19850 people (9925 males, 9925 females).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat 1: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. Mitigating Measures: 1.1: All attacks should be reported to the Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism. The Education Sector, Cluster and Working Group should use this information to advocate for safe education.1.2: Learning facilities should have safety protocols in place. These protocols should be adequately resourced and regularly practiced. Resources may include walkie-talkies, basic firefighting equipment, basic first aid supplies, flash lights, exit signs, etc.Protection Risk/Threat 2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Students participate in bullying of students.Mitigating Measures: 2.1: Learning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct and policy on bullying. 2.2: Teachers and school personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. The application of learning should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. Protection Risk/Threat 3: Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving.Mitigating Measures: 3.1: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.
