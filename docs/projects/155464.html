Project Summary

Refugee and asylum seeker children and youth are not only disadvantaged, but their educational needs
and achievements remain largely invisible in most national plans. The project intends to improve the socio-economic opportunities and literacy rates of refugee children and youth in Tongagara refugee camp by reinforcing the education in emergencies interventions currently being implemented. The El Nino drought crises is likely to increase the risk of school drop-out especially among girls as it exacerbates their vulnerability to sexual abuse and exploitation leading to increased early marriages and child pregnancies. The purpose of the project is to strengthen the comprehensive school safety framework minimizing risk in Tongogara Refugee Schools of education service delivery disruptions and strengthen the accelerated learning programmes from Early childhood education to secondary education and the catch-up classes. The project will specifically target children aged 5 -17 years in and out of school in Tongogara refugee camp. The people in need and target population is 4,841 refugee and asylum seeker children from DRC, Rwanda, Burundi, Mozambique and from elswhere residing in Tongogara refugee camp.

Needs

Education provides stability and security when everything else in the lives of refugee and asylum seeker children seems to have fallen apart. They need a peaceful and reliable education environment while their parents try to cope with the reality of displacement – seeking food and shelter which can be exacerbated by natural shocks such as drought in the country of settlement. A comprehensive school safety framework, emergency education stocks and equipment are therefore essential to improve the disaster preparedness of schools in Tongogara Refugee Camp. The target group 4,841 (2,319 girls; 2,522 boys) aged 5 to 17 years old in Tongogara refugee camp are in need of life-saving skills and information such as how to avoid danger, how to prevent disease and where to find help. Furthermore, the school teachers from Tongogara Early Childhood Development Centre, primary through St Micheal's Secondary and support staff need training in psycho social support, disaster risk reduction and multi-hazard data analysis. The capacity building exercise for teachers is needed to ensure that they are able to help address the psychological and social needs of young children and adolescents recovering from the trauma of conflict. Refugee children may have missed out on months or years of education before fleeing their countries. To help them bridge the gap of many missed years of schooling, more flexible forms of education are essential such as accelerated education, catch-up and bridging programmes.

Activities

The proposed activities under this sector include;- Facilitating the development of a comprehensive school safety framework-Training of teachers in disaster risk reduction and multi-hazard data analysis-Supporting and facilitating accelerated education, catch-up and bridging programmes - Training of teachers and para-professionals in accelerated learning, catch-up and bridging programmes-Provision of education materials-Setting up and equipping guidance and counselling rooms for provision of psycho-social counselling support- Refurbishment, establishment and equipping of educational infrastructure to provide a safe learning and study environment for early graders, primary and secondary learners and to cater for the new arrivals- School fees support for new arrivals, in and out of school refugee and asylum seeker children in Tongogara Refugee Camp
