Project Summary

Similarly to other parts of Sudan, Abyei suffers from a myriad of problems, but unlike any other part of Sudan it gains limited response because it is cut off from the rest of Sudan. For the past four years Global Aid Hand has been conducting a health program in North Abyei with the support from different partners. In the latest developments of this program health infrastructures have been developed, with Diffra and Mekennis now having medical doctors to attend to patients, well equipped and functional laboratories for investigations and health cadre that is motivated and competent. The health facilities of Diffra, Mikenis, Alradaya and Umkhair now have delivery rooms and an obstetrics theatre is being put up at Diffra to reduce referral to Al Muglad. While health services have increased there still exists a big gap in coverage especially in areas where vulnerable host communities settle. This project aims support and facilitate sustainable comprehensive health packages including primary health care, reproductive health and nutrition for vulnerables, IDPs and host communities in Abyei, with special focus on women, girls and children.This project will complement the current ongoing project in Abyei area through which the primary health services are delivered to the target communities through 7 primary health care centers GAH would like the support to continue the implementation of multi -sectorial, multi locality integrated project whose core is health and nutrition. The need for this project is embedded in the fact that the prevalence of diseases is getting high. In addition, the increasing number of referred cases shows the need for more work on the health services in these areas.
This project it to ensure all basic interventions of health and nutrition are being served in high quality to make sure there's reduction in the rate of diseases and referral cases.
Health and nutrition activities will be conducted in all areas targeted while all other sectors will be added in Abyei. The selection is based on assessments conducted on 15th Dec 2018 to identify gaps and other already existing interventions in the areas.

Needs

For the past four years Global Aid Hand has been conducting a health program in North Abyei with the support from different partners. The challenge still remains due to the big difference in the gap and the service provision, that includes the low capacities of health and nutrition staff as well as the extremely low numbers of HFs clearly indicated in the assessment made by GAH for the health facilities and the population which found that there’s on health facility for 40,714 people while the standard is – in accordance to the WHO-10,000 persons for one health facility, and one rural hospital that provides health services for 290,500 people, while standard is 100,000 patients for one hospital. There are only 2 medical doctors for 290,500 patients, one medical assistant for 75000 patients and one midwife for 13,377 women and girls in reproductive age. There are also no OTPs in Abyei which makes access to nutrition services extremely difficult, leading to an increase in the rates of malnutrition. The increase in malnutrition cases as well as the outbreaks of communicable diseases and the poor access to health services in general makes the health situation in Abyei very worrying. This is made worse by the existing conflicts that demand people to flee their homes in addition to destruction of their assets. According to the medical records in PHCs, anemia is a common disease and there are no specific programs to deal with it. In addition Guinea worms and schistosomiasis infections cases have been reported need more attention since Abyei area is swampy during the rainy season. The PHCs receive HIV positive cases but there are no specific interventions or prevention methods. There are no ways of postpartum monitoring of the health of mothers and their newborns. Vaccinations do not reach residents in remote areas .inaccessibility of roads during the rainy season due to the poor infrastructure exacerbates the challenges. The situation is further worsened by the governance of Abyei which is under the control of the Abyei Joint Oversight Committee (AJOC) and thus not part of the line Ministry coordination mechanism (GAH has managed to create a relation and now MoH supports the area) According to the nutrition assessments conducted in Abyei by GAH and WFP done in July/August 2017 there was a worrying increase in the number of malnourished cases particularly among children and pregnant women.The assessment report showed a high prevalence of acute malnutrition (GAM >10%) 10/13 households assessed compared to 12/13 IDP. Critical levels of acute malnutrition were recorded in 5 settlements- Mekennis, Diffra, Umkhair and Radaya. Critical levels of SAM were recorded among Mekennis( 4.3%) and Diffra( 4.2%). Sustained prevalence of critical SAM levels in these households suggests that nutrition support services to treat these children need to be scaled up. There are no MUAC clinic teams and no RUTF available. CMAM teams have not received their incentives.

Activities

The health interventions in Abyei are to be continued and strengthened by GAH and the other partners in 2019. Current project supports running of 5 clinics and we would like to support the same in this intervention and increase coverage to remote locations. GAH has been working with support from the MoH in vaccination campaigns. The MoH has also supported our work with medical staff that has been trained by GAH. GAH- supported by MoH , WHO and UNFPA- has been able to construct a theater room, and the drugs were provided with the support from AJOC and the medical supply in Sudan. GAH would like to continue the delivery of basic health care services including maternal and child health, support referral to secondary health facilities, conduct routine immunization in mobile health clinics for those in remote areas. Conduct health education trainings for health staff. For women and girls in reproductive age: support antenatal clinics and the provision of pregnancy supplements, provide them with access to safe deliveries, as well as the required vaccinations, introduction of Newborn care and acquiring the remaining equipments for the finalization of the theater room. Increase the HIV/AIDS testing facilities and provide psychosocial support teams, and proper interventions for positive cases, provision of supplements for anemia cases, efficient testing for malaria, guinea worms and schistosomiasis cases,and drugs for treatments. Introducing incinerators in the HFs to adequately dispose of medical waste.For nutrition this proposed project will enhance the infrastructure of 2 OTP centers. The Project will fully apply the CMAM, offer SFP and OTP services and awareness raising sessions to be conducted. Products for use will be sourced from UNICEF and WFP .GAH will implement these activities inside the PHCs and the services will be available for all those seeking it, while maintaining their dignities, and make sure that no further harm is inflicted on beneficiaries.
