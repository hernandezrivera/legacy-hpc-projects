Project Summary

In line with the ﬁrst pillar of “No Lost Generation Initiative” and with the three main Sector Objectives, the context analysis and the current demographic trends, this intervention aims at re-establishing basic education services in access, quality and inclusion in Rural Damascus and Hama Governorates while promoting resilience mechanisms to education and protection needs within the targeted communities. The selected areas are 4 sub -.districts of Rural Damascus (Kafr Batna, Harasta, Arbin, Duma) 7 of Hama (Suran, Hamra, Karnaz, Oqeirbat, Muradah, As – Suqaylabiyah); targeted locations are both rural and urban areas and is possible they change upon new redeﬁnition of needs and accessibility. The 45% of them is in severity scale 4, 36% in 5, 9% in 6 and 9% in 3. The only area with severity scale 3 is Zamalqa (Arbin Subdistrict) which have been heavily impacted by the Ghouta Crisis. The selected areas have a high percentage of returnees; hence the capacity of the available educational services has been overstretched, impacting in the quality of the services, on the level of OSSC and on children at risk of dropping-out. This intervention will provide No Formal Education support to children and youth (5 – 18 y.o), along with rehabilitation of 5 schools (light and hard works depending on technical assessment) and capacity-building for 440 educational personnel, 3100 youth and adult will have access to life skill training and basic literacy and numeracy, increasing the quality of educational provision, reducing school drop-outs and providing pathways for OOSC children to re-enroll in formal education, and creating protective and accessible learning environments for children, their families and communities. This intervention has been identiﬁed in line with the sector priorities and gaps analysis in the selected areas and it is part of the INTERSOS Country Strategy meaning that complementarities with other interventions will be seek. The activities are in line with national (MoE) and international standards and respond to the needs identiﬁed by INTERSOS through multisector assessments conducted. Risks in the design and implementation of the intervention have been identiﬁed along with relevant mitigation measures and a protection mainstreaming approach will be promoted through all the implementation. INTERSOS will set up a multi - level monitoring system combining different project M&E tools, such as training reports, rehabilitation reports, field visit reports, PSS activities report and data collection on specific indicators identified in the PAT (Project Appraisal Tool). The intervention will promote the reinforcement of existing school – based governance bodies like parents and teachers’ associations or promoting their establishment where not existing, increasing their capacity to adopt conflict-sensitive approaches and enable them to address the specific needs of children who experienced violence. Accountability and beneficiaries feedback mechanism will be promoted, accordingly to the context. All target groups will be informed on project activities and specific feedback/complaints mechanism will be set up in the formal/no – formal education settings. Children’s views and meaningful participation will be encouraged and facilitated through the establishment of Child Friendly Feedback Mechanisms (CFFM) in schools (whenever feasible and welcomed by the school management) and in other educational non – formal learning spaces. The children will be able to provide feedback about their learning environment (teachers, recreational activities, educational materials, safe environment) in a safe and confidential way. CFFM foresees four main communication channels, as listed here following: 1) suggestion box, 2) trusted person, 3) monthly visits, 4) interviews.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria left 5 million school-aged children in need of education assistance, 1.8 million of whom are OOSC and 490,000 are in HRA. Infrastructure, supplies, and the capacities of existing educational personnel are severely overstretched, with 1 over 3 schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for other purposes. During two RNA conducted in November ‘19 and January ‘20 in Rural Damascus and Hama governorates, INTERSOS has registered the prevalence of high rate of drop out and OOSC particularly amongst the age group 11 to 16 years old and associated with child labor (agriculture mostly). According to KIs, children and adolescents show clear signs of psychosocial distress including aggressive behavior. Schools’directors reported among the priorities, the lack of teaching personnel, qualified and able to cope with the special needs. Most of the visited schools were partially or totally damaged, surrounded by debris or metal objects; the functioning ones do not guarantee conditions to recover the normal educational activities. Wash facilities in targeted schools are in urgent need of repair to protect children and encourage enrollment of OOSC, especially girls. Moreover, buildings are not disability sensitive, preventing access to students with specific needs. Existing schools have a shortage in school assets such as doors, windows, benches, ceilings have sign of crash and humidity and stairs are damaged or unusable. Learning materials and school kits aren’t available, this is related to destruction and robbery practices during the crisis. Rooms are overcrowded, hosting 40-50 children and desks and benches were often shared between 3-4 children. No formal education (SLP), remedial classes, including basic literacy and numeracy have been identified as a need in most of the targeted localities as a result of dropouts related to unavailability of schooling services during the conflict period.

5 - Implementation of activities

The implementation of activities will be tailored accordingly to the needs. Remedial classes will be provided to in school children in order to support to catch up in some subjects of the national educational curricula. Remedial classes will be provided after school hours in classes/other safe learning spaces according to educational level and age. SLP, catch up programs and NFE will be provided to 6900 OOSC in need to continue learning and progressively allow them to go back to the education system. The identification of OOSC will be done through inclusive outreach activities in the targeted communities. 1000 children in school and OOSC will receive PSS though recreational and awareness sessions, using 1600 kits of recreational materials, done after schools’ hours by INTERSOS and SARC mobile PSS units, as well as 220 adults. The intervention will provide 6900 students kits and stationery in order to create a safer environment and mitigate the drop – out and motivate OOSC in their reintegration efforts. For 3100 adolescents above 15 years old and young adults the intervention will provide basic literacy and numeracy courses and vocational trainings to allow them to enter in the job market. Basic life skills and small business trainings for identified PoCs, youth girls and women, will be organized in order to strengthen cross-cutting competences with the aim of providing new livelihood opportunities. To education actors (5 PTA/TRC) and teachers (440), the intervention will facilitate EiE topics, gender-sensitive inclusive education, PSS, pedagogical skills, classroom management, positive discipline, life skills, citizenship, and CP to support teachers' understanding of and capacity to identify child protection risks and properly refer to specialized care. Finally, the intervention foreseen rehabilitation 5 schools (45 classrooms) oriented to provide insulation, enhancing the security standards, enhancing the hygiene condition of the WASH facilities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The implementation of activities will be tailored on the identified needs. Provision of remedial classes and self-learning programs (SLP) will be provided to boys and girls in need to catch up with core subjects of the curriculum and/or are at risk of dropping out. Remedial classes will be provided to students after school hours in classes according to the students’ grade. SLP activities will enable children to continue learning and prepare for the national exams. Identification of OOSC will be done through inclusive outreach activities in the targeted communities. Identified children will receive structured PSS though awareness and recreational sessions done after schools’ hours by INTERSOS and SARC mobile PSS units. The intervention will provide students and school kits, improving learning environment and mitigate risks of drop-out for In-school children while at the same time supporting and motivating OOSC in their reintegration efforts. For youth and young adults, the intervention will provide basic literacy, numeracy and basic business skills training to allow them to enrol in accelerated learning programmes and/or vocational training. Basic life skills training for identified PoCs, especially youth girls and women, will be organized in order to strengthen cross-cutting competences with the aim of providing new livelihood opportunities. To community and education actors (PTA) and TRC (teachers resource centres), the intervention will facilitate EiE topics, gender-sensitive inclusive education, PSS, pedagogical skills, classroom management, positive discipline, life skills, citizenship education programme and CP to support teachers' understanding of and capacity to identify child protection risks and properly refer to specialized care. Finally, the intervention foreseen rehabilitation 5 selected schools oriented to enhance the insulation of the classrooms, enhancing the security standards, enhancing the hygiene condition of the WASH facilities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: the areas of intervention remain stable and access is safe. There is no change in the selected location and schools of intervention and local education authorities maintain the planned priorities for the activities identified. Schools and the education facilities are accessible for the targeted students for NFE, including remedial classes and SLP. Local communities, stakeholders, community leaders accept new education services made available by the intervention. Beneficiaries can attend the proposed activities because the timing and the frequency is suitable for them. There is no high turnover among teachers, PTA members, TRC members and project staff. Risks: security in some of the targeted communities deteriorate. Due to security reasons, activities foreseen must be rescheduled, suspended or postponed. Major change in acceptance inside local communities, among stakeholders, community leaders towards the NFE activities and new learning patters made available. The prices for materials and items for construction and rehabilitation have high fluctuation. Mitigation measures: INTERSOS will follow up and monitor on the population movement, high level of information sharing. INTERSOS will follow-up on the security context and prompt communicate with relevant stakeholders to reschedule activities in schools. INTERSOS will attend to the sector meetings and having consultation with other relevant partners to monitor any change in the education policies and patterns. INTERSOS will guarantee good and transparent relations with stakeholders, informing them on the planned activities. INTERSOS will monitor the price fluctuation of construction materials in the market and monitoring the progress of construction in selected schools.
