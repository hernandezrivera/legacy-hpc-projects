Project Summary

The Provision of cash and food voucher transfers are intended to support the cost of a nutritious diet, as well as factoring in other household needs to strengthen food security and livelihoods, and prevent the use of negative coping mechanisms. Action Against Hunger is proposing to deliver cash-based assistance,support for income generating activities and livelihoods to households in Yobe State as well as support the strengthening and revival of markets in selected LGA's.

Needs

Nine years of conflict between the Nigerian military and non-state armed groups in northeast Nigeria has taken a heavy toll on the population. Approximately 1,926,784 individuals (364,635 households) remain displaced in North-East Nigeria and the ongoing conflict is the primary reason (94%) for displacement. Borno state, is the most affected and hosts the highest number of IDPs (1,441,635) and in Yobe State there are 137,588 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 24, 2018). As per the IOM DTM Round 24, August 2018, 54% percent of IDPs needing assistance are female, 27% are children under five, 81% are children plus women. The host communities support 91% of the IDPs, placing burdens on households and existing infrastructure that are already stressed. The Livelihoods and Markets Recovery Assessment conducted stresses the need to work through key market stakeholders to support market recovery.Action Against Hunger proposes to continue interventions that increase short-term purchasing power to meet immediate food needs, increase dietary diversity, support the restoration of livelihoods and increase marketing strengthening programming. Action Against Hunger has conducted baseline assessments, LinkNCA and Nutrition assessment was conducted by NBS/Nutrition sector. All of those assessments shows high rate of malnutrition which is exacerbated by the crisis directly in Gujba and Yunusari while indirectly through inflation in Fune, Tarmua and Nangare.The proposed program will cover households that meet the vulnerability criteria in selected LGA's of Yobe State, with cash transfer for food and cash for work initiatives along with IGA (off farm, On-farm and non-farm activities). Action Against hunger proposes an inception phase envisioned to span from February 2018 to the End of June 2018 where a resilience study, a cost of diet and hygiene study and bi-weekly market price monitoring shall be carried out. The outcomes of the inception phase studies shall be the determinant of the nature of social protection activities. Action Against Hunger is proposing conditional and unconditional cash-based transfers to ensure coverage through the predicted crisis period.

Activities

Action Against hunger is proposing conditional and unconditional cash-based transfers and/or IGA (off farm, On-farm and non-farm activities) to ensure coverage through predicted crisis period e.g. the lean season, during periods of conflict and during outbreaks of disease. The objective of the intervention is two types one is directly related to humanitarian situation in Gujba where household will be support through cash voucher based on the food basket. While on the other side the interventions will focus on areas which are not directly and/or recently affected by humanitarian situation but the actions will be via Income generating activity, supporting agriculture activities and market support. The focus will be on the populations that are most vulnerable especially Pregnant and Lactating women. The following activities will be implemented to benefit 11,235 registered house hold (which will include cash vouchers for the focus will be 4,865 HH based on the food basket, while IGA will support families based on the basic needs, and PLW will get support of 5000 NGN each month to have nutritious food for herself and her child. The social safety net support will focus on the first 1000 days (from time of pregnancy till the age of child is 2 years) to 17,000 Pregnant mothers with cash vouchers worth of 5,000 NGN every month. To ensure the accountability to communities Action Against Hunger will establish Complaints response mechanism to ensure transparency and accountability. • Market assessment and value chain analysis • Engagement of local traders and market actors• Sensitize stakeholders relevant Ministries, State and LGA authorities as well as beneficiaries on social protection schemes and the provision of demand driven basic services.• Disburse smart cards with cash/e-voucher to beneficiaries • Capacity building and support to relevant market actors • Participate in coordination meetings and undertake needs assessments• Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring as needed to assess changing dynamics and adjust distribution accordingly • Feedback Mechanism established, monitored and issues resolvedAction Against Hunger actively engage and coordinate with other partners through Food security working group. At the LGA level we already have coordinate with other partners to avoid duplication of efforts and support. Most of the areas we work with other partners to identify the wards, and provide support to the whole ward. The details of our activities up to ward level are shared with FSS on monthly basis. In some of the areas we have humanitarian and early recovery projects in the same LGA, for that we generate database of house hold which can be supported via humanitarian approach and while others get the early recovery initiatives. Complaints response mechanism is established with a view to improve the programming, assisting in understanding the beneficiary and community perceptions, promoting beneficiaries empowerment and assisting in the early detection of problems such a targeting criteria, misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, food diversion and fraud. AAH tries to provide meaningful access to vulnerable household, and we do not discriminate against any groups and it is performed in such a way that they are not considered to be perceived as doing so. We also take in to consideration whether women and men may have different capacities to access cash compared to in kind. this is ensured by providing separate sitting areas access points along with discussion and agreement with community leaders which also has women participation. As mentioned earlier AAH has established the system of CRM (Complaints Response Mechanism) which identifies the key areas which challenges. As part of Accountability to Affected Populations, Action Against Hunger has already put in place steps to ensure that their participation is considered in actions e.g. Community leaders forms the committee to identify the area most affected.
