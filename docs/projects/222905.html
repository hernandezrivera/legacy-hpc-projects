Project Summary

The project will apply an integrated approach to promote skills & livelihood development among Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar, while also addressing the health & protection challenges that are both exacerbated by, and contribute to, the lack of skills and livelihood opportunities.
Intervention 1: Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL):
Young people will have access to UNESCO's Accelerated Adult Learning (AAL) package within 7 youth centers and Community-based learning centers. Capitalizing on its organizational technical expertise, UNFPA will supplement the package by GBV and SRH concepts into the modules and materials, thereby providing multiple layers of benefit to the program participants to promote their protection, health, and livelihood. The package will integrate the modules from existing life skills education programs already being implemented by UNFPA in Cox’s Bazar. These programs - i.e. the Girl Shine Programme for adolescent girls & young women; the Champions of Change program for adolescent boys & young men; and the Girl Shine Caregiver Program for male and female caregivers - equip participants with skills & knowledge on gender equality, GBV, SRHR, menstrual health & management, and other essential social and emotional skills. The program will run for 6 months per cohort and the graduates will receive graduation kits to enable them to continue practicing their skills after completing the program. The program will also provide referrals to specialized health and GBV facilities, ensuring participants have access to essential services, including gender-based violence support and SRH care, when needed.
Intervention 2: Non-Formal Technical Training:
Women and men will have access to non-formal technical training through 9 Women-Led Community Centers (WLCC) and a Multi-Purpose Center for men & boys in refugee camps and host communities. These are innovative facilities established to contribute to advancing gender equality and social norms change. They offer training to women & men of all ages, such as tailoring and jute production including essential numeracy and literacy, for a duration of 150 hrs in 3 months. Such activities engage men in training usually intended for women, thus breaking gender stereotypes and barriers.
UNFPA plans to diversify the training based on the needs and wishes expressed by women and men, in line with the Skill Development Framework. The activities are an entry point to offer psychosocial support, life skills & GBV awareness, fostering psychological wellbeing, promoting self-reliance and contributing to household incomes, and mitigating GBV risks.
In addition, UNFPA plans to scale-up the non-formal technical training in 20 WFS which are currently providing unstructured programs. Through WFS, women will also have access to integrated & age-responsive GBV/SRH services including case management, MHPSS, life skills, CMR, voluntary family planning, STI syndromic treatment and referrals.
Integrated Service Delivery:
The project will align with rationalization principles. GBV and SRH services and referrals will be made available. Beneficiary selection will ensure that not only are the most vulnerable groups targeted, but that the beneficiaries of the programme are able to access and avail of comprehensive services that will improve their overall quality of life.
The program has four target population groups:
1. Youth (18 to 24)
2. Vulnerable households and/or individuals in camps, including new arrivals, pregnant and lactating young women and girls, young caregivers of adolescents and children, members of female- and youth-led households.
3. Vulnerable adult women, with a focus on women of reproductive age, from particularly vulnerable households, i.e. female-headed, women & men with disabilities, young and first-time mothers, older women, large households.
4. Referred individuals and households who have been targeted by other interventions on SRHR, GBV, etc.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The 2024 ISNA indicated that only 15% of households have members who can access skills training. Among those, women represent just 8% of total engagement. This indicates a significant gender gap in access to skill development opportunities, with 46% citing insufficient training facilities as the reason for not attending skills training programs.The assessment also revealed that 94% of households rely on humanitarian aid as their main source of support, corroborating findings from the REVA-7 Report (July 2024) that indicated 97% of Rohingya households and 69% of affected host communities in Cox’s Bazar were moderately to highly vulnerable, meaning they are unable to meet their essential needs without humanitarian assistance. Worsening the situation, 32% of households reported that their debt status is increasing, further limiting their ability to access basic necessities and highlighting the urgent need for sustained humanitarian support and livelihood opportunities. FGDs conducted by UNFPA in the camps in October, 2024 continued to highlight an unmet need for structured skill development and provision of basic literacy and numeracy. Moreover, there was a request for additional skill development programs for male participants, including market linkages and opportunities. While from 2023 to 2024 food insecurity among refugees decreased, 73% of households reported buying food on credit, while 14% reduced expenditure on health, female hygiene products and education to meet basic needs (ISNA 2024). Lack of livelihood opportunities may increase stress and exacerbate existing patterns of GBV and domestic violence. In FGDs conducted by UNFPA in camps in 2024, both women and men shared appreciation as the WLCC provided them with skills in handicrafts and tailoring, and with financial support for their families, while also contributing to reducing GBV. Women asked for opportunities for further skills training for income generation, thus reducing financial pressure that exacerbates GBV risks in their homes. This suggests a need to enhance the provision of non-formal technical training for self-reliance of the refugees, GBV risk mitigation and psychosocial support for both women and men. Engaging men and boys in these efforts can help reduce stigma around seeking GBV support and foster a more supportive environment for survivors. While more livelihood and skills development interventions are being introduced under the coordination of the LSDS, 85% of the Rohingya households could not be targeted with the vocational training such as literacy, numeracy, and life skills which are significantly lacking in the community. This is especially true for youth as 97% of Rohingya youth are not enrolled in education as of 2024, and also facing challenges to access livelihood opportunities, creating a gap between their potential and the available resources. Finally, as per UNFPA FGDs, safety concerns remain a key issue in the camps. Men & boys are concerned with physical violence, forced recruitment, and exploitation by organized groups, as well as drug and human trafficking. For women & girls, concerns include GBV, domestic violence, exploitation, and GBV threats from organized criminal groups, which are often systemic and gender-specific. Organized criminal groups are also using GBV against women and girls as a way to coerce their male family members to join the groups. Providing non-formal technical training to women and men, particularly to young people, in safe spaces such as the WLCC, MPC, YC and WFS, is a way to provide healthy alternatives, positive coping mechanisms and psychosocial support in the midst of the safety concerns.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project promotes the protection of affected populations and prioritizes safety, dignity and rights of young people. The principle of "do no harm" is incorporated throughout the project cycle, and is applied to facilities, services, and outreach activities. Through the project, UNFPA will continue to ensure that protection mechanisms and principles are in place by applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits & related IASC protection standards across UNFPA-supported facilities and services. The non-formal technical training for both refugees and host communities will be provided in well-established facilities for GBV prevention and response. In these facilities, the affected population has access to a wide range of comprehensive and integrated GBV/SRH services, including clear referral pathways, updated twice a month. Referral pathways include specialized protection-related services for legal assistance, safe shelters and advocacy. UNFPA’s protection mainstreaming strategy will be monitored through regular needs and vulnerability assessments and participatory discussions with the target populations. The results of these assessments will be used to further support the special needs of vulnerable groups, such as women and girls, young people, persons with disabilities, gender diverse population, members of female-headed and youth-headed households, among other groups. Finally, UNFPA will continue to promote the engagement of the affected populations through community engagement and other awareness-raising efforts on protection topics and referral mechanisms. Within the heightened protection and GBV risks in the camps for women and girls, and youth , UNFPA will continue to coordinate closely with the GBVSS and the Protection Sector as a current key member of both SAGs, and contribute to the Peace & Security Campaign.

2.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA co-chairs the Youth Working Group (YWG) under the inter-agency coordination mechanism to mainstream the concerns of young people across sectors. UNFPA both adheres to and supports the capacity development of partners on the IASC Guidelines on Working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises to provide age-responsive and adolescent-friendly services. UNFPA has a repertoire of resources, including toolkits and checklists, on disability inclusion that it implements in its planning, implementation and monitoring mechanisms through the “twin track” approach. All facilities utilize the Washington Group Questionnaire across facilities to identify persons with disabilities. The non-formal technical training provided in the WLCC and WFS include persons with disabilities (4.1% current reach out of total, 5% target in 2025). UNFPA also facilitates the participation of people with disabilities and young people in its regular feedback and consultation mechanisms to ensure that their voices are heard throughout the project life cycle. When possible, the project will hire community teachers who are refugees and prioritize the recruitment of staff with appropriate language, gender, age and cultural competencies, including staff with disabilities, to work with Rohingya populations. UNFPA gathers Sex Age Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) to better understand the service uptake among different groups and to ensure programmatic improvements are made.

UNFPA embeds a Human Rights Based Approach across its programming, ensuring participation, accountability, non-discrimination, and empowerment. To ensure inclusive community engagement, UNFPA engages with diverse community members, including persons with disabilities and gender-diverse populations, from the project design phase through the implementation, monitoring and evaluation phases, including in preparation for JRP using the following tools:1. Client satisfaction surveys for UNFPA-supported facilities and activities.2. In the facilities, visibility is given to hotlines such as UNHCR and Alapon.5. Close collaboration with IPs to regularly collect and utilize community feedback.In alignment with UNFPA’s AAP commitment, we are in the process of recruiting an International Specialist. Furthermore, a Social and Environmental Safeguarding personnel worked with UNFPA units and partners in 2024 to adhere to the organization's Social and Environmental Safeguards policies and procedures.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA upholds a zero-tolerance policy towards Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment, extending this commitment across organizational levels and stakeholders. The agency prioritizes SEA prevention and provides immediate assistance to victims, collaborating with partners to foster a safe workplace. Since 2017, UNFPA has been an active member of the ISCG PSEA network in Cox's Bazar appointing PSEA Focal Points to lead the efforts in preventing SEA. PSEA training is mandatory for all UNFPA personnel, emphasizing reporting wrongdoings and survivor-centered support. The Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) facilitates reporting of misconduct through an online platform and hotline that is accessible to all. PSEA focal points are tasked with receiving complaints and ensuring rapid reporting to the OAIS and the Country Representative. The organization has a retaliation policy in place to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers from any form of retaliation. UNFPA is dedicated to enhancing staff capacity on SEA issues and empowering partners, service providers and volunteers to raise awareness within Rohingya and host communities. Community-based awareness programs utilize diverse resources to inform communities about policy. Regular monitoring ensures compliance with PSEA policies across all supported facilities, complemented by quarterly meetings among UNFPA and Partners to address challenges and strategize. Periodic capacity assessments are conducted to support partners in upholding necessary PSEA protocols. To align with global standards in SEAH investigation, UNFPA partners participate in specialized training organized by the Network. Certified personnel may join SEA investigation units of other organizations. . UNFPA contributes to technical groups within the PSEA Network, including: PSEA Communication with Community Task Team, Technical Review Team for SEAH Investigation ToR, Task Team for Drafting the Sexual Harassment Law.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure full adherence to DRR/DRM standards, including the Government of Bangladesh’s National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation, and the EEN Climate Action Strategy (2023-2025) for the Rohingya response. The UNFPA-led GBVSS contributed to revise the Emergency Preparedness & Response Protection Plan (EPRP), which includes preparedness planning with 57 GBVSS partners & identifying GBV focal points for PERU. UNFPA partners are actively engaged & part of PERU and received training by UNFPA.UNFPA Community Mobilizers are trained to support the response to disaster-related emergencies to support communities in the acute and post-disaster phases. Basic DRR information is also shared with the beneficiaries of UNFPA’s interventions such as its Life Skills Education program, while UNFPA-supported facilities are used as temporary shelter during emergencies. UNFPA also prepositions and stores customized Dignity Kits and emergency Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) Kits that are quickly distributed to its project beneficiaries during emergencies to reduce the vulnerability of affected women and girls. Finally, UNFPA will continue to explore and adopt implementation modalities based on contemporary understanding and best practices in DRR, climate change adaptation, and the application of circular economic models. UNFPA actively promotes eco-friendly practices by discouraging the use of plastic and polythene banners. Solar panels are installed in all the safe spaces for women and girls, and adolescents and youth, their construction utilizes transparent roofing to ensure ample sunlight thus helping reduce reliance on fossil fuels.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

UNFPA provides lifesaving SRH/GBV services to the affected population and has in place strong measures to ensure the continuity of the provision of services at all times, while taking into account staff security and safety. UNFPA has an updated business continuity plan, latest updated in September 2024, to ensure the smooth continuity of UNFPA operations at all times. All UNFPA-managed facilities have in place preparedness measures for fire response, including fire extinguishers and other emergency fire tools. Emergency exits are available in the majority of facilities - whenever the space makes it possible - to ensure the safety of the staff and beneficiaries in case of emergency. UNFPA facilities can also be used as shelters in case of natural or human-made emergencies that require the population to leave their own shelters. Due to the restrictions on access to the WFS, those spaces in particular host women and girls, and boy children 5 years of age or under.Volunteers update the referral pathways every 2 weeks, to ensure that all contact details of service providers are updated as much as possible to ensure quick action in case of emergency referrals. Please also refer further to the “Climate change adaptation & mitigation” Section which highlights some preparedness and response measures adopted by UNFPA, which overlap with this section as well. For emergency funding, UNFPA relies on the provision of CERF funds as part of the UN System to respond to time-critical SRH and GBV needs.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project follows UNFPA’s global strategy for adolescents and youth, placing young people at the heart of its programs and embracing the rich diversity of all adolescents (aged 10 to 19) and youth (aged 15 to 24). It recognizes the varied needs & aspirations of this demographic and is dedicated to addressing the inequities they face. Designed in accordance with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on working with and for young people in humanitarian and protracted crises. The project involves youth throughout the humanitarian response process—from the initial design stage to implementation, monitoring, & evaluation. This engagement includes: Conducting activities in youth-responsive facilities throughout UNFPA's areas of intervention.Hold consultations with young people – grouped by age and sex of participants – to identify needs and barriers to education.Build links in the response plan between learning and future employment and livelihood activities.Give young people opportunities for employment as volunteers, mentors and trainers,etc., within livelihood n and training programmes.Establish mechanisms for safe meaningful engagement and enable youth participation in program design, implementation, and evaluation, ensuring their voices shape decisions through capacity building on leadership, life skills, advocacy and programme implementation. As co-lead of the Youth Working Group (YWG) within the inter-agency coordination mechanism, UNFPA ensures that the project aligns with best practices for coordinated programming aimed at Rohingya and Bangladeshi host community youth. The YWG is committed to advocating for the youth agenda and integrating the needs and capacities of young people at both strategic and operational levels across various sectors of the humanitarian response. It promotes the implementation of IASC Guidelines in relevant sectors and prioritizes meaningful youth participation in its activities and decision-making processes.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project supports self-reliance/resilience through: 1)Incentives for 40 community volunteers in WLCC (24 camps, 16 hosts) and 241 youth program volunteers (156 camps, 85 hosts). UNFPA will provide training to the volunteers to enhance their skills to provide GBV-related awareness raising sessions and to increase their engagement in the management of the facilities. 2) Support of Women Support Groups, Community Watch Groups and Self-Help Groups, to boost community leadership & social cohesion. 3) Provision of a wide range of self-reliance skills, including technical & life skills, as part of the psychosocial support activities provided to women & men, as articulated in this project. 4) Plans to open a display corner in Ukhiya to generate income for women participating in self-reliance, in line with local regulations.5) Functional literacy programs to improve refugees' ability to access services, manage livelihoods, and reduce aid dependency.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

The UNFPA project aligns with Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Plan, which highlights a need for holistic and inclusive development targeting both the Rohingya refugee and host communities. The document stresses that the district is affected by both humanitarian and development challenges and prioritizes: i) strengthening the economic resilience, ii) improving human capital and productivity, iii) promote social cohesion and emphasized that all the initiatives will be gender inclusive, addressing the needs of children, youth and elderly, and gender based violence concerns, which are fully in alignment with what have been proposed in the proposal. The Plan is also committed to contribute to SDG5 “Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls”.
