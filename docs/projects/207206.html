Project Summary

The strategic focus of Save the Children (SC) is to promote quality and durable solutions to essential WASH services. SC will continue the existing WASH services in camps 17, 20, 25, and 27 that will be safe, inclusive, and resilient services by latrines, bathing, tube wells, water supply, and waste management facilities functional by adequate repair, maintenance, improvement, and renovation. Water quality in households will be prioritized as the critical stage of the safe water chain, and provisions made for gender-specific safe WASH spaces. SC will enhance the quality of services by ensuring quality benchmarks and bi-weekly functional monitoring to ensure the safety, privacy, and specific needs of vulnerable members of the community. It will ensure dignified and improved access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene management and practice. WASH program focuses on a cost-efficient participatory program delivery to boost emergent community ownership and open new avenues of program sustainability. The program will ensure conflict-sensitive, sustainable, and gender-segregated WASH facilities for the Rohingya camps and adjacent host communities. Environment-friendly fecal sludge and solid waste management will also be considered a priority issue. SC will manage solid waste and fecal sludge to prevent negative environmental impacts through robust planning, risk mitigation, and monitoring that will prevent any health hazards and outbreaks. SC will operate green fecal sludge treatment plants to manage the sludge of latrine pits and septic tanks. As a part of an innovative approach and to make the solution environment-friendly, instead of using any chemicals SCI applies biological conversion, physical filtration, and absorption with anaerobic processes and converts wastewater as safer for vegetable cultivation. In addition, garbage disposed of in communal waste pits will regularly be collected to produce composting fertilizer from organic waste. Moreover, the project will prioritize to use of solar power rather than fuel power for operating WASH facilities. SC will be rationing safer water and compost fertilizer for vegetable gardening after the greenish treatment of the community human sludge and solid waste that will be a part of a 360-degree circular economy which will contribute to three zero theories i.e., zero unemployment, zero carbon emission, and zero poverty. SC will conduct regular hygiene promotion sessions for all. However special sessions will conduct for children and adolescent girls. SC provided menstrual hygiene management sessions to train them on how to make reusable sanitary pads. SCI continues hygiene promotion awareness sessions for children through demonstration, role play, storytelling, singing songs, etc. Furthermore, a bi-weekly cleaning campaign will be conducted led by the WASH committees, hygiene promotion volunteers, and nonpaid youth volunteers who will mainly work with local resident groups.Different groups like children, adolescent girls, WASH committees, and non-paid youth volunteers will engage in new roles for bringing positive changes in the community such as cleaning campaigns, WASH facilities cleaning, garbage pickup, and drain cleaning, which are mainly conducted by hygiene promotion volunteers. The program will cover hygiene promotional activities for host communities inside the camp and adjacent to the camp, especially in schools. Save the Children strictly follows the unified design and climate-resilient infrastructures and keeps a contingency plan and stockpiling to respond immediately to any disasters. We reflect on gender, age, disability, and accountability, with emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters, public health risks, and disease outbreaks. SC gives special attention to the person with disabilities by making inclusive WASH facilities. All the activities will be implemented in close coordination with WASH sector and government agencies.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Save the Children currently serving 17,271 participants to ensure access to and use safe water, sanitation facilities as well as adopting hygiene practice in camp 17, 20, 25, 27 and adjacent host community. Improved water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) facilities in water-scarce areas are one of the most important barriers to improving the standards of people's lives. In the dry season of each year, there is a water crisis happening in Teknaf areas which is a challenge since there is limited reliable access to surface or groundwater sources. Surface water is available during the monsoons while groundwater is not suitable for extraction in large volumes since the aquifer has saltwater, being close to the sea. To get sufficient safe water one additional borehole is required at camp 27 as there two boreholes are workable out of 3. This is a solution that has a lot of potential since it is sustainable and improves the well-being of the communities. Fecal sludge and solid waste management is very much needed to stop the spreading of sludge waste, achieve a sustainable solution to ensure a safe water supply in the camp, mitigate the risk of disease outbreaks, and keep environment-friendly settings in densely populated camps. In the rainy season, several WASH facilities are damaged due to heavy rainfall which needs to be functional in a short time as well as an additional budget. On the other hand, adding the emergency need immediate intervention due to natural and man-made disaster in the camps. Hygiene promotion behavior change intervention, considering equitable access for vulnerable people (children, people with disabilities, PLW (Pregnant and Lactating Women, old aged), durable solutions like water supply systems/network using renewable energy, fecal sludge management unit, female-friendly sanitation spaces, child participation in planning and monitoring program, and provision for menstrual hygiene management. 12% of Female-Headed Households (FDMNS) and 26% of households’ overall report inadequate water for domestic tasks (WASH Sector Strategy for JRP, 2023). Moreover, older individuals and persons with disabilities face additional hurdles, including insufficient attention to the incontinence challenges of older people, the distant placement of WASH facilities, and overall inaccessibility. Women and girls encounter significant challenges when utilizing overcrowded communal facilities and unhygienic WASH facilities. The deficiencies extend to the lack of privacy in communal spaces during the day, and heightened concerns about harassment and violence at night due to inadequate lighting, limited gender-segregated toilets, and insufficient safety measures. 38% of female members feel unsafe using WASH facilities that are not separated by gender and are not well-lighted at night. To avoid being seen bathing and defecating, women reportedly wash and defecate inside their shelters, restrict food, and water intake, and restrict movement during their menstrual period. 14% of refugees reported not having enough water to meet domestic needs and on average 51 persons share one bathing unit due to lack of space. Only 62% of women and girls access MHM kits as per standards and 37% of female refugees reported not having received menstrual hygiene kits at least one time over the last six months. There is a challenge to keep functioning WASH facilities in camps as most of the facilities were constructed in 2017 and 2018 that’s why gradually decreased their strength and increased the requirement for repair and maintenance. Alongside, cleaning campaigns with hygiene promotional activities need to continue to induce behavioral changes and better hygiene practices. As per the WASH sector strategy, SC is responsible for all the population in the assigned camps 17, 20, 25, and 27. Prioritize community engagement in site selection, and implementation of interventions to grow community ownership and sustainability of program.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project proposes to continue the existing WASH services at SCI’s assigned areas [four camps of Teknaf and Ukhia Upazila’s as per transition mapping of the WASH sector]. However, will continue the discussion with other actors and the Area Focal Agency to find out a more efficient way of rationalization process. Save the Children will strengthen the coordination and productive relationships with the government authority, AFA, INGO, and NNGO actors in camps and the host communities. SC will continue to work closely with the Department of Public Health Engineering (DPHE), the WASH sector, and the technical working groups to ensure adequate design and implementation of WASH infrastructures and software interventions aimed at Rohingya populations.SC will prioritize field-level coordination with government authority (CIC, Upazila administration) site management, WASH focal, and local government. SC will assess the WASH infrastructure regular basis to identify the non-functional areas of improvement and needs as well as take initiatives to immediately repair them to ensure safe water, sanitation, waste management, and promotional activities. The project aims to ensure quality, regular, sufficient, equitable, and dignified access to WASH services for all women, men, girls, and boys, inclusive of all age groups and persons with disabilities, in the Rohingya refugee camps and targeted host communities and build their resilience through training and capacity development. Save the Children will enhance community engagement in all stages of project activities through the WASH committee, user groups, nonpaid youth volunteers, adolescent girls’ groups, and children's groups to reduce SCI role and increase community role in program operation, Safe programming, Cost-effective program delivery and overall Opening the avenue of program sustainability.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

WASH in communities often overlooks gender-related needs, resulting in poor basic services and increased risks for women and girls. SC believes it is important to address the needs of girls, women, children, the disabled, and other vulnerable groups in preventing WASH, Dengue, and other infections in healthcare settings. Save the Children requires all staff and volunteers involved in the project to take pieces of training on child safeguarding and discuss preventive measures. If cases arise, they are reported in accordance with the guidelines and privately addressed by the person in charge. To this end, safeguards will be integrated into all phases of the project to ensure that the needs, risks, and threats of women, girls, and other vulnerable groups are considered and addressed. All ‘Save the Children Staff and Visitors receive mandatory ‘Safety and Security’ orientation training by the Safety and Security Manager on the first day of their office. In the Cox’s Bazar Area Office, the security manager develops the context-specific security plan and SOP. The Security Manager is responsible for the safety of the field staff. S/he coordinates and collects security information from ISCG, government agencies, military, Law Enforcing Authority (LEA), and other organizations, monitors and analyzes the security situation, and then shares the synthesized information with the management and disseminates information and advisory to the field staff on ‘need to know’ basis. SC will establish a useful feedback and complaints mechanism for all users including children and adults, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries, to collect feedback and modify the program as needed. On the other hand, SC will have a strong linkage with safeguarding and protection staff to address any protection issues. Safeguarding and gender considerations will be mainstreamed across all phases of activities under the project.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The initiative aims to meticulously identify vulnerable groups, including individuals with disabilities, older persons, and adolescent girls, utilizing established tools such as the Washington Group questions within the designated project areas. Furthermore, it will conduct a comprehensive assessment of safety and accessibility barriers that hinder these at-risk groups from accessing WASH facilities. Notably, active involvement of the affected individuals will be integral to this consultation process.The resulting insights from these assessments will inform the development of targeted WASH services, aligning with sector standards to address the specific challenges faced by these groups. A key focus will be on prioritizing the construction of segregated wash facilities within camps, designed to cater to the distinct needs of different genders, will coordinate with site management for solar support, thereby contributing significantly to the mitigation of abuses.The project will extend guidance to participants on the proper and hygienic utilization of WASH facilities, leveraging robust community engagement efforts. Additionally, an essential component involves providing training to adolescent girls on pad production, empowering them to independently maintain hygiene. Equipping women and girls with skills in homestead gardening will further fortify their resilience.Recognizing the critical role of community mobilizers and staff, the project will ensure their training in gender and inclusion. Moreover, the initiative will actively promote the participation of women, girls, older individuals, and those with disabilities in WASH committees.To overcome identified obstacles, the project will allocate resources to addressing the unique challenges faced by these vulnerable groups. We will coordinate with pertinent platforms, such as site management and the energy and environment sector, capable of providing technical support in addressing these challenges.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

As a part of accountability systems, Save the Children has adopted a robust Feedback and Reporting Mechanism (FRM). Feedback and reporting channels are used as a means of accountability and to maintain community dialogue and understanding of beliefs, practices, and behavior. For this project, accountability mechanisms will be applied to ensure that feedbacks are actively collected, whilst ensuring the safety of program participants, stakeholders, partners, and staff. Save the Children believes that accountability not only gives voice of children and communities but also creates an opportunity for community members to influence relevant decisions affecting them, and whether and how Save the Children work with them. In practice, accountability covers – (a) regular, timely and accessible information sharing, (b) participation – discussing, deciding, and working together, and (c) proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints, (d) monitoring, evaluation and learning, and (e) building staff competencies for accountable programming. Specific for this project a comprehensive information sharing plan for Feedback and Reporting Mechanism (FRM) will be developed and mobilize the community to ensure their participation as part of ownership development. Usually, feedbacks received by Save the Children are processed, analyzed, and referred to relevant sectors on a quarterly basis and key findings share with sector-level field staff to close the feedback loop on an individual/community level, as feasible. Save the Children will support accountability mechanisms (16 mechanism for Adult and Children as, community meetings, exit interviews, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation, child friendly mechanism and post distribution monitoring) that are accessible to even older persons and people with disabilities, as an inclusive platform for community members to raise their feedbacks in a safe and dignified manner.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Save the Children has an integrated contingency plan for all sectors with the WASH sector to respond to disasters and regularly conduct training and simulation for the Emergency Response Team. Save the Children also has the preparedness actions fixed and agreed upon by relevant sectors/functions such as Finance, HR, Admin & logistics, IT, resource mobilization, safety & and security, Media Comms, etc. We keep and maintain stockpiling to ensure emergency response and provide WASH support in any emergency situation like cyclone, landslide, earthquake, flash flood, monsoon flood, and tidal surge [e.g., construction materials, Water treatment plant, aqua tab, hygiene, kitchen kit, cloths, etc]. As part of emergency preparedness, we conducted training for volunteers and committees in camps. In designing the response interventions, SC considers the risks and vulnerabilities at the local level adopts do-no-harm principles, and considers environmental compliance. Save the Children has a separate humanitarian sector for preparedness, early action, and disaster response. WASH sector of Save the Children under the roof of the humanitarian sector.“ SCI staff receives regular ERT (Emergency Response Training ) and attends EPP workshop (Emergency Preparedness Planning) to continue our readiness to respond any crisis and maintain quality with set standards”.
