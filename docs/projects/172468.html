Project Summary

The provision of Sexual and Reproductive Health services is essential in humanitarian setting, it is an effective approach for preventing deaths, diseases, and disabilities associated and resulted from unintended pregnancy, obstetric complications, sexual and other forms of gender-based violence, HIV infection, and a range of reproductive disorders. UNFPA jointly with partners, will ensure the provision of comprehensive SRH services in humanitarian settings, with utmost consideration to the COVID19 prevention and response measures.
The intervention primarily targets women, girls in reproductive age, youth and adolescents, and men and boys. Sudan HNO 2021 indicates total people in need at 8.1 million, health sector target is 5.7 million. UNFPA will target 1.6 million with more than 80% are women and girls in reproductive age. The geographical coverage follows the HNO identified locations with severity ranking three and above.
All SRHR intervention will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant partners. The planned interventions prioritize and aim reducing SRH mortalities and morbidities through: timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery services, timely referral to lifesaving services, support the availability of Family Planning services, improving Clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV, availing HIV and STIs prevention and response, improving Antenatal and postnatal care, Post abortion care, and other SRH services. These will be achieved through strengthening the capacity of SRH care providers with emphases of midwifery services, timely availability of lifesaving SRHR supplies, timely deployment of temporary clinics and mobile clinics, supporting the referral system, and demand creation through awareness raising and educational activities. Maternal Morbidities such as Obstetric fistula will be given especial attention by UNFPA.
The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence.

1 - Needs

The Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, indicates 8.1 million individuals whom are expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance and basic health service in Sudan. Of whom 1,944,000 are women at reproductive age. Reference made to MISP calculator 2019; total of 258,107 pregnant women are estimated to be in need for access to antenatal care, support for safe vaginal delivery, and postnatal care services. Around 77,432 pregnant women are estimated to either experience obstetric complication or require caesarean section. It is also estimated that total number of 42,768 cases of sexual violence who will seek care during 2021.Sudan is characterized with high maternal mortality rate (295 per 100,000 life birth as per 2017 UN estimates). In reference to the 2019 Maternal death surveillance report, it identified the major causes of maternal deaths. The most prevalent cause was Obstetric hemorrhage (40.4%), followed by hypertensive disorders (14.3%), then sepsis (11.2%). Moreover, it confirmed that (64.1%) of maternal deaths had no ANC before, and (73.1%) were attributed to limited accessibility (delay at home, or delay in reaching health facility). As indicated in the HRP 2021, the humanitarian crisis in Sudan negatively impacts the already fragile health systems’ capacity to provide basic health services. The health system hardly handles the multiple emergencies affecting the country. During 2020, the cost of healthcare increased by 90% due to inflation. The availability of essential medicines ranging between 15% and 50%. Significant gap in coverage with Basic emergency neonatal and obstetric care services is observed, with less than 37% of the need. States with severe gaps in EmONC availability against the recommended numbers. Gaps in provision of CMR services is identified nationwide through the regular CMR mapping, at both supplies, and trained cadres, the latest mapping indicated less than 20% coverage. Sudan remains prone to various risks and hazards such as floods, localized armed clashes and inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks and hazards, disease outbreaks, food security, malnutrition and protection risks. The deteriorating economic status worsened the situation. Annual flooding affects people, during 2020, heavy rains and flash floods affected 557000 individuals in 17 out of 18 states. An assessment conducted by UNFPA indicated 57 health facilities were partially destroyed by floods and heavy rains. More than 18,000 individuals tested positive for COVID19 until November 30th, 2020. Case fatality rate around 7%. The indirect impact of COVID19 on WGRA’s health is high. The initial suspension of provision of essential PHC services such as ANC was attributed to the observed increase in maternal death, the temporary suspension of PHC services resulted in limited capacity for detecting high risk pregnancies. The implication of lock down was clear on access to public transportation hence access to care. The number of functioning referral facilities was also reduced due to frequent stock-out of lifesaving SRHR supplies, and drop-out of care providers who contracted COVID19. Around 1.3 million positive Malaria cases were diagnosed during 2020 until September, pregnant women and U5 children are identified as at high at risk. Viral hemorrhagic fevers cases reached 2,863 affecting mainly northern and eastern states, as well as Darfur states.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The planned activities include: 1. Support the SRH coordination mechanism at states and national levels to ensure the coordinated provision and expansion of SRH response 2. Strengthening national/ states contingency and preparedness planning focusing on Minimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up to comprehensive SRH. 3. Capacity building of health care providers through formal training, supportive supervision, on-the-job training and mentorship on relevant SRH topics including EMONC, FP, STI management, Clinical Management of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc. 4. Support referral system through enhancement of locally initiated system to all emergency obstetric cases. 5. Rehabilitation and equipping of health facilities including delivery rooms, maternity wards, blood transfusion unit and theaters. 6. Support the functionality of Family Planning model centres within selected health facilities inside the camps. 7. Establishment of integrated SRH temporary and mobile teams at states with fragile context, including procurement and deployment of mobile/temporary clinics/hospitals/PHC. 8. Procurement and delivery of emergency reproductive health kits, micronutrients, equipment and supplies. 9. Support Obstetric Fistula treatment in states with high humanitarian burden. 10. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior. 11. Address Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specific needs and vulnerabilities in humanitarian settings. 12. Conduct evidence based researches and studies on SRH.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNFPA aims to make a positive contribution in the area of environmental impactmitigation. It’s work on sexual and reproductive health helps promote resilience amongvulnerable populations. Access to voluntary family planning, maternal health care andquality education are key ways to empower vulnerable women and young people, makingthem better equipped to prepare for and respond to environmental -related crises. At theprocurement, level the organization works with suppliers (International and nationallevels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions. Manufacturers workingwith UNFPA have active and documented waste-water programmes and have agreed toreduce raw material usage. UNFPA also produced and disseminates a guide for safedisposal and management of unused unwanted commodities. Throughout the project,planning and implementation process UNFPA closely monitors and set the mitigationmeasures that needed to avoid, reduce or compensate for environmental impacts toenhance the environmental and community benefits of the proposal.

Accountability to beneficiaries: UNFPA and theactors will ensure that the affected individuals and communities are engaged in themeasurement of the quality of interventions serving them, including mechanisms forsuggestions and complaints from the population. 4.
