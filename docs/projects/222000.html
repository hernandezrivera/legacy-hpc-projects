Project Summary

Mission Trust Aid will implement this project in an inter-sectoral way and coordination with relevant clusters, recognizing that schools are platforms through which WASH, nutrition, hygiene, health, child protection, and psycho-social issues can be addressed and provided to 17496 learners, 142 teachers, 8 PTA/SMCs in the two counties of Fangak, and Nasir in Jonglei and Upper Nile states respectively. There will be establishment of 1 temporary learning space to create a conducive and protective learning environment for the learners. 142 (65 female and 77 male) teachers will be trained in education pedagogy. To improve learning, this project aims to distribute 350 kits of learning and recreational materials, and 47 Early Child Development kits. With the current gaps in sanitation facilities in schools, MTA plans to construct or rehabilitate 2 restrooms that are gender sensitive while taking into consideration the needs of children with disability to create a conducive learning environment, MTA will also access some add-on slabs from WASH Cluster and or UNICEF to provide disability friendly latrine stance within the school. These DFLs will be positioned in an accessible location for disadvantaged children within the targeted schools. All Latrines constructed will be equipped with hand washing stations and joint establishment of school hygiene/peace clubs to improve children and environmental hygiene. MTA will also ensure Child Protection integration within the schools aimed at improving learning services, reducing the need for learning spaces for children, and facilitating access to learning materials, to 4817 vulnerable children in Jonglei and Upper Nile States (Fangak, and Nasir counties) South Sudan. Internally Displaced People (IDPs), host communities, and returnees in these proposed hard-to-reach locations will be provided with education and Child protection services. The intervention will work with school stakeholders, including the County Education Departments (CED) and functional community structures (PTAs, SMCs, Boma and Payam leaders, etc.), focusing on developing skills in the management of learning infrastructures.
These Actions are in line with the Education Cluster CO1: Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level, and need, CO2: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle, CO3: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response.

1. Needs

According to UNICEF, in 2024, South Sudan faces profound humanitarian crises, compounded by the returnee and refugee influx from the Sudan conflict. Urgent assistance is needed for nine million people, including 4.9 million children, 2.2 million women, and 1.4 million individuals with disabilities. Conflict, insecurity, epidemic outbreaks, protracted flooding, heatwaves, and the destabilizing impact of the Sudan conflict on South Sudan's economy are rapidly exacerbating vulnerabilities, especially among children. According to UNICEF South Sudan will face a complex year in 2025, with growing humanitarian needs, hyperinflation, and significant aid cuts. The political environment is fragile, with attention focused on fulfilling the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (2018) and hosting the country's first elections in December. Nine million people, including 4.9 million children will require humanitarian assistance, 15 percent of whom have a disability. Political instability, violence, widespread flooding, and disease outbreaks are compounding the complex, chronic needs of the most vulnerable people, especially children. Livelihoods are severely impacted and access to education, nutrition, water, sanitation, and health services is hampered. Children are at a high risk of abuse, violence, exploitation, and psychosocial distress; and 2.8 million children (52 percent girls) are out of school. Women and children are the most vulnerable to gender-based violence.South Sudan is home to a displacement crisis with 2 million people internally displaced. Further, conflict in neighboring Sudan has exacerbated the situation. From April 2023 to 27 March 2024, a total of 622,714 individuals (including 161,699 girls and 160,425 boys) crossed into South Sudan in immediate need of assistance and protection. Displacements have further strained existing services; 46 percent of health facilities are moderately operational, and less than 10 percent of the population has access to improved sanitation. These conditions increase the risk of disease outbreaks and record high rates of malnutrition. Upwards of 7 million people are expected to experience acute food insecurity.Women and children on the move face serious deprivations and are exposed to major protection risks. Those arriving in South Sudan have endured long and often dangerous journeys. The arrivals into South Sudan are in urgent need of health services with alarming rates of malnutrition recorded, ranging from a proxy Global Acute Malnutrition rate between 17 - 27 percent at border entry locations. Protection screening has identified many highly vulnerable cases, including unaccompanied and separated children, missing children, high levels of trauma, as well as reports of gender-based violence and abuse. To date, most people demonstrate high levels of food insecurity and cannot meet basic needs. South Sudan is ranked the second most susceptible nation to the effects of natural hazards, including droughts, heatwaves, and flooding. In 2023, 11.8 percent of the child population was displaced due to flooding. In 2024, more than 60 percent of the population is expected to be acutely food insecure, and 2 million lactating women and children under age 5 are expected to suffer from wasting.

2. Project activities

Mission Trust Aid will contribute to the Education Cluster's objectives of providing quality teaching and learning services to enable emergency-affected children and youths to get access to safe, protective, inclusive services that are appropriate to their age and to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies. These are in line with the South Sudan Education Cluster Cluster strategy 2023-2025, the activities will include: 1. Provide temporary learning spaces (TLS), semi-permanent structures that are safe, inclusive, and gender sensitive. 2. Provide gender-segregated and accessible WASH facilities (latrines, hand-washing facilities, girls' change rooms) for girls and boys 3. Community mobilization targeting out-of-school children and youth to improve access to education 4. Enrolment of boys and girls in ECD and primary education 5. Distribute sanitation/hygiene kits for boys and girls in schools to prevent communicable diseases 5. Train teachers, SMCs, PTAs, and learners on referral mechanisms 6. Implement school gardening programs 7. Provide teacher training on psychosocial support (PSS) 8. Provide teaching and learning materials for learners and teachers 9. Conduct EiE-related teacher training and continuous professional development 10. Support the development of national and state-level EiE preparedness and response plans 11. Train School Management Committees (SMC), Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and education authorities on school management, governance, climate change, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
