Project Summary

The goal of this project is the reduction of morbidity and mortality among the Syrian population in Idleb and Aleppo, and this will be accomplished by enhancing the coverage of the existing primary healthcare system through a network of 200 community health workers serving a population of 132,000. Each CH team will conduct 5 HH visits per day for 22 days per month, reaching a total of 132,000 HH visits. A HH will be targeted by 4 visits per year, making the total number of targeted families 33,000, each includes an average of 4 individuals.
Since 2018, UDER has managed a network of 115 community health workers in Dana and Atareb. On monthly basis, the CHWs are conducting an average of 7,400 HH visits, reaching an average of 31,800 beneficiaries. 59.2% of the services were provided to IDPs. The program gained the acceptance and support from local communities, and has effectively improved the referral system from the community to standard healthcare. It has also served in obtaining critical demographic and health related data to support the services provided by the health system.
The community health project is currently providing community health services according to the standard guidelines developed by the Community Health Working Group (CHWG) in Gaziantep with support from World Health Organization (WHO).
CH services are linked to the existing primary healthcare system to maximize the coverage and impact of PHC services at the outreach level and to empower local communities, particularly individuals from vulnerable groups in taking responsibility for their own health.
The project forms a foundation for integrated primary health care and CH services for community-based management of NCDs, CDs, Family health and vaccination through outreach and facility-based services.
CH activities, including health promotion, health behavioral change, and community health referrals, will be conducted according to the standards developed by the CHWG. The CHWs will play a key role in improving the health referral system in Dana, Atareb, Azaz and Afrin sub-districts to ensure continuity of care for the population in the targeted area with a focus on IDP concentrations and remote, rural communities. Of sub-districts in Idleb and western Aleppo, Dana, Atareb and Azaz sub-districts have received the highest number of IDPs during early 2020 waves of displacement from Idleb and Aleppo.
These newly affected IDPs comprise the main community groups who will benefit from this project. Through community health referrals, beneficiaries will have access to UDER’s referral pathway between the health centers and the next level of care within and outside the targeted sub-districts.
The project will deliver 132000 HH sessions, each covers 4 individuals, to a total population of 130,000. Each individual will receive 4-5 visits over one year.
Before selecting communities, UDER coordinated with other NGOs in the area to ensure no duplication. The information showed that Azaz has 79 CHWs, Afrin has 105, Dana has 132, and Atareb has 91 CHWs. UDER had also conducted a training to 167 CHWs from 21 NGOs using the standard curriculum of the CHWG to encourage actors to adopt the standard CH guidelines. The recruitment of CHWs in Atareb and Dana was done with participation from local councils, health directorates and health providers to ensure that the CH team is well accepted and supported.
Each CHW is provided with a tablet which allows for daily data collection and timely supervision and feedback. Team leaders escort CHWs during daily visits and prepare daily supervision reports while CH supervisors conduct random visits, meet community members and measure their satisfaction. The CH coordinator is the overall responsible for monitoring of activities at the field level and they conduct regular visits to local councils, camp managers and community leaders to receive feedback about the activities and recommendations for improvement and better coverage.

4 - Needs

According to OCHA from 1 December 2019 to 2 February 2020, 586,000 people fled their homes in NW Syria because of ongoing hostilities. Many of them have been displaced many times throughout the Syria crisis, eroding the resilience of families and communities. 163,877 of them were displaced into Aldana and 27,290 were displaced into Atareb subdistrict where UDER is currently implementing the largest CH project in the area.UDER’s CH data between Sep and Dec 2019 in Atareb and Dana which included 29,203 HH visits covering 130,530 individuals (50.19% male, 49.81% female, and 68,773 children), shows that 1,572 children didn’t complete their vaccinations. 887 pregnant women out of 3,914 screened were not seeking antenatal care, and were referred for routine care or for reasons related to danger pregnancy signs. During the same period, 4,182 cases of hypertension and 2,181 cases of diabetes were detected and provided with targeted CH messages.Patient exit interviews collected at the UDER-supported health facilities and household visits, show that transportation presents a significant challenge to accessing fixed facilities for vulnerable communities especially for women and young girls. They have to rely on relatives or ask strangers to transport them. In many cases, women, girls, young children, and elderly people must be escorted by a male relative in order to access healthcare which limits these populations from accessing the available PHCs outside their communities. Cultural misconceptions and lack of trust with the current health system push patients in NW Syria to fail to adhere to treatment plans and avoid vaccination programs. A well-targeted health education plan is needed to raise awareness of communities of common diseases and best health practices for preventing the spread of communicable diseases. 50 health facilities were forced to cease operations amid displacement and hostilities, which signifies the importance and needs of CH service.

5 - Implementation of activities

UDER has 100 CHWs in Aldana and Atareb. Additional 100 CHWs will be added in Azaz and Afrin to respond to new IDPs. Each 10 CHWs are managed by a Team Leader. CHWs work in pairs, one delivers the educational session and the other collects data through Kobo system. A team is assigned to 250 households to maintaining records and follow up on their health needs according to the standard SoP developed by CHWG. Team leaders are responsible for managing CHWs, facilitating visits, communicating with community leaders and health facility staff, handling security incidents and collecting daily records CHW reports. They meet weekly with health staff at the referral health facilities to review the types of referred cases and suggest improvements to CHWs’ performance. Supervisors analyze data collected at the district level, and report to health directorates and to UDER. They conduct refresher training to the CH team every six months during the project, and they are regularly improving messaging and IEC material according to the project requirements and feedback from beneficiaries and local health providers. Targeted communities were selected based on community feedback and information from local councils. Staff were hired with participation from local councils and community representatives.Six behavioral change messages are prioritized based on the community information. This includes pregnancy, hypertension, diabetes, nutrition, vaccination and danger signs for child-health. They are delivered through HH visits. CHWs are already trained on family mapping and the survey tools. They received a refresher training in July, with another planned in March 2020.CHWs will refer the cases-in need for specialized care to the nearest PHC facilities including Sarmada, Termanin, Dana PHCs. Select cases from vulnerable individuals will be provided with transportation to allow them to follow the referrals and overcome barriers related to transportation. CHW will follow up on these cases.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Abuse or harassment by CHWs towards the HH members.Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- Recruitment of female CHWs and limit the recruitment of male workers. Two workers per household visit and conduct random supervision visits by supervisors;- Train staff on PSEA, code of conduct and ethical data collection;- All staff commit to UDER’s PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies;- Contract include a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct of SEA;- Enhance Complaint and Feedback mechanism;- Hot line for allegations of SEA misconducts is in place;- Regular surveys conducted by MEAL team to monitor the conducted visits. Surveys include question about team members behaviors, misconducts and their respect of privacy;- Allegations are seriously investigated, where aid workers are subjected to contract termination and further disciplinary actions if allegations were proven. 2- Household data is exposed to leakage or destruction.Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- Record all beneficiary data on an online database through Kobo surveys and using tablets, and stored on an online server that can be accessed only by Turkey staff. This prevents data storage on hard or soft copies in Syria which are exposed to be stolen or destroyed. To enhance confidentiality, beneficiaries’ names are only accessed by the information team. Family records are coded anonymously before sharing with other parties. 3- CHWs are harassed by the community or local authorities.Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high.Mitigation measures:- CH team members are largely recruited from the local community to make sure trust is exchanged between them and communities they serve. The project is backed by local health authorities (independent Health Directorates) and community leaders through ongoing communication and trust-building behaviors.- CH teams have visible IDs at all times.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions include:- Permission for UDER to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues.- Current conflict doesn't affect program activities.- Donor funds continue to be provided for NW Syria.- Community acceptance for this project continues.Risks and mitigation measures: There is a potential continuum of violence escalation in Idleb through a military offensive on southern Idleb or fights among Organized Armed Groups as the Government of Turkey (GoT) leads an effort to eliminate sanctioned armed groups. UDER’s chosen locations for this project (Atareb and Dana subdistricts) are located near to the Turkish border and away from potentially targeted locations. Continuous massive IDP movement to northern Idleb and Aleppo might put increased pressure on the health system in northern Syria. This requires maximizing the coverage of CH services, primary and secondary healthcare systems. UDER will ensure that the network of CHWs continues to be in place to be ready to respond to potential waves of IDPs. Close communication will be maintained with Health Directorates, local councils and community leaders to ensure that our teams are covering the communities with the highest concentration of IDPs. Given that a functional mobile phone network does not exist in Idleb and Aleppo, UDER will equip the CH team with communication devices (local mobile phone internet subscriptions) to ensure that they can contact the team leaders, provide information about their location, and receive immediate guidance as needed. Team Leaders will be primarily responsible for handling and reporting security incidents related to their teams, and they will receive support on safety and security measures, including proper communication practices.
