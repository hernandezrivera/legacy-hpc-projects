Project Summary

BRAC’s Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector (LSDS) has been active in Cox’s Bazar since 2017, focusing on food security and self-reliance for refugees and host communities affected by the crisis. Under the LSDS, BRAC will implement a project aimed at achieving the sector’s objectives while aligning with its strategic goals of localization, fostering a humanitarian-development nexus, building resilience among the affected Rohingya and host communities, and strengthening coordination among humanitarian actors and stakeholders.
BRAC aims to equip a total of 5,046 Rohingya refugees and 2,864 host community participants s in Ukhia and Teknaf with transferable and portable skills that meet labor market demands in their respective contexts. This initiative is designed to facilitate sustainable return and integration in Myanmar when conditions allow while fostering hope for the future and enabling dignified, productive lives within the camps. The project focuses on enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance for both communities. BRAC will support skills and livelihood activities across various locations, including Camp 1E, 2E, 3, 4 Ext, 5, 7, 8E, 9, 12, 14, 15, 16, 26, and 27, as well as the host communities in Kutupalong, Haldia Palong, Jalia Palong, Raja Palong, Ratna Palong, and Hnila union.
BRAC will target youth and adults, both men and women, by offering non-formal and formal skills training aligned with the GoB-UN Framework on Skills Development for Rohingya Youth and Host Communities. Based on identified needs for specific skill trades, the project will provide centre-based vocational training in areas such as garment and sewing machine operation, community health work, masonry, general mechanics, solar mechanics, plumbing, electrical work, agricultural production, and handicrafts. In parallel, there will be a six-month training program focused on digital literacy and communicative English. Each formal skills training course will consist of 360 hours, while informal courses will be 120 hours. The project will also facilitate formal assessments for participants, with successful candidates receiving Level-1 completion certificates. These certifications will empower participants to pursue further education and training opportunities and negotiate higher wages as skilled workers in both the camps and Myanmar.
To enhance the livelihoods of both host and Rohingya communities, the project will promote small entrepreneurs by providing in-kind support to launch small businesses and scale up the existing businesses. Additionally, interventions in the host community will incorporate disaster-resilient and green entrepreneurship practices within vocational skills development activities. This holistic approach aims to strengthen beneficiaries' resilience against external shocks, reduce environmental degradation, and improve food security and income.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya population in Bangladesh has seen a considerable increase, as reported in the Joint Government of Bangladesh-UNHCR Population Factsheet. In August 2017, the population was recorded at 767,324 individuals within 154,264 households. By September 2024, this number had risen to 967,765 individuals across 195,095 households.The December 2023 J-MSNA report indicates significant employment challenges in both Rohingya refugee camps and host communities. In the camps, 18.3% of households were unable to find employment, while 42.1% of households in host communities lacked access to income-generating opportunities. Additionally, 2.8% of households in the camps and 6.2% in host communities could not afford to participate in conditional cash programs, such as cash-for-work, compared to previous data from 2023.Moreover, 71.2% of households in refugee camps and 36.2% in host communities reported barriers to income opportunities. In refugee camps, 53% faced obstacles, and 27% had long waits for volunteer engagement and cash-for-work programs. In host communities, 29% encountered obstacles to income opportunities. Furthermore, 6% of households in refugee camps and 1% in host communities reported a lack of opportunities for women due to the absence of female staff. Women in both the host and camp areas often face social stigma and conservative attitudes that restrict their participation in economic activities. Additionally, their access to the job market is severely limited due to factors such as lack of higher education, insufficient skill development opportunities, lack of women-friendly trades, low social acceptance, and inadequate family support.The Joint Government of Bangladesh-UNHCR Population Factsheet indicates that in September 2024, there were 967,765 Rohingya individuals in Bangladesh. This significant population size has social, environmental, legal, and financial impacts on the country. The skills and livelihood development of these individuals largely rely on humanitarian assistance from the Bangladesh government, donor organizations, UN agencies, and various NGOs. A needs assessment by UCEP/UNHCR in 2022 revealed that 76% of Rohingya youth haven't received any skills training since arriving in the camps. This project will select trades for skills training based on the UN/GoB framework, demand by the Rohingya youth, the portability of skills to Myanmar, needs assessment, and potential for volunteer engagement and market linkage within the camps.Current projects show a high demand for both skills and non-formal training for participants from both host and Rohingya communities. Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally lack regular income opportunities and necessary skills for improving their situation. Additionally, half of the refugee workforce is not involved in income-generating activities.Thus, providing skills development opportunities in various trades, usable in camps and upon repatriation, is crucial for improving livelihoods and self-reliance. Expanding formal and non-formal skills training, socio-economic empowerment, and self-reliance activities, particularly for uneducated and unskilled individuals, youth, and women, is essential for enhancing access to economic opportunities. BRAC will implement various activities focused on women and Persons with Disabilities in both camps and host communities to ensure equal opportunities and empowerment. Including Persons with Disabilities will help them contribute to their communities and families, transforming them from dependents into valuable resources.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

As of September 2024, approximately 1 million Rohingya refugees live in 33 densely populated camps in Cox’s Bazar District, with 44% being adults of working age. Each year, around 20,000 young Rohingya reach working age, yet face major barriers to self-reliance due to limited educational opportunities and legal restrictions on formal employment within the camps. To address these challenges, BRAC has launched initiatives to promote self-reliance and empowerment among Rohingya youth aged 18–35. Through targeted skills training, BRAC equips young people with practical, transferable skills for self-sufficiency and a hopeful future. The project actively engages youth aged 15-24, offering vocational and life skills relevant to labour markets in Bangladesh and Myanmar, empowering them to live dignified lives in the camps and preparing them for eventual reintegration in Myanmar when possible. The project includes formal vocational training in trades (agriculture crop production, and sewing machine operation) that are in demand within the camps and in broader labour markets, ensuring skills are aligned with recognized standards (IASC guidelines). Non-formal training offers hands-on, practical learning on sewing machine operation and hand stretch crucial for employment and self-reliance within the camp setting. BRAC also provides essential digital literacy training, enabling youth to navigate technology effectively for educational and employment purposes. Furthermore, the program enhances English language proficiency, broadening educational and job opportunities within the camps and beyond. Through these comprehensive initiatives, BRAC aims to build a resilient and skilled generation of Rohingya youth capable of contributing meaningfully to their communities. By empowering them with relevant skills, BRAC supports their social and economic empowerment, fostering a future where they can lead dignified lives with purpose and resilience, even in challenging circumstances.
