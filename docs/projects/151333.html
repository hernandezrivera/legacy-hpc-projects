Project Summary

This project aims to protect and restore community productive assets through provision of livelihoods inputs like farm inputs, temporary job creation and improved storage capacity of food and seeds for farmers with specific care of Women headed households and other vulnerable groups and to improve resilience through development of alternative livelihood strategies for vulnerable population, realized via vocational training, agricultural training, business management training as well as through preparing the population against shocks like floods through infrastructure development.

Needs

Decades of conflict have destroyed infrastructure, homes, social services and livelihoods, making Somalis increasingly vulnerable to recurrent shocks faced each year. The outlook for 2018 is also bleak due to La Nina development in the region. Restoring the farmland productive capacity of Somalis to better prepare for, respond to and recover from shocks is a precursor to improving food security and nutrition, as well as reducing future humanitarian needs. Food security and nutrition slightly improved according to the latest assessment by FSNAU-FEWS NET in June-July 2018. Some 4.6 million people; 40% of Somalis are acutely classified under IPC 2. The nutrition situation of Hirshabelle state has continued to deteriorate and assessment results showed that an estimated 32,000 children under the age of five are acutely malnourished, including 17,000 who are severely malnourished and face an increasing risk of morbidity and death. In 2017, Crop production was poor in Hirshabelle as the Shabelle River level in Middle Shabelle region has reached the lowest in two consecutive dry seasons of 2016 and 2017 and therefore unable to support irrigation farming. In 2018 after Gu rains, downstream in Jalalaqsi, Mahaday, Jowhar and Balcad districts, the river flooded and washed away all farm land downstream. Riverine communities became most affected because they have the least options of coping with shocks. Large scale of humanitarian intervention has been made since April 2018 preventing further deterioration in many part of the state. In the project area of Balcad district, about 56,000 people are in crisis while 29,000 people are in emergency situation. This population have critical prevalence of acute malnutrition of GM of 16.5% or higher and are considered hotspot in need of urgent nutrition and food support intervention.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The severe drought and the ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million in Somalia. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and cities. Humanitarian conditions in many of the informal settlements and public buildings occupied by the displaced throughout Somalia continue to be dire; and may be further aggravated for communities who are forcibly evicted from their location of settlement or continue reside with insecure tenure. In IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. This project will target most vulnerable households with keen attention to IDPs who are able to rebuild their farmland.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Displacement affects individuals of all ages and sexes, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and needs may evolve over time. Many IDPs, whether recently displaced or in a protracted displacement situation, are compelled by a lack of access to adequate assistance and livelihoods, to resort to negative coping mechanisms with serious protection repercussions. For instance, children are at a heightened risk for recruitment into fighting forces, child labor, early marriage and dropping out of school. Schools serves as a safe space for children and is critical to ensuring that children displaced by or affected by drought and conflict do not only have access to education but are better able to cope, survive and recover from the crisis impacts and contribute to longer term peaceful development of Somalia. Despite substantial progress on mainstreaming durable solutions in national development frameworks, the context of ongoing insecurity and drought means that safe and dignified durable solutions (return, local integration or settlement elsewhere) to displacement limited access to services and humanitarian assistance, and diversion of assistance. As a result, many are forced to move from their communities and some also move preemptively for fear of violence being deliberately imposed on them.The project will largely focus on households regardless of gender or age but with special attention and focus on female headed households since they are much more vulnerable.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

As a result of general insecurity, inter-clan conflict escalations, indiscriminate attacks including through improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and aerial bombardments, as well as military offensives and withdrawals in the context of the conflict between the non-state armed actors, the Federal Government of Somalia (FGS) and supporting actors. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. This is further aggravated regarding violations for marginalized groups where societal inequalities result in reduced protection. This project will involve government line ministries in supervision roles and building resilience of communities.
