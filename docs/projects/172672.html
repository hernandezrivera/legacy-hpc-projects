1 - Needs

Sudan remains a large protracted humanitarian crisis which continues to have a significant impact on the already; fragile health systems’ capacity to respond to the needs of the people and the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The health needs are constantly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy, conflict, civil unrest, floods, and disease outbreaks. On September 2020, WHO classified Sudan as grade 2 acute health emergency. According to the Central Bureau of Statistics, during 2020, the cost of health services increased by 90 per cent. further impacting the 70 per cent Out-of-pocket expenditures (OOP) of current health expenditures (CHE)i of previous years. During the same year, the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach only 57 per cent by September. The first positive case of COVID-19 was confirmed in March 2020. as of Thursday November 26th, total COVID-19 confirmed cases reached 17,404 including 1,235 associated deaths, CFR: 7.1%. per cent - one of the highest in the region. The highest CFR were reported from Central Darfur (50 per cent), North Darfur (32 per cent), and East Darfur (24 per cent). Throughout the ongoing response to the pandemic, lack of designated facilities, health staff, and necessary equipment affected the outcome and contributed to the high fatality rate. To tackle the COVID-19 caseload, only 160 functional ventilators are available in Sudan with 93 (58 per cent) of them in Khartoum. No ventilators are available in North Darfur, and River Nile, Blue Nile, West Darfur, WestKordofan, and South Kordofan states each only has two functional ventilators. The availability of health cadre and healthcare workers is still a challenge in Sudan hindering the capacity and efforts to scale up the response. According to figures by the federal and state ministries of health, there are only 5,457 doctors, 12,601 nurses, and 17,343 midwives working in the public health system (0.81 doctors + nurses+midwives per 1,000 population, against the 4.45 per one thousand population WHO target as recommended in the WHO Health workforce requirements for universal health coverage iii). The lowest ratios of medical professionals per 1,000 people, were reported in White Nile, West Kordofan, East Darfur, Northern, and Central Darfur. The DPT3 vaccine (iv) coverage plummeted during 2020 to reach 63 per cent and the coverage of measles vaccination was only 71 per cent. The decline was reportedly due to the deteriorating economy's impact on vaccination programs as well as, COVID-19 contingency measures such as restriction of movement, and reallocation of the healthcare workers to assist during COVID-19 response.In August 2020, the Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH) declared an outbreak of Vaccine-Derived PolioVirus type 2 (cVDPV2) after the report of positive samples from patients with Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP), environmental samples, and healthy children. By mid-September total confirmed cVDPV2 among AFP patients reached 22 cases across 10 of Sudan’s 18 states indicating a wide spread of the virus. By the end of week 34 of the 2020 epidemiological year, 900,000 malaria cases had been reported. This accounted for 13.3 per cent of all patients visits to health facilities. Despite the increased use of sentinel surveillance sites - an additional 128 reporting health facilities were setup (up from 1,659 at the beginning of 2019 to 1,787 in 2020) - the facility-based disease surveillance system only covers around 30 per cent of health facilities with significant disproportionality between urban and rural coverage. across CARE operation state, South Darfur , East Darfur, South Kordufan , and Kassala the need remain high, including drugs shortage, lack of staff, poor facilities infrastructures, lack of Medical equipment and supplies; furthermore Sudan including CARE support state have been affected by the disease outbreaks including COVID-19, VHFs, Polio and Malaria, therefore CARE Support is needed.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Under this project CARE is planning to support the provision of PHC services including referral to secondary level care, this include OPD, EPI, RH (ANC, PNC, Delivery, family planning), dressing and short stay services, COVID-19 awareness and education, PPE, RCCE/CBS, provision of drugs and medical supplies, provision of rehabilitation and minor construction, and responding to diseases outbreaks. the Project is will support EMNoC services in Care Operation areas, as well will support SRH/RH services at community and facility level. The project will include also training of health cadres in Health related training including but not limiting to PHC/ MISP/CMR, EMoC, EPI, COVID-19, Outbreak response, etc..

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountabilitymechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training &capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consultingthem in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and trainingcommunity committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improvingaccess to services through information dissemination on available services, goodselection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized.Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened notto enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrinesand water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed anddiscussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that theservices are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building forstaff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also beoriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able torespond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using aconfidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internalmechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place willbe promoted throughout the implementation period

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE will be working and coordinating with all interagency partners to ensure accountability maintained for the affected people including SEA and abuse complaints. CARE has Measurement in place to protect beneficiaries and communities from sexual exploitation and abuse as the following: - all CARE staff has signed letter that explicitly prohibit SEA of beneficiaries by staff and staff of affiliated entities (such as contractors, vendors, suppliers, etc.). and CARE has policies and training package that have been disseminated to staff and well understood amongst staff; all CARE staff Has received training and orientation on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; CARE has also appointed PSEA focal persons at country and state level, whom are doing, regular in person support and follow with the team members. CIS has reporting mechanism in place for reporting PSEA, at field level, CARE Medical assistants in charge of the clinic are the focal person at the field level, whom work closely with all his/her departments staff to ensure PSEA policy are implemented and any cases related to PSEA are reported timely to CARE focal person at state level.
