Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian WASH assistance to 113,788 beneficiaries (37,550 women, 28,448 men, 23,895 girls, 23,895 boys - 12% of whom are estimated to be either elderly or disabled) to mitigate the suffering of populations affected by natural or man made disasters and reflects all 3 outcomes of the Multi Year Humanitarian Response Plan strategy. Under Outcome 1 IOM will provide life saving assistance to people newly displaced by conflict or natural disasters in an effort to recreate pre emergency conditions using early recovery approaches to improve the overall access to WASH services, connecting the need for short term emergency interventions with long term solutions and applying development principles by working directly with local communities to facilitate ownership and accountability. The objective is to not only focus on WASH emergency interventions but gradually contribute to socio economic inclusion of forcibly displaced populations and returnees by directly increasing access to basic services for the displaced and host communities and indirectly increasing employment opportunities. Under Outcome 2 IOM seeks to improve and sustain access to safe water in target communities especially in Durdeb, Abyei, North Darfur and South Darfur as these communities primarily rely on agriculture and livestock, therefore water is critical to their livelihoods. Under Outcome 3 improving and sustaining access to safe water and sanitation facilities and hygiene messages will address malnutrition related risks and increase community resilience to disease outbreaks. IOM will consider emergency water provision, access to proper sanitation facilities and hygiene campaigns; coupled with the establishment and rehabilitation of water points. This will include environmental impact mitigation such as the provision of solar technology to power water points and monitoring ground water to moderate depletion levels. Exit strategies will be developed to alleviate aid dependency in protracted emergencies by strengthening self-reliance and facilitating community solutions in a more holistic approach.

Needs

The needs assessed for the proposed interventions are based on the gaps observed in 2017 and 2018, especially tailored to female headed households as 80% of IDPs camps are made up of women and children. Women, children and the elderly remain the most vulnerable groups in need of assistance and protection. In locations where easy access to safe water is lacking vulnerable communities resolve to collect water from unfiltered water sources, increasing the risks of water borne disease outbreaks and exacerbating malnutrition rates among children. IOM currently provides assistance in South Darfur and is planning to continue its interventions where there are a total of 204,765 people that have very limited to no access to safe water, sanitation facilities and in need of targeted hygiene and garbage collection campaigns. In Otash Camp, there are 82,463 IDPs reside in the overcrowded camp, with new IDPs arriving from EJM. Voluntary returns from IDP camps to places of origin are also reported, a trend that is expected to increase in 2019 WASH support will be required to support residents and returnees.In North Darfur, IOM will be focusing on Ruanda and Debenaira Camps to continue addressing the gaps in sanitation and hygiene, whilst monitoring water quality and availability. IOM also proposes to provide life-saving assistance to vulnerable residents IDPs living among host communities in localities and villages around Tawilla in order to address the vast gaps in water, sanitation and hygiene. Low level of awareness on proper hygiene and sanitation, the lack of food and potable water, and the lack of access to basic services severely affects many IDPs and residents living in North Darfur. In East, West and Central Darfur, IOM will continue to increase access to WASH services for IDPs, returnees and host communities.In South and West Kordofan there are at least five locations that require increased access to potable water and hygiene campaigns for returnees, IDPs and host communities. In the two Kordofan States, high number of IDPs and refugees still reside with hosting communities, putting further pressure on the already limited resources. As the population increases additional water sources are needed to support the growing population, open defecation is widely practiced and there is a dire need for hygiene and garbage collection campaigns. The chronic underdevelopment of the overall Eastern Sudan region has contributed to the scarcity of water resources and lack of infrastructure severely affecting communities’ livelihoods in the area. Access to safe water and other basic services remain among the lowest in the country; Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates are above the 15% international emergency threshold levels. The lack of access to basic services and the deterioration of two essential water treatment plants in the locations have contributed to the current Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) crisis.

Activities

1. Provide access to water through the construction or rehabilitation of water points such as 250 hand pumps and 40 water yards that use sustainable and environmentally friendly fuel sources (solar technology) to run water schemes. 2. Monitor water depletion levels/ ground water availability to improve resource management and evaluate the impact of constructing new water points on the local environment. 3. Provide 5,000 household with sanitation facilities to reduce open defecation and improve community sanitation by constructing more gender, age and disability sensitive latrines with durable materials including preventing the latrines from contaminating water sources. 4. Assess hygiene practices at household level and conduct hygiene campaigns that include topics on methods to prevent water borne diseases and the fecal oral link that may exacerbate malnutrition, especially targeting women and children. IOM’s hygiene teams will be mobilizing women as key promoters of hygiene.5. Use Community Approaches to Total Sanitation (CATS) to engage the community in designing the responses and supporting construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of the structures and promoting safe hygienic practices. 6. Increase community awareness of safe hygiene practices and engage the community in systematic garbage collection campaigns. 7. Propositioning WASH kits to assist at least 7,000 vulnerable HHs for possible renewed displacement and prevention of water borne diseases. 8. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) tools are implemented to measure impact of emergency interventions. 9. Community members will be trained to establish waste and water management committees, consisting of both men and women, to collect tariffs from community members to support maintenance of facilitates and develop contingency and durable solution plans. 10. Train female and male hand pump mechanics, chlorination technicians.11. Draft of Operation and Maintenance guidelines -WASH Study

Impact on environment/natural resources

New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper / responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’s economic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to fluctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible.
