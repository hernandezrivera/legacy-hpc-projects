Project Summary

The goal of is to respond to urgent protection needs of persons affected by conflict and violence in Gaza, and strengthen the protective environment for conflict-affected communities. NRC will provide frontline protection services in Gaza, particularly comprehensive protection case management and community-based interventions, including civilian self-protection, to improve resilience and community self-protective capacity and overcome barriers to access services.The project aims to reach 12421 persons in need (unique beneficiaries) and offer training and capacity building to 160 persons (members of Gaza Protection Cluster).NRC will target Palestinians in Gaza affected by the protracted protection crisis and conflict, emphasizing services to the most marginalised. Protection case management will target people at risk of violence, coercion, and deliberate deprivation, and will consider compounding vulnerabilities such as age, disability,chronic illness,and acute poverty in prioritization efforts.General protection Case Management is provided in recognition that protection risks are not limited to those addressed by the Child Protection and GBV AORs, and will instead seek to prevent and mitigate risks such as arbitrary arrest and detention,restrictions on FoM, and conflict-related violence. However, NRC will also support strengthening of the interagency referral pathway, ensuring that all cases requiring child protection and GBV support will be promptly referred to specialized agencies according to existing SOPs.According to case plans created with clients, some might receive cash or in-kind assistance to support protective outcomes. This assistance would be in addition and unrelated to cash or material assistance received from other humanitarian or social protection programmes. Cash might be used to support a client to access a service necessary to resolve a protection risk, or recover from an experience of violence. Cash assistance under protection cash management will not be used to supplement basic needs shortfalls.Community-based interventions will include two pillars.1)NRC will work with Ma’an to offer structured PSS to Palestinians affected by conflict, displacements, and human rights violations. Specific activities will include life skills courses for adults, positive parenting sessions, and awareness-raising sessions on different topics.2) NRC will begin a pilot of our Civilian Self-Protection programme, which includes the creation and facilitation of community-driven self-protection action plans.The Community-based interventions will be a source of identification and screening the protection case. MA’AN is one of the child protection actors according to the established referral pathways in Gaza. Under this project MA’AN will receive child protection cases and NRC will use the same referral forms. NRC anticipates that cases requiring protection case management services will be identified outside of the GPC project; this intervention ensures that all persons needing protection case management, and not only those supported by the GPC, will be eligible for assistance. Moreover, NRC would like to grow its protection services at the community level, both alone (civilian self-protection) and through partnership (Ma’an community-based protection). Finally, NRC would like to be able to share knowledge and skills around general protection case management with the sector more broadly, and support the work of the Protection Cluster with capacity building. 1 - Needs

The protection situation in the Gaza Strip is defined by 15 years of Israeli blockade, punctuated by recurrent paroxysms of violence between Israel and Palestine. Trapped in a chronic protection crisis, individuals and families face physical assault and violence, ERW contamination, displacement due to insufficient or destroyed shelter, prolonged exposure to severe psychosocial distress, violation of socio-economic and legal rights, and deprivation of access to critical and basic services. Individuals at heightened risk and those with compounding vulnerabilities, including PWD and with chronic illness, elders, women, youth, children, and displaced families, are falling through the cracks of service responders, due to lack of capacity and resources of the authorities and absence of social welfare services. While multi-sectorial response has been provided to vulnerable people in Gaza, specialized protection case management support for people with specific needs is limited.The August 2022 Protection Analysis Update noted the further deterioration of the protective environment in Gaza,even prior to the recent escalation that killed dozens, left hundreds wounded and/or displaced, and resulted in further restrictions on FoM. The PAU estimates that some 988,282 persons (approximately 50% of those in need are women and girls, 15% are PWD, and 3% are elderly), or nearly half of total residents, are in need of protection assistance, including support recovery from conflict-related violence and displacement, arbitrary arrest and harassment, restrictions on FoM and assembly, and acute psychological distress (Cluster, August 2022). Many HHs are still struggling to recover from the May 2021 conflict escalations. The 2022 MSNA, found 40% of HHs reported at least one member experiencing signs and symptoms of trauma in the year prior to data collection.A recent NRC assessment found that 53% of Palestinians in Gaza are living below the poverty threshold, more than three times the number in the WB. Gaza ranks now as the worst employment rate in all MENA region and as one of the worst in the overall world. Individuals and families are increasingly resorting to negative mechanisms or becoming fully dependent on humanitarian aid in their effort to cope with shocks and crisis. The socio-economic vulnerabilities above must be seen as compounding and catalyst to increasing protection risks, as individuals and families are increasingly resorting to negative coping mechanisms. Indeed, the MSNA referenced above found 40-60% of HHs were employing crisis or emergency livelihoods coping strategies in the 30 days prior to data collection. The most common negative coping mechanism is making debt and purchase food and goods on credit, followed by reducing non-food expenditure to meet food needs, sending family members elsewhere to eat including begging, selling assets and spend savings. The NRC assessment calculated that it would take an average family six years to recover from debt. Specific population groups, including women, children, and PWD are considered at risk for experiencing protection threats, with 5.3% of HHs identifying specific safety and security concerns for children, 38.4% for children with a disability, 15.7% for women. A rapid needs assessment by Humanity and Inclusion and disability partners (16-23 May 2022) identified nearly 800 PWDes and injuries in need of assistance including mobility and support devices as well as non-food items and cash support that are not addressed sufficiently by existing social protection and cash assistance programming. Further, the oPt has one of the highest burdens of MH issues and psychological disorders in the Eastern Mediterranean Region, among both children and adults, with 54% of Palestinian boys and 47% of Palestinian girls (aged 6 to 12 years) presenting emotional and/or behavioural disorders. According to a Sawa newsletter from earlier this year, MHPSS needs, and instances of suicidal ideation have increased in 2022 compared to 2021.Gender analysis -Ranking 119/188 countries in the UN Gender Equality Index, gender plays a major role in vulnerability levels and exposure to risk in Palestine, exacerbated by pre-existing disparities in access to rights and entitlements,education,and economic opportunities between women and men. Vulnerable women and girls continue to experience sexual and GBV, exploitation and abuse, and denial of resources. Women headed HH are 200,000 in Palestine, accounting 11% of total HHs. The nature of the Palestinian protracted crisis has exacerbated conditions that are conducive to increasing protection risks for all Palestinian women, men, girls, and boys, including violence, exploitation, and severe stress. May2022 escalation have disproportionately impacted women, children, elderly people, and PWD. According to Care’s rapid needs assessment ,88% of the women are in need for urgent PSS. Alarmingly, 65% of the surveyed weren’t aware of GBV services in Gaza, 85% didn’t know how to report a case of SEA. Adult men are also impacted by the protracted crisis. Considering their traditional roles as head of HH and breadwinner, the loss of shelter and livelihood has impacted greatly on their PSS well-being.This has also resulted in additional risks on women and children, with increased tension within the HH and broader community. Adolescent girls have been requested to do HH chores and care for vulnerable family members, while dropping out school and be posed at risk of early marriage. Boys are also asked to contribute to prioritize work to support to the HH economy at expenses of education etc. To date, women make up many protection case management clients (not related to GBV), while PWD constitute 10% of protection case management clients. 2 - Activities

1. Protection Case Management In 2023, NRC will build upon its existing Protection Case Management intervention to support the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals affected by conflict, violence, and violations of human rights and humanitarian law. NRC will provide Case Management services to the most vulnerable beneficiaries facing protection risks. According to NRC’s global approach, protection case management is a client-centred, iterative process composed of a range of potential activities, such as accompanied or assisted referral, targeted PSS, and cash or material assistance. NRC Case Management and Referral system relies on a robust set of tools, guidance, and capacity building opportunities. The Protection approach is built in line with the Global Protection Cluster guidance and standards, and the UNHCR/IRC Case Management manual for people with specific needs. NRC Palestine can also count with the support of a global specialist who is currently revising the NRC Package for Protection Case Management. Protection case management is a structured and sustained method for providing responsive and remedial support to a person at heightened risk of a rights violation to manage and ultimately claim their rights leading to their safety, dignity and ensuing resilience.When conducting protection case management, an individual person is supported by an assigned caseworker. During the case management process the caseworker supports the client in assessing their situation and identifying the risk and protective factors in their life.The caseworker acts as a liaison between the client and service providers, linking the client to these services, advocating for timely and quality care, and following up in a coordinated way. NRC will target 240 individuals with protection case management, such that 60% of cases will be women and adolescent girls, 15% people with disabilities (though disability will not be considered as a protection risk), and 5% elderly persons, in line with those most at-risk according to recent needs assessment. Overall, it is anticipated that 60% of cases (144) will need some form of cash or material assistance. Cash and other material assistance will only be offered with a case management plan, except in the event of an acute emergency, such as significant escalation in the conflict.Cash and other material assistance would be in addition and unrelated to cash or material assistance received from other humanitarian or social protection programmes (MPCA). Cash is UNCONDITIONAL, and might be used to support a client to access a service necessary to resolve a protection risk, or recover from an experience of violence (i.e. cash for rent for persons who have been evicted). Cash assistance under protection cash management will not be used to supplement basic needs shortfalls.2. Strengthening of the Referral Pathway-NRC will support capacity development among members of the Gaza Protection Cluster. In 2021 and 2022, NRC offered trainings on protection in humanitarian action, protection case management and psychosocial support. NRC will offer one training and/or workshop related to protection case management per quarter, including refresher trainings of previous topics. . In total, NRC anticipates training 160 members of the protection cluster, including 60 protection partners on self-care and stress management, in addition to other topics including general protection case management, in consultation with the Protection Cluster (50% women), and 100 on protection mainstreaming (50% women), including PSEA and child safeguarding. 3. Protection Community Based Mechanisms and Psychosocial Support. NRC will address key PSS needs of conflict and displacement affected communities in Gaza with the aim to improve the psychosocial and general wellbeing of affected individuals and their capacity to cope with the shocks and stressors in both protracted conflict and displacement and acute emergencies. Psychosocial support activities will be comprised of non-focused community and family support (level 3). Activities will be provided by MA’AN. Ma’an’s staff have considerable expertise in social and psychological support, life skills, and GBV and child protection. In total, Ma’an will serve 12,301 unique beneficiaries (60% women). Of these, 200 persons will receive structured PSS, including 120 referred from NRC’s general protection case management services. Community events will reach 600 people.4. Civilian Self-Protection-, NRC will undertake a pilot of Civilian Self-Protection (CSP). This approach, with which NRC has had success in several protracted conflict situations, supports communities to optimise their existing capacities and experience to mitigate the risks associated with their self-protection activities. A CSP programme follows a broad four-stage process, which emphasises relationship and trust building with local actors, duty bearers, armed actors, and community members. NRC will initially undertake CSP baseline assessments and community targeting activities, and then work with targeted communities to develop and implement self-protection strategies. Actions taken under CSP action plans can include community-level monitoring and documentation of violence and IHL violations, as well as community-driven advocacy initiatives. All staff on the project, NRC and Ma’an, will receive PSEA and child safeguarding trainings. All beneficiaries will be alerted to NRC’s PSEA and child safeguarding policies, their rights under those policies, and reporting mechanism.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Specialized protection services entail social-work type of support for the most vulnerable people and work at direct contact with beneficiaries, through prompting behavioural, attitude and knowledge changes, NRC aims at enhancing conflict-affected people’ resilience and contributing to their psychosocial well-being. Protection case management services support persons facing acute protection risks beyond those experienced by the general population, as well as those whose vulnerability renders them unable to cover from protection risks without sustained support. Protection cases must be referred by other actors/sectors (for this project, referrals will not be accepted from the Gaza Protection Consortium programming) who have identified persons with protection needs. Case management offers a level of supportive intervention, which can include targeted PSS, coordinated referrals to other actors, and cash or other material assistance, that is more than the sum of its parts. All referrals within protection case management will be conducted according to the protocols that have been established by the Protection Cluster and relevant AoRs.Services are delivered by trained and specialized Protection Staff through information sharing, psychosocial support, and facilitation of linkage to additional services. An estimated 60% of individuals receiving Protection Case Management will receive some form of material assistance, whether cash or in-kind. Estimates are based on current assistance and MSNA results. Note that the above estimates refer ONLY to material assistance within Protection Case Management, and not other elements of the project, under which cash and in-kind assistance are not anticipated at this time. In facilitating a better protection case management system, NRC will offer training and other capacity building to members of the Protection Cluster, to ensure that various case management services are properly coordinated.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

For the purpose of accountability, NRC adopts participatory approaches and community consultations to ensure that people from the community and stakeholders are regularly consulted and engaged from the assessment phase throughout program implementation and evaluation phases.Through the Complaints and Feedback mechanism NRC is committed to strict standards of transparency and accountability in all its projects where everyone has the right to file a complaint and receive a response. In 2020 NRC Palestine has launched an active Digital Complaints and Feedback Mechanism, informative sessions on the use and application of NRC’s CFM were conducted for all staff and targeted populations in WB and Gaza. Through this digital CFM, beneficiaries can seek information requests, provide feedback and complaints through automated WhatsApp and Phone calls, including a designated email address and Complaint boxes at NRC offices to be used by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries during office walk-ins. All case workers will be trained in prevention of and response to SEA, especially with regard to trauma-sensitive and age appropriate techniques for case management of women, children, and GBV survivors (though NRC WILL NOT provide CP or GBV case management). All clients will be informed of reporting mechanisms, as well as their rights as case management clients, and NRC’s commitments regarding PSEA.Sensitive complaints will be treated as such and are processed through a “fast track” mechanism. All CFM staff will receive required training to be able to respond to sensitive complaints in person, and inform the PSEA FP to process the cases immediately. Such complaints will be classified as “NRC staff-related” and as “sensitive”. Once the complaint is marked as sensitive:For SEA and Child Abuse cases, the CFM FP sends an email immediately only to the PSEA FP (or Country Director) for follow up according to existing NRC internal procedures.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In2022, NRC conducted a country-wide protection mainstreaming self-assessment that included a significant component of Do No Harm. Based on that self-assessment and subsequent document review, the country office created a mainstreaming/DNH action plan. The Action Plan is intended to ensure all activities uphold NRC’s protection mainstreaming minimum standards and are rooted in the centrality of protection. Specifically in this project, all Protection specialized Staff with solid background in social-work, psychosocial support and community-based activities have been hired and are supported by a Protection PDM and regional case management adviser. A Protection risk analysis matrix have been developed to assess risks and mitigation measures developed including: a solid capacity building plan in place on Protection in humanitarian action, referrals, case management and psychosocial support and on NRC internal Protection SOPs and Global Protection standards and guidance; establishment of supervision mechanisms; update of the existing referral pathways, SOPs and relevant MoUs. A range of meetings with key stakeholders have been conducted including Protection Cluster, UNICEF and MoSD. NRC Staff can also access to a tailored psychosocial counselling provided by a specialized external contractor, to mitigate the impact of stress and exposure to grief, loss, and trauma, promoting self-care and avoid burn-out. NRC Protection Team will also receive trainings on GBV and Child Protection to be able to identify and refer cases requiring GBV and CP support according to AoRs guidance and pathways. Within Case Management, cash and other kinds of material assistance can be used to address protection concerns related to a person’s life, physical safety, psychological well-being, liberty, dignity and other fundamental human rights. These include situations where the concerned individuals or HHs are resorting to negative coping mechanisms generate protection issues, such as accepting exploitative job practices, self-restricting from accessing essential services, using child labor or child marriage, resorting to survival sex etc. due to a lack of resources and alternatives. The provision of cash and in-kind assistance will be accompanied by a protection action plan where individuals and/or households are supported to identify long-term strategies to address their protection concerns beyond the short-term cash intervention. The delivery of cash should further be coupled with individual counselling on safety and security measures in accessing and managing cash, how the assistance received can support the individual and/or household to address their protection need, and how to access services through referrals and follow up to other services providers if applicable. Being part of a broader Case Management support, there will be a regular follow-up of the case provided by the case worker and supervision on the use of cash and a post distributing monitoring process. Community-based responses, including CSP, include regular DNH analysis ‘stops’ to ensure teams regularly assess the risk to communities for engaging in certain activities and avoid risk transfer. NRC’s Protection Case Management SOPs, Community-based and PSS SOPs, will guide Staff and partners in the provision of protection support. Additionally, Staff will be trained on NRC Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA policy and reporting mechanism. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners, and associates (e.g., contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA policies and related mechanisms are included in the project design. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MEANINGFUL ACCESS: eligibility criteria for Protection intervention have been carefully established and adapted to the Palestinian contexts, ensuring the most vulnerable people and hardest to reach communities will be identified and supported in proportion to their need and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). Special attention will be paid to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services (e.g., elderly people, PWD, youth at risk, etc.).Protection programme is designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to- reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduce vulnerability and build resilience. Information dissemination and awareness: Protection main priorities will be to empower women, men, girls, and boys to have equal access to information and services. Protection will develop information and communication products to support IDPs/refugees and host communities’ access to basic services, rights, and entitlements. The project will also use a community-based and participatory approach to identify and train members of the communities to disseminate information, recognise protection concerns and refer cases to specialised service providers. Emphasis will be placed on the rights and issues of women and other vulnerable groups such as PWD and elderly people. Subject to needs assessment, the project may also provide financial and organisational support to implementing partner to strengthen their capacity and ensure sustainability of the intervention. Disability mainstreaming-NRC Protection Team has received training on inclusion and age, gender, and diversity approach and principles of disability inclusion will be mainstreamed across the project: awareness raising will be conducted on the rights of PWD, barriers faced in the society, and inclusion strategies. Participation: people with disabilities will be included across the all program cycle, and universal design and reasonable accommodation analyses conducted to prevent lack of access. PWD will be referred to specialised actors as necessary. NRC has also built through the GPC a partnership with Humanity and Inclusion on provision of multi-rehabilitation therapy, psychosocial support, and medical assistive devices. Cases in need of these service will be referred to HI. Data will be disaggregated for gender, age, refugee status and disability. NRC’s intervention seeks, to directly address gender disparity and promote gender equality to ensure the do no harm principle is upheld. Through a gender mainstreaming approach, NRC promotes a concerted effort to ensure that gender equality is promoted in all stage of the programme cycle. NRC will be engaging directly with girls, boys, women, and men on identifying gender specific needs and considerations across all programming. Specific focus will be invested in deliberately targeting female beneficiaries, female headed HHs, and women at risk. Activities will be tailored to encourage women and girls’ participation and will be designed to reach women and girls in their homes and communities as a central component to NRC’s protection programming. Gender sensitivity of this action is visible through: •Designing activities to equally benefit girls, boys, women, and men. A capacity building approach which seeks to equalize the power dynamics between genders and promotes gender equality safely without placing girls and women at higher risk of violence because of benefiting from these services. •Ensuring that we include mobile as well as static services to reach individuals, often women with caregiving responsibilities or people living with disabilities. •Hiring, to a minimum standard 50% female staff, and ensure female staff presence at all project sites. •Staff training on PSEA and dissemination, mechanisms and implementation of related activities, including awareness raising sessions. •Ensure staff are trained on GBV.
