Project Summary

According to the ESWG data shared in December 2019, the protracted crisis keeps a total of 7,060,019 children and teachers in need: 227,743 teachers, 262,474 from 3 to 4 years old children, 6.569.803 from 5 to 17 years old children are in need of assistance inside Syria. Children’s safety, wellbeing, and resilience are threatened by an array of risk factors. The lack of education has a differential impact on boys and girls, it could lead to early marriage for girls often as a coping strategy and to an increase in child labor for boys, including being pushed by financial pressure to join armed groups in exchange of sustenance, shelter and a salary. Out-of-school children are at heightened risk of protection risks, such as gender-based violence and child labor, while lack of gender-segregated facilities can be a negative factor for girls’ attendance.
Hostilities have seen over 1 in 3 schools damaged, destroyed or occupied, thus highlighting the need for school rehabilitation. DRC education intervention which increases the protection of children, and to the extent possible builds their internal capacities to cope with challenging circumstances and to self-protect, is therefore urgently needed. Without targeted learning programs to transition children back to formal education or assist them in not dropping out, children are at increased risk of a range of protection threats. In schools across Dara’a, Homs, Hama, and Aleppo surveyed by DRC, children’s inability to keep pace with education due to psychosocial issues, child labor, and early marriage was the 3 highest causes of dropping out of school. FGD participants across Homs and Hama consistently reported that unaddressed psychosocial support needs amongst children in their communities were a major child protection concern. It is therefore vital that students are supported too (re)integrate into the formal system and to provide teachers with the skills and knowledge to help children remain in school.

5 - Implementation of activities

DRC’s project includes the provision of non-formal non-accredited education programs (self-learning program (SLP), remedial education) to 34400 children (5-17 years, girls/boys). The NFE programmes will take place in MoE schools, therefore DRC will coordinate with the MoE for the school selection process, including conducting the school needs assessment and qualitative community FGDs. Following data collection, DRC will analyze the findings and finalize the target exact locations in coordination with the MoE. DRC will assess schools long-listed by MoE, targeting schools based on scoring criteria that account for a number of variables and rehabilitate 13 schools, establishing or rehabilitating 156 classrooms together as well as construct gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities in the same schools. In total 122 schools will be provided with running costs (cleaning material, recreational kits and stationery for school personnel) while 34400 children will receive school supplies, out of which 3500 children who participate in the OOSP will receive textbooks. 1562 teachers will participate in professional development opportunities as well as receive incentives for participating in the DRC non-formal education programme. 20 Parent-Teacher Associations (PTA) will be established in the targeted schools aiming to establish school-based governance and accountability mechanisms to support school operations. In all NFE programmes, the teachers and school administration will be responsible for the management of the classes, supervision of children and following up on attendance and absenteeism. DRC will conduct weekly classroom observation visits, collect student attendance and teacher attendance data and conduct focus group discussions (FGDs) with students, parents, and teachers to review the impact of the intervention.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Changes in the context situation can lead to increased risks, including displacement and restrictions of movement for the population and local partners. Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMeasure: Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the project's beneficiaries have sufficient resources to enact those plans.Risk: Teachers or other education personnel and students participate in physical punishment or bullying of students.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMeasure: Teachers and school personnel trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. The application of learning should be monitored as part of ongoing professional development and support. Risk: Unsafe learning spaces and insufficient/non-existent WASH facilities contribute to children not attending learning facilities.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMeasure: Learning facilities should be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This may include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electric, water, etc. School grounds should have fencing. All works should be supervised and monitored by technical personnel. The organization should ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work started.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Security situation deteriorates and DRC staff cannot reach the target areaMeasure: Continuous coordination with DRC Safety Department and local authorities to ensure access is prompted where feasible. DRC will maintain constant communication with donors should change in the areas of intervention be required.Risk: No access to government schools /significant implementation delays due to lack of cooperation with the Ministry of EducationMeasure: Establish open dialogue with MoE, regular meetings and timely reporting. DRC renews the annual MoU, a key partnership instrument, outlining project activities and expectations for cooperation. Submission of approval requests in advance and close cooperation with the ESWG.Risk: Children do not attend the programmeMeasure: DRC will design a schedule that is flexible for children to attend either morning or afternoon shifts. Children may be involved in child labour or domestic work therefore not able to attend classes. DRC will sensitise communities about the classes and encourage regular attendance by students. Risk: Teachers absenteeismMeasure: DRC will coordinate with the DoE/Schools to recruit teachers that are from existing schools/neighbourhoods. DRC Field Staff will do weekly monitoring, including reviewing staff attendance register. Risk: Increasing inflation and high depreciation of Syrian Pound with consequent increase of prices in the market. Contractors working in school rehabilitation may be affected by the constant increase in prices and might decide to withdraw from their contractual obligations, creating a delay in the implementation of the activities.Measure: DRC will conduct regular prices assessment in the market to monitor the situation. Maintain good relations and dialogue with suppliers.
