Project Summary

Following the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN) approach that IOM strongly supports and actively promotes in Cameroon, and building on the programmes and activities implemented in the recent years, IOM plans comprehensive emergency interventions for 2024 focusing especially on coordination, shelter, mental health support (health), psychosocial support (protection) and WASH interventions. IOM’s multisectoral response aims at contributing to reinforce life-saving assistance and to improve well-being of displaced and the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis in the Far North region of Cameroon. Critical data on displacement situation, alerts as well as humanitarian needs following our DTM methodology will be provided to the humanitarian community in the frame of the Coordination Sector. IOM will also distribute shelter and NFI kits using cash-modality solutions when possible (bearing in mind that lack of civil documentation poses a huge challenge) and help the construction for most vulnerable people. In the WASH response, IOM will drill and rehabilitate boreholes, construct water points, latrines and other infrastructures and conduct hygiene sensitization and promotion activities. Finally, IOM multisectoral assistance will also include mental health activities including direct MHPSS assistance using PMTs, reinforcing
referral mechanism and capacity building of health actors (Health sector), while also directly providing psychosocial support to populations impacted by crisis by reinforcing safety and social network as well as strengthening community capacity to provide psychosocial support to victims of GBVs (Protection sector). These interventions are complementary to other programmes IOM implements in the region, focusing on DDR or social cohesion and by creating synergies within such initiatives, aim at achieving the HDP collective outcome set by the National HDPN Taskforce of Cameroon : “the most vulnerable populations in the convergence areas or in other regions affected by the crises recover indiscriminately their fundamental rights and improve their physical well-being and social welfare.”
The project will target various localities in the Far North region considering the level of needs and vulnerabilities observed and consulted with coordination with each sector of planned interventions. When feasible and appropriate, participative targeting workshops involving local community members and Humanitarian, Development and Peacebuilding partners in the localities will also be held.
IOM respects its procedures to handle complaints on SEA starting with a focal person to receive and handle complaints and the building of knowledge of IOM staff and IPs about IOM’s policy and mechanisms to prevent SEA, including training on the proper conduct to handle SEA disclosures. IOM will also make usage of the Far North GBV referral pathway to refer SEA cases.
Through activities, direct feedback from beneficiaries and community members will be gained via IPs and also directly by IOM staff during field visits and visits of the M&E/AAP independent team in accordance with the wider IOM AAP emergencies workplan. AAP focal points will be assigned in Maroua and Yaounde respectively, with clear SOPs to be in place to capture complaints from beneficiaries and to take any appropriate action. Members of IP will be trained on topics such as the proper handling of disclosures of gender-based violence (GBV) incidents, aspects of confidentiality, and respectful communication in high-stress environments.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2013, ongoing hostilities and violence in the Far North Region continue to trigger lower magnitude displacements and community shocks due to sporadic attacks by armed groups designated as terrorist organizations (AGDTO) such as Boko Haram and the Islamic State West Africa Province. Attacks are not only limited to clashes with Government security forces but are carried out on nearby communities, with as a result significant number of displaced persons uprooted as well as returnees. Furthermore, intercommunity violence between different ethnic groups over lack of resources and competing livelihoods is occurring more periodically, most recently in the Logone Birni arrondissement. Insecurity in this region is exacerbating the already limited access of displaced populations to essential social services as looting and destruction of goods and public infrastructure are becoming more recurrent, further harming community cohesion and leading to higher inter-community tensions. Currently, all humanitarian response actors are struggling to meet the large humanitarian needs due to a low level of humanitarian funding and an overall low visibility. Low funding has meant that there is a greater need for the reinforcement of a Rapid Response Mechanism which requires reliable and quickly available data on alerts. IOM’s last multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted in the Far-North region (August 2023) showed a variety of needs relative to individual sectors. Shelter/NFI was identified as the second priority need with situation still remains worrying for the households surveyed. This can be explained by the increase in mobile populations over the last 6 months, and by the fact that many mobile households live in makeshift shelters: 45% of refugees outside camps, 29% of IDPs and 24% of returnees. For the health sector, 49% of women give birth in a health facility which is still a long way from the target of 100% of women giving birth in a health facility, assisted by qualified personnel. Those who manage to get to the nearest health facility in an emergency remained at 68%, and yet 82% take more than 15 minutes to reach the health center. As outlined in the protection sector response plan, reduce GBV risks, reduce exposure of children to arm aspects of war and improve protection and respect of fundamental rights for all the persons affected by crisis are the main issues to be addressed. In particular, there is a very limited capacity in the health centers in terms of mental health, and IOM has been providing capacity building to staff and specific drugs to these centers. For food, a large majority of the communities consulted (81%) recognize education as a right for every child and agree that it is important for every child to have access to quality educational services. This trend remains constant, regardless of the status of the stakeholders involved. The three main sources of household food are own activity (69%), purchase at the local market (19%), work for food (4%), and it has been noted that the majority of households surveyed consume the cheapest food as a coping strategy, accounting for 88%. Overall, food stocks are insufficient in all departments, with the majority of households having no stock at all (85%), and over half of households (58%) having stock with a duration of less than one month. The majority of respondents consume the least expensive foods as a coping strategy, accounting for 88%.
