Project Summary

Project aims at restoring basic water and sanitation services and supporting good hygiene practices for highly vulnerable populations in areas of return and facilitate takeover of care and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities by local authorities in Ninewa, Salah Al Din, Anbar and Kirkuk governorates. The restoration and rehabilitation of damaged WASH infrastructure in conflict-affected areas of the four governorates will contribute to WASH cluster objective number 2 to support highly vulnerable displaced families who are willing to return to their homes but are unable to do so without sufficient access to basic WASH services. Access to a clean and easily available water supply is one of the most important aspects of daily life, and to support those who seek to return Tdh will focus specifically on rehabilitation of WTP/pumping station/boreholes, water networks, school and , health center WASH facilities, improve solid waste management in the school, health centers and in the communities, and roll out hygiene promotion in close cooperation with DoWs, DoEs, DoHs, mayors. Furthermore the project will support water and waste management at household level with the local communities. The response will also seek to maintain a WASH emergency response capacity an contribute to cluster objective number 3 by pre-positioning WASH NFI kits in high risk areas.

Needs

The project covers portion of Ninewa, Salah Al Din, Anbar and Kirkuk governorates, with a specific focus on areas of return with high extent of damage: Telafar, Sinjar, Baiji, Shirqat, Tooz, Hawija, Anah, Rawa, Qaim districts, which witnessed multiple outbreaks of fighting since 2014, causing high extent of damage to local infrastructure. Most of the residents fled the districts in the early stages of the war as the living situation became untenable. As of November 2018, the number of returnees is steadily increasing with governmental incentives, creating more pressure on the provision of public services. October 2018 DTM database recorded 1.5 million returnees in Ninewa, 1.3 million in Anbar, 580 000 in Salah Al Din and 320 000 in Kirkuk. Public infrastructure needs to be renovated to ensure minimum standard of health, education and water access. Furthermore the local authorities are only slowly resuming their responsibilities in service provision due to their limited capacities. Water access in these areas is unreliable and sporadic and 50% ( according to WASH Cluster) of the population experience challenges accessing drinking water, especially due to the fact that there is only limited time of running electricity per day to power the water pumps. Though water can sometimes be collected from water trucks, this is not a sustainable solution for the water concerns and underlines the need for repair of water infrastructure According to the Department of Water for the four governorates, a gap remains to be bridged in terms of public water supply. Some of the Water Treatment Plants (WTPs) were or are being repaired by the government or other NGOs in the area, with some repairs still to be carried out. This means that majority of suburban residential areas where Tdh responds are receiving untreated water pumped from the river, creating a serious public health risk (such as diarrhoea and skin conditions). According to multiple REACH Assessments people perceived low quality of drinking water as a cause for multiple health problems. Challenges remain even for the areas with functioning WTPs as some households remain without connection to the public water network. This is due to cuts in the water pipelines caused by the conflict. This further underlines the urgent need for repairs. Tdh has been carrying out repair work of both WTPs and water networks in all four governorates and has carried out assessments for future works on specific parts of the public water infrastructure. Based on DoE and DoH data of the four above mentioned governorates, the schools and health center still suffer from major infrastructure damages and extreme needs were highlighted specifically for WASH facilities. Many schools are open with hundreds of students every day with very limited access to latrines and/or drinkable water, even at minimum emergency standards,, putting students at health risks. Furthermore the lack of sanitation facilities creates a protection risk as these cannot be separated for boys and girls. According to Tdh assessment many schools had problems dealing with waste management leading to yet another factor increasing the health risk. UNDP is largely present in the areas but cannot cover all schools. Tdh is ready to cover needs with installation of emergency latrines or toilet block rehabilitation in the above mentioned districts, as well as any other minor repairs as needed.

Protection Risk Analysis

Continuous risk analysis informs Tdh programming all over the programme cycle. The risk analysis takes into consideration protection risks of beneficiaries and staff and it is supported at coordination level and field level by dedicated staff such as: Risk Advisor, Field Coordinators, Liason Officers. Also, a Risk Department at HQ level ensure technical guidance and streamline with internal and inter-agency procedures. Also, dedicated risk analysis are designed to ensure compliance with internal Child Safeguarding policies and PSEA policies. Risk analysis takes into consideration specific needs of boys and girls, women and men, persons living with disabilities. Specific needs of vulnerable groups are also considered and in particular marginalised group, minorities, groups non protected by social floor. The following risks for 2019 within theWash response have been singled out as medium-high ranked: 1) Operational access: security as well as clearance from the authorities continue to be intermittent in most of the areas of implementation, with a specific focus on Hawija, Tooz, Baiji, Sherqat, Al Qaim, Sinjar and Tal Afar. 2) Equitable access to services: For highlighted affected area of return an equitable access to safe and sufficient water remains one of the priority needs for post conflict affected populations, especially for the vulnerable groups who cannot afford water trucking. The limited access would affect the female headed households and elderly more than other groups. Furthermore the access to services is often prioritized by local authorities based on ethnic and religious criteria in the four above mentioned governorates. These vulnerable groups would thus face higher health risks which makes them even more vulnerable. 3) Safe access to water and sanitation for women and girls at household and school level are a concern, especially since gender segregated facilities are not always available. Lack of these facilities affect more extensively the girls when it comes to enrolment and absenteeism at school. 4) Access of persons with disabilities to WASH infrastructure is limited and lack of household level water access makes them vulnerable and dependent. At schools the lack of accessible sanitation facilities makes the attendance almost impossible for this portion of the population. 5) UXO risk is a prevalent risk in all 4 governorates where Tdh is intervening, especially when it comes to infrastructure repair since it entails extensive ground engineering work.

Mitigation Measures

On institutional level the response to potential risk is supported at coordination level and field level by dedicated staff such as: Risk Advisor, Field Coordinators, Liason Officers. Also, a Risk Department at HQ level ensure technical guidance and streamline with internal and inter-agency procedures. Also, dedicated risk analysis are designed to ensure compliance with internal Child Safeguarding policies and PSEA policies. 1) Operational access is monitored by dedicated trained staff through a set of key actions carried out by a dedicated team at coordination and field level, among them: field coordinator, liason officer, risk advisor. Daily monitoring of safe access to the area of intervention it is carried out at base level and through inter-base networking. Access is prepared at the beginning of the operations and all along the programme through the establishing of field-based networking of key informants and by continuous coordination with local authorities, communities national and international actors. Incidents are avoided also thanks to a spread efforts in making Tdh’s mandate and action appropriately known by key stakeholders. Security plan and contingency plan are active to respond to crises and incidents. Acceptance, as an important element of access, is built on a set a fundamental activities, designed to ensure accountability to affected communities principles to be put into practice. Continuous consultation with communities inform programming. A flexible approach allows Tdh to re-designed activities according to feedbacks and suggestions. Feedback mechanisms are systematically integrated into Monitoring Plan and evaluation plan. Tools and methodologies are expressively designed and contextualised to ensure voice to women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, marginalised groups. Also, Tdh’s approach within the targeted areas of implementation is conceived to build up on existing resources, including community-based resources and governmental resources. Locally hired staff represent a key informant in ensuring that activities and approaches are culturally appropriated and conflict sensitive. 2) Tdh always assess the needs in the area of response and selects the location with highest needs among the vulnerable groups. Furthermore Tdh seeks a constructive continuous dialogue with local authorities focusing on ensuring the equitable access to WASH services for the whole population regardless of their religious or ethnic affiliations. 3) Especially at school level Tdh carries out a joint child protection/WASH assessment to identify all protection concerns and always seeks to segregate the sanitation facilities for boys and girls. A challenged access to most marginalised groups it is mitigated by continuous risk assessment, that it is designed to take into account expressed needs and concerns of affected communities, local authorities, national and international actors, host communities. Consultations systematically seeks to collect voices from IDPs communities, host communities, returnees, boys and girls, adolescents boys and girls, women and men, marginalised groups and minorities. 4)Tdh includes accessibility features in its technical design of WASH facilities at schools. Blanket coverage of the residential areas with water network rehabilitation and household level access should decrease the gap of access to water between person with and without disabilities. 5) Tdh coordinates with local security forces as wel as iMMAP for the latest updates on UXO risks and areas cleared to minimize the risk of injury as much as possible

How is your project accountable to affected population

Tdh is accountable to affected population, whose involvement as a key participant in all phases of the project cycle in crucial to ensure meaningful participation of the beneficiary community. Tdh incorporates and promotes the five IASC Commitments to Accountability to Affected Population and framed its entire response within a Tdh Quality and Accountability framework. 1) Leadership/Governance: Tdh promotes leadership and governance, and ensures that staff is aware that programs and approaches must be in line with accountability to affected population principles. This means a commitment to build on the capacities of local population and strengthen the resilience of people affected, whenever possible. Collaboration and consultation with local actors is crucial to ensure that the implementation of activities meet the needs of the affected population and will encourage beneficiaries to actively participate. Participation of boys and girls, women and men and person with disabilities to decision making is systematically sought. 2) Transparency: Tdh ensures the transparency of communication with the affected population to help both Tdh and beneficiaries make informed decisions. There is a focus on providing clear and transparent information about consequences and risks, particularly related to essential services for beneficiaries. At all stages of the project cycle, continuous and transparent communication on Tdh’s activities and objectives will be conveyed to local authorities all other actors operating in the area, with feedback mechanisms also in place to allow for reciprocal information sharing. Tdh will also observe full coordination and information sharing with relevant cluster and sub-cluster actors both at national and governorate levels. 3) Feedback and Complaints: Feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child friendly and adequate to the age and the gender of beneficiaries are set up. These mechanisms are designed to be inclusive, accessible, confidential and will not expose participants to any harm. Feedback and complaints will be systematically considered, and focus group discussions will be regularly conducted with beneficiaries to gather feedback and report on achievement in integrating feedback in the response. This will allow Tdh to assess the level of beneficiary satisfaction and adapt the design of activities and approach to implementation to meet the current needs of beneficiaries. Tdh will take all necessary measures to ensure operationalization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) and PSEA policy, including the identification of a Child Safeguarding focal point, the setup of safe and accessible reporting mechanisms and the adoption of measures to ensure data protection, confidentiality and dignity. All Tdh employees, partners and visitors are carefully briefed on Tdh’s CSP and must read, understand and sign the policy before starting any assignment, collaboration or visit. 4) Participation: Affected populations will play an active role in decision-making processes, ensuring that principles of accountability is incorporated at all stage of the project cycle. To ensure comprehensive representation of beneficiaries, a particular focus will be given to child participation, specifically girls and children with disabilities. During all activities, children will be encouraged to participate and speak out. A wide range of participatory activities will be implemented to make sure that all opinions, and above opinions of those more marginalized, are considered. Community-based design activities are prioritized. 5) Design, Monitoring and Evaluation: A participatory design, monitoring and evaluation of activities is ensured through ongoing consultation with beneficiaries. Focus groups discussion, bilateral and groups informal meetings and other activities will constantly aim at improving the capacity of the project to better meet beneficiaries’ needs and collect best practices and recommendations.
