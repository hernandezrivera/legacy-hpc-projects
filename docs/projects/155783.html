Project Summary

Federally Administered Tribal Area (FATA) has been merged in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Province as a result of 25th Amendment on 31 May 2018.
Between 2017 and 2018, some 60,121 families have returned to their areas of origin in five tribal districts of KP. 162,326 children in age-bracket of 3-18 years returned to 5 KP Merged Districts. (Source: UNHCR, 2018 Fact sheet). The erstwhile FATA, EMIS report for 2016-17 shows that more than one million (1,038,933) children in the age group of 04-14 are out of school in the KP merged Districts areas. The overall drop-out rate in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 76% (74% Boys, 80% Girls).
According to erstwhile FATA, EMIS Report 2015-16, 1,036 schools (425 girl’s schools) in KP merged districts are non-functional due to the prevailing situation. The overall Retention Rate from Kachi to Class-5 in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 32%. The latest RRU reports indicate that there are 943 educational institutions which are partially/fully damaged in five KP Merged Districts. It is noteworthy that these 943 schools are partially or fully damaged whereas 1,145 schools are non-functional, mainly because of teacher’s unavailability.
In order to address the above-mentioned issues and problems, Friends Welfare Association(FWA) will intervene in two KP Merged districts, i.e. Khyber and Orakzai. Friends Welfare Association will target a caseload of fifteen hundred (1500) children in two (02) before mentioned KP Merged districts. In target two KP merged districts.
In the proposed project, FWA will Constitution/Revitalize the School Management Committee /Taleemi Islahi Jirgas in the selected schools. In case, where PTC-SMC/TIJ do not exist or are not functional, FWA will organize PTCs/ (TIJs) through community engagement (social mobilization of communities). The organization will address infrastructure needs of schools identified in the school improvement plans through establishing TLCs by installing Pre-Fabricated Structures where schools are fully damaged and provision of gender-appropriate WASH facilities. Cash stipend will be provided at primary level, and for girls at secondary level. This initiative of conditional cash transfer scheme will be undertaken to enroll and retain children, particularly girls’ students in schools who would be otherwise dropout.
Provision of teaching and learning supplies including School Supplies, recreational kits, school bags and stationery will be ensured at target 30 schools in target two KP merged districts. FWA will provide continuous professional development to teachers, head teachers at target 30 schools to refresh their knowledge and skills in participatory teaching methods, multi-grade teaching methodologies, psychosocial support delivery, mine-risk and DRR. In order to enroll/retain the out of school children who are in upper school going age or over-age and have never been to any schooling before or dropout, a system of Accelerated learning will be incorporated in target 30 Government schools of the target two KP merged districts.

Needs

The educational status in KP Merged Districts is already lower than the national averages, have suffered significantly because of insecurity and displacement. In KP merged districts, the majority of the population remains illiterate – according to the Pakistan Social and Living Standards Measurement (PSLM) 2012-13 Survey Report, the adult literacy rate (10+ years) for KP Merged Districts is 24% (female 10.5%, male 36.7%) while Net Enrollment Rate is only 32% (female 24%, male 41%). Between 2017 and 2018, some 60,121 families have returned to their areas of origin in five tribal districts of KP. 162,326 children in age-bracket of 3-18 years returned to 5 KP Merged Districts. (Source: UNHCR 2018 Fact sheet). The erstwhile FATA, EMIS report for 2016-17 shows that more than one million (1,038,933) children in the age group of 04-14 are out of school in the KP merged Districts areas. The overall drop-out rate in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 76% (74% Boys, 80% Girls). According to erstwhile FATA, EMIS Report 2015-16, 1,036 schools (425 girl’s schools) in KP merged districts are non-functional due to the prevailing situation. The overall Retention Rate from Kachi to Class-5 in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 32%. The latest reports indicate that there are 943 educational institutions which are partially/fully damaged in five KP Merged Districts. It is noteworthy that these 943 schools are partially or fully damaged whereas 1,145 schools are non-functional, mainly because of teacher’s unavailability. As per the KP merged districts, Vulnerability Assessment (August-September 2017) report around half of the respondents expressed dissatisfaction with the quality of education due to lack of basic facilities, lack of teachers, teaching materials and damage to school infrastructure. In terms of damaged infrastructure, the RRU data shows that 943 educational institutions were partially and fully damaged in five KP Merged Districts.As according to UNHCR 30th September 2016 Fact sheet, 162,326 children, in age-bracket of 3-18 years returned to 5 KP merged Districts. The girls and boys of the returnee families need access to safe, inclusive and quality education to reduce the risk of exploitation and abuse, establish routine, provide a supportive environment, and equip them with life skills. The Protection Cluster Report (2014) observed that psychological stress among children remains a major concern for displaced children. This issue must be addressed both in displacement and, even more, upon return to the areas of origin. The current education situation is further aggravated by persistent attacks on schools, causing closures and compelling teachers and students, especially girls, to stay at home. The latest reports indicate that there are 943 educational institutions partially/fully damaged in five KP merged districts where teaching, learning materials and hygiene and sanitation have been compromised due to militancy and security operations. It is noteworthy that these 943 schools are partially or fully damaged whereas 1,145 schools are non-functional, mainly because of teacher’s unavailability. The multi-cluster assessment of KP merged districts’ returnees in August 2015 confirmed that education facilities were badly affected; most school buildings were assessed to be either partially or completely damaged. The assessment also confirmed that the situation is compounded by a lack of education supplies and security threats to students and teachers especially female teachers and girls. The assessment team noted that there was a slight increase in schools’ enrollment compared to the period before displacement, especially for girls.

Activities

Activities1. CL2/CA1Establishment of transitional school facilities, and rehabilitation of damaged institutions with adequate WASH facilities:This activity will address the infrastructural needs of ten (10) schools of which at least fifty (50) girls schools identified in the school improvement plans through establishing TLCs by installing Pre-Fabricated Structures, small rehabilitation work and provision of gender-appropriate WASH facilities.Where schools are fully damaged, the project will provide transitional school facilities, which are aligned with the government’s ‘Guidelines for Education Sector – Rehabilitation and Reconstruction of Schools’. Reusable materials (wood, iron roofing, prefabricated structures) will be encouraged so that the material can be utilized elsewhere or at the same vicinity as additional space after the schools are reconstructed/rehabilitated. WASH (Water, Sanitation and Hygiene) in schools is a component of child-friendly school interventions that supports efforts of ensuring children go to schools that provide a sustainable safe, healthy and comfortable environment where children grow, learn and thrive. At target schools allocated to FWA in target two KP Merged Districts, WASH facilities in schools will be provided to access safe child friendly drinking water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities along with hygiene promotion in order to improve health and wellbeing of the children (boys and girls), enhance girls’ attendance, boost educational achievement, and promote gender equity. 2. CL2/CA2Cash stipend to all primary-level children and secondary level girls for enrollment and retention: Cash stipend will be provided to fifteen hundred (15,00) children at primary level, and for girls at secondary level at target two KP merged districts. This initiative of conditional cash transfer scheme will be undertaken to enroll and retain children, particularly girls’ students in schools who would be otherwise dropout. 3. CL2/CA3Capacity Building of Teachers on child friendly teaching, psycho-social support, mine-risk and DRR: FWA will provide professional development trainings to teachers, head teachers from targeted 30 schools to refresh their knowledge and skills in participatory teaching methods, multi-grade teaching methodologies, psycho-social support, mine-risk and DRR. 4. CL2/CA4Provision of Accelerated Learning education to over-age, dropped out children:In order to enroll/retain the out of school children who are in upper school going age or overage and have never been to any schooling before or dropout, a system of fast track learning or Accelerating learning will be incorporated in the target 30 Government schools of two KP Merged Districts.5. CL2/CA5Formation of school management committees for improved school-community linkages:The need assessment will be conducted to help establish the presence/status of SMC-PTC/TIJ in the target 30 schools of target two KP merged districts i.e. Khyber and Orakzai. In case, where PTC-SMC/TIJ do not exist or are not functional, FWA will organize or reactivate SMC-PTCs/ (TIJs) through community engagement (social mobilization of communities). This would involve community dialogue, one-on-one sensitization sessions, house to house visits and public awareness raising. The newly organized SMCs-PTCs will be notified with the district Education Offices of the target two KP Merged Districts. The PTCs/ TIJs will be organized/reactivated as per the government’s guidelines. 6. CL2/CA6Provision of teaching and learning supplies:A robust mechanism of coordination with Government will be developed by linking the SMCs-PTCs/TIJs and concerned Education Departments of target two KP Merged Districts. Provision of school supplies (school-in-a-box), recreation kits, school bags and stationery will be done to target 30 schools.
