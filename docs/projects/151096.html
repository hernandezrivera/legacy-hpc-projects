Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable and affected populations population in the regions of eastern Ukraine most affected by the armed conflict (Donetsk, Luhansk, Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia and Kharkiv regions) where UNFPA has already built the key elements of a response mechanism as part of its humanitarian interventions. It will place an increasing emphasis on strengthening community resilience, with particular attention to supporting most vulnerable population.
Differentiated approaches to programming will continue to be applied in 2019 considering the prevailing humanitarian situation and its evolution:
• The primary focus for humanitarian response will remain to be on the communities along the contact line (Donetsk and Luhansk regions).
• Critical needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and host communities members will be prioritized in other three conflict affected eastern regions (Kharkiv, Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia) with a more gradual transition toward longer term solutions for response to GBV.
• As humanitarian access to the NGCA for protection activities remains restricted, the project will seek alternative opportunities for partnerships with local actors; will reach out to those in need through remote solutions such as national and regional hotlines and social media; and will use existing access points such as healthcare facilities for delivering aid.
UNFPA supported models of service delivery will eventually build trust to those services among survivors and trigger their willingness to report their cases and seek help. Engagement of non-state actors (local NGO’s, FBO’s, CBO’s) to act as service providers and/or actors on GBV prevention at the local community level, working with affected population of those communities, their opinion leaders, ATO veterans and their family members will strengthen the prevention pillar of the overall GBV response mechanism and strategy and diversify sources of support to those at risk of GBV.
NGOs and community groups representing the voices of IDPs and survivors of GBV will be directly involved into the project work through inclusion in its governing structures and working groups, elaborating policy and guideline documents at the national and regional levels as well as through involvement in project monitoring activities.
Finally, targeted communication and advocacy campaign will eventually support change of attitudes towards GBV, eliminate stereotypes among community members, particularly young people and lead to less tolerance towards GBV in the affected communities . Awareness raising sessions and events will continue to be conducted with communities where PSS MTs operate, as well as with staff of difference service providers to promote responsive behavior and active participation in monitoring the GBV situation in their communities and providing/referring those in need to respective services.
A monitoring and evaluation plan, defined through previous interventions to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, are used to ensure quality of project results. This plan describes the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the project implementers and beneficiaries and between them. Monitoring visits will be conducted by UNFPA M&E team to follow up on the outcome and impact of project activities. Additionally evaluation of PSS mobile team model will be conducted in order to analyses and verify its functionality and impact, measure the achievements and flaws, draw out lessons learned, and most importantly to develop evidence-based practical recommendations for further institutionalization and development of the model integrating it into the existing system of PSS services provision in conflict effected regions. The process will engage all PSS project stakeholders at the local communities levels, local service providers, GBV response referral networks, PSS MT members, PSS service clients, local communities where MTs operate, possibly local media. While all stakeholders are important sources of information for the evaluation, the direct beneficiaries – GBV survivors will be put in the center of attention.
A feedback mechanism from project beneficiaries (GBV survivors, local service providers and partners, local authorities responsible for GBV response and coordination) will be operating, including through regular GBV WGs and GBV SC meetings, National Toll Free Hot Line on prevention of violence to make sure that the opinions of target vulnerable population are heard and taken into consideration. Complaints and suggestions related to the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to.
In order to achieve maximum complementarity and potentiate the results, the project will seek to coordinate, and to the extent possible work jointly, with other complementary projects and initiatives in the area of gender equality and GBV (OSCE, Council of Europe, EUAM, UN agencies, etc.) or those working to rehabilitate and develop local social infrastructure and institutional capacity (such as GIZ, UNDP, UNHCR, UNICEF)

Needs

Gender based violence remains a significant protection concern and imposes life threatening risks to the most vulnerable among IDPs and host communities members in the conflict affected areas of Ukraine, particularly in the areas close to the contact line. High concentration of military and armed groups – coupled with a proliferation of weapons, weak law enforcement and impunity – has increased the risk of GBV for people living along the ‘contact line’, particularly women, adolescent girls and young men. Additional risks include the worsening economy, high unemployment and limited livelihood opportunities. Women, girls and boys experiencing different forms of violence are often forced to remain in unsafe living environments because there are no alternatives. Displaced women and adolescent girls are particularly at risk (based on GBV cases reported to UNFPA mobile teams, among IDPs 88% of victims were women and girls). There is a lack of access to vital information and services, including shelters for GBV survivors. The conflict has also led to heightened levels of domestic violence due to increased tolerance of violence in society, easier access to weapons and PTSD among former combatants. Stigma surrounding GBV, and a lack of services and referral pathways prevent survivors, including women and girls, from receiving adequate support. Survival sex, including by minors, is being reported, further increasing risk to mental, physical and reproductive health. Of special importance is a need to continue scaling up psychological support services for GBV survivors. Furthermore, service delivery needs to be coordinated and integrated in order to ensure the client gets comprehensive treatment including physical protection and shelter; medical care, including HIV/STI treatment (e.g. post exposure prophylaxis); psychological counselling; social support; and legal redress (from either a one stop shop service or through referrals). Survivors of violence and those at risk of violence need to have access to “safe spaces”. Access to life-saving information and services, including shelter for GBV survivors, improvement in referrals and multi-sectorial coordination remain a key protection need. As of today, about 5,393 survivors (816 IDPs) were reached via primary PSS through PSS MTs in 2 eastern oblasts of Luhansk (GCA) and Donetsk (GCA), and 4,880 survivors (581 IDPs) in 3 conflict-effected regions of Kharkiv, Zaporizhya and Dnipro. Only 88 out of them received complex psychosocial assistance services from UNFPA-supported shelters in Sloviansk and Mariupil and 208 survivors (19 IDPs) received complex psychosocial assistance services from UNFPA-supported shelters in Kharkiv, Krasnopavlivka, Kryvyi Rig and Berdiansk

Activities & outcomes

1. Essential GBV services (PSS mobile teams and referral to health, “safe space”, protection, livelihood) are available and accessible for the most vulnerable women and girls living in the areas close to the contact line (also along the contact line).2. Regional GBV WGs are established within reginal coordination bodies and have the capacity to lead multi sectoral response to GBV.3. Regional costed Actions Plans (programmes) on multi sectoral response and prevention of GBV developed.4. Essential GBV service packages adopted in five eastern regions.5. General and specialized PSS service providers have the capacity to effectively deliver integrated services for the protection of GBV survivors.6 Regional healthcare system has the capacity to identify survivors of physical and sexual violence and provide them with services according to international standards.7 Policing and justice services, including legal support to GBV survivors, are integrated as part of multi sectoral GBV response and prevention systems at the regional level.8 National level government led information and awareness raising campaign on GBV response and prevention launched in 5 eastern regions.9. Local service providers (including providers operating along the contact line) equipped with information materials on GBV and incapacitated to implement community level GBV prevention, advocacy and communication actions.
