Project Summary

The recent military operations in North-East Syria has put the region into turmoil and made it uninhabitable for the native people who had just begun settling down. Many civilians (including women and children) lost their lives as a result of the regional unrest. As the families living in the area saw that war and destruction are once again at their doorsteps, they faced the unavoidable choice of leaving their homes to safer places, like Ar Raqqa and Deir Ezzur, where the military operations are not taking place.
The seriousness of the security situation in the North East region forced the families to leave at such a panicky pace in which they could barely pack anything. As a result, they arrived at the shelters lacking essential items to live. The main needs identiﬁed among the newly displaced households are items such as blankets, mattresses, water jerry cans, and cooking ware. As the number of newly displaced HHs is very big, neither the local authorities nor acting partners in the region have been able to accommodate everyone’s needs so far.

SWEDO intends to take immediate initiative and respond to the needs of 2,000 IDP households in Deir Ezzur and Ar Raqqa. SWEDO’s preparedness and qualifications to undertake this project come from its long history in distributing items on needy people together with its extensive knowledge and access to the target areas. SWEDO is well known for its speed at deploying staff to the field and the efficiency at which the projects are implemented. This project will target the newly displaced HHs and will strive to equip them with the essential non-food items that they need. All the items which the project intends to distribute on the needy HHs will be those included in SNFI Cluster’s Kits Guide. The project, acknowledging and reflecting the gender-and-age-specific needs, will support vulnerable households by alleviating their sufferings as well as enhancing their living conditions.

4 - Needs

Since the escalation of the conflicts in October 2019, very large waves of displacement occurred from the areas of conflicts in the north, which are Ras Al Ain, Tel Abiad and Tal Tamer. Most of the IDPs have traveled south, some of them to Deir Ezzur and others to Ar Raqqa. According to data collected by SWEDO from recent HNAP and REACH reports, the locations which contain the greatest number of vulnerable newly-arrived IDP households are the cities of Hajin (4,500 IDP Households), Al Sour (3,000 IDP Households), Al Busayrah (6,000 IDP Households) and Diban (4,000 IDP Households) in the Deir Ezzor Governorate with a total estimated number of 17,500 vulnerable IDP households. Also, Ar Raqqa City hosts about 3,600 IDP households. The assessments showed that Shelter and non-food items were among the three main priority needs in about %60 of the communities which the IDPs are living in. The availability of the non-food items was found to be high in the local markets, but the affordability among the IDPs was found to be very low. The main core relief item needs identiﬁed in these locations are items such as blankets, mattresses, water jerry cans, cooking ware and most importantly, hygiene items. On the other hand, the main shelter needs were lack of lighting, privacy, locks and heat insulation inside the shelters. Not to mention those who are lacking shelter, as well as those who have rented a shelter but are unable to continue paying the rent. The gap is very big and pervasive, and it hasn’t been fully responded to so far since neither the local authorities nor acting partners in the region have been able to accommodate these needs of the IDPs in Deir Ezzur as well as outside camp settlements. Deir Ezzor is known to be the hardest location to reach within NES by humanitarian actors due to the ﬂuid security situation. It is therefore of urgent importance that an NGO having access to Deir Ezzur take initiative and respond to the needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

SWEDO already has ongoing projects and active oﬃces with staff in NES. To start this project, an oﬃce will be established and the staff necessary for this project will be hired. Following that, SWEDO will coordinate with the SNFI cluster as well as the local authorities to ensure duplication of services does not happen and also to agree on geographical locations for the activities to take place in. An assessment will be conducted to identify the most vulnerable households. At the same time, the core and essential items kits will be purchased either locally or from the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, depending on the market research considering factors such as price and quality. Once the kits are ready, they will be delivered to the identiﬁed households. Based on the assessment, it will be decided whether the IDP households will core and essential NFIs or life-saving and life-sustaining shelter. All in all, 2,000 crisis-affected IDP households will have their core and essential non-food items needs met. The end result will be that the vulnerable households’ suffering is alleviated and their living conditions are enhanced. All the activities will be documented: photos will be taken and signatures will be collected.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK: Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members.MITIGATION: One standard kit will be distributed on all the target beneficiaries. Likewise, the target beneficiaries will be those having similar needs. RISK: Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees.MITIGATION: Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. RISK: Abuse of Authority by distribution staff (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion).MITIGATION: Staff will be trained on GBV and will sign the organization's Code of Conduct. At the same time, there will be a dedicated phone number for complaints will be broadcasted on the beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be able to bring any wrong act into the attention of SWEDO.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. RISK: NFI quantities may not cover all IDPs in the targeted locations since the number of IDPs is changing. MITIGATION: SWEDO will coordinate closely with the SNFI cluster to cover the most vulnerable IDPs by arranging the distribution with other NGOs to distribute the same items in the same period. 2. RISK: Distributions can be delayed due to security situations, bad weather, and logistical obstacles.MITIGATION: The project team will put a clear plan for the distribution to avoid any logistical problems and follow up on the security situation closely to distribute the supplies safely. 3. RISK: Unexploded Ordnance (UXO) and Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) risks in the areas of operations.MITIGATION: Civil NGOs that are specialized in cleaning such threats will be coordinated to remove them safely before starting the renovation, preventing any risk of death or injuries of householders and hired laborers. 4. RISK: Access restrictions to Northeast Syria from Peshkhabour border-Dohuk, Iraq.MITIGATION: SWEDO will engage locally hired staff members in areas of operations to ensure easy access to project sites. 5. RISK: Interference of local authorities and armed militias in beneficiary selections.MITIGATION: SWEDO will make sure that all involved beneficiaries and stakeholders are well briefed about the impartiality, integrity and non-political foundation of SWEDO and the good level of transparency it follows in serving its beneficiaries that will reject any interference or pressure (relevant policies and guidelines are applicable and in place). 6. RISK: Project staff could be involved in corruption and making deals with supplies, beneficiaries, and other stakeholders.MITIGATION: Training will be provided to prevent any improper act (implement SWEDO Code of Conduct and whistleblower policy).
