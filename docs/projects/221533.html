Project Summary


Gender based violence in schools (SRGBV) and Child sexual abuse (CSA) keeps children (disproportionately Girls) out of school and prevents them from realizing their full potential. Furthermore, the ongoing conflict in the NW/SW region has exacerbated both the risks and incidents of CSA for children in conflict affected communities. Even more, increase internet penetration, greater access to technology (social media platforms and tech gadgets) and encrypted online chat services e.g. WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram etc., has increased children’s exposure to CSA, allowing perpetrators to operate in relative anonymity.
This project will:
- harmonize stakeholders’ knowledge on the severity and urgency of tackling CSA,
- address current knowledge gaps on negative outcomes of CSA through community sensitizations,
- promote reporting and help-seeking culture for children in/out of school;
- build the capacities of parents and caregivers on positive parenting and online supervision of children,
- strengthen teachers capacity on handling disclosures and discoveries of CSA and
- use social and behavior change (SBC) interventions to clarify and change socio-cultural and religious norms and myths that condone with CSA.
- Provide case management for child survivors of SRGBV and CSA
- Provide mental mental health and psycho-social support services for child survivors and their families
The project will also harness peer education largely through school-based campaigns to increase children’s knowledge on personal safety both online/offline, including how to prevent abuse, where to seek help and how to safely report incidents.
Communities including perpetrators will gain knowledgeable and awareness on the legal, health, education and psychological consequences of CSA and their role as active by-standers mostly through audio-visual educative talks and community mobilization for action. The latter will promote restorative justice for child victims and facilitate healing for survivors, strengthen their protection and shift response from survivor centered to survivor led

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The crisis in the north west and south west regions has let to untold suffering for the civilian population in the two regions including multiple internal displacement with women and children bearing the greatest brunt of the crisis.Children, disproportionately girls have been severely affected by the negative effects of the crisis including heightened discriminations against young girls in education (school-related GBV), child sexual exploitation and abuses in and out of schools, increase school absenteeism /dropout due to sickness such as sexually transmitted diseases, teen pregnancies or unsafe abortions by young girls.Owing to the above, CASAF conducted a needs assessment to understand the factors responsible for driving up incidents of SRGBV/CSA and to co-create interventions that go a long way to mitigate this appalling form of violence against children. The assessment results captured a series of factors contributing to increased vulnerability of children to GBV in schools and Child sexual abuse including:- Lack of knowledge by frontline actors including teachers and parents to effectively manage and respond to disclosures and discoveries of incidents of child sexual exploitation and abuse.- Lack of CBCPM in communities or inadequate capacity by existing community based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM)- Increased exposure to online child sexual abuse materials and other sexually explicit contents.- Increased access to tech gadgets and online social media platforms including encrypted Chat/messaging applications e.g. WhatsApp.- Lack of knowledge on personal safety from online/offline child sexual exploitation and abuse- Lack of safe, accessible and confidential reporting channels that promote help-seeking in the face of abuse.-poverty and hunger pushing parents to incline their children to contribute in family income mostly through negative coping strategies such as child marriages and child labor.- Lack of adequate and appropriate response services.- Lack of victim protection and support services including comprehensive healing, recovery and social reintegration programs.This project will adopts a holistic, multi-faceted and collaborative approach that attempts to implement community led solutions that comes from the communities and work for the communities, leveraging existing community based structures and strengthening other frontline actors and partners to ensure sustained impact to End school related GBV and child sexual abuse (online/offline), and other risks that increase the vulnerability of children to both physical and sexually related violence and abuse in conflict affected communities in the NW and SW. -
