Project Summary

The Rohingya refugee population shows high levels of vulnerability based on the REVA II and MSNA results. The Fill the Nutrient Gap analysis found that almost none of Rohingya HH could afford a nutritious diet. Under the existing conditions, Rohingya refugees remain 100% reliant on food distribution to sustain the daily minimum required kilocalorie in-take per individual. The REVA II results indicate that over 40% of host community population is extremely vulnerable. As per the Fill the Nutrient Gap analysis conducted by WFP (Oct, 2019), 1 out of 2 Bangladeshi in host communities cannot afford a nutritious diet.
To minimize the food insecurity problems in the host community and Rohingya, BRAC will undertake a holistic humanitarian approach, in combination with other partners, to facilitate improved economic and food security and dignity at the household level, as well as greater social cohesion and inclusivity within and across communities. The responses towards food security of the host community and Rohingya will include different livelihood and self-reliance intervention like agriculture and environmental restoration, skill development, ultra-poor graduation, general food distribution, IGA and Input support etc.
Under JRP 2020, total projected beneficiaries will be 26,270 for promote Skill Development opportunities for Rohingya and enhance the Livelihood and resilience of host communities. Based on need assessment, BRAC will cover different camps like 1E, 2W, 4, 4 ext., 5, 6, 7, 8W, 8E, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 20 ext., 21, 22 & 26 and host community like Jaliapalong, Rajapalomng, Haldiapalong, Ratnapalong, and Palongkhali of Ukhiya sub-districts and Whykong, Nhila, Teknaf, Baharchara and Sabrang union of Teknaf sub-districts respectively. To implement the JRP 2020, a total of 6.15m USD will be required for successful implementation of food security interventions.
Gender equity and equality is core focus of BRAC's food security and self-reliance strategy. The Project target people are mainly female participants of vulnerable HH for creating skill development opportunities to enhance Self-reliance and livelihood support. The other gender will be highly consultative and focused gender equality based on the need and prospect of engaging in income generating activities with Cross-cutting issues
For environmental restoration and climate change mitigation, BRAC will implement slope stabilization and camp greening by planting tree seedlings, vetiver and cover grasses. BRAC will support in nursery establishment and tree seedling raising, tree plantation and distribution and cover around 20-hectare plantation areas.

Needs

The food and nutrition security of both refugee and host community is a growing concern. Under the existing conditions, the refugee population remains 100 % reliance on food distribution to sustain the minimum required kilocalories per individual. The hard-hit host community is also struggling to ensure basic needs of their family due to the increase of the commodity price and to cheaper refugee labor market, making clear that the response actors will also need to increase their focus on host community food security. Sustaining the food assistance needs is threatened by the funding gap and is limiting expansion of livelihood activities that could foster self-reliance. Breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency and threatens the survival of the vulnerable refugees. The multifaceted efficient and need-based initiatives led by the response actors will ensure food security both foe the refugee and the hard-hit host community. The least vulnerable households will be gradually phased out from unconditional food assistance, toward self-reliance activities within the camps. As, there are plans to shift from in-kind Programme, livelihood/self-reliance programming is needed to provide Rohingyas access to local markets, and provide self-reliance support to enhance their physical access to main commodities.A key finding is that the recently displaced Rohingya population is overwhelmingly young. 50 percent of the Rohingya are less than 15 years of age. More than a quarter of Rohingya households are headed by women, as opposed to 17% in host communities. The prevalence of women is more than 50% among both Rohingya and hosts, with a much lower share of elderly (particularly in the age group 30-39) and a higher share of children below the age of 10 among the Rohingya (CBPS, November’ 2019).BRAC developed the livelihood strategy in light of the recent livelihoods’ assessments. BRAC internally conducted one rapid assessment and was part of a joint livelihood assessment with UNHCR, Save the Children, WFP and WV. The REVA II results indicate that over 40% of host community population is extremely vulnerable. As per the Fill the Nutrient Gap analysis conducted by WFP (Oct, 2019), 1 out of 2 Bangladeshi in host communities cannot afford a nutritious diet. To date, BRAC’s livelihood work focused on a few key areas: intensifying its programmes within the host communities, ensuring livelihood opportunities through its own programming, and improving nutritional security and self-reliance through introduction of resilient food security program.
