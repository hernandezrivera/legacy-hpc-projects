Project Summary

Over the area of Northwest Syria, women and children continue to undergo multiple
protection risks and rights violations as a crisis influence. GBV and child rights violations
sit as stringent apprehension. This environment leaves the psychosocial needs of many
girls and boys unmet, leading to experiences of profound distress. Recruitment and use
of children increased sharply in some areas; where children, particularly adolescent boys,
are used in both combat roles as well as in support roles. UNFPA’s 2019 Whole of Syrian
Voices report also noted child marriage as a major protection concern, placing girls at
particular risk to domestic violence, divorce, early pregnancy, curtailed educational
opportunities and psychological harm.
Furthermore, both returnees and IDP communities across NW Syria face mounting
difficulties in meeting their protection needs due to the fact that protection services are
stretched, and the provision of humanitarian assistance is primarily focused on delivering
shelter and food supplies to the most vulnerable. Due to limited protection
capacity/presence on the ground it remains a challenge to ensure that vulnerable
populations (including host communities) have meaningful access to sustained
protection interventions and have the means to build and maintain accountability
systems for these interventions. Needs of affected populations are further exacerbated
by lack of effective legal support, which has been identified as a major gap in both Idlib
and Aleppo Governorate.
Solution
Overall Objective:
Reduce protection risks and vulnerability for persons through the provision of integrated
protection services, including CP case management, following protection centered,
and link cases that require cash assistance with the cash assistance program
that Orange is implementing in the area of intervention

4 - Needs

According to the OCHA 7th, Situation report for 29th, Jan.2020 more than 390k IDPswhere displaced starting from December 2019 from Marret, Alnouman and surrounding andthe Northern and western countryside of Aleppo continues to receive huge influxes ofIDPs every day. Those people are in need of basic services and displacement isnegatively affecting local communities. In addition, the economic situation is getting worse due to the high inflationof the Syrian pound. The mentioned situation is posingvery high risk of GBV, CP risks and a great need for general protection and referral services. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview, child marriage, child labor, and family separation rates are very high.Child Marriage: 69% for adolescent girls, 28% for adolescent boysChild Labour: more than 20% for boys under 12, more than 55% for adolescent boys (12-14), 83% for adolescent boys (15-17), 18% for girls under 12, 39% for adolescent girls (12-14), 60% for adolescent girls (15-17)Family Separation: 30% for boys under 12, more than 50% for adolescent boys (12-17), 30% for girls under 12, 49% for adolescent girls (12-17) Apart from the risks mentioned, children also face psychosocial distress, displacement, sexual abuse, physical violence and are deprived from education. Economic problems are the main reason for most of the vulnerabilities and protection risks. Orange is currently implementing the needs assessment used by the protection taskforce in Azaz district of Aleppo countryside community to inform the design and adaptionof protection, CP activities in the area. The intervention is intended to providegeneral protection and child protection services for people in need through establishing acommunity center and lunching PFA mobile teams that reach out to local communitiesand IDPs.

5 - Implementation of activities

Outcome 1. Protection Monitoring, Outreach and cash assistance Outreach teams consisting of outreach workers, caseworkers, and M&E assistants will conduct household visits, community-level need assessment and information dissemination sessions about services based on the needs. In addition, team will identify protection risks and vulnerabilities and invite beneficiaries to the community centers. Identification of the risks will be through outreach activities, household visits, community meetings, phone calls and internal-external referrals. The team will map available services targeting IDPs and host communities in the wider area and establish referral pathways with them. Outcome 2. Child protection services:CP case management services will be provided by caseworkers for individuals identified with protection risks requiring specialized case management, in line with CP sub-cluster case management guidelines. The specialized caseworkers will provide support for children with specific needs, such as physical disability. Staff members will be trained to ensure that children with protection risks such as separated from family and unaccompanied children, children with disabilities, children who are exposed to physical, sexual and emotional violence are identified, registered and adequately referred, that determinations of the best interests of the child take place Outcome 3. Provision of psychosocial support and parenting programsPsychosocial support trainers/psychological counselors will provide structured, sustained psychosocial support programs for children who are facing psychological distress and difficulties. PSS program will support children’s emotional, behavioral and cognitive well-being. Outcome 4. Staff capacity buildingThe project staff will be trained on code of conduct, child safeguarding and child protection, case management. Based on the needs and requests of the staff, Orange will provide relevant capacity building activities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security risk to Orange staff and facilities: Orange will follow regular security updates, attend Cluster meetings and keep the staff informed about the recent developments. Besides, information security is part of Orange’s contingency plan and the team will continue tracking the risks associated with implementation and suggest suitable countermeasures Unavailability of Experienced staff: Orange teams will work ondeveloping clear TORs based on previous experiences and will reach out to our vastnetwork of local communities and other projects teams and partners to find the suitable Public health risk: Orange will follow the instructions of World Health Organization and local authorities about public health measures. Orange will disseminate information to community to raise awareness. The physical risks of community centers which are like power cut, heating problems, physical damage and infrastructure problems, etc.: Orange prioritize safety and security of the beneficiaries and staff and apply alternative strategies to make sure continuity of implementation such as mobile teams activities
