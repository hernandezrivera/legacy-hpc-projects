Project Summary

ARC’s proposed WASH program is designed to address critical water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities in Eastern Equatoria State (Kapoeta South); to improve overall WASH services for women, men, boys and girls; and reduce cases of malnutrition and mitigate GBV violence as well as reduce tensions between host community and IDPs who are co-existing. In collaboration and close coordination with local authorities including women groups, youth and community leaders, ARC will undertake WASH activities to improve the overall wellbeing and public health of the target population. The planned activities will include repair and maintenance of water supply systems, conducting regular water quality testing, improving waste management and disposal activities, conducting clean-up campaigns and improving general hygiene promotion awareness. Community engagement and participation will constitute the heart of our program implementation strategy. ARC in coordination and collaboration with the local authorities and community members will establish or support existing WASH Committees to undertake operation, repair and maintenance of WASH facilities. Promotion of behavior change – especially towards using or constructing of household latrines will be a critical thrust of this intervention to enable community members to tackle frequent/protracted seasonal cholera-outbreaks in Kapotea East, Mayom and Abiemnohm counties. This undertaking will be established through community participation in program design and management. The project will also include a strong capacity building component for the local established WASH Committees, which involve provision of technical training to Sanitation Groups, Hygiene Promoters and Hand pump Mechanics to effectively undertake their water supply, sanitation, waste management activities. Volunteers will be trained in Hygiene and GBV referral systems, and will be engaged and supported to undertake hygiene promotion activities and increase community awareness of issues around child marriage, gender and referral mechanisms for survivors of GBV.
ARC will focus on the hygiene awareness building and capacity building of the local stakeholders including IDP and host communities through the integrated Health, Nutrition and WASH household messaging campaign. ARC will implement targeted messaging and interventions per PHAST methodology, as well as the innovative approaches promoted under the UN Every Women, Every Child Strategy. The health consultation and treatment records, including the SAM and MAM statistics, and the weekly epidemiological overviews will serve to guide interventions of the ARC WASH teams, prioritizing locations with the highest reported numbers of cases and seeking correlations. Besides serving as the basis for deployment of the sanitation and water technical/ engineering teams to identify and repair faulty infrastructure, the findings will also guide the deployment of our new Health, Nutrition and WASH (HNW) Community Volunteers. At the household level, our team will perform the Targeted and Timed Counseling (TTC) for women in pre-and post-natal period concerning adequate practices benefiting their newborns/infants/toddlers, and inclusive of their intimate partners and other family members including the persons with special needs. The TTC will therefore combine the elements of PHAST with the Baby WASH approach in this context, aiming to deliver a concerted effort to reduce morbidity and mortality, and stunting of growth of children during the first 1,000 days.
The proposed activities will complement ARC’s existing OFDA – funded WASH programs in Central Equotoria and expand our reach within Eastern Equatoria in Kapoeta East, Mayom and Abiemnohm in Unity State to reach to communities at high risks.

Needs

In Kajo-keji in Central Equatoria state, and Budi in Eastern Equatoria State, ARC along with other implementing partners conducted a needs assessment in October 2018 to review the humanitarian needs of the affected and underserved population. In Kajo-keji, the assessment covered the IDP settlements in Korijo in Lewolo payama and in Budi the assessment covered three payams—namely Homari, Loutokie and Nachicod. Results show that the humanitarian needs are dire for WASH services. In Kajo-keji, the main water source for drinking and other domestic uses is ‘surface water from river and streams’ cited by 95%, which are unprotected water sources. There is no existence of hand pumps or protected wells, and people walk long distance as far as 3 to 2 km to fetch surface water from river Lema. In Budi where ARC is present with WASH intervention since June 2018, the assessment results still show huge gaps in water supply and quality intervention, despite the efforts made to close gaps in water supply. A good number of hand pumps exist in the operational area in Nachichod, Loutokie and Homari payams, but majority of them are not functional due to shortage of spare-parts and absence of pump mechanics, a situation which forced many people to opt for drinking water from unprotected sources such as rivers and streams. The overall findings in all locations show that, females are cited at 97% to be responsible of water fetching, with girls under 18 years old at 20% and adult females over 18 years old constituting 77% followed by adult men at 3%. For Adolescents and Children under 18 years old who are responsible for water collection, the following issues and concerns were identified as risks associated with this task: risk associated with children getting lost in the bush, side effects from carrying too heavy jerry cans by children, fatigue from water fetching due to water points being too far away from shelters. In all the locations assessed in Kajo-keji and Budi, the distance to water sources was identified as a big issue, and overall 53% of the households reported collecting water from a distance of ‘between 1km to 3 km’ and 35% accessing water sources from a distance over 3 km. This distance poses a heavy burden on community members doing other businesses especially adult women and adolescent girls. Sanitation needs is a big issue in both Kajo-keji IDP settlement and Budi communities. The most common defecation practices cited by respondents was the use of open defecation (OD) and most of people do not have latrines at their households in almost all the areas covered by this assessment. In Budi results show that 72% of the respondents stating that they do not have latrines while only 8% reported having latrines at their homesteads. For those who do not have latrines, most of them mentioned the issues related to cultural beliefs and ignorance of the importance of having latrines. In Kaj-keji, and in particular in Korijo IDP settlement, 91% of the respondents do not have latrines while the remainder (19%) had latrines. Those who have latrines have mention that, they provided slabs and tarpaulin by ARC in September 2018, but the materials were not enough to cater for all the IDPs, and IDP have no means or resources to buy their own materials to construct their latrines. Results from the assessment indicate that the most common WASH related diseases that people suffer from during the previous month diarrheal disease (43%) followed by Malaria (20%). Based on the available data, it is shown that there is high rate of WASH related diseases in all areas covered under this assessment. ARC is planning a range of activities to be implemented with this funding. This project will help address the above mentioned gaps through provision of a full WASH package.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

ARC closely cooperates and coordinates with local stakeholders for the project, design, planning, implementation and handover to local authorities and beneficiaries. ARC involve the local authorities (governmental and community leaders) to encourage ownership of the response. Dissemination of Information to beneficiaries through project design and planning--specially for assessment, is ensured through community leaders, and local authorities. During implementation community members receive information for distribution of project materials through announcement boards at camp sites and local authority offices, or community fora such as meetings and worship places. Another central aspect of ARC’s response is beneficiary involvement. ARC directly collect information on the needs and preferences of IDPs and host community members in consultation with community members through Focus Group Discussions (FGD), Key Informant Interviews particularly with women and girls. Beneficiaries can also raise their questions and concerns about the project during Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), which includes household interviews and FGD.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed WASH intervention is derived from and contextualized by the local practices in terms of technology choices and implementation approaches in South Sudan especially in areas where this program will be implemented. WASH infrastructure including water supply facilities, emergency latrines, garbage disposals, and institutional latrines in WGFS will be designed reflecting local culture and traditional customs in South Sudan while meeting the SPHERE access and proximity standards. Water supply facilities will be designed in consultation with women and local authorities to address distances and technology options. The emergency latrines will be constructed using tarpaulin, plastic slabs and woods for superstructure and the pits will be excavated to a depth of 3 to 3.5 metres to avoid reaching water table. Latrines will be located at a reasonable distance to encourage users to use them. Hygiene promotion approaches will be designed using locally hygiene promotion messages and agreed upon IEC materials to reflect the local culture and practices. ARC will design Hygiene Promotion Training Manual (HPTM) adapted to the local context, inclusive of local culture and practices.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC works to minimize the risks to vulnerable populations in its water and sanitation programs by developing an in-depth understanding of the community dynamics in relation to individual and group interests and sensitivities such as ethnicity, religious beliefs, economic status etc., in order to ensure equitable access for all. Through its WASH technical designs, ARC strives to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV, create safe and friendly environments for women and children, and make the facilities accessible to individuals with special needs. ARC adheres to the set of guidelines that are an intrinsic part of our program design paradigm. These include the IASC/ Global Protection Cluster Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, WASH Thematic Area Guide (TAG), and the Handicap International guidelines on design of interventions for people with disability. Illustrative actions ARC takes to ensure protection and gender are mainstreamed in WASH programming include:• Separate communal latrines for women and men constructed at suitable locations where health teams will provide their services regularly. • Positioning of latrines at visible locations in the village to discourage GBV perpetrators to commit act of violence in clear view of the community, while at the same time, ensuring dignified access to women, adolescent girls and other users through installation of partition screens between latrines used by men/ boys and by women/ girls, or positioning the block for women/ girls separately from the block for men/ boys where more suitable.• Solar lights and strong door locking system at each latrine cubicle.• ARC communal latrine design includes positioning of baskets inside the latrine cubicles/ blocks designated for women and girls to dispose sanitary items in dignity, with clean water jerry cans positioned inside.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The participation of the beneficiaries and local actors is an essential part of the project planning and implementation. ARC involves the relevant actors (government, community leader, traditional authorities and community members) in the design and implementation of projects in order to strengthen their ownership. The IDPs participate mainly through Focus Group Discussions (FGD), especially women and girls. During post-Distribution Monitoring the beneficiaries are able to formulate their questions and needs, which is also possible in the FGDs and Interviews on household level. The WASH Committees in Kajo-kejis IDP settlement as well as in Budi communities are an important step towards Self Organization and taking responsibility program implementation. The Committees conduct monthly meetings, identify WASH needs, discuss about topics which are priority to the communities and possibilities for Improvement and lessons learnt. The Committees work closely with field officers who are in direct contact with the with beneficiaries to prioritize needs and take action to design implementation work plans.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The affected people have the possibility to contact the field staff on daily basis to describe their needs. Further they can talk with specific Sector leaders in the camps, who are in contact with Camp management and ARC field staff. ARC conduct continuous Monitoring where the beneficiaries can express critics and concerns about their satisfaction with the project implementation. Further anonymous Mechanisms, such as Feedback Boxes on central places in the camps and communities are used. ARC is eager to improve any feedback mechanisms, so the beneficiaries are able to articulate their needs independent from aid organizations. ARC developed Feedback Mechanisms in all areas of operations to allow community members to voice their concerns about project design, implementation and evaluation.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC stands committed to collecting gender specific data on vulnerable populations. Our data collection tools are designed and developed in consultation with affected community members to capture robust data that can be disaggregated with respect to sex, age, ethnicity, female-headed households and geographic area when applicable. ARC uses a mixed methods approach to data collection that allows triangulation to ensure validity. All data collected by ARC on its project beneficiaries is Sex and Age-Disaggregated (SADD) to ensure that ARC’s responses appropriately and effectively meet the needs of vulnerable affected women, men, boys and girls. Data is collected on a routine basisin colnsultation with community members using the following approaches: Key informant interviews, Separate Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) for men and women, Post distribution monitoring 2 weeks after distribution activities are completed, with a focus on hygiene kit, waste management and hygiene promotional material distributions, Direct observation, Survey and Feedback and complaint mechanisms. To ensure personal information is documented and stored in an ethical manner, ARC will introduce a secure GBV Information Management Service (GBVIMS) and continue to utilize established case management tools. Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ARC recognizes the need for protection risk analysis that considers the specific needs of different population so as to develop mitigation measures to either eliminate the risks altogether or to reduce the intensity of it. The protection of all persons affected and at risk must inform humanitarian decision-making and response, including engagement with States and non-State parties to conflict. ARC will contribute to an improved protection environment by aiming to achieve collective outcomes on priority issues. Through collective protection and gender risk analysis, ARC will ensure that humanitarian responses do not exacerbate risks and reinforce patterns of violations. However, this will require specialized and integrated programmes, protection mainstreaming across sectors, and promoting an age-gender-diversity needs-based and conflict-sensitive response.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access constraints in South Sudan encompasses a wide range of dimensions (from bureaucratic to physical) and each of them must be taken into consideration to address the broader challenge of access in the country. ARC will focus on building a general holistic access advocacy approach to mitigate a silos approach that is still too prevalent and creates competition and narrows the focus for a problem that must be addressed by engaging all actors. Key to this will be adopting an inclusive approach with all type of actors including engaging decentralized actors. ARC and partners will be vigilant in identifying and where possible negotiating with the leadership of the day as pertains the bureaucratic impediments and will share such information for advocacy purposes. ARC will use available channels of communication to escalate to Juba level such issues for advocacy. Access barriers related to limited services will require mobilization of resources to expand the services to areas not reached with services. ARC will share such information accordingly through the cluster for advocacy and prioritization during funds allocation

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

The target beneficiaries will be informed and involved in the process of project monitoring and evaluation. Also, beneficiaries will be made aware about the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) system which ARC will analyse from time to time to measure beneficiaries’ access barriers to services. Through period program activity reports, ARC will documents the bureaucratic and physical access impediments, its magnitude, scope and impact on programming. All the information will be documented in the Complaints and Response Mechanism database held by the MEAL Coordinator at a central location in Juba. Appropriate action will be taken accordingly. However, sensitive issues will be channeled through the channels provided by ARC.
