Project Summary

IRC will provide the following services: specialized CM services; cash assistance to meet immediate protection needs; individual, group and activity-based PSS services; and life skills education for men, women, boys, and girls; provide lifesaving, reliable and responsive information services and raise awareness on protection risks, and mitigation strategies and available services and how to safely access them.
Throughout this project, IRC will regularly monitor rights violations and the protection situation in NWS to understand the changes overtime; improve access to primary and specialized services for those who would not otherwise seek services; inform response planning; and advocate to enhance the protective environment across Syria.
The IRC will ensure that PwD are part of the needs assessment, design, implementation, coordination, monitoring, and evaluation throughout all project phases. Leveraging their leadership, skills, and experience ensures their active participation in decision-making and planning processes.
Engaging PwD provides input on the design of program, services, and infrastructure. This ensures that accessibility and inclusivity are integrated from the outset.
Also, the project will conduct a comprehensive assessment to identify existing barriers faced by PwD. This includes evaluating physical infrastructure, accessibility, communication channels, and programmatic aspects.
IRC will ensure protection is mainstreamed across interventions in the proposed target locations. Identified yet unmet needs, protection risks, or human rights abuses among marginalized populations will be addressed directly through referrals to internal and external providers, contributing to broader community-wide risk reduction initiatives.
Protection monitoring, which employs harmonized tools developed jointly by the protection cluster is one of the main pathways that informs and connects needs with assistance. IRC will support individuals identified with specific survival needs through information provision, referrals to services, and where appropriate, in-kind assistance identified during the CM process (items may include clothes, blankets, hygiene kits, etc.)
In NWS IRC will send monitoring teams to new displacement situations where they will provide Psychological First Aid (PFA), information to IDPs and identify individuals in need of urgent assistance. In terms of operational challenges, security conditions disruptions pose risks to project implementation. Mitigation measures include ongoing security planning, flexible project design, engagement with local authorities, signing MOUs outlining roles, and adopting a low visibility strategy. Movement safety concerns are addressed through necessary assessments, vehicle preparation, and staff briefings.
Furthermore, IRC has a Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) strategy in place to address the risk of exploitation and abuse by staff members. This strategy includes maintaining a zero-tolerance policy towards all forms of beneficiary exploitation and abuse, providing clear reporting mechanisms, and ensuring staff uphold the highest ethical standards. Overall, the organization prioritizes protection risk analysis, PSEA strategy implementation, and mitigation measures to ensure the safety and well-being of beneficiaries and staff while delivering essential services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

IRC’s “Do No Harm” approach is underpinned by regular contextual analysis through stakeholder analyses, updated security assessments, monthly protection monitoring, regular communication with local authorities and information sharing across partners and humanitarian networks. The meaningful participation of affected populations across different stages of the project cycle and through feedback and response mechanisms also enables regular monitoring of the project impact. During implementation, community members are leveraged as key implementers, and will be engaged in various monitoring and evaluation initiatives to get their feedback and perceptions on the quality of protection services. Such initiatives include satisfaction surveys, focus group discussions, post-distribution monitoring, safety audits, learning activities, KAP surveys, training evaluations, and regular collection of complaints and feedback through IRC’s accountability channels Inequalities, discriminatory practices and abuse against women and children have a negative impact on safety, health, and ability of women and girls to recover from crises and shocks throughout the MENA context, including in Syria. Men are usually the primary decision makers while women’s primary roles continue to revolve around motherhood and managing a household (HH). Gender inequities and GBV have been exacerbated by the conflict in Syria and crises in neighboring countries. Harmful gender-based discrimination like early marriage, the restriction of female mobility without accompaniment, and coercive sex continue to interface with the economic vulnerability and instability of Syrian women and girls and highly impact their wellbeing. Young women and girls often must forgo an education due to HH chores, early marriage, and early pregnancy, leading to a lack of understanding of their basic rights, including reproductive rights.
