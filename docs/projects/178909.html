Project Summary

The purpose of the WASH intervention is to deliver humanitarian WASH supplies and services to most vulnerable people, and provide support to water, sanitation and sewage systems to ensure continuous services for affected people in Syria. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable populations throughout Syria through the delivery of a multi-faceted WASH response. The different components of NRC’s WASH project are intended to contribute to the following HRP WASH Sector Objectives: 1 = 1.3 repair and rehab of sanitation/sewage and solid waste; 2 = 2.1 distribution of hygiene materials and messaging on COVID; 2.3 – market based assistance for WASH services; 4= support for WASH facilities in schools.
NRC will deliver this project through a direct implementation modality:
Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access. NRC will provide assistance to vulnerable conflict and displacement-affected populations, including IDPs, refugees, members of host communities and refugee/IDP returnees, and non-displaced households suffering the effects of the conflict. All interventions will seek to prioritize the most vulnerable households. Where appropriate, multi-sectoral vulnerability scoring techniques will be used that integrate vulnerability indicators
This project includes:
- Emergency response through the provision of one-off support as part of an integrated, multi-sector response in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness and Response Plans (EPRPs). Comprises of three Result Areas: Temporary WASH services, household assets (Hygiene NFIs) and support to Education in Emergencies (WASH Services for Temporary Learning Spaces).
- The provision of medium-term shelter support at household and settlement-level to populations living in and out-of-camps. Comprises of two result areas: Provision of WASH infrastructure, services and hygiene items in camps and communal shelters.
- The provision of longer-term assistance at household and settlement-level in urban and peri-urban out-of-camp areas. Comprises of three result areas: community WASH infrastructure and services, rehabilitation of household WASH facilities, and school rehabilitation (WASH Facilities) (see NRC’s Education project submission).
Project will include consultation with community, KIIs as well as with relevant authorities. Special attention will be given to ensuring that interventions are accessible by all and that there are functional complaint and feedback mechanisms set in place.

4 - Needs

2021 HNO estimates 12.2M people require WASH assistance. 1.75M people living in IDP sites face different challenges (in NWS due to continuous hostilities, and in NES due to disruption of the largest water supply systems). UNOCHA estimates that up to 37% of the off-camp population relies on alternative and often unsafe water sources. This is even more crucial given the C-19 pandemic. Deterioration of economic conditions challenge vulnerable communities in terms of accessing safe & equitable WASH services (16% of HHs’ income spent on WASH services & supplies). In IDP sites, PwD and elderly (16%) lack access to adequate sanitation facilities and, as female-headed HHs (9%), more constraints in accessing other WASH services. Substandard conditions of sanitation facilities are concerning for females, due to risks of sexual harassment, exploitation, violence and humiliation. Sanitation, distribution of untreated water & lack of sewage management have contributed to increased public health risks, causing water borne diseases; at least 70% of sewage is untreated. 50% sewage systems are not functional. 14% of garbage is disposed of inappropriately. WASH infrastructures are crucial to facilitate socioeconomic recovery and addressing the needs of IDPs & spontaneous returnees. 38% of children attending school complain about WASH related issues (Early-2020 WASH Household Assessment). Most common issues reported: toilets cleanliness, lack of functioning toilets, barriers to effective handwashing (lack of soap, water or no facilities), and protection oriented aspects of dysfunctional facilities. In 2019 and 2020 NRC assessed 77 schools, 73 of which were identified as having poor water and sanitation facilities below SPHERE standards.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will contribute to the WASH Sector strategic objectiveSO1: through the activity repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems. In areas with severely damaged community sewage networks NRC will rehabilitate the main sewer lines in coordination with the Municipality and local contractors. NRC will ensure that communities are engaged from the design phase to encourage ownership and households’ contribution to connect their houses to the main sewer line. NRC will also support rehabilitation/reestablishment of solid waste communal collection systems.SO 2: through the (1) Distribution of essential WASH non-food items (NFI), hygiene promotion and messaging on C-19 will be provided through the appropriate modality depending on the context; In-Kind or Vouchers/Restricted Cash and/or Hygiene campaigns. (2) Improve access to humanitarian life-saving/emergency WASH facilities and services, including strengthening Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures, through; (a) life-saving/emergency water facilities and services, (b) sanitation/sewage facilities and services and (c) solid waste management and vector control facilities and service; all these activities will be delivered at formal/informal camps where all residents will benefit from these services. SO3: WASH coordination structures facilitated and enhanced; Strengthened prevention and mitigation of WASH-related protection.SO4: by supporting rehabilitation of WASH facilities and services in schools. This will be done in close coordination with Education Sector and will be accompanied by HP trainings for the schools staff and provision of cleaning kits.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk (1): Limited gender modified and disaggregated sanitation facilities within formal/informal camps, and lack of sufficient lighting might result in safety risks, particularly for women and girls. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) Consultation with diverse representatives of affected population. 2) Increased provision/budget for gender modified and separated facilities. 3) Increased provision/budget for communal lighting gender separated facilities, access routes and other areas. 4) CFM in place. Protection Risk (2): WASH activities might cause unintended impact on local markets (e.g. cost inflation) or contribute to 'war economies'. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1) Conduct Systematic market monitoring to understand its dynamics and impact. 2) Choose Suppliers based on an extensive procurement process, overseen by Compliance Unit. Protection Risk (3): Distributions exclude vulnerable populations in need of assistance due to inability to reach them Likelihood: Medium Impact: Medium Mitigation Measures: 1) Independent registration and outreach, reducing reliance on gate-keepers, 2) CFM in place, 3) multiple days for distributions 4) include questions related to exclusions in PDMs 5) conduct messaging to those eligible for distributions ahead of the distribution dates. Protection Risk (4): gathering of beneficiaries (i.e. information sessions, distributions) increases the risk of spreading COVID-19. Likelihood: high. Impact: High Mitigation measures: 1) SOPs for safe implementation during C-19 developed; adapted implementation modalities 2) Hygiene promotion activities

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC’s Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, and child safeguarding policies. Several sessions took place in 2020 to raise awareness of PSEA and child safeguarding to staff, in addition in sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC participates in PSEA network meetings, regularly reports and shares information on good practice and challenges related to PSEA. NRC’s Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and child safeguarding reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams. In addition to inquiring during post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs) whether people were asked to exchange anything for aid. Attempts by non-traditional humanitarian actors (e.g. local councils and authorities) to interfere in beneficiary selection and eligibility are reported and addressed as they come. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks who recipients of aid think provided the assistance, in order to identify possible areas of concern. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM have started and are expected to conclude by mid-2021. WASH: Issues related to exchange of assistance for sexual favours, threats or actual removal from beneficiary lists

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Threats to health, safety and security of staff, partners & beneficiaries linked to conflict or criminal activities: dedicated staff, at coordination and field level, to ensure a close context follow-up and implementation of NRC Security policy, risk assessments, updated security & contingency plans, security & safety training for staff. Limitations by local authorities preventing NRC to access/ work in specific areas and to independently select beneficiary or staff: networking with peer organisations through different fora to advocate for unified policy positions, meeting with local key stakeholders to reinforce humanitarian principles, transparent recruitment policy and beneficiary selection criteria, clear red lines identified while negotiating with local authorities, careful liaison work. Border closure: Local market analysis, Advocacy engagement at UN level, Stockpile of basic items when relevant. Neighbouring states push for large-scale return of refugees to Syria (including coerced return): Collaboration and coordination with other NRC country programmes in the region. Development of specific contingency plans. Adaptation of programmes. Fraud/Corruption/Bribery: Enforce NRC zero tolerance of corruption policy & ensure partners implement its measures, promote the “whistle-blowing” policy, corruption risk mapping. Loss of access in targeted areas due to context developments, legal restrictions or loss of acceptance: good understanding of context, contextual analysis, security plans & procedures, contingency plan, community acceptance and good relationship with local authorities. COVID-19 Restrictions (internal or external): SOPs for safe implementation during C-19 developed; adapted implementation modalities.
