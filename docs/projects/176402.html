Project Summary

Proposed actions will target beneficiaries primarily made up 2000 girls, 2000 boys, 500 men and 500 women in Mvolo County, Western Equatoria State; who have been affected by COVID 19 pandemic with consequent closure of schools, conflict, flooding, unprecedented levels of food insecurity and malnutrition, diseases related to poor WASH access, forceful recruitments into armed forces and groups, protection concerns including gender based and sexual violence among others.
The action will be in line with the Education Cluster 2021 strategy by focussing on working with key stakeholders as the government gears on a safe and COVID sensitive re-opening of schools. The actions is pegged on supporting key cluster objectives that entail improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for 7,000 emergency and COVID-19 affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities, and youth (girls and boys) by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5; improving the quality of education services of at all levels education system and protect the wellbeing of all children, including children with disabilities and teachers' capacity to deliver quality services and prevent the negative impact of the COVID-19 crisis on learning environments by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5; and strengthening the education system’s response capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency and the transmission of COVID-19 pandemic on learners, teachers, and communities.by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5.
CARD has been operating in Mvolo since 2014 with ongoing FSL funding in addition to previous UNICEF supported education actions in Western Equatoria. Through the coordination office in Mvolo in addition to having competent staff, logistics and community goodwill, in addition to having good relations with the host communities, local leaders, RRC officials and state/county ministries of Education and General Instruction.
These strong networks coupled with established local networks will be engaged and mobilized to rapidly implement the proposed activities. In addition, CARD will ensure involvement of beneficiaries in planning, implementation, and monitoring. CARD takes a “do no harm” approach, by employing community-based protection measures that aim at understanding humanitarian issues from the community’s perspective and contributing to the development of risk reduction strategies with and for the community. CARD’s strategy will also ensure upholding core principles or programming via quality interventions, ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations, inter-cluster synergies, protection of beneficiaries including GBV and an innovative integrated multi sector approach. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures-gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, AAP including taking into account Gender Based Violence mitigation measures.

1 - Needs

Most vulnerable girls, boys, men and women including people with special needs continue to grapple with multiple shocks ranging from heightened spread of COVID 19 cases, active armed clashes and inter-communal violence that have had significant implications on schools, coupled with floods, with consequent destruction of basic amenities including boreholes, schools and learning spaces. This coupled with unprecedented food insecurity, have conspired to further aggravate the situation of children including those with disabilities, schoolchildren, and youth (Ministry of Health Updates, 2020; OCHA Reports, 2020; Education Cluster Briefings, 2020). Prolonged closure of schools has in addition led to key protection concerns including adoption of negative coping strategies such as early/ forced marriages and GBV concerns, which have included abduction and use of children in armed hostilities (Save the children report on COVID 19 Impacts, 2020). Women and girls as well as children have particularly been vulnerable with reported abductions in Mvolo and its environs, including killings and psychosocial distress particularly among children (boys and girls). In addition, schools lost several months in 2020 due to the pandemic (Save the children report on COVID 19 Impacts, 2020; Ministry of Health Updates, 2020; Coordinated Assessment Reports, 2020). Internally displaced children and youth including women and men have particularity been vulnerable to likelihood of water borne disease outbreaks, which will be heightened with the current pandemic (UNICEF Situation Reports, 2020). The action will be in line with the Education Cluster 2021 strategy by focussing on working with key stakeholders as the government gears on a safe and COVID sensitive re-opening of schools; pegged improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for 7,000 emergency and COVID-19 affected IDPs, returnees, and host community children; improving the quality of education services of at all levels and protecting the wellbeing of all children and prevention of the negative impact of the COVID-19; and strengthening the education system’s response capacity of the school community and education actors to mitigate the impact of existing emergency and the transmission of COVID-19 pandemic on learners, teachers, and communities.by 2021 in counties under IPC 4 and 5. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CARD staff will ensure target beneficiaries are sensitized and are aware of their rights including those relating to PSEA. PSEA focal points will be identified at Boma, village and school levels, with PSEA issues raised to them confidentially including providing confidential reporting channels through phone contacts and email contacts or through relevant local authorities such as chiefs, religious leaders among others. Issues raised will be investigated including ensuring beneficiaries are informed on outcomes In addition, CARD staff and associated personnel have signed a code of conduct that includes PSEA including having in place communication to target beneficiaries on their rights and reporting mechanisms in case of violations, which include PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CARD will ensure vulnerable members including people with disabilities are engaged and actively participate throughout the project cycle, including being part of implementation committees and have their feedback taken into account. Their targeting will also involve involvement of key community lead focal points in addition to ensuring disabled are not excluded from services provided, which include transport facilitation for those with mobility issues, or tailored household level service provision and engagement.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CARD will hold consultative sessions at the beginning of the project at village, Boma and school levels with the communities who will be engaged during inception to share views and inputs on the implementation.Throughout the action, key feedback and compaints desks will be established at the targeted schools in addition to various target Bomas, to support in collection of both feedback and compalints in addition to follow ups of raised concerns which will be communicated to the community. Key focal points for feedback and complaints will include women, men, girls, boys including people with special needs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback including any complaints raised will be tackled CARD in consultation with implementation committees including making appropriate changes where necessary to ensure beneficiary views are taken into consideration. Presented views and action points will be discussed with benefciaries including additional dedicated clarifications in collaboration with key AAP focal points at Boma, Village and school levels.
