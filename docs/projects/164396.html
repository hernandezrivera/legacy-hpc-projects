Project Summary

SHAFAK organization will respond to the increased child protection concerns of the crises affected children including Children with Disabilities through center-based activities in Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) and community outreach teams (mobile teams) deployed in Harim, Dana, Salquin sub-districts in Harim district, Idleb and Marat Masrin sub-districts in Idleb district, Atarib and Daret azza sub-districts in Jabal Saman district, and Azaz sub-district, in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. Shafak will provide child protection case management and referral in the targeted Sub-districts, these services will target children with protection issues (e.g. severe psychological distress, CwDs, neglect or other forms of violence, UASC, child labour, child marriage, abuse).
CM services will be complemented with provision of emergency case management fund to respond to the immediate and urgent needs of children including CWDs. The case fund will include but not limited to; transportation for children to other services, assistive devices (wheel chairs, hearing aids, diapers), milk for in need babies, clothing.
There are limited and insufficient recreational and PSS services for displaced and out of school children and their families, leaving those children and adolescents exposing to exploitation and abuse. Different methodologies of recreational and age appropriate structured and no structured PSS activities for children and adolescents will be organized in and out door. Activities include, art, sport, recreational exercises. Structured PSS will be organized mainly within the CFSs, different curriculums will be used such as; IRC PSS manual, I DEAL and child resilience, ect, where each group will be receiving between 8 to 12 sessions.
Positive parenting skills sessions will be provided to caregivers to reduce the physical and humiliating punishment of children in the home, to improve the capacity of parents and caregivers to practice positive parenting, and to improve the quality of parent/caregiver-child relationships.
Also SHAFAK will strengthen the capacity of the local communities about the child concerns. Firstly, by establishing the male and female Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in the targeted communities to prevent and respond to child protection needs at the local and community level. The committees will play a critical role in child protection, with members representing all sectors in the community who have a role to play in protecting children - including children themselves. And secondly: train the key persons in these communities and the workers in care centers, schools, hospitals and all persons who are in direct touch with children about the Child Protection Minimum Standers (CPMS) and Psychosocial First Aid (PFA), Safe Identification and referral (SIR) taking into consideration the recommendations received from the local councils, children, caregivers to enhance the quality of provided services. SHAFAK will use the monthly reports of the Protection Monitoring task force on the latest developments that threaten child protection and will provide services according to the need.
Establish CPNs: SHAFAK will establish four CPNs in Azaz and Atareb sub-district and 12 CPNs in Idleb governorate, they consist of both males and females. The CPNs will responsible for monitoring the child protection concerns in their communities and work closely with CP staff through holding the monthly meetings and report any concerns related to the child protection.
overall the project will target 36600 children and parents/caregivers (4500 men, 6900 women, 12600 boys and 12600 girls) with the proposed activities.

4 - Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. The crisis has deepened poverty, fueled harmful coping mechanisms and increased the risk of a suite of child protection concerns such as child labour and child marriage, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk. Access to basic rights and services are further jeopardized for children with disabilities and children that cannot access official birth certificatesSeparation from caregivers continues to be described as a persistent child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria. Events leading to family separation indicate the interconnectedness and diversity of vulnerability factors with death (24 per cent for boys and 22 per cent for girls), economic need (20 per cent for boys), child marriage (22 per cent for girls) and child recruitment (14 per cent for boys) among top cited reasons for family separations.Girls and boys with disabilities are often at heightened risk of forms of violence, abuse, neglect or exploitation. Many struggle against marginalization, stigma and discrimination. (Draft HNO 2020)From 1 December 2019 to 14 January 2020, almost 350,000 people were displaced in northwest Syria, 80 percent of whom are women and children. • Multiple displacements and the lack of access to basic services further exacerbate individual and community needs, increasing risks for death, injuries, disabilities, GBV and trauma, particularly among children. • Violations against children rights continue to be reported. (Situation Report, As of 15 January 2020)

5 - Implementation of activities

Project activities will be delivered through the static CFSs and through the CP outreach teams and will include:1- Psychosocial support: Different methodologies of recreational and age appropriate PSS activities for children will be organized in and out door. Activities include, art, sport, recreational exercises. The structured PSS sessions will include a maximum of 20 children and will receive between 8 to 12 sessions, 1 to 2 sessions per week, using different PSS curriculums such as; IRC PSS manual, I DEAL and child resilience, ect. Adolescents will be also targeted with the PSS activities, age and gender will be considered when planning and conducting the sessions.2- Parenting Skills Programmes: The program aims to improve child wellbeing by improving caregiver psychological wellbeing, increasing caregiver use of positive parenting practices for children who have experienced traumatic events. Parents will be divided in groups of twenty-five and provided with 8 to 10 sessions.3- CP Case Management: Shafak will provide CP CM, referrals, and IPA in the targeted Sub-districts, these services will target children with protection concerns (e.g. severe psychological distress, neglect, UASC, child labour). The children will be identified through the project activities in the child friendly spaces and outreach.4- Awareness raising: Regular daily awareness sessions will be conducted the impact of key child protection issues on children’s life.5- CP Networks: SHAFAK will establish four CPNs in Azaz and Atareb sub-Districts and 12 CPNs in Idleb governorate to support the CP staff in delivering the CP activities. Training will be delivered to CPNs, education staff, and other key persons on CPMS, CPIE, PFA, SIR and CM.MEAL: MEAL activities will be conducted to measure the impact of the delivered activities through sessions feedback and pre/post-tests for PSS activities, in addition to applying the complaints mechanism which includes (complaints boxes, brochures).

Likelihood: Low, Impact: HighMitigating Measure: Same Risk (1) Risk (3): Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Likelihood: High, Impact: HighMitigating Measure:1. ensure training packages are in line child protection minimum standards and consider remote managed context2.Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.3.Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services.4.Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.Risk (4): Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately.
