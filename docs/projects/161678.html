Executive summary
This program will bring sustainable water supplies, improved hygiene, better sanitation practices and better access to education to 15,000 students, teachers and other people across five major IDP Camps in Borno State.
Tulips International Foundation will deliver results through an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) and school management approach over a 12 months period at a cost of $0.4 million. Specifically the programme will:
1. Establish School Managememt and Water Resources Management Committees that represent the key stakeholders, and facilitate the development of a Water Resources Management Plans through a participatory planning process and through provision of expert input to analyse the feasibility of different options
2. Provide secure access to safe water through renovation and construction of water points and infrastructure for enhancing groundwater recharge, through an effective Water Resources Management Plans.
3. Promote improvements in hygiene and sanitation practices 4. Document and share lessons learnt within and outside Nigeria
TIF has good experience of working in the WASH sector within Borno State and can mobilize rapidly due to strong links with the communities, traditional title holders and government. TIF has worked together previously on other projects and have proven capability in learning from, supporting and implementing other projects within Brno State. To ensure sustainability, the programme will work to strengthen sector management and co-ordination across the five IDP Camps enabling cost-effective approaches applied by the programme to be continued and shared more widely in support of national goals.
A common M&E framework which considers the requirements for payment by results will be designed during the start up phase. Monitoring and evaluation will be employed as input for progress reports, and will also contribute to internal control and learning.
Monitoring will not only track the quantity and quality of outputs, assumptions and context factors, it will also disaggregate data by gender, age, and locality.
To achieve the above objectives, Tulips International will:
o Provide 35,000 teachers and learners across five IDP Camps in Borno State with learning and teaching materials in three years
o Establish temporary safe and protective classrooms with adequate basic WASH facilities for both formal and non-formal education, targeting 5,000 children in Borno State respectively;
o Train teachers and school-based management committees (SBMCs) in Borno on WASH, sycho-social support and life skills to cope with the negative effects of the conflict;
o Empower communities in Borno State through school management committees to protect education and assist in the establishment and maintenance of clean and child-friendly school environments;
o Support parents and guardians with WASH facilities to increase enrolment and retention rates;
o Provide sanitary kits to adolescent girls in Borno State to encourage them to stay in school;
o Provide WASH facilities materials to school going children within five IDP camps in Borno State and support peace/sports clubs in schools;
o Empower teachers and school management committee members with knowledge on referral mechanisms for issues related to WASH, GBV and child protection.
o Advocate to donors and humanitarian actors to take up education development needs manifested in North East Nigeria most especially in Borno State

1 - Needs

The lack of adequate access to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) constitutes a silent emergency for children and other people living within IDP Camps in Borno State. According to the UNICEF Nigeria Humanitarian Situation Report, 1-30 September 2019, humanitarian access continues to decline due to security challenges and due to the restrictions placed on humanitarian partners. The suspensions of two major INGO partners (Action Against Hunger and Mercy Corps) have had a significant impact on the provision of humanitarian services, including the UNICEF sectors. According to the report, about 1,500 children are estimated to be suffering from severe acute malnutrition, due to disrupted and deeprived nutrition assistance in Borno and Yobe states each week, with severe medical complications and high risk due to the impact of the suspension on eight stabilization centers. As at September, 2019, 4.2 million Children have been estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance (Humanitarian Response Plan, 2019). Over 7 million People are in need of humanitarian assistance in Borno, Adamawa & Yobe (HRP, 2019) with over 1.8 million Internally displaced people in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states (IOM DTM Round XXVIII, August 2019) In northern Borno, violence continue to cause displacements, particularly in and around areas where Nigerian military forces have recently withdrawn ( Gubio, Nganzai and Magumeri Local Government Areas). Over 4,800 individuals (i.e. 1,392 households) fled their homes in September, 2019 in the said areas, with the largest movement of 986 individuals recorded on 10 September. Although, Health and WASH sector partners continue to respond to cholera, acute water diarrhoea (AWD) cases, the overall impact of the outbreak remains far below 2017-2018 levels. The lack of access to safe and clean water, hygienic sanitation results in increased levels of infectious diseases such as cholera, diarrhea and hepatitis amongst the IDP Population and children are the worst affected. In Borno state, over 21 suspected cholera cases were reported in Maiduguri1. This is in addition to 524 cases of AWD2 in Banki (69), Monguno (52) and Gwoza (52) cases although, with no associated deaths. Oral Cholera Vaccine (OCV) campaigns have taken place in the Maiduguri Metroploiis and Bama LGA in Borno state. In many cases, among the worst affected are children of school age who do not have access to safe water and quality education. The outbreak of cholera in Brno IDP Camps were was caused by multiple underlying factors including overcrowding in internally displaced persons' (IDP) camps, a weak national health system, poor sanitary conditions and the lack of clean water and other basic services. The inadequate state of latrines, coupled with the harsh weather condtions creates a need for open defecation as a common practice among primary school-aged children within IDP Camps. In the IDP Camps, most sanitation facilities are built some distance away from the living spaces and children have to walk over 50-100 meters to reach them. This includes travelling when the weather conditions are bad and these WASH facilities were not designed for use in extreme weather conditions. Thirdly, hand washing facilities are either non-existent, ruined or limited within the IDP Camps and children cannot wash their hands properly. During school or classrooms hours, children roam the street instead of being in school, also students roam the streets, the IDP Camps to search for water, creating a gap in their access to educational services.
