Project Summary

Building on the approved project presented last year for 2022-2023, the activities implemented during 2022, and the overall Protection Sector strategy and priorities, the project aims at developing and strenghtening a comprehensive Protection program in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates, focusing on Risk Education (RE) and Child Protection (CP) as key pillars to carry out tailored community-based activities. All the targeted sub-districts fall under severity score 3-5 . An estimated 5% of the beneficiaries has a disability.
Regarding RE, the extensive use of explosive weapons since the beginning of the crisis generated explosive hazards, posing a threat to kill or injure civilians, humanitarian response operators, and disrupting livelihood activities. As school is the most suitable environment to deliver RE to children, teachers need to be properly trained and extensive campaigns, targeting young people throughout the school year, need to be implemented. For this, the Action includes:
- Training of 650 teachers in protection-related topics of RE, allowing themselves to raise the children’s ability to deal with threats posed by IEDs, landmines, and ERWs;
- School-based RE awareness campaigns: each trained teacher will be able to transfer the acquired knowledge to an estimated number of 50 children, reaching 32.500 students.
WWGVC will constantly monitor the training and implement focus group discussions with children to evaluate the impact of the information transferred by their educators and dedicated tools to evaluate the knowledge increase on the topic. Standardized training materials and kits to deliver awareness sessions to children will be provided by UNMAS/UNICEF and approved by the Ministry of Education (MoE).
A second component of the Protection program foresees community-based CP activities focusing on a diversified range of protection needs and tailored to different groups based on age, gender, social role, and responsibilities. WWGVC will carry out events and awareness initiatives (in schools and public safe spaces) as follows:
- Training of 1800 teachers on CP contents, mainly focused on protection needs of children with disabilities (CWDs) and specific sessions on PSS contents;
- School awareness campaigns on CP and social inclusion of CWDs: teachers trained in CP will conduct a campaign targeting 32.000 students;
- Community-based PSS activities: 10.000 out-of-school children and children at risk of drop out will be reached with tailored events implemented with trained SARC volunteers’ support (volunteers selected among the SARC PSS teams in the targeted areas);
-Community-based awareness sessions on CP and social inclusion of CWDs: 3150 community members (mainly educators and parents) will be reached by FGDs facilitated by WWGVC staff and DoE volunteers;
-Support to community-led initiatives: WWGVC will support the implementation of small-scale initiatives implemented by vulnerable teenagers and youth to promote life skills and training as well as engagement and active participation. A pilot of 10 community based initiatives will be promoted.
All the activities will be structured in a way to adapt to different target groups of beneficiaries and to the specific context (e.g. rural/urban) and social dynamics of the community where they take place.

3 - Needs

Based on data from the HNO 2023 and NA conducted by WWGVC in the areas, protection issues are a major concern for communities: civilians continue to be exposed to effects of hostilities resulting in displacement, trauma, violence, and discrimination. This has been worsened by the ongoing economic crisis, which has led to loss of livelihoods and negative coping mechanisms (child labour, early marriage, etc), and by the COVID-19 pandemic/Cholera spread, which have increased risks for many vulnerable communities. This is linked with WWGVC's education intervention, as protection risks are one of the main factors preventing access and retention in school. Based on current estimates, approximately 1 in 3 communities is potentially contaminated by explosives, which have devastating consequences on the lives of children and their families. The crisis is endangering the mental and physical health of children and youth: caregivers are reporting violence and bullying among children as worrying behavioural patterns, and psychosocial distress among children is reported in at least 25% of the HHs. This is particularly relevant for CwD, as evidenced by FGDs and KIIs conducted by WWGVC in the selected areas, as they face increased stigma and social discrimination. This also occurs in the Education system, where teachers are not prepared to deal with the needs of vulnerable children affected by the crises, nor with the specific pedagogical and psychosocial requirements of CWDs.

4 - Implementation of activities

In order to address protection risks related to explosive devices, the Action includes Activity 4.1 (Conduct risk education for at-risk communities), while to address protection concerns for girls and boys, the Action includes Activity 1.1 (Community-based and school-based protection campaigns) and Activity 2.1 (Training of teachers on protection issues). These activities are implemented in close cooperation with the MoE and the Doe, under whose umbrella WWGVC operates. Trainings for teachers on PSS, IE, RE, and other relevant topics, will be conducted with the support of DoE. School based campaigns will be delivered by trained teachers, who will share the contents with the students through a cascade effect.Quality programming, sound management, monitoring, and evaluation mechanism are essential throughout the program cycle. WWGVC’s MEAL Department is structured to allow control on the implementation of monitoring activities at the field level as well as a quality check on the monitoring of indicators. Provided that the required authorizations are granted, the MEAL Manager will perform regular field visits to ensure the correct implementation of monitoring activities, conduct trainings and routine M&E activities, development of data collection tools, establishing of information management mechanisms leading to efficient use of data and development of informative reports aiming at strengthening internal decision-making process, external and internal accountability.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

High turnover and loss of capacity lack of specialized and well-trained staff/service provider (L: High, I:High). Mitigation: conduct training needs assessments, consult with authorities to design programs on their perceived training needs; link with other capacity building initiatives. Creation of wrong expectations among beneficiaries, who could wrongly attribute to teachers delivering RE sessions the ability to remove explosive ordinance; or become overconfident themselves. (L:High, I:High). Mitigation: Clear explanation of intervention and objectives to beneficiaries; Tailoring of messages/RE materials used in schools according to age/gender; Involvement of survivors of explosive hazards as a role model for behaviour change. Existing practices increasing due to limited community engagement of certain practices as protection concerns. Girls'/boys' exposure to protection risk due to limited initiatives to enhance children's participation in their own protection. Lack of sustainable intervention due to limited involvement of stakeholders. (L:Medium, I:High) Mitigation: Ensure service mapping, so that risk mitigation/communication with communities can be proactive; Use sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency. Existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles. (L:High, I:High). Mitigation: Training of staff to act as trainers/ facilitators; Regular engagement of communities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

RISK ASSESSMENT:Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations. Main risks related to PSEA: abuse of power from staff or authorities, lack of reporting, staff does not comply with policy, partners does not have strong PSEA policy, communities not aware of CFM, lack of access to assitance for survivors. WWGVC has a global PSEA and safeguarding policy, that must be signed by employees and contractors, aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including: • placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations reporting any concerns cases • planning and organizing activities minimizing risks for children avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children.Preventions measures include: AWARENESS:new staff is introduced to the policy. TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events. SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices. CFM is in place and communities are informed on how to access it to report complaints.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

AS. 1. Security remains stable Risks: Security situation deteriorates to the point to prevent the implementation of some of the activities 2. Access to areas is safe for staff Risks: Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some intervention areas due to military operation 3. Internal return of people increases because of improved political and safety situation Risks: Major change in demographics in the area of intervention requires modification in the strategy. 4. Collaboration of the Authorities to set up criteria for the selection of the beneficiaries, monitor the activity, and approve it. RISKS: Major change in strategy of authorities and local partners (in particular SARC and MoE) toward the planned interventions and reject it, and/ or delay in approval and bureaucrat requests. MITIGATIONS: 1. WWGVC ensures coordination with partners and international agencies (UNDSS) in order to monitor the situation. WWGVC adopts a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident. 2. Policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, WGs, and coordination mechanisms to stay updated on the reasons which may refrain teachers, educators, and volunteers from attending activities and, generally, discuss any issue occurring in the implementation as it is planned, and/or adjust sessions in accordance with any Sector decision. 3. Thanks to dialogue and exchange with Sector and other agencies, WWGVC ensures good relationship with authorities.
