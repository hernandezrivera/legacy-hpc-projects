Project Summary

BRAC under JRP 2021 will target the Rohingya and the host communities in Cox's Bazar through WASH services, give more emphasis on community engagement with sustainable access to basic life-saving inputs and improvement of the quality of socially inclusive WASH infrastructures. The strategic focus will be on the quality and durability of interventions and building resilience and community engagement with a specific focus on gender and inclusion. Particular focus will be given to outbreaks response (Acute Watery Diarrhoea – AWD and pandemic situation of COVID 19) as well as preparedness to drought, monsoon and cyclone. BRAC will prioritize implementation functionality of the existing WASH services through regular operation and maintenance, promotion of hygiene activities and management of solid waste in the camps. In host communities, the project will support the efforts of social cohesion and attempts to integrate the services between the camps and the host community program. Additionally, this project will address the concurrent pandemic COVID-19 issue regarding the WASH facilities in the camp area and host community as well through strengthening the existing effort, particularly in awareness-raising, hand washing practicing, social distancing. WASH sector strategies and the guidelines will be followed in all stages of the implementation considering CHS standards. BRAC will take into consideration of context specific implementation and high quality monitoring, sustainable and gender-responsive, public service infrastructure to most affected host community by the Rohingya presence, especially to those people living close to camps to contribute to better co-existence.

1 - Identified Needs

"As per WASH ISCG sector, 88% of households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf area. In 2021, the main target is intended to ensure operation and maintenance of the existing facilities with standard quality of water supply. In line with this, A few gaps related to distance and adequacy of water is yet to be addressed. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with hand-pumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without faecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. Domestic waste water re-used for homestead gardening also needed for proper waste water management. Despite 99% of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached (18 persons per latrine and 38 persons per bathing cubicle), many qualitative challenges remain with 22% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas. Sanitation coverage remains challenging due to lack of space in the camps with the highest density (e.g.: 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 9, 10). One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the faecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades and improvements. Considering the daily solid waste generated in the targeted area, SMW remains one of the main challenges as only 51% of the refugee households dispose waste in designated area; 3% and 27% reporting the presence of garbage near their houses. In line with this, integrated solid waste management including recyclable/compostable waste management with business chain development needs to be initiated. Appropriated hygiene practices remains low despite the fact that progress have been observed, which is partly due to fear of COVID-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% refugees have increased hand-washing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% report having access to soap. Those improvements need to be continued and confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. Effective Community engagement is highly needed to ensure the functionality of the WASH facilities and continue the hygiene practices. Community engagement also needed in proper waste management from source to final disposal. The large majority of households in the host communities (88%) access water from tubewells/hand-pumps, but only 77% of households report having enough water for their domestic need (only 74% in Teknaf where groundwater is scared). Over the past year, this figure has significantly increased (from 63% in 2019). Despite high pumping in the aquifers below mega-camp, groundwater availability has been proven sustainable however, impact on the surrounding communities is significant due to depression from pumping cone requiring deeper tubewells and better hand-pumps in host’s communities (especially in the north of Kutupalong’s camp).About 63% households have basic access to sanitation meaning 14% households HH practicing sometimes-open defecation. Regarding waste management, 11% report having often-visible waste near their house. In addition, waste recycling and composting system is yet to be introduced for proper waste management through income generating schemes. Hygiene items and campaign have not supported host communities much, but findings show encouraging data as 96% of the HH reported having access to soap. Hygiene knowledge seems also better in host community than in camps as 81% host community respondents being able to mention three critical times for hand-washing. Community-led sanitation approach where absent needs to be continued to sustain the existing systems. More awareness building and practice along with COVID-19 control measures is needed to response with the current pandemic situation."
