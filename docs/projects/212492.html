Project Summary

Aligned with the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives of the Education Sector, this project aims to bolster educational infrastructure and support mechanisms across several regions: Aleppo (Jabel Samaan), Lattakia (City, Jableh, Hafeh), Hama (Massiaf, Sqailbieh, Hama city), and Rural Damascus (Kesweh, Sahnaia, Zamlka, Arbin). These areas have been meticulously chosen based on critical sector priorities, targeting communities with limited or non-existent educational services, high rates of out-of-school children (OOSC), and those where current learning conditions are unsafe or not conducive to education. This strategic selection is grounded in insights from the HNO 2024 and the broader educational sector's priorities.
The project's core mission is to refurbish or enhance 40 classrooms to promote better learning conditions, incorporating features to support learners with disabilities. Furthermore, 23 schools will be outfitted with resource rooms and supplied with vital stationery, IT, and educational resources, ensuring that children with disabilities have full access to education.
A key pillar of this initiative is the professional development of 230 educators, sharpening their core competencies to boost classroom performance. To support their dedication, educators will benefit from financial incentives, including transportation subsidies and direct cash payments. Additionally, a cash-for-education scheme will assist 1,150 out-of-school children, aiming to reintegrate them into the educational system.
The project extends beyond infrastructure and educator support, encompassing the emotional and psychological well-being of 9,200 children through school-based psychosocial support programs. These initiatives are designed to cultivate a nurturing and supportive educational atmosphere. In partnership with the Ministry of Education (MoE), TdH is set to deploy compliance and feedback mechanisms, such as complaint boxes and hotlines, complemented by comprehensive training for staff in Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), alongside a structured reporting system for handling incidents.
To ensure the safety and protection of all involved, TdH will proactively manage identified protection risks, embedding effective mitigation strategies throughout the project's lifecycle. This holistic approach not only addresses immediate educational needs but also fosters a safe, inclusive, and supportive learning environment for every child

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The overall objective of the project is to increase equitable access to quality education in safe and protected environment for crisis-affected children in Aleppo (Jabel Samaan), Lattakia (City, Jableh, Hafeh), Hama (Massiaf, Sqailbieh, Hama city), and Rural Damascus (Kesweh, Sahnaia, Zamlka, Arbin). The intervention will provide a quality education that supports children’s emotional and cognitive development. The most important approach is to provide a protective and safe environment for the children who are affected by the conflict through:-Ensure that schools are safe and protective due to the child needs and rights.(especially for girls and children with needs) -Strengthen inclusion of children with disabilities in learning programs,- Equal access (Education for all)-Ensure qualitative access by training the teachers how to deal with children based on their age characteristic and needs however below Tdh IT identified the Protection risks and mitigations R1: Local partners lose access to the project locations and are unable to implement activities.M1: The MoE displayed an outstanding ability to implement interventions, despite constraints and limitations. However, if the MoE lost access to project locations, TdH would either identify other suitable partners or, in coordination with them and with local actors, identify other locations with same high needs, where the programming could continue.R2: Bureaucracy and delay in getting needed approvals from local authorities, M2: TdH already obtained approvals to work in the target location, in addition, TdH will advocate through the MoE to have the needed approvals based on the signed MOU, R3: Security risks: TdH security officer prepared Early warning mechanistic to response to threats, including kidnapping, detention, intimidation, and serious injury, M3: the security situation has been considered relatively calm but unpredictable and partners can conduct their work in relative safety, TdH Security person and Security Focal points in the project locations regularly analyze the operating environment and have established safeguards to prevent and respond to risks of insecurity which may arise, addressed through location-speciﬁc safety and security management plansR4: Discrimination of community groups and only a few groups beneﬁt in the targeted communitiesM4: Engage community committee in beneﬁciaries selection (with diverse members of the community to ensure diverse perspectives and perceptions are considered, select staff for all activities (especially volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social proﬁle. R5: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services M5: TdH establishes a speciﬁc complaint and feedback mechanism; as CFRM (complaint and feedback response mechanism); through transparent communication channels (feedback and complaint boxes, hotline, onside observation.
