Project Summary

ACF’s Nutrition Project in Cox’s Bazar is focused on reducing acute malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality among children under five within both Rohingya and host communities. Collaborating with government partners, ACF will strengthen support at Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazila Health Complexes and across all 11 unions in the host community. Key areas of focus include growth monitoring, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), and micronutrient supplementation for children aged 6–23 months, along with vital nutrition support for adolescent girls, pregnant, and lactating women.
At the Upazila level, a specialized SAM unit will deliver advanced care for children with severe acute malnutrition, low-birth-weight infants, and cases referred from community-based outpatient programs. Stabilization Centers will provide essential interventions for infants under six months, such as the Supplementary Sucking Technique (SST), wet nursing, and relactation support. Through the Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) model, ACF will adhere to Ministry of Health (MoH) protocols for both inpatient SAM treatment and community-level SAM identification, alongside IYCF and Growth Monitoring and Promotion (GMP) activities in community clinics. These services will include breastfeeding promotion, complementary feeding practices, cooking demonstrations, hygiene promotion, micronutrient powder (MNP) distribution, and Sexual and Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) services, with an emphasis on iron-folic acid (IFA) supplementation for adolescent girls and pregnant women. A gender-transformative approach will underpin these efforts to expand access to high-quality health and nutrition services.The project also prioritizes increasing awareness of gender-based violence (GBV) and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), with active male engagement in case identification and referral efforts. ACF will conduct coverage surveys in both Rohingya camps and host communities in coordination with the Nutrition sector and with IPHN/NSS approval to ensure alignment with national standards.To enhance community resilience, ACF will strengthen the capacity of government frontline health providers and school students at host community with a comprehensive nutrition training package. In the camp nutrition sector partners staff will be trained nutrition in emergency (NiE).
Ensuring inclusive engagement of men, women, and individuals with disabilities at every stage of service delivery is central to the project. Community sensitization will be bolstered through action-oriented male forums, model mother initiatives, mother-to-mother support groups, and meetings with community and support groups. Together, these efforts will promote sustainable, positive changes in nutritional practices and outcomes across the communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The 2023 Integrated SMART survey results from Cox’s Bazar reveal high levels of malnutrition that require urgent, multi-faceted interventions. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates in Teknaf (12.8%, 95% CI: 9.4–17.1) and Ukhiya (11.1% 95% C.I 8.3-14.7) are both at “high” levels according to WHO/UNICEF standards. This is compounded by an increase in underweight prevalence, with Teknaf rising from 21.9% to 24.8% and Ukhiya from 25.8% to 26.1% between 2021 and 2023. Such statistics necessitate an immediate scale-up of nutrition interventions, including growth monitoring, supplementation programs, and the expansion of Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) services. The survey also identified insufficient access to minimum acceptable diets and dietary diversity for women of reproductive age. In Ukhiya, only 22.3% of children meet the minimum acceptable diet standard, and the dietary diversity for women (48%) remains concerningly low. This directly impacts the nutritional status of both mothers and children, underscoring the need for targeted nutrition support, particularly in micronutrient supplementation and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices. In the refugee camp, between 2017 and 2022, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) decreased from 19.3% to 12.4%. However, by 2023, the wasting rate surged to 15.1%, classified as “very high” by WHO/UNICEF standards. Additionally, severe wasting nearly tripled, from 0.7% in 2022 to 2.0% in 2023. Program data from January to May 2024 indicate a 26% increase in admissions for severe wasting and a 45% rise in moderate wasting cases among children aged 6–59 months compared to the same period in 2023, suggesting a continued rise in cases and the potential for further deterioration in 2025.The gender dimension of the crisis is equally concerning. According to a 2020 inter-agency report, the influx of Rohingya refugees has exacerbated existing socio-economic and security challenges in host communities, with increased reports of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and intimate partner violence (IPV). Women in the host community often face barriers to accessing justice, with many relying on informal, patriarchal village courts where their voices are frequently silenced. This social and structural marginalization prevents women from seeking legal redress and exposes them to ongoing violence. A lack of gender-responsive health and protection services further compounds their vulnerability, with limited access to safe spaces and support mechanisms.To address these challenges, urgent and targeted interventions are required. These should include expanding access to nutrition services, particularly for pregnant and lactating women, and ensuring comprehensive, gender-sensitive protection programs that empower women and girls. This should include mental health support, referral and linkage services, and safe spaces for survivors of SGBV and IPV. Furthermore, engaging men and boys in gender transformation efforts will be key to shifting harmful social norms and fostering a safer, more inclusive environment integrated with nutrition services.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ACF is committed to engaging with affected populations throughout the project cycle, focusing on understanding their risks and challenges. With over a decade of experience, ACF integrates a protection-sensitive approach in its operations, including staff training to identify and mitigate risks, considering age, gender, and diversity. ACF is dedicated to safeguarding dignity and rights, aligning with the “Do No Harm” policy. It provides psychological support through MHPSS services and ensures a fully operational protection referral system, offering tailored support to those in need. Safeguarding is a key priority, ensuring all individuals can access services without fear of harm. ACF prioritizes the protection of children, adolescent girls, and vulnerable women, who are at increased risk of child labor, early marriage, trafficking, and gender-based violence (GBV), including intimate partner violence (IPV) and sexual violence. ACF will ensure effective referral and linkage with existing services to protect these groups and empower them to assert their rights. Programs will address their unique vulnerabilities and provide access to essential services, including health, nutrition, and support for people with disabilities. Additionally, ACF will conduct awareness-building activities on protection issues such as recognizing and reporting GBV, child protection measures, preventing early marriage and trafficking, and promoting safe practices for women and children in collaboration with existing protection actors. These efforts will empower communities to identify risks, seek support, and create safer environments for all.

2.3 - Age & Disability

ACF will actively engage with affected populations throughout the project cycle, prioritizing the inclusion of age- and disability-specific considerations. The program will focus on the most vulnerable groups, particularly children under five and adolescent girls aged 10-19, to address their unique risks and challenges. ACF’s protection-focused approach, developed over a decade of experience, ensures that all individuals, including those with disabilities, have equitable access to services. To identify individuals with disabilities, ACF staff will use The Washington Group Questions on Disability, enabling a standardized and accurate identification process. Once identified, tailored interventions will be provided to ensure no one is deprived of essential services. This includes direct support, referrals, and linkages to specialized services through collaboration with existing actors. ACF staff are trained to recognize the specific needs of diverse age groups and disabilities, ensuring that all assistance is inclusive and responsive to the population's varying requirements.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

ACF is committed to ensuring accountability to affected populations by embedding robust community engagement and feedback mechanisms into its humanitarian interventions. Programs are designed with active participation from diverse community groups, including male forums, mother support groups, community leaders, and adolescents, ensuring their voices shape program priorities and design. A comprehensive Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) guarantees that concerns are captured and addressed transparently. Accessible communication channels—email, mobile phone, letters, verbal reports, and complaints boxes—enable all stakeholders to raise issues. The Complaints and Feedback Mechanism Committee (CFMC) responds promptly, preparing an initial report within 24 hours to assess complaints, including cases of Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Serious cases are prioritized, with processes completed within 24 hours to protect those involved and maintain investigation integrity. Feedback is provided to complainants, and all communications are systematically recorded for transparency and accountability. ACF ensures cultural and linguistic sensitivity, delivering communication and awareness campaigns in the local language. In Rohingya camps, translation support from volunteers and staff with basic language skills ensures clear, direct communication. Participatory monitoring ensures interventions are aligned with community needs, reducing acute malnutrition among vulnerable groups while upholding international standards, such as SPHERE. This approach reinforces ACF’s dedication to accountability and impactful service delivery.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF has established a robust policy and mechanism to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), in full alignment with the standards set by nutrition sectors and the PSEA networks. This commitment underscores ACF’s dedication to the dignity and rights of all individuals involved in its programs. To ensure the effective implementation of this policy, ACF in place a range of technical measures and operational procedures designed to prevent, detect, and respond to any incidents of sexual exploitation or abuse. Key actions will include regular staff training on PSEA, ensuring all personnel are well-versed in identifying, preventing, and reporting any potential cases. ACF will also implement clear and confidential reporting mechanisms, allowing affected individuals to safely disclose any concerns. These mechanisms will be accessible to all beneficiaries, with particular attention given to the most vulnerable, including women, children, and marginalized groups. Furthermore, ACF has established rigorous monitoring and accountability systems to track the effectiveness of PSEA measures and ensure compliance with operational protocols. ACF will also work closely with local authorities and other humanitarian actors to strengthen referral pathways and ensure timely and appropriate support for survivors.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

ACF takes a proactive approach to climate change adaptation and mitigation, integrating these into its humanitarian and development programs to ensure the long-term resilience of affected populations. Building on its extensive experience in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), ACF collaborates closely with its DRR team to establish and implement a comprehensive contingency plan that prioritizes emergency preparedness, response, and early recovery efforts. This plan is designed to align seamlessly with the nutrition sector's emergency response strategies, ensuring that services such as SAM corner are fully functional and equipped to deliver continuous care, even during crises. A key aspect of ACF's strategy is the integration of Disaster Risk Management (DRM) principles into its undernutrition and emergency operations. ACF recognizes that waiting for the impacts of a disaster to be felt before responding is both unethical and inefficient. By understanding the dynamics of undernutrition and emergencies, ACF gives emphasis on proactive steps to minimize suffering before disasters occur. This anticipatory approach also serves to protect the gains made by previous humanitarian and development efforts, safeguarding investments made by communities, governments, and aid partners. This training equips staff with the necessary skills to integrate climate change considerations into nutrition programming, ensuring that communities are prepared to face climate-related shocks and that services are responsive to emerging needs. Concrete actions are integrated into ACF-supported facilities, including the dissemination of key messages on climate resilience and nutrition during regular health and nutrition community forums and awareness sessions.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Emergency preparedness and response are integral to ACF's humanitarian strategy, enabling swift, effective actions to mitigate the impact of crises on vulnerable populations. ACF emphasizes proactive planning through robust contingency frameworks, continuous monitoring of early warning systems, and rapid activation of response protocols. The Emergency Preparedness and Response Team (EPRT) is a specialized, highly trained unit ready to deploy resources, execute emergency actions, and coordinate effectively with key stakeholders. In collaboration with the Ministry of Disaster Relief and Rehabilitation (MoDRR), Disaster Management Committees and Need Assessment Working Group, ACF regularly updates contingency plans, ensuring alignment with evolving risks. The EPRT maintains seamless coordination with sectors, clusters, and working groups, and is prepared to mobilize HQ emergency funds and implement anticipatory actions. This readiness ensures timely responses to emerging threats.For the nutrition program, ACF’s approach integrates preparedness at all levels. This includes ensuring the uninterrupted operation of SAM corners at upazila health complexes, mobilizing trained human resources and volunteers, mobile nutrition team to meet increased needs, prepositioning contingency supplies, and conducting targeted training on Nutrition in Emergencies. Guided by ISCG recommendations and sectoral priorities, ACF will ensure preparedness, rapid response, and early recovery, activating anticipatory action plans efficiently to protect and support at-risk populations.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively engages adolescent girls and boys as key agents of change to promote positive nutrition and health-seeking behaviors within their communities. Recognizing the critical role of adolescents in driving long-term health improvements, the initiative focuses on raising awareness about the dangers of malnutrition, the benefits of a diversified and balanced diet, the importance of sexual and reproductive health, and fostering active community participation in health-related activities. Adolescents will be mobilized through the establishment and revitalization of adolescent clubs, where they will receive tailored training and support to enhance their knowledge and leadership skills. These clubs will serve as platforms for promoting individual behavior change and disseminating vital health and nutrition messages. Adolescent leaders will be identified and equipped to lead sensitization campaigns, organize peer education sessions, and engage with their communities to encourage positive health practices. By empowering adolescents to take on active roles in community awareness and advocacy, the project aims to create a ripple effect of positive change, strengthening collective health outcomes. Through their involvement, the initiative ensures rapid sensitization, increased community engagement, and sustainable improvements in nutrition and health behaviors, particularly for vulnerable populations.
