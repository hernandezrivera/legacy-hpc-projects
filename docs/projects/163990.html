Project Summary

DRC intervention includes both lives – saving WASH assistance and more durable and equitable solutions such as maintenance and light rehabilitation of existing WASH systems. The conflict-affected population is assisted in compliance with the Sector and MoLWR standards. DRC aims to address overburdened communities with a high ratio of returnees and IDPs in the rehabilitation of sewage and water systems. DRC target number for 2020: 1.200.000 individuals
IDPs assistance follows WASH Sector Standards and may be provided in camp settings, collective shelters, and private unfinished/demolished buildings in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as emergency response, according to the Sector’s directive. Prioritization is set on water supply, water storage and provision of sanitary facilities considering also the additional needs of disabled populations. DRC target number for 2020: 12.500 individuals. The intervention will concentrate in areas where DRC has a current presence: Aleppo, Deir Zour, Homs, Hama, Rural Damascus and South Governorates (Daara).

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020. The identification of the actual needs is undertaken in the potential project location after funding is secured. The proposed governorates and sub-districts are the current DRC areas of implementation, thus the continuation of the work in the same area is anticipated in 2020. According to the comprehensive WASH assessments that were conducted in mid-2019 by the Sector, 10.4M people are in need of WASH assistance in Syria. Major wash identified needs for newly displaced populations and IDPs living in IDP sites are related to access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness. IDPs informal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance, specifically women and girls. The WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and effects of poor water resources management. 42 percent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. At least 80 percent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. Such factors pose significant public health risks and have negative cascade effects on communities in terms of public health, inequality, and poverty.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities related to both, durable and equitable WASH solutions, such systems repair and more life-saving responses, are carried out by local construction companies. DRC team is conducting the technical assessments to identify the needs which form the relevant BoQ and drawings of the specific scope of work. In compliance with DRC procurement guidelines, in a comprehensive and transparent process, the company submitting the best offer for the execution of the said work is contracted. In each governorate of intervention, the DRC technical unit comprises a responsible team leader and field staff in sufficient number to monitor and oversee the contractor’s performance with regard to quality, progress and other contract relevant processes (material delivery, etc.). The evaluation of the outcome of the final executed work is taken over by the DRC internal MEAL department in conducting a satisfactory survey and other monitoring processes involving different stakeholders.DRC has a large portfolio of those activities since 2017. Technical interventions in both, Shelter and WASH, are executed through local construction companies. As a result, since DRC is prioritizing quality programming since 2017, direct capacity-building measures during the execution of activities are neglected.DRC has a large portfolio of those activities since 2017. 6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Water distribution not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities: LH: high, Impact: high, mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consulting separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design implementation phases; Establish complaint & feedback mechanism; Monitor safe and equitable access and use of water distribution points.Toilets present GBV and/or health, safety risk; Access to toilet is not available to all 24/7: LH: high, Impact: high - mitigation measures: Ensuring agreed context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensuring consulting women and adolescent girls during design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are set.Explosive hazard contamination during assessment and implementation of rehabilitation of water supply and sewage infrastructure: LH: medium, Impact: high, Mitigation measures: staff and contractor trained to recognize UXOs, distribution of explosive hazard awareness materials. Coordination with mine action sub-sector.Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empowers the position of armed groups or other actors: LH: medium, Impact: high, mitigation measures: Establishing strong links with authorities and operators; Include them in targeting; Ensure carefully handling sensitive information.Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators(during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups : LH: low, Impact: high, mitigation measures: agreed MoUs with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Presence of child labor: LH:high Impact:high - mitigation measures: sign the DRC code of conduct with Contractors.FCRMs in place and DRC filed visits.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Non-availability of good performing contractors - Maintaining a good relationship to good performing contractors, contractor evaluation for procurement processes2. Expanding sanctions of key construction material to Syria - Discussing the new development within the TWG of both Sectors3. The long approval process for work execution - Taking into account the long approval process in the work plan, advocacy and awareness-raising in the sector coordination mechanism, maintaining a good relationship with the ministries in the field4. Increasing inflation and high depreciation of Syrian Pound with consequent increase of prices in the market. Contractors may be affected by the constant increase of price and withdraw from their contractual obligation, creating a delay in the implementation of the activities. 5. Non-acceptation of DRC delivered work by beneficiaries - Remaining in compliance with Sector’s guidelines, informing in advance local authorities and beneficiaries on the planned intervention, referring to MEAL findings of previous similar projects, feedback mechanism.
