Project Summary

The economic crisis in Zimbabwe has resulted in increased vulnerability in both rural and urban households though with more severe adverse impacts on the poor and most vulnerable households. This crisis combined with climate shocks (extreme rainfall and prolonged drought) and epidemics (cholera and endemic typhoid) continues to deepen the humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe. The drought conditions are impacting nearly 5.5 million people (including ~2.6 million children) in rural Zimbabwe who are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance and protection especially during the critical period January to March 2020 as food and water security are threatened. A total of 2.2 million people in urban areas are cereal insecure, and about 1.5 million households are living below the Food Poverty Line and are unable to meet their basic needs.
The drought is affecting the provision of safe drinking water as 24.5% of 42,069 enumerated water points are seasonal and only 18.21% of these are good yielding and perennial. This increases the risk of WASH-related diseases – especially in 23.8% of households lacking improved access. Sixteen percent (16%) of households have to travel more than 1km to fetch water from the nearest primary water source and 32% over 500m. In urban areas electricity and chemical supply challenges have led to a significant decrease in piped water supply leading populations to higher reliance on unsafe sources and increased vulnerability for girls and women. Water production for Harare had reduced by half in September 2019, compared to the same period last year. This has increased reliance on alternative water sources, many of which are unprotected and pose a risk to diarrheal diseases. Diarrheal diseases have been increasing in Harare (September - November) with significant concerns in cholera hotspot suburbs. Thus, the risk of water-borne disease outbreaks only increases as the rainy season is developing
The project aims to ensure that people affected by humanitarian situations have access to safe water, a means to practice good hygiene and improve health and hygiene practices. There is need to restore and improve the availability of water in areas that have been severely affected by the drought. There is need to strengthen sustainability of access to safe and adequate water supply. Emphasis will be on upgrading and repairing and or rehabilitating water sources that have proven to be high yielding perennial sources to serve more people. There is also need for water sources to be inclusive i.e. accessible to people with disability, elderly and most vulnerable. There is need to promote hygiene practices for the affected populations to be protected from communicable diseases. There will be distribution of hygiene enabling NFIs and hygiene education pertaining to safe and hygienic child care and feeding practices to prevent occurrence of diarrheal & other water related diseases. The community based governance structures or WASH Committees will be strengthened to ensure sustainable use, operation and maintenance of WASH infrastructure.
