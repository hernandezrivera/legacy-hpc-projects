Project Summary

In 2022, Shafak will continue to deliver nutrition lifesaving services in northwest Syria and it plans to support 121,917 individuals including 60000 children under five, and 61917 pregnant and lactating women including people with disabilities.
The project targeted locations and intended activities are outlined as follows:
- Aleppo, Jebel Saman , Atareb: Activities will be implemented through six Rapıd Response Teams (RRTs), one Outpatient Therapeutic Program Center (OTP), and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program through WFP supplies-TSFP).
- Idleb, Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin: Activities will be implemented through one Stabilization Center (SC), one OTP Center, 6 RRTs, and TSFP.
- Aleppo,Afrin, Jandairis: activities will be implemented through two RRTs.
- Aleppo, Azaz, Aghtrin: activities will be implemented through one RRT.
- Aleppo, Azaz, Suran: activities will be implemented through one RRT.
- Aleppo, Azaz, Mare: activities will be implemented through one RRT.
- Idleb, Harim, Dana: activities will be implemented through two RRTs.
- Idleb, Harim, Armanaz: activities will be implemented through two RRTs.
Shafak selected the locations of this proposal according to its current existing supported health facilities and nutrition teams funded by other donors.
Through the proposed intervention, Shafak intends to achieve the following objective, which aligns with nutrition Strategic Goals to ensure continuity and increased coverage of nutrition services for prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) among children aged 6-59 months and among pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the supported Shafak health facilities and through RRTs.
Shafak's Nutrition response is being implemented under operational guidance that stipulates adaptations in the context of COVID-19 in order to mitigate the risk of infection while ensuring continuity of life-saving nutrition services, particularly services for treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition, micro-nutrient supplementation, and IYCF-E.
The RRTs’ activities require the list of supplies on a monthly basis which Shafak receives from UNICEF regularly through PAC warehouses in northwest Syria. The supply monthly needs will be calculated and submitted to UNICEF and the nutrition cluster through the established channels.
WFP will secure supplementary feeding supplies (plumpy sup.) to Shafak nutrition teams and centers. WFP will support 3 Rapid response teams in the same areas of Shafak FSL project funded by WFP (General Food Assistant-GFA). This partnership with WFP will complement Shafak intervention by applying 2 approaches of the CMAM program: Blanket supplementary feeding program and targeted supplementary feeding program in the same areas of proposed RRTs.
In 2023, according to Shafak strategy and budget availability, it is expected to reach the same target through supporting the same health services and facilities.

4 - Needs

As HNO 2022 Final draft, The prevalence of malnutrition especially chronic type is a significant public health challenge in Syria.The PIN in Jandairis 31327 Aghtrin 31,857 Mare' 23,125 Suran 16,317, Atareb 60,871, Armanaz 25,464, Dana 373,334, Maret Mesrin 109580.According to the last Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) conducted between (02nd June – 13th June 2021),The overall Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate by Weight for Height (WHZ) was 2.5 % (1.3 - 4.5 95% C.I.)and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 0.4 % (0.1 - 1.7 95% C.I.)The GAM rate by Middle Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC)was 4.7% (2.7 – 8.1 95% C.I.). The prevalence of wasting among pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) was 12.4% (7.7-19.3 95% C.I.). Overall, there is an obvious deterioration in nutrition indicators in 2021 compared to the last SMART survey conducted April 2019 in Idleb and Rural Aleppo,witan increasing rate of GAM by MUAC from 0.9% (0.5 – 1.8 95% C.I) to 4.7% (2.7 – 8.1 95% C.I),while the GAM rate by WHZ increased from 0.73% (0.4 – 1.3 95% C.I) to 2.5% (1.3 - 4.5 95% C.I), both differences by MUAC and by WHZ were statistically significant (p=0.004 for MUAC) (P=0.034 for WHZ), this increase maybe can be explained by the wave of displacement started in 2019 and the economic deterioration due to the inflation and the decreasing of the purchasing value of the Syrian pounds, all these causes had been exacerbated by the Covid-19 outbreaks that led a lot of people to lose their work

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Barriers to access or exclusion of some vulnerable and minority groups from receiving health care for various reasons, such as children and individuals with disabilities and restriction of access to particular places where boundaries of control have altered. Impact: strong, Likelihood: medium. Mitigation: Analyze and disaggregate all data according to gender, age, and handicap. Analyze the requirements of vulnerable and minority groups throughout the program cycle, identifying risk factors and breaches of human rights that have an influence on service provision for beneficiaries. Risk 2: If sensitive data and information involving patient identities, nutrition conditions, and nutrition care provider names falls into the wrong hands, people and communities may face harassment, disgrace, abuse, and even death. The likelihood is medium. The impact is significant. Mitigation: Electronic data and information should be adequately safeguarded and backed up. Risk 3:As a type of protection/risk/threat, violence against centers and nutrition teams Providers Impact: High Probability: High Mitigation Efforts: a- Using the designated de-confliction mechanism, as well as nutrition sector advocacy activities with all parties to the conflict in order to engage them on protection problems and the compliance of international humanitarian law (IHL). b- Ongoing covering of nutritional attacks. The occurrence is being tracked.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Shafak has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse. Support will be provided for PSEA measures to protect beneficiaries and carers, including technical assistance with safeguarding and PSEA policy review and development. At the time of recruiting, all staff members sign the Shafak PSEA code of conduct, and a protection expert gives orientations and regular PSEA training sessions on child protection policy and the PSEA code of conduct. Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Assumption: Borders remain open and favorable to reach to the communities.Risk: the border between Turkey and Syria becomes closed, or the project location becomes a conflict zone, and thus the project may be subject to delays and complications due to expected escalation.mitigation: Shafak may need to secure additional amounts of supplies to be stored inside Syria for easy access for when the fighting escalates. Shafak need to account for time delays due to fighting. Emergency protocols, contingency plans and resources must be put in place alongside drills to ensure the safety of the staff and the project. Security assessments need to be conducted regularly and the project should be adapted accordingly to ensure the safety of the staff and the beneficiaries. Compensation plans will be adopted in the case of staff injury or loss on duty. 2-Assumption: staff chose to remain at the facility or RRTs locations.Risk: Lack of skilled professionals in these locations due to prolonged crisis and nature of the conflict. mitigation: Shafak have resorted to recruiting health and Nutrition workers from neighboring villages and areas and recruiting lesser-skilled workers. 3- Assumption: COVID-19 disease is contained and no infections reach project staff; Risk: project activities, staff and beneficiaries get infected with the COVID-19 disease.mitigation actions: RRTs: will activate protocol shared by the nutrition cluster to implement nutrition activities during COVID-19.
