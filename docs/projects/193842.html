Project Summary

The project will contribute to the HRP strategic objectives 2, and 3, and the Shelter cluster Objective 2. It will do so by supporting vulnerable Bedouin and herding households living in Area C in the North of the West Bank exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns to meet their basic needs and enhance their coping capacities. The project will provide tailored upgrade to substandard shelters in addition to protective materials/tools.
The project is based on the recent shelter needs assessment conducted collectively by the shelter cluster partners during April and May 2022. This assessment identified the most pressing shelter needs of vulnerable households living in substandard shelters in the targeted communities (see needs assessment section). The communities identified are herding and Bedouin communities mostly living in shacks and tents. The type of shelter and their specific setting increase households’ exposure to consequences of potential adverse weather conditions such as flooding, winter storms, extreme heat in the summer as well as access to basic services and geographical isolation. Furthermore, isolated vulnerable households are more likely to be attacked by settlers and most often in times where only a few people are around, in many cases only lonely women, children, older people, children, and persons with disabilities (PwD). At the same time, demolition procedures is a common practice by the Israeli Civil Administration (ICA) for herding and Bedouin communities, which adds another burden of an ongoing vulnerability. In order to respond to the identified needs and to contribute to the HRP and cluster objectives, PUI will provide the below support.
The project will upgrade 41 substandard shelters in order to reduce the exposure of vulnerable households to man-made and natural disasters, harsh weather conditions, risk of water borne diseases and health complications, by enabling them to live with adequate standards which respect their safety, dignity, wellbeing, and privacy. The scope of the upgrading will have an average value of 6,000 USD per household. The interventions will be carried out using a mixed approach of cash and in-kind supplies, as appropriate and feasible. The activity will be tailored to address different group’s needs. For example, upgrading WASH facilities may be introduced to address the concerns of women. PwDs and elderly will benefit from tailored assets and equipment to adapt the shelters according to their specific needs. The project will have another layer of protection through providing protective materials and tools to the households following the technical assessment exercise at the beginning of the project. This will address the vulnerability of communities who are most at risk of settler violence and/or demolition. The selection of protection material and tools will be tailored to the needs of each family/household. This may include fencing, first aid kit, fire extinguishers, solar lights, flashlights, etc.
The project will be implemented in 6 communities living in Area C: Umm al 'Obor and Al Qaffaf in Tubas Governorates. Jabal al Aqra'a, Fraseen, Al Mansura, and Umm ar Rihan in Jenin Governorate.
It should be noted that this project will be implemented in complementarity and in synergy with the work done by PUI under the West Bank Protection Consortium (WBPC), which includes providing emergency response to households affected by settler violence as well as tailored protective measures at community level.

1 - Needs

In April-May 2022, PUI participated in a Substandard Shelter Assessment led by the Shelter Cluster in Area C, in 311 communities. PUI contributed by surveying 32 communities in North West Bank and analyzing the data shared by the cluster to identify the communities in need of shelter upgrading and rehabilitation. The assessment showed that out of the 25,669 residential shelters surveyed, 13% of them were considered substandard and in need of rehabilitation. Particularly vulnerable groups were herding and Bedouin communities (85% of substandard shelters). PUI narrowed down the list of communities through various criteria: non-CPA communities, to avoid duplication with WBPC partners; communities with 50 or less households per community; communities where the prevailing type of shelters is in bad condition; communities affected by settler violence, settlements expansion, the separation wall, and / or demolition and confiscation in North West Bank. The final list of selected communities include 6 communities living in Area C: Umm al 'Obor and Al Qaffaf in Tubas Governorates. Jabal al Aqra'a, Fraseen, Al Mansura, and Umm ar Rihan in Jenin Governorate.Within these 6 communities, 213 families (representing 1,285 individuals) resided in 183 residential shelters. Out of these 183 shelters, 41 are considered substandard. The type of shelters in need of rehabilitation are as follow:- Concrete: 8 shelters- Tent: 16 shelters- Mud and concrete, mud and zinc, roof tiles, wood: 7 shelters- Zinc, asbestos, and mud: 10The findings of the variability to shelter minimum standards in the 6 communities (183 shelters) targeted by this action shows that 90% of the surveyed shelters have frame materials that are not able to support the weight of the roof and any additional live or dead load expected during different seasons like wind and snow. 100% have a roof that is not suitable to control heat (thermal insulation). 90% have roof and external walls that are not sealed and there are leaks. 100% have external walls that are not insulated for thermal comfort. Additionally, 80% of the surveyed shelters proved that they do not have a minimum 5 m2 of covered space per person (including kitchen, corridors, bathroom, or toilet facilities). 80% do not have smooth, level anti-slip floor in all spaces (suitable for children, older persons and people with movement disability) with minimum one layer protection from natural soil. 100% do not have solid and lockable external door into the shelter. While, 100% have window units that are not sealed (from rainwater and wind). In addition, 100% of those shelters do not have functioning mobile or fixed internal or external latrine per shelter and no kitchen with adequate space that may be located inside the house unit or outside the unit based on household preferences, including counter and connected to water sources and drainage system.The assessment was conducted at a community level. The next step will be to carry a thorough assessment at the household level at the beginning of the project, to identify the exact needs per shelter in these 6 communities affected by coercive factors such as settler violence, demolition, and IF military operations and are unable to rehabilitate and upgrade their shelters. The assessment will consider specific age, gender and diversity needs by leveraging on the experience of PUI to ensure an inclusive approach that prioritizes the needs of the most vulnerable.

2 - Activities

• Activity 1: Preparation Phase: Community outreach, technical and specific needs assessment.PUI will reach out to the 6 targeted communities through different channels, such as posters on public infrastructure, mosques and village councils, posts via social media, through meetings, and door-to-door visits when feasible, as well as through CBOs with information regarding project’s objectives and criteria for eligibility. In addition, local committees will be established at community level to ensure active community participation and accountability in all phases of the action.Moreover, the local committees founded for the purpose of the action, will have members representing the diversity of the communities, including representation of different groups based on gender, age, and disability, and will be consulted to identify common needs regarding the implementation and secure accountability towards the community.The assessment of specific needs and design of the intervention per shelter will be based on a comprehensive technical assessment at household level, including socio-economic criteria. Beneficiaries will be consulted on the most appropriate intervention to have a positive impact on their shelter and living conditions. In addition, PUI will include households’ members in the consultation to identify the appropriate responses and take into account special needs/preferences of different groups, such as people with disabilities (PwDs), women, girls, older persons, persons with chronic illnesses, etc. PUI will scrutinize every part of the process to mitigate any risk of doing harm. Furthermore, whenever designing the intervention, a focus will be put on the security risk analyses, as the whole project is targeting locations particularly affected by the coercive environment of the Israeli occupation in Area C (including notably the legal status of the area/community/houses according to Israeli law and related risks).All of the involved actors (staff and committees) will be briefed on PUI Ethical policies. In addition, actors have to sign the organization’s Code of Conduct; and its policy on Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH). • Activity 2: Shelter rehabilitation at household level.PUI will upgrade 41 substandard shelters located in the 6 targeted communities mentioned above. The scope of the upgrading will be tailored to the needs of each family, with an average value of 6,000 USD per household. A mixed approach of cash and material supply will be followed, as most appropriate. At the beginning of the project, during the assessment phase, PUI will identify the beneficiary households that include skilled workers who are able and willing to implement some or all of their shelter upgrades. In other cases, where the beneficiary cannot follow up the implementation, PUI will proceed and hire contractors to carry out the shelter rehabilitation work.Diverse groups including women, men, girls, and boys, older persons and people with disabilities will be consulted throughout the design, implementation and evaluation of this activity. Special care will be granted to specific needs in relation to the different groups and in their use of the shelters, both for privacy, socializing and work. This project will place particular attention on families with people with disabilities or chronic illnesses and female-headed households as these are the beneficiary families with members spending more time at home in the area of intervention. Special attention will be put on specific needs of vulnerable beneficiaries and their participation will be encouraged for the design and implementation of activities. Rehabilitation works will include providing latrines, insulation, ventilation, flooring, windows, and doors (non exhaustive). They will seek to achieve good standards of health/personal hygiene by rehabilitating the kitchen and/or bathroom, and improve privacy - separation between females and males, separation between different families in the same housing unit, lockable door for toilet/shower, at least one room with lockable door, etc. Technical guidance will be provided to the targeted households throughout the implementation of activities to ensure the quality of the work, through regular field visits and phone calls.The project will provide protective materials and assets to the households following the technical assessment exercise led at the beginning of the project. This will complement the upgrading activity and will mitigate risks for households most at risk of settler violence and/or demolition. The selection of protection material and tools will be tailored to the needs of each family. This may include fencing, first aid kits, fire extinguishers, solar lights, flashlights, etc.The project will comply with the minimum SPHERE standards for adequate housing, Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards, gender considerations, and adequate access to housing for people with disabilities.PUI will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware of the intervention and of any aspects that might affect them. In the case of households with pending legal procedures, lawyers will be consulted before any intervention. PUI will keep assessing and adapting to any challenges they might encounter during the implementation through continuously consulting the affected communities. PUI has an active complaint and response mechanism in the West Bank for its current activities. PUI will ensure the communities benefited from the shelter project can access all necessary information and give feedback requiring additional follow up.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

The interventions will be carried out using a mixed approach of cash and material supplies, when appropriate and feasible. The percentages of sharing modalities is based on PUI’s previous experience. The conditional cash assistance will be used as the main modality for shelter upgrading work. This to ensure a dignified assistance with a variety of options and selection of materials. For the in-kind delivery (for instance, first aid kits, fire extinguishers, solar light, fences), PUI will select a supplier through an official procurement procedure. The supplier will provide protective assets and materials to beneficiaries based on their assessed needs.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s feedback and response mechanism (FRM) is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. The MEAL team will strive to ensure that feedback, whether positive or negative, will collected and followed up on by PUI staff independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be shared with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Manager and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. MEAL department will provide monthly anonymous FRM report to support the project quality check, and participate in the close up meeting to integrate feedback for further actions. The yearly strategy exercises will also be the opportunity to discuss and integrate received feedback into future programming.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEAH is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed (i.e. the Protection Cluster Service Directory) and complemented by other resources identified by PUI if needed. Furthermore, all PUI field staff have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partner, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people (for instance by including the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities in its questionnaires to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities); conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes disability mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. As mentioned above in Activities, the project will comply with the minimum SPHERE standards for adequate housing, Shelter Cluster minimum agreed standards, gender considerations, and adequate access to housing for PwDs. Moreover, the local committees founded for the purpose of the action, will have members representing the diversity of the communities in terms of gender, age, and disability, and will be consulted to identify common needs regarding the implementation and secure accountability towards the community. In addition, PUI will include households’ members in the consultation to identify the appropriate responses and take into account special needs/preferences of different groups, such as people with disabilities (PwDs), women, girls, older persons, persons with chronic illnesses, etc. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, conducting inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
