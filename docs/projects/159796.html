Project Summary

This project aims to provide food assistance livelihood production skills to the poor communities living in Qansaxdheere District. Qansaxdheere district is located in Bay region of south west State of Somalia. The district is under South west administration. Somalia has been characterized by recurrent conflict, chronic food insecurity and high levels of poverty. Somalia is also prone to natural hazards such as droughts and floods. The recent report for Somalia released by Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) indicates that approximately 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2019 . Further, the report indicates that an estimated one million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through mid-2020. Also, the drought has had a negative impact on the pastoral areas since there is no pasture for the livestock. Evidently, most of rural Somalia is classified as Stressed (IPC Phase 2), while Guban Pastoral, Bay-Bakool including Qansaxdheere district, and parts of Hiiraan, Galgaduud, and the Jubas are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) . This includes 1.2 million people in crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher).

The main livelihood of the communities living in Qansaxdheere district consists of farming, agro-pastoralist, pastoralist, and small-scale businesses. Aw Mos Development and Relief Organization (ADRO) conducted a rapid assessment in Qansaxdheere district towards the end of September 2019. The overall goal of the assessment was to assess the current situation of the poor host communities both {pastorals and Agro- pastorals} and identify the needs and the existing gaps. The results of the rapid assessment indicated that:
a) The recurrent drought led to low production of food.
b) There is acute shortage of water and pasture for the livestock due to shortage of Gu rainfall season thus affecting the life of poor farmers.
c) The agro- pastoral communities have lost most of their herds due to drought.
d) Lack of assistance to improve the production of food crops and livestock.
e) Lack of income by the poor households due to lack of jobs and income generating activities.
f) Low market price for the livestock due to weaker health condition hence becoming quite difficult for the families to get money in order to buy food for the family.
Aw Mos Development and Relief Organization therefore seeks’ to transform the lives of the poor communities living in Qansaxtheere District through the implementation of the Food Assistance Project (FAP). The FAP will be a strategic intervention in addressing the needs of the residents of Qansaxtheere District as indicated in recent report findings through cash and voucher distribution with 200 poor and women headed household.
ADRO will train farmers with technological and innovative ideas in solving community needs and alleviation of poverty and identify and train 40 women groups on how to grow green vegetables in a kitchen garden using drip irrigation.
ADRO will identify and train 1000 households on how to grow drought resistance crops such as millet and beans.
The primary outputs of the FAP will be 1) documentation of the 200 households. The completion of the modern farming skills training and implementation of the plan;
3) Identify 200 household who will be recipient of the Cash and Voucher transfer. The short term outcome will be 200 households relieved from hunger.
The other Intermediate outcome will include income and food security improvement in 1800 households as they embrace food production skills training in poverty alleviation. Long term outcomes will include replication of the farming methods and establishment of more kitchen gardens that can improve nutrition status.

Project Activities

a) Conduct skills training to 1000 poor and most affected farmers in Qansaxtheere district invest in growing drought resistance crops. For the last 15 years, sorghum growing has been affected adversely as a result of unpredictable climatic conditions causing below average rainfall that could produce the traditional crops.ADRO will identify 1000 poor vulnerable farmers from 5 villages and the town who will be trained on how to grow drought resistance crops such as millet and beans in order to counter the harsh weather conditions caused by climatic hazard. The training will be facilitated by 6 agricultural officers and agronomist who will be recruited by ADRO teach to good agricultural practice that is adaptive to climatic changes. b) Distribution of drought resistance crops to the 1000 farmers each receiving 5 kg millet and 2 kg beans.The growing of the drought resistance crops will be implemented by the 1000 trainees’ farmers as a pilot project in collaboration with ADRO. ADRO plans to provide the millet seeds, bean seeds and to each of the trained farmer. The seeds will only be for one hectare of their farm. c) Conduct skills training of the 40 community groups on how to establish a kitchen gardenADRO plans to identify and train 40 community groups on how to establish a kitchen garden. Each group will be requested to nominate 20 contact gardeners from the different section of the town. After the training, each person will be required to set aside a small size of land near their house and plant the green vegetables such as kales, spinach, peppers and tomatoes. The plants will be watered using recycled waste plastic containers for each plant for drip irrigation using water from the borehole. ADRO will provide the seedlings and equipment for drip irrigation. d) Identify and distribute Cash and Voucher Assistance to 200 households for the first six month of 2020. Most families in Qansaxtheere district are in need of humanitarian assistance. ADRO plans to identify 200 low income poor and women headed household who will be recipients of Cash and Voucher Assistance on a monthly basis. Each family will be given USD 64 per month as part of minimum expenditure basket. ADRO will identify and engage a reliable project committee. The selection will be conducted by ADRO in consultation with project committee and local authorities. The main selection criteria will be women headed household,families with malnourished children and poor farmers and household with no asset. ADRO will conduct regular monitoring through weekly,cash distribution monitoring ,collecting PDM after every cycle, weekly reports and monthly work progress reports.Qansaxdheere town has a functioning market with all kind of food types but the poor beneficiaries do not have income to buy there daily needs. The market has enough food stocks has good supply with Retailers and smallholders who will provide food items to the beneficiaries of the program according to the prices agreed with our organization with out causing price hike and sustain any higher demand and the interactions will be beneficial for both sides, since beneficiaries will redeem their food needs from the market, while the retailers/ traders will make profit with the guaranteed sales for a defined period of the project implementation. It's a kind of economic support by the project to the market, and a source of food provision by the beneficiaries improving market dynamics through the increased cash movements that comes with the projects cash transfer.

Mitigation Measures

The following are the mitigation measures to counter any emerging risks which can destroy the reputation of the FAP or prevent its effective implementation.1. Communities are forced to leave their place of residence to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor)a) ADRO will ensure that lists of the beneficiaries of Cash and Voucher Assistance is kept confidential and personal data will solely be used for Food Assistance Project onlyb) ADRO will ensure that the mode and frequency of distribution minimises safety riskc) ADRO will ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work d) ADRO will develop livelihoods programmes in a way that protects and supports household caring responsibilities and promotes family unitye) ADRO will identify and prioritise the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups 2. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistancea) Provide clear information to the distribution actors and affected population explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving food assistance or being selected for livelihood projects. ADRO will ensure that this information is communicated to women and girlsb) Ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. c) Identify central locations - beneficiaries will not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution pointsd) ADRO will ensure that distributions of CVA, seeds and fertilizers will take place in daylight hours thereby giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfalle) ADRO will ensure that the ration size for seeds and fertilizers is appropriate for the beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for themf) ADRO will work closely in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from food Assistance Project3. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasa) Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response b) In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community FSL workers to monitor, report and refer protection concerns (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures c) Reinforce the capacity of the community - encourage collective livelihood projects over those that benefit individuals. Collective practices contribute to community protection and resilience, particularly in rural community economies which are closely linked with social networks, community structures and cultural practices d) ADRO will offer livelihood opportunities that are suitable for persons with disabilities and older persons – these groups are often excluded from such projects and find it difficult to source funds or other inputs e) ADRO will ensure that all staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries
