Project Summary

The shelter needs of the vulnerable population exceed the humanitarian actors’ capacity. An estimated 45% of the affected population in the 4 assessed LGAs (IDPs in camps/host communities and returnees) is either sleeping outside, living in makeshifts shelters, staying in shelters that are either partially or completely damaged, or sharing shelters. The shelter conditions recorded are thus poor with less than 3.5m2 of space without any vector control environment (stagnant water, waste/garbage within the vicinity etc.) in certain areas. The region is also prone to natural disasters (fire/flood/winds) that further deteriorate housing conditions (10,757HHs were reported affected by floods by OCHA flood overview situation report No2 at the end of August 2019). In addition, given the current security context, it is extremely risky, in terms of protection, for the IDPs, especially the women, to go in the bush outside the trenches to get the material (branches, wood) necessary to rebuild their shelters. This response intends to be on the one hand adapted to the differences of context and the actual humanitarian presence and on the other hand complementary with other sectorial responses.

1 - Needs

The complex emergency and conflict in North-East Nigeria started in 2009 and has resulted in the displacement of over 2 million people. Overall, 7.1 million people are in dire need of assistance across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. IDPs' situation is critical, as they are living in severely congested temporary camps, prone to regular epidemics, rain, flooding, and fire incidents. Due to increased hostilities and insecurity, the number of registered displacements continues to increase, and is expected to continue, as the Nigerian Government Forces adopted the strategy of establishing “super camps” to strengthen their defenses in population concentration, thereby removing troops from remote areas.Recent series of assessments conducted in North East Borno (Shelter GAPs in NE Nigeria July) by shelter/CCCM & NFI partners reported 97,817 households in need of shelter in Borno's 14 LGAs. The emergency shelter distributed by partners have a short lifespan. Many shelters that were distributed years ago are in dire need of replacement and repairs. Fire outbreaks in April- June and then November 2019 destroyed thousands of shelters in Gajigana, Monguno, Dikwa and Maiduguri. In an assessment carried out by SI in its areas of intervention, 20% of female respondents reported living in emergency shelters compared to male respondents (6%) and 56% of respondents reported damage to their shelters caused by storms (61%), flooding (12%), conflict (11%), and the environment (11%). The CCCM partners identified congestion in the camps and land acquisition to construct humanitarian infrastructures as the main bottleneck for humanitarian shelter response. As a result, the provision of an adequate humanitarian response to these needs is impacted.

2 - Activities

Emergency shelter Kits: With continues movement of persons from one location to another, the number of new arrivals continue to increase across different locations. SI has been intervening through this activity and will continue to provide emergency shelters kits in coordination with Shelter/CCCM & NFI sector, a strategy which has proven to be successful in all areas of SI intervention. For the proposed project year 2021, SI through its proposed 1,500 Emergency Shelter Kits will continue to respond to the expected movements of population in its 4 areas of intervention as important influxes have been witnessed all across Borno State. Transitional Shelter: SI will also provide 250 T-shelters to the community and train carpenters to support the construction work at the community level while unskilled labor will be from same community. SI will provide technical support to the targeted most vulnerable households in the construction of the shelters with a design respecting SPHERE standards and dedicated support teams. Shelter repair kits: will thus be distributed for communities to be able to maintain longer their habitation; Provision of Shelter Repair Kits: SI plans to distribute 1500 shelter repair kits in Maiduguri, Dikwa, Monguno and Ngala for shelters in high need of rehabilitation. Prepositioning and Distribution of 4000 NFI Kits: SI plans to distribute 4000 NFI kits in Maiduguri, Dikwa, Monguno and Ngala. There is a huge need for NFI kits. SI proposes to preposition 2000 NFI kits for pipeline to be used in emergencies while 2,000 kits will be distributed to cater to IDPs' needs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SI prioritizes the needs of various vulnerable groups in their programming. Protection of children, women, and people with special needs is a cross-cutting theme across all programs to increase the efficiency of humanitarian assistance. Ignoring the specific needs of vulnerable population groups can have disastrous implications in terms of protection and survival for the victims of humanitarian crisis. Under this project, SI will ensure that participatory methods and human-centered approaches are used to access and incorporate specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle with a specific focus on gender and vulnerable population during community discussions and assessments.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI will collect the need of the community since the assessment phase through community consultation and participatory approaches. This would be done by using the mechanism to accurately collect the different community groups' specific needs, considering gender and age specificity, and other specific vulnerabilities. SI will establish through community participation, community-based complaint management committees that members will comprise of elder statement and women of integrity whom are known and well respected by the community members. These people are usually the point of call as traditional means of settling the conflict in the community. SI has already established feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCRM) and feedback, so that community can provide feedback about SI intervention through different channels, i.e., CRM boxes, Hotline numbers, feedback collected by MEAL team on a regular basis. All Solidarites staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse/etc. if needed. SI employees have been trained about PSEA risks. SI is a member of PSEA network in Nigeria. The needs analysis and assessments conducted by SI capture the differences between men and women, and both groups are consulted. Although all the beneficiaries will benefit from the proposed intervention equally, women will be involved in identifying the locations for Water and Sanitation services. Gender and age disaggregated data on programme coverage and impact will be collected, analysed and routinely reported on and programme activities monitored for improvement in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men, girls and boys. SI will use at least 1% of this budget towards protection mainstreaming activities.
