Project Summary

This project is hoping to improve the lives of more than 121,731 stayed in camps in the northwest of Syria, which poses an additional burden on the area. The targeted 129 camps (2023) in
Aleppo and Idlib governorates are either an extension for pre-established camps or new
camps established by the IDPs in haste to end a place to stay and start a new life, the
camps were added in the ISIMM database, and the project will establish an effective camp
management system to support humanitarian efforts and organize the work by an
effect. The camps are the last resort of the people who left their homes and assets
searching for a safer place away from the danger to the lives of their children, women, and
the elderly.
SECD is aiming by the support of the donors to put an end to the threats to 121,731 IDP’s (2023)
lives and dignity by implementing complementary activities to improve the targeted
camps' infrastructure, ensure provide protection for the women, children, and elderly with
focusing on the inclusion mainstreaming for people with disabilities through the
following key activities in the 2022 year :
-Establishing camp management and training them on the necessary knowledge to
targeted camps
-Prepare a fire response plan in the targeted camps by establishing 129 fire point
extinguishing points and training for a fire safety plan.
- Provision of comprehensive support to existing or establishing fire mitigation and
response system
in IDP sites.
-Developing fire warden systems, and creating/supporting fire committees, which will be
responsible for providing awareness sessions for IDPs on fire hazards and how to
respond to incidents.
- Provision of fire response resources (such as fire extinguishers, fire blankets, fire
extinguisher tools, and first aid kits) about 23000 fire blanket will be distributed
-Provision of integrated care and maintenance through the inclusion of camp management in basic
infrastructure in IDP sites.
- The camp management's respective committees will be supported and equipped to
conduct cleaning and/or small maintenance for drainage systems, reinforcing areas of
the site impacted by flooding or other incidents through small-scale intervention, ensuring
access to facilities (latrines, showers) for women and children, mitigating protection risks
by improving and maintaining lighting, fixing fences, organizing camp gates in sites and
refilling fire extinguishers and replenishment of other fire response
-Provision of relevant vocational training and tools to camp management committees to
participate in the infrastructure upgrade/repairs in the sites. This would also potentially
contribute to building a skilled-workers portfolio for Cash-for-Work initiatives.
All the activities will be done based on the needs, and SECD with coordinate with the
CCCM cluster will choose the camps of the urgent need for a response.
The intervention targeted the first-priority camps in need of the proposed activities
according to the respective cluster recommendation and this was supported by the in
depth need assessment conducted by our MEAL department
This intervention is saving lives by considering the protection and focusing on a cost
the effective and sustainable solution to be provided to the displaced people who are living
under the danger of being infected by the fire incidents, the good, and the inability to
access the basic services.

3 - Needs

On September 2022, the MEAL department at Syrian Engineers for Construction and Development (SECD) conducted a needs assessment in the self-settled and planned camps in Jisr Ash-shughur district (Badama, Janudiyeh, Darkosh, and Jisr Ash-shughur) sub-districts, Idleb- Maaret Tamsrin and in Jebel Saman districts (Daret Azza and Atareb) sub-districts to assess the most challenges in terms of camp management and fire point plans’ services and determine gaps and the main needs of IDPs living in the camps. The questionnaire has been deployed on KOBO to fill in the information directly. Then data were sent to the MEAL department in turkey The assessment is at the KIIs level and the total sample size is 136. The KEY FINDINGS 1. According to the data analysis 69 of the assessed camps have no contingency plans. Also, according to the KIIs.2. In regard to camp committees, analyzed data show that 72% of camps have no committees at all however 13% of assessed camps have committees according to the KIIs.3. According to the KIIs,61% of the assessed sites reported that they do not have any fire points while % 36 of them mentioned they have. 4. According to the KIIs, 62% of the assessed sites reported that they do not have contingency plans while 37% of them mentioned they have. SECD will include the beneficiaries from the very beginning of the launch of the project. 2. The absence of a drainage system in 53% of camps will put these camps at risk of flood.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

SECD conducts a security assessment for its projects by receiving information on security situations from its network of local councils and civil society activists, as well as by remaining continuously apprised of security situations through bulletins released by the international community. For emergency relief projects, when a security situation arises that threatens the safety of staff, the staff is redirected to a nearby, safer location until the area is safe enough to conduct the aid or distribution project. SECD takes time to advise its staff of ways to mitigate against security threats – such as avoiding specific routes, avoiding certain groups, or selecting locations or building types that are lesser targets.SECD developed Safety and Security Plan that governs operational activities from our base in Gaziantep Turkey, Aleppo, and Idleb Offices; as well as protocols for travel, communications, and operations within Syria to let our staff work under the above-mentioned conditions; as the staff is selected from people in the same region where we are working; SECD works with coordination with local councils CCCM, SNFI, WASH and AWG while that gives us more legitimacy in the ground .Risk 1: Beneficiaries are targeted by armed/terrorist groups as a result of project participation Likelihood: lowImpact: MediumMitigation Strategy: The project will operate in coordination with the related local councils, so beneficiaries' interaction with SECD is unlikely to cause harm.
