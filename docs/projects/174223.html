Project Summary

Project Summary: Intervention and target
This project will target 200 pregnant women and 5000 children based in Kumbo East Health area which covers the following villages, Shisong, Kingomen, Nkuf, Bamngam, Mbah,Nkar, sop, Jakiri. A mobile screening team will be formed by Shisong Hospital, these will reach farm houses where lots of people have resettled; given that these are even areas where we have a lot of the above named health problems (Malaria parasite infections, low hemoglobin levels and skin infections).
The population will be sensitized through the media, churches, meeting houses, community Health workers on the importance of this screening. Days will be communicated for them to gather at the Shisong Hospital for screening or at any convenient and safe outreach station/hospital where screening and treatment will take place for the IDPs settled in farm houses.
Purpose
The purpose of this project is very much linked to the effects of the socio political crises
With the advent of so many houses that have been burnt and property destroyed, the insecurity that has continually rocked the inhabitants of Kumbo, the following problems have ensued:
• Many families have relocated leading to overcrowding in the hosting homes, poor hygienic conditions, poor nutritional habits consequently lowered immunity. Many of them have found farm houses or farm huts safer and so they spend longer times in these places and only dash to their real homes to collect necessary items. These farm houses were not constructed for the purpose of hosting the concerned families therefore there is a big challenge of space, household basic needs exposure to insects and parasites. The living conditions are generally poor likewise hygienic conditions. Fungal infections have become the order of the day in this health area, part because of poor hygiene and part because of cross infections as a result of over-crowding.
• Linked to this is the problem of poor health seeking patterns brought about by financial constraints due to constant inability to work farms in the face of the socio-political uncertainties, no market to sell their little cash crops and the general drop of currency flow in this area.
• Poor nutrition is also a big challenge; reason for the poor Hemoglobin levels and the lowered immunity of this population.
• It is therefore hoped that this project will make such an impact if it functions in connection with the food security program.
The general objective is therefore to screen, treat and prevent re-infections.
Specific objectives
• To carry free screening for Malaria, Hemoglobin levels and skin infections
• To treatment diagnosed cases
• To distribute mosquito nets to the children and retrain pregnant mothers on the use of insecticide treated nets.
• To link with the Hospital to collect the Insecticide treated mosquito nets to the pregnant mothers in hard to reach areas.
• To educate on environmental/personal hygiene, diet, suitable dressing for farming to prevent mosquito bites
• To refer complicated cases to the hospital especially cases of severe malaria.
• Do follow up visits to the communities
• To monitor, evaluate and report to partners

1 - Besoins / Needs

Description of the project and needsThis project targets pregnant women and children 0-12 years old. These groups of persons have suffered most as a result of their biological vulnerability. Children who are school drop outs have taken up farming as a way of survival, they spend very long hours in the farms which are around thick bushes likewise their living spaces; this exposes them to lots of mosquito bites. A whole day of work in the farm for children in this rural area means eating nothing of nutritional value. The double effect of low immunity and malaria infection has proven to be very devastating to their state of health.If Safe motherhood is to stand as a priority, it is important to manage the problems of these women who are living in very difficult situations.In the Hospital and Health centers we have been recording a lot of cases with malaria and anemia in pregnancy and resultant post-partum Hemorrhage, and preterm labour among those who make it to Antenatal consultations. Our greatest worry has been those who are in hard to reach areas. In response to this the St Elizabeth Catholic General Hospital Shisong organized free out-station screening, but the health needs of this underprivileged population have been enormous.Most of the pregnancies in this project area are adolescents who in addition to their physiological needs have no economic background. The loose lifestyle out of school has impacted very negatively on teenage pregnancies.The Government has been doing a lot in the provision of Fansidar for intermittent preventive treatment and mosquito nets for pregnant women but there is yet a lot to be done. Presently there is a rupture in the supply of Fansidar so the women have to pay for their prescription. Most pregnant women have relocated, many are settled in the farm huts and most of the women who received mosquito nets in their earlier pregnancies have had their homes burnt. Many of these because of financial constraints hardly start ANC on time and even when they finally start, are not able to pay for the ANC related services.Many families are not able to bring the children to the hospital and the ones who come are unable to pay for their health needs. These end in quaks who mis-manage and push them to severe malaria or resistant malaria with far reaching consequences. There is no program that covers children for insecticide treated mosquito nets.The TSSF have not remained indifferent, they opened a maternity home in the Hospital for the homeless during their pregnancy and post delivery period. Their clinics bills, feeding and other needs are taken care of by the organization of the Tertiary sisters of St Francis. This Religious organization has also put in place an outreach program with target being those who have relocated hard to reach areas. Given that as a religious organization they also have limited resources, this project will go a long way to enhance this work.Activities:Free screening for Malaria, Hemoglobin levels and skin infectionsFree treatment of diagnosed casesDistribution of mosquito nets to the children and retraining pregnant mothers on the use of insecticide treated nets.Link with Hospital to collect the Insecticide treated mosquito nets to the pregnant mothers in hard to reach areas.Health education on environmental/personal hygiene, suitable dressing when going to the farms to prevent mosquito bite and diet in pregnant women and children.Referral of complicated cases to the hospital especially cases of severe malaria.Follow up visits to the communitiesMonitoring, evaluation and reporting to the partnersThe expected outcomeA high coverage of the population in hard to reach areas; at least 80%An 80% reduction in new incidences of malaria, anemia and children with skin infectionsIncreased uptake in the ANCReduction in the number of those who deliver in facilities not suitable for deliveriesReduction in post partum hemorrhage, preterm births from malaria and anemia in
