Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide health services for both genders Male and Female in Aleppo and Idlib Governorates through a package of services as follows;
1- Support Shamarin specialized hospital code: B0204014 to support 17400 (8526 male, 8874 females, 696 elderly, 4350with disabilities) beneficiaries in A'zaz district. The main services are necessary specialized medical services, especially in the following fields:
• # ER cases 7200
• # Specialized clinic consultations 7200
• # Major Orthopedics operations 960
• # Minor orthopedics operations 1920
• # Lab tests 12000
• # X ray 7200
• # Referred cases 120
2-Run one Hemodialysis center in Jarabulus code B0208014: This center will serve 50 ESRD patients both acute and chronic kidney diseases through supporting a hemodialysis center in Jarabulus district and will provide 3200 hemodialysis sessions.
3-Support one rehabilitation and Prosthetics center B0204009 in Azaz: the center will provide free of charge prostheses to people with amputation or paralysis to mitigate their physical, social, and psychological suffering of them, besides training sessions for them.
# of prosthetic prosthesis delivered 360 (167 male, 133 female)
# of training sessions 1080
# of patients receiving rehabilitation services 400 (196 male, 204 female)
# of physiotherapy sessions. 2400
4-Manage the vaccines Cold Chain management in NWS: this sustainable mechanism aims to store and distribute effective vaccines to all accessible areas and to ensure high immunization coverage in NWS (northwest Syria). By running this cold chain, the EPI (Expanded Program Immunization) program will be supported through;
•Support 4 cold rooms to Storage of UNICEF-provided vaccines to the Expanded Program of Immunization in northern Syria, and COVID-19 vaccines
•Maintenance of the cold chain system in 155 vaccination centers inside Syria
•Cover the transportation of the vaccines to all the 155 vaccination centers which provide EPI services for children under one year old (targeted 162,936), pregnant women 162,936, one polio campaign CU5 815,242, and eligible adult for COVID-19 vaccines 1,720,000.
5-Cover the management technical activities of the EPI central team in Aleppo governorate (Supporting effective and equitable routine immunization services to eligible children):
EPI immunization activities inside N. of Syria are implemented through vaccination teams, which administratively are belonging to NGOs implementers, and technically follow to SIG technical committee. In Aleppo governorate, and in absence of government, a supervisory team is formed to manage the technical issues of the program ( governorate supervisor, in addition to logistic, social mobilizer, data officer, and finance officer), in addition to governorate level there are 5 supervisory teams at district level ( district supervisor, logistic, social mobilizer, and data entry ), these supervisory teams manage the technical aspects of EPI in the field under supervision and support of SIG technical committee in Gaziantep in Turkey. This work is technically supervised and supported directly by WHO experts.
Description of Beneficiaries:
•Children under one year of age for one year to complete the vaccination schedule (67,068)
•Pregnant women (67068)
6-Support one polio vaccination campaign: the campaigns are vital to eliminating the polio outbreak strengthening immunity status among Syrian children and decreasing morbidity and mortality from poliovirus
# of conducted campaigns 1
# Vaccinated children 239,246
7- Run one specialized Mental health facility in A'zaz to provide secondary mental health care both OPD & inpatients for acute and chronic severe mental health problems, and Substance abuse disorders rehabilitation unit.
M&E plan using the project indicators and measures will be developed and followed on monthly basis. PAC M&E officer and 4 field officers will supervise the plan implementation

3 - Needs

The COVID-19 pandemic and the worsening economic situation is further stretching and disrupting the already weak and fragile health system in Syria, thus affecting the availability and quality of health services across the country, as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population. according to HNAP2022, 4,186,617 people live in NWS, and out of the 2,684,931 are IDPs. The total population living in Jarablus is around 106,677 people including 45,361 IDPs.The hospital of Shamarin is the only facility in north rural Aleppo and Afrin with the capacity to provide these specialized services (major orthopedics surgery and cardiac care unit). This hospital is playing a critical role in supporting the secondary health care service as one of 4 hospitals for a population of over 500,000 beneficiaries.In northwest Syria, there are 17 hemodialysis centers providing hemodialysis services and the number of patients is 702 with the total number of dialysis sessions as 6058 per month (TWG)Also, mental health care is still considered a huge gap in NWS response. the proposed A'zaz mental health center will work to support this gap and facilitate access to specialized services on several levels. This facility covers a population of 730,000 including both IDPs and the host community, as the only specialized facility in A'zaz , Afrin district. This project will serve both genders ( male and female), and will ensure well PSEA mitigation and prevention measures are conducted.

Storage of UNICEF-supplied vaccines to the EPI in NWS b. Vaccine transportation from Turkey-Syrian borders to the cold rooms and EPI centers 5- Provide routine immunization services to all children <1 year of age, pregnant women by supporting the supervisory team of EPI at the governorate and district level 6- Implementation of one Polio campaign in Aleppo province in the north of Syria: To immunize targeted children under 5 years of age (239,246) in all accessible areas in Aleppo in the North of Syria. 7- Shamreen Mental hospital is the only hospital in A'zaz, Jarablus, and Afrin district and covers a population of 500,000 IDPs

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

PAC safety&security officer will manage the protection plan of this project. The field team will be trained on how they best deal with serious and life-threatening situations. They will be provided with regular updates and recommendations to assure their safety and security. Coordination with Civil Defense in areas of implementation will be followed up to make sure their prompt access and response whenever needed. Other coordination with the health facilities, camp management, and emergency response focal points will be enhanced. Risk management plan summary considers the following: 1. Attacks on the beneficiaries and/or project personnel due to a deteriorated security situation.' Likelihood: low Mitigation: Immediate cessation of activities and waiting for a change in the situation.2. Destruction of fixed facility structures, plundering of furnishings due to a deteriorated security situation. Likelihood: low Mitigation: Suspension of activities; analysis of the situation, rehabilitation and/or replacement of missing equipment. 3- COVID-19 pandemic:Likelihood Medium: Mitigation; PAC will ensure all staff vaccinated and social awareness activities are included. Also, will mobilize the BNFs to get vaccinatedRegarding child protection, and PSEA, PAC will ensure well implementation of nutrition cluster protection matrix recommendations by integrating child protection and PSEA in all the activities levels and ensuring a safe and sound environment for children and mothers.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PAC is an active member in the NWS PSEA network and maintains close monitoring activities over the PSEA in its ongoing and planned projects. For this project, the PSEA strategy can be summarized as follows; Risk #1; Beneficiaries and community members have no access to the PSEA complaint and reporting system:Mitigation: PAC has clear signs and a feedback system in its fixed facilities that show how the reports can be provided through email, phone, social media, and feedback boxes. Also, the mobile team will distribute IEC matriels to encourage the BNFs to share their feedback Risk#2: PAC newly contracted staff have a poor experience in PSEA. Mitigation: PAC has a clear policy presented to every staff that shows the PSEA mitigation mesuears. Also, the PAC PSEA code of conduct is well written and all the staff signed on it. In addition to that, PAC has mandatory online PSEA training that should be completed by every newly contracted staff training link: " https://agora.unicef.org/course/info.php?id=18168", and all PAC staff should do annual refreshment training. furthermore, PAC has 4 PSEA focal points in the field to support the field workers in full adherence with PSEA guidelines. Risk #3: The investigation process in the reported PSEA is not well managedMitigation: PAC has well-trained PSEA officers. Also, any PSEA case reported to PAC team will be processed according to PAC's PSEA guidelines, and will be directly reported to the PSEA network to have their support
