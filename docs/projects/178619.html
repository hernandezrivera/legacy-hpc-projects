Project Summary

The project supports IDPs and host communities in Idleb and Rural Aleppo with WASH services, both emergency services for IDPs in camps and out of camps. People in camps will receive drinking water through water trucking or water pumping where possible, water storage tanks, constructing septic tanks, latrine blocks, desludging, garbage bins and garbage collection in addition to conducting hygiene promotion sessions. Hygiene kits and diapers for the elderly and PWD will be distributed three times.
In communities out of camp, both IDPs and host communities will benefit from rehabilitation and operation of water pumping stations, and the rehabilitation of sewage network. This project will contribute to the improvement of the COVID-19 preparedness and prevention capacity of IDPs and host community residents, through ensuring the continuity of the WASH services and provide hygiene and protective materials as per the recommendation of the COVID-19 Global Humanitarian Response Plan to prevent the transmission of COVID-19.

4 - Needs

After ten years of conflict, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages “With 36% of general population relying on alternative to piped water and often unsafe water supply modalities to meet or complement their water needs, at least 70% of sewage being discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems not functional, and about 29% of garbage inappropriately disposed, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support “ HNO 2021. As of 21 December 2020, some 2.7 million people remain displaced in northwest Syria. According to the CCCM Cluster that monitors 1,379 IDP sites that host 1,673,680 IDPs or 318,577 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 23,660 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. (Feb 2021).The outbreak of Covid19 made the situation worse, until the end of January 2021, 20,997 confirmed cases with COVID-19 were reported in Northwest Syria ( ACU). WASH needs in institutions, like schools or healthcare facilities, remain very high. With ongoing COVID-19 pandemic appropriate WASH conditions in healthcare facilities critical. HNO 2021. HIHFAD conducted a multispectral assessment in March 2021 in Harim and Idleb districts, both in camps and communities, and found that Provision of safe water in camps is a key priority. The use of water trucks was reported most frequently in 95% of camps, 30% of them are paid by the IDPs. The majority of people depend on septic tanks for drainage. Nearly 80% of KIs said that sewage networks are facing various problems in both camps and communities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will be based on the technical needs assessments by HIHFAD, the targeted camps will receive the following services (water trucking, water storage tanks, Latrine blocks and septic tanks desludging of septic tanks, garbage bins, hygiene promotion and hygiene kits. A sewage line will be installed in Abnaa Mahein camps in Dei Hassan. Moreover, garbage bins will be distributed in all targeted camps, and they will bedischarged three times per week (for every 10 HHs 1 garbage bin of half a barrel). HIHFAD field team will be responsible of implementing the activities of the projects. Latrines will be purchased from Turkey according to the design which includes three latrines+1 for people with disabilities and are gender segregated.The project will take into consideration factors that hinder PWD from benefiting and participating in the project, by ensuring representatives of PWDs in water committees in the camps, and tailoring the activities to reach them as the location of PWD latrines, and tent to tent distribution of kits for them.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of contractors: HIHFAD inform all contractors and included a paragraph in the contract to forbid any supplier from contracting children in daily labour. Movements of garbage trucks and water trucks in congested camp: HIHFAD discuss with beneficiaries and camps managers the best routes leading to the water storage tanks, latrines and garbage bins in order to avoid any potential risk.Health risk associated with garbage removal: HIHFAD ensures that contractors involved in SWM activities wear appropriate Personal Protective Equipmentlike gloves and face mask during work. Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. Water distribution not safe to access or not accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution. Mitigation: Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of water collection points remains at minimum distance.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are several risks that threaten the safety and security, these are the major risks: 1 – IEDs and remnants of war, to be considered one of the biggest and most dangerous threats at all since the region witnessed long-term fighting and clashes which were the main cause of spreading these materials among the villages and the towns. 2 - Disputes between the factions of the Syrian Free Army from time to time, these problems often endquickly and on the same day without escalation of the military position. 3 -Retaliatory military operations and assassinations by the sleeper cells belonging to the Kurdish militias located in the remote areas. HIHFAD maintains detailed security protocol for its field staff and patients.
