Project Summary

Establishment of strong health systems in a country plays a major role in reduction of mortality and morbidity rates in any country. South Sudan has struggled to achieve this due to ongoing political unrest that has severely affected health service delivery and impacted negatively on the lives of vulnerable and affected populations.
Flooding have also had their toll on the heath infrastructure, destroyed medical supplies and displaced populations that includes health workers. This has greatly affected health service delivery leaving the communities with great need of integrated humanitarian assistance that includes health, WASH, Food assistance and Nutrition services.
More than 52.6% of the total South Sudan population are in serious food in security with some of the counties even facing starvation (IPC analysis 2020). This is interlinked with lack of health services that can lead to mass casualties as vulnerable and food insecure commodities cope to get access to food and health services.
Cueibet and Rumbek North Counties have been having health challenges due to lack of health facilities and nonfunctional health facilities. This has affected health service delivery and people are forced to long distances to get health services exposing them to even greater protection risks especially to the girls and women.
South Sudan has huge challenge of communicable diseases with malaria being a big challenge and contributing to almost 56% of the morbidities reported in the country and measles and other vaccine preventable diseases like Hepatitis B and E being reported in the country.
The health infrastructure in the country has been affected by the COVID 19 pandemic and this continues to be a big challenge to the country with lacks enough well equipped health facilities to support proper management of the viral disease.
Mother and child health care is a big concern with high maternal and child mortality rates as well as lack of trained health workers like midwifes contributing to this high rates as well as poor referral pathways causing delays in the health service delivery in the country.
Women Aid Vision therefore would like to bridge this gaps by providing mobile health services in Cueibet and Rumbek North Counties. WAV will ensure that the people who are not able to reach the health facilities are reached with live saving health services.
This will include Provision of outpatient consultations to the vulnerable and affected populations, ANC and PNC services that will include folic acid distribution to pregnant mothers, Vaccination to the vulnerable and affected children. Vitamin A supplementation, Malaria treatment, COVID 19 awareness, capacity building of health staffs, Weekly EWARS and IDSR report submissions.
WAV will also support mental health services, integrate GBV, WASH and Nutrition services in her programming to ensure that children under five years, pregnant and lactating mothers have been screened for malnutrition and referred to nutrition centers for management. WAV will carry out case management of rape and referral of GBV victims also offering Psychosocial support to the vulnerable populations.
All the activities will be done in collaboration with CCUAM and the County Health Department to ensure that the most vulnerable in the community are reached with live saving health interventions avoiding duplication of services.
WAV intents to reach 20000 vulnerable and affected populations in the two counties with live saving mobile health services of which will be 12000 male and 8000 female. WAV will ensure disability inclusion with 731 people with disabilities male 431 females and 300 males supported with health services and assistive therapy where possible.
This will be through 2 Mobile clinics in each of the counties and 1 PHCU in each county to offer integrated health services.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has weak health systems that can be attributed to the political instability that has destroyed health infrastructure, displaced health workers thus affecting health service delivery in the country. In 2021, the situation was made even worse by the flooding that has affected more than 760000 million people (Flood Situation report November 30th 2021) Lakes state has been among the states worst hit by the flooding with the state having 147000 people affected by the flooding.The flooding situation has severely destroyed health facilities and medical supplies, displaces populations including health workers and other essential health service providers. They have also cut off populations from accessing health services thus posing a big challenge to the vulnerable and affected populations.As the country is recovering from political unrest, another public health emergency emerged in 2020 in form of COVID 19 pandemic that has strained the health systems in the country and Rumbek Center and North Counties are also greatly affected by the COVID 19 pandemic due to restricted movements in may to august 2020, delivery of medical supplies and movement of health workers became a big challenge thus affecting health service delivery.Food insecurity and high GAM rates are also a big challenge and according to the IPC analysis, Cueibet and Rumbek North counties have been classified in Emergency IPC phase 4 which therefore calls for integrated health service delivery(IPC Report October 2020).The country faces huge gaps in the immunization services. According to National Immunization coverage Survey 2017, the penta 3 vaccination coverage is estimated at 49.3% a very low coverage compared to the immunization target of 80%. Rumbek East was which boarders Rumbek Center and Rumbek North experienced an active measles outbreak in January 2020 and this can be attributed to low immunization coverage and lack of cold chain facilities (IDSR week 2 2020).With poor Maternal indicators (MMR 786/1000 live Births UNICEF 2020), and very low number of midwifes, there is a huge need to support health facility deliveries, According to health facility functionality bulletin Rumbek Center and Rumbek North are having 27% and 14% skilled birth deliveries respectively. This is a very worrying situation as it increases the chances of maternal mortality rates.According to a IRNA report done by the ICCG in Jan 2022, 12 460 persons have been displaced in the county due to the flooding. This has exposed the displaced communities to acute health needs. The displaced communities lacked essential drugs, key personnel to support health service delivery was lacking and the health facilities were flooded exposing medical supplies to high humidity.There is therefore need to offer mobile health services to the vulnerable and affected populations in Cueibet and Rumbek North to ensure that the vulnerable and affected populations who are food insecure and have been displaced by floods and conflict access health services thus reducing the morbidity and mortality rates in the two counties. This project intends to target 20000 beneficiaries men 6000,women 10000 boys 2000 and girls 2000 where 20% of people living with disability will also be targeted.

2 - Project activities

• Health education sessions will be offered during the mobile health clinics to ensure that the communities get to know the importance of health seeking behaviors and COVID 19 Prevention messages.• Outpatient consultations in the mobile health clinics • Vaccination services will be offered to children to ensure that there is a control in the vaccine preventable infections.• ANC services will be given to the pregnant mothers• Vitamin A supplementation to the children under five years• Folic Acid supplementation for the pregnant mothers• Skilled Birth attendant Deliveries to be conducted in the mobile health units and in the community and referral of complicated cases to the health facilities.• Mental health services will be offered in the health facilities • The facilities will offer case management of rape and SGBV services to victims including referral and linkages to protection partners for support.• Screening for malnutrition and referral of the SAM cases with medical complications to OTP centers.• Disease surveillance through EWARS and IDSR reporting and 5W monthly reports• Training of health workers on IDSR, CMR, IPC and other capacity building sessions• Coordination with other partners on ground and the County Health Department as well as the health cluster.• COVID 19 awareness on prevention measures in the community and in the health facilities• EPI services in the mobile units and at the static facilities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project life cycle will utilize WAV beneficiary feedback mechanism where the project will commence with the inception where participatory assessment will be conducted in Rumbek North and Cueibet Counties to assess the current health concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups patterns and the risk factors for violence and abuse while feedback on the process will be collected from the beneficiaries.WAV will strengthen the existing community-based protection structures and women groups in Rumbek North and Cueibet counties to support activity implementation through trainings to enhance long term sustainability and to ensure that feedback from the beneficiaries is collected to inform programming and to ensure the affected population benefits. WAV will utilize its feedback and complaint mechanisms through strengthening the existing Health and GBV referral pathway mechanisms survivor centered approaches, GBV basic concepts and the key principles in dealing with the GBV survivor will be engraved within the case management interventions. Regular consultations will be provided within the facility management committees meeting monthly to discuss key interventions implemented within the period, achievements, challenges, recommendations ,gaps and opportunities within the given intervention will be discussed to ensure responses are informed by their voices, recommendations and experiences will be captured in existing beneficiary feedback tools on key programming interventions to ensure quality service provision is adapted for this project. WAV will continue to work closely with other partners to harmonize this tools and strengthen responses to ensure integrated programming. The key stakeholders will inform programming and contribute significantly to the responses and adhere to “DO NO HARM”.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Prior to this application participatory assessment through focused group discussion (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) was conducted in Rumbek North and Cueibet counties with 30 participants involved drawn from different ages ,gender and diversities to ensure there is commitment in placing accountability to people affected by disaster and conflict at the core of its emergency policy and practice, from preparedness and the onset of an emergency throughout the phases of the programme cycle. The current GBV concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups (women headed HHS ,people living with disability, elderly women, pregnant and lactating women) patterns and GBV risk factors have been identified through beneficiary feedback mechanism. WAV will continue to ensure that this project utilizes a people-centered approach, sensitive to the dignity of all human beings, the varying needs of different segments within the community, and the importance of ensuring that women, men, girls and boys can equally access and benefit from Health Services services provided.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Addressing sexual exploitation and abuse in Rumbek North and Cueibet Counties requires robust measures to promote and protect women’s and girl’s rights, response mechanisms when those rights are breached, the services and programs to meet the needs of women and girls who have suffered violence are in place. WAV will ensure the component on PSEA is part of the Meetings, awareness raising sessions and training activities to ensure the information is disseminated to the community also at the women and girls friendly spaces they will be IEC materials on PSEA translated in local language so that women and girls can be able to read and understand.WAV also has a PSEA policy and the staffs have been trained this will be utilized to manage the conduct of WAV staff when interacting with beneficiaries in the field.WAV has a reporting mechanisms where these cases are reported to the human resources for action who in turns shares the information with the executive director for disciplinary action.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WAV feedback mechanism will be used for this project to guide in service provision to the beneficiaries .A complaint mechanism desk will be set up for information collection at the women and girls friendly space where different opinions will be collected and solutions generated.
