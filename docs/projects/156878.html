Project Summary

The OHCHR proposal aims at strengthening the protection of rights under IHL/IHRL across the humanitarian response to the Syria crisis, including by contributing to the 2019 HRP Strategic Objective to ensure human rights protection is central and upfront in the response to support analysis and mitigation of protection risks, promote accountability of duty bearers, ensure a victim-centered response and enhance protection mainstreaming by all sectors. This including by ensuring that the needs of particularly vulnerable groups, such as women and girls, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities are adequately addressed.
A central element of the project is to support a consistent, effective and robust implementation of the SSG protection strategy adopted in 2017 and advise the humanitarian leadership at Syria and WOS level on advocacy firmly grounded on IHL and IHRL through direct substantive technical support in the three hubs.

Needs

Protection of the rights of individuals under IHL, IHRL and Refugee Law is at the core of the humanitarian response. Serious and systematic violations and abuses of international law are committed by all parties to the conflict. Of particular concern is the continued lack of respect by parties to the conflict for the fundamental IHL principles in the context of hostilities, which is resulting in the killing and injuring of thousands of civilians and the extensive destruction of and/or damage to civilian objects, including hospitals, schools, water and sanitation structures and markets. Since before the conflict began, discriminatory laws and practices encourage impunity for gender-based violence in syria and prevented women and girls from accessing justice. In the context of an armed conflict marked by gender-based violence, social stigma and legal obstacles serve to isolate victims and bring them further harm. Persons with disabilities are disproportionately affected during situations of conflict and emergency. Aggravation of existing impairments due to environmental change, stress and limited access to and disruption of services, in addition to the creation of new impairments from ongoing violence, all contribute to the burden of disability. Women and girls with disabilities are particularly vulnerable to experiencing violence, exploitation and abuse. Despite these risks, data on persons with disabilities in conflict situations remains limited. In the absence of more specific evidence, the global prevalence rate estimated at 15% by WHO in 2011 is frequently used in the Syrian humanitarian response. However, this figure is not designed to account for increases related to protracted conflict. Rates of disability in Syria must also be interpreted in light of the high levels of contamination with unexploded ordinance, which will contribute to the creation of new disabilities for years to come. In 2018, consultations with the humanitarian leadership (RC/HC, RHC & DRHC) and humanitarian partners, including sector/cluster coordinators and members – i.e. protection (and its sub-sectors), health, education, CCCM, Shelter/NFI, WASH and relevant protection-mandated UN agencies i.e. UNHCR, WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA, OCHA, as well as donors such as ECHO, were conducted on a regular basis directly or indirectly in the three hubs. OHCHR Human Rights Advisors were also consulted and provided advice to sector coordinators in the development of advocacy notes integrating IHL and IHRL messages. The project is in line with the HRP Protection sector objective: Sector objective 1: The protection of the population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearersSector objective 2: Strengthened capacity of humanitarian actors and duty bearers at national and community level to asses, analyze, prevent and address protection risks and needs.

Implementation of activities

The project provides for the deployment of three P4 Human rights advisors (HRA) posts in support of the three humanitarian coordinators (HCs) in the Whole-of-Syria (RC/HC Damascus/Beirut, RHC Amman and the DRHC Gaziantep). The P4 HRAs will perform advisory functions for the humanitarian leadership team and sectors/clusters at Hub and WoS level in each location, in close coordination with the OHCHR and human rights and humanitarian actors. This would enhance greater contact and sharing of information and analysis between humanitarian actors and human rights actors and as such enhance the overall quality of analysis. Specific activities include: 1. Provide technical advice on IHL and IHRL and integration of human rights in the humanitarian response to the WoS humanitarian leadership team (RC/HC, DHC, DRHC) and relevant WoS/hub-level sector/clusters, including by drawing on guidance from the international human rights system, in cooperation with the protection Sector Cluster coordinators in Syria, Cross border and at the WOS level;2. Provide human rights reporting and analysis with key humanitarian actors, including on human rights-related needs and gaps in the response, as well as recommendations to address them;3. Contribute to initiatives by humanitarian actors – particularly the protection Sector - aimed at addressing protection gaps, for example, awareness raising, Protection Risk Analysis, protection needs assessments, advocacy, policy development or engagement with UN Civilian Military Coordination mechanisms;4. Participate in Whole of Syria and related Protection sectors/clusters and contribute to implementation of the SSG Protection Strategy, Protection sector/cluster works plans;5. Organize training for relevant humanitarian partners, including the humanitarian leadership on the centrality ofprotection and IHRL and IHL;6. Contribute to OHCHR reports and coordinate inputs from humanitarian actors;7. Provide other support to humanitarians as required, including by planning, designing and contributing to country visits by special procedures of the UN human rights system with a view to enhance human rights advocacy;8. Facilitate the OHCHR led Human Rights Reference Group (HRRG) in Gaziantep as a stand-alone informal group and any other relevant national information-sharing and advocacy platforms on human rights to support Syrian civil society. Protection Risk Analysis

The main assumption is that the political and security context remains extremely violent and volatile in the coming years despite potential developments in the political process facilitated by the United Nations and the Office of the Special Envoy on Syria. In that light, OHCHR’s involvement with the humanitarian community in support of protection interventions and the promotion of IHL/IHRL will continue to be of particular relevance. In addition to the security situation, there are two main perceived risks associated with this action/project: (1) Imposed changes to the operational set up for the HRAs either due to a potential overall restructuring of the humanitarian country team and related coordination mechanisms or to the lack of access for OHCHR staff to Turkey, Jordan and Lebanon; (2) Increased exposure by humanitarian partners, in particular Syrian CSOs and individuals involved in IHL/IHRL-related activities. OHCHR's support to the HCT was carefully crafted to ensure it responds to a clear need identified by the HCT as well as by the IASC. The humanitarian coordinators were involved in drafting and approving the Terms of Reference for each HRA and work closely with them, with the exception of the HRA working with the HC due to the geographical distance (HC being based in Damascus and the HRA in Beirut). On security, the UN maintains strict regulations and procedures aimed at ensuring the safety of all staff. Should the security situation deteriorate measures such as the evacuation of international staff implementing this action/project could apply. OHCHR would continue to operate from another location/hub. There is an agreement for OHCHR to operatefrom Beirut, Turkey and Jordan for the deployment of its HRAs. The protection of humanitarian partners involved in IHL/IHRL activities led by OHCHR is essential and risk is mitigated through the delivery of neutral and impartial IHL/IHRL training and capacity building activities as well as through dialoguewith the host states.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Concerning the risks on beneficiaries of OHCHR HRA human rights capacity building activities, it is worth noting that individuals participating in previous OHCHR human rights related capacity building and monitoring activities have been occasionally subject to intimidation, increasing restrictions of movement (in the region) and sometimes arrest by Syrian or other authorities. Adequate risk mitigating measures should be in place to ensure their protection. In addition, due to unforeseen circumstances beyond OHCHR's control including security concerns may prevent participants from attending workshops. OHCHR is aware that the modalities of delivering capacity building activities may need to be adjusted in order to respond more effectively on the ground due to the volatile security situation in Syria and in the region. In this Action, OHCHR is proposing to roll out capacity building activities in the various locations, travelling experts, consultants, trainers and OHCHR staff thus limiting the travel of participants, in particular Syrians in order to mitigate these risks.
