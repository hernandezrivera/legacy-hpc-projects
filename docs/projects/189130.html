Project Summary

The NRC seeks to improve the overall protective environment in South Sudan by not only responding to people's most immediate material needs during a crisis, but also by promoting their safety and dignity, as well as preventing, reducing, or mitigating the protection risks they face. It is also to address the root causes of displacement and to contribute to the creation of conditions conducive to long-term solutions for displaced people. To achieve the project objective, NRC will focus on three key components i.e. 1) General Protection; Housing, Land and Property; 3) Gender Based Violence.
NRC proposes a community-based approach to displaced and conflict-affected populations protection, including static, mobile, and emergency responses. Protection context assessments will be done in all locations, as well as, where possible, community-based protection monitoring, in which community focal points will be trained to report on protection needs and risks. Protection incidents will be documented and analyzed in order to better understand trends and evolution in the protection context, and the findings will be disseminated to communities and relevant stakeholders.
NRC will provide at-risk populations with both awareness-raising sessions and individualized assistance in obtaining national identity documentation. Foster improved ability to exercise HLP rights for individuals and groups affected by displacement with priority given to the most vulnerable, including female and child headed households, the elderly, individuals with physical and mental disabilities, and those who are extremely poor. NRC will target conflict-affected areas and areas of potential return.
NRC will also carry out GBV prevention and risk mitigation activities in 6 counties located in hard to reach areas. Activities will include awareness raising, referrals to response services, cash assistance to GBV survivors, distribution of dignity kits and training on GBV basic concepts among others.
To achieve the main objective, the will focus on the below key activities
1) Carrying out of information and awareness raising activities; awareness will focus on GBV (GBV risks, available response services, GBV referral pathways, timely reporting), HLP (social cohesion and collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms, land rights issues) and general protection (protection risks, available services)
2) Provision of cash assistance (individual protection assistance) to persons at GBV survivors and other persons at heightened risks including referral of GBV survivors for response services
3) Training frontline workers and community focal points on basic GBV concepts, GBV guiding principles and responses services.
4) Training of state-level authorities, community leaders and representative on HLP, alternative dispute resolution
5) Facilitating peaceful coexistence and inclusive resolutions of local conflict through training and mentoring the local community in collaborative dispute resolution (CDR) mechanisms
6) Distribute dignity kits to women and adolescent girls.
7) Provision of legal aid and counselling

1 - Needs

People in South Sudan are still dealing with the multiple compounding shocks of ongoing food insecurity, macroeconomic shocks, three years of flooding, disease outbreaks, increased sub-national violence, and threats and attacks on humanitarian workers and assets, resulting in an overall protection crisis. The implementation of the 2018 Revitalized Agreement on Conflict Resolution in the Republic of South Sudan continues, albeit slowly. The appointment of governors for all ten states, as well as legislators for the national parliament, are key milestones for the transition to be completed by 2021. The critical formation and graduation of unified forces are among the remaining pre-transitional tasks. The country has embarked on a constitution-making process, and democratic elections are slated to take place in 2023. Returns from displacement sites and neighboring countries are ongoing and steadily increasing, which is likely to pose additional challenges to long-term social cohesion, such as increased competition over resources such as land. Land use is still a major concern. It is a source of income, subsistence, and survival, as well as an indicator of community, belonging, and location. Because of its cultural and historical significance, the land is inherently contested and politicized, and access to housing, land, and property is unequal, far beyond economic constraints. Gender and ethnicity are important factors in determining who has access to housing, land, and property (HLP). There is a significant gap between law and practice when it comes to women's land rights; despite legal provisions recognizing equal rights for women, widespread knowledge and application of these laws remains limited. The inaccessibility of basic identity documentation by displaced populations due to fees that vulnerable groups could not afford, as well as the fact that the institutions that provide it are all located in urban areas. This makes registering land parcels difficult for returnees and extremely Host communities. Loss of assets as a result of conflict or flooding, combined with economic crises (and price inflation for staples), forces households to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Community social and economic breakdown and displacement can also lead to increased intra- and inter-community tensions, restrictions on freedom of movement, and secondary displacement. Ethnic segregation remains a concern, particularly in relation to the pressure on IDPs to return to specific locations, actions that could ethnically re-engineer the country with unsettling potential protection consequences. The struggle for access to and control of land has shaped the nation's history and will have an impact on the implementation of long-term solutions. Respondents reported that food insecurity, a lack of WASH services, and a lack of livelihood opportunities were all caused by protection risks, exposing all segments of the population. Men and boys face forced recruitment, restrictions on their freedom of movement, ill-treatment, and murder. Because of the significant cultural power imbalance between men and women, women and girls experience widespread GBV at a higher rate. Women and girls are among the most adversely affected in the South Sudan given the high rates of GBV and harmful traditional practices that are discriminatory and harmful to women and girls. The limited availability and scope of GBV and protection services in remote and hard to reach remains a major hindrance to meaningful and equitable access to protection (GBV, HLP, legal, general protection) services for displaced, returnees and other affected populations. While efforts are in place to prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV, women and girls in hard-to-reach areas remain at higher risk of GBV due to prolonged conflicts, natural calamities, harmful traditional practices that discriminate against women and girls, family separation among others.

2 - Project activities

Under General Protection: NRC will collect, verify, and analyze protection information in order to identify protection risks faced by IDPs, returnees, and other affected populations. The NRC will conduct regular protection monitoring in the targeted areas and share reports with internal and external stakeholders. Individual protection assistance (IPA) will be provided to people in need of immediate protection assistance. IPA interventions will take a client-centered approach to empower the client – and, where appropriate, the caregiver. The NRC will work with community based committees and ensure that the committee membership takes into account age, gender, and diversity considerations, as well as the inclusion of marginalized groups. The Protection Committees will be able to identify and analyze protection risks in their own communities, develop their own action plan for dealing with negative coping mechanisms, and promote positive community-wide coping mechanisms. The NRC will educate and train community focal points on data collection tools, minimum data protection standards, and referral systems. To address beneficiaries’ information needs, NRC will carry out information dissemination activities. Community focal points and community-based protection mechanisms will support in implementing information dissemination activities. NRC will introduce Center-based social activities targeting PWDs, elders, and their caregivers. The community center will provide an opportunity to share their experiences, learn from one another, and build social networks to promote emotional healing and social acceptance from the family to the community. Under HLP: NRC will conduct gender-sensitive awareness raising on social cohesion and collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms, land rights issues, and locally available services for beneficiaries regarding specific entitlements, means of claiming, legal or administrative requirements, and locally available services for each respective target county (including referrals to other partners). NRC will also keep tailoring awareness-raising sessions on CDR and social cohesion and provide Legal Counselling to beneficiaries identified during HLP and CDR and Social Cohesion awareness sessions. Legal Assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable individuals or groups who need further legal support on Housing Land and Property matters through a range of modalities. NRC will build on the existing capacity of community structures through training community leaders and local authorities to improve understanding of HLP, the Legal Frameworks and practices in South Sudan. To address the root causes of community disputes, NRC will build on the existing capacity of community structures. Under GBV, GBV prevention and mitigation activities will be implemented in 6 Counties; the following GBV activities will be implemented a) women, men, girls and boys will be referred for GBV response services; b) dignity kits with essential hygiene items and additional items for COVID-19 tailored to the local needs women and adolescent girls (disaggregated by age) will distributed as part of addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation measure; c) women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness-raising activities; d) provision of cash assistance (individual protection assistance) to GBV survivors.

The tool will be shared during quarterly reviews of the project. d) A Detailed Activity Implementation Plan will outline key activities and tasks to be undertaken to achieve the planned results. This tool will track activity implementation to ensure planned activities are implemented on schedule and where appropriate adjustments are made.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NRC has quality benchmarks in place regarding accountability standards. Engagement with local communities is crucial. NRC ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle and that consultation is ongoing. Representatives from the local population will be engaged as part of the CRM to provide feedback or request clarification on the project from NRC staff and will be fully engaged throughout the project activities Monitoring visits will also address accountability questions to establish the level of knowledge of the beneficiaries of the selection criteria, their entitlements, Code of Conduct. NRC is committed to having a culture of evidence-based learning and accountability.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

South Sudan is above all in a protection crisis and NRC is aware that the complexprotection context in South Sudan has the potential to both negatively impact or benegatively impacted by the intervention. All staff associated with the project will participate in a DoNo Harm training with particular emphasis on gender-related protection concerns andconflict sensitivity. Gender-informed conflict sensitivity assessments will be conductedregularly for all activities in all locations, with activities adjusted accordingly. Some ofthe key protection and conflict risks associated with the proposed activity designinclude housing land and property (HLP) disputes and conflicts linked to wider landaccess and use issues. Returns from displacement sites and neighboring countries willlikely lead to HLP disputes, as the land and properties of many returnees have beendamaged or occupied, with women and girls experiencing heightened risks due tosocial and cultural norms. As part of the mitigation measures, NRC is proposingactivities that aim to remove barriers to HLP that impact the ability of vulnerablegroups and raise awareness on land rights. As designed, the proposed project hasseveral activities explicitly intended to mitigate risks of main activities; social cohesionactivities, such as inter-community dialogues and formation of youth peacecommittees. Likewise, collaborative dispute resolution training for local leaders willwork to counter conflict cycles and maximization of mutual benefit. Put simply,various activities will not be implemented discretely, but in such a way that they aremore than the sum of their parts. Due to the prevalence of GBV and disempowermentof women at the household level throughout South Sudan, as well as the well-documented risks associated with livelihoods activities targeted at women in a rangeof settings, NRC is particularly aware of the potential for exacerbating domestic andintimate partner violence. Teams will be trained in gender-sensitive implementationand how to identify and approach potential survivors of GBV. In all project locations,NRC will conduct qualitative service maps of services available to survivors andconduct a workshop of local actors to facilitate a functioning referral pathway.Dedicated teams will further work with communities to prevent and mitigate GBV: first,training local leadership on prevention of GBV and especially the prevention of sexualexploitation and abuse; and second, conducting targeted community dialogues withmen, women, girls, and boys to challenge gender norms and mitigate harmfulpractices. Both community-level activities will be undertaken with the intention topromote equal, safe participation of men and women of all ages in the project, as wellas to further prevent GBV and support the creation of greater gender parity andempowerment. Risks of SEA are also present within any provision of humanitarianservices. Project staff will therefore be trained on PSEA including refresher trainingfor the number of staff members who have already participated in PSEA awarenessand training. Awareness-raising on SEA and accountability of humanitarianorganizations will also be conducted in targeted communities to mitigate this. A safeFeedback Complaints and Reporting Mechanism will be established within targetcommunities. Beyond this, NRC will conduct regular activity and location-specificprotection risk assessments considering the specific risks associated with eachactivity and coordinating mitigation efforts. It is also acknowledged that the currentCOVID-19 pandemic environment poses a risk to individuals' physical and emotionalsafety and access to project activities. COVID-19 prevention measures will be adheredto within project activities.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

NRC works to protect women, girls, and survivors of GBV from harm, to support them in their healing and recovery from GBV incident(s), and to help them thrive after the emergency. To achieve this goal, NRC will advocate for women's, girls', and survivors' voices to be heard, empower them to lead their own recovery, and ensure they are equal participants in decisions and actions affecting their lives. NRC will not provide GBV case management; however, it will be an active agent in GBV prevention, referring GBV-related cases to the attention and services of partners who provide psychosocial and case management services. The referral will include timely access to health care for GBV victims. NRC will continue to participate in joint/multi-sectoral (IRNA) assessments in various locations affected by floods with reports shared with respective clusters for response planning. The NRC emergency mobile team will also continue monitoring the situation and displacement country-wide through clusters, partners, ERRM consortium (NRC, Medair & DRC), REACH, SFP, partners on the ground, etc. NRC mobile team then responds for WASH, EiE, SNFI, and FSL based on the displacement trigger through conducting a rapid assessment followed by a response based on the assessment findings. Another immediate support provided by NRC has been through multi-purpose cash transfer to cover basic household gaps to among others flood-affected communities. In terms of medium to long-term flood mitigation interventions, NRC programs work closely with Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR) mechanisms to map out communal vulnerabilities such as floods, identify areas or locations that are at most at risk come up with local response and mitigation measures. These measures include using CFW/FFA approaches to construct flood control dykes, tree planting, shallow wells, and road dyke construction among others. The community is also trained on various aspects related to floods such as causes, community DRR plans, response, and mitigation measures. In addition, NRC builds capacity and promotes climate adaption practices such as climate-smart agriculture, such as agroforestry, tree planting, minimum tillage, zero tillage, mulching among others all geared to mitigating floods.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Managing flood risks including flood related displacements may entail altering the likelihood of flooding affecting a community and/or mitigating the effects of flooding by reducing the community's vulnerability and exposure to flooding. Effective flood risk reduction methods are highly location-specific. There is no one-size-fits-all solution, and a combination of measures is usually required to reduce risk. As a result, the NRC will work with various stakeholders in the targeted areas to implement a variety of measures to reduce flood-related risks to people, property, and infrastructure. NRC believes that flood risk management decisions should be made in consultation with flood-affected communities. As a result, periodic consultations and discussions with various segments of the community on how to prevent and mitigate flood-related displacement will be organized. In collaboration with various sectors and partners in the targeted locations, NRC will ensure that the targeted people are properly consulted and will play a role in developing mitigation strategies ahead of the impacts.

NRC is a national cluster leader and a key partner in the field. NRC will continue to collaborate closely with other humanitarian actors, such as civil society organizations (CSOs), available development actors, and relevant government ministries, departments, and agencies, both at the cluster and field levels. Using the Protection Cluster coordination and performance, NRC works to improve the effectiveness of humanitarian response with the goal of improving community protection, including through improved individual and community capacities to identify, mitigate, and address urgent community needs. NRC will take advantage of this opportunity to assist communities in mitigating, adapting to, and recovering from disasters in order to restore and build community resilience.
