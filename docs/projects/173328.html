Project Summary

On a project that directly benefits 800 female headed families (IDP's) (age 25+), 300 girls(age 15-24) 50 boys (age 18-25), 100 returnees and 50 disabled persons in 20 host communities within Bui, Donga Mantung in the North West region, Manyu and Meme in the southwest and Noun in the West region, H4BF rehearse dignity of vulnerable IDP’s. H4BF will improve access to Quality GBV services and strengthen their resilience, H4BF shall provide standardized dignity kits, access to quality PSS to women, girls, boys, men of reproductive age as well as strengthening their resilience through livelihood support activities.
H4BF proposes “Access to specialized MHPSS case management and reintegration of IDPs, survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA in Mezam, Bui and Donga Mantung in the North west, Limbe and Buea in the South and Mifi in the west region,” in alignment with the Cameroon HRP priorities for the country. Over 12 months, Access will provide quality MHPSS and SRHR services 9000 (6000 children and 3000 women and girls) and will enhance the reintegration of IDPs into their communities, including people affected by armed groups and survivors abductions, GBV and SEA.
H4BF will improve the mental and physical health of IDPs. Utilizing a community-based approach, H4BF will provide quality emergency MHPSS services and specialized case management to IDPs through trained community outreach workers (CWs) and psychologists. Access’ CWs will provide psychoeducation awareness sessions to IDPs and will teach coping skills to community members through a gender-sensitive, peer-support group model. For individuals presenting complex mental health needs, Access’ roving psychologists will provide individuals with specialized and gender-sensitive mental health care.
Access will also support IDPs to access SRHR services through mapping existing SRHR and HIV/AIDS services that are suitable to the needs of IDPs women and girls affected by GBV and armed groups’ violence. Roving nurses will provide SRH services to IDPs including accurate SRHR information and individual counseling, prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV/AIDS, and accompaniment to relevant services for emergency cases. H4BF will support people impacted by armed groups to have access to income generating activities and supplies through vocational training targeting female heads of households and provide micro-loans to individuals to leverage the received training. Livelihood activities will be culturally and geographically appropriate.
We envision each location within project target area with women in a safe haven for women and girls to receive desperately needed services strengthen community systems, and access information about their rights. Each week between 100-300 women, attend programming at each center, run by 2-3 staff members and supported by locally recruited community focal points. Each WGFS offers a core set of services complemented by additional activities based on requests from the community.
After the implementation of the above-mentioned intervention, about 80,000 people will be reached children (27,000 girls; 35,000 boys) and (900 men; 9000 women) will be reached by 2021.
This is a community directed project and all stakeholders in the community will be involved throughout the program cycle from planning, to implementation and evaluation. At H4BF gender-based issues are considered in the design of policy, strategy, plan or program and gender equality is promoted in its intervention. GBV caseworkers, supervisors and interpreters whom will be 80% female will be on GBV Case Management based on the Case Management Guidelines. The project build confidence, ensure proper confidentiality and safety in all the steps involve, H4BF will set-up/accommodate a safe and confidential space for service provision that women and girls can access without stigma or fear of being identified.
