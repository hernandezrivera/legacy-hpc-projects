Project Summary

The last 3 years of humanitarian response plan has seen needs increasing in all the ten states of South Sudan. A number of factors have led to this high hunger levels where over 70% of vulnerable households can not even boast of having a meal per day. There are pockets of localized insecurity from armed groups that continue to cause anxiety and displacement of the vulnerable persons. According the latest IPC data 2020/2021; effects of covis-19; the economic crisis in the country, serious impact of flooding of livelihoods have led to a collapse of the social safety nets of the South Sudan population. The communities are in dire need of humanitarian assistance but access challenges remain a big menace in many parts of the nation. Aweil North where HACT has static operation in multi-sectoral programs has seen the county grapple with increasing number of child headed women, single mothers, pregnant women, children suffer severe acute malnutrition due to lack of sufficient nutrition, intensified conflicts, unprecedented flooding and high food prices which the very poor households can not afford (HACT IRNA January 2020).
HACT joins the 2021 HRP in the strategic orientation of FSL cluster for scaled up response to multi-faceted needs in Aweil North by enhancing and sustaining emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; undertake capacity building trainings in entrepreneurial livelihood expansion and growth; targeting 15000 vulnerable lactating women, single mothers; children; youth, elderly, and PWDS to urgently involve in quick impact and resilient agronomic, livestock production, fishing and DRR activities.

1 - Needs

The IPC analysis projects that by mid-2021, an estimated 7.24 million people or 60 per cent of the SouthSudanese population will face severe acute food insecurity and will need urgent assistance. Many of thefamilies have exhausted their emergency coping strategies. Around 1.4 million children under age 5 areexpected to be acutely malnourished in 2021, which is the highest number in three years. The deterioration of the food security situation in the country is alarming; compounded other multiple vulnerabilities such as climatic shocks (floods and droughts), disrupted livelihoods and loss of productive assets;low agricultural production, high food prices exacerbated by depreciation of south sudanese pounds against foreign currency significantly affecting poor households and effects of Covid-19 pandemic which have made social safety nets to be over stretched. According to IRNA in January by HACT in Aweil North; over 80% of the households surveyed hardly can have one meal for the family per day; already there are signs of children and lactating mothers being malnourished and if this situation is not addressed there could be rising cases of morbidity and mortality amongst these most vulnerable households especially lactating women, under 5 year children, elderly and PWDs. Access in Aweil North has greatly improved; areas flooded are now dry. Farmers reported having lost alot of livestock and major crops such as 'dura'-sorghum and groundnuts were all destroyed. The KII we had with local authorities and the beneficiary population in Aweil North payams such as...... put the current copying mechanisms of the HH mainly in gathering wild fruits, a few households are fishing in the ponds and selling them as main source of livelihood.Given the needs, HACT proposes to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support and build the capacities of the communities in line with FSL strategic objectives and priorities targeting 15,000 most vulnerable households in integrated FSL intervention with cross cutting multi-sectoral linkages in WaSH, Nutrition and health.

Training of animal health workers and disease survelliance- Under SAD assessment & post distribution monitoring- Accountability with information provision, participation and complaint & feedback mechanisms ensuring inclusion of vulnerable target groups and marginalized groups- SAD capacity building, skills & knowledge development and community engagement

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HACT has ascribed to PSEA policy for its entire management structure (board, senior management, and operations staff). Together the staff and volunteers are encouraged to advocate for child rights, women, girls and other vulnerable groups for safety in access to FSL services especially during targeted distribution of farm implements, tools and seeds. We encourage local leadership in our locations to champion exemplary protection activities. While conducting our work, incase a beneficiary reports or our staff notice a violation of the rights of any of our beneficiaries; we support the survivors with referral information either to protection specialist within our inter-cluster coordination mechanism or refer the case for legal redress based on the survivors level of vulnerability while upholding confidentiality of information

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Already HACT has enormous hands on experience in relation to complaints and feedback mechanisms in post conflict and humanitarian crisis contexts not limited word of mouth- person to person who we always refers to as community gatekeepers for service delivery, coordination meeting also provide us with useful gaps sharing and our meeting chairs ensure all constituencies voice their concerns during the review meetings. These help the illiterate to be onboard

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HACT an active humanitarian member in RSS has in place AAP structure to ensure the participating community is fully involved in our FSL programming. We usually engage the beneficiaries before project design with sharing gaps in needs assessment and prioritization by themselves, build on already local copying mechanism they have in addressing food insecurity challenges and use this information throughout the project life cycle. Farmer leads (comprising youth, women, elderly, PWDS representatives) together with County agric staff have supported information sharing through meetings, workshops, assessments, monitoring visits and direct implementation of the project. Locally available information platforms we use include person to person contact (most effective amongst our farming groups); phone call for some few especially local leadership and help desks during communal project activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Our common forms of feedback from the project beneificiaries have professionaly on may occassions come to the project team notice through M/E repots, interactions with beneficiaries at service points. Each time these information come to our notice, we align it with our Risk management framework { Asess; measure, mitigate and monitor}; project senior management use this to devise solutions based on our risk matrix and engage priority stakeholders to improve the project outcome. For instance; risks related to poor targeting and missing representation of most vulnerable households in any round of FSL NFI distribution; the post distribution monitoring exercise helps us to fix the risk and target the beneficiaries in the next round of distributions.
