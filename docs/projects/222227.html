Project Summary

The project will be aligned to Health Cluster objectives of providing quality health care to crisis-affected people, preventing/responding to health threats, strengthening the health system. It will realize an effective coordination and integration between development and humanitarian action by complementing the ongoing program in support to the local health system when this latter will result not sufficient to respond to people needs, addressing the most neglected groups in the most remote sites, in particular in case of emergencies increasing the demand for assistance and disrupting life-saving services. This will be done by proposing a range of different services provision modalities (static and mobile), expected to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current health care coverage at all level of the health system and/or resulting from emergency events, in particular for those people whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging. Project impact will be maximized through a multisectoral approach, with the result of services being highly comprehensive, as addressing health conditions and the inter- related complications (ie malnutrition), and offering quality care, being informed by hygiene promotion and IPC principles, becoming the first setting for an effective prevention of the main infectious diseases. At this regard, the project will guarantee timely and effective prevention, containment and management of outbreaks. Surveillance and early warning will be strenghten, with the engagement and coordination of various stakeholders.
CUAMM will promote existing SMOH/CHD led coordination mechanisms (County Monthly Review Meetings, State Quarterly Review Meeting, State Health/Nutrition Cluster) strengthening and expansion, as privileged platforms for multisector dialogue, with SMOH/CHD and its partners interacting with actors pertaining to other clusters and bringing in their resources and expertise for effective addressing health problems rooted causes
In general, project design and implementation modalities will promote the proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities and this will facilitate the selection and implementation of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable intervention options, focusing on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better and more sustainable outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Local actors’ technical and operational support will be one of the core strategies informing the proposed action, contributing to its effectiveness and to the quality of supported services and activities. Amongst these actors also local NGOs/associations, to be mapped, appraised and the accompanied in taking over the implementation of an increasing quote of field based activities, facilitating the realization of the recommended integration with other health determining sectors.
The project is expected to reach 76000 people, out of whom 20% will be U5 children and 50% female, with at least 1% of patients accessing outpatients services being people with disability (760)

1. Needs

LAKES STATE is a priority humanitarian setting. The intersectoral prioritization process placed 7/8 counties in position 2 or 3 and the same will be in IPC severity level 4 in mid-2025. People in need are 614.203. WHO 2024 analysis of Health Sector Response to Floods shows that Lakes is and it is bordering with areas prone to flooding and this is persisting since quite long time (in 2021, 4/8 Counties were targeted by SSHF interventions). This deprives families from subsistence resources, hampers provision of and access to quality basic services and lead to internal migration. As per IOM Mobility Tracker (09/24), Lakes is hosting 145.760 IDPs. They are spread within local communities, competing with them for the already scarce resources, limited because of raids/lootings and environmental factors. To consider that, due to past/current fighting, the majority of households are headed by women, more exposed to violence and likely to be food insecure. Poor nutrition intake and WASH conditions are key determinants of most vulnerable high morbidity and mortality.The main needs are:1_People do not have regular access to quality health care, as confirmed by Lakes low health indicators: institutional delivery 21%, curative consultation rate below 1 (NUMERATOR: DHIS2 2023; DENOMINATOR:National Bureau Statistic). PHC facilities are inadequate in number, distribution, conditions to provide preventive/curative services. In 2022, the 27% have been left out of any funding mechanism, in 2024, those remaining saw their resources dropping by 80% and drugs consignments becoming smaller and sporadic. The most remote areas, where IDPs settle, do not count any functioning HFs and patients travel long distances to get the due assistance, which is not even reachable during rainy season. The State referral system is penalized by the lack of funds. BHWs’ network is limited as well, not sufficient to cover the entire territory, with security/environmental factors keeping volunteers close to town. The functioning HFs, besides not being sufficient to respond to the increasing demand, are often working discontinuously and under standards, hosted in poor buildings, missing key equipment, quickly running out of essential supplies and managed by unskilled personnel. These conditions challenge the quality and the comprehensiveness of the provided services, rarely addressing disability, mental health, GBV, adolescents health. 2_Poor access to and utilization of health services, coupled with the peculiar environment, makes the State highly prone to outbreaks (cholera in the past, measles ongoing all along 2023). Preventive measures are not consistent. EPI coverage is insufficient (60% Penta3, 37% Measles): some antigens are provided in too small quantities, not sustaining daily activities nor allowing prepositioning, HFs based vaccinators are reluctant to conduct outreaches because of lack of reliable transport means and motivating perdiem (issue exacerbated by the current funds reduction). Mass vaccination campaigns have disrupted routine vaccination, creating the precedent of unsustainable costs, forcing health authorities and staff to keep revising the daily immunization calendar and confusing the population, seen vaccination as an extraordinary event rather than a routine one. The limited HFs/BHWs spread over the territory limited the health education coverage, with perpetuation of poor health seeking behaviours. 3_CHDs lead supervision/coordination mechanism are in place, but still weak (and further challenged by the reduction of funds) for local health system being responsive and resilient to crisis. Sectors integration is sporadic, with consequent limitation to local authorities’ capacities of addressing the above mentioned problems. Similarly, emergency response is consisting in vertical/isolated one shot interventions, focused on the contingent issue, instead of more concerted efforts, with longer term perspective of reconstruction and preparedness

2. Project activities

Activities align to HC 2025 key priorities and contribute to achieve sectorial objectives and respond to identified needs. Targeted PHC Facilities/villages will be selected with the HC, after needs assessment and discussion with beneficiaries. Priority will be where existing services are not adequate, as neglected by funding mechanisms, overloaded because of IDPs/outbreak/flooding/violence, damaged by adverse events. Because of the peculiar status of health care providers, direct service delivery will be preferred.SO1: improved equitable access to essential quality and comprehensive life-saving services. The BPHNS will be provided to different extent at different levels (Continuum of Care) through different delivery modalities, bringing them closer to people, entry point for PWD, people with mental disorders and GBV survivors, who will be offered generic assistance and tailored care to their specific needs, eventually referred as per locally defined pathway. ACTIVITIES: 1.1Targeted PHC HFs will be enabled to provide the due services by addressing the gaps hindering their attendance capacities. Health staff will be hired/trained/supervised, essential drugs/supplies procured/distributed, complementing what provided in kind by other actors, old/looted equipment replaced, damaged infrastructures repaired. 1.2Free of charge ambulance service will be sustained for prompt attendance to emergencies, contributing to vehicles costs and staff reward/training. 1.3BHWs network will be expanded and sustained for ICCM/health education, with the engagement/training/equipment/supervision of volunteers, whose distribution will be tailored to that of targeted communities. 1.4Where lacking HFs or where existing HFs will not be functioning or sufficiently responsive, CHDs will be supported to deploy mobile clinical teams; dedicated staff will be hired/trained and provided with supplies, equipment, transport/communication means. The services to offer will be defined under HC guidance.SO2: reduced excess morbidity and mortality through timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases. ACTIVITIES: 2.1Static/outreach routine immunization will be guarantee at all targeted PHC HFs and included in the mobile clinics package, by hiring vaccinators, advocating UNICEF/WHO for regular antigens provision and cold chain maintenance, running different transport means to bring immunization to remote site. 2.2CHDs will be sustained for surveillance (including IDSR) with distribution of GL/tools, surveillance officers training/mentoring, transport and communication means. 2.3In case of outbreak, responsive/mop up campaigns will be organized for containment/prevention, while RRT will be activated for case tracking and first line management by engaging/training dedicated staff and providing due supplies. SO3: enhanced resilience and humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms . CUAMM will integrate the emergency intervention to the ongoing development programs (HSTP and other), without duplication, to promote synergies and optimise available resources. ACTIVITY: 3.1Reinforcement of multidisciplinary supervision/coordination mechanisms for stakeholders’ concerted/multisector actions under CHD/SMOH stewardship. Health actors will interact with those of other clusters for integrated activities contributing to SOs achievement (SO1_ Malnutrition prevention and treatment, Protection for GBV comprehensive attendance; SO2_WASH for IPC). CUAMM projects are designed and implemented with SMOH/CHD to ensure intervention endorsement and local actors’ involvement, with constant communication flow and open discussion. Implemented actions quality will be guarantee with technical/operational support to SMOH/CHD, while services responsiveness/acceptability will be promoted engaging beneficiaries in the planning and monitoring (community feedback mechanism) phases and local NGOs/associations during implementation
