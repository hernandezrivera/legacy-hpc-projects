Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development intends to implement a project to address the urgent nutrition needs in Banadir, Galgadud, Hiran, Mudug and Sool regions of Somalia. Since the collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991, repeated conflict in conjunction with poor food security, limited healthcare and nutrition service availability and has resulted in Somalia having among the worst health-related indicators in the world.
According to the FSNAU-FEWSNET-2021- Post Gu assessments findings, nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis or worse (IPC phase 3 or higher) outcomes from now and through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, the latest HNO 2022 report predicts approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years, which is total acute malnutrition burden in whole of Somalia, who will likely be facing acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022 if urgent treatment and emergency nutrition support/assistance are not provided. The increase in the trajectory in the number of children in need nutrition assistance in 2022 could be attributed to lose of households purchasing power, high fertility rate, recurrent perennial environmental shocks, conflicts, economic deterioration compounded with the COVID-19 pandemic. The report paints gloomy picture for the rural communities in Somalia who tend to face the highest levels of deprivation of access to services, including preventive and curative nutritional services.
Mercy-USA will provide treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) through OTP (Outpatient Therapeutic) and SFP (Supplementary Feeding) services in line with the Basic Nutrition Service Package (BNSP) in 26 fixed facilities and 8 mobile outreaches that will provide nutrition assorted services. Mercy-USA will refer complicated cases of SAM to the Stabilization Centers for treatment. To equip the capacity of the workforce, Mercy-USA will train and support nutrition workers to identify, provide lifesaving treatment and follow-up on cases of malnutrition. Mercy-USA will also integrate preventative services including providing IYCF counseling and micronutrients supplementation at MCH centers. The social mobilization at community level will be done to promote healthy nutritional practices. Mother-led MUAC approach will be used for referrals from households. Due to prevailing serious preventable-vaccine-preventable diseases are still prevalent in Somalia. Mercy-USA will thus support the provision of routine EPI activities. Mercy-USA in partnership with BRA/MoH will facilitate outreach nutrition and EPI teams to serve the people who are far from the facilities.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by people with disabilities in accessing nutrition services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation. Access to children with disabilities and their caretakers and/or disabled pregnant and lactating women will be ensured and increased through outreach activities and behavioral change communication campaigns.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

1. - Projects activities

• By 2022, the project will ensure to screen 177,309 (boys; 80,142, girls; 97,167) of all children aged under 5 at both facilities and community levels who will undertake an anthropometric measurement and MUAC, and those who will be acutely malnourished will be admitted into OTP & SC (SAM) and TSFP (MAM) programs for 2-4 months.• By 2022, a total of 49,646 (boys; 19,821, girls; 29,825) children aged 6-59 months who are acutely malnourished will be admitted and treated for SAM (Severe acute malnutrition) at the OTP (outpatient therapeutic program) and SC (Stabilization Centers) after all children whose anthropometric measurement is Z-score of less than 3, MUAC less than 11.5cm. Children who are identified as having severe acute malnutrition should first be assessed with a full clinical examination to confirm whether they have medical complications and whether they have an appetite. Children who have an appetite (pass the appetite test) and are clinically well and alert should be treated as outpatients. Children who have medical complications, severe oedema, or poor appetite (fail the appetite test) or present with one or more IMCI danger signs should be treated as inpatients and will be referred to the stabilization centers.• By 2022, a total of 124,291 children aged under five (boys; 51,811, girls; 72,480) with moderate acute malnutrition will be enrolled into the TSFP program; also those cured from the OTP program. The project will follow strictly the WHO guideline in admission and treatment of children aged 6–59 months which defines moderate acute malnutrition as moderate wasting (i.e. weight-for-height between –3 and –2 Z-scores of the WHO Child Growth Standards median) and/or mid-upper-arm circumference (MUAC) greater or equal to 115 mm and less than 125 mm.Support the provision of moderate acute malnutrition rehabilitation at TSFP sites. • By 2022, a total of 30984 pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) will be reached at both the facility and community levels and these PLWs will undertake an anthropometric measurement and MUAC and those who will be acutely malnourished will be admitted into the TSFP program for 2-4 months. All pregnant and lactating women reached will receive IYCF/IYCF-E messages through IYCF counseling sessions.• The project will support 1,200 mothers to be trained on the proper use of Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and screening of children for malnutrition at the household level before referring to the facility for follow-up and management.• Deworming of all children aged between 12 to 59 months old who visit the facilities or are reached through outreach mobile services.• All Children 6-23 months will receive Micronutrient Powders (MNPs) as per Somalia new IMAM guideline.• Give pregnant and lactating mothers (PLWs) multiple micronutrient tablets.• All pregnant women will be de-wormed during 2nd and 3rd trimesters.• The project will support two training on SFP/OTP trainings for 6-days for 104 nutrition care staff including OTP nurses, facility in charges and nutrition workers to provide appropriate nutrition rehabilitation at TSFP sites.• Also, the project will support two trainings on new IMAM guidelines for 4 days for 58 nutrition care staff to provide quality Therapeutic care at the facilities and community levels.• Provision of uninterrupted therapeutic and supplementary food rations at the nutrition sites.• Regular joint supportive supervision with Banadir Regional Administration (BRA) or Ministry of Health (MoH).• Conduct monthly and quarterly supportive supervision by both field and country office team.
