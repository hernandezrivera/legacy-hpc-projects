Project Summary

Sudan has more than 1 million refugees with the recent experiences of floods and refugee influx from Ethiopia due to the conflicts in Tigrey Region. In any emergency and humanitarian situation, the risks of maternal death and increased risk of gender based violence are already well documented by GBV expert (IASC GBV in Emergencies). The project will contribute in three strategic goals, Life-saving (78%), protection (20%) and Living conditions (2%), of the country Refugee Response Plan in Sudan. The specific objective of this project is to address the critical life-saving and essential reproductive health and Gender Based Violence (GBV) services to prevent maternal death, GBV, and enabling conducive environment for women and girls accessing information and services. The project targets refugees, asylum seekers and host communities in the prioritized localities, identified by RCF for 2021 Humanitarian Response for refugees in 11 States (37 localities). The key interventions are integrated SRHR -GBV service delivery (Health, especially SRHR, Clinical Management of Rape, MHPSS and GBV Case Management and referrals), strengthening women center activities, community based protection networks, women's groups, SRHR supplies and commodities, Dignity Kits, Capacity building of Key health psycho social service providers, mobile/temporary clinics, Community Awareness and campaigns, strengthening referral mechanism with government service delivery points for GBV-SRHR. The project budget has dedicated 60% for SRHR, including the CMR; and 40% for GBV prevention and response; from which 20% of the services will be directed to the vulnerable host communities, adjacent to the refugee sites to mitigate conflicts over competing resources. The implementation of the activities will be conducted through (I)NGOs and Government partners with the consistent technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts more than 1 million refugees and asylum seekers in Sudan, who are in dire need for humanitarian assistance as document in the humanitarian needs overview 2021. Among these vast numbers of refugees and asylum seekers, 35% of the population are children under 18 years, 28% are the women of reproductive age. Huge numbers of households in the refugee communities are female-headed (52%) with 1% child-headed households. The participatory assessment report “Being a Refugee in Sudan” (UNHCR, 2019) had also explored several challenges for refugee people across Sudan, especially GBV experiences and the negative coping mechanisms for women and children, such as forced and early marriage, transactional sex and prostitution, schools drop out, risky forms of child labors and begging, etc. Moreover, GBV subsectors indicated that GBV services are absent in 90% of localities in Sudan. This situation has potential negative impact with the recent refugee influx from Ethiopia who are now in East Sudan. UNFPA’s assessment (Sitrep#2) in the East Sudan has identified needs for the critical and life-saving SRHR and GBV services, which are very weak at this moment. TO enable refugees and asylum seekers having a dignified life, it is essential to ensure pregnant mothers, GBV survivors and women and girls have access to information and services. UNFPA has targeted 37 high & Medium priority localities in 11 states, which are the priority areas for 2021 humanitarian response plan (RCF, 2021). The SRH-GBV programmes for refugees estimated more than 39,000 pregnant women who will be in need timely access to lifesaving and essential SRH services, receive SRHR consultations (ANC, PNC, assisted delivery, including SRH supplies). Around 5500 pregnant women are estimated to experience birth related complications including need for caesarean section, and post abortion care. Moreover, it is estimated that more than 28000 might require access to family planning services, and treatment of sexually transmitted diseases. Health system in Sudan is very fragile, and coverage with lifesaving services for SRH is very low around 30%. Moreover, there are critical gaps in providing GBV case management services, which are essential to support women with long term psychosocial and other referral services, and supporting the survivors to re-integrate into the normal lives. This intervention if implemented should address pregnancy related complications including caesarean section and normal deliveries, prevention and treatment of STIs for both women and men, preventing neonatal complications, provision of family planning services, post abortion care, referral services. The project will serve more than +100000 medical consultations to refugee women, girls, boys and men, including the persons with disabilities, with the primary focus of SRH consultation & referral. This project estimated to support +4500 sexual violence cases with CMR services. Beside medical response to GBV, this project will support +4000 GBV case management and specialized MHPSS services to survivors and at risk women and girls through trained case workers. The project will also enable the integration of GBV component into the temporary and mobile clinics, and SRHR integration into the women centers to create multiple entry points for service delivery for women and girls. The key interventions are Mobile Clinic, Women Centers, Capacity Building, strengthen referral mechanism, campaign and awareness, Dignity kits and RH kits, including CMR kits. This project will also support 20% of the adjacent host communities with the refugee sites to prevent community conflicts and contribute towards social cohesion.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Output 1. Increased access to and utilization of quality GBV services and improved conducive community structure to prevent GBV in the targeted localities for refugee women and girls, especially GBV survivors. Activity 1.1. Establishment and operation of women centers to enable women with increased access to information and services .Activity 1.2: Vocational skills training for women and supporting with start-up capital for the most vulnerable women.Activity 1.3: Supporting women and girls with dignity kits, Psychosocial support and GBV Case Management Services, including Referral services cost.Activity 1.4. Training of GBV service providers and social workers, including the refresher training. Activity 1.5. Capacity Building of targeted community based protection network and women's groups, and direct Support to facilitate their work. Activity 1.6. Community sensitization session, public Awareness raising campaign- national and state level, develop IEC materials (flip chart development, including consultants cost to contextualize and translate materials and printing) Output 2. Improved access to, and utilization of lifesaving and essential sexual and reproductive health services.Activity 2.1. Timely deployment of temporary and/or mobile clinic services, referral services and health care providers to areas with limited access to lifesaving and essential SRH services. Activity 2.2 Strengthening the capacity of health care providers on provision of essential and lifesaving SRH services, such as EmONC, CMR, Post abortion care, family planning, ANC/PNC, etc.Activity 2.3. Improve the availability of essential and lifesaving SRH supplies including interagency reproductive health kits, personal protective equipment and infection prevention supplies, family planning, hospital equipment and supplies. Activity 2.4 Strengthen existing health facilities and improve their capacities through rehabilitation and refurbishments, hospital equipment and referral services for refugees.Activity 2.5 Engagement of target beneficiaries in demand creation for SRHR services as well as other emerging health issues such as COVID19, and ensuring their active role in establishing community based referral mechanisms for obstetric complication.
