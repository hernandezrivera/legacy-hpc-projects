Project Summary

Major causes of morbidity in the proposed program locations are communicable and infectious diseases including measles, respiratory tract infections, malnutrition and diarrhea complicated by dehydration and fatigue . Children under five as well as pregnant and lactating mothers are particularly vulnerable to diseases that could be prevented by immunization. This vulnerability is worsened through malnutrition and drought and is elevated in areas that are difficult to access because of political, security, and geographical constraints.
The proposed health program will thus provide high-quality and no-cost health services through 13 MCHs and 5 mobile clinics/outreach services. 6 static MCHs and 2 outreach mobile clinics will be in Gedo region, 10 static MCHs and 4 mobile clinic in Mudug, 3 static MCHs and 3 outreaches in Hiran and 7 static MCH and 2 mobile clinics/outreach services in Banadir, all the facilities and the mobile will be having treatment of malnutrition cases especially SAM cases management.
We will be doing referral of the complicated cases to the nearest hospital.

Needs

Despite the ongoing heavy Gu rains in most part of Somalia, urgent nutrition support is required, as most areas have critical levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 15% or higher . Results from 30 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners between November and December 2017 indicate that the overall nutrition situation in Somalia has shown some improvement, with 8 out of 30 nutrition surveys showing Critical levels of GAM (15-30%) compared to 20 out of 31 nutrition surveys conducted in June-July 2017. At a national level, the median prevalence of acute malnutrition has improved from Critical (17.4% GAM) in June-July 2017 to Serious (13.8% GAM) in November-December. Accordingly, the number of children under the age of five that are acutely malnourished is estimated at 301,000, including 48,000 who are severely malnourished and face an increased risk of disease and death . Mortality rates have declined compared to previous seasons, though the Crude Death Rate is still serious (0.5 to <1 per 10,000 per day) in 14 out of 30 population groups surveyed. The Under-Five Death Rate (U5DR) is also Serious (1-<2 per 10,000 per day) in 5 out of 30 population groups surveyed.

Activities

1. Provide equitable access to basic and lifesaving health services at 13 static MCHs. 2. Provide equitable access to basic health and lifesaving health services through 5 mobile clinics that will support 23 outreach sites for populations in rural areas with limited access to health services. 3. Capacity building of health staff through a 3-day refresher MCH training and OJT for follow-up on identified gaps.4. Provide a 3-day CHW training at the onset of the program for 39 CHWs.5. Rehabilitation of Hobyo health facility to accommodate emergency obstetric care.6. Provide treatment for epidemic-prone and communicable diseases at 13 MCHs and 5 mobile clinics to support disease prevention and control through early warning systems and links to regional MoH.7. Provide support for national vaccination campaigns in addition to routine immunization services for children <5 and PLWs at 13 MCH facilities and 5 mobile clinics.8. Improved, integrated, quality basic nutrition services for children and pregnant and lactating women including IDPs are available.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

At the end of the project, a strong cadre of community-based structure will be built for service delivery. A community-based participatory approach will be employed through strengthening community capacity by capacity building own staff, MoH staff, establishing and training CBWs and CHWs. Mercy-USA shall gradually designate CBWs, CHWs to continue the delivery of basic services e.g. screening, including preventive, protective and promotive actions, and their roles and responsibilities will be transformed, from direct implementation to technical support, through continuous support on knowledge transfer and capacity development activities, monitoring and coordination within the existing structures.This participatory and community-centered approach will ensure community ownership and sustainability. furthermore, to enhance information and coordination at local level, monthly and quarterly consultative meetings that bring together all project stakeholders from the community will be conducted to share lessons learnt and jointly decide way forward for the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Security conditions due to major militant threats, attacks, or exercises, violence and loss of life do not prevent project implementation and Mercy-USA will implement program activities through community acceptance and will work with the local communities to ensure the safety of operations. Local, regional and federalauthorities accept Mercy-USA working in the target areas and if there will be withdrawal of support from government or key community representatives, host and IDP communities will be engaged at every phase of the project cycle; aid will be provided transparently and sensitively according to level of need.Operational environment free of criminal threats to staff and beneficiaries, Mercy-USA will do regular and ongoing staff security and safety training; active and rigorous evacuation plan detailing roles of local and international staff; close engagement with international humanitarian actors.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Mercy-USA recognizes the importance of addressing gender barriers especially via community sensitization that targets both men and women. We shall apply gender segregated community consultation meetings enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities. Measures are also put in place to maintain gender balance in terms of staff recruitment so as not to favour certain community or clan. With so many IDP’s in Somalia gender equity is just a phrase to the Somali communities leaving in this IDP camps, so many international organizations are coming out to protect the vulnerable IDP’s.
