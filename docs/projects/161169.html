Project Summary

VSF Suisse proposes to respond to unmet child protection needs across the country, in line with protection cluster/ Child Protection 2020 HRP strategy. The protection needs of boys and girls is exacerbated by conflict/ floods induced family separation, destruction of community assets, disruption of key livelihood options, basic services and community based networks that provide support services to children’s protection needs.
Proposed action will target 36,0000 beneficiaries (15000 girls, 15000 boys, 3000 men and 3000 women) in Pibor, Leer, Manyendit, Panyijiar and Koch Counties which are currently experiencing mass flooding in addition to having in the recent past been experiencing resurgence of inter-communal conflict with consequent grave human rights violations including child abductions, recruitments, killings, separation, multiple displacements among other significant child protection needs.
Proposed activities will entail current allocation priorities through; provision of the structured and non-structured psychosocial support interventions mainly in the forms of setting up/strengthening existing child friendly spaces; Life-skills and resilience building PSS activities, PFA etc.; identification of vulnerable children and provision of timely and adequate comprehensive case management support. In addition, VSF Suisse will purpose on strengthening existing community based child protection structures which will play a key role in community mobilization, engagement and awareness interventions.
VSF Suisse is currently on ground in the targeted counties and recently successfully accomplished a UNICEF PCA, which is part of a multi-year reintegration programming and proposes to sustain and scale up the momentum of child protection services. VSF-Suisse has nine years track record of partnership with UNICEF and SSHF among other donors implementing child protection activities in Unity and Pibor. Previously implemented actions responded to the needs of children associated with the armed forces and groups among other vulnerable children through reintegration, case management, psychosocial support services and strengthening community based networks.
VSF Suisse has a long standing presence, experience and established grassroots networks in proposed areas. Linkages and complementarities will be created between the ongoing FAO and OFDA livelihood projects; in addition to closely working with other partners and agencies in Education, WASH, Health and Nutrition, Protection among others to provide synergies and holistic outcomes including referrals where appropriate.
The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, gender equality and girls/ women empowerment, acknowledging the different roles of girls, women, men and boys and how they are differently affected by conflict and ensuring conflict sensitive programming by upholding Do No Harm ethos.

1 - Needs

Protracted conflict compounded by prevalent cyclical waves of inter-communal violence, revenge killings, children and women abduction including forceful recruitments and cattle related fighting in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Panyijiar and Pibor, continues to negatively impact vulnerable populations, particularly among girls and boys who have been have witnessed various atrocities and at risk of post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety disorders. These have been further exacerbated by current mass flooding which as affected at least 16,000 households in southern Unity and Pibor with subsequent inundation of crop fields and villages and significantly damaged other basic life support amenities with resultant increased vulnerabilities, unprecedented food insecurity and extreme Global Acute Malnutrition levels, which recorded highest levels in Unity (16.6) and Jonglei (19.4) (IPC, Sept 2019). As needs are worsening, the current heavy rains have cut off accessibility, with occasional fatal road ambushes being reported with looting of humanitarian aid and killings, further complicating humanitarian access and deliveries to the targeted counties. This is in addition to humanitarian workers facing increasing difficulty in accessing affected populations, with recently worse affected areas which suffered violent cattle raids such as Pibor, Boma, Panyijiar among others, complicating efforts to reach the most vulnerable and compounding existing needs. This has been attributed to several threatening letters issued by various youth groups relating to humanitarian operations in the county including various leaders interfering with humanitarian operations and politicization of humanitarian operations based on newly established administrative boundaries (OCHA Humanitarian Briefs, NAWG June 2019). Most vulnerable and displaced populations’ needs are further worsened by protection concerns including GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse, predominantly among girls and women whereby over 2,300 cases of GBV were reported in 2018, a 72 percent increase compared to 2017; although under-reported due to stigma and lack of legal assistance. Women and girls continue to face increased risks of harassment, assault and sexual violence, early marriages with estimated that 52% of girls are married between the age of 15 and 18 (Child Rights Coalition, 2016), poor rule of law which continues to prevent perpetrators from being held accountable for violations, including conflict-related sexual violence and the patriarchal and retrogressive social and cultural norms that values girls as sources of income and wealth than their rights (HRP, 2019). Boys, girls including youth continue to face various protection concerns including rampant abduction, recurring unlawful recruitment and use of children by armed groups, arbitrary arrests and detention; further compounded by age-set fighting which continues to present a problem in Pibor, with recent clashes reported between the Lango and Kurenen age-sets in Pibor (South Sudan Humanitarian Bulletin, 2019 Issues). These events continue to negatively impact children’s psychosocial wellbeing and mental health attributed to violence, witnessing killings, destruction of homes, and loss of family and friends. Additionally, difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods, recreation, and other services contribute to children’s and caregivers’ stress on a daily basis. This calls for immediate efforts to respond to the situation, including strengthened psychosocial support, case management and strengthening community based networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children in line with the 2020 HRP strategy. The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict sensitive programming by upholding Do No Harm ethos.

2 - Activities

• Provision of Individual / group counselling/psycho-social support (PSS) & referrals among conflict affected 20,000 people (1500 men, 1500 women, 8500 boys and 8500 girls).• Provide cash based individual protection assistance as part of case management among 50 boys and 50 girls.• Carry out awareness raising sessions on child protection and psychosocial support among targeted 15000 boys, 15000 girls, 3000 men and 3000 women• Monthly protection monitoring, assessment, information sharing / reporting, awareness raising & mainstreaming in the targeted counties• Conduct PSS/CBPSS/Comprehensive Case Management among targeted boys and girls with protection concerns • Assess, identify and establish women and girls friendly space & child friendly spaces within the vicinity of schools/ temporary learning spaces (TLS), cattle camps, temporary IDP Protection bases and displacement villages and negotiate access for boys and girls for their recreational activities in all the targeted counties. • Conduct mapping and establishment of functional / existing referral mechanisms among various multi-sectoral platforms• Train community based child protection networks, local administration and volunteers to prevent and respond to recruitment of children is enhanced through training on case management, referral pathways and linkages with child protection service delivery systems• Identify, verify and register in collaboration with UNICEF at least 200 CAFAAGs anticipated to be released/ released in mop up exercise in Pibor and Unity and provide them with initial socioeconomic material/ livelihood support and reintegration support.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will ensure consultations including beneficiaries’ rights recognition disclosure of various support packages/ activities during mobilization and sensitization in presence of beneficiary trustees and local leadership. The project will in addition ensure that their rights are protected in addition to enabling them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at activity/ distribution sites and appropriate contacts. VSF Suisse will also engage Cluster leads, communities, local leaders in ensuring that the most vulnerable populations are targeted and reached. An active Complaints and Feedback desks shall be established at various distribution/ training with results analysed to inform a better response.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will prioritize displacement-affected communities by ensuring unimpeded and timely access to prosed services; however, host communities including returnees will as well be targeted. These will include internally displaced persons with whom the project will ensure protection of their rights and dignity in addition to ensuring congregation of IDPs among other target groups does not expose them further to risks/ protection concerns using Do No Harm approaches.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As with other projects implemented by VSF Suisse, the organization will work with the relevant stakeholder groups, line ministries of Gender, Child and Social Welfare, NDDR, UNICEF at both national and state levels, Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), beneficiaries and local leadership structures, acknowledging their immense contribution in project implementation. These will be involved across all stages of implementation including during monitoring/ evaluation phases including during anticipated CAAFAG releases. Activities will be conducted in collaboration with child protection actors including community based child protection networks in addition to them taking lead in these initiatives, that will maintain implementation momentum, with a smooth project closure, hand over and sustainability.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Proposed interventions are aligned to the Child Protection Sub-Cluster 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan Objectives, Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare, recently launched PSS Manuals and Reintegration of Girls formerly associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups among other guidelines and strategies. These will respond by prioritizing vulnerable populations who will be supported with an array of activities including PSS, referrals, case management, awareness sessions, trainings among others.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Unity and Jonglei States are quite unstable with key elements of protection concerns including rampant cases of Gender Based Violence, child recruitments, abductions among other grave human rights violations.Hence therefore, to mitigate protection concerns across all targeted areas, VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host, IDP and returnee communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities, returnees and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

In view of the current fragile situation in the country, sustainability efforts will focus on strengthening existing community based child protection networks/ structures and beneficiary participation including linkages and close collaboration with line ministries of Gender, Child and Social Welfare, NDDR Commission. In addition, activities will focus on strengthening Community Child Protection Volunteers and Local Authority institutions for continuation of service delivery including responding to child protection concerns.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly, illiterate); local community leaders, child protection committees, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation.
