Project Summary

Through this proposed action, REACH (a joint initiative of ACTED, IMPACT Initiatives and UNOSAT) will support the coordination and technical implementation of a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) in the West Bank and Gaza. The assessment will consist of a multi-sectoral household survey employed among a representative sample of the affected population in both the West Bank and Gaza. The MSNA will be conducted in partnership with OCHA oPt and within the framework of the wider development and humanitarian architecture. The Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) will be the partner accountable for completing data collection.
The proposed MSNA will aim to achieve the following objectives:
1. Directly inform joint humanitarian-development response planning based on the nexus approach that the United Nations Country Team (UNCT) has adopted for next year, through the provision of comprehensive, multi-sectoral household data. MSNA data will therefore aim to inform the development of both the 2022 HNO/HRP and the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework planned for 2023-27;
2. Provide a detailed inter-sectoral analysis of the magnitude and severity of humanitarian needs among crisis-affected populations in oPt, to support the calculation of sectoral and overall People in Need and severity calculations
3. Provide a detailed vulnerability analysis to identify at-risk and ‘furthest left behind’ population groups, as well as geographic areas with the most acute needs and vulnerabilities, to subsequently inform assistance targeting and response planning efforts; and
4. Support inclusive and people-centred assistance provision based on the principles of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), through the collection of information regarding preferred communication, information sharing, and assistance modalities.
REACH will produce the following key outputs: 1 household dataset; 1 static summary findings dashboard; series of presentations to key stakeholders; package of sectoral factsheets; and 1 final analytical report.

1 - Needs

The situation in occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) is characterized as a protracted protection crisis. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2.2 million Palestinians out of a total population of 5.2 million were estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance, 43% of whom were classified as being in severe need. The population faces multi-faceted and compounding humanitarian and protection challenges, resulting from “more than 50 years of Israeli occupation, insufficient respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel Security Forces and Palestinian armed groups.”1 These factors put vulnerable Palestinian households at continual risk of conflict and violence, displacement, food insecurity, and poverty, requiring a robust humanitarian response to aid those in need. However, “record-low funding levels” put further strain on the availability and provision of critical services and assistance.2 In addition to the humanitarian context, Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza are also facing a number of socioeconomic and environmental challenges. The Government of Palestine, Palestinian civil society, and international organisations are thus working towards achieving sustainable development targets in the midst of ongoing insecurity and occupation. As such, and as outlined in the 2020 Atlas of Sustainable Development, addressing immediate humanitarian needs in oPt must also be complemented by efforts to build resilient communities through bolstering social justice and rule of law, tackling climate change, and eradicating poverty.3 The global COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated humanitarian needs and pre-existing vulnerabilities among the Palestinian population and poses an additional risk to sustainable development. Following the declaration of a state of emergency in oPt on 5 March 2020, the humanitarian community has mobilized to support necessary interventions outlined in the COVID-19 Response Plan. Although the presence of acute humanitarian needs is evident, significant information gaps exist due to the lack of a comprehensive inter-sectoral needs analysis to inform the oPt response. Similarly, granular household data to inform development assistance planning and to support the monitoring of progress against global SDG indicators is also needed. To address these gaps, REACH proposes to conduct an MSNA within the framework of the humanitarian-development nexus. The assessment will be conducted in close partnership with UN OCHA and through the mandate of the UNCT/HCT, to occur in 2021. The REACH Initiative was formed in 2010 as a joint initiative of ACTED, its sister organization IMPACT Initiatives (Geneva-based think-and-do-tank), and UNOSAT in order to promote and facilitate the development of information products that enhance the humanitarian community’s decision-making and planning capacity. IMPACT is programmatic lead of the initiative, in charge of all strategic and programmatic oversight, the HR management of REACH international staff as well as of dissemination and external relations at the global level for REACH. As sister organizations and global partners, IMPACT and ACTED coordinate on field implementation. In all field operations of IMPACT/REACH programs, ACTED provides operational support (country registration, office space, accommodation, cars, national HR recruitment, security). This global partnership is formalized in a global MoU and project specific agreements. Additionally, ACTED expects to sub-contract PCBS to formalize this component of the partnership. Finally, the protracted protection crisis in oPt resulting from decades of occupation, combined with traditional patriarchal norms and legal framework, have led to gender inequality. It is estimated that at least 37% of women are or have been victims of GBV, with particularly high occurrences among vulnerable communities such as internally displaced persons (IDPs) (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020). Moreover, the COVID-19 crisis is disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and deepening pre-existing vulnerabilities, especially for women, the disabled and the elderly. As such, the proposed MSNA will ensure to focus on these differing needs as part of the assessment. 2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Assessment Coordination: REACH will seek the mandate to conduct the MSNA through the UNCT, the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) and further endorsement of the MSNA TORs through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) and the UN Data Group. Decision-making on technical matters will take place through the UNCT Data Group. An MSNA Technical Working Group will also be established to support coordination with MSNA partners throughout the assessment process. MSNA partners are anticipated to include the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), who will lead the data collection exercise, as well as Clusters, partner NGOs, IM and assessment actors, government agencies, UN agencies, and local organisations. Through this coordination effort, REACH will consult partners and take into consideration other nationwide assessments, such as MICS and SEFSEC, to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication of other assessments. Additionally, data collection is anticipated to be coordinated by REACH, either remotely or in-person depending on confirmed data collection timelines and operational considerations. Activity 2 – Technical Implementation: REACH will lead the research design, including sampling strategy and data collection methodology, as well as development of the assessment indicators, household survey tool, and analysis plan. PCBS will be accountable for conducting the household data collection. A random sample of households will be drawn using probability-based techniques, to be determined based on further discussions with OCHA and partners regarding security and access considerations. Disaggregation by geographical areas will be reflected as per the sample. At minimum, the sample will be stratified at the governorate level, with the aim of producing findings that are statistically generalizable with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error per strata. Additional stratifications and/or an additional sample of households will then be drawn in priority communities or areas where more granular findings are of interest; therefore, while the geographical stratification will be governorate/admin 2 level, vulnerable geographical areas identified by the ICCG/data group will be addressed by over sampling in Area C, H2, EJ, and Gaza. Approximately 8,500 households are anticipated to be interviewed. MSNA findings will be aggregated and presented at both the Oslo area level and the crisis level. Data collection will take the form of one of three scenarios – face-to-face, phone/remote, or a mixture of the two – depending on security, access, and public health considerations. REACH will develop an adapted country-specific COVID-19 data collection SOP, based on our established global COVID-19 SOP, to be followed by all field teams, outlining mandatory public health and social distancing measures. Contingency data collection methods will be developed for areas that are not directly accessible, due either to COVID-19 restrictions or wider security constraints. REACH will engage closely with partners to develop key MSNA sectoral indicators, which will also include the integration of development indicators in both the data collection tool and analysis. The starting point for this process will be the review of established indicator frameworks. These indicators will form the basis of the MSNA analysis plan and the household survey tool, which will be finalized in consultation with Cluster Coordinators and IMOs. Pending completion of the data collection by PCBS, joint analysis of the MSNA data is anticipated to take an integrated approach, bringing together key humanitarian and development stakeholders to analyse urgent humanitarian needs alongside vulnerability, risk, and resilience. In addition to vulnerability analysis, a partial gender analysis will be conducted as another analytical outcome. This holistic process will therefore support the nexus approach to response planning envisaged in oPt next year, whilst also ensuring that humanitarian programming under a nexus framework remains guided by needs-based approaches. As a distinct analytical output, REACH will also support OCHA in conducting joint inter-sectoral analysis specifically for the HNO, using the MNSA data to inform calculation of the overall inter-sectoral PIN as required, in line with the JIAF framework or other calculation models that may be utilised. The needs analysis produced will therefore inform HNO data requirements, and information collected will inform analysis on the humanitarian consequences of needs.Activity 3 – Output production: The MSNA data will be used to produce the following information products: Final household dataset; Static summary findings dashboard displaying Cluster-selected sectoral indicators by area and population group; Series of presentations to OCHA, Clusters, and other key stakeholders on preliminary and final findings; Package of sectoral factsheets presenting key MSNA findings, including composite indicators such as Living Standards Gap and household Capacity Gap; Final report providing detailed analysis of inter-sectoral needs and other cross-cutting thematic areas.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be firstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including Palestine. The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of the activities. Partner I/NGOs supporting data collection will be informed and trained on CRM in order to share information with respondents and encourage respondents to report any issues or concerns during the MSNA’s data collection process. Several channels will be available to respondents to file a complaint or raises concerns: through the data collection tea (who will then refer it to ACTED) and ACTED’s dedicated phone line open 24/7 (incl. via SMS/WhatsApp), dedicated email address, complaints box (within ACTED offices). All MSNA respondents will be explained their right to provide complaints in confidentiality. It is worth stressing that the CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, complaint tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanism, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and ensure ACTED’s work has maximum impact.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

1. Informed consent will be a central principle guiding the data collection process – enumerators will inform survey respondents of the purpose and objectives of the assessment and confirm their willingness to participate before commencing the interview. Enumerators will also inform respondents that they can retract their consent to participate at any time during the interview. 2. Phone numbers of respondents will only be collected if the respondent consents to providing it; phone numbers will only be kept on file (for participation in future assessments) if the respondent consents to this.3. Enumerators will only interview adult respondents (18+ years in age). 4. All field staff (including REACH and partner enumerators) will participate in PSEA and AAP awareness modules as part of the MSNA data collection training. 5. Mixed-sex data collection teams will ensure that female respondents may be interviewed by female enumerators, as requested or required. 6. Assessment indicators and the corresponding household survey questionnaire will be designed to be culturally appropriate and sensitive to social norms and values. 7. All personal identifiers (e.g. village name, GPS coordinates, phone numbers, etc.) will be removed from the final dataset before it is shared with partners or external audiences.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Data and analysis from the MSNA is intended to provide humanitarian actors the necessary evidence base to perform impartial, needs-based targeting, and in in turn ensure that the most vulnerable groups and/or groups with the most acute needs are prioritized for assistance
