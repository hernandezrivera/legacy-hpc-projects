Project Summary

This project aims to ensure that there is increased equitable access to health services at health facility, at community level and through integrated outreach activities conducted by mobile teams among IDP and Host (rural and urban) populations in South Central Somalia. The project design takes on a targeted approach with a focus on: (1). Control of communicable illnesses (diarrhea, acute respiratory illnesses, malaria and measles). (2). Integrated management of common childhood illnesses (Eye, ear, skin, worm infections, anaemia and chronic childhood diseases) (3). Expand access to EPI, contributing to control of vaccine preventable diseases (4). Expanded access to reproductive healthcare services for women of reproductive age. (5). Community Disease surveillance and response – An emergency Rapid response component is built in to allow quick scale up of lifesaving health services (e.g. through CTCs/ORT corners/mobile ORT and isolation wards) during rapid onset emergencies. (6). Increasing access to Emergency Primary Health Care including managing non communicable diseases (diabetes/Hypertension etc.) and mental, neurological and substance use (MNS) disorders at OPD level. (7) Strengthening treatment & referral of Mental Health & psychosocial support services at community & facility level through locally recruited and incentivized front line psychosocial workers who will be trained on psychological first aid and basic counselling skills.
The service will be provided across all supported health centers. In this way, IMC aims to contribute to reduction in mortality, human suffering and socio-economic effects among disaster and conflict affected populations in Somalia. IMC proposes to continue to use a multi-layered approach to mitigate excess morbidity and mortality in south-central Somalia. It includes: (1) Bringing health services closer to the communities to address the urgent health needs of conflict and disaster affected and other vulnerable population in south-central Somalia through uninterrupted delivery of free, high quality integrated health, nutrition, protection and WASH services. (2) Positively influencing individual, household and community behavior change to boost the absorptive and adaptive capacity of vulnerable people to manage implications of risks/shocks through innovative culturally acceptable interventions. (3) Strengthening local capacity to respond to emergency primary health needs in South Central Somalia by working with community structures like health committees and coordinating with other humanitarian actors and the MOH Somalia to complement and cover gaps in health, nutrition, protection, and WASH services. (4) Improving capacity of non-specialized general health care staff by providing training in selected priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders using WHO’s mhGAP–Humanitarian Intervention. (5) Enhancing collaboration with stakeholders at the local level to improve coordination, prudent use of resources and increasing synergy between various actors for the maximum benefit of the target communities. (6) Identifying the gender excluded and the reason for their exclusion from access to services and opportunities in the Health sector, designing policy and/or project-level responses that attempt to address the barriers in the project cycle implementation, carry out monitoring and evaluation (M&E) to check whether planned resources and actions have reached women, men, boys, girls, the poor and all the excluded and adjusting/redesigning on continuous basis basing on M&E findings. (7). IMC will ensure that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities including deaf, blind, and elderly, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as giving them priorities when queuing, provision and use of ramps, wheel chair, grab bars, separate toilets for male & female and disable friendly toilets seats.

1. - Projects activities

The activities include; 1. Provide diagnosis and treatment and necessary follow-up of common illnesses including Mental Health & psychosocial support, in women, men, boys and girls form both IDP and host populations. 2. Ensure access to skilled birth attendance through quality Basic Emergency Obstetric Services (BEmONC) for women of reproductive age. 3. Provide training and ongoing supportive supervision to male and female healthcare workers at facility and community level on integrated management of childhood illnesses. 4. Expand coverage of vaccination in children aged 5 years and below. 5. Deploy male and female CHWs to facilitate health education/promotion at health facilities and FHWs at community level. 6. Improve access to vital ANC and PNC services at community level to improve early access to maternal and reproductive healthcare. 7. Form representative (gender, clan, disable groups, IDP and host balanced) community health committees/health center committees to provide oversight of implementation activities. 8. Respond to disease outbreaks through stand-by emergency rapid response teams and mobilize community to respond to mitigate impact of disease outbreaks including response to acute watery diarrhea, measles, COVID-19, war wound, population displacements and floods among others. 9. Ensure gender inclusion in project design, implementation, reporting and closure, e.g. via organizing different stake holders meeting (from men, women, disable, youth (boys & girls), elderly) in the life cycle of the projects.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

On the Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors, IMC propose to provide mobile health and referral services to reduce chances or frequency of beneficiary movements. IMC will also engage all actors including armed ones in the service implementations by creating awareness at community level and emphasizing the significance of the activities. On the Exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, same strategy for distance will be engaged and other disable friendly services will be provided at health facilities including linking with other actors to support disable persons in the community.
