Project Summary

The 2017 UNICEF situation report states that the health services in South Sudan remained extremely weak and fragile, and even where functional, only a narrow, ad hoc range of services were available that could be intermittently accessed by people. Many areas remained inaccessible due to the worsening conflict and economic and political crises. In 2017, operational costs also doubled as very expensive air assets were required to enable programmatic responses. Women and children were among the worst affected by this situation, with their very survival at stake. South Sudan has among the highest maternal mortality and child mortality rates in the world. Young children are most prone to common infectious diseases (malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhoea) with high rates of malnutrition increasing the risk of severe illness and death. The prevailing health systems challenges are exacerbated by lack of adequate water and sanitation, mainly the source of water supply, including water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, poor healthcare waste management, extensive malnutrition and a looming threat of viral hemorrhagic disease including Ebola, necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health securities to mitigate disease spread and enhance beneficiary experience are in place.
In 2018, WHO in its 2018 country cooperative strategy stated that South Sudan has some of the worst health outcome indicators globally: Maternal mortality ratio stands at 789 per 100,000 live births, whereas neonatal and under-five mortality rates are 39.3 and 99.2, per 1000 live births respectively (2014). A significant disparity in health status across socio-demographic factors and geographical location is well documented. The country has experienced recurrent humanitarian crises which have deepened and spread since December 2013. Approximately 2.6 million people – about one in every five people in South Sudan - have been forced to flee their homes since the conflict began, including nearly 1.6 million internally displaced people (with more than 50 percent estimated to be children) and more than one million refugees in neighbouring countries. The humanitarian crisis has had devastating effects on food security, child survival, access and utilization of health services and escalation of the economic situation. Some 4.8 million people - more than one in every three people in South Sudan - were estimated to be severely food insecure; 67 out of 80 counties have inadequate or no health care services at all due to dilapidated structures, destruction and closure of health facilities; widespread outbreaks of diseases such as cholera, malaria, measles and other communicable diseases. Communicable diseases remain a major public health problem and are the leading causes of deaths. WVSS will rely on the core pipeline and on its own procured stock of medicines to ensure that health facilities are adequately stocked with essential medicines.
Through the 2019 humanitarian response plan, WVSS intends to reach the conflict affected and other vulnerable populations of Warrap state ( Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj North, and Twic counties), Western Equatoria ( Yambio, Nagero, Nzara, Tambura) and Upper Nile (Tambura) with primary health services package through PHCUs, PHCCs, and Mobile teams. WVSS will address the looming threat of viral hemorrhagic fever diseases such as Ebola and Rift Valley Fever through prevention and preparedness activities. WVSS will also address seasonal upsurges in epidemic prone diseases (malaria/meningitis/Hepatitis E) compounded by food insecurity increasing susceptibility to disease and under five mortality. WVSS will also address emerging needs for health assistance fornon-communicable diseases requiring expansion of emergency health assistance including non-communicable pipeline essential medicines and commodities. WVSS will also provide an integrated response to health and protection securities including targeted joint response and evaluation of the implementation of essential clinical packages. WVSS will ensure that there is predictable access to essential health services through targeted risk assessment of communities and health facility functionality.

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety Audits are completed for each project site and/or service point, withrecommendations used to informed the continued use of the location and anyadaptions that are required to improve the safety of the site / service point (orreaching this location). Safety Audits are completed prior to establishment ofthe project site or service point (ex. WGSS) and repeated quarterly or ad hoc, forexample in the instance of population displacement or significant event thatmay be perceived to change local dynamics. Safety Audits are complementedby Safety Perception mapping, which captures the safety issues or unsafeareas perceived by beneficiaries, which may be different from observation; thisis completed quarterly or ad hoc as necessary and used comparatively toinform project implementation and any adaptations as necessary.
