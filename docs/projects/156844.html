Project Summary

Although much of its land is semi-arid, the Syrian Arab Republic was, until 2011, a country with a strong agricultural base and an important exporter of both crop and livestock products. Despite the poor rainfall and the conflict-related depredation of the last four years, crop and livestock production still plays a vital role in the country’s greatly diminished internal economy and in the survival of its population. Even though one-third of the Syrian population now depends on humanitarian food assistance, there are still significant areas where farmers are able to produce and supply food to the market. There is therefore the need and opportunity to support such production and the associated livelihoods of millions of rural families. Support provided to agricultural production will help alleviate the scarcity of basic food commodities and reduce dependency from imports and food aid. Agriculture plays a pivotal role in addressing many facets of the crisis. This includes protecting and restoring food security, employment, economic growth, natural resources and social cohesion. Support to homestead and micro-gardens will give internally displaced persons (IDPs), host families and vulnerable populations which have reduced access to land the means to produce nutrient-rich foods to complement their diets with a very quick turnaround.
This project presents an integrated approach to address food insecurity while protecting and gradually restoring agricultural-based livelihoods and strengthening the capacity of people to manage and recover from shocks. By integrating immediate relief with livelihood-recovery activities, the proposed action will contribute to the strengthening of resilience of the targeted communities.
Building on previous successful experience in Dar'a, EPDC will empower vulnerable communities in more accessible and stable areas of Hassakeh to rebuild their lives and restore a sense of normalcy, preparing Syrians for a post-conflict context. This one-year project will provide livelihoods and governance support to vulnerable communities in Hassakeh Governorate to improve their ability to respond to shocks and stressors in the future. EPDC will: provide income generating support to 2,200 men and women through agricultural inputs, establish kitchen gardens for 1,200 people and support 120 people in off-farming

Needs

Prior to 2011, agriculture was the main source of employment for 47 per cent of the population. Continued destruction of the economy and cessation of trade has led to high levels of unemployment; the unemployment rate was estimated at 53 per cent in 2015 but has increased the last two years. Repeated shocks, including multiple displacements and reductions in livelihood opportunities, are contributing to an uncertain future for the majority of Syria’s population. As a result, people are resorting to harmful food and livelihoods-based coping strategies that often exacerbate the protection risks households are exposed to. Harmful coping mechanisms include spending savings, borrowing money, reducing non-food items, and depleting productive assets. There are reports of high proportion of households engaging in irreversible coping strategies to meet food needs, including selling property or land and engaging in high risk activities. In rural communities, households are no longer able to make a living through agriculture alone due to low productivity and incomes, as well as poor access to cultivable areas due to insecurity.It is estimated that 9.8 million people are in acute need of early recovery and livelihood support and an estimated three in four people of working age are not involved in any income generating activities. According to the HNO Syria has seen the proportion of the population living in extreme poverty double from almost 34 per cent pre-crisis (less than US$1.90 per day) to approximately 69 per cent today. Combined with an upsurge in the cost of living, this has led to a severe erosion of people’s purchasing power. For instance, up to 1.2 million families cannot afford rent costs, with a recent report on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues in northwest Syria reporting ‘unaffordable housing’ as a concern in 100 per cent of sub-districts surveyed. Furthermore, the proportion of income spent on food has soared as incomes and household food production have decreased, while food prices have increased dramatically. Before the crisis, about 25 per cent of households spent over half their annual income on food. In 2017, an estimated 90 per cent of households are spending more than half of their annual income on food. About 50 per cent of households have reduced the number of meals they consume each day, and more than 30 per cent restrict the food consumption of adults to allow children to eat. Negative coping mechanisms disproportionately affect the most vulnerable groups, such as children. Across Syria some 82 per cent of the 4,185 communities surveyed perceived the occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance within their communities, with almost 65 per cent of communities perceiving this as a ‘common’ issue. In addition, some 69 per cent of communities perceived the occurrence of early marriage, exposing girls to loss of self-esteem, significant personal protection risks, health issues and depriving them of an education. Furthermore, it is estimated that 15 per cent of Syrians in the labour force are engaged in ‘armed struggle’ or ‘illegal’ economic activity (fighting, smuggling, trafficking), in many cases as this is the only livelihood opportunity available to them.

Implementation of activities

EPDC will build on similar pilot project started in December 2018 in Hassakeh and its needs assessment Result 1: Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training. Activity 1.1: Conduct needs assessment in selected locationsPrimary assessment to understand the needs of the population, agricultural inputs needed and available in the local market, most successful crops in the village, source of water for irrigation and distance to markets will be conducted in the selected location prior to project implementation. Meeting with local authorities will also be conducted and MoU will be signed accordingly. Advertisement for registration will be conducted in the villages for a specific amount of time to enable interested farmers to apply. Vulnerability critieria will be collected and rated by M&E team to select the most vulnerable for participation in the project. Activity 1.2: HH kitchen gardens establishedVegetable production is one of the most lucrative activities in which individual households and even IDPs can participate. Syrians in the pre-war context had high consumption rates of vegetables (Cafeiro 2009). Even in the damaged marketing systems resulting from the conflict, demand for vegetables remains remarkably robust (FAO, FSLA 2017). Beneficiaries will benefit from assistance in training, tools, fertilizer, drip irrigation equipment, etc as needed and depending on preliminary needs assessment. The farmers will be trained in vegetable production and water management, seedbed preparation, and postharvest practices. While small amounts of fertilizer will be provided, farmers will be taught to compost and apply manure to improve soil structure and reduce fertilizer expenditures.Activity 1.3: Vegetable producers trained on income generating activitiesVegetable producers will be grouped together for extension purposes. The most cohesive groups will form marketing groups with the objective of selling jointly at higher and more lucrative prices. In addition, members will (A 2.1.1.1.1) receive extra training in business skills to encourage them to treat vegetable growing as a business and to consider other value add activities Activity 1.4: Support to mid-scale farmersThe project will support 1,200 mid-scale farmers in selected villages. Selection of the beneficiaries will take place according to the criteria set by the working group to ensure coordinated approach. General selection criteria based on vulnerability of beneficiaries: 1) households without current stable income 2) Female Headed Households 3) households with 3 or more children less than 5 years old 4) households with at least 1 disabled or chronically/acutely ill person 5) households with at least 1 elderly person 6) households that include pregnant or lactating woman. The farmers will be trained in vegetable production and water management, seedbed preparation, and postharvest practices. While small amounts of fertilizer will be provided, farmers will be taught to compost and apply manure to improve soil structure and reduce fertilizer expenditures. Result 2: Support Income generating activities including vocational training. Activity: Women off-farm production groups established & supportedThe project seeks to increase women’s business activities by grouping women together to form food processing groups and providing them the necessary equipment to make high margin products. Selection of the product will depend on a market study and value chain assessment prior to the implementation of the activity.

Protection Risk Analysis

Income generating activities can cause tension between communities thus EPDC to avoid community tensions will share selection criteria with the community in advance, To avoid misusage of beneficiaries’ data EPDC will ensure keeping lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use and apply effective data protection measures, EPDC will ensure all staff, and volunteers involved in the project and in direct contact with beneficiaries have received training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA)

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:● Conflict lines shift due to conflict activities, affecting the safety/mobility of program staff and areas of proposed intervention● Loss of support from the local governing authorities or councils● Beneficiaries do not want to participate in the project● Duplication or gaps in activities due to miscommunication amongst humanitarian agencies and stakeholders● High fluctuations in prices of items needed for the project as the waring context escalates● Limited availability of medicines ● Delays with distributions caused by checkpoints/delays in obtaining necessary approvals● Lack of full time medical staff and specialization Assumptions:● The local security situation remains stable within this current time and beneficiaries will not face large scale displacements from the target areas.● The local governing authorities support the project in a continuous manner● Coordination and communication lines with other agencies, other services and with local authorities stay in place● Acceptance and engagement of targeted communities. It is assumed that through investment of resources, sound project design and use of appropriate techniques, the benefits of the action will be accepted by the community. Mitigation:• EPDC will implement a robust contingency plan to fluidly relocate selected vulnerable project locations in the event of a shift in security context which could compromise the selected areas.• EPDC will continue to utilize their strong relationship and reputation with the Ministry of Health and Directorate of Health to identify other opportunities although the risk is deemed very low.• EPDC will meet local government officials on a regular basis and update them on the progress of activity implementation. They will also involve them if there are any modifications from the initial plan.• EPDC will disseminate information about projects within the community and will also organize community events to build acceptance and provide services, in coordination with local authorities.• EPDC will remain an active member of the coordination system at the national level led by UN OCHA. At the local level, EPDC will continue to coordinate with humanitarian actors already implementing projects in the areas of operation.• EPDC continues to strengthen its on-site Field Monitoring capacity to ensure a standardized but flexible approach for verifying programmatic and operational aspects of each hub-level program
