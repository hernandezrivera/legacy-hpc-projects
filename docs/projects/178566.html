Project Summary


In the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, and in response to the objective to support mitigation of the socio-economic impacts stemming from economic and financial crisis in addition to restrictive measures to prevent COVID-19. Based on Rescate previous experience from Dayr Hafir market project, we received requests from Aleppo City Council to implement the Market project in Ashrafieh to assist in providing job opportunities for 350 beneficiaries, and most important to urge cleanliness in the neighborhoods because the old market open space is a sold waste garbage landfill causing aggressive negative impact on the general health situation in the area. Rescate receive a pre-approval on the market project from the city council. Thus; Rescate based its studies on the ERL activities and indicators per HRP objectives:
A. Improve access to basic and social services and infrastructure B. Strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery. Hence, Rescate has visited the location among other options and priority areas to build this intervention in line with needs expressed by communities. The intervention is planning to respond to lifesaving needs for a market in Aleppo city (Ashrafieh) in response to the emergency needs of the community to have a market including facilities for management (Market Committee), WASH and renewable power/electricity and cleanliness (solid waste disposal), to institute direct customer/farmer/producers’ relation.
Rescate has set the plan in coordination with the city council, the municipality and the community leaders including women and PWDs who are working in the streets to secure minimum living costs. The location is considered based on the municipality and community consultations in addition to the studies about the nearby public services as identified (water, sanitation, power/electricity, cleanliness and environment measures were conducted to decide on the infrastructure rehabilitation levels. A detailed BOQ prepared with market studies for costs, the location scheme including facilities for 9 types of sales professions including full consideration to PWDs needs special WCs and ramps with rails around the market for their safety. The project aims at securing 50/50 male-female shares, the Market Committee shall includes women representatives. The socio-economic study results show women have a fundamental role in the targeted area because they work in the back scene on farming, livestock husbandry and dairy products in addition to preparing legume and fruits for sale (packaging, bundles, food production including dried herbs, sweets, and syrups etc.). Therefore; rehabilitation of the market per say will create an optimum place to display women and men products including PWDs, hence will positively impact on all community production and will improve their income. During consultations and the community involvement in designing the project, women and PWDs were represented and “welcomed working back-to-back with male peers”, this is extracted from meetings during the field need assessment. The market committee will be encouraged to persuade that each stand will have females to work with males and/or PWD at site because each stand requires at least two individuals to run it, women are encouraged to share work at the stands so they guarantee direct income and access to benefit from their work.
Rescate is committed to submit 4Ws and reports to cover progress and achievements, M&E, financial and narrative reports to the sector and donors. Rescate coordinates with DINGO to ensure no duplication of services amongst the acting partners.

4 - Needs

Yes, need assessment is available for this project. Rescate team has been working with the city council, the municipality and the community leaders at the proposed location of the market. The team prepared detailed BOQ for the works at the market, drawings, and plan for the distribution of stands as per the communities suggested professions and urgent needs. The market will be established upon the international Marche standards with light rehabilitation but covering the basic services as per community expressed needs especially the following:Location is set by the city council in response to community vetting and preference especially that the local stakeholders are unable to rehabilitate the market. Sellers are now scattered in the streets near the main square in the neighborhoods creating traffic jam. But their activities are limited to vegetable, fruits, plastic kitchen ware like pots and some cutlery, and detergents, in addition to clothes’ stands on carpets. The situation around the market does not avail facilitated opportunities for women, or PWDs to work because they are obliged to transport the products in and out of the market in the morning and evening, not to mention cost of transportation. There is major lack of services, such as: WCs, cleanliness, WASH, Solar lighting and power for refrigerators for dairy and meat sales. Lack of proper outlets/exits/entrances. Lack of any kind of organization at site that facilitates shopping for customers. Lack of cleanliness arrangements such as garbage bins, a tractor and bobcat for solid waste disposal. Rescate carried out detailed discussions with the city council, municipality and community leaders including women and PWD. City Council granted approval to Rescate.

5 - Implementation of activities

In response to ERL Sector Strategic Objective 3 Increase resilience and access to services for affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities. And achieving the SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES 3.2. Access to market-based livelihood opportunities and production is enhanced. The project activities are focused on support income-generating activities through appropriate modalities, increasing beneficiaries’ resilience by scaling-up interventions to contain the impact of COVID-19 crisis and of the general economic deterioration affecting livelihoods. Activities are as follows: 1) Coordination with stakeholders especially MOLAE, the City Council Committee and the community.2) Set the selection criteria for the targeted beneficiaries with the City Council committee. 3) Rescate will intensify the targeted beneficiaries amongst the already vulnerable and worst affected by Covid-19 response impacts such as loss of jobs, shortage of essential supplies and restrictions on movement, with particular focus on persons with disabilities, female headed households, elderlies, and displacement affected communities. 4) Assign the works implementation to a local contractor via procurement procedure.5) Form Market committee including females and PWDs.6) Set the Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism to allow for lodging complaints. The mechanism functions under the Project Manager and has close coordination with the operational team to resolve complaints in a timely and consistent manner.

The intervention will consider support for women and girls amongst others and will contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving community living. Support the resilience of affected local communities, households and individuals within the humanitarian response by protecting and restoring livelihoods and enabling access to essential services and rehabilitation of socio-economic infrastructures. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In terms of social risks, the assumption is that the targeted population will accept the criteria for beneficiaries’ selection. There is a mid-risk that this will not be the case, and the impact of this would be grave. The mitigation measures taken during the project identification were: 1) to meet with the targeted population, market committee, and local authorities in order to identify the needs and establish the beneficiary selection criteria together, to avoid future tensions; 2) to establish a common and clear transparent beneficiary selection criterion. During the implementation of the project unattended people may feel discriminated and tensions arise. There is a mid-risk that this could happened, and the impact again would be grave. For mitigation measures, ONG Rescate will: 1) do periodical follow up visits to the site of intervention, in the first visit (and subsequent if needed) will remind the targeted population, market committee, and local stakeholders about the selection criteria previously agreed; 2) establish formal and informal methods of complain (through regular meetings during the visits with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, interviews, focus group if allowed by Covid-19 restrictions, an email and WhatsApp number available). As soon as Rescate learns of any tension that may be arising, Rescate Syria and HQ (if needed) will immediately take action according to the situation, informing the donor at all times. Another assumption is that Covid-19 will not disrupt the project. It is difficult to assess beforehand the probability for this to happen and its consequences. Rescate will take prevention measures since the beginning to avoid contagion: PPE will be distributed for the activities.
