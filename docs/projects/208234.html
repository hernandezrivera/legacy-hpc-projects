Project Summary

The overall aim is to reduce mortality, morbidity and undernutrition by providing life-saving nutrition and health services in health facilities and communities.
1. Affected populations have improved access to quality primary healthcare services in the targeted areas for reduction of morbidity and mortality
2. Affected populations have access to quality nutrition treatment and preventive services through strengthening the capacity of health facilities
The program will be focusing on;
- Provision of quality primary health care services that includes basic management and prevention of communicable diseases for all age groups from children to adults,
- Providing medical equipment, supplies and pharmaceuticals and nutritional supplies to the health facilities through the award and in coordination with SMOH and LGA authorities.
- Sexual and Reproductive health services; antenatal and postnatal care services, support to uncomplicated vaginal delivery services and minor perinatal complications,
- Support to referral services for specialized care.
- Support to Routine Childhood Immunization in collaboration with SMOH and WHO.
- Support to provision of clinical management of minor physical injury
- Prepositioning of treatment kits and supplies for Cholera and Measles outbreak in the targeted LGAs
- Provision of nutrition services through IMAM approach in targeted communities.
- Supplementary Nutrition assistant program through CVA modality for PBW (Porridge MOM)
- Reinforcement of technical capacity through sector/sub-sector specific technical trainings for quality service delivery.
- Building institutional and community capacity for promotion and provision of appropriate IYCF-E integrated with priority health and hygiene practices.
Target:
1. Children 0-59 months of age for malnutrition treatment and other children for provision of primary health services.
2. Women: key target of the project, both in its direct activities (e.g. Care Groups) and preventive and curative health services, as well as with the women empowerment agenda applied across the project.
3. Men: men in the targeted communities will be provided with primary health care services for the management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) and communicable diseases.
4. Health workers and community-based structures e.g CHIPS, CV etc
5. People with Disability and Special Needs.

1 - Needs

The decade of conflict has had a devastating humanitarian consequence in the BAY state, however, in 2023, the security incidence and fatalities has improved compared to the peak of the conflict in 2014/15, though, conflict and violence continue unabated in some part of the region.With Borno state Government commitment to close all camps in the state by 2024, the closure may rush internally displaced persons (IDPs) back to areas not fully stabilized. Without adequate planning, these returnees risk facing a shortage of resources and potential security threats, further exposing them to more risks. The IOM DTM R43 reported 14% increase in the number of returnees to Borno state, 0.7% increase in Adamawa and 0.3% in Yobe state compared to the previous rounds This is mainly due to insecurity and conflict, also these numbers might have increase due to the Niger coup of July 26th (Interagency Rapid Needs Assessment Report August 2023) Hence, this might have also increase the number of vulnerable populations in need of humanitarian assistance.A recent LGA level SMART survey conducted by ACF in 4 LGAs of Yobe state during harvest season (November 2023) shows high prevalence of acute malnutrition rates. The highest and lowest prevalence of GAM were found in Machina and Fika LGAs at 18.1 % and 9.3% respectively. The prevalence of severe acute malnutrition ranged from 1.1% in Fika to 4.8% in Machina LGA. Similarly, the nutrition surveillance data collected in September 2023 among those displaced from hard-to-reach areas indicated a nutrition crisis in some LGAs of Borno state, with an estimated global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence rates of 19.8% and 37.7% in Bama and Gwoza LGAs, respectively. The GAM prevalence for Borno state still remained in a serious phase of cGAM 11.1% [9.4-13.1] (NFSS R13, Aug 2023) as compared to NFSS round 12 (cGAM 14%). In Yobe state, the report also shows an improvement in the nutritional situation at the state level cGAM of 9.7% [8.0-11.6] when compared to the previous NFSS report during the same period which was 13.1%. However, some LGAs in Borno recorded a deteriorated situation, MMC/Jere recorded combined GAM of 17.2% [12.9 – 22.2] and central Borno 12.4%. Likewise Northern Yobe have also recorded very high cGAM level of 14% [10.8-18.0] and SAM level of 4.9% (the highest in the BAY states) and central Yobe 11% [8.9 – 13.7]Furthermore, the nutrition sector admitted 380,040 children with severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) between January and October 2023, a 24% increase in comparism with the previous year. Among these, ACF contribution to the SAM admissions was 69,184 (18%) of the sector admissionsThe major contributing factors driving the current malnutrition situation in the northeast region are poor food consumption patterns in terms of diversity, high prevalence of child morbidities (fever/malaria, diarrhea) and poor hygiene practices. On the other hand, acute food insecurity is a medium risk factor contributing to acute malnutrition, as most of the LGAs were classified in Phase 3 of the Cadre Harmonisé. The insecurity in the region also continues to impact the nutrition situation, with continued displacement as a result of the 2022 camp closure and limited access to food and basic health-care services.The prevailing humanitarian crisis is further intensified by the occurrence of disease epidemics and outbreaks, notably Malaria, Measles, and Diphtheria. According to OCHA, there has been a resurgence of diphtheria, leading to a tragic loss of lives. Specifically, the report indicates that more than 200 children have lost their lives due to the resurgence of diphtheria, with 65 fatalities in Borno state and 141 in Yobe state since November 2022. This alarming statistic highlights the devastating impact of the disease on vulnerable populations, particularly children. This data paints a distressing picture of the ongoing health crisis, emphasizing the urgent need for targeted interventions.

2 - Activities

The interventions proposed by Action Against Hunger will provide lifesaving health and nutrition activities including the provision of treatment and prevention of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) services in health facilities (outpatient therapeutic programs/OTPs and stabilization centers/SC) and communities, building the capacity of health workers to provide quality physical and mental health services, health facility rehabilitations and provision of medical supplies and equipment. AAH interventions will contribute to the achievement of the objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors for 2024. The response will also directly contribute to specific objectives of the Health and Nutrition Sectors’ response plans as outlined in the HRP, and the Nigeria Nutrition in Emergencies Sector Strategy and Response Plan (2024). AAH will continue to support the State Ministry of Health and SPHCDA to expand the provision of primary health care beyond urban areas to newly accessible areas for adequate health assistance where there are gaps not covered by any other partners to cover all population age and demographic groups, while providing standard packages for groups such as children under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLW), elderly, people with disability and special needs.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The program is committed to ensuring gender and social inclusivity throughout all its components, emphasizing the collection and reporting of data in a manner that reflects this commitment. The data will be meticulously disaggregated by various factors, including age, gender, disability, and other vulnerability markers, such as being the head or member of a single-parent household or an unaccompanied/orphaned child.Given the prevailing social dynamics, which often result in lower participation of women and girls in many communities, particular attention will be given to forming project teams with a composition of up to 70% female staff. This strategy aims to ensure that services are accessible to the targeted beneficiaries, taking into account the specific needs and perspectives of women and girls.To comprehensively understand and address gender-related issues, Action Against Hunger teams will conduct a baseline gender analysis/assessment in the initial quarter of program implementation. An interim progress assessment will follow at the end of the first year, and finally, an end-of-project assessment will be carried out at the project's closeout

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Participatory monitoring will ensure to get feedback from beneficiaries. This will be done through a feedback and complaint response mechanism established for all projects as a way of soliciting, receiving, processing and responding to the feedback and complaints. These are embedded in the monitoring system and beneficiaries and stakeholders are sensitized on this feedback mechanism and toll-free number so that they understand how to express concerns relating to the project. Feedback on ACF activities will also be solicited during post distribution monitoring and meetings with communities. Discussions with beneficiaries/ community leaders will be done regularly to address emerging issues and to ensure community obligations and participation in project implementation. This approach has helped to establish a relationship of trust between staff and communities in various projects and to improve the impact of the response.
