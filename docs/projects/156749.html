Project Summary

The project will respond to food security needs in Afrin district particularly Sharan, Bulbul, and Raju subdistricts through in-kind distribution of food assistance to IDPs and host community households on a monthly basis and for 3 sequent months, This intervention will contribute to the improvement of food security condition in targeted location and aims at supporting 12,500 beneficiaries with immediate life saving and life-sustaining food assistance.

Needs

According to the humanitarian needs overview, Syria is now the world’s most complex humanitarian crisis and the world’s largest displacement crisis. HNO 2018 also estimates that some 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance, of whom 10.1 million are in need for food security assistance. Furthermore, the 2018 Syria response plan indicates that as per 2018 estimates, 6.5 million people in Syria are food insecure, while a further 4 million people are at risk of becoming acutely food insecure.According to demographic data of the Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2018, there were an estimated 323,000 individuals living in Afrin, of whom 193,000 are classified as people in need and more than 125,000 as IDPs. On the 17th of March, the Turkish backed opposition forces captured Afrin city, the center of Afrin district. After the end of ‘Operation Olive Branch’ Bahar Organization’s MEAL team used the OCHA Inter-sectoral and Multi-sectoral questionnaire tools, WHO Rapid Health Assessment tool, WASH cluster WASH assessment tool in urban communities, and the rapid and in-depth nutrition assessment recruiting multiple key informant subjects throughout a series of personal and remote interviews. Bahar has recruited ten teams inside Afrin district to carry out the interviews. At that stage, the food markets in all sub-districts were not functioning, and main food commodities are unavailable in sufficient quantities in the nearest markets. The need assessment indicated that almost all people in all sub-districts are facing difficulties to accessing enough food due to: • Local production of food has decreased • Basic food commodities are not available in markets in a sufficient quantity. For this reserve allocatio Bahar has updated its data on targeted location in order to capture the common coping mechanisms and identify most vulnerable groups. Nowadays, the most common coping mechanisms used to cope with lack of food are: • Skipping meals • Eating less preferred food • Reducing meal size • Reducing the number of meals On the other hand, it was also reported that the most vulnerable families to accessing food currently are: • Recently arrived IDP families• IDP families in the area for an extended period (6 months or more). • Host or IDP families large in number of members (more than 7 members).• Host or IDP Families with very young and/or old members.• Host or IDP Families with disabled • Host or IDP families with women as head While, the portion of people who are facing challenges accessing food on a regular basis (for example, not eating 3 meals per day) is over between 41% to 50%. The main constraints for growing products in the district are: • Inaccessibility to agriculture lands (security reasons). • Agricultural tools/materials are not available at all.A secondary source providing information on Sharan indicates that most markets are not accessible and food commodities are at high prices when available.

Implementation of activities

The project will be initiated with local procurement of food rations through Bahar’s standard procurement procedures. The chosen food ration is the standard food basket content as per the Food Security Cluster guidance note which contains cereals, pulses/legumes, fat and mics. Food rations will be delivered by the supplier to Bahar’s warehouse in Afrin city on a monthly basis and distribution teams, as well as a project office, will escort the trucks heading to final distribution points in each targeted community. On the other hand, Bahar’s M&E teams will accompany distribution processes and will be monitoring the process for better control and application of improvements.Monthly reporting and information sharing with the FS cluster will be done via the 4Ws matrix to ensure a coordinated response and proper reporting of assistance.

Protection Risk Analysis

A number of mechanisms will be used in order to assure accountability to the affected population and other stakeholders. Firstly, prior to distribution onset, all beneficiaries will be made aware of the system and procedure for complaints and will be given an opportunity to ask further questions to clarify. Secondly, expectations of the complaints process will set so as to avoid frustration and disappointment if expected solutions are not implemented.One mechanism of the complaint is via the M&E manager’s direct phone number. All beneficiaries will be given the direct contact number (including Whatsapp) to directly voice any complaints. all complaints made via this method will be recorded and documented by the M&E manager, including how it was responded to and within what timeframe. All information from such complaints will be kept confidential, in strict accordance with data protection policies, and that information is only stored for as long as is needed. A second mechanism is a complaint made directly to the field office manager. The field office location together with the office managers full name and contact number will be given to each beneficiary. Once again, complaints made via this mechanism are also recorded in the same manner as with the first mechanism.A third mechanism is to directly complain to field officers or technical staff during any stage of the project. this can occur during any interaction between Bahar’s staff and the beneficiaries. Complaints via this channel will be handled in the same manner as the previous two channels.Fourthly, a feedback questionnaire will be offered to 10% of beneficiary upon completion of each of the major activities. These feedback surveys will be analysed by the M&E team and any formal complaint found will be forwarded to the M&E manager to classify and address accordingly.Furthermore, the local authorities/councils are also a channel that can be complained to by our beneficiaries, since they will be made fully aware of the projects objectives, targets, and expectations from all parties. So it also possible to receive complaints from the local authorities behalf of the beneficiaries, provided documentation is provided. These complaints will also be recorded, including response time frame, solution, and stored as long as necessary. When the complaint falls outside the control and responsibility of Bahar, this will be explained clearly to the complainant and, where possible and in agreement with the complainant, referred on to the appropriate organisation/body. Additionally, the following steps will be taken:1. Consultations will be conducted with community members in order to determine the appropriate distribution approach and set expectations of the project.2. Coordination and consultation with the local authorities.3. Bahar will manage complaints in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages. The Bahar field M&E manager contact details will be shared with all beneficiaries as a complaint mechanism. Moreover, the phone contact of Bahar's Project Manager will be shared with all the beneficiaries in order to report any problem they might face.3. Bahar takes all complaints seriously and acts upon them according to defined policies and processes mention previously. Moreover, the complaints that do not fall within the scope of the Bahar’s policies are referred to a relevant party in a manner consistent with good practice.4. Regular weekly/monthly reports by the project manager will be shared with the project coordinator in Gaziantep.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Border crossing points with Turkey will remain open.• In-kind food rations is made available.• No disruption of supply chain, transport and distribution of commodities.• No further deterioration in the security situation.Risks:• Over-registration of more powerful groups, leading to unequal distribution.• Crowding of beneficiaries around final distribution points.• Food needs to be stored and can, therefore, be stolen or looted.
