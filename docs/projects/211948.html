Project Summary

The non-governmental organization will respond to the educational needs of children affected by the conflict, with the water crisis, the outbreak of cholera and scabies in 3 governorates in the northeast of the country, increasing fears of infection in educational centers, and the earthquake that struck Turkey and northern Syria, affecting the already fragile educational infrastructure and making many schools are out of service. Education services, including early childhood development, will be provided to 11,657 beneficiaries in Al-Hasakah and Raqqa governorates.
There has been a 4 per cent increase in the PiN, compared to last year. This emphasizes the urgent need for emergency education services to support up to 7.246 million school aged children and teachers, including Children with Disabilities (CwD). The PiN comprises 6.9 million children 5 – 17 years (46 per cent girls, 54 per cent boys) and includes 230,000 education personnel, There are more than 2.4549 million Out of School Children (46 per cent girls, 54 per cent boys) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out. The governorates with the highest percentages of OoSC include Idleb (69 per cent), Ar-Raqqa (48 percent)with (48 per cent female and 52 per cent male).Syria HNO2024.
The NGO will link the children to education through a self-learning program. The NGOs will help children between the ages of 6 and 15 years to reform their basic education in learning points (children's tents) and community learning centers in Raqqa city and surrounding informal camps,
The education infrastructure was already insufficient prior to the earthquakes. An estimated 3,700 schools are in need of rehabilitation/repairs.51 In GoS-controlled areas, 890 schools are permanently damaged. Additionally, there are 470 schools damaged in north-east Syria.Syria HNO2024.
The NGO will also ensure that education assessment kits are distributed to both education workers and children to help them maintain attendance with their education. The NGO will focus on building the capacity of education staff to support quality education and optimal use of positive classroom management mechanisms. In order to maintain a safe environment and better educational outcomes for children, NGOs will rehabilitate schools according to standards to be safer and more sensitive to gender-based violence.
Scientific evidence suggests that the type of experiences a child has in the first five years of life plays a crucial role in later development, with 85-90% of brain development occurring during this period. Conflict situations usually expose children to extremely stressful and terrifying events that traumatize them. Young children growing up in crises are exposed to a range of complex risks that threaten their development and long-term well-being. In early childhood, research on the biology of stress shows how major adversity, such as extreme poverty, abuse, or neglect, can impair the structure of the developing brain and permanently put the body's stress response system on high alert.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The NGOs will provide educational activities including early childhood development activities in Raqqa and Al-Hasakah governorates in a fixed early childhood center and two mobile centers in Qamishli villages, which will include ALP and SLP non-formal education programs taking into account specific approaches to respond to the needs of children with weak educational levels and also child labor and children with disabilities. This will be done by providing formal education in a non-formal framework in 2 educational centers, and through a mobile team providing education in informal camps, in addition to rehabilitating schools and their affiliated water, sanitation and hygiene facilities, distributing teaching and learning supplies, and providing schools with security and safety supplies, providing educational centers with fuel during the winter, providing students with stationery, training school staff and management, providing them with educational aids, and forming PTAs in the targeted locations. Moreover, NGOs will carry out early childhood development activities targeting children and caregivers alike. Whether, and establish committees for early childhood development. NGOs will consider multiple modalities in implementation: fixed programming allows education services to be delivered sustainably, and mobile teams allow NGOs to respond flexibly to the needs of children who lack access to centers due to long distance or lack of on-site educational facilities. NGOs have a strong monitoring system that collects, analyzes and reports data regularly. It will consist of accountability and data quality control. In addition, the NGO will create tracking devices that will be used to track education program delivery outcome data to clients. Through the use of multiple tracking devices. NGOs will consider collecting digital data for greater accuracy and accountability. NGOs will inform their clients and the community of NGO clients' response mechanisms to allow them to communicate their feedback on program delivery, needs and concerns.Thus, the NGO will provide high-quality early childhood development programs to mitigate the impact of crisis and displacement, and help build the foundation for children's future academic success, health, prosperity and well-being. The NGO will help children aged 3-5 years through programs targeting children and supporting the development of their social emotional skills, in addition to programs targeting their caregivers as well.
