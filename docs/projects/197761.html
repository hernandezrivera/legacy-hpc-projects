Project Summary

The project will ensure vulnerable populations facing, or at risk of acute food insecurity are provided with urgent agriculture livelihood and nutrition assistance, adopting a humanitarian-development nexus and its contribution to peace to ensure the populations are equipped with appropriate capacities to better withstand and manage future shocks and risks.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project takes into consideration the fluidity of the current situation in northern Mozambique in terms of the movement of the IDPs and the need for flexibility in supporting returnees, as well as supporting Host Communities whose resources are currently overstretched. FAO through this project seeks to support 976 550 people to allow displaced populations, Host Communities and returnees to produce cereals and pulses during the short cycle and main planting season in 2023, prior to which fresh vegetables and beans can be immediately accessible as these can be planted throughout the year subject to availability of water. The activities will contribute to household food security and improvement of their nutrition status as well as also ensure self-reliance. For diversification, beneficiary households will be provided the necessary conditions and skills to restore fishery assets to serve IDPs, Host Communities and Returnees. The inclusion of host communities as beneficiaries of the intervention will contribute to the improvement of the fragile food security status of local communities, as well as help to mitigate social tensions between host communities and IDPs. Support will also be provided to restore and preserve livestock assets and production of vulnerable households. The beneficiaries will be provided with small stock, including the necessary conditions and skills to restore their production capacities.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The emergency agriculture input support package will include seed and planting material of early-maturing agro-ecologically appropriate varieties of maize, bean, cowpea and horticultural crops as well as agricultural tools (hoes, sickles, watering cans). This will ensure households take advantage of the winter and main summer agricultural seasons, while also enhancing production and productivity through extension support. The project will build on existing operational modalities and systems and depending on the prevailing security situation, in-kind distribution and or electronic vouchers will be used for beneficiaries to access agriculture inputs from local suppliers. Fisherfolk communities will be re-equipped with appropriate fishing gear for sustainable catch, while forming associations and councils that coordinate, provide oversight and enforce sustainable fisheries management. Provision of smallstock (chicken and goats) will be facilitated to meet immediate food needs, while rebuilding herds and flocks. Livestock assets will be safeguarded through animal treatment and vaccination campaigns.Proposed project will foster a combination of the following tailored activities targeted for coastal and non-coastal districts:1. Agriculture inputs distribution (in kind/e-Voucher)2. Certified seed production3. Community based seed banks 4. Promotion of conservation agriculture (CA) 5. Promotion of post-harvest technologies 6. Provision of small livestock (goats, chicken) 7. Restoration of fishery value chain (cold chain and preservation technologies, fishing gear, boats)8. Provision of continuous technical assistance9. Promotion of sustainable livelihoods and income diversification - Pilot production of honey.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Addressing gender-based violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse is critical given its prevalence in conflict-affected areas and its potential for devastating the lives and livelihoods of affected people. Specifically, in the agriculture sector, such impacts can lessen the productivity of survivors, diminish the resilience capacities of individuals to sustain their livelihoods as a result of illness, injury, stigma, discrimination and violation of their rights. Those at risk include not only women and girls, but also men and boys, elderly, unaccompanied children, disabled, minorities and displaced persons. The project contributes to protection of individuals from GBV, sexual exploitation and abuse as part and parcel of its efforts to restore and promote sustainable livelihoods. Protection is mainstreamed throughout the project, ensuring at-risk group’s specific needs are taken into consideration and risks mitigated, where and when possible. FAO will work closely with other UN agencies and organizations such IOM and UNHCR that undertake protection activities in the same communities where the project will be implemented.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FAO applies the accountability to affected populations (AAP) framework comprising of seven commitments: Strengthening leadership and governance to embedded good practices;• Greater and more routine transparency;• Feedback and timely response;• Fair and representative population;• Accountability to affected communities mainstreamed in design, monitoring and evaluation;• Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse; and • Collaboration with partners.Targeting involves community engagement through participatory rural appraisal to allow beneficiaries, as well as non-beneficiaries to better understand why they were or were not targeted based on pre-determined selection criteria to help reduce potential internal conflicts. FAO has developed a monitoring system to strengthen AAP, which includes on-site monitoring during the distribution of inputs to obtain rapid feedback from beneficiaries. Post-distribution monitoring assessments also provide feedback on perceived positive aspects of assistance, as well as shortcomings, and the feedback informs alterations to the design and implementation of activities.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

While the project does not primarily target people with disabilities (PwD), consideration will be made to ensure inclusivity, leaving no one behind. PwD will be prioritized during beneficiary selection process including ensuring their participation in the decision making process, including post implementation monitoring phase.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

FAO Environmental and Social Management Guidelines (ESMG) ensures projects meet expected outcomes while safeguarding environmental and social standards. These guidelines facilitate early and systematic identification and assessment of Environmental and Social (E&S) risks and integration of the management of these risks into the project cycle. This project has gone through screening by FAO’s Lead Technical Officers (LTOs) to rule out risks/negative of the proposed interventions based on available informing and using the E&S checklist. FAO Environmental and Social screening Checklist questions are intended to guide the LTO in classifying the project as either “Low”, “Moderate”, or “High” risk based on FAO Environmental and Social Standards. This project is classified as “Low” risk, namely – the project has no or minimal potential negative environmental and/or social impacts, either upstream or downstream. The project will not be controversial in terms of interests of stakeholders, and in case of minimal impacts, the risk remains low because there are widely known and readily available good practices that will be used to address those impacts, and track record that implementers of the project (e.g., farmers and fishermen) know how to apply and do engage in these good practices.
