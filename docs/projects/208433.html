Project Summary

The project will provide case management, S/GBV response, MHPSS and socioeconomic reintegration services to unaccompanied and separated children including S/GBV survivors and children formerly associated with armed groups and armed forces and their caregivers.
According to the HRP 2023, (Child Protection needs have increased in 2023 due to the sustained military offensive leading to the high influx of people from NSAG, inflation, extreme poverty rates and relocations of IDPs, etc. Priority needs include case management services for victims of abuse, neglect, and violence, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative community care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), socio-economic reintegration of former CAAG and other vulnerable children addressing child marriage, trafficking, child labour, low birth registration rates, social norms affecting children’s rights and critical support to children with disabilities.)
The Northeast Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview HNO indicates also 2.1 million people will need child protection services in 2023 with 13 LGAs in Adamawa, 21 in Borno, and 14 in Yobe having the highest severity ranking of child protection needs due to the absence of child protection systems and community-based safety nets, causing 1.3 million children to need child protection services. The project will contribute to the HRP target of reaching 1.5m people in need (1,050,000 children, 53% girls and 450,000 caregivers, 53% women) by supporting boys, girls, adolescents, youths and caregivers to re-establish a sense of safety and normality through:
I.) Social Economic Reintegration for Children Associated with Armed Groups.
II.) Case management/GBV services.
III.) Mental Health and Psychosocial Support.

1 - Needs

The 2024 Northeast Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicates 1.8 million People in Need (PiN) of critical protection services including case management, family tracing/ reunification/ alternative care, Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS), and socioeconomic reintegration of Children formerly Associated with Armed Groups (CAAG). At the core of these needs are abuse, neglect, and violence against children, driven by a prevalence of child marriage, trafficking, child labor, social norms affecting children's rights, and other life-threatening experiences that impact the immediate and long-term development of children. Most abuses are reported to be perpetrated by family members or relatives of children, further aggravating the psychological effects of the experience especially survivors of sexual violence. Mental health challenges and psychological distress among children and adults remain unattended and, on the rise due to prolonged exposure to economic hardship, stress, and traumatic experiences. Many children are living with this challenge in unsupportive family systems due to poor relationships and a lack of skills by caregivers to manage the situation.The Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CPAoR) targets to reach 1.2 million women, men, girls, and boys with protection services across 41 prioritized LGAs in the Northeast: 10 LGAs in Adamawa (Michika, Mubi North, Mubi South, Numan, Song, Yola North, Hong, Madagali, Gombi, and Demsa.), 19 LGAs in Borno (Abadam, Bama, Chibok, Damboa, Gubio, Guzamala, Gwoza, Jere, Kala/Balge, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, MMC, Marte, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala, Biu, and Askira/Uba.), and 12 LGAs in Yobe (Bade, Bursary, Fune, Geidam, Gujba, Gulani, Jakusko, Tarmua, Yunusari, Yusufari, Potiskum and Damaturu) with protection services including Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) for separated and unaccompanied children, community-based alternative care for separated and at-risk children, appropriate needs-based care and support for children with disabilities, comprehensive multi-sectorial case management support to address abuse, neglect, exploitation, child labor & child marriage for children at risk, birth registration and certification for children under 5 years, life skills education for adolescent girls and boys, age-appropriate structured recreational and creative psychosocial support activities for children, and psychosocial services/ positive parenting lessons for caregivers of vulnerable children.

2 - Activities

The response is designed around the three strategic objectives of the 2024 HRP and targets 15,132 individuals (2775M, 1255W, 6142B, 4960G). Prevention and response actions will be carried out to address the needs of vulnerable men, women, boys, and girls through case management, MHPSS services, and gender-based violence prevention and response. In line with the Sector Strategic Objective 1. (Ensure timely access to preventive and comprehensive quality response service for boys and girls including children with disabilities at risk of abuse, discrimination, exploitation, neglect, and violence.) A robust case management system including alternative care and family reintegration services will be established in project communities to promptly respond to identified protection cases and facilitate the restoration of family links for unaccompanied and separated children. The response will also contribute to Sector Strategic Objective 2: (Enhance the well-being of conflict-affected children, adolescents, and caregivers through promoting constructive social norms and behaviors that aimed at eliminating violence, abuse, and detrimental traditional practices within parents, caregivers, children, and communities.) A GBV action group led by men, women, boys, and girls will support the monitoring of GBV trends and advise the response priority areas however, targeted awareness on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, child protection, and gender-based violence will be incorporated into the community action plans developed by the communities. Identified critical stakeholders will be trained on expressed capacity needs, based on a capacity needs assessment procedure. The Sector Strategic Objective 3: (Ensure that boys and girls affected by conflicts, including those who have experienced grave violations of child rights, receive meaningful support through community-based reintegration services.) will be addressed by SCI, Partners, and community members through a community-led identification process SCI and partners will facilitate access to specialized services on a case by case basis while linking affected men, women, boys, and girls to essential services including food, education, health, and nutrition services within and outside the response.The key activities to be implemented are:1. Conduct GBV awareness and sensitization on child protection issues, prevention of sexual exploitation and Abuse, and existing referral pathways for girls, boys, women, and men.2. Provide dignity kits to survivors of gender-based violence (boys and girls)3. Train stakeholders (adolescent boys and girls, teachers, healthcare staff, law enforcement, traditional and community leaders, staff, and field volunteers) on GBV prevention and response.4. Establish safe spaces for women and girls.5. Engage and train Outreach volunteers on GBV awareness, monitoring, and reporting procedures.6. Conduct psychosocial support and safe family sessions for caregivers and children.7. Provide specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial support to unaccompanied, separated, and at-risk children.8. Provide counseling services to address distress among unaccompanied, separated, and at-risk children.9. Recruit and train psychosocial support facilitators on PFA, Life skills, and child-friendly space (CFS) methodology10. Procure and replenish CFS materials for psychosocial support.11. Family tracing and reunification services for Unaccompanied and separated children.12. Provided comprehensive multi-sectorial case management support (abuse, neglect, exploitation, child labor, and child marriage) for Unaccompanied, Separated, and at-risk children.13. Conduct focused group discussions (FGD) with men, women, boys, and girls to ascertain the sociocultural and economic factors leading to abuse, and exploitation.14. Provide socio-economic reintegration support for CAAG and SGBV survivors.
