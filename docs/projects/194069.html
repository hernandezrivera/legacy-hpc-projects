Project Summary

The aim of this Health project is to deliver lifesaving critical health services to IDPs, returnees and host communities through clinics, EMOCs, and mobile clinics to reduce high rates of morbidity and mortality. The total target beneficiaries is 450,000 individuals (88198 men, 91800 women, 137702 girls, 132300 boys). A comprehensive healthcare service package will be delivered through Primary Health Care Centers (PHCC) including support with supplies and equipment such as essential drugs, medical supplies, and furniture to promote and achieve infection protection and control. In collaboration with SMOHs, seconded healthcare staff will be identified and supported with monthly incentives and trainings to better support health care management. EPI campaigns will be supported across target areas, vaccinators will be trained to improve quality and increase coverage. CARE will work closely with community members and Youth Associations to support community health education, outreach, and mobilization, including on protection and GBV. Protection and Youth Committees will provide integrated GBV awareness raising and sensitization in targeted areas using household visits, community leader’s meetings, and public events. CARE will establish and train community Protection Committees to conduct community sensitization on PSEA, GBV risk mitigation and prevention. CARE will also provide SRH services including training service providers/midwives, provision of essential drugs and supplies, clean delivery kits, and EMOC. For strengthening the wider health information and management, CARE will adopt a well-structured health information system (HIS) to capture data and submit weekly Epidemiology reports to SMOH and WHO

1 - Needs

The health situation in Sudan remained in continues deterioration in 2022 due to a couple of factors including inflation, flooding, and localized violence such the recent tribal conflicts in Blue Nile, West Kurdufan, West and South Darfur. A multi sector-needs assessment conducted by CARE in December 2021 (South Darfur) and April 2022 (East Darfur) shows that only 36.4% of the population in South Darfur have access to a health facility, and in East Darfur 71.6% reported that they do not have enough money to receive health services. There is a severe lack of specialized doctors available in both areas and as a result there is heavy reliance on trained or traditional midwives. Women and girls of reproductive age do not receive postnatal care and 82% (SD)/84% (ED) do not receive clean delivery kits (CARE 2021/2022). It is estimated that 1.5 million women and girls lack access to reproductive health services along with GBV case management in Sudan (HRP 2021). WHO reported that some health facilities throughout the country are unable to offer Expanded Program of Immunization (EPI) due to lack of medical supplies (11%), lack of staff (39%), and lack of equipment (24%) (Aug 2022). EPI depends primarily on implementation by international organizations for almost all vaccines administered in Sudan. Within the last five years Sudan has experienced outbreaks of cholera, chikungunya, rift valley fever, malaria, dengue, diphtheria, vaccine derived poliovirus type 2, and hepatitis E (WHO 2022). Health related risks are increasing in 2022 with possible more deterioration in 2023, some indicators of diareses outbreaks reported in Sudan such as Monkeypox, Dengue fever, and recently suspected case of Ebola. The current week healthcare system considered not capable to respond to any health emergencies unless an external support is available. The decline in service provision by the public sector is forcing the population to seek health services at the private sector which is expensive unaffordable among displaced communities rely solely on humanitarian support. The increase of medicine and treatment cost attributed to projected increase in inflation and economic crisis, poor investment in infrastructure, inter-communal conflicts, and potential new refugee influx.• Since the beginning of the armed conflict is Sudan the health service provision across the country and specifically in the country’s capital Khartoum has been heavily impacted with discontinuity of several critical programs due to violence and attacks on health, disruption of supply chain, and poor accessibility by the population to facilities that can provide needed services. According to the FMOH, around 12,000 people receive renal dialysis sessions and treatments for renal failure in Khartoum. The majority of the patients are currently deprived from such services to due to lack of supplies and ongoing violence, only 330 cases managed to seek medical assistance in Gaziera and Gadaref states. Disruptions affected the power grid, water piping systems, fuel availability, and mobility of health staff rendered around 70% of the health facilities across Khartoum out of service. The main maternity hospital ( Un Durman hospital) is not operational risking further increase in maternity mortality. The situation is further exacerbated by lack of blood banking and ambulance services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provide comprehensive primary health care (PHC) and reproductive health (RH) services through static health facilities and mobile medical units (MMU).The Comprehensive PHC services include, Mother, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) services; through provision of Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) that address the common childhood diseases, and provision of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), which address maternal and neonatal health issues, through provision of Antenatal Care (ANC), Postnatal Care (PNC), Family planning (FP), Deliveries and prevention and syndromic treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs)). Management and prevention of communicable diseases, management of noncommunicable diseases, including trauma care. Health education and behavioral change communications at health facility level. Referral of gender-based violence (GBV) survivors will occur, with their informed consent through focal points who are trained in Clinical care of Sexual Assault Survivor (CCSAS) and are aware of confidentiality as well as health and protection need of survivors. A roving gynecologist will coach and mentor healthcare providers on CCSAS. Care will provide essential medicines, medical supplies, consumables and equipment for management and prevention of communicable diseases at the supported health facilities and mobile teams. Care will provide training to health workers and CHWs on various health topics related to prevention and management of communicable diseases including but not limited to cholera and diarrheal diseases. Care will support surveillance system of communicable diseases through strengthening the Early Warning, Alert Reporting System (EWARS) at the supported health facilities, MMUs and communities. Care will also focus on preventive measures to decrease incidence and morbidity associated with non-communicable diseases by providing support for noncommunicable disease treatment through the provision of basic medicines for hypertension and diabetes and medicines/consumables for injuries and trauma. Care will contribute to the promotion of behavior to prevent communicable diseases which include pneumonia, diarrhea, ARI, measles, STDs, TB, leishmaniasis, Dengue, Rift Valley and Chikungunya. The support includes, provision of medical supplies, essential medicines, consumables, equipment, furniture, stationery, cleaning materials as well as provision of basic and refresher trainings to midwives and health workers on basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC), and minimum initial services package (MISP) of reproductive health. Care’s support will enable these health facilities and mobile teams to provide antenatal care (ANC), post-natal care, family planning including short term and LARCs (long-acting reversible contraceptives), and management of sexually transmitted infections through syndromic approach. These measures will include rehabilitation of toilets and washrooms at static facilities, addressing sanitation needs at mobile facility operation points, and establishment or rehabilitation and operation of Health Waste Zones where needed
