Project Summary

The ongoing insurgency in Northeast Nigeria is widely reported as the main driver of displacement. This insurgency has been followed by communal clashes, disasters and, in recent times, inter-communal conflicts. These clashes involve confrontations between ethnic and religious groups, often triggered by land and border issues, and increasing tensions between herder communities and sedentary farmers.
The majority of IDPs displaced by the insurgency are located in Bama, Damboa, Gwoza, Jere, Konduga, Maiduguri M.C., Monguno and Ngala local government areas (LGAs) of Borno state. The populations displaced as a result of communal clashes are concentrated in the north-western part of Adamawa, the southern part of Borno, Taraba, Gombe, and western Bauchi states. According to IOM DTM mobility tracking, while communal clashes accounted for 4 per cent of the displacements before the year 2021, they were cited as the main trigger of displacement by 35 per cent of the IDPs displaced in 2023.
IOM considers the insufficient capacity of the host communities in areas of return to be a direct risk of the overall progressive resolution of displacement in the Northeast. This project seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience of conflict and flood-affected populations in Northeast Nigeria by restoring infrastructure, promoting early recovery, including through economic revitalization restoration of livelihoods, strengthening government capacity, promoting social cohesion and peaceful coexistence, and facilitating resilience-building. Early recovery and livelihood interventions under this project will include Cash for Livelihood, Cash for Work (CFW) climate-smart businesses including Individual Livelihood Assistance (ILA) and other income-generating activities (IGA) in the form of vocational skills training, apprenticeships, and business support assistance for start-up or scale up as a means to achieving peaceful reintegration and social cohesion through engagement with various populations on the move. Through the adoption of cash-based interventions, IOM strives to assist affected populations in a more dignified manner, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. Government plays a crucial role in developing and establishing stable mechanisms for economic and social development. The level of management capacity is a key element of success; IOM strives to promote cross-sectoral collaboration and systems to strengthen support and localization – the support will be guided by need assessment with the key stakeholders and the targeted government might include the economic recovery ministries, environment, and climate change.

1 - Needs

An estimated 1.3 million people in the BAY states (of whom 2.2 million are internally displaced) are in acute need of humanitarian and protection assistance. The October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) identified 19 LGAs (2 – Adamawa, 10 – Borno, and 7 – Yobe states) to be currently under a food crisis while 48 LGAs are projected to be under food crisis across the BAY states from June to August 2024. This is similar to the previous year’s analysis where an estimated 4.4 million people were food insecure (CH phase 3-5) during the lean season (June-August). As of June 2023, the DTM identified a total of 2,295,534 IDPs in 471,346 households and 2,075,257 returnees in 341,895 households. The IDP population included IDPs in camps and camp-like settings (921,201 individuals or 40%) and IDPs residing in host communities (1,374,333 individuals or 60%). The returnee population included IDP returnees (1,866,796 individuals or 90%) and returnees from abroad (208,461 individuals or 10%). The increasing number of returnees and reintegrating populations calls for adequate capacity to cope with and strengthen the receiving communities. It makes already-existing socioeconomic divides worse, further taxing a populace that is already among the most vulnerable and impoverished in the zone. Additionally, social cohesiveness had been severely damaged, making social contact more difficult. This was made worse by a culture of violence associated with land, property, and community issues as well as reprisals for acts of violence during conflicts. The critical needs for crisis-affected groups remain livelihood support and job creation, access to credit and vocational training, support to productive assets, and linkages to the public and private sectors for value chain addition and sustainability. Revitalization of the economy through the restoration of markets and critical infrastructures, such as hospitals, schools, roads, and waste management systems are also key in achieving a voluntary, sustainable, and dignified return. Community cohesion becomes essential when disputes cause communities to become divided. Effective, open, and inclusive local governance systems can support the re-establishment of social cohesion and guarantee equitable access to resources and services which IOM strives to contribute to. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods, promotion of inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in HC, returnees, and host communities, support the restoration of local governance administration and services, and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Specific measures to restore livelihoods include short-term employment creation, providing market-based skills, including vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women, derived from community consultation.

2 - Activities

IOM seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience building of conflict and disaster-affected populations in Northeast Nigeria and to set a preparedness pace for reoccurring flooding/other natural hazards through a series of community support interventions. As people continue to return to their places of habitual residence from before the insurgency or reintegrate into their new community, there is an urgent and significant need to restore infrastructure and promote early recovery, including through economic revitalization, restoration of livelihoods, and resilience building. Livelihood interventions under this project will include cash for Livelihood, Cash for Work (CFW), climate-smart businesses including Individual Livelihood Assistance (ILA) and other income-generating activities (IGA) in the form of vocational skills training, apprenticeships, and business support assistance for start-up or scale up. IOM will encourage the Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA), facilitate their registration, and provide seed funding for the sustainability of the group. Cash-based interventions are a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. They can also promote peaceful co-existence between IDP, returnee and host communities, as well-designed and run cash-based interventions have a multiplier effect, jointly engaging all members of the community and directly benefitting the local economy. Vocational training, apprenticeships, and other skills enhancement activities, along with Public-Private Partnerships, will be encouraged to ensure the sustainability of the intervention. Women, youth and girls will be engaged and trained in market-driven skills as per the outcome of a thorough labor market assessment to be conducted as part of the project to create human capital as necessary for the early recovery phase. Each targeted beneficiary of vocational skills training and apprenticeship will also receive business start-up capital to establish small-scale businesses. CFW will support activities such as comprehensive upgrades and equipping of key public infrastructure and will contribute directly to recovery in conflict and flood-affected areas.In addition, IOM will work towards enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization, and development partners, through a people-centered approach. To supplement economic recovery interventions, IOM will additionally support the restoration of social cohesion and local governance mechanisms in the areas of intervention. To this end, technical and logistical support will be provided to community peace committees to address social cohesion concerns. Furthermore, to ensure protection is guaranteed in recovery efforts, IOM will build the capacity of law enforcement bodies, including traditional/religious leaders, to prevent and respond to gender-based violence (GBV) in compliance with human rights standards and through a victim-centered approach. The intervention will also be guided by the Labour Market Assessment alongside other needs assessments in the locations of intervention. IOM will align its response plan to the four strategic objectives of the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector as follows:1. Support livelihood stabilization;2. Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in host communities, returnees and host communities;3. Support the restoration of local governance administration and services;4. Improve access to infrastructure and basic social services through rehabilitation and reconstruction of public service facilities.
