Project Summary

AL Resala Foundation (RF), aim through the proposal, to continue provide health services in RF three PHCs is located in Aleppo, Idleb (Alresala, Afrin and Al Manarah). RF has worked to provide primary health care services that are the cornerstones of health services in any health system. These services include early detection of diseases, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation and promotion of health behaviors as well as reproductive health services for women through the provision of services Prenatal care, periodic child screening, immunization, postnatal or abortion services, family planning services, women's.
The main project components include
• Primary health care services (PHCs) facilities, and mobile clinic for IDPs and nearby resident populations of Afrin. Al Resala, Bulbul, Sharon, and Al Manarah camp
• provide Mental health need psychosocial support “PSS” for IDPs and nearby residents the IDPs in Afrin. Al Resala, Bulbul, Sharan, and Al Manarah by Conduct capacity building training for mental health, physical rehabilitation
More specifically the project components
1. Continuation of primary health care services for two PHCs and two mobile clinics. level 2 based on the essential package of services. The two stationary pre-fabricated PHCs, and mobile clinics will continue to provide services for target population of 51093 IDPs and 35,504HH. The PHCs, and the mobile clinics will provide basic general, three centers reproductive and pediatric health care services and continue to be part of the functional referral system to secondary level health care. It will also continue providing public health awareness for preventive measures through outreach, health education and disease screenings by community health care workers (CHW)
2. Continuation of Mental health care services of PHCs, and the mobile clinics on the essential package of services for target population of 44,675 IDPs and 28,563 HH estimated number of people with chronic illness or psychological condition 2044 people. stationary prefabricated PHCs, and mobile clinics. The centers will provide mental health and psychosocial interventions in the form of psychotropic medication, basic emotional support, By Improving the access to services and support seeking behavior of mental health patients by increasing community awareness about mental health issues and decreasing stigma, improve access to services through awareness-raising initiatives and anti-stigma campaigns

4 - Needs

The most commonly reported barriers to accessing healthcare were a lack of transportation to health facility, and the high cost of transportation. Primary care facilities were the most commonly reported types of facilities available in the assessed communities, while public hospitals were most commonly reported as being accessed in nearby communitiesensuring that health services continue to be provided to people in need in the three communities in NW Syria. This is necessary to provide essential life-sustaining and life-saving primary healthcare services to the increasing numbers of IDPs / host communities in Idleb and Aleppo. Besides the physical trauma, mental trauma continues to be reported. An average of 14% of population across the governorates of Idleb, Aleppo and Raqqa reported a high level of anxiety or depression on a daily basis, with 73% stating the crisis as the cause. Thus the project will improve by training of MHPSS for health workers at the health facilities, Mental health orientation for community members, and Psychological First Aid training for community. disrupted water networks and waste management, combined with food insecurity and continued high levels of displacement, leave millions vulnerable to epidemic-prone diseases and place heavy demands on surveillance networks and immunization activities. Chronic shortages of health care workers, particularly in Northeast Syria, prevent full functionalization of health facilities and essential services with reproductive health and maternal health services heavily impacted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. Discrimination and inequality in providing services to particular groups or individuals. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High.2. Harassment and sexual abuse during consultations. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High. 3. Limited access for vulnerable (pregnant women, singles women and PWD) to physically access the PHCs and beneﬁt from Services. Probability: Moderate. Risk severity: High. 4. Abduction for a ransom of personnel during travel to the health facilities. Probability: Moderate. Risk severity: High. 5. Military attack on health facilities resulting in injuries and death of facility personnel and patients/escorts. Probability: Low. Risk severity: High.Mitigation measures:RF Field coordinators will conduct spot-check visits to the PHC. Exit interview will be held with the beneﬁciaries to verify the equal and appropriate treatment by staff. The conﬁdentiality of complaint mechanism will be enhanced including a Whatsapp and phone hotline. RF will continue announcing the independent reporting site, Ethics Point, for maintaining anonymous complaints and will provide awareness sessions for beneficiaries on using this site. RF will conduct training for staff on PSEA and RF’s Code of Conduct. All personnel will sign and commit to RF's PSEA, child protection and Code of Conduct policies and RF will adopt a very strict termination and dismissal procedures towards any misconduct. RF will continue to improve the PHC structure to be disability-friendly and provide training for staff on dealing with PWDs. RF will try to activate an ambulance system for vulnerable people or provide transportation for patients in need. RF will complement this project with its existing CH project to support the referral system from the community to the PHCs. Monitoring and Evaluation surveys will be conducted by RF Health department including the provided services and staff treatment including PSEA questions. A services map for MHPSS and GBV support
