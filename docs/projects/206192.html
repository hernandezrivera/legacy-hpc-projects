Project Summary

CAO has outlined several approaches to ensure that 2024 children, adolescents, and women in South Sudan are safe and protected from violence, exploitation, abuse, neglect, and harmful practices.
To achieve this goal, CAO plans to integrate Protection, child protection, and GBV with other clusters, such as Health, WASH, Nutrition, and FSL. CAO will expand case management for child survivors of SGBV and strengthen partnerships between education and protection, encouraging them to work together to improve children's psychosocial well-being and protective environment in schools. CAO aims to reach 75,000 with 60% females and 40% Males including, Children, adolescents, and adults from the IDPs, Returnees, and Host communities across the seven counties with protection interventions.
To facilitate these interventions, the CAO will establish Integrated Child Help Desks at service points, including schools, health centers, and distribution points. They will also strengthen the community-based Child Protection Committee network (CBCPC/N) and focus on building the capacity of frontline protection and child protection/GBV workers while enhancing community volunteers/networks to expand coverage in underserved areas.
CAO recognizes the need for scale-up of PSS activities in CFS, Schools/TLS to build resilience and enhance the well-being of children who have gone through difficulties. They will strengthen community-led innovations by community structures to improve ownership and sustainability of prevention purposes.
There is also a need to provide GBV prevention and risk mitigation measures to reduce the suffering of GBV survivors. CAO will implement primary GBV prevention programs, such as Engaging Men and Boys through Accountable Practice (EMAP), Women Economic and Social Empowerment (EAE), and Community Care Programme, to address harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality. They will prioritize integrating GBV actions and survivor support across sectoral interventions, along with dignity kit distribution, distribution of fuel-efficient stoves, conducting joint safety audits, and implementing the recommendations.
CAO will focus on the following thematic areas: Protection Assistance, Child protection in humanitarian action, including gender-based violence services, grave child rights violations, UASC/FTR, MHPSS, Cash assistance and GBV response, prevention, and risk mitigation.

1. Needs

South Sudan is facing multiple challenges, including conflict, violence, flooding, hyperinflation, and COVID-19. Vulnerable populations like children, gender-based violence survivors, and conflict victims are disproportionately affected. This has led to declining health, living standards, and coping mechanisms. In 2023, 9.4 million people will require humanitarian assistance, 500,000 more than in 2022. 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys, need assistance. Persons with disabilities make up about 15% of those in need. The persistent violations of children’s rights remain a critical concern in South Sudan. Children and adolescents are often targets of abduction, recruitment into armed forces or armed groups, family separation, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation, and violence. From January to September 2022, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 302 grave violations affecting 246 children (141 boys and 105 girls). Boys between 13 and 17 years old are significantly at risk of conflict-related violence, including abduction, killing, and recruitment. A total of 66 children (64 boys and two girls) were verified as victims of recruitment. Some 65 children (55 boys, ten girls) were killed/maimed, 45 children (22 boys, 20 girls) were abducted, and 73 were victims of rape/sexual violence. An estimated 3.7 million children, adolescents, and caregivers in South Sudan are most at risk of violence, exploitation, and abuse, requiring sustained child protection services. There are severe protection concerns for women and children, especially SGBV and child protection. The large number of vulnerable women-headed households within conservative and patriarchal communities has heightened the acute exposure of women, girls, and children to protection threats., including sexual violence, SEA, Intimate partner violence, economic violence, forced marriage, and harmful traditional practices. Ideally, the IDPs, women, men, boys, and girls depend on the community for essential resources, which causes mental distress. Women and girls fear to report rape cases due to the weak judicial systems. This puts them in a position of increased vulnerability in an environment where the only access to justice is via community-based fairness adjudicated by the male boma chief or administrator, who often does not favor women and girls. As the number of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) increases, there is a need for life-saving child protection and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of more than 3 million vulnerable girls, boys, and their families. Integration of gender and protection support services in education, psychosocial, sexual, and reproductive health services, and disability inclusion. CAO will continue to address and respond to multiple protection risks faced by children and adolescents through the expansion of comprehensive case management services, including family tracing, reunification, and alternative care services for unaccompanied and separated children and other at-risk children, roll out the held protection information management systems, provision of quality MHPSS based on the needs. Structured MHPSS and referrals for specialized preventative and responsive child protection programming will be promoted through established child protection and GBV case management referral pathways, the use of PSS static and mobile teams in collaboration with the health, education, and other relevant sectors in hard-to-reach areas of Fangak, Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Uror, Akobo, Ulang, and Nasir counties in Jonglei State and Upper Nile. CAO will monitor and report on grave violations against children and strengthen community-based reintegration of children released from armed forces or armed groups.

2. Project activities

Protection:Develop harmonized key messaging informed by findings from protection monitoring analysis.Develop key protection awareness raising messages and information by working with communities of affected populations and protection partners. Implement activities targeted at identifying and referring persons with specific needs (PSNs) by working with protection partners. Establish or enhance existing community-based structures and implement activities that constitute alternatives to displacement by working with protection partners and communities of affected people. Enhance community resilience, PSEA, peace-building activities, and training through the AGD approach by working with protection partners, development and peace actors, authorities, and existing community-based structures. Child Protection:Provide children with disabilities and those at risk with individually specialized case management services and MHPSS, including cash-based assistance, by working with protection partners and communities. Identify and document UASC and provide them with family tracing and reunification services by working with protection partners and communities of affected people. Update child protection referral pathways by working with protection partners and communities of affected people. Provide children with disabilities and those at risk with PSS services, especially in child safe spaces, by working with protection partners and communities of affected people. Develop and implement child protection-relevant messages and awareness-raising activities by working with protection partners, authorities, and existing community-based structures. Organize and implement CPiE training for community leaders or humanitarian and government staff by working with protection partners, authorities, and existing community-based structures.Gender-Based Violence: Strengthen GBV awareness through visits, interventions, campaigns, and mass media.Strengthen primary GBV prevention interventions. Strengthen specialized GBV response services and referral pathways by working with GBV partners.Strengthen psychosocial support service provision through capacity building and collaboration with the MHPSS working group. Strengthen the reach and coverage of dignity kits distribution and programming. Integrate Cash and Voucher Assistance into GBV programming by working with the Cash Working Group. Scale up the reach of fuel-efficient stoves, revamp the GBV AoR Fuel-Efficient Stove Task Force, and expand the availability of women and girls-friendly spaces. Strengthen GBV referral pathways through conducting GBV service mapping and establishing/updating GBV SOP. Provide capacity-building training on GBV integration in sectoral responses.
