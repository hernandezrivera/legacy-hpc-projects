Project Summary

PCDCR targets children & adults affected by conflict related violence & violations in the area C of northern West Bank. The project addresses the urgent needs & bad psychosocial status of children & adults in area C of northern West Bank resulting from recent escalation of hostilities, continuing COVID19 along with the protracted protection crisis & occupation as stated in HNO2022 & HRP2021. Such coercive environment causes negative mental wellbeing, negative coping mechanisms, & explosion to harassment. The context has become even more urgent due to worse social, economic, psychological, & political situation. The most eight critical communities of area C in the northern of West Bank are targeted based on HNO2022 & OCHA2021 statistics where people particularly children face many conflict-related protection challenges at different levels. The situation deterioration grows due to Israeli annexation plans & unstable escalation of hostilities
The project participates in fulfilling the needs identified in HNO2022 in regard to child protection. The project contributes to provide protection response mechanisms to children & adults affected by conflict in area C of northern West Bank through raising awareness interventions, PSS(group counselling), & PSS (individual counselling). Both genders are targeted in reasonable percentages with a special attention to the engagement of people living with disability (physical & mental). In addition to protection, gender, PSEA, disability, the age is mainstreamed also through targeting affected boys, girls, men, & women. The gender is addressed through identifying gender needs, impact, roles of girls, boys, men, & women e.g.early marriage for girls, labor for boys, more responsibilities on women, more economic pressure on men, & different conflict impact on each of them will be highlighted to consider their gender variant needs. The project goal is attained through the following strategic objectives: 1.The wellbeing of children & adults who have been affected by conflict related violence & is improved through PSS services. 2.The knowledge of children & caregivers who have been affected by conflict-related violence is enhanced on child protection issues
The most affected 8areas targeted in the project cover Beita & Huwwara in Nablus governorate, Kafr Qaddum & Azzun in Qalqilya governorate, Ya’bad, & Birqin in Jenin governorate, & Almaleh & Yarza in the northern Jordan Valley in Tubas Governorate

1 - Needs

Children and adults across Palestine suffer from the protracted protection crisis and conflict-related violence in the West Bank especially in the marginalized and Seam Zone areas as area C. The situation becomes worse with the continuing COVID-19 pandemic and the unstable escalation of hostilities where high rates of protests, clashes and arrests were occurred. According to HNO 2022, 36% of West Bankers are at risk of conflict and violence as follows: 81,360 children (41,496 boys & 39,864 girls) and 48,000 caregivers across the West Bank are in need of child protection and MHPSS services. Between July 2020 and June 2021, 7,948 children were affected in the oPt, 81 were killed and 1,248 were injured with the highest numbers among boys rather than girls (HNO2022). During 2021 escalation of hostilities, 13546 were injured and 72 were killed in the West Bank (OCHA dashboard). In addition, HRP 2020 assured that 2,45 million are need where 50% of them are children and 928K are affected by conflict-related violence. Accordingly, the direct violence and threat against children and adults in area C of the West Bank along with the consequences of COVID-19 pandemic causes devastating psychosocial wellbeing for children and their parents. For instance, during July& August 2021, SAWA has responded to around 3784 children in need on PSSThis coercive environment negatively affects the children lifestyle and coping mechanisms leading to school dropout, child labor in too bad situations exposing to severe forms of violence, and harassment. 8% of households in the West Bank has at least one child working and they reported their security concerns for boys and girls related to verbal harassment, sexual harassment, being robbed, kidnapped, and threated with violence especially among girls as indicated in REACH 2021 MSNA. PCBS chair press 2021 indicted that the school dropout rate in the scholastic year 2019/2021 was 1,309 students with higher rates among boys in the primary school and higher rates among girls in the secondary school across oPt. Such rates are related to the child labor among boys and early marriage among girls especially during the remote learning model due to COVID-lockdown where 48,5% of households indicated that the absence of the internet prevented the children from education. At the same time, COVID19 pushes people to spend long times online where they also suffer from violence. Since the beginning of 2021 to Sept., 176 cases (76 boys & 100 girls) & 208 (120 men & 85 women) asked the police for help due to online violence (Palestinian police, 2021)For the most vulnerable locations in the West Bank, the project addresses the most affected areas in area C of the northern West Bank as being ranked by OCHA casualties since January 2021 and up to now. The most children and adults in need dwell in area C of the northern West Bank as in the following eight communities: Beita and Huwwara in Nablus governorate, Kafr Qaddum and Azzun in Qalqilya governorate, Ya’bad, and Birqin in Jenin governorate, and Almaleh and Yarza in the northern Jordan Valley in Tubas Governorate. The abovementioned situation and environment in the area C of northern West Bank cause the key problem addressed by this project: Children and adults living in area C of northern West Bank suffer from lack of receiving protection response interventions to prevent and mitigate the effects of conflict-related violence. This issue has arisen from the increasing need for the psychosocial services for both children and adults including caregivers, and weak awareness of child protection issues. PCDCR will bridge this gap through this project aiming to contribute to provide protection response mechanisms to children and adults affected by conflict in area C of northern West Bank through raising awareness interventions, PSS services (group counselling), and PSS (individual counselling). Both genders are targeted with reasonable percentages based on the recent statistics and rates indicated above i.e. 51% of the targets for boys and 49% for girls. Hence, the project targets from the identified needs 370 children and 304 adults as follows: 112 children in the PSS group counselling (57 boys and 55 girls); 66 children in the PSS (individual counselling) (34 boys, and 32 girls); 192 children in the raising awareness (98 boys and 94 girls); 112 adults in the PSS group counselling (56 men and 56 women); and 192 adults (96 men and 96 women). The project considers the protection and PSEA concerns at all stages of the project with a special focus on persons living with disabilities in order to ensure safety for the beneficiaries and staff and to ensure accessibility.

2 - Activities

Being a Palestinian NGO with solid experience and wide network at different levels across the West Bank, PCDCR has the capacity and ability to access the 8 targeted locations in the area C of northern West Bank, to reach the targeted population- namely children and adults including caregivers affected by the conflict-related violence-, and to ensure the successful implementation of the project. By doing so, PCDCR achieves the project goal, the SO1, and the Protection Cluster Objective 2 to participate in targeting the needs of HNO 2022 and HRP 2021. To that end, PCDCR will implement the following activities considering the gender, age needs, protection concerns, PSEA for all engaged parties with a special focus on persons with disabilities. Gender considerations are addressed through their needs e.g. the interventions for boys focuses on education & risks of labor while for girls discusses the early marriage. For men & women, the different impact of increasing pressures (conflict, economic, responsibilities, COVID, etc.) on each of them will be discussed from their perspective to meet their various gender needs. All the proposed activities will be conducted in physical considering health protocols as social distancing, masks, sanitizers, wide halls, etc. unless COVID-19 lockdown is imposed, an emergency response plan will be adopted. 11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PCDCR adopts the principles of humanitarian action including equality and impartiality. Hence, PCDCR will ensure & enable equal and impartial access to assistance and services by the targeted vulnerable groups (children & adults including persons with disabilities). This target depends mainly on the needs and priority to the most urgent cases of distress. The children & adults will be reached through coordinating with local and village councils and CBOs in order to target the most conflict-affected people in need without any discrimination on the basis of nationality, race, gender, religious belief, class or political opinions. Accordingly, PCDCR will coordinate with the local entities in the targeted locations to identify the most affected children & adults who are in need. Additionally, PCDCR has a MoU with the Ministry of Education and Ministry of Social Development; thus, the PCDCR could cooperate with the ministries to reach the children & adults in need referring to their documents and records. Furtherly, PCDCR will provide individual PSS (individual counselling) as needed for children/ adults and referring only to their priorities & needs regardless of their backgrounds. As persons with disability (PwD) are at higher risks of vulnerability & face more difficult challenges than persons without disabilities, PCDCR adapts IASC guidelines on inclusion of persons with disability in humanitarian action. Thus, PCDCR integrates persons with disability at different levels: 1-Analysis: Both children with disabilities & Parents/ adults with disabilities are involved in the focus group discussions to identify their necessities and tailor the PSS interventions based on their needs. 2-Resource: There’s a budget allocation for private transportation/ sign language interpreter for PwD to allow them to participate & benefit from our interventions. 3- Implementation: The data is disaggregated not only by gender & age but also by disability. PCDCR selects physically accessible locations for PwD to engage in the activities. Also, we provide in-home individual PSS for people in need and referral to other entities as needed. 4- Monitoring & Evaluation: PCDCR will consider the feedback & satisfaction of people with disabilities about provided services. We may conduct sub-plans based on their feedback to improve the provided services. Such sub-plans will be approved by OCHA.

Being a member in PSEA network, PCDCR coordinates with the network in referral, as needed.Internally, PCDCR has developed a manual for children protection policies and procedures including children safeguarding policy, code of conduct, and whistleblowing policy in 2021 as part of building the capacities of PCDCR and building on our experiences in terms of children and women rights (Women protection policies to be adopted). The procedures include trainings for PCDCR staff, contractors, partners and so on child rights protection and PSEA, and signing documents. This document is adopted recently in 2021 and PCDCR still does not provide PSEA training yet. However, the training will be a must for all project staff.
