Project Summary

The project aims to improve emergency response, protection, and the promotion of long-lasting solutions through a coordinated multi-sectoral response by enhancing the delivery of CCCM activities while taking into account recent advancements in the operation. Given the conditions in NE, it is not realistic to abruptly stop using traditional CCCM methods in camps and structures that approach camps, especially given that many camps will remain operational through most of 2023. A proven strategy to encourage long-lasting solutions and community togetherness through equitable targeting is to include the host communities, returnees, the population that has relocated, and those who are striving to integrate into the community. The project will cover 1 LGAs in Borno (Gubio). Through this project, PALRI will address the Sectors overall objective of improving the living conditions inside IDP sites and facilitate the immediate and targeted delivery of services to the displaced population. PALRI will continue to support emergency life­saving interventions of the wider humanitarian community through the maintenance, and management of humanitarian camps in Gubio LGA. To immediately address needs and gaps identified through the need assessment conducted. PALRI will facilitate and utilize the assessment conduced to support current management and coordination to respond to increasing need the IDPs and camp-like settlement, the needs of women, girls, men and boys of all ages will be addressed all throughout the planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Through the CCCM and regular site monitoring, needs and gaps will be reported and shared with partners and all stakeholders including all disaggregated data. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp and camp-like settlement.

2 - Activities

Site facilitation (managing out-of-camp sites, site upkeep and repairs, distribution of Dignity kits items, community leadership training, site planning, complaints and feedback mechanisms, focus group discussions, sensitization and awareness raising, camp/community coordination, and information sharing) (out of camp sites). Building capacities helps staff, partners, and authority leaders deliver CCCM activities in a more responsible and efficient manner. Maintenance and rehabilitation of infrastructure, site planning, land advocacy, mapping of currently available suitable lands, relocation, fire response and prevention, flood mitigation for host community locations that are most in need of help during the rainy season (including women, girls, boys, and men of all ages and backgrounds, including People with Disabilities), and participation of the affected population (including women, girls, boys, and men of all ages and backgrounds). Community mobilization via leadership and leader selection sensitization and awareness-raising, collaborative community mobilization activities that promote harmony and cohesiveness among leaders in camps and camp-like structures, including leadership in out-of-camp settings. Training for community leaders, volunteers, religious leaders, youth, and women leaders on how to adequately discharge their responsibilities , the understanding of camp management, protection, GBV, trafficking, PSEA, referral system, community participation among other cross cutting issues. . 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The design of this project was informed by the fundamental protection principles that guide the delivery of humanitarian aid, and as such, those principles will be followed during project execution. The entire project will be planned, carried out, and evaluated by PALRI using a rights-based approach. For each of the protection principles, the following steps will be performed to guarantee thorough protection mainstreaming throughout this project: PALRI will continuously monitor the security situation in the area of intervention and mobile teams will use secure and permitted roadways to access persons in need while putting safety and dignity ahead of doing harm during the project's implementation. To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. To make communication with women and girls easier, the assessment teams' composition will be balanced by gender. Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The establishment of a community-based feedback mechanism, assistance to partners in the inclusion of useful and accessible complaint and reporting mechanisms, regular meetings with community leaders and beneficiaries, and focus group discussions will all help to improve accountability to affected populations. PALRI shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk. All reported issues will undergo thorough investigation and prompt follow-up. Complaints should be classified into categories based on their importance and sensitivity and addressed in accordance with protection principles. PALRI will conduct post intervention monitoring and evaluation to ensure proper feedback.
