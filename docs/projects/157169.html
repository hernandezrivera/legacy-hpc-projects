Project Summary

The proposed project aims at to respond to the HRP 2018-2019 O1 by providing emergency and life-saving assistance for the South Sudanese refugees in West Kordofan State.
The project also will link to the three objectives of the Refugee Response Strategy in Sudan by providing protection and basic services for new arrivals (O1),
addressing ongoing needs and improve services (O2), and
contribute toward self-resilience amongst refugees and their host communities (O3)
The intervention aims at focusing on West Kordofan state where Concern has been supporting refugees in 2018 through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH intervention. Though the number of new arrivals reduced since 2016, the gap to meet minimum standards in health/nutrition and WASH services is so significant that humanitarian service delivery is needed in 2019. Linking to RCF strategy, the intervention will focus on out-of-camp assistance and will focus on strengthening asset building and coping mechanisms.
The project will focus on life-saving health and nutrition interventions in our currently running health and nutrition facilities for the most vulnerable women and children U5 and other vulnerable groups (single headed family, women headed family, elderly with medical needs etc.). The health component of the project will focus on the most urgent life-saving needs for both refugees and host population including within the existing health facilities through PHC services, extended immunization programs, maternal and neonatal care, support community disease prevention including sexually transmitted disease, and behaviour change communication for hygiene and health promotion.
Bringing integration with nutrition the beneficiaries of health services will be invited for nutrition screening particularly mothers with children 5U, and will be admitted to the nutrition program as per needs. CMAM program has been a proven success for our intervention therefore it will continue to be our driving nutrition program with OTP and SFP services. Promotion of IYCF interventions will be promoted with cross-cutting gender mainstreaming and protection messages.
Cross cutting issues such as protection, with special focus on child protection against violence, abuse and exploitation will be in a focus while working in the health and nutrition facilities through taking preventative measures, clinical response and referrals where possible and needed.
Linking health, nutrition to WASH through this multi-sectoral intervention, will provide water sources for local communities and will be extended to existing health and nutrition facilities. Strengthening community water source management systems (water user committees), with the involvement of refugees, will be organized to reduce tension and create equal opportunities for all in decision making. The establishment/revitalization of the WUCs will also link to the establishment of water charging systems, for improved self-resilience and sustainability. This activity will also give opportunities for refugees for a greater social cohesion with host communities. Community and HH level latrine provision and related sanitation and hygiene education for refugees will be a pillar for the WASH intervention that aims at to reduce the risk to water born disease and related health concerns, and to ensure that support to refugees and host communities meet their own needs in a safe, and dignify way. Environment protection measures will be taken while implementing the project and beneficiaries will be introduced simile techniques that reduce water waste around water sources and home consumption.

Needs

According to national statistics, access to basic health services continues to be a major challenge for the conflict affected communities where rural hospital coverage at 40%; only 48% of primary health units functioning; and only 35% of those which are functioning having the recommended number and type of health workers (WHO). Concern has been providing integrated health, nutrition and WASH services e.g. in Meriam town that has the only rural hospital and nutrition centre for nearly 100,000 catchment population including nearly 5,000 SS refugees. While operating in the region, Concern experienced dire lack of medicines, medical supplies and basic consumables for population without medical safety net (insurance coverage). Lack of qualified medical staff for PHC services and other life-saving curative services remain a serious concern. In order to improve the critical health situation there is a need to continue an integrated, community based PHC service delivery, primarily for the refugees and their host community population. Provision of medical supplies/consumables, with improved storage facilities, and constant capacity building of health staff is a critical need to be able to deliver health services. Due to the weak health service the referral system is not working adequately therefore this area also needs to be strengthened and link it with nutrition and WASH activities. During the 2018 nutrition service delivery Concern experienced critical malnutrition gaps amongst SS refugees and an average of 80-100 admission cases/month registered from which an average of 5-6 cases required stabilization in our centres. The new SS refugees arrival particularly pregnant and lactating women and children U5 are in a very critical nutrition condition require nutrition support and vitamins. Therefore Concern proposes to continue the nutrition service deliveries in 2019 respond the urgent life-saving nutrition needs for the refugees and host population. In 2018 Concern focused on institution and community WASH improvements although the huge gap in water sources provision (water yards, hand pumps) around health institutions and in the communities created a need for further improving water services. Besides water provision, there is a critical need to knowledge and attitude change among the vulnerable SS refugees relating to hygiene practices that links to health and nutrition through provision of sustainable, protected and innovative sanitation services, personal and environment hygiene services.

Impact on environment/natural resources

During the project design and implementation climate change adaptation approaches will be considered particularly for the water source renovation and management. During the technical assessment and technical design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of the important elements to consider. Other aspects related to designing and constructing HH latrines will use locally available, natural resources to reduce the negative impact on ecological footprint. Nutrition and health activities will also consider the environment by using environmentally manageable materials for building renovation, and waste management (debris) will be organized during and after the renovation. However the project is not directly impacted by the environment, during the health and nutrition capacity building training environment protection particularly related to medical waste management (storage and incinerating medical waste) will be considered, and a daily waste disposal will be organized around the health and nutrition facilities.
