Project Summary

Specialized Women’s Protection and Empowerment (WPE) prevention and response services will be delivered to support women and girls at risk, such as elderly women, pregnant women, breastfeeding women, women and girls with disabilities, and female headed households (HHs) with minors in charge and without resources. The project will support the integration of reproductive health and WPE services so that gender-based violence (GBV) survivors can access safe and timely clinical care for the harmful health consequences - both physical and emotional. WPE staff integrated into health facilities will provide lifesaving GBV response services, including GBV case management and non-focused psycho-social support (PSS) to at-risk women and girls. At the same time, focused PSS sessions, recreational activities, and awareness-raising will be delivered through a Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS). The IRC will also bolster protection services by providing general and specialized GBV training sessions to internal and external actors, including GBV core concepts, referral pathways, disclosure and safe referrals and other and GBV trainings required to provide services for women and girls.
The project will also deliver Child Protection (CP) services through the implementation of CP activities. The IRC will ensure that children are safe in their homes when they experience harm. This will be achieved by supporting children facing protection concerns to have access to quality services and a robust Child Protection response inclusive of case management and a Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS) where the IRC's Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) curriculum will be implemented. The IRC will also engage community members to further mitigate potential risks for children and adolescents. At the core of the SHLS model is the SEL intervention, which follows a curriculum designed to strengthen five SEL competencies through lessons and games. The SHLS also serves as an entry point to identify children at risk and to refer them for specialized services, as well as supporting children receiving case management services. Case management services will be central to the child protection services provided. Through child-, family- and community focused strategies and coordinated case management service delivery mechanisms, the IRC will identify children at-risk of harm or who have experienced harm and will provide immediate support, ensuring the individual needs of children are assessed and addressed. Targeted children for case management include boys and girls who show signs of psychological distress, children who have experienced abuse and exploitation, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and children engaged in the worst forms of child labour.

1 - Needs

Protracted and new displacements continue in Sudan due to conflict and natural disasters. Assessment reports from the IRC, REACH and UNFPA indicate that children, women and girls are disproportionally affected, especially by recurrent displacement. Across Sudan, common child protection concerns include the separation of children from their family, child labor, and early marriage. While UNFPA’s Voices from Sudan (2020) report states that specific risks for women and girls include forced/early marriage, domestic violence, and Female Genital Mutilation/Cutting (FGM/C). In addition, the assessments show that overall participants have limited knowledge regarding the availability of GBV and CP services. It highlights that lack of information, stigma, shame and the potential for retaliation from community and family, contribute to significant underreporting. the MSNA (REACH, 2021) report states that unmet needs in the Protection sector are particularly high with a national average of 30% of HHs whose protection needs are not met. In the Sudan context where insecurity has caused recurrent displacement and protection crises since 2003, limited access to basic needs is often associated with the destruction of property and services. This has in turn resulted in significant strain on services, including GBV and CP services— leading to the deterioration of the protection environment for children, women and girls. This has increased the reliance on harmful coping mechanisms resulting in increased protection risks and calls for an urgent intervention through specialized GBV and CP services. The intensity of the violence children, girls and women have been exposed to, coupled with the lack of access to basic needs, has severe psychological distress consequences among the population increasing as communities grapple with family loss, multiple displacements, lack of control over their lives and the breakdown of social coping mechanism. With extensive experience in implementing WPE and CP programming, the IRC is well-positioned to respond to the protection needs of at-risk children, women and girls and enhance the overall protection environment. The IRC plans to increase access to protection services by prioritizing highly vulnerable groups and improving the lives of children, women and girls through access to quality GBV and CP prevention and response services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Through this intervention, the IRC will ensure that children, women and girls are safe in their homes when they experience harm. This will be achieved by supporting children, girls and women facing GBV or CP concerns to have access to quality services and a robust protection response, inclusive of delivering GBV and CP case management and non-focused and focused PSS to at-risk children, women, and girls. The IRC will also provide prevention and risk mitigation activities, including safety audits, to identify specific CP and GBV risks supporting targeted risk reduction actions. Individual case management support sessions will be offered according to an individual case plan in line with the IRC's case management guidelines. Safe referrals to meet the needs of affected populations will be made whenever informed consent is given, and particular attention will be given to ensure UASC and reproductive health needs are met. The IRC will conduct activities directly through a SHLS and WGSS and private counselling rooms embedded in health facilities and will work with men, boys, women and girls through community outreach activities and community leaders by forming committees. Community engagement interventions and committees will promote acceptance and ownership of the program, increase ability to reach isolated, stigmatized or marginalized individuals, and will ensure that their needs are identified and advocated for. Overall, the IRC will: Output 1: Offer protection prevention services to children, women and girls• Activity 1.1: Raise awareness on protection topics with communities and key stakeholders. Topics will include messages on how to prevent family separation, and GBV risks amongst other relevant subjects as identified through assessments and working groups.• Activity 1.2: Provide life skills and recreational activities for children and women, deliver structured PSS sessions for caregivers, boys and girls through the IRC’s safe spaces. These focus on the use of positive-coping mechanisms that reduce the use of violence as well as providing information and strategies to prevent harm (e.g., by increasing confidence, safety and social network ).• Activity 1.3: Bolster protection services through dedicated community-based protection intervention including women and girls’ groups, child protection committees as well as other committees within intervention sites so that women and girls and boys safety and access to quality response services are in line with humanitarian principles and survivor centred approach and to identify protection risks.Output 2: Provide protection response services for children, women and girls • Activity 2.1: Provide GBV case management for GBV survivors in a safe and confidential space, providing quality, compassionate care and support that helps survivors to recover and heal. As part of the case management process make multi-sectoral referrals, including to clinical care. when a survivor gives informed consent. GBV, case management supervision will also be delivered to support quality of GBV case management delivery.• Activity 2.2: Deliver CP case management for children who are at risk of experiencing abuse, violence and neglect or children who have other protection risks, such as being UASC.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. Community outreach will include messaging on prevention and mitigation of gender-based violence and child protection risks. All IRC staffing working on the project will be trained on core protection concepts, gender mainstreaming, and referral procedures. The IRC will integrate activities within its referral system, allowing the team to refer potential cases to the health team. The IRC will maintain and regularly update a protection mainstreaming action plan and seek to improve the program processes including gender-equitable community participation, information dissemination, and provision of inclusive infrastructure for persons with disability and the elderly. All field-based staff will receive information about available protection and response services in order to make safe and ethical referrals upon beneficiary request as well as receive service mapping from the WPE and CP team, to ensure the relay of information on access to services if/when asked during HH visits. Finally, all staff are trained in protection and humanitarian principles to apply in their everyday work. While IRC’s approach to conflict sensitivity integrates the humanitarian principle of “Do No Harm,” the IRC recognizes that effective sensitivity can also contribute to strengthening communities and making a modest contribution towards resilience. The IRC will consult a range of stakeholders for different perspectives to inform its work, promoting dialogue and communication as methods to conduct work and address issues.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The IRC will conduct regular feedback sessions with caregivers, women and girls within the safe spaces to ensure activities and services are adequately responding to their needs. Women, girls and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of the safe space and activities provided within it. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of GBV on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate and address the consequences of CP risks and GBV. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including focus group discussions (FGDs), KIIs, feedback boxes, and a hotline. Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels.
