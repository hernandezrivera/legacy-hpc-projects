Project Summary

Protracted crisis in Gaza has affected 80,000 vulnerable women, 210,000 children including 25,000 new-born babies. Fatality rate for the pregnant women is increasing due to the blockade imposed by Israel and continued warfare in Gaza . According to UNRWA, 16.9% of the pregnant women are diagnosed with high-risk pregnancy, 4.4% are diabetes type II, and 7% are hypertension, 42% are anemic, and 18% are with malnutrition . There were 56,000 pregnant women, out of which 15,000 women did not have an access to sufficient care. More than 15,000 new-born babies and infants under 5 years old need acute nutrition support each year. 14,000 children is extremely vulnerable with chronic malnutrition and stunting , and more than 324,000 are lacking micronutrients . Decrease in food supply in Gaza is so sharp that 1.3 million people are currently not able to secure stable food , and children with minimum required nutrients intake is just 14% .
Their health conditions have worsened also due to the deteriorating economic situation. The quick survey conducted by CCP Japan indicates 79% of the surveyed households have no income and unemployed, and 1,488 households has less than income for $500 per month. It is extremely difficult for such vulnerable families to sustain their daily life.
The proposed project intends to address comprehensive needs for mother and child health and sustain their livelihood for the short term. It provides 1,500 women with an appropriate antenatal care and 700 women an appropriate postnatal care according to the WHO guidelines. 7,000 infants from 0 to 5 years old of the women who received antenatal services are assessed for their nutrition status. Medical teams follow up on estimated 700 infants who needs medical attention every two months after the first visit. Infants’ situation are closely monitored by the medical teams and necessary medications and supplements are prescribed. In addition, estimated 500 women and children who receive the-above antenatal, postnatal, and nutrition support would receive psychosocial support. Further, vulnerable 1,200 households receive necessary food items at local super markets through distribution of vouchers.

1 - Needs

Armed conflict in Gaza strip in May 2021 and August 2022 exacerbated already dysfunctional medical institutions due COVID-19 outbreak for the past several years. Palestinian National Authority has stated that MoH in Gaza had not secured a stock of 123 essential drugs and 174 medical consumables for more than one month. Only 40% of the essential drugs and 30% of the necessary medical consumables have currently been secured in stock. 80,000 vulnerable women, 210,000 children including 25,000 newborn babies had been affected. In addition, many pregnant women and lactating women have been facing double crisis of obesity and malnutrition. 28% of lactating women have been lacking an iron nutrient. Fatality rate for the pregnant women is increasing due to the blockade imposed by Israel and continued warfare in Gaza . Main causes of pregnant women’s death are bleeding, postnatal sepsis, thromboembolism, hypertensive syndrome of pregnancy, difficult birth . According to UNRWA, 16.9% of the pregnant women are diagnosed with high-risk pregnancy, 4.4% are diabetes type II, and 7% are hypertension, 42% are anemic, and 18% are with malnutrition . Anemia and low weight of the pregnant women increases risks of early delivery and low birth weight for newborn babies, and it could lead to miscarriage, and birth of stunting or weakened babies. Besides, the pregnant women not controlling their weight have high risks of complications such as pregnancy diabetes or preeclampsia, and could also lead to difficult delivery, post delivery bleeding, or unable to breastfeed babies . According to Gaza MoH, there were 56,000 pregnant women, out of which 15,000 women did not have an access to sufficient care. While more of the child delivery cases are treated in the hospitals, most of which only supporting delivery, every woman usually has to leave the hospitals 6 hours after the dry due to lack of funding for providing follow-up cares. There are more than 15,000 newborn babies and infants under 5 years old needing acute nutrition support each year. 14,000 children is extremely vulnerable with chronic malnutrition and stunting , and more than 324,000 are lacking micronutrients . Most of the under-5-year-old infants are lacking vitamin A and D , and 72.6% are anemic . New-born babies (under 28 days old) fatality rate is 12 per 1,000, and for infants under 5 years old, it is 24 per 1,000 . 75% of infants under one year old are with malnutrition , and more than 246,000 children need nutrition support . Decrease in food supply in Gaza is so sharp that 1.3 million people are currently not able to secure stable food , and children with minimum required nutrients intake is just 14% . In addition, only 39% of new-born babies (under six month old) are fully breastfed . Risks of newborn babies without breastfed getting diarrhea and acute respiratory infections are twice more than breastfed babies. On the other hand, 14% of lactating mothers are with malnutrition. Such malnutrition has been passed through generations. With increase in poverty and unemployment rate within Gaza, the increased food insecurity level has exposeed children more to anemia and malnutrition. Socioeconomic determinants of health are highly relevant to health status of the entire population especially children. Therefore, malnutrition and anemia among women and children in Gaza has been increasing due to the prevailing economic and political turmoil coincided with the consequences of COVID-19 pandemic, aggrevated by the military clush in May 2021 and August 2022. CCP Japan has conducted quick survey for 1,611 households for their economic situation. 79% of the surveyed households have no income and unemployed. 33% are with persons with disabilities. 1,488 households has less than income for $500 per month. 49% are sstill displaced after the conflict in May 2021. 55% are purchasing food by borrowing cash, and 21% are decreasing quality of meals, and 12% are receiving food donation from relatives, and 12% are cutting at least one meal per day. It is extremely difficult for such vulnerable families to secure food items to sustain their daily life, particularly for the female-headed households and those with persons with disabilities. CCP Japan has continued implementing the projects supporting women and children at vulnerable households since 2016 in the areas in which many vulnerable households reside such as Shujayah, Daraj, and Rafah. It has assisted more than more than 2,900 pregnant women received several medical follow up at the clinic several times before delivery. More than 2,000 women received several home visits by medical teams or care at the clinics. More than 23,000 new-born babies and infants had been assessed and 4,700 children had received nutrition support including periodical visits for doctors and provision of nutrition supplements. More than 80% of the infants received support have improved their nutritional state. More than 2,300 vulnerable households receive food items and met their food and daily life needs for at least 2 months. On the other hand, as it has been difficult for mothers and children to improve their health status due to the indicated aggrevated social and economic turmoil leading to the increased anemia and malnutrition cases. While most of the cases that CCP assisted before has shown clear signs of improvement in health indicators, those beneficiaries are still facing difficulties to cope with such a protracted humanitarian crisis. Not only MCH services, but also does fresh food provision contribute to building a foundation for their long-term improvement and coping abilities. In sum, due to current deteriorating economic and social situation for the vulnerable households, even improved health conditions cannot be sustained. Comprehensive health services and food provision is indispensable to address current needs for mothers and children in Gaza.

2 - Activities

Component 1: Health and Medical Support 1-1. Provision of Antenatal Care a) In the venerable areas such as Shujayah, Daraj, and Rafah, 1,500 women receive appropriate antenatal care.b) Doctors provide diagnosis and medical advice according to WHO guidelines. c) Based on the benchmark of the hemoglobin level 11g, medical teams give an advice to pregnant women for improvement and prevent symptoms of hypertension, pregnancy diabetes, and severe anemia. d) Based on doctors’ diagnosis, medications are prescribed such as antibiotics, antipyretic, bactericidal disinfectant, antifungal agent, antiprotozoal agent, synthetic adrenocortical hormone, iron, folic acid, calcium, vitamins, etc. Target beneficiaries 1) Vulnerable pregnant women without an access to appropriate antenatal care neither at public nor private institutions. 2) Prioritize female-headed households and women with a high health risk. 1-2. Provision of Postnatal Care a) 700 women receive appropriate postnatal care. Medical teams make individual home visits for those who cannot come to the clinics and also receives periodical visits at the clinics, and provide them diagnosis and medical follow up. Postnatal care basically entails 3 visits. b) According to the WHO guidelines, medical teams provide advices on lactating amount and times, babies growth indicators, parenting, umbilical cord care, sanitation, medical follow-up for newborn babies, psychological self-help for mothers, family planning, nutrition intake and vaccination for newborn babies. c) Based on doctors’ diagnosis, medications would be prescribed such as antibiotics, antipyretic, bactericidal disinfectant, antifungal agent, antiprotozoal agent, synthetic adrenocortical hormone, iron, folic acid, calcium, vitamins, etc. d) A hotline for mothers who cannot come to the clinic provides them medical consultations and prescriptions via phone. Beneficiaries: 1) Those who receive the above antenatal care (1-1)2) Those who cannot receive any care and support from public and private institutions. 1-3. Nutrition Support for Infantsa) 7,000 infants from 0 to 5 years old of the women who received antenatal services above 1-1 are assessed for their nutrition status. At the first visit, infants’ height, weights, head size are measured and doctors give a diagnosis and medical follow-up. Hemogrobin level is also measured. b) Medical teams follow up on estimated 700 infants who needs medical attention every two months after the first visit. c) Medications include but not limited to: Iron, antibiotics, antihistamines, antitussive expectorants, synthetic adrenocortical hormones for external use, antifungal agents, nasal drops, vasopressors, antipyretic analgesics, anti-inflammatory drugs, and other nutritional supplements or foods. d) Severe growth retardation cases are referred to the specialized medical institutions based on doctors’ diagnosis. e) Provide a MCH handbook in which data related to children’s growth and nutrition status as well as vaccinations are recorded.f) A hotline for mothers who cannot come to the clinic provides medical consultation and prescription via phone. Beneficiaries: 3) Infants from 0 to 5 years old4) Infants with hemogrobin level lower than 11g5) Infants lacking essential nutrients 6) Those without public and private support 1-4　Psychosocial Support for Pregnant Women, Mothers and Childrena) Estimated 500 women and children who receive the above antenatal (1-1), postnatal (1-2), and nutrition support (1-3) are assessed their psychological state. The “moderate” and “severe” cases receive individual counselling by psychologists at the clinics. For the cases needs specialized attention, they are referred to the specialized institutions accordingly. b) Upon encountering GBV cases, by securing beneficiaries privacy and ensuring their safety, they are referred to the specialized institutions for acute support and protection.c) A hotline for those who cannot come to the clinic and wish to secure their privacy provides individual consultation via phone. <br> 1-5 Training of the Community Health Activators, and workshops within their communities for establishing Coping Mechanisms. a) 30 women and men are selected as the Community Health Activators who are community members and experienced with pregnancy, childbirth, and nutritional intake for the infants.b) They will be trained to obtain the knowledge about MCH with individual counselling skills and workshop facilitation skills. c) After that they will conduct workshops after the training at the local clinics and CBO’s room for raising awareness about MCH for 2,200 community members. Component 2: Emergency Livelihood Support 2-1.　Food Items distribution a) 1,200 households, mainly beneficiaries receive the above antenatal care (1-1), receive necessary food Items for worth $120 USD for one round at local super markets through distribution of vouchers.b) Contracting a few local supermarkets, beneficiaries come and select the food items according to their needs within the designated cash value. c) Each time, items a beneficiary receives will be checked out at the cashiers. The total monetary value of the checked out items will be paid to supermarket each month. Therefore, d) Exclude luxury items such as cigarettes and cosmetics. Social workers and project staff monitors onsite and provide beneficiaries advice on available items. e) Social workers follow up with each household for ensuring distributed items meet each household’s needs via phone call, survey as well as individual home visits. Beneficiaries: 1) Beneficiaries for the above component 1 particularly affected by the conflict in May 2021 and August 20222) Female-headed households3) Households with no income and no prospect for securing income4) Households with persons with disability 5) Households currently not receiving any food items assistance

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

To address comprehensive needs for pregnant women and women after the delivery of the vulnerable households, service provision is mainly needed. The project focuse on the direct service delivery to those beneficiaries. Food distribution via vouchers have been effective and efficient to cover basic needs for mother and child health. Basic needs of mothers and children's needs are mainly driven by lack of their ability to secure healthy and nutritious foods, and this would contribute to the long-term lateness and retardation of children's growth. Necessary food items vary on situation of each household. The project would distribute the voucher to beneficiaries useful for the designated categories of items within the-set monetary value so that they can select from wide varieties of items according to their needs.
