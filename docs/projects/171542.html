Project Summary

Food assistance needs in Somalia are already high with an estimated 3.5 million people in rural livelihood zones, urban areas, and IDP settlements in need of food assistance. In riverine areas, farmers have suffered from multiple flood events during the Gu and Hagaa 2020 seasons, which damaged Deyr 2019/20 off-season crops and Gu 2020 main season crops. As a result, the main Gu harvest in July/August is significantly below average in these areas. While the floods disrupted recessional cultivation activities and inundated crops, the rains are supporting late planted Gu main season crops and Gu off-season crops and alleviated fears that the forecast of a below-average Hagaa season would deplete soil moisture and negatively affect off-season crops. In general, farmers have benefitted from improved access to water for irrigation. Worryingly however, the damage to road infrastructure and market access, have adversely affected terms of trade with higher prices of maize, and significantly lowering the prices for goats and daily labour wage rates by up to 30 and 102 per cent below the five-year average.
WRRS will seek to offer response to the needs that has affected both the host community and the IDPs who have sought to take refuge in these proposed areas. This will be attained through improvement of HH access to food by provision of unconditional and conditional cash transfers to the beneficiaries of the targeted district of Bay and lower Juba. Besides this those who are pastorals will receive restock of animals offered with the vaccination, and poultry support with major focus of the female headed HH which will be accompanied by capacity building on best farming practices; veterinary care and pest control. The need to offer sustainable access to livelihood assets in hard to reach areas of Lower Juba and Bay will remain crucial and need urgent humanitarian response especially after the flood that has caused a lot of damage to the previously restored livelihood.
WRRS will work closely with all the stakeholders and other actors to ensure that there is harmornize service delivery without overlap.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Given in-kind’s inability to address the needs of communities facing crisis, cash transfers have become more preferable among beneficiaries with the flexibility offered. Furthermore, with the increase in mobile money penetration in Somalia, cash transfers have increasingly become more popular in the modern era, unlike in-kind transfers which involve more costs, are not as safe as mobile money transfers, and take longer to distribute. Markets in Somalia have remained functional and there is adequate infrastructure to enable this modality to work efficiently.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

WRRS will ensure that adequate market survey has been done to get clear pricing for accurate and realistic cash disbursement. This has been seen as a guide towards developing efficient and effective responses to crises, particularly for populations in IPC 3&4. Monitoring of the markets will be done regularly through local market price monitoring, the CWG and FSNAU, and this will be helpful in ascertaining if markets in target locations are sufficiently elastic and competitive in order to respond to increased demand with sufficient quantity and quality of goods without resulting in inflation.

Mitigation Measures

ender disparities are on the rise in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. WRRS through the project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context. Vulnerable community members generally refer to those disadvantaged with compromised access to resources to meet their daily basic needs. The project staff will ensure that during community mobilization and sensitization, all community members are given equal opportunity to participate. WRRS has been working in areas where security and conflict has been a major factor affecting the beneficiaries. Accountability for violations is limited, with traditional or formal mediation and justice mechanisms having been disrupted, or having become disrespected or inaccessible. Building community structures including local temporary learning space that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conflict and violence. Communities fleeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence. Accountability to affected population is a key aspect in the trans-formative agenda. The project intends to involve the community in all stages of the program cycle, so that they understand the aim and the outcome of the entire project and embrace its as it rolls, they will also understand the guiding principle behind the decisions made.The project intends to use already existing community governance structures and use them as community committees. These will be involved in recruitment of staff, community mobilization and monitoring of the project.Their presence will represent the interest and role of the community in successful completion of the project. The project will also have a feedback mechanism , from which complaints and comments from the community will be collected, and fed back to the same. This will be done in a highly confidential manner with very few staff in the management allowed to interact with this process. WRRS will address the complains as they come and embrace the comments for the sustainability and acceptance of the services within the community since these are live saving interventions that will help the households have a sustainable livelihood income.
