Project Summary

South Sudan food security situation has deteriorated due to multiple shocks, including flooding, conflict, displacement and a high cost of living. The situation is more exacerbated by influx of returnees and refugees from Sudan putting more pressure to the limited resources. SAADO intervention is aimed improve food security and livelihoods at household level for the vulnerable population at risks of hunger and Malnutrition through the following FSL cluster objective; CO 1. Humanitarian Assistance through food assistance, CO 2. Emergency livelihood- Veg and crop livelihood inputs, FKT and Livestock, CO3 Resilience support through capacity strengthening i.e IGA and Market support.
We will target the most Vulnerable Women, Girls, boys, Men and PLWD of the Returnees, IDPs and the host communities affected by shocks including Flood, conflict and influx of returnees and refugee in the hard-to-reach areas of Malakal, RENK, Rubkona, Leer, Mayom, Fangak, Pigi, Pibor, Aweil North and Aweil East counties
The beneficiaries for this project will be 42456; 4636 girls ,17441 women, 4280 boys &16099 men respectively. With a budget of USD $1,273,680 over a period of 12 month from Jan to Dec 2025, this project we will focus in providing main season crops, vegetable and fishing kits and dry season fast maturing crops, vegetable kits and fishing kits for immediate benefits. Provision of vegetable and crops seeds will improve food production while fishing kits will contribute to better nutrition at HH level and in other cases improve HH income due to the sale of excess produce. SAADO shall also set up kitchen garden in the targeted 60 nutrition centers and schools’ facilities. In the nutrition centres, we will involve mother to mother ( M2M) support groups members who will be key in establishment and taking cares of the kitchen gardens. To improve Knowledge of eating balance diet, nutrition education session will be provided. SAADO will liaise with FAO pipeline for in kind support for the crop, fishing and vegetable. The crops kits, vegetable kits and fishing for main and dry season will be given to 7076 HH(3680 female HH and 3396 Male HH). The modality to be used will one crop kit, vegetable and fishing kit per HH in the main season and one vegetable and fishing kit per HH in the dry season. Training of farmers on agronomic practices and fish preservation and Training of youth and women on IGA will be offered during project implementation. Fruits tree planting will be emphasis to reduce climate effect and help to improve children nutrition. Animal vaccination through FAO support will be key activity.
For accountability to the affected population (AAP), SAADO will create a committees compose of 50% women, 20% Men and 30% people living with disability (PLWD) to oversee the project implementation as from its initiation. The beneficiaries will be passing on their concerns and feedback to the committee after a series of consultation, then the committee will share feedback with the partner to enhance the quality of the project. The involvement of the community will be key in building trust and creating a sense of ownership thus increasing the sustainability. The FSL intervention will be implement in conflict sensitive manner to reduce the negative effects to the targeted beneficiaries. On inclusion of disability, barrier analysis and implementation of action plan will be key in this intervention. To enhance PSEA, training on PSEA will be provided to the direct staff and key community member who are directly involved in this project. SAADO will mainstream protection in this project by contacting risks analysis and continuous monitoring access, Safety and dignity of all targeted beneficiaries. SAADO has prominent presence in this targeted location thus enabling to implement the project at ease. Integration of FSL activities with other sector including Protection, WASH, GBV, Health, Edu and Nut will be key to enhance synergy in the targeted

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity situation in South Sudan is deteriorating, driven by high food prices, a severe economic crisis, climatic shocks - primarily widespread flooding - conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation, putting additional pressure on an already fragile country.The food security situation is expected to worsen more in the year 2025 across the country. According to the latest IPC report (November 2024) projects in the peak of the lean season period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate, and an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phases 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe); Malakal 10,000, Pibor 12,000, Uror 10000 and Returnees 31000. During this period, the remaining SAADO targeted counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and IPC 3 acute food insecurity About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. These levels are expected to remain elevated in the areas between October 2025 and January 2026, during which some people are likely to continue to experience emergency food insecurity generally the level of severe acute food security in the country remain high and projected to be status quo in 2025, necessitating urgent scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties especially Pibor, Malakal, Aweil east, Aweil North, Pigi, Renk, Fangak, Leer, Mayom and Rubkona which highly share the populations in Emergency( Phase 4) and Catastrophe( IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity which is exacerbated by the continued influx of returnees and Refugees from Sudan crisis According to UNOCHA SS, Flood Snapshot report (As of 15 November 2024), In SS, about 1.4M people remain affected by Flood across 44 counties. More than 379,000 people are flood displaced. Recent assessments in RENK identified over 32,000 flood affected people. The UNOCHA report state that Aweil North 31k people are affected by flooding, 91k in Aweil East, 40k in Pigi, 28k in Rubkona, 32k in Renk, 43K in Mayom and 154k in Fangak, all being displace to higher ground. The floods have led to submerging of food crops and animal dying of diseases of which is the only source of community livelihood leading to population displaced severe food insecurity and elevated level of Malnutrition. According to UNHCR and IOM dashboard 877 240 individual, have crossed into South Sudan from Sudan, putting more pressure to already struggling SAADO targeted counties. Limited food which has been more exacerbated by higher number of returnees predisposed women and girls to walk further to forage for food, firewood and other necessities to survive, exposing them to a greater risk of rape and other forms of GBV. Lack of food will lead to higher level of Malnutrition in which currently IPC report projects an estimated of 2.1 million children aged 6-59 suffer or expected to suffer elevated levels of Acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2une 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
