Project Summary

"This project aims to enhance community safety, resilience, and self-reliance through sustainable lighting solutions, skill-building initiatives, and private sector engagement in Cox’s Bazar. By addressing protection risks, particularly at night, and fostering productive engagement among refugees and host communities, the project contributes to creating safer, more empowered environments.
Key activities are as follows:
Installation, Repair, and Maintenance of Solar Lights: The project ensures safety and security by installing, repairing, and maintaining 747 public solar lights across camps (299 lights) and host communities (448 lights). This includes community-based oversight to sustain the functionality of these lights and ensure well-lit public spaces. Additionally, household (HH) solar lights will be provided to vulnerable individuals to address specific lighting needs.
Community-Based Solar Light Maintenance Training: To ensure sustainability, selected community members—particularly youth—will be trained in solar light repair and maintenance. This capacity-sharing initiative will enhance technical skills, promote self-reliance, and provide productive opportunities for both Rohingya and host youth. The training focuses on building community ownership of lighting infrastructure to ensure its long-term upkeep.
Enhanced Night-Time Safety for Women and Girls: By ensuring adequate lighting in key public spaces, including pathways and WASH facilities, the project addresses risks of gender-based violence (GBV). This activity directly supports safer access to essential services for women, girls, and other vulnerable groups, fostering their dignity and empowerment.
Private Sector Engagement in Humanitarian Efforts: The project prioritizes collaboration with private sector stakeholders to mobilize resources, funding, and expertise. This includes co-creating innovative solutions to improve community resilience and enhance the overall impact of humanitarian interventions.
Expected Outcomes:
 Improved Community Safety: Safer, well-lit public spaces reduce nighttime protection risks for 49,786 refugees and 7,500 host community members.
 Increased Youth Engagement and Skill Development: Solar light maintenance training provides valuable technical skills to youth, promoting productive engagement and self-reliance.
 Stronger Humanitarian-Private Sector Partnership: Partnerships with the private sector enhance the project’s effectiveness, ensuring sustainability and innovation in humanitarian responses.
Population Coverage:
Refugees: Total: 65,942 (Girls: 13003; Boys: 12947; Adult Women: 18595; Adult Men: 17833; Elderly Women: 1808; Elderly Men: 1756).
Host Communities: Total: 7131 (Girls: 1649; Boys: 1893; Adult Women: 1523; Adult Men: 15536; Elderly Women: 265; Elderly Men: 265).
The project will work in close coordination with the Government of Bangladesh (GoB), the Site Coordination and Camp Care and Maintenance (SCCCM) sector, the Energy and Environment Network (EEN), and other relevant stakeholders. By emphasizing community-based approaches and fostering collaboration with private sector actors, the project ensures the long-term sustainability of its interventions. This integrated approach not only addresses immediate protection concerns but also builds resilience and self-reliance among the refugee and host populations in Cox’s Bazar."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

"The prioritization of project is based on the priorities expressed by the community, protection monitoring data, ISNA, assessment report and insights from Oxfam field teams. Now in its seventh year, the protracted crisis is a protection crisis with over 931,574 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar. Protection concerns as a top priority in the refugee camps especially at dark. The stateless Rohingya live in extremely overcrowded camps. Protection risk top priority in the refugee camps compared to host. The Concerns included crime and violence (61%), potential affiliation with criminal groups and restriction of movement. Lack of lighting in the community was explicitly identified as a critical concern, with 57% of respondents mentioning it as a major risk. Reports of people with disabilities falling and injuring themselves even in the presence of caretakers have been documented in the joint protection monitoring reports since 2022. This includes accounts of women -including women with disabilities- facing sexual harassment, fearing kidnapping or sexual assault due to the lack of lighting.The main reasons include presence of criminal groups and unfunctional solar light, only 51% are fully functional, 12% partially and 27% non-functional and 10% missing (Solar light monitoring dashboard), A 13% decrease from 2022 to 2023 in female family members feeling safe at night using communal latrines (WASH Sector). During dark times and if there are no lights, some men are involved in locking the latrine doors while women and girls are inside and take advantage of the situation to commit gender-based violence. (The Joint Protection Monitoring Report (Protection Sector, April-June 2024).Site Management and Energy & Environment Sector are limited in repairing non-functional SSLs. The care and monitoring of the lights require a community-led approach to develop shared responsibility and co-ownership of community facilities like solar lights. Considering the above-mentioned needs and priority Oxfam propose the intervention. "

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"In Oxfam Bangladesh’s programs, Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) is a key principle, ensuring that communities—especially marginalized groups like women, girls, and vulnerable populations—are meaningfully engaged in interventions addressing their specific needs. Oxfam is committed to inclusive programming, characterized by transparent communication, clear expectations, and robust feedback channels for community input and concerns. Oxfam incorporates a Community Feedback Mechanism (CFM) throughout the project design and implementation phases, enhancing program relevance and impact. Accountability is emphasized not only within Oxfam’s operations but also among partners, who are encouraged to practice downward accountability by involving communities and local authorities at all stages. Key strategies include community consultations via focus groups and interviews, with data disaggregated by age, gender, and disability to ensure inclusive dialogues. Oxfam also follows a localization approach, building the capacity of community groups to address issues like WASH, livelihoods, gender, and protection. Through structured consultations, community perceptions are gathered to ensure concerns drive program design and delivery. To further AAP, Oxfam’s CFM offers accessible channels—group discussions, mobile outreach, and household visits—allowing marginalized groups to raise concerns. Data from the CFM is analyzed monthly to close service gaps. A dedicated AAP system ensures feedback collection by trained volunteers and coordination by expert staff. Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) is regularly conducted to capture program insights. Feedback is gathered through various channels, including complaint boxes, interviews, help desks, and hotlines, ensuring transparency and responsiveness to community needs"

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

"For this project, Oxfam, along with its partners, will ensure accountability towards PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) and will take timely actions when necessary. Exclusive training on safeguarding and child rights policies will be provided to staff, non-staff contacts, and community volunteers involved in the project. At the community level, all community group members will be oriented on Oxfam’s safeguarding policy, including key concepts related to sexual harassment, abuse, exploitation, and the appropriate reporting mechanisms. A confidentiality policy will be embedded within this orientation to ensure safe and secure reporting. Additionally, Oxfam proposes to carry out a PSEA risk analysis for the project activities and establish mitigation measures to address any identified risks. As an active member of the PSEA Network, Oxfam will also contribute to joint planning with other programs to address SEA-related risks and promote a safe environment for all stakeholders. By embedding PSEA principles within the project’s design and implementation, Oxfam ensures that protection from sexual exploitation and abuse remains a priority, fostering a safe, accountable environment for participants and staff."

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

"Yes, the project incorporates Climate Action by using solar energy systems for both communal and household purposes, contributing to renewable energy use and reducing reliance on fossil fuels. Additionally, the project emphasizes environmental sustainability by ensuring the reuse of spare parts from solar lights, minimizing waste and promoting a circular economy. E-waste management will be an integral part of the project, with support from the Energy and Environment Network to ensure responsible disposal and recycling of electronic components. These initiatives not only address environmental concerns but also promote climate change adaptation and mitigation by reducing carbon emissions and fostering sustainable practices within the community."

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Oxfam and Partners are jointly taking actions at a regular frequency to addressing emergency preparedness and responses. We have capacitated Emergency Response Team (ERT) and they will be in close connection with the PERU, DMU at camp and DMC (Disaster Management committee) at host community from time to time. During the project tenure, project participants will be capacitated on DRR initiatives. By integrating these measures, the project aims to enhance community resilience, minimize the impact of emergencies, and ensure a rapid and coordinated response to protect lives.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project actively engages youth (15-24 year-olds) by providing them with the opportunity to acquire valuable technical skills in solar light repair and maintenance. Through hands-on training, youth will learn how to install, repair, and maintain solar lights, a crucial skill for sustaining community infrastructure. This engagement not only equips them with practical, marketable skills but also fosters leadership, responsibility, and self-reliance. Youth will play a central role in the ongoing maintenance of solar lights, contributing to the safety and well-being of the community. By involving youth in these activities, the project strengthens their sense of purpose, encourages active participation in community development, and helps mitigate negative coping mechanisms, fostering long-term resilience.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, this project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by equipping them with skills and resources to maintain essential infrastructure within their community. Refugees will receive training on solar light repair and maintenance, enabling them to independently manage and sustain lighting systems vital for their safety and mobility. Additionally, vulnerable households will benefit from household solar lights and emergency preparedness kits, further supporting their resilience and self-sufficiency while preserving their dignity in challenging conditions.

This project aligns with Cox’s Bazar District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022) by fostering sustainable development and resilience within the host community. By strengthening social cohesion through community-based protection mechanisms and conflict resolution initiatives, the project enhances stability between host and refugee communities. Collaborative partnerships with local authorities and development partners ensure that activities are integrated with district-level planning, reinforcing alignment with Cox’s Bazar’s broader development objectives.
