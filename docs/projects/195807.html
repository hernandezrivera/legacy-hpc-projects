Project Summary

The Community Engagement Strengthening Project (CESP) is a response to the growing number of community-based organizations (CBOs) and local non-governmental organizations (LNGOs) across Syria, which have recently emerged in response to the Syrian crisis. In line with the global humanitarian community’s efforts around meaningful localization, CBOs, and LNGOs are taking on prominent roles in humanitarian and early recovery programming through design, targeting, leading consultation, and overseeing implementation. These community actors play a significant role in restoring confidence, sustaining the momentum of recovery and transformation, and bringing divided communities together around activities that represent a mutual benefit. CESP aims to strengthen community platforms that are inclusive, accountable, and responsive to community needs and aspirations. CESP builds on AKF’s early successful pilots through a three-step results-based implementation process of ‘Form, Build, and Action’.
Within two years of implementation, the project will CESP will:
• Form/reform (8) new CBOs, 2 in Damascus, 2 in Rural Damascus and 4 in Hama governorate.
• Build the capacity of 14 newly formed CBOs and existing LNGOs.
• And through a qualified process, provide (30) community small grants (CSGs) to CBOs and LNGOs (12) in Damascus and Rural Damascus and (18) in Hama governorate to identify, design, implement and monitor community-based humanitarian and early recovery projects which meet immediate community needs.
50% of the activities will be implemented during the first year of the project period as follows:
Form/reform (4) new CBOs, 1 in Damascus, 1 in Rural Damascus and 2 in Hama governorate.
• Build the capacity of 7 newly formed CBOs and existing LNGOs.
• provide (30) community small grants (CSGs) to CBOs and LNGOs (6) in Damascus and Rural Damascus and (9) in Hama governorate.
AKF, through its Do No Harm approach, has cultivated a deeper understanding of prevailing authorities, power structures, and possible risks to beneficiaries and project staff. AKF will actively mitigate the unintended consequences of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure broad community participation. CESP will ensure a gender-inclusive approach to these activities by ensuring:
• CBOs formed with adequate representation of women and adolescent girls.
• Organizational capacity assessments of CBOs/LNGOs designed to assess the understanding, knowledge and skills that members have on gender equality and women’s empowerment.
•And community-based consulting groups for the award of CSGs have adequate representation of women and adolescent girls. CESP incorporates evidence-based design and implementation and continual beneficiary feedback loops. The project utilizes comprehensive evidence-gathering mechanisms which prioritize primary evidence gathering to determine relevance, impact, safety and efficiency of project design and implementation.

3 - Needs

Early Recovery and Livelihood needs remain large across Syria, with an estimated 11.6 million people 51% Female needing ERL support (HNO 2021). About two thirds of households lost at least one income sources, and half lost more than 80% of their income. This contributed to eroding socioeconomic foundations of Syrian people, affecting their ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access social services. This has led to more strains on social cohesion and community security, as well as stresses affecting the social capital of Syrian communities. Due to multiple challenges placing pressure on local communities, there are gaps in social services uniquely municipal work like solid waste removal. Salamieh has a population of 231,000, 51.6% females3.1% PwD14.5% IDPs and 7%returnees. The living cost increased last year as well as the prices of commodities and services while the average income remained the same making it difficult for vulnerable families to afford minimum requirements. According to AKF Syria’s 2021 Rapid Need Assessment conducted in Salamieh a total of 41,990 households/17,6617 individuals 51.6% females are vulnerable vulnerable Today. The humanitarian landscape in Syria is more diverse with LNGOs working with the UN system and INGOs to address challenges. These LNGOs need building up community platforms while they have a sub-contracting relationship and unable to fully mature since donors are partners with INGOs.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

AKF staff, CBO/LNGO members may be accused of bias in selecting beneficiaries or defining community needs. Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria, engaged of all stakeholders. Regular and confidential monitoring and feedback/compliant reporting system will help identify cases of exploitations and take actions against them. AKF and the consultation groups will assess the proposals to ensure relevance to community needs and ensure neutrality in selecting beneficiaries.Gender-related cultural and traditional norms and roles may discriminate/limit women’s participation in the CBO/LNGO or the small grants/community initiatives. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. AKF will explain and ensure women representation in CBO/LNGOs and promote their active involvement during the formation and implementation phase (at least 30 % females). Women will be asked preference of specific work/task to ensure their comfort. AKF will facilitate gender equality awareness-raising.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation.If the mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within the Salamieh District is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation can be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk 2 : Inflation and exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Likelihood: High, Impact: low. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors exchange rate gains and losses to the impact of fluctuations on the project and revise as needed. Infation risk ismitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF manages reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation. Risk3 Lack of adherence on the part of MOSAL to deliver required permits results in either delayed or protracted approval processes for the legalization of CBO/LNGO projects. Mitigation: AKF will cooperate with CBOs/LNGOs to adjust implementation timelines till approvals are secured otherwise will have to allocate the fund for other priority projects of CSGs.
