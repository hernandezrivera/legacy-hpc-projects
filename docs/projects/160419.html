Project Summary

The aim of the project is to provide lifesaving assistance for 2517 (7550 individuals) the most vulnerable families in Donetsk NGCA. The overall cost of the project is 700,000 USD and will include social institutions rehabilitation, hygiene promotion messages and distribution of hygiene kits.
Education institutions (schools and kindergartens) will have rehabilitation of toilets thus conflict-affected population will be benefited from direct improvements to sanitary facilities. This activity will contribute to WASH Cluster objective #1. “Ensure immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision, for conflict affected people.” In addition, SCI will target households with children that are elderly-headed or female-headed or with a member with disabilities, and distribute hygiene promotion messages followed by hygiene NFIs. These activities will contribute to WASH Cluster objective #2. Provision of critical WASH-related supplies and information for the prevention of water- and sanitation-related diseases. Furthermore, the project will ensure that hygiene needs (including for women, girls and elderly) of the most vulnerable families are met and are in line with WASH Cluster recommendations.
1. To ensure conflict-affected population (men, women, boys, girls) benefited from the provision of hygiene items;
2. To promote hygiene messages to conflict-affected population;
3. To ensure conflict-affected people in social institutions benefiting from direct improvements to sanitary facilities.

1 - Needs

Assessments made by SCI and other agencies have shown that items are generally available in markets but the purchasing power of many households (HH) has decreased substantially. 23% of HH living on both sides of the line of contact reported that expenditures on hygiene items were only second to food (REACH 2019). Hygiene item prices in Donetsk NGCA are 25% above the average level (ACTED 2018). Focus group discussions (FGD) in NGCA report a decrease in the availability and access to NFI including 21% of HH lacking essential winter shoes, 13% having difficulty accessing markets, and 20% needing hygiene items (REACH 2017). In NGCA areas a joint market monitoring study found that process of hygiene items in Non-Government Controlled areas are 25% above the national average and not everyone has access to all materials: diapers are especially expensive, and people affected by the conflict are forced to take tough decisions about what to spend their money on. (WASH Cluster Guidelines (2018). In MSNA in NGCA (REACH 2019) WASH needs appear to be stable, with limited changes in access to water amongst urban households since 2017. Children, in particular children with disabilities (CWD), are among the most vulnerable populations in Donetsk NGCA. For instance, economic insecurity and stress in the HH can lead to higher risks of domestic violence against children and SGBV. There are 3.1M people in need of protection, including 465,000 children (209,250 boys; 255,750 girls) (2019 HNO). The crisis in eastern Ukraine has a disproportionately high impact on the elderly, given that they represent 26% of the population in need (2019 HNO). Elderly-headed HH with children are particularly vulnerable due to difficulties to access pensions (especially those living in NGCA), face disability, potential incapacity to work, and have specific needs (health, hygiene etc.) that they might find difficult to afford. In Ukraine, people and CWD are often excluded from assistance as they are “invisible” due to mobility restrictions and stigma (2019 HNO). It is estimated that 95% of HH with disabled members do not receive assistance in NGCA (REACH 2019). Children living in residential care are at heightened risk of abuse (Disability Rights International 2015). 45% of HH in NGCA as now female-headed HH (FHH) (REACH 2018). FHH face particular challenges with 39% currently unemployed, 33% reporting shelter damage and a lack of basic infrastructure, and 21% exhibiting poor and borderline levels of food consumption, placing them at greater risk to food insecurity and extreme winter conditions, in comparison to male-headed HH. 14% of households in GCA and 13% in NGCA have unsatisfactory hygiene practices, including the use of unhygienic menstrual hygiene materials, poor water handling or lack of access to basic hygiene products such as soap, laundry detergent, toilet paper or shampoo. This problem is exacerbated for households in rural areas, poor households, as well as households with disabled and elderly people (WASH Cluster Humanitarian Needs Assessment study 2019). According to Education Cluster 84% or a total of 771 education facilities reported that they need rehabilitation for some of the infrastructural components. Top 3 categories are: roof (46%), windows (45%) and WASH facilities (45%), while 40% or 374 education facilities reported need in rehabilitation of heating system.

2 - Activities & outcomes

1) Conducting hygiene promotion messages to conflict affected population. Targeted affected households will receive relevant information and knowledge on hygiene through participation in hygiene awareness sessions that will highlight:-How to store and use water hygienically: for drinking, cooking, washing the house.- Food hygiene: clean surfaces and tools, storing food, flies issues.-Household sanitation: disposal of dirty diapers, menstrual hygiene items, garbage and emptying septic tanks hygienically.-Personal Hygiene: Keeping your body clean: whole body, hair, face and ears, fingernails, and teeth.-Hand washing: when to wash hands and why we need to use soap.-Intimate hygiene: menstrual hygiene and sexual health.Main activities:-Frame work agreement with implementing partner -Identification and pre-selection of beneficiaries-Baseline-Delivering hygiene promotion messages-Endline.2) Distribution of Hygiene items.The project will use comprehensive approach and envisage distribution of hygiene items both on household and institutional level. Based on WASH Cluster recommendations institutional and family hygiene kits composition will be used accordingly. For those families with infants and small children as well as elderly members or people with disabilities hygiene kits will include diapers. The number of hygiene kits distributions will be defined based on needs and after consultations with de-facto authorities and local implementing partners. Main activities:-Framework agreement with implementing partner-Assessment of locations, coordination with de-facto authorities and other NGOs, registration of beneficiaries.-Selection of beneficiaries based on vulnerability criteria, baseline.-Selection of supplier for hygiene items procurement and delivery-Sign frame work agreement with supplier-Distribution of items-Vendor’s payment and reconciliation-Post distribution monitoring\endline, monitoring of proper hygiene items utilization.3) Rehabilitation works of education facilities. The rehabilitation works will include WASH repairs in educational institutions in Donetsk NGCA which include rehabilitation of sanitation facilities that is in line with norms and standards for educational institutions, including assets for children with disabilities (restroom access, handrails, water system repair etc.) based on needs and after consultations with de-facto authorities and local agencies for better coordination.It is planned to allocate 20K $ per one facility (based on previous experience) in order to address water and sanitation needs.Rehabilitation will take place in Donetsk NGCA that have been damaged by the hostilities and will include renovation of wash infrastructure, depending on the renovations needed in each school. In NGCA, schools will be selected in coordination with de-facto authorities and preferably along the contact line. All the works will be carried out in line with the Save the Children Quality Learning Environment framework and wash standards, meeting the safety standards of Save the Children, Education Cluster, WASH Cluster, Child Protection sub cluster and Ministry of Education, and will ensure to the extent possible accessibility for children with disabilities. Rehabilitation will be supervised by Save the Children's staff and partners and will include a technical assessment, technical specifications and BOQs. The rehabilitation works will be conducted by a contractor. Save the Children will ensure safeguarding of beneficiaries to ensure that no child is put at risk during the rehabilitation works and that mechanisms are in place to report risks.
