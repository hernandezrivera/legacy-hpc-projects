Project Summary

CMMB has carefully designed project activities that contribute to protection cluster’s priority objectives PO1 and PO2 in Mvolo, Yambio and Ezo counties. Building on over 10 years of experience addressing GBV needs in Western Equatoria, CMMB proposes to focus on providing Comprehensive GBV case management services including clinical management of rape, psychosocial support, women and girls friendly activities and cash based intervention through 3 women and girls friendly spaces and 7 anchor health facilities integrating health, nutrition, and child protection activities. These interventions will contribute to PO1.
Under PO2, beneficiaries will be linked to other services e.g. health, livelihood, nutrition etc. based on need and availability of services. Strengthening referral systems to enhance safe referrals for survivors; community engagement and capacity building of staff and community structures will ensure targeted communities are adequately prepared to prevent, mitigate, and respond to GBV issues. Women and girls friendly spaces will also act as central linkages to other services e.g. nutrition and livelihood in addition to providing PSS and GBV case management services. With a protection lens, the project targets women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of sexual violence in addition to vulnerable men and boys in the highlighted key activities in Mvolo,Yambio and Ezo counties.
To strengthen community-based protection structures/ networks while sensitizing communities on key GBV issues through various forums, 4 Women safety committees will be established at the community level and 1 communities care program rolled out to strengthen community engagement towards prevention and response of GBV. Over 4000 community members are expected to benefit. Protection monitoring will help in identifying GBV risk factors engaging affected communities and designing suitable response mechanisms, increasing advocacy, and providing critical information for humanitarian interventions. The project aims to provide GBV life saving services to 12478 beneficiaries from Mvolo,Yambio and Ezo counties.

1 - Needs

As noted by the 2021 South Sudan HNO, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 in 2020 affecting close to 4million people. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labor and child marriage.The Human Rights Watch report on 30th November 2020, highlighted that sexual violence, including rape, gangrape, sexual slavery, sexual exploitation, genital mutilation, forced nudity and other forms of GBV including child and forced marriage, are widespread in South Sudan. Since the first lock down, WES has experienced an increase in GBV incidences that continue undeterred, escalated by negative cultural norms, loss of livelihood and school closure due to the pandemic, increasing insecurity in the region and poor protection services for the most vulnerable. According to the April-September Humanitarian Situation Monitoring report by REACH in WES, the most mentioned protection concerns for women in WES was domestic violence (44%) and sexual and gender-based violence (13%). For girls, teenage pregnancies and early marriage was the prevalent protection concern (61%). Another report by a South Sudanese civil society organization (Nov 2020) according to HRW, reported that since COVID 19 restrictions were imposed in March 2020,at least 1535 girls were subjected to child marriages, pregnancies, and sexual exploitation in Western, Eastern and Central Equatoria states. While the real figures in WES are not known, the situation is expected to be much higher now. The report also noted issues affecting men/boys were violence with neighbors (26%), alcohol consumption (21%), lack of employment, economic hardship, and idleness. Protection related issues like abduction, recruitment and use of children by armed groups, exploitation and abuse have been widely reported. Loss of household incomes may increase protection risks e.g. survival sex, child marriage, child labor and sexual exploitation. Girls, women, and persons living with disabilities are unable to afford hygiene related supplies. Although Yambio town has presence of few GBV services, the needs are great especially in the far payams where GBV case management, PSS,CMR, functional referral pathways as well WGFS are minimal. Inadequate health trained personnel and long distances to access basic services hinders access to survivor centered lifesaving services. From CMMB’s service delivery reports, domestic violence has been noted as most prevalent, low reporting levels of GBV incidents, limited access to justice compounded by lack of adequately trained law enforcement officers, long distance to police stations and sometimes demand for money for form 8. In Ezo county, gaps in service provision are prevalent e.g. access to GBV specialized case management and PSS services. No WGFS to provide safe space for women and girls and key GBV information. Mvolo is one of the hard to reach counties with limited access to GBV case management,PSS,CMR and WGFS services. Perennial inter clan conflicts, early/forced marriages, prolonged absence of GBV services and IPC reports that Mvolo county is expected to be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity compound protection needs. CMMB implements Maternal, Child health and Child Protection and GBV activities funded by UNICEF that have created awareness on the support services for rape survivors among other services however more remains to be done. CMMB’s health clinics have received and treated several rape cases, including cases of children. CMMB is the Co-lead for CPWG and Covid-19 and best placed to address GBV related issues and Covid-19 referral pathways for care and support of vulnerable women, girls, and child survivors

2 - Project activities

CMMB will implement activities in 3 counties of WES working with beneficiaries(vulnerable girls, women, the elderly, and people living with disabilities),State government, protection cluster and respective communities to increase protection for targeted groups. The activities include: Comprehensive GBV case management, clinical management of rape(CMR),referrals and establishment of women and girls friendly spaces(WGFS) in Mvolo, Yambio and Ezo counties.138 beneficiaries; 84 women, 41girls, 4 men and 9 boys will receive individualized GBV case management services. To enhance psychosocial wellbeing of women, children, and men, provision of survivor-centered PSS services will be prioritized focusing on healing, empowerment, and recovery. 1250 beneficiaries; 750 women and 500men will be targeted with PSS including recreational and livelihood activities through 3 WGFS and anchor health facilities to help survivors including adolescent girls, boys, women, and other youth re-build their resilience. Key information regarding available services, referrals, basic GBV prevention measures, basic nutrition information and PSS will be shared. Other partners e.g. food security and nutrition will be incorporated to offer specialized basic trainings, necessary tools/supplies, and professional guidance. 400 girls and 400 women are targeted.Cash based individual Protection Assistance/NFIs will be provided to 100 vulnerable beneficiaries selected through established vulnerability criteria.To facilitate safe referrals,4 GBV referral pathways will be established/strengthened linking survivors to available services e.g. child protection, nutrition and health in the targeted locations. Child survivors will receive child protection services through CMMB’s CP program and the team will visit WGFS on specified days to support children who might accompany their mothers/caregivers. 30 staff, community volunteers and frontline workers will be trained on GBV case management and a further 40 trained to provide individual or group specialized PSS etc incorporating COVID-19 prevention measures, psychological first aid, prevention of sexual exploitation, self-care, and stress management and key GBV concepts for communities. Community engagement and awareness raising on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and how to access support in line with the “do no harm” principle will target 2900 women, 2500 girls,700 boys, and 700 men. Available services, referrals, women rights etc. through community outreach/campaigns in schools, safe spaces, churches under strict adherence to COVID-19 protocols will also be rolled out. Under the communities care model developed by UNICEF on GBV prevention, 15 week community discussions will be conducted clustered into 7 groups to raise awareness and promote positive norms fighting against sexual violence. After the community launch of the program 2220(Girls-600,Women-760,Men-430,Boys-430 community members will be targeted for sensitization by trained groups. Training on reusable sanitary pad making for adolescent girls and women will help them deal with their menstrual cycles in a dignified. 1100 dignity kits will be requested from the GBV sub cluster and distributed to needy women and adolescent girls. CMMB will continue to actively participate in coordination forums e.g. GBV working group, protection cluster as well as co-chair the state child protection working group. A monitoring and evaluation system will be established to ensure that all data collected are disaggregated based on age, sex and type of service received, using cluster approved tools. The data will guide decision making aimed at improving service delivery to meet beneficiary needs. Submission of relevant reports to the GBV sub cluster, protection cluster and other stakeholders will be adhered to. To ensure accountability to affected population, CMMB will strengthen the established ‘community complaint and feedback mechanism’ as well as establish new ones.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

i. Functioning markets continue to be supplied as they have not been locked down, and goods from Sudan are encouraged through easy access at the borders in the North.ii. Preferred and local food items are available although purchasing power continues to dip with the deepening economic crisis. Hence cash transfer will help access better nutrition and sustain market access.iii. Markets use local currency, and the same currency will be used in the cash transfer scope.iv. Markets are within the targeted Payams and so access, safety and security is currently unaffected.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash or vouchers provides individually tailored immediate support to survivors of GBV and reduces the risk of negative coping strategies that exposes vulnerable women and girls to GBV. Cash and vouchers require functional markets and the target areas selected in this project have access to functional markets. Yambio also has cash transfer agents that will reduce the risk related with cash and voucher security risks. Cash and vouchers approaches respond better in Covid-19 restrictions and lockdowns, as families can stock dry and staple food for longer periods, and buy them time, while they resuscitate their livelihoods.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Community awareness raising sessions on key GBV issues will include messages on PSEA, existing mechanisms to handle incidents, reiterate on confidentiality and safety of beneficiaries and established channels. This will also be disseminated through the WFGS and targeted health facilities.To reduce potential risks of SEA all staff will be trained on PSEA. Appointment of PSEA Focal Points across the project sites will be done who will also conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men. They will ensure that communities are aware of their rights and entitlements through posters, community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas and recommending protective actions to combat SEA will be a key focus in all project cycle phases. Working with targeted communities the Focal Points design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints (action plans for who, what will do, where, and how) with a contact for reporting any suspicions, concerns, issues, questions, and cases. All staff must sign a code of conduct that will spell out what is expected of them as relates to SEA matters. Effective prevention mechanisms, including effective recruitment and performance management and establishment of internal complaints and investigation procedures will be a priority. An effective and anonymous complaints mechanism to allow and encourage individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially, and safely will be strengthened. All such complaints should be immediately brought to the attention of the Program Director either directly by the beneficiary or by the person to whom such an incident is reported or through the PSEA focal points. It has been established that the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is greater for girls, boys, and women of concern.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community feedback mechanisms are developed through the volunteers, community meetings, focus group discussions, displaying available complaint options, using pictures and suggestion boxes where appropriate. Project start up workshop involving all relevant stakeholders share project details that will enable the community to hold the project team accountable during project implementation. Joint quarterly progress reviews and meetings address key concerns/gaps and jointly develop appropriate recommendations/follow up actions that are reviewed in the next meetings. The community volunteers will also play a key role in connecting communities with project activities at various stages e.g. needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation. Upholding confidentiality and protection of beneficiaries will be enhanced through engaging smaller numbers of participants, using anonymous notes, coding of information and placing suggestion boxes in confidential locations under lock and key that will be managed by the community complaint and feedback assistant. Multiple reporting channels will also enhance confidentiality.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the onset of the project, a startup workshop will be conducted with key stakeholders where key project activities will be explained, roles and responsibilities of each stakeholder defined including the set up of the community complaint and feedback mechanisms. Focus group discussions with specific beneficiary groups will further explain the interventions and how they can participate in activities and monitoring the various aspects. They will be consulted on appropriate complaint and feedback mechanisms that will suit their needs. The committees will draw members from the various beneficiary groups who will be further trained on the process of soliciting, receiving, processing andresponding to the feedback and complaints received.Women and girls accessing the safe space will receive information through the weekly meetings on key project activities and how to participate in providing feedback on services rendered. Women’s safety committees will ensure key GBV messages and concerns are identified and reported while appropriate strategies are jointly developed to address any protection risks. Quarterly review meetings will also provide opportunities for information sharing. Targeted radio messages will sensitize communities on various key messages as well as churches and other community events observing COVID 19 guidelines including information shared via megaphone by community mobilizers. Children will be reached through schools and PSS sessions while women groups/leaders will be ambassadors for peace and conflict resolution messages. The community complaint and feedback committee members will identify, record, and report any community concerns related to assistance provided. Through established channels ,CMMB will address the concerns or channel to those concerned.For instance, at the women and girls’ friendly spaces women and girls can easily share GBV concerns, lessons and build social support systems that addresses individual issues. In the targeted community women and girls are rarely involved in decision making, to enhance participation separate meetings for girls and women will be conducted and encouraged when addressing sensitive GBV issues. Men and boys will also be separately encouraged to champion women and girls’ protection against GBV as well have separate forums to discuss their GBV concerns. When appropriate joint forums can be held. Age and gender specific messages will be designed with beneficiaries on key GBV concerns, safe referrals, and available services. Pictures will be used to reach children and people with low literacy levels while literate ones will have access to written materials. Drama plays and songs will compliment gaps from the other forms of messages/communication when the COVID 19 situation improves.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Continuous community engagement and provision of feedback has been instrumental in identifying needs and guiding design of appropriate GBV activities. Community feedback mechanisms will continue to be enhanced through the volunteers, community meetings, focus group discussions, displaying of available complaint options and suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations at the women and girls’ friendly spaces and other sites. Project start up workshops involving all relevant stakeholders are platforms to share project details that enable them, and communities hold the project team accountable during implementation and follow ups. At this workshop roles and responsibilities of various stakeholders are clearly defined and appropriate feedback mechanisms shared. Available feedback options are shared including contacts where the beneficiaries can directly report their concerns without involving project teams. Regular weekly meetings at the women friendly spaces focusing on each target group ensure their concerns are shared and jointly addressed or shared with relevant stakeholders. Services are in centrally agreed locations with beneficiaries to reduce conflict and each target group consulted to cater for their individual needs. Regular interactions and meetings will ensure beneficiaries amicably solve their issues with the project team as well as among themselves.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project focuses on affected population’s ability to identify, understand, and support their own protection needs. Local groups e.g. women groups are supported to enhance the protection of persons affected and at risk as they better understand the local context. Different segments of affected populations are meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being through focus group discussions, key informants, and participation in activities. At risk populations have been identified, how and why they are at risk considering their specific vulnerabilities including those experienced by men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as internally displaced persons, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other minority groups.The project identifies and addresses barriers to access. The WGFS will be in safe and central locations to ensure beneficiaries do not walk long distances. Lack of appropriate information sharing protocols that might expose sensitive information discouraging survivors seeking services will be minimized through approved information sharing protocols and GBVIMS tools. Limited access to project sites by people living with disabilities, old people and young children will be addressed through engaging community volunteers to identify and report these people. The project activities address issues of inequalities and protection threats by identifying the threats, who is affected, how they are affected and why through community-based protection structures e.g. women safety committees, adolescent girls and young women empowerment initiatives, self-esteem, and rights awareness including men and boys engage clubs and women groups.Protection risk analysis has been considered to ensure that targeted populations are not exposed to more harm through the services provided. WGFS may seem to be only targeting women and girls through the name and activities that are conducted however measures have been taken to encourage participation of men through men engage clubs that will share the safe spaces on an agreed upon modality. Each activity conducted within the project will be within acceptable parameters of various groups of populations who will be consulted prior to the activity. Community safety audits will identify protection risks of each group and address them. Local conflict resolution mechanisms will supersede any foreign ideology to ensure fitting into the local context and reducing harm. Sensitive information will be shared through the agreed upon information sharing protocols and coding. Efforts have been incorporated to avoid open discussions of sensitive GBV cases/information through case conferences with those responsible for offering direct services to survivors. Establishment of women and girls’ friendly spaces that target survivors of GBV e.g. rape or trafficking and putting in place means that facilitate access for people with disabilities ensures unhindered access to services and reduces stigma.GBV risks, threats and grave violations identified through protection risk analysis are minimized through monitoring, reporting, coordination, and advocacy with relevant authorities to secure populations when providing assistance. Culturally appropriate burials, religious ceremonies and practices, and non-harmful cultural and social practices are supported for survivors of violence to boost their self-esteem. Child survivors of GBV are supported to participate in structured and protective activities at the child friendly spaces integrated in WGFS activities to enhance their psychosocial support. Safe referrals for CMR, treatment and mental health services are facilitated through established referral pathways. Integration of nutrition, health, and child protection services with GBV interventions ensures maximum support for targeted population.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CMMB runs an MNCH program through providing clinical services in 10 health facilities that will provide medical care to women of reproductive age and children under 5. All other project beneficiaries will be referred to HPF clinics, most of which co-locate with CMMB MNCH clinics and provide free services. Through the women and girls friendly spaces and targeted health facilities, GBV case management, PSS and medical support will be offered to survivors of GBV. Based on need, consent, availability and relevance, referrals will be done for other services i.e. legal support, safety and medical care for service deliver points not linked to health facilities. CMMB will work closely with UNDP for GBV related legal services. CMMB will also advocate for more protection by the State government, in particular the Ministry f Gender and social welfare in Yambio. Appropriate protocols and guidelines will be followed in providing both protection related and other services. These services will take into account age, gender, and priority.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Through the livelihood and cash assistance that will be provided after analyzing various shocks, identified beneficiaries will be provided with support that will help alleviate their level of suffering. Cash or vouchers provides individually tailored immediate support to survivors of GBV and reduces the risk of negative coping strategies that exposes vulnerable women and girls to GBV eg early marriage and survival sex.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The design of the project activities has taken into consideration the needs of various vulnerable groups hence delivery will be customized to suit identified needs. Where possible integration of service delivery will be emphasized to maximize use of resources and avoid beneficiaries covering long distances between various services. Community structures e.g. volunteers will be engaged to reach old people, sick, people with disabilities and young children with services to ensure they do not miss out on accessing GBV, health and Nutrition services.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

CMMB is implementing a 3 year Nutrition program in WES funded by UNICEF, in the targeted counties, including Mvolo. Through a CMMB Integrated program approach, this project will refer all identified incidence of acute malnutrition in the targeted communities of both Children under five, as well as pregnant and lactating women and the elderly. The program will work with the Nutrition program to ensure mothers for children under 5 are provided with education on nutrition related awareness, including food preparation, kitchen gardening and hygiene. This project will also collaborate with CMMB’s WASH program to ensure the HHs of children with severe acute malnutrition are provided with water purification sachets for clean drinking water. The project also aims to undertake a cash distribution activity to support identified vulnerable families, including children under 5 in severity levels 3 and 4.
