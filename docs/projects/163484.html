Project Summary

The boycott of schools within the English Regions of Cameroon following the escalation of the Anglophone crisis has entered the third year without any hope for children to have access to education. More than 100,000 children in the nursery, primary and secondary levels have been deprived of their basic rights to education due to armed violence and threats. As the crisis worsened, there has been a rapid increase of the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) seeking refuge in the other regions of Cameroon, especially the Littoral, West and Centre regions.
These IDPs are very vulnerable considering that they have lost most of their property including identification documents as they escaped from violent clashes in their initial communities. This makes it very difficult for IDP parents to support their children’s education.
A survey carried out by NADEV and 6 other CSOs revealed that, more than 90% of Urban IDPs family heads from the English regions of Cameroon are barely begging to sustain themselves, their children and other dependents. It is thus obvious that it would be practically impossible for them to provide essential education for their children without support. Tens of thousands of school going age displaced children may end up permanently dropping out of school.
This project is designed to re-insert 250 internally displaced children into primary school and 150 into secondary school.
The project will target IDPs in two regions: the West and the Littoral Regions. These regions are the nearest safest host communities for vulnerable IDPs from the South West and North West Regions of Cameroon. For effective implementation, NADEV will be involved in community mobilization, follow up and partner with school authorities in the above-mentioned regions to ensure that some of these children are enrolled in school.
4. Rationale for the proposed project (what are the factors that underline the reason to start the project)
• First and foremost, education is increasingly viewed as the “fourth pillar” or a “central pillar” of humanitarian response alongside the pillars of nourishment, shelter and health services. So there is need for these children to return to school.
• Secondly, these children are being deprived of their education which is their hope for a brighter future.
• Also, from the findings of a quick survey of urban IDPs a good number of Internally Displaced children living with new families are being forced to sell on the streets; fleeing girls cohabit with men for survival and other youths, some as young as 12, are being recruited into the armed groups. Therefore, education provides opportunities for children, their families and communities to begin the trauma healing process, and to learn the skills and values needed for a more peaceful future and better governance at local and national levels.
• Furthermore, denying children access to quality education as is the case with the children of the North West and South West regions, increases their vulnerability to abuse, exploitation, and disease with long term effects including: intergenerational transmission of illiteracy, unemployment/precarious financial position, low self-esteem, increased banditry and juvenile delinquency.
5. Project goals & outcomes
Goal: To ensure quality education for vulnerable internally displaced children from the English Speaking Regions of Cameroon into schools.
Objective: Provide access to safe, protective and inclusive education for 400 internally displaced children.
Outcomes:
• 400 IDPs children (250 primary and 150 secondary) have access to quality education.
• Number of displaced children enrolled in schools is increased.
