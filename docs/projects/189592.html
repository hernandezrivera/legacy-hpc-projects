Project Summary

This project targets 43,000 vulnerable beneficiaries in four counties identified to have been severely affected by lack of food that was worsened by natural disasters and human conflicts. The livelihood activities were interrupted by flood which destroyed crops and vegetables, resulting into death of animals, spread of waterborne diseases and has left many people especially women, children, elderly persons and people with disabilities more vulnerable. Cultivation of crops and vegetables have failed due to the massive floods, as arable land was claimed by water. Lack of fishing gears denied the people from getting food from the nearby rivers and swamps. Relief food from WFP remains inadequate to meet their humanitarian needs. As the result, the harvest was insufficient and far lower to meet the consumption and dietary needs of the people. Inter-communal conflicts also exposed the people to protection risks and varying levels of vulnerability. IDPs, host communities and returnees are in dire need of food assistance and protection. The purpose of this project is four-fold: (a) to provide food assistance through provision of cash transfers and vouchers to 2,150 vulnerable households, (b) to provide agricultural inputs to 8,600 households, (c) to enhance the capacity of 1,000 farmers , (d) establishment of eight (8) nutrition gardens and (e) to provide vaccination and treatment services to 8,000 livestock. . In response to climate change, the project will incorporate climate smart agriculture techniques so that an environmental protection is factored in it. Safety and gender based violence will be mainstreamed in the implementation of of the project. The intervention will require USD 675,000 for one year

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022 for South Sudan, indicate that 8.9 million people are estimated to be in need of humanitarian aid in 2022, compared to 8.3 million in 2021. This increase is largely driven by compounded shocks triggered by continued sub-national conflict, widespread (third consecutive year) flooding, deepening food insecurity, inflation, and lack of access to basic services greatly contributing to the increase of needs. These effects of shocks weaken people’s livelihoods and general survival. Affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources. Besides the foregoing HNO 2022 analysis the needs in the ADA targeted counties for this HRP are further informed by other corroborative analyses: REACH, FSL Cluster and ADA internal needs assessments. The REACH report of December 10th 2021 - [Ref: https://www.reach-initiative.org/what-we-do/news/780000-people-are-affected-by-flooding-in-south-sudan] – provide insights into the counties targeted by ADA in this HRP. It points to the fact that 70,000 people were ‘struggling to survive’ in Ayod County that had more severe floods than in previous years causing widespread livelihood collapse, compounded by inability to depend on traditional coping strategies and income generating activities. It further stresses that flooding prevented people from cultivating their lands since 2020, resultin in populations converging on highlands and further to Sudan. Given the trends of floods it is difficult for communities to continue living in Fangak, alternative measures for livelihood are important. Besides, the report noted that many households will now go two years without cultivation, whilst facing a reduction in access to cattle, whilst increased dependency on wild foods and other coping strategies have been limited due to the remaining flood waters. The REACH report noted that Canal-Pigi County had seen two years of consecutive flooding since August 2020 and at the time of the assessment still remained flooded, with flood waters unlikely to recede anytime soon. Flooding had led to widespread collapse of livelihoods, severely affecting the ability of households to maintain livestock, and decreasing community and household coping capacities. Due to flooding, dependence on traditional coping strategies and income generating activities had reportedly been severely affected. The loss of traditional livelihoods along with reportedly minimal access to humanitarian services in Pigi/Canal is increasing food insecurity in the county. Flooding has led to displacement in the county with the majority of communities moving to the highlands along the Nile and Sobat rivers, as well as along the Jonglei canal. ADA can affirm (based on presence in Pigi/Canal) that that humanitarian response remains challenged by the floods with disruptions and delays in services. Despite the challenges, ADA has presence would like to use its presence as a launching pad to support the FSL interventions under this HRP.The FSL Sectoral Analysis Clasification for HNO 2022 – 27th Jan 2022 (https://fscluster.org/south-sudan-rep) provides an analysis of FSL targets for different counties in South Sudan in 2021. All four proposed counties of ADA choice for the 2022 HRP are classified by the FSL Cluster as either ‘counties of extreme concern’ or ‘counties of severe concern’. In Jonglei State, Ayod, Fangak and Pigi/Canal were amongst the 13 ‘counties of extreme concern’. It is noteworthy to report that ADA was part of the 22 FSL partners that provided food assistance in Jonglei State in 2021 and would like to continue to do so in 2022. Meanwhile, in Upper Nile State, Nasir/Luakpiny County was one of the 18 counties of ‘severe concern’. In 2021, ADA was part of the 14 FSL partners that supported food assistance in Upper Nile State and would like to continue filling the food gaps in 2022. 5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The assistance in cash or vouchers is proposed for less logistical and time consuming costs. Transportation of supplies in South Sudan is too expensive and time consuming due to lack of road infrastructure and security conditions that restrict movement of the people to reach the beneficiaries in their localities or camping places. The cash will meet the diet diversity requirements for beneficiaries who are not able to produce or hunt for food from far production location Cash is is easy to handle by vulnerable person

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

It was assessed and found that markets exist in all the targeted locations. The beneficiaries will be able to buy goods from the local markets from the local traders.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries' feedback and requests will be incorporated into projects implementation plans during the delivery, monitoring and community engagement process. ADA programme teams will adapt relevant and useful information that can help both ADA and beneficiaries to implement activities safely and in dignified manner considering the locally available opportunities and newly emerging conditions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Beneficiaries' help desk will reach individuals living with disabilities. All the people with disabilities will be identified by caseworkers and will be given special consideration during the assistance especially during the distributions. During the documentation of feedback from beneficiaries, qualified local translators to ensure the information required is delivered in its original meaning. Confidentiality will be fully ensured especially for girls and women.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Awareness campaigns will be organized and messages will be disseminated through public announcements using microphones, radio and through women and youth groups in the community. Posters/banners and PSEA flyers will be distributed and posted in public areas such as school, health facility and church premises. ADA will train new staff and will properly make use its well experienced case workers to mentor new staffs and community volunteers to be involved in the project implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Formation of community based project implementation management committee is one of strategies that will create transparency and will well informed the beneficiaries on project implementation process. Help Desk will be established for documentation, processing and monitoring of feedback or complaint mechanism from beneficiaries during the services delivery. ADA will immediately respond to complaints through its feedback mechanism system.
