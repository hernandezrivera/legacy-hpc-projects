Project Summary

CRS proposes a twelve-month emergency response project to provide shelter to 7,500 conflict-affected Internally Displaced Person (IDP) households in Chiure and Balama. This program will benefit from the work, influence, and geographical reach of local implementing partner Caritas Pemba. The proposed project will expand CRS ongoing’ humanitarian response programming supported by USAID and other private donors targeting IDPs affected by the conflict in Cabo Delgado.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The gap of assisted families with immediate shelter NFIs and Emergency Shelters is still big and the number of IDPs continues to increase especially in Cabo Delgado, a further expansion in humanitarian capacity and presence on the ground is critical to prevent a further deterioration of the humanitarian situation. In this HRP for 2022, CRS aims to assist 37,500 individuals (7,500 HHs) with essential household items and support with the construction, retrofit and/or upgrade of shelters that withstand harsh weather conditions which will contribute to secure safe, dignified, and healthy living conditions of the population affected by the insecurity crisis, but also that are extremely vulnerable to natural hazards. In addition to these efforts, CRS will ensure that local artisans and population, increases their knowledge and applies the Build Back Better principles.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

1. Participatory identification, assessment and selection of beneficiaries based on pre-defined criteria of vulnerable affected households in close coordination with local stakeholders, other agencies and related Clusters and integrated within CRS overall response plan.2. Procurement and receiving of appropriately designed shelter NFIs based on needs identified, contextual appropriateness and value for money in terms of transportation and speed of delivery.3. Procurement of locally available shelter construction materials (wooden poles (estacas), bamboo, roofing and fixing materials, tools, etc.).4. Transportation and distribution of shelter materials as per distribution plan and following COVID 19 IPC procedures.5. Carry out distributions to families in a dignified and secure environment, accompanied by information/ awareness raising on how to use materials distributed to increase the benefit they provide to the beneficiaries and the length of time the materials remain in a condition fit for use.6. Ensure active participation of the most vulnerable groups in distributions and beneficiary selection, and promote women related working groups and their integration in program active. 7. Carry-out post distribution monitoring and analysis to inform future programming

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Regarding project design, CRS ensures that the shelter Kit content is tailor made to mitigate protection risks and takes into consideration the needs of vulnerable groups.Greater protection concerns are mainstreamed into the multi-sectoral teams (in addition to having protection representation within CRS structure), who conduct referrals and services specifically targeted for those vulnerable persons. CRS is an active member of the Protection cluster and shelter-protection assessment visits inclusive of HH surveys that focus on vulnerability criteria and protection aspects are performed to ensure integration of these aspects into the design of the program activities.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

CRS protection teams will include PSEA sessions in all capacity building and awareness raising activities as there is a need to organize focused PSEA activities to reduce the risk of exploitation of the displaced communities. CRS will organize PSEA workshops for Reference Groups at district and at provincial level. These workshops will include PSEA training, case discussions, and establishment of action plans. In this way, CRS will support and involve all relevant government bodies which are members of the Reference Groups in prevention and response of sexual exploitation and abuse. As a member, CRS will coordinate these activities with the PSEA Network.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People with disabilities will be targeted and have a higher score while selecting the most vulnerable houses. These people, if with no labour capacity, will receive technical and labour support for the construction of their shelters. And estimated 20% of the population with disabilities will have been assisted under our programing.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

CRS will seek strict adherence to the Sphere Standards for Humanitarian Assistance which identify the environment as a cross-cutting issue to be incorporated to all humanitarian assistance. In that sense, CRS will encourage the use of assessment tools to assess the environmental impact of the construction related program activities. This tools focus from the selection of the shelter sites, the construction of structures reviewing the possible negative environmental impacts, the management considerations while occupied and also considers the possible decommissioning or dismanteling of the structures to reutilize the resources. The use of this tools will engage local actors and communities to understand the different conditions particular to the working areas.
