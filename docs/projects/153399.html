Project Summary

The proposed actions are intended to be a short term emergency intervention building upon already existing health and nutrition activities provided by MAGNA in Duk County by responding to new prioritised, assessed and unmet needs or gaps in Health service delivery with an overarching objective to reach out to 10000 vulnerable under-served populations by reducing morbidity and mortality among women, men, girls and boys through implementation of two cluster clinical packages consisting of one PHCC in Duk Pagaak and 01 PHCU in Duk Pajut with focus on areas reporting high malaria incidences for a period of one year. These interventions fully respond to cluster top priority activities to; Implement essential primary healthcare emergency clinical packages targeting the most vulnerable displaced populations and Immunise the most vulnerable displaced groups (Children U5,PLW).
Implementation of this project will integrate nutrition, mental health, WASH and protection activities to improve equity and universal access to health care scaling up responsiveness for preventive and curative services, which aligns with all the three South Sudan Health Cluster’s Strategic response plan objectives to: Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialised and integrated services and Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks.

Needs

Duk County has been hit hard by South Sudan war entering its 6th year by virtue of its location at the boundary between opposition and government controlled areas, security situation is tense compounded by recurrent cattle raids and inter-communal clashes resulting into displacement.(http://www.reachresourcecentre.info/system/files/resource-documents/reach_ssd_profile_yuai_multisectorial_overview_july_2016.pdf). Further assessment by education cluster conducted in 2016 also revealed massive population displacement Southwards from neighbouring Ayod County further increasing the number of IDPs in Duk. Nutrition situation is dire with IPC 4 rating according to 2018 IPC assessment. (http://fews.net/IPC). These findings fully agree with SMART survey results conducted by ACF on 23rd April 2017 revealing GAM at 26.1% and SAM at 5.2% which are both above the WHO Global acute malnutrition prevalence thresholds, this is likely to worsen with increasing incidences of inter-communal violence and continuous cattle raiding. Consultations between MAGNA and local authorities in Duk between August and September 2018 revealed significant gaps in health service delivery and inadequate outbreak response preparedness; the population of 120156 people is serviced by only 7 functional Health facilities out of original 12 with average walking distance of about 6 hours which is below standard. This is among the factors behind the poor ANC attendance observed in 2018 January to August Duk DHIS data review. Even the existing health facilities are impoverished with limited staffing and lack basic medical equipment for quality health service delivery. Further review of Duk 2018 DHIS data revealed that;• Immunisation coverage is very low: less than 20% of pregnant women received TT2 while only 15% of children less than 12 months completed Penta3. (Duk County 2018 DHIS Reports).• Ongoing inter-communal clashes are making health facility access even more difficult coupled with the vast geographical landscape and seasonal flooding of the Jonglei canal dividing Duk into 2 parts by water mass.• Limited availability of community based health activities in Duk, in other parts of Duk such services have virtually ceased due to World Bank-IMA funding cuts, affecting vaccination coverage with less than 15% of children under 12 months provided with Penta3 which is below target.• High burden of communicable diseases according to REACH assessment conducted between January – March 2018, Malaria is the leading health concern mortality at 46% in assessed settlements, followed by diarrhoea at 24% and Malnutrition at 12% and other causes 18% (http://www.reachresourcecentre.info/system/files/resource-documents/reach_ssd_aok_situation_overview_of_jonglei_state_in_january-march_2018.pdf). Based on these gaps and health cluster response plan 2018, MAGNA intends to reach 10,000 people (4960 F, 5040 Male inclusive 4700 children U18) with quality lifesaving emergency health interventions provided through 01 PHCC, 01 PHCU in Duk Pagak and Duk Patuet targeting IDPs and host community. Recognising the unique needs of women in South Sudan, and persons with disabilities, particular effort will be made to reach them in order to ensure their dignity and security. MAGNA will continuously consult with female and male beneficiaries of all ages, including persons with disabilities, to ensure that proposed interventions meet their needs without access constraints. MAGNA will ensure a gender balance among implementing field staff to enable beneficiaries to freely express their needs in an appropriate manner in keeping with cultural norms, and all MAGNA staff will receive protection training, including in making referrals as needed to various support programs.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

MAGNA has incorporated various mechanisms to ensure accountability to targeted communities in Duk, awareness will be provided to community members, beneficiaries on ways of presenting complaints and feedback, this will include timely and accessible information on organisational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to able to make informed decisions and choices. Suggestion box will be installed at all sites of the project in suitable areas that can be accessed easily by all members of the community, the MEAL officer will take lead in ensuring timely collection of the feedback from the suggestion box, sharing important information with the Nutrition Coordinator and preparing dialogue between MAGNA and targeted community on feedback regarding matters arising. MAGNA staff will also be trained on proactive ways of seeking the views of affected populations using methods such as focused group discussions in order to understand community needs and improve policy and practice in programming. MAGNA consulted 483 community members in Duk comprising cross cutting members of the community with fair representation of age and gender, the findings have been used to modify the project design incorporating joint monitoring and evaluation of the goals and objectives by MAGNA and community representatives on an on-going basis and reporting on the results of the process.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through this project significant efforts have been made to ensure that messages provided are context specific and tailored towards assessed needs of the community. Behaviour change communication messages will all be translated into the local language and health education will predominantly be provided in the two main local languages to ensure that the messages is delivered to the intended beneficiaries. Additionally consultations will also be conducted in the local language for better understanding between health service providers and the patients.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

MAGNA has zero tolerance for all forms of protection abuse whether perpetrated against a beneficiary or co-worker. SGBV and abuse violate human rights and are a betrayal of the core values of MAGNA. Mainstreaming protection will be central to response activities and all staff, volunteers and service providers working for this project will be mandated to sign an act prohibiting all forms of SGBV and abuse as part of MAGNA’s cooperative arrangements with individual contractors, service contract holders and implementing partners. MAGNA through its human resource department will conduct rigorous pre-employment checks of new staff and personnel to prevent the rehiring of known perpetrators. All MAGNA personnel and volunteers under different projects will be required to undergo mandatory protection training and sensitisation to create awareness. Health services will be considered as potential entry point for GBV victims looking for assistance, with Health staff to be trained on GBV cases management and referral pathway which will enhance screening and referral of beneficiaries with protection concerns for appropriate care. Special attention will be given to prevention of neglect among vulnerable groups to accessing Health services among care givers and Health staff. MAGNA will not collaborate or partner with any offenders of SGBV and all forms of sexual exploitation are strictly prohibited, constitutes misconduct and will be addressed as a dispensary action of gross misconduct. Staff and volunteers will be obliged to report allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse to the Country Director through available confidential internal reporting hotlines. MAGNA will thoroughly investigate all allegations of protection concerns perpetrated by its staff, volunteers or contractors and imposes tough disciplinary measures when allegations are proven.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In the process of conceptualising the community needs and priorities of targeted area, MAGNA consulted different cadres within Duk over a period of 2 months reaching a total of 689 individuals (328 Women, 218 Men, 72 Boys and 71 Girls) Different methodologies were used including 28 individual questionnaires administered to key informants comprised of Duk CHD Head, 3 CHD Staffs, 02 Commissioners for Duk Pagaak and Duk Padiet, 12 Health facility staffs (from 3 facilities), 6 WASH staff from CRS and 4 Chiefs. 38 Focused group discussions where conducted separately for various age groups and gender each comprised of between 10-25 participants (16 FGDs with women groups, 11 FGDs with Men groups, 6 FGDs with Boys and 6 FGDs with Girls). One on one discussions were also conducted with Jonglei State minister of health on needs and priorities. Additionally, DHIS data review was conducted in Duk jointly by MAGNA and CHD to obtain statistics of key indicators for decision making.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

MAGNA is committed to ensure accountability to all stake holders including the affected populations, the proposed intervention will embrace information sharing regarding the overall project, scope of work, budget and personnel involved in implementation. Various approaches tailored towards supporting feedback/complaint mechanisms have been put in place and will include joint supervision, coordination and monthly review meetings with staff, local leaders and village health committees who form a strong link between health service providers and beneficiaries. MAGNA seeks to enhance community ownership and promote participation by engagement of key community structures such as VHCs and Nutrition volunteers, vaccinators, health promoters in mobilisation and health activities at Boma level which will create a valuable interface for information collection and feedback mechanism to improve on programming and implementation approaches. Through this project, women and men in Duk will be given an opportunity to actively participate in decision making regarding the project during the course of the project execution. With the involvement of the community structures spear headed by the CHD, monthly and quarterly monitoring of activity progress will be conducted and feedback provided. Monitoring reports will be shared with beneficiaries and utilised to redirect resources and strategies on corrective approaches to achieve the desired project objectives. Besides, we shall be keen in creating donor visibility within the community. Therefore, during the implementation of this project, beneficiaries will be informed about the health cluster and donor logo will be placed on all printed materials e.g. T-shirts, BCC materials, IEC materials, sign posts as at the supported health facilities

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community engagement has been fully integrated in the monitoring and evaluation design of the project through the following mechanisms; A set of desired qualities will be shared with the community members to guide them in the selection of community volunteers who will be involved in routine in implementation of programme activities, through this direct involvement the volunteers will be an eye of the community in monitoring of activities. Community members will be further engaged in joint support supervision visits, key community representatives will be incorporated into the technical monitoring team comprised of MAGNA, CHD and at the end of each joint supervision session they will provide remarks on their observations and appropriate way forward. Briefly describe how

Human rights, safety and dignity of all beneficiaries regardless of origin, nationality, age and gender will be respected and observed across the targeted areas of implementation. MAGNA will ensure that all targeted IDPs and host community beneficiaries are free from all forms of exploitation and abuse from staff by orientating staffs on context and internal policies. Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through this project, leadership and coordination among humanitarian actors, government and local authorities is strengthened to ensure a streamlined response to the crisis, especially to mitigate protection risks to women and girls. Ensuring that all stakeholders are involved in monitoring and work to prevent exploitation and discrimination against beneficiaries. This project will incorporate recommendations and implement the IASC Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Interventions in Humanitarian Action across all sectors ensuring women, girls and other persons with specific needs are able to freely access services without restrictions to eliminate gender disparities in accessibility of services. MAGNA will collect sex- and age-dis-aggregated data on all aspects of humanitarian programming in order to track and monitor flows and needs of IDPs and host community. Support the consideration and inclusion of gender concerns within all the projects contingency plans. Based on the findings from the targeted area of project implementation, MAGNA will deploy a female staff responsible for reproductive health activities including SGBV and ANC activities, this decision is based on the community perception that a midwife is female and some mothers having privacy concerns being attended to by male midwives. Awareness raising on availability and competence of male midwives will also be provided to prepare the community should there be a male midwife in future. Deploy staff with considerations to ability to communicate in the local language to enable smooth flow of information during consultation between patient and health service providers without alteration of information. The project will ensure that all humanitarian staff are always identifiable to the beneficiaries through visibility vests and t-shirts that are as consistent as possible in all countries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The on-going insecurity between various armed tribal groups in Duk County is one of low-level intensity but it has seen the most serious armed confrontations affecting the county in the past years. Concerns arising from this cattle raiding include continuous displacements of civilians, killing and other human rights law violations, sexual and gender based violence, child abductions especially by Murle and grave violations against children. This context of conflict is characterized by a climate of impunity, lack of access to livelihoods services. Traditional norms about the roles of girls and women have an impact in restriction of access to health care. These ideas hold women back from participating and contributing to important health activities, MAGNA through this project will encourage equal participation of both gender with additional support for women. MAGNA will boast women’s role in health decision making by ensuring that women and men or equally represented during community meetings, health education sessions and consultations. During assessment MAGNA observed that women spend long hours collecting firewood and water affecting their active participation outside family matters therefore all activities under this project have been designed with care to ensuring that timing is adjusted to favour both men and female participants while not it does not interfere with domestic chores. Through protection training on SGBV which has been integrated into this project, awareness will be provided to create safe communities where women and girls can live free from violence increasing the participation of women in decision and policy-making at all levels. In all the two health facilities under this project care and treatment services for victims of gender-based violence will be provided. The project will also strengthen women’s access to information and employment opportunities that will allow them to share more broadly in the benefits of economic growth.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project activities will address inequalities or protection threats through different mechanisms stipulated below;• No form of segregation will be allowed during staff recruitment and female candidates will always be highly encouraged to apply for positions.• All project activity data will be dis-aggregated by sex, age to demonstrate equality and equity in service utilisation and ensure that all beneficiaries reached in different groups are represented.• Policy of free health care for all is respected to prevent exclusion due to lack of financial means.• Ensure that as much as possible, there is a gender balance in staffing within the facility. • Interventions under this project will ensure that health care is availed as close as possible to the users to offset prohibitive transport costs that may inadvertently lead to exclusion. • Ensure that the established referral system is functional and additional innovative community based referral systems will be explored and encouraged to increase access to services. • Ensure that community members are involved and selection of committees will be done in a representative manner to allow participation of vulnerable groups and to provide a platform for their needs to be articulated.• Maintain active participation by the health committees in the programme activities and in the programme cycle and especially in monitoring and evaluation as well as giving regular feedback.• Ensure that health messages that affect women and children are also addressed to the men since in this community they are the decision makers while also being careful not to perpetuate the gender stereotype.• Information will be provided on available health services to avoid exclusion based on lack of knowledge.• Outreach services will be conducted targeting those who may not be able to reach the health facilities including women who are unable to leave their homes and visit the facility as a result of their caretaker role.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Based on operational experience in Duk County, MAGNA recognizes that additional work is needed to accurately reflect patient concerns with access burdens. The monitoring and evaluation mechanism of this project has been designed to capture data measuring beneficiaries' access to health services which is a critical issue in achieving the expected targets. Considering that access constraints have direct implications on unmet health needs, delays in receiving the appropriate care, inability to access preventative services, unreasonable financial burdens to the beneficiaries, and preventable hospitalization. MAGNA monitoring and evaluation mechanisms will capture data on beneficiary’s timely and appropriate access to healthcare. This will include access to several aspects of the health service such as structural barriers impacting access such as location of health facility, distance, transportation, waiting times, or quality of care eg service delivery hours and the ability of health care workers to fully understand and address patient needs, patient-health worker communication and relationship, cultural beliefs and knowledge levels on range of health services. Systematic collection of these data will be conducted and regularly reviewed by the project manager and feedback provided to service providers and beneficiaries. Through this project additional data on top of routine demographic data will be collected on disability, mental illness, gender base violence and other protection concerns in a professional way ensuring patient privacy. The availability of this data will provide a basis for better understanding of patterns in healthcare disparities and timely address of the access constraints.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

A vulnerability criteria will be implemented to ensure that all actors engaging in the response identify, prioritise and respond to individuals who have heightened protection risks without discrimination. The project data will be collected ensuring sex- and age dis-aggregated data in order to track and monitor flows and specific needs of vulnerable populations. Observed protection risks during consultations have been adequately addressed in site planning and management ensuring no access constraints are posed with gender concerns adequately integrated within the plans to ensure dignity and privacy of beneficiaries. MAGNA will establish a cross-cutting protection risk mechanisms, including SGBV case management and referral pathways integrated within the project design to support response and prevention of SGBV. Implement the Minimal Initial Services Package (MISP) for prevention of maternal and infant mortality; prevention and treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and HIV; prevention and response to pregnancy-associated complications; and provision of family planning commodities and information to mitigate risks faced by survivors. Furthermore, MAGNA will ensure that information about services are disseminated, available and accessible in the 2 local languages, Arabic and English through signs, loudspeaker broadcasts and interactions with humanitarian actors. Culturally appropriate educational materials, including information is available.
