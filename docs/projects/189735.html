Project Summary

The situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate because of unresolved sub-national conflict, climate change, economic instability, the COVID-19 pandemic and lack of basic services.
Conflict between the government and non-signatories to the Agreement, as well as signatories, persists, the country continues to see serious human rights violations against civilians, including the selective granting and denying of humanitarian access. 2021 saw between 1,660 and 3,815 conflict related deaths
Latest data from the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSNMS)+ Round 27 show that conflict and climate change could contribute to famine conditions in new areas of the country. The third consecutive year of flooding is affecting 39 of the 78 counties and, due to lack of appropriate preventative or flood management infrastructure, has led to new displacements of over 835,000 people. In the most flood-affected areas, households have eroded capacities to recover from previous and continuing shocks, and cannot return to areas of origin in the coming months. Additionally, drought in parts of Eastern Equatoria State is threatening livelihoods and leading to increased number of food insecure people.
The nutrition situation is also expected to deteriorate in 2022 as some of the key causes of malnutrition continue to remain dire, especially the worsening food security, WASH, increased displacements due to flooding and violence. Children under 5 and pregnant and nursing mothers continue to be at the highest risks especially in IDPs, refugee and returnee populations.
The FSNMS+ round 27 indicates that 27 counties have, during the post-harvest period, global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates exceeding the emergency threshold of over 15%. This translates into 1.3 million children and 675,000 PLW needed to be treated for acute malnutrition. By the lean season, the situation is expected to deteriorate.
In response to the continued concerning nutritional needs in South Sudan, WFP's project will facilitate the provision of nutrition prevention and treatment services to populations affected by conflict, displacement, climate change and insecurity. The project will target residents, IDPs, refugees and returnees (for treatment only) in order to prevent malnutrition related morbidity and mortality.
WFP will continue to work with cooperating partners to support an emergency nutrition response through a preventive supplementary feeding programme and treatment services for moderate acute malnutrition targeting children
aged 6-59 months, pregnant & lactating women, and people living with HIV/TB across
South Sudan.
In hard to reach areas, WFP will conduct direct delivery of life-saving nutrition services through the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM). The project implementation will be coordinated closely with the Nutrition cluster and use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (health, education, FSL, WASH).

1 - Needs

The project aims to address the nutritional needs of vulnerable women and children in populations affected by food and nutrition insecurity resulting from conflict, insecurity, climate change and displacement. The Food Security Monitoring System Round 27 (data collected in late 2021) showed that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was at 11.3 percent while Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was 1.1 percent, reflecting a slight decrease over the same period in 2019 when the prevalence rate was 12.6 percent for GAM and 3.3 percent for SAM. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 8.4 million people (including refugees) will experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season, representing an increase of 8 percent as compared to the 7.7 million in 2021. The main drivers are climatic, conflict & economic shocks: floods and dry spells, high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence. Food insecurity is greatest when these shocks and stressors are compounded and underlined by high levels of existing vulnerability brought about by the protracted crisis since December 2013. Of the 72 counties surveyed by the FSNMS+, only 18 counties have seen significant or minor improvements, while 26 counties are of extreme or severe concern. The most extreme counties of concern are in the States of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj) with the highest priority counties being Fangak, Canal/Pigi and Rubkona. Households who are in counties of extreme and severe concern have exhausted coping strategies and need immediate food assistance, well ahead of the lean season. It is recommended to start the provision of lifesaving assistance as soon as in January 2022 and sustain it until the first rains (June 2022) or the next harvest (around September 2022). The nutrition situation is also expected to deteriorate in 2022 as some of the key causes of malnutrition continue to remain dire, especially the worsening food security, WASH, increased displacements due to flooding and violence. Children under 5 and pregnant and nursing mothers continue to be at the highest risks especially in IDPs, refugee and returnee populations. The FSNMS+ round 27 indicates that 27 counties have, during the post-harvest period, global acute malnutrition (GAM) rates exceeding the emergency threshold of over 15%. This translates into 1.3 million children and 675,000 PLW needed to be treated for acute malnutrition. By the lean season, the situation is expected to deteriorate. WFP plans to cover all areas of high malnutrition needs across all ten states in South Sudan.

2 - Project activities

In collaboration with cooperating partners, WFP will: 1. Provide children aged 6-59 months, PLHIV/TB and PLW screened in communities and identified as moderately malnourished with an appropriate treatment protocol, inclusive of specialized nutritious food (SNF), through the Targeted Supplementary Feeding programme (TSFP) under the CMAM approach. The expanded criteria will be used as strategy in some areas when WFP/UNICEF /Nutrition Cluster jointly agree. 2. Provide preventive ration of specialized nutritious food to children U2 and PLW in counties with GAM rates above 10% through a preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme in the aim of preventing acute malnutrition. The Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme will be implemented in combination with the General Food Distribution in areas of high food insecurity. 3. Support an outreach network of community nutrition volunteers to provide nutrition and health message, outreach services, actively screen and identify those at risk or with acute malnutrition and ensure proper referral to health and nutrition services. 4. Ensure the project implementation will use an integrated approach through synergies with activities supported by other clusters (Health, FSL, Education, WASH, Protection, NFIs). 5. Ensure revised delivery modalities will allow for prepositioning in locations with irregular access year-round, in strategic hubs with the aim of reducing pipeline shortfalls during the rainy season. River deliveries and airlifts are conducted to UNS, Unity and Jonglei States to the final distribution point where access is impossible by road to ensure the programmes are not disrupted. 6. Monitor and provide joint supervision through a network of field monitors, programme and nutrition staff to ensure implementing partners activities and stocks are meeting the high quality and reaching the most nutritionally vulnerable individuals. 7. Contribute to enhancing the nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information through supporting the the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System. WFP undertakes the FSNMS twice a year, once in the lean season and once post harvest and collects a range of food security and nutrition indicators to inform the situation in country and thereby programmatic adjustments as needed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Certain aspects of project assistance such as proximity of nutrition sites, availability of shelter, inclusion of particular topics during awareness raising, are informed by community needs/situation. Feedback from beneficiaries will be considered and where feasible changes are made.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

WFP monitoring and data systems have been adjusted to include the Washington Group Questions to increase visibility and response to persons with disability. With regards to disability inclusion, WFP is finalizing guidelines on disability inclusion that can be used by WFP and its cooperating partners. The document will also list Food Security and livelihood activities that Persons living with disability can be actively engaged in line with WFP’s Policies. The guidelines will also provide a pool of indicators that Programme units can adopt to measure progress.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WFP is cognizant of its potential to advancing protection outcomes for the population of South Sudan due to the breadth of WFP access to populations, extensive size and coverage through operations and programmes and, the highly valuable commodity of food and cash assistance. WFP conducts regular protection assessments to capture information on WFP’s current and possible impact on dignity, integrity, safety, AAP, conflict (and Peacebuilding) and other key information. This is then utilized to inform activity design, adjustments and implementation that reflects community realities and preferences (as much as possible). WFP operates with the assumption that GBV is ongoing in all locations of assistance as a product of gender inequality and the social, political and economic environments in which it is operating. As such, WFP applies a Do No Harm approach through participation in interagency GBV Safety Audits and checklists to identify areas requiring attention and mitigation for GBV harm minimization. WFP has increased its investment in researching, partnering and focus on conflict sensitive programming.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To strengthen community engagement, WFP will continue to support the formation and engagement of functioning of project management committees (PMC), comprising women, men, youth, persons with disabilities, and other individuals identified as vulnerable, thereby ensuring that the PMC is representative and inclusive, and empowering the most vulnerable to have their voice heard. The committees facilitate engagement, problem resolution, and communication with WFP to facilitate effective delivery of life-saving food and nutrition assistance. Before and during distributions, WFP and its partners provide information to the affected community on programme objective, activity, eligibility criteria, entitlement, and where and how to lodge grievances and suggestions. This information is disseminated through various communication channels, including radio broadcasts in the IDP camps and POCs; and through community meetings, help desks, hotlines and appointed community mobilizers in remote locations.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

FP will mitigate contextual risks by monitoring the political situation and updating its business continuity plan accordingly. Conflict, protection analysis and rapid food insecurity analysis will help ensure that WFP's activities do not exacerbate conflict or tensions and do not have unintended negative effects on people living in conflict areas. Supply chain management and innovative programme modalities will provide flexibility to respond to changes in conditions. Advance financing, procurement through the Global Commodity Management Facility and pre-positioning will help to ensure that food and nutrition products are available as needs and transport conditions change. Transport capacities will be augmented and means of delivery adapted as needed. WFP will lobby for more functional corridors. Inadequate funding could prevent WFP from fully meeting the needs of vulnerable people. Operational shortfalls and pipeline breaks may damage WFP's reputation and its relationships with communities. To mitigate these risks, the country office has been continuously engaging with partners highlighting needs and emphasizing the importance of allocation of resources early in the year. A refined prioritization strategy will also serve as a risk mitigation measure in case of resourcing shortfalls. To mitigate programmatic risks and facilitate the timely delivery of food and nutrition supplies to people in need, WFP carries out regular conflict and protection analysis. Through its dedicated access team it negotiates with local authorities to ensure access to affected populations and staff security and works with local cooperating partners to ensure access to affected populations and to improve targeting. Risk assessments for transport, storage and delivery help to ensure that operations continue with minimal disruptions and food assistance is timely delivered
