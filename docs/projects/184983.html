1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

NRC’s Education in Emergencies (EiE) programme will specifically target conflict-affected children and adolescents aged 6-17 not accessing any kind of educational opportunities, those who have never been enrolled, or those who have previously dropped out. Those at risk of not returning to school in light of COVID-19, displacement status, or psychosocial distress will be prioritized – particular focus will be paid to adolescent girls, who face a myriad of Protection risks when not enrolled, such as GBV, child labour, and early marriage. Boys also face specific risks such as recruitment into armed groups and child labour when not in a safe and protective learning environment. NRC will endeavour to ensure a 50/50 split between boys and girls enrolled in EiE programming. Both host community (HC) and IDP children will be targeted, with a minimum of 20% HC, particularly in areas where an acceptance strategy is required for access. A Do No Harm lens will be applied to all EiE actions. Additionally, the INEE Minimum Standards will serve as a foundation for all EiE actions. NRC will provide safe, quality and protective EiE activities, including construction of Temporary Learning Spaces and emergency WASH facilities, rehabilitation of classrooms affected by conflict, distribution of student kits and teaching and learning materials, provision of classroom supplies and materials, provision of catch-up and remedial classes for conflict-affected children, and training of teachers on classroom-based psychosocial support and EiE principles.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

NRC will implement lifesaving Education in Emergencies (EiE) activities in Hard to Reach (H2R) areas of Cabo Delgado to ensure conflict and displacement affected children and adolescents, particularly girls, have access to quality education in safe and protective learning environments. Where no formal school exists, NRC will work with authorities to construct and administer temporary learning spaces (TLS) while long-term solutions are sought. In formal schools, NRC will provide construction and rehabilitation of classrooms and emergency WASH facilities to support new/re-enrolment, particularly in areas where education facilities have been targeted in the conflict. NRC will provide access to quality formal and non-formal (NFE) humanitarian learning opportunities through provision of teaching and learning materials (TLM) including student and teacher kits, provision of catch-up and remedial classes, and training for teachers on the INEE Minimum Standards and Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC) modules. In cooperation with government, NRC will train teachers on Better Learning Programme (BLP) socio-emotional and psychosocial support (PSS) learning sessions to help students manage stress and enhance overall wellbeing and study skills. Where classroom-based PSS is insufficient, NRC will work closely with Child Protection and MHPSS actors to provide necessary referrals for children experiencing psychosocial distress or who have been exposed to other Protection risks; where exiting referral pathways do not exist, NRC will work with local partners and sectors to establish them. While NRC anticipates targeting the Districts of Mueda, Nangade, Midumbe and Palma, the focus will remain on H2R areas with high needs and limited presence of humanitarian actors. As areas become newly accessible, or as returnees become a reality, NRC will remain flexible in terms of areas of intervention so as to serve those populations most in need in Cabo Delgado. This includes currently inaccessible areas such as Mocimboa de Praia and Quissanga, and H2R areas such as Macomia and Palma, which are projected to have increased access in 2022.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

NRC will aim to achieve gender parity in all its programmes while recognizing gender-specific needs and risks. All data collected by NRC is disaggregated by gender, to analyse to what extent NRC is achieving its gender equality goals, to identify potential barriers to equal access, and adjust project implementation if necessary. Various protection risks that affect specific vulnerable groups among the broader target population will be mitigated for throughout the programming cycle. Girls may be more exposed to the risk of sexual abuse and exploitation, while boys may be more exposed to the risk of forced recruitment by armed groups, particularly given the limited livelihoods opportunities and the perceived socio-economic exclusion of youth. Social pressure may force adolescents and youth in particular to take risks (or create protection risks for others, e.g. early marriage) to fulfil their bread winning obligations. NRC will work closely with communities to create positive perceptions of Education programming and to encourage parents to ensure their children, especially girls, go to and stay in school. NRC is member of the Cabo Delgado PSEA Network and has appointed a focal point for both the organization and the Education team. NRC staff have received the TOT of PSEA from both the Network and NRC. NRC commits to provide training on PSEA to all its NRC Mozambique team members. NRC employees will also be inducted on the NRC Code of Conduct (CoC) that includes articles on PSEA and Child Safeguarding, with a specific separate policy governing both. Effective and transparent HR policies will be set in place (including policies on nepotism and conflict of interest PSEA) to help prevent SEA. NRC will also provide training to teachers on PSEA through TiCC modules, which include a specific sub-section on Teacher CoC. Regular monitoring and support to schools, as well as training on local-level referral mechanisms will also be provided to teachers and school communities. As part of a Do-No-Harm approach, and for a mainstreamed Conflict Sensitive Education (CSE) approach, NRC will work with both IDPs and host communities so as not to exacerbate inter-communal tensions. As displacement becomes protracted and depletion of resources more severe, the possibility that in some locations geographical or ethnic origin could become a discrimination/marginalization criterion cannot be ruled out. NRC will design its intervention paying particular attention to vulnerable and traditionally marginalised groups. Safe programming provisions will ensure inclusion of vulnerable groups based on assessment, with focus regardless on women, girls and persons with disability. By [re]enrolling students in a safe and protective learning environment, NRC will be able to provide life-saving information to children at an age-appropriate level, and reduce the impacts of Protection risks and negative coping mechanisms that can result from prolonged periods out of school. Women and girls in Mozambique are often subjected to gender-based discrimination, excluded from full participation in decision-making at both household and community levels, and more generally denied control over their own resources and opportunities. NRC will ensure participation of women and girls to mitigate Protection risks through targeted CFM, mother’s groups and PTAs, and enrolment campaigns for adolescent girls.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

NRC will ensure meaningful participation of affected populations in key decisions and processes as well as regular sharing of clear and accessible information with them during all phases of the programme cycle. NRC will establish and implement an internal child-friendly compliant and feedback mechanism (CFM) to ensure that communities receiving assistance from NRC have access to a formalized procedure and mechanism that provides a safe, accessible and effective channel. While there are various methods of CFM (suggestion boxes, text message/free-phone calls, focus group discussion, community meetings, etc.), NRC will do community consultations to identify appropriate context-based CFM, with a focus on child participation. NRC will ensure all vulnerable groups including men, women, boys and girls have equal access to CFM, which is accessible, safe, transparent and responsive. NRC will strive to provide a response to all feedback and complaints received within a reasonable timeframe. NRC holds regular consultations with relevant governmental authorities, including the Provincial Directorate of Education (DPE) in Cabo Delgado, and the District Service for Planning and Infrastructure (SDPI) as well as the District Services for Youth, Education and Technology (SDEJT) in relevant Districts. NRC has also been regularly participating in relevant Education Cluster meetings and workshops, including major contributions to the PSS Working Group. NRC is the current focal point for the Education Cluster in northern Cabo Delgado in H2R Districts. These coordination mechanisms further strengthen NRC’s access to, communication with, and accountability toward affected populations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Persons with disabilities (PWDs) may be more exposed to the risk of marginalization and exclusion. Due to a mix of socio-cultural stigmatization and physical barriers hampering mobility, PWDs are often invisible in the community and relegated to the domestic sphere. NRC will ensure physical access for PWD to TLS and rehabilitated classrooms is considered during the construction process through consultative feedback cycles. NRC will strive for gender-segregated latrines and latrines with disabled-access where possible. NRC will utilize the Washington Group of Questions to identify children with disabilities (CWD), and work with schools to identify appropriate referral mechanisms where relevant. NRC will consider the impacts for CWD on distributions, provision of materials, and during teacher trainings.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

NRC will prioritize use of locally made or available materials in all procurements to reduce impacts of shipping and importation, and to ensure a market-based approach to programming. Construction activities will take advantage of locally available materials, with a focus on those which are easily renewable, such as bamboo. WASH in Schools activities will take advantage of local rainwater collection and purification approaches to further increase sustainability. School gardens will be considered as an add-on to TLS construction when conditions are favorable for sustainability.
