Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing the delivery of quality protection services, improving the protection environment and supporting durable solutions for IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees and vulnerable host community members in North East Nigeria in the context of COVID-19.
UNHCR interventions will strengthen the protection environment by identifying and minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed in relation to the COVID-19 pandemic, and by expanding the breadth and quality of protection response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa through:
1. Protection Monitoring in COVID-19 affected areas,
2. In-kind assistance to vulnerable & COVID-19 affected persons of concern or those at risk,
3. COVID-19 Personal Protective supplies and equipment in support for partners, protection desks and other protection safe spaces,
4. COVID-19 intensive awareness-raising and sensitization,
5. Advocacy for Protection,
6. Provision of individual documentation,
7. Provision of birth certificates,
8. Border-monitoring and entry point surveillance,
9. COVID-19 intensive Human-rights monitoring

1 - Needs

The protection crisis in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States the Corona Virus and the ensuing COVID-19 pandemic has added a new layer to an already challenging protection environment. COVID-19 is the infectious disease caused by the most recently discovered coronavirus. This new virus and disease were unknown before the outbreak began in Wuhan, China, in December 2019. Coronaviruses are a large family of viruses which may cause illness in animals or humans. In humans, several coronaviruses are known to cause respiratory infections ranging from the common cold to more severe diseases such as Middle East Respiratory Syndrome (MERS) and Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS). The most recently discovered coronavirus causes coronavirus disease COVID-19. Nigeria recorded its first case of Corona virus in Lagos on 27 February 2020. As at 19 March 2020, eight cases were confirmed in the country. As of April 23, 2020, the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control reports a total of 981 lab-confirmed cases, of which 753 are active cases, 197 discharged. The pandemic has caused 31 untimely deaths in the country. The northeast is particularly affected by the pandemic. As of April 23, one COVID-19 case has been confirmed in Adamawa. Borno totals 12 laboratory confirmed cases, of which 10 are active and 2 have tragically passed away. On 19 March 2020, the Federal Government announced measures restricting entry to Nigeria for travellers from 13 worst affected countries in Europe, Asia and the USA. These include China, Italy, Iran, South Korea, Spain, Japan, France, Germany, USA, Norway, the UK, the Netherlands and Switzerland. On the same day, the UN Resident Coordinator announced exceptional measures to address the COVID-19 threat in Nigeria. This includes, inter alia, self-isolation for UN staff arriving from R&R, change of R& R station to Abuja for all UN staff in field offices, and the suspension of all non-essential international travel. This also includes a restriction on travel within Nigeria for UN staff to only essential travel (for activities rated PC1 or PC2 in the Programme Criticality Plan). While the pandemic disrupts normal life in the country, the restriction of movements, accepted as a lifesaving measure necessary to curb the spread of the infection, is further curtailing the access of populations to essential protection services including legal services, documentation and birth certificates and exacerbates suffering given that movements of protection staff has been complicated by the COVID-19 curbing measures, including social distancing and confinement. While the pandemic disrupts normal life in the country, the restriction of movements, accepted as a lifesaving measure necessary to curb the spread of the infection, affects freedom of movements especially at border areas, and hence the right to access to territory. Some of the protection problems and risks identified in connection with the COVID-19 pandemic are as follows:(i) Refoulement of Asylum seekers(ii) Denial of access to the territory to Nigerian Refugee returnees;(iii) Stigmatization of newly arrived people from neighboring countries affected by COVID-19;(iv) Risks of COVID-19 spread due to porous borders and cross border movement despite borders closure;(v) Heightened risks for the most vulnerable among the people of concern;(vi) Difficult access to means of livelihood due to border closure and restriction of movement as preventive measures to counter COVID-19;(vii) Overcrowded camps and reception centers heightening the risk of spread of COVID-19. Through this action, UNHCR intends to improve the protection of People of Concern in COVID-19 affected areas, increase the access of PoCs to protection services and ensure enjoyment of essential rights and basic services.

2 - Activities

A wide range of activities will be implemented under 5 main axis of intervention namely favorable protection environment, Fair protection processes and documentation, Security from violence and exploitation, durable solutions and Community Empowerment and Self Reliance and eight objectives. Protection from effects of armed conflict through protection monitoring and protection by presence: UNHCR will • Strengthen its protection monitoring system and enhance presence in the field to support affected populations with the identification of protection risks and incidents as well as human rights violations faced by their community members through community-based protection monitoring systems (Protection Action Groups); capacitate them to be able to refer/respond to issues, and provide comprehensive protection response including through referrals to other stakeholders; • Continue and strengthen detention centers and facilities monitoring as well as efforts to seek access to military screening centers/detention; • Analyze information on protection risks and incident trends as well as develop and disseminate high quality protection products as a basis for evidence-based advocacy and programming in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming within other sectors. Quality of registration and profiling: UNHCR will • Probe and track vulnerable IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees through the rolling vulnerability screening process to allow for delivery of assistance tailored to specific needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response by UNHCR and Protection Sector partners. • In collaboration with the competent national institutions, continue the registration of spontaneous refugee returnees to ensure the planning of comprehensive reintegration programs. Access to legal assistance and legal remedies: • Ensure enhanced access to quality legal aid and services, including legal advice, counselling and legal representation on civil and criminal matters. This includes legal assistance on housing, land and property issues among others including through mobile courts. • Conduct advocacy interventions for IDPs, IDP returnees and refugee returnees’ access to national justice systems. • Contribute to the capacity building of relevant actors on ADR mechanisms to support the resolution of minor issues within the community. Civil registration and civil status documentation: UNHCR will scale up its support to national institutions for the issuance of civil status documentation namely birth certificates and certificates of indigene. Capacity building activities of relevant national institutions will be conducted to support advocacy as well as extended to humanitarian actors to ensure that assistance is provided in line with international standards and that core protection principles are adhered to by service providers. Durable solutions for returning refugees and IDPs: UNHCR will • Engage in advocacy and efforts to establish the conditions for the pursuit of durable solutions both for IDPs and refugees; • Establish and reinforce existing cross-border coordination mechanisms with offices in neighboring countries of asylum for Nigerian refugees; • Develop and/or strengthen partnerships with development actors to enhance the Humanitarian -Development Nexus; • Provide reception and transit facilities for refugee returnees, equipping the way stations and reception. • Provide in-kind assistance to vulnerable & COVID-19 affected persons of concern or those at risk. • Monitor the protection situation at border points.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

UNHCR will leverage on its active membership of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and its Age Gender Diversity mainstreaming policy too. UNHCR will strengthen community-based feedback mechanisms, which will allow men, women, boys and girls to express any complaint; beneficiaries will be informed of the existence of such mechanism, and it will be ensured that they are comfortable in accessing it. Through these, UNHCR will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of protection-based materials. UNHCR and partners will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. UNHCR’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through Participatory Approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict and insurgency.
