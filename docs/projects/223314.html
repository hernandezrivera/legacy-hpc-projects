Project Summary

LABDI is a national organization that operates across the BAY states and implements activities in areas of nutrition, WASH, Protection, and Education. In this project, LABDI will engage in improving access to integrated quality nutrition preventive and curative activities, PBWG including IDPs, returnees, and host community members will be the key targets of the project.
LABDI will implement all the nutrition activities. Beneficiaries will be admitted into the nutrition programs and receive treatment according to the national guidelines while preventive nutrition services will be provided to caregivers and beneficiaries to prevent and reduce the incidence of malnutrition. LABDI is implementing the nutrition project by strengthening the screening, identification and referral of acute malnutrition among children under five, Pregnant breastfeeding women & girls (PBWG); ensuring that malnourished children receive timely treatment & quality nutrition services; and ensuring provision of support for appropriate, safe feeding for children 0-23 months (specific focus on <6m>The project will provide capacity building to government healthcare providers and in the prevention and treatment of severe acute malnutrition through promotion of appropriate infant and young children feeding (IYCF) practices, community mobilization, identification and referral of SAM cases and treatment of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children less than 5 years in Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and also ensuring those with medical complications are referred to Stabilization center.
LABDI will establish a robust network of Community Care Groups (CCG) which will serve as the primary platform for nutrition service delivery, provision of quality messaging on optimum maternal and child nutrition, in addition to enhance linkage to other sectoral interventions. LABDI will engage and train community health workers (CHW) to provide technical and operational support to these groups. LABDI will also prioritize mothers and caregivers of underweight (low weight-for-age) or wasted (low weight-forheight) 1-5 months old infants as well as mothers and infants with breastfeeding di culties for intensi ed, needs based MIYCN support; as thee groups of children, if not catered for, would translate to become part of the caseload of acute malnutrition once they turn 6 months. To achieve signi cant impact from this MIYCN intervention, LABDI will engage and train ‘’MIYCN Promoters’’ (IYCF-P) whose goal is to ensure needs based MIYCN support is provided to the above stated groups, based on their needs. Another crucial role of the IYCF-Ps would be consistent growth monitoring of these children to closely watch the progress of the children and their caregivers. LABDI will engage and train a team of Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMS) to conduct house-to-house screening of the mid-upper arm circumference of pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWG), and children 6-59months to identify and enroll those who are malnourished in care groups. The CNMs and health workers will also carry out anthropometric assessment (WFA and WFH) of children 0-5 months to identify undernourished (underweight or wasted) children to enroll their caregivers for intensi ed and needs-based MIYCN support.
Thrice during the life cycle of the project, LABDI will conduct mass MUAC screening for PBWG and children 6-59 months old to identify and enroll them into TSFP. In the same vein, LABDI will carry out mass WFH and WFA screening of under-6 children to identify undernourished infants and refer them and their caregiver for intensi ed feeding support either in stabilization centers or in community care groups.
It is intended that LABDI will engage bene ciaries in backyard gardening activities and train them in various livelihoodenhancing skills, including gardening.

1 - Needs

A significant humanitarian and protection crisis has resulted from the protracted conflict in the northeastern region of Nigeria; with an estimated 7.8 million people expected to need humanitarian assistance in 2025, according to OCHA. Children (especially girls) along with women continue to be the most affected; together, they account for 83% of the population in need. There is an urgent need for protection, especially for women and girls for which access to services, justice, and proper protection are still lacking.A major result of these protection concerns is the increasing burden of malnutrition among women and children over the past5 years. According to the Integrated Food security Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (2023), at least 206,000pregnant and lactating women are likely malnourished in the BAY states, indicating a 16% increase from the previous year. The analysis covered a total of 133 Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Nigeria including 62 in the northeast and 71 in the northwest. Between May and September 2024, more than half of the LGAs, 63 percent (84 LGAs), were classified in either IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious) or IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). Of these, 10 LGAs in the northeast were classified in Phase 4 (Critical), and 21 LGAs were in Phase 3 (Serious). The primary contributing factors to acute malnutrition in these regions include poor food consumption in both quantity and quality, inadequate feeding practices, poor health services, prevalence of diseases, and low health-seeking behaviors. Moreover, the current economic situation, coupled with food insecurity, limited access to water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, and persistent issues like banditry, protracted conflict, population displacement, flooding, and general insecurity, exacerbates malnutrition by restricting access to vulnerable populations. It is estimated that nearly 5.4 million children aged 0-59 months are currently suffering and are projected to continue suffering from acute malnutrition through 2025. This includes nearly 1.8 million children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). In addition, 787,000 pregnant and lactating women are also acutely malnourished and in need of treatment. The number of acutely malnourished children in need of treatment has risen by 23 percent, with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) cases increasing by 69 percent compared to the last IPC AMN analysis in 2023. Additionally, there is a 35percent increase in acutely malnourished pregnant and lactating mothers requiring treatment. The projection period of October to December 2024 indicates that acute malnutrition levels in both the northeast and northwest regions are expected to remain largely stable compared to the current period of analysis, except for Northern Yobe where the situation is likely to deteriorate. During the second projection period, from January to April 2025, the situation in the northeast is expected to remain similar to the first projection period except for Mobbar and Nganzai in Northern Borno. For the northwest region, a slight improvement of the acute malnutrition situation is likely during this second projection. In the Northeast region, the situation is particularly severe in the Borno State with 10 LGAs (Maiduguri, Jere, Damboa, Gubio, Kaga, Konduga, Mafa, Magumeri, Marte and Monguno) showing a Critical (IPC AMN Phase 4) classification and seven (Bama, Dikwa, Gwoza, Kala/Balge, Ngala, Mobbar and Nganzai) classified in IPC Phase 3 (Serious).
