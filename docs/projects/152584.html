Project Summary

September 2018 IPC findings estimate 6 million people to be facing Crisis and Emergency (IPC phases 3 and 4) acute food insecurity, out of which 4.3 million of people are facing Emergency (IPC phase 4) acute food insecurity in South Sudan. Humanitarian situation in Ayod county of Jonglei remains critical due to repeated devastation and displacement. Population displacement is rampant with people moving to areas perceived safer. With most of the population having lost their livelihood and with missed planting seasons consecutively for three seasons, coupled with insecurity, the host community and IDPs have been left food insecure and the malnutrition levels alarming. Previous nutrition response has been stretched by influx of IDP’s and host community. Most families are without employment; most population heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance. (WFP 2018).
CMD will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Out Patient Therapeutic Program (OTP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM without medical complications. In addition, CMD will integrate Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting the 15 OTP sites and 4 SC’s within Northern and Southern Ayod this includes Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Kandak, Mogok, Jiech, Gorwai, Padek, Kuachdeng, Haat, Kharmun, Mhaar, Nyawit ,Ayod town and Wan-Machar where CMD has existing health facilities while SC will be situated in Pagil, Mogok, Jiech and Padek to treat 491 SAM children with medical complications. CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH interventions in Ayod mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and SCs run by CMD.
CMD proposes to continue delivering nutrition services in Ayod County- Kandak, Menime, Pagil, and Normanyang and scale up activities in Mogok and Padek in collaboration with MoH, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of OTP and SC sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization on: IYCF Practices for prevention of Malnutrition, socio-mobilization for vitamin A supplementation and advocacy for the proper use of LLITN. With UNICEF and WFP collaboration, they will enable CMD provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a SAM caseload of up to 2,454 (Including 491 children with complicated severe acute cases) in need (< 5 years both boys and girls) and MAM caseload of up to 6,466(<5 years) and PLW’s caseload of 4,978. CMD will train Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs) and Mother-to-Mother support groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM and Maternal Infant and young child feeding practices so as to provide quality nutrition services. Health staff and Nutrition staffs will be capacitated for integration of SAM and MAM management into health programmes especially on malaria screening and referral of severe cases to OTP and Stabilization centres.

Needs

Nutrition indicators in Ayod remain below globally acceptable thresholds; one of the worst in South Sudan. September 2018 IPC findings flags Ayod County as hosting populations with critical food needs (IPC phase 3), one step below the emergency level (IPC 4). Acute malnutrition continues to worsen with evidence showing Ayod County is likely to fall under extreme Critical level (IPC Phase 5). Nutrition cluster target caseload indicates that the GAM rates in Ayod is 16.2, SAM rate 3.6, MAM rate 12.6% and prevalence of malnutrition in PLW is 35.2%. If the current drivers of food insecurity worsen through the end of 2018 in Ayod, and in the absence of humanitarian assistance, there is a heightened risk of emergency as large populations are already experiencing severe food insecurity. Settlements within Ayod are using extreme coping strategies such as only children eating, and spending days without food more than doubling between August (36%) and September (45%). The high SAM and GAM rates have been attributed to severe food insecurity, suboptimal infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF) widespread insecurity, displacement, and poor access to services, high morbidity and extremely poor diets. Access to food and water is very limited women and adolescent girls walk for long distance source for food and water.IPC projections place Ayod under IPC phase 3 with possibility of sliding into phase 4 in the event of absence of humanitarian support. Communities cultivate once a year, with seasonal erratic patterns hampering harvest. Lack of clean and safe water adequate for domestic and livelihoods use is directly linked to high malnutrition. CMD will integrate FSL into nutrition where vegetable seeds and tools will be distributed to women admitted with malnourished children, at the OTP sites, demonstration of kitchen garden training will be done at the nutrition sites, where mothers will be encouraged to practice home gardening to reduce malnutrition in Ayod community. CMD will provide beneficiary lists identified to ensure that those nutrition caseloads are included in food and livelihood support.

Activities

The project focus in reduction of malnutrition to children (0-59 months)/ under 5 years in Ayod county, targeting 15 bomas (Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Haat, Kandak, Mogok, Padek, Kharmun, Mhaar, Ayod town, Gorwai, Jiech, Wan-Machar, Kuachdeng and Nyawit.). The project will establish 4 SC’s ,15 OTP’s and BSFP in Mogok, Padek and Pagil Payams. The sites will continue provides services to the affected population within and outside the villages. The project is expected to run for 1 years in order to reduce the number of SAM cases in Ayod. Integration of nutrition into FSL, WASH and HEALTH will be encouraged through capacity building of the MTMSGS, CNV’s, Health and Nutrition staffs. Health and WASH education will be encouraged and shared to the caregivers/mothers during admissions within the 5 OTP sites. The program will ensure all caregivers in the Stabilization centres and OTP have received seeds and tools admission of SAM cases and will be encouraged to practice kitchen gardening.The trained CNV’s will provide services through moving to the villages, screening, identify, passing messages on MIYCN to mothers, follow up of the recoveries and refer SAM cases to the OTP sites. Mother to Mother Support Groups trained will be able to provide messages on the important of IYCF to the caregivers/mothers at the facility level and community level. CMD Nutrition staffs and health staffs trained will provide quality services to the SAM cases within the OTP’s and Stabilization centres. During admission nutrition is integrated into health and so screening of malaria is done the MUAC, WHZ-score, referral of SAM cases who are severely malnourished is done within Health and Nutrition compound where CMD provides all these services.CMD M&E officer will be visiting the field monthly and with field supervisors will monitor the Nutrition activities in the 15 sites (Pagil, Menime, Normanyang, Haat, Kandak, Mogok, Padek, Kharmun, Mhaar, Ayod town, Gorwai, Jiech, Wan-Machar, Kuachdeng and Nyawit) and share the reports to CMD Juba office, UNICEF/WFP focal point person, Nutrition Cluster and UNOCHA.CMD will leverage on its ongoing responses on thematic areas of WASH, Health and Education. CMD currently runs WASH interventions in Ayod mainly through the provision of WASH services at nutrition OTPs and SCs run by CMD.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Protection and sexual exploitation will be focused at the OTP sites.CMD will establish breastfeeding sites or shade for all caregivers during distribution, admission and during MIYCN and IYCF session. The organisation has a PSEA policy in place, alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures applies at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. Specific responsibilities have been assigned for PSEA, and the organisation ensures staff, volunteers and associates understand and work to PSEA requirements. CMD engages communities to ensure that communities are fully aware of the expected behavior of the staff, how to complain if commitments are not met and ensuring appropriate awareness raising tools are available. This also applies to partners, suppliers and contractors, by assessment of capacity to implement PSEA measures and inclusion of clauses on PSEA in contract agreements. The project seeks to further identify potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, and has planned activities on PSEA awareness and sensitivity. CMD has developed a comprehensive complaints mechanism that aims to instill an organisational culture in which all complaints are taken seriously and acted upon.

Briefly describe how

The project has identified specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications.
