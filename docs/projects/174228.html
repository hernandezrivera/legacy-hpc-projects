1 - Needs

A specific needs assessment has been conducted to discover the need gaps present in Borno and Adamawa State. Humanitarian needs across all sectors are increasing as a result of the latest waves of displacement and the ongoing crisis and the Covid-19 Pandemic has further slowed down the pace of humanitarian intervention, specifically in the Shelter and NFIs, WASH, Protection, Food Security, Health and Nutrition sectors. The fresh population movements and the high influx of IDPs are leading to camp overcrowding and congestion in some of the hosting sites such as indicated in the DTM R31 shows the need for urgent action.The humanitarian situation remains critical in northeast Nigeria, with an estimated 7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, 2.6 Million in need of Shelter assistance and more than 1.8 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs). Adamawa State alone hosts more than 1 million IDPs – approximately 67% of the total number of IDPs in the northeast. Additionally, the Adamawa State Government has begun repatriating Nigerian refugees from neighboring countries in large numbers (more than 80,000 expected), especially to border areas. Local Government Areas (LGAs) such as Monguno is highly trafficked routes for both returnees and IDPs moving from Maiduguri back to their home LGAs; they absorb hundreds, if not thousands, of new arrivals weekly.This displacement, coupled with, until recently, lack of access by humanitarian responders to areas controlled by Boko Haram, has resulted in critically low levels of essential, lifesaving services. Humanitarian assistance is increasingly and urgently needed as more areas are coming under Government of Nigeria (GON) control, and thus becoming accessible to relief actors. In Adamawa alone, there are anestimated 1. Million individuals in need of food assistance, and 85,000 children are severely acutely malnourished. Life expectancy and key health indices are poor in the northeast region, and this situation has been worsened and complicated by the insurgency, due to disruptionsin the health system and other social inequities. Healthcare and water supply infrastructure has been destroyed, and skilled healthcare workers have been displaced through migration, disability, or death. Health commodities/medical supplies are largely absent or in short supply, due to weak logistics systems. With the approaching rainy and lean season, critical health challenges are malnutrition, communicable diseases (measles, malaria, and cholera from poor WASH conditions) and access to safe drinking water.In recent months, partners have continued to scale up and increase humanitarian assistance in multiple sectors in the Northeast Nigeria; however, a large proportion of the population that resides in less accessible areas of Borno state face an elevated risk of famine (IntegratedPhase Classification Phase 5) in 2018. Currently in Borno State, there is a high risk of death for infants and young children furthercompounded by the collapse of the healthcare system, food insecurity, poor hygiene and sanitation, and poor child feeding practices, among other factors. Facing such a critical lack of services combined with the high volume of new arrivals, Hope And Rural Aid Foundation recognizes the need to expand operations in its current operating LGAs.Its been observed that there is wide spread ignorance on prevention and response of the the COVID-19 Virus across the population, hence the need for adequate inclusion of a preparedness and response plan in the execution of the project.

Regular intra-camp coordination meetings with different stakeholders and already setup leadership structures inside the camps and ensuring coordination with the Shelter/CCCM sector3. Regular site monitoring and reporting; Develop, update and circulate service mapping tool, the service mapping, or “4W”, is a tool elaborated by the CCCM agency at site-level4. Site Maintenance and Mitigation; Selection and training of community focal points and formation of safety/site maintenance committees5. Establishing proper referral pathways across all sites to ensure mitigation of risks of protection, GBV, health cases through engagements with Protection and Health actors present at sites of the project intervention6. Conduct training/capacity building sessions on community engagement, problem identification and solving7. Ensure protection mainstreaming into all camp activities9. Female headed households, people with disabilities and recently displaced households and IDP households that have never received NFIs will be prioritized.
