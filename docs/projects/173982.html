Project Summary

Five years after the opening of a country office, OCHA in Cameroon has become instrumental among the humanitarian and development community. As needs continue to grow, OCHA has a key role to play in the coordination of the humanitarian response as well as by liaising with key stakeholders to maintain humanitarian space and advocate for the rights of those directly affected by the three crises within the country. The complexity and magnitude of the three concurrent humanitarian crises requires coordination adapted to the different contexts and based on humanitarian principles. The continuous high humanitarian needs in Cameroon highlight the importance of strong coordination mechanisms capable of supporting an effective emergency response in line with humanitarian principles. The HCT under the leadership of the Humanitarian Coordinator will continue to provide overall strategic guidance to the humanitarian community, ensure that humanitarian principles are respected and maintain high-level engagement with relevant Government interlocutors. OCHA Cameroon will contribute to a better funded, more strategic humanitarian response that mainstreams protection, and is carried out in complementarity to development action. OCHA will continue to provide essential services to the humanitarian community in Cameroon, including the coordination of the Humanitarian Programming Cycle, the dissemination of key information products and the facilitation of joint multisectoral needs assessments. OCHA will continue to promote the development of a shared analysis of the distinct needs, vulnerabilities and risks by supporting gender-sensitive data collection and analysis. Playing the role of lead agent in the implementation of several specific results of the HCT’s gender in humanitarian action roadmap (2021-2022), OCHA will help the operation increase the quality and efficiency of its response, in line with the IASC gender policy. OCHA’s presence in Yaoundé, Maroua, Buea, Bamenda and Kousseri will enable it to provide critical coordination support on regional humanitarian needs analysis and response activities and promote consistency between field operations and strategic decision-making bodies. Coordination services will focus on improving capacity to address operational challenges on the ground, including through reinforced data collection and information management – particularly on access constraints to facilitate real-time solutions to access challenges. Existing rapid response mechanism will be further strengthened to ensure coordinated, dignified and timely assistance to affected populations. OCHA’s central role also allows the organization to promote the centrality of protection, Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and the humanitarian-development-nexus. OCHA will use its comparative advantage to facilitate humanitarian access and civil-military coordination and will continue to build the capacity of local responders.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon is affected by three, concurrent, complex humanitarian situations caused by the conflict and violence in the Far North region; hostilities in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions and the influx of over 280,000 refugees from the Central African Republic (CAR) in the eastern regions (East, Adamawa and North). Humanitarian needs are compounded by structural development deficits and chronic vulnerabilities that further challenge the long-term recovery of affected people. The preexisting condition has been heavily impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic which will remain in 2021. Access constraints identified in the Far North, and the NWSW regions include insecurity, physical access challenges and bureaucratic impediments with severe consequences on the civil documentation status of the overall population. Restricted movements and security challenges are equal impediments for the population to access basic social services and for the humanitarian actors to provide services.
