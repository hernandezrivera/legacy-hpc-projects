Project Summary

The Food insecurity and humanitarian situation in Sudan has deteriorated rapidly in the first half of the year 2020. The devastating combination of recurrent climate shocks, conflict, economic downturn, disease outbreaks, exacerbated by the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and floods, has driven rising food insecurity levels across the country. In addition, the lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods, daily labour opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population.
Hunger has increased across Sudan and over 7.1 million people are severely food insecure at the peak of the lean season (October to December). The figure, the highest ever recorded in the history of the IPC analysis in the country, represents an increase of 22 per cent if compared with the same period last year. At least 1.3 million people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 4), while around 5.8million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3). Another 17.4million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC 2) and any additional shock could push them to severe levels of hunger.
COVID19-related restrictions starting in March and April 2020 having an adverse impact on food security and livelihoods of the most vulnerable households. Movement restrictions had impacted farmers’ access to markets for critical inputs and pastoralists’ access to water and traditional grazing areas.
North Darfur, the target State of the project is one of the six State that classified as IPC 3 and above which are the highest prevalence of food insecurity.
The purpose of this project is to restore livelihoods and protect livestock of conflict affected communities in three localities: Kutum, Mellit and Kalimendo – North Darfur, through rehabilitation of hafir and construction of a twin hafir to increase water capacity for winter irrigation of vegetables, livestock drinking and human drinking in addition to vaccination and treatment of 150,000 head of animals; 50,000 head of animal in Kutum, 50,000 head in Melleit and 50,000 head in Kalimendo localities.
According to FSL sector PiN based on IPC phase 3 and above. For all localities IPC June - August 2019 analysis was used included the above target project localities.
1 - The FSL targets for Kutum locality is 47,300 individual out of which the project targets 8,000 individual.
2 - The FSL targets for Mellit locality is 29,200 out of which the project targets 8,000 individual
3 - The FSL targets for Kalimendo locality is 13,200 out of which the project targets 13,200 individual.
The total targets of the project as direct beneficiaries = 29,200 individual (men 6,000 women 8,000 boys 7,000, girls 7,000 and elders 1,200).
Sustainable Action Group (SAG) organization is a front line of humanitarian responder on the geographical area of North Darfur since 2005 through which the organization acquired good relationship and expert on coordination and collaboration with all humanitarian actors on the area.
In order to achieve the purpose mentioned SAG will:
• Coordinate with HAC to assign the Technical Agreement and get permissions of access to project localities
• Coordinate with State Ministry of Animal Resources to vaccinate and treat 100,000 head of livestock in Kutum, Mellit and Kalimendo
• Coordinate with Ministry of Planning and Infrastructure for consultation to rehabilitate hafir and construct a twin hafir to increase water capacity for winter irrigation of vegetables, drinking of human and livestock in dry season.

1 - Needs

The Food insecurity and humanitarian situation in Sudan has deteriorated rapidly in the first half of the year 2020. The devastating combination of recurrent climate shocks, conflict, economic downturn, disease outbreaks, exacerbated by the consequences of the COVID-19 pandemic and floods, has driven rising food insecurity levels across the country. In addition, the lockdown measures to prevent the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods, daily labor opportunities, reducing household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population. Hunger has increased across Sudan and over 7.1 million people are severely food insecure at the peak of the lean season (October to December). The figure, the highest ever recorded in the history of the IPC analysis in the country, represents an increase of 22 per cent if compared with the same period last year. At least 1.3 million people are facing emergency levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 4), while around 5.8million people are facing crisis levels of acute food insecurity (IPC 3). Another 17.4million people are estimated to be under stress (IPC 2) and any additional shock could push them to severe levels of hunger. COVID19-related restrictions starting in March and April 2020 having an adverse impact on food security and livelihoods of the most vulnerable households. Movement restrictions had impacted farmers’ access to markets for critical inputs and pastoralists’ access to water and traditional grazing areas. Households’ access to income from informal farm labour. Many households in the pastoral areas are likely to continue facing difficulty meeting their minimum food needs as a result of very high staple food prices, reduced access to labour, lower than normal terms of trade and seasonally low access to animal products during the lean season. The vulnerable and poor whose income and livelihoods heavily depend on a daily wage, especially people living in urban and peri-urban areas, were affected by COVID-19 containment measures first and then with floods and risk not being able to provide a daily meal for their family The country remains prone to natural disasters including seasonal flooding. In 2020, Sudan experienced the worst floods in a hundred years. By October, close to 900,000 people were affected in all 18 states. According to the available information from states, the total crop area affected by flood till the mid of September 2020 is estimated at three million and three hundred and nine thousand feddans constituting around 5.5% of the planted area, livestock losses about 35136 heads. According to preliminary findings of mid-season assessment for the 2020/2021 agriculture season, an average-sized harvest is expected, the high cost of production in addition to the high cost of transportation with the continuous macroeconomic challenges will contribute to above average prices. This will further reduce purchasing power and access for poor household, pushing many to resort to coping strategies such as reducing meal frequency or food quality, consuming seed stock, selling tools or other assets, etc. Based on HRP Addendum, women and girls are more likely to experience a worsening of existing inequalities and disproportionate secondary impacts of floods and restrictions compared to men and boys and even worse for other marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities and those in extreme poverty. The containment measures had limited their work and economic opportunities. Projections indicate that the informal sector will be particularly impacted, and estimates show that women make up 50 percent of this workforce; this will affect household income an even longer-term impact on women.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Most vulnerable returnees and host communities in Dar El Salam,Rural El Fasher and Kalimendo localities - North Darfur, their livelihoods assets will improve and sustain and their ability to buy food increased. Output 1: 100,000 head of livestock owned by 2,000 households in Dar El Salam, Rural El Fasher and Kalimendo localities - North Darfur received vaccination against infectious diseases and treated of parasitic worms. Activity 1.1: Conduct coordination meetings with Humanitarian Affairs Commissioner (HAC) to get permission of access to project areas. Activity 1.2: Conduct coordination meetings with State Ministry of Animal Resources for consultation and collaboration to prepare the vaccination campaigns. Activity 1.3: Conduct coordination meetings with State Ministry of Animal Resources to prepare 200,000 doses of vaccines .Output 2: Farmers from the three localities received vouchers to buy agriculture inputs.Activity 2.1: Contract with Venders and suppliers to prepare agriculture tools and seeds based on need of beneficiaries.Activity 2.2: Select 1000 most vulnerable farmers (host communities and returnees households) using selection criteria.Activity 2.3:Estimate the value of one voucher for each farmer depend on budget line.Activity 2.4: Assist farmers to go with their vouchers to venders and suppliers to receive their agriculture tools and seeds.Activity 2.5: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) after one to three month of distribution.Output 3: Most vulnerable conflict affected households in Kalemendo locality receive cash transfer through cash for work opportunity by removing of 10000 cubic meters of soil and gravels from bottom of Blash Hafir in Kalemendo locality.Activity 3.1: Conduct community mobilization to inform community about the activities.Activity 3.2: Select and register 300 households for excavation of 10000 cubic meters of soil from bottom of hafir.Activity 3.3: Transfer cash for the above 300 households depend on their performance.Output 4: Dissemination of COVID-19 among communities is reducedActivity 4.1: Conduct awareness sessions on target communitiesActivity 4.2: prepare and distribute antiseptics and other COVID- 19 Preventive for target households Activity4.3: prepare and distribute antiseptics and other COVID - 19 Preventive for community institutions e.g schools, health centers..etc.
