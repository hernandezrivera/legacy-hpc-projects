Project Summary

To enhance community resilience to shocks by increasing access to nutrition by integrating with health, and food security and livelihoods services in Tonj North of Warrap State, Kapoeta East and North of Eastern Equatoria State, and Wau of WBeG State. The project will be contributing to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition specific services. To address the suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, IRSS will continue to increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition services for children, adolescents and women in the prioritized counties experiencing multiple deprivations, including high acute malnutrition rates, food insecurity, morbidities, and poor WASH conditions. The response will constitute provision of preventive services maternal, infant and child nutrition and treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition.
Overall, the response will target 89,282 SAM, MAM and PLW cases across various needy population segments, including the non-displaced, returnees, and internally displaced persons. More so, the IRSS will make deliberate efforts to intensify coordination and integrate programming with the Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Health, and Protection clusters to ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical multi-sectoral basic services that will contribute to the reduction of acute malnutrition and ultimately reduce morbidity and mortality.
IRSS will closely monitor the nutrition status of the population, more importantly children under five years and pregnant and lactating women and strengthen the nutrition response through the Food Security Nutrition Surveillance and Monitoring System (FSNMS) and use of routine program data. The children under 5, adolescent boys and girls and PLWs are also vulnerable to consume minimum food intake due to natural and manmade disasters like flood, drought and conflict. As a result, an estimated 2.17 million people, including 1.4 million children under the age of 5, and 737,812 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment. The proposed project will address acute male nutrition through Early detection and treatment to ensure nutrition governance at community level.

1 - Needs

The IDPs, Returnees and Host communities of the proposed project area are vulnerable due to flood, drought and conflict as a result, an estimated 121,701 people, including 70,718 children under the age of 5, and 50,983 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment in the Kapoeta East and North of Eastern Equatorial States, Tonj North of Warrap State, and Wau of Western Bahr-Ghazal State. Their situation is worsened by localized drought, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as COVID-19. Moreover, climatic shocks have resulted in extraordinary flooding, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to basic social services including education and water, sanitation and hygiene, nutrition and health services. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 per cent was reported in people in forty-one counties in six states plus Abyei Admin Area (Upper Nile, Jonglei, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Eastern Equatoria states). The leading cause of morbidity is malaria, followed by acute respiratory infections and acute watery diarrhoea. In 2023, morbidity rates are expected to increase proportionally for each of these causes as they did in 2022, compared to 2021. People are at constant risk of an outbreak of infection. In 2022, two million people were internally displaced; of these 1.4 million required urgent support, and 1.8 million were returnees, of these 1.2 million had urgent needs. In addition, there were 6 million vulnerable host community/non-displaced people, of whom some were older persons or infirm, or persons with disabilities. Out of total IDPs and Returnees including non-displaced persons, the proposed project will cover 89,282 children under 5 and PLWs who are malnourished and facing acute food insecurity at levels 3, 4 and 5.To address the acute malnutrition of the people in need the Islamic Relief South Sudan will be providing support to the food insecure malnourished children and PLWs through introducing nutrition sensitive kitchen garden and sensitizing the community people for hygiene practices and optimum maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN). The project will also focus on early childhood development through providing playing materials for the children. The project will help the community people for early child protection and gender based violence and risk mitigation at nutrition sites. The objectives of the proposed project:(i) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2023;(ii) To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2023;(iii) To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2023.(iv) To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2023.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The proposed project will integrate the mainstreaming of gender and protection across all the interventions. The intervention aims to target women and men as an affirmative approach in ensuring that they are equally participating during the assessment and implementation of all activities as well as monitoring and evaluation. Islamic Relief worldwide will pay particular attention to tackling GBV in Nutrition. The project will also ensure that women are not only recipient of assistance but have their voices and preferences heard and reflected in the project planning, implementation, and monitoring. IRSS will also mainstream conflict sensitivity into the proposed project planning, implementation, and monitoring. IRSS will ensure that core humanitarian standards and do no harm principles are fully applied. The local IRSS staff who might be from the different target communities will also be trained on CHS and conflict sensitivity. In all the interventions female and male will be able to access services. Both genders will be considered in recruitment, capacity building and mentoring through this funding. IRSS will ensure appropriate training is provided for staff on protection threats both genders, mainstreaming, the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to avoid doing harm, and for the project to take into account the specific norms, traditions and gender relations according to each context whilst maximizing inclusion and participation, various tools and methods will be used to assist in this endeavor. For instance, the Gender Marker Code (GAM) for this project is: 4(M), reference number H2091-5435-4599, which will significantly contribute for gender equality, including cross age groups throughout this intervention. As a complement, a gender, age and diversity analysis will be conducted in the early stages of the project. Data will be collected from FGDs with women, men, girls, boys, local leaders and government officials (of all ages, ethnicities, religions and abilities) separately in order to understand their respective gendered needs, threats, risks and vulnerabilities, capacity and coping mechanisms. In GBV cases, IRSS will adhere to the survivor-centered principles and always seek to listen, ensure female staff is present to provide support where women and/or girls are hesitant or culturally unable to interact with male humanitarians or volunteers. IRSS will use the GBV referral pathway process after providing basic care based on the facility capacity in the PHCUs. IRSS will engage males, particularly leaders in the community, as agents of change in health and nutrition outreach activities related to the prevention of GBV (including outreach about unequal food consumption dynamics within the home. In planning stage, IRSS will ensure active participation of women, and men through engaging them in discussion related to project planning various committee that includes mother to mother support groups, and community health committee. IRSS will stress on engaging volunteers from community especially women and equal opportunities for recruitment for males and female. In Implementation. IRSS has a gender and protection policies in place that guide staff conduct and equal employment opportunities. The participation of women and girls in community health management committee, address inequality and protection threats in the target community as members of the committee are being train and aware on handling the challenges in the community, support in monitoring and provision of feedback.
