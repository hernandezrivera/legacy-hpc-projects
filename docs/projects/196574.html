3 - Needs

The number of people in need of basic assistance in Syria is estimated to further increase in 2023 given increased living costs and inflation. To contribute toward increased basic needs, the provision of multipurpose cash assistance will allow households to meet their increased costs for and reduce their negative coping strategies. By basing selection on vulnerabilities established through a basic need scoring system, the project prioritizes the most vulnerable to exposure while ensuring sufficient capacity of the local markets. The economic crisis and ongoing inflation have in the past year continued to further exacerbate the situation, aggravating peoples’ dire living situation and leading to increased food insecurity, protection needs, and amplified negative coping strategies. To address this level of vulnerability of Syrians and increase investment in local communities, this project will deliver life-sustaining assistance through basic assistance as the core modality of assistance. The intervention is planned in line with the recommendations of the CWG, and approved by government authorities. To ensure community engagement and that individual needs are understood, this project engages with households during project design and activity implementation. PDM findings and a complaint and feedback mechanism in place will enhance assistance programming based on actual needs. Needs are identified in CACH's needs assessment and are based on HRP 2022-23.

4 - Implementation of activities

The activity entails the provision of basic assistance through MPCT to 983 HHs for 6 months following the transfer value recommended by the CWG. The selection will be based on vulnerabilities established through a basic need scoring system, targeting extremely vulnerable HHs (score 10+) with a poor or borderline FCS. Step 1: Select beneficiaries in need of life-sustaining assistance according to needs-based scoring system. HHs will be identified through different channels and they will all be assessed based on the scoring system. 2: Potential eligible beneficiaries will be informed about their nomination and the requirements to participate in the intervention through awareness sessions. All the process including the KYC requirements, and FCRMs will be explained, and consent will be requested from all eligible beneficiaries to move forward. 3: Teams will conduct regular monthly market monitoring through market surveys to ensure markets remain functional and are not affected negatively as an effect of the assistance. Results will be shared with the CWG. 4: Transfer of funds to eligible beneficiaries and follow up on the no show report to ensure all beneficiaries are receiving the assistance. Finance and program reconciliation to take place monthly. 5: PDMs to be conducted monthly, Outcome monitoring, and FGDs to be conducted. The monitoring will be split (80% CS & 20% CACH IFMs) to validate the data being gathered. 6: Reporting to take place as per the agreed reporting cycles.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to perceived unfairness in distribution of assistance. M1: Community and religious leaders as well as communities will be transparently and well informed of the objective nature of the criteria and of the process to distribution. Selection criteria are transparent, and groups are fully informed. R2: Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). M2: CACH and CS ensure that their staff are aware of the CoC, internal measures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation are in place. R3: Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety). M3: All the FSP offices must be near the targeted beneficiaries so that they may withdraw the transfers and ensure a safe journey to or from the FSP offices. HHs are free to choose who receives the assistance, ensuring safe access to FSPs and reducing the risk of HH tensions or protection risks. R4: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain population. M4: Scope of outreach activities in coordination with stakeholders in each location will be expanded; hard-to-reach/marginalized categories for assistance will be prioritized in scoring system. R5: Risk of corruption at the stage of beneficiary selection. M5: Strengthen over-sight mechanism and outreach to affected population. CACH and CS ensure that their staffs are aware of the Code of Conduct and develop a range of measures to minimize risk of corruption.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA is a key priority for CaCH and finds expression in regulations and mechanisms to prevent and address all forms of abuse and sexual misconduct committed by representatives or partners involved in the delivery of our work. The policies are underpinned by CACH’s CoC which is clearly stating that any form of SEA is tolerated (zero-tolerance strategy). All staff and partner organizations need to sign up to CACHs CoC, wherein it is clearly stated that reports of misconduct are investigated with all due diligence. To reduce the likelihood of abuse, a complaint mechanism will be in place to ensure that beneficiaries and staff have a safe space to indicate irregularities. R1: Assessment / outreach teams miss identification of hard-to-reach (HTR) groups during outreach and/or assessment phase (most vulnerable do not receive assistance). M1: Scope of outreach activities in coordination with stakeholders in each location will be expanded; HTR / marginalized categories for assistance will be prioritized in scoring system. R2: Assistance increases household tensions. M2: HHs with instances of CP or GBV will be referred to case managers, or to specialized protection agencies, PDM will monitor intrahousehold relationships. R3: Abuse of authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). M3: CACH and CS ensure that their staffs are aware of the CoC, internal measures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation are in place.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: GoS restricts activities of the NGO sector and interferes in project implementation. M1: CS operates under the umbrella of the Church and maintains transparent communication with the GoS; this will be continued. While GoS supports the activities of CS in general, measures to maintain a low profile in Syria, maintain discrete communications, and avoid reference to donors if needed. R2: Escalation of conflict results in disruption or suspension of activities / access within target areas becomes constrained. M2: In case access to project activity locations is not possible, activities will temporarily be suspended. The project will aim to maintain its staff to ensure that operations can be resumed as soon as it is safe to do so. In case of substantial impact on the action, a modification process will be triggered. CS has dedicated representative sub offices in the targeted areas responsible on negotiations progress with communities, authorities, directorates, and national level. R3: Further devaluation of SYP / higher fuel prices increases prices / market shutdown. M3: Closer monitoring of needs and shifts in patterns of expenditure, price and market; adjust transfer value; maintain a flexible methodology that can switch between cash and in-kind when needed. R4: GoS restricts CVA as a modality and available transfer mechanisms are limited in some project areas M4: In case of no access for a prolonged time, in-kind house to house assistance will be considered.
