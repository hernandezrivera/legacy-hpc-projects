Project Summary

This project aims to support the implementation of integrated Primary Health Care (PHC) services by strengthening and integrating preventive nutrition services for vulnerable groups to reach most affected governorates and in need of humanitarian response focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in 16 health facilities accompanied with 16 mobile clinics and 8 independent mobile teams located in accessible and hard to reach areas in NWS and NES. This project will provide nutrition activities in NWS and NES to child and pregnant and lactating women.

Needs

4.7 Million girls and boys under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of undernutrition and in need of comprehensive curative and preventive nutrition services to continue during 2019. Around 92,000 children under the age of five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition during 2019, out of which 19,000 girls and boys are at risk of death due to severe malnutrition if not treated appropriately. Without appropriate care, acute malnourished children are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under the age of five years suffer from micronutrient deficiencies. It is estimated that 1.6 Million mothers will require maternal nutrition services and skilled support for optimal infant feeding and caring practices. Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018. However, there are some pockets where SMART surveys, rapid assessments, and nutrition surveillance information show an increase in chronic malnutrition rates such as Eastern Ghouta (36%) and Tel Abyed (32%) which exceed the very high stunting rate classification according to WHO 2018 thresholds. Chronic malnutrition was a problem in Syria even before the crisis and increased rates have been observed recently. In addition to data that indicates ‘High’ stunting levels in Lajat, Dar’a in 2017 (27.5%) and in the sub-district of Jarablus, Aleppo in 2018 (25%), stunting rates confirmed to be ‘Very High’ in Tel Abyed (32%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) in 2018, as indicated by SMART surveys and using the 2018 WHO-UNICEF stunting thresholds. All the above rates are considered high as per the new WHO/UNICEF classification of stunting 2018. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency. Maternal and child malnutrition is impacted by deterioration in security in some areas such as Idlib and rural Aleppo, increase in needs among returnees and internally displaced people, as well as coverage of nutrition programs such as in underserved North-East Syria and northern Rural Aleppo, including Afrin. Women and children living in hard-to-reach areas, overburdened communities, and those ‘one the move’ deprived of basic services are in urgent need of nutrition interventions to ensure that their nutritional status remains stable and improves. Considering aggravating factors such as ongoing displacement, anticipated returns, change in control, challenging access to nutrition/health services and food, high food prices, diminishing employment opportunities and low service coverage of nutrition partners, are forecasting an increase in undernutrition cases especially in hard-to-reach areas, where the severity of the situation is considered 'critical'. SEMA after working for long period in nutrition sector and collecting data of children and women, identified huge gaps especially on breastfeeding awareness, the lack of knowledge about complementary feeding and the daily meals that should be provided for children, and the using of wrong inherited practices on feeding infants and young children in the targeted communities. These gaps increased during the war due to the decrease in public awareness to the nutrition issues. Hence SEMA decided to raise the awareness on nutrition and IYCF issues on the community level in the targeted areas.

SEMA will approach the activities of the project firstly by considering the project as an important foundation for integrated primary health and nutrition services and community-based and outreach activities, it will include a network of 100 Community Health Workers (CHWs), to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counselling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at community level. These CHWs will be linked with 16 health facilities. The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health staff on IYCF counselling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and CHWs will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help raising awareness of the related communities. Relying on CHWs will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities. All proposed facilities located in the north west are currently functional, most of the staff are already recruited, While in the east the health facilities are run, but the nutrition staff will be recruited. SEMA will also conduct capacity building of the health staff on IYCF at the beginning of the project and will conduct refresher training at midpoint. Through implementing this project, SEMA will develop the management of SAM cases through referring these cases to the facilities, and that’s will be achieved through hiring one of the CHWs as a nutritionist in each facility who will be trained on IYCF, CMAM and nutrition raising awareness issues, in addition to hiring CMAM focal point who will be the responsible for following up all the activities closely and providing the technical support to all project staff. The project in the meantime will aim to proceed the support of distribution nutrition supplies plumpy nut, plumpy doz, and micronutrient powder for infants and PLWs. Furthermore, the project will contain training of health staff on IYCF and CMAM, referring the malnourished cases to the referral centre and support the additional staff with salaries. The capacity building program will proceed to refresh the previous training conducted last year, and train the other new staff hired in the new project, to achieve the outcomes of the program. Specifically in this project, more than 90% of the recruited staff are females and from the targeted communities, in order to obtain more acceptance from the targeted communities.

Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. SEMA, in general, responds to such issues by upholding individual rights in accordance with the humanitarian principals. In that regard SEMA implements all of its activities, taking into consideration doing no harm to the target communities, and providing all services and activities to beneficiaries regardless of their gender, religion, sect, ethnic group, political or regional affiliation. In addition, SEMA works to improve gender equality in term of recruitment, considers protection mainstreaming in all of its projects, provides PSEA training for all staff, provides self-care sessions for staff in order to help them feel more comfortable when dealing with beneficiaries, and introduces staff on how to refer CP and GBV cases to active protection programmes whether implemented by SEMA or other active organisations. Specifically, in this project, more than 90% of the recruited staff are females and from the targeted communities, in order to obtain more acceptance from the targeted communities especially that most of the beneficiaries are females and children.
