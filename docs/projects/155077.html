Needs

The emergency situation appears to have stabilized and there has been no significant changes in the demographics since early 2018 except the relocation of households to new camps during the monsoon season. The Nutrition Sector (NS) partners provide lifesaving interventions and scale up nutrition services across all camps to prevent mortality and ensure nutrition wellbeing of the target population.A SMART survey completed in April/May 2018 shows among makeshift settlements, only 1 in every 14 children 6-23 months of age achieved the minimum acceptable diet (4 of 7 food groups and 3+ meals per day ), and 50% of infants are not exclusively breastfed. While the most recent survey results show an overall improvement in the nutrition situation since October/December 2017, malnutrition remains a serious public health concern. Stunting remains near or at the WHO critical threshold, indicative of long-term nutrition issues in this population which pre-date this crisis. As stunting increases the risk of wasting by 3.2 times, and concurrent stunting and wasting has a higher mortality risk than wasting alone , it is imperative that attention is now given to a comprehensive approach to addressing nutrition needs of this population addressing both wasting and stunting, along with micronutrient deficiency. While young children and pregnant and lactating women remain priority target groups for nutrition support, adolescent nutrition needs warrant attention to mitigate the inter-generational cycle of malnutrition.
