Project Summary

The project addresses main problem of "Limited access to community based integrated CP/MHPSS services of most vulnerable covid-19 and conflict affected women and children." It responds to time critical needs identified in needs assessment conducted on 30-31/5/2021 with AISHA MH clinic’s clients. Overall objective is " Improved mental health and psychosocial well-being and resilience of most vulnerable Covid-19 and conflict affected women >18-59 and >59 years and children 10-17 years " It will be achieved “only if” outcomes are realized: 1. Enhanced wellbeing and capacity of Humanitarian actors and front-line workers from CP/MHPSS service providers to implement CP/MHPSS counselling and case management across all CP/MHPSS responses and protection mainstreaming; 2. Improved emotional, mental and social well-being; and enhanced resilience of most vulnerable covid-19 and conflict affected women/men >18-59 and >59 years and children 10-17 years; and 3. Increased sensitization and responsiveness of local and national duty bearers towards improved CP/MHPSS support services for most vulnerable covid-19 and conflict affected women >18-59 and >59 years and children 10-17 years.
The project targets directly 5200 BNFs (2100 women, 1300 men, 900 girls and 900 boys, out of them 10% (520 PwDs), identified based on vulnerability criteria: women/ men18-59 and >59 years & adolescent girls/ boys 10-17 years impacted by covid-19 and recent escalation including IDPs, Injured persons, family members of martyrs & injured, PWDs, SGBV survivors, FHH according to their location, status, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from COVID-19 and hotspots and severely impacted by the recent escalation in all Gaza Strip with focus on Gaza, Gaza North and ARAs.
AISHA is well suited to implement the project as it manages a licensed specialized mental health clinic, competent CP/MHPSS specialized staff and experience in working with covid-19 and conflict affected women and children across Gaza Strip and coordination with related state actors.
The project links to HRP 2021 Strategic Objective 2, Protection Cluster Objective 2 & 3, and its eligible actions, and standard activities/ indicators.
Activities are: I) capacity building for 60 CP/MHPSS actors on CP/MHPSS guidelines & minimum standards, CP/ GBV case management, Well-being & self-care, PSEA, clinical supervision and recreational activities for AISHA staff, Capacity building of 60 Humanitarian staff (non-protection specialists on protection principles and mainstreaming); II) Provision of blend of remote and in-person MHPSS counselling/ case management services via 5 mobile clinics to 2000 persons in the 5 governorates (structured individual PSS, specialized mental health to 200 women & children; case management services to 200 women, & 200 children; therapeutic yoga to 100 women & children; CVA to 100 women; dignity/recreational kits to 200 women,100 girls/100 boys’ households; Provision of structured awareness raising workshops for the 3000 (1000 W,1000 M, 500 B, 500 G) on violence, positive copying mechanism, protection measures, existing CP/MHPSS services & referral pathways, and Hygiene. III) community engagement, through CwC tools, accountability, advocacy and lobbying campaign.
AISHA ensures a human rights framework, participatory M&E, staff codes of conduct/ethics, PSEA, feedback/ compliant mechanisms, orientation & training of CP/MHPSS workers, zero problems in mental health and psychosocial well-being among its staff/volunteers, community mobilization & ownership, dissemination of information to affected people on their rights/ interventions and positive coping mechanisms, safety/security & full access to services based on humanitarian principles, gender equality, protection & gender mainstreaming, disability inclusion, COVID-protective measures & ethical considerations for CP/MHPSS REMOTE WORKING MODALITIES (Informed consent, professionalism, privacy & confidentiality)

1 - Needs

The impact of the conflict in Gaza has worsened the existing mental health crisis. Prior to the escalation, but subsequent to the COVID-19 pandemic, amid increased pressure on access to basic services, households violence and negative coping mechanisms, according to AISHA unpublished research there is increase of suicide thoughts and attempts' rates amongst adolescent especially girls, and according to some surveys 82% of adolescents in Gaza reported overall poor-very poor mental well-being. In Gaza, around 8,250 persons remained displaced as of end-August after May 2021 escalation. According HRP, HNO, and Gender Alert, there are considerable needs for Child protection & MHPSS interventions including specialized case management, and approaches targeting adolescents and caregivers, particularly males. The CP-AoR estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys ad 332,200 girls) across the oPt are in need of child protection and MHPSS services, (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need, Additionally, 137,000 caregivers in Gaza, are also in need of various MHPSS services. The escalation directly affects the service provision availability, quality, and accessability. Such context has etrimental impact on employment, income, education, In many cases forcing women and girls, including those with disabilities, to adopt negative coping strategies that expose them to further exploitation and abuse. AISHA conducted specific needs assessment on June 2021(updated in Sept 2021) with its frontline workers (24-55 yrs.) and MH clinics' cases, through staff support activity immediately after the May escalation, as well as follow up on clients in case management and psychosocial services. Findings of assessing safety and wellbeing of frontline workers, shows that most staff and their families were still in psychological shock stage, many were having sleep problems and nightmares, somatic symptoms were also commonly expressed. Some staff members were close to bombardments areas, & to evacuate to safer houses with relatives or friends. CP/MHPSS staff expressed the need for wellbeing, integrated security and self-care-WESIS, PFA, and more interventions to help them recuperate and resume their activities. A series of posttraumatic healing sessions & supervision were designed to keep staff involved and safe including Wellbeing and PFA. More than 300 registered clients and their families (203 women 18-59 Yrs, & 79 children; 63 girls, 34 boys 10-17 yrs.) were contacted by phone were counselors offered support and psychological first aid. 58% of them expressed immediate needs for personal items, some had evacuated to relative houses for safety and were preparing to return to their homes. Women 18-59Yrs. complained of sleep problems, somatic pains and aches, constant vigilance and startle responses, mood changes during the day with intense fear reactions at night. A common complaint was inability to concentrate and think clearly with recurrent memories of frightening images and events. Another group that was contacted and assessed were women 18-59 Yrs.with loss of their family members, and women who were injured and their families. Also, children (boys &girls aged 6-17 yrs.) who were injured and being treated by the MoH, assessment and intervention with these women and children is ongoing, but initial impressions reflect the need or immediate psychological first aid and trauma work both preventive and therapeutic.Post-traumatic symptoms were very common in children (boys &girls aged 6-17 yrs.) including bed wetting, fear reactions, hyperactivity, weeping and crying and sleep problems. Patents find their children more difficult to manage especially protecting them from harm and COVID-19 exposure. The bad and impacted economic situation from May escalation combined with covid-19 increase the vulnerability and poverty of individuals which let to increasing rates of violence rates and stresses among women and children.TDH-RNA 2021 stated that the risk of COVID-19 transmission increased, considering displaced people seeking shelter inside & outside the DES and the substantial interruption of the vaccination program. Water supplies have been disrupted because of damage to the system and power outages. Some families have no access to safe drinking water. Females are particularly affected by a shortage or lack of water because of menstruation and the need for privacy, & because of their domestic and childcare duties. Personal hygiene is affected in terms of bathing and washing clothes There are not enough sanitary kits or hygiene. The current situation might lead to disease transmission and the outbreak of COVID-19 again. There is huge need for hygiene kits to keep everyone safe. TDH-RNA recommended 1) Provide CP/MHPSS support to children, families, frontline workers. 2) Provide appropriate PFA, recreational activities with and for children & family members. 3)Within a gendered approach, respond to the CP and MHPSS needs of men and boys. 4) Provide tailored case management to GBV cases among affected women and children with protection concerns. 5) Provide MHPSS to children & their families. In addition to that, AISHA need assessment recommended 1) the provision of structured awareness raising for women, men 18-59 yrs, boys and girls 10-17 yrs on violence, positive copying mechanism, protection measures, existing CP/MHPSS services & referral pathways, and Hygiene. 2) CVA assistance to the most vulnerable case and GBV women and Children impacted badly by the escalation and covid-19 which also impacted their wellbeing. 3) develop the capacity of Humanitarian actors (women and men aged 24-55 yrs.) on protection mainstreaming and self-care and wellbeing and stress management, PFA, and PSEA, and capacity development for CP and MHPSS on Child protection minimum standards and Guidance on MH service provision.
