Needs

Trends of food security indicators, as shown by June data (Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping Unit (VAM), June 2018), suggest that overall food security amongst the Rohingya refugee population has improved, which is likely due to the regular and effective general food assistance program through in-kind and voucher programs. Under existing conditions, the refugee population remains 100% reliant on food assistance to sustain the minimum daily required kilo-calories per individual and for diet diversification. This dependency is due to several factors such as: lack of opportunity for own food production, limited financial and physical access to food, movement restrictions, protection issues and limited access to cooking fuel and sources of energy. Thus, sustaining the food assistance of the population is paramount as breakage in the food supply pipeline could deepen the existing emergency and threatens the survival of the vulnerable Rohingya FDMN. To address dietary diversity, response actors are moving forward with complementary activities to the general blanket food assistance through supplementary/fresh food vouchers and hot meals provision. However, the roll-out has been somewhat challenged by competing priorities of the immediate food needs and funding commitments. According to most recent family counting data, approximately 52% of the Rohingya refugee population are women and girls with the largest gender discrepancy being among the population of working age (age 18-59) where 23% are female. Overall 80 per cent of the Rohingya population are women and children. Key vulnerable groups have been identified as single mothers (16%), person with serious medical condition (5%), older persons at risk (2%), persons with disabilities (4%), older person with child (2%), separated children (2%), unaccompanied children (1%), single male parent with infant (1%)_UNHCR Bangladesh Population Fact Sheet as of 31st October 2018. • 89,732 people are living in 211 majhee blocks where they eat fewer than two meals a day, use negative coping mechanism for food, have no income source and rely on humanitarian assistance and have limited access to markets (ACAPS & NPM Analysis Hub : Situation Overview and Summary Indicators _NPM SA Round 12); • From 29 October - 12 November, FSS reached 148,111 households (666,499 individuals) through GFD.• 27,826 refugee households (125,217 individuals) received paper vouchers for complementary food as a top-up to the GFD to support diet diversification.• Only about 13 % of the total population received complementary vouchers to support diet diversification, which serves as a top up to the GFD. There is a need to scale up this intervention to reach more people. (Iscg situation report_15 Nov 2018) Ensuring the complimentary voucher to support the diet diversification is very important. Through the voucher system QC proposes to increase the caseload of complementary voucher with fresh food items and target the most vulnerable (HHs with children under 5, people with disabilities/chronically ill/elderly/pregnant& lactating mothers) categories and those with limited access to food, with an aim on enhancing diet diversity.
