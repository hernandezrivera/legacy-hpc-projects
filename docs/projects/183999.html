Project Summary

This project aims to support the provision of Basic Health Services with focus on Primary Health Care and COVID19 response focusing on localities with severe shortages of PHC services in the states of Khartoum, North, West Darfur, Kassala, Gedaref States. This project will target 150,000 most vulnerable groups of people in the selected localities. HDPO believes that there is no simple solution to reduce/eradicate various health challenges. As such, in order to improve the provision of basic health services, it is necessary to address the range of health issues in an integrated, sustainable manner aimed at improving accessibility and affordability of quality PHC services by the most vulnerable people of the communities. HDPO will collaborate and coordinate with locality and state development committees in identifying and mobilising people for project intervention. At locality and state level, the already established networks and links with the committees mentioned will be strengthened by HDPO in all stages of the project like from designing, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

1 - Needs

The republic of Sudan is home to hundreds of sub-Saharan African ethnic groups, characterized by political and security challenges in recent decades. In addition to displacement arising from insecurity, the scattered population has recently suffered from outbreaks of several diseases like cholera, dengue fever, Rift Valley fever, chikungunya, malaria and pandemics like Covid19. Several challenges to improve healthcare access include; poor implementation of policies/guidelines, and coordination between health and other sectors such as education. The availability of qualified health cadres and healthcare workers is still a challenge in Sudan hindering the capacity and efforts to scale up the response. According to figures by the federal and state ministries of health, there are only 5,457 doctors, 12,601 nurses, and 17,343 midwives working in the public health system (0.76 doctors + nurses+ midwives per 1,000 populations, against the 4.45 per 1000 populations) which below WHO recommended Health workforce requirements for universal health coverage. Even though over 70% of the population resides in rural areas, 70% of health workers work in the urban areas with 38% in the capital, Khartoum. Moreover, 67% of the staff works in secondary and tertiary care creating a significant gap in PHC provision (FMOH, 2019). Under maternal and child health in Sudan, one and a half million pregnant women of reproductive age lack access to both Essential obstetric care, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (CEmONC). Only 13.4% of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities during 2021 with 40% had home deliveries not attended by a qualified healthcare person (Multi Sectorial Needs Assessment, 2021). Children under five years of age are exposed to vaccine-preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization and a high prevalence of malnutrition. In Sudan, PHC is the first point of contact for patients with the healthcare system. It includes the dressing stations, dispensaries, PHC units, and health centers. The latter forms the referral point from the lower facilities. The importance of PHC is that it provides the essential care to all, including vaccination of children, nutrition, reproductive health, integrated management of childhood immunization, management of common diseases and prescription of essential medications (Ebrahim et al., 2017). The core components of PHC is health promotion and provision of basic health services, which is limited in Sudan. For example, only 81.6% of PHC units provide vaccination for children and 67.3% provide family planning services. The PHC units are decreasing either due to cessation of function or in comparison to the population growth, alongside outbreak of pandemics like Covid19. Despite the steady decline in reported covid19 cases throughout the year 2021, the case fatality ration stayed at 7.5 per cent recording one of the highest globally. The COVID-19 vaccination coverage is standing at around 2% below the national vaccine plan 20% by the end of 2021. COVID-19 has affected the capacity of health system to provide essential health services, specifically impacting outreach and immunization services and household income at the community level. The shortage of PPEs and other essentials medical products and supplies poses a tremendous challenge to improving health outcomes. Only 33.2% of the population live in urban areas yet the minimum PHC package is provided by 19% of PHC facilities which hamper the adequacy and quality of PHC services. Following the consultation meetings HDPO conducted with the MoH in Darfur, it was discovered that there are huge gaps in terms of access to PHC in those localities. Therefore through this project, HDPO will support the provision of basic health services with focus on PHC and COVID19 response targeting 150,000 venerable people.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is a term used by the UN and NGO community to refer to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.
