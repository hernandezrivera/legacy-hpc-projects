Needs

Multi Cluster Needs Assessment (Remainees, Returnees, IDPs) September 2018Rapid Overview of Areas of Return (ROAR): Ba’aj, Sinjar, Telafar and surrounding areasWorld Vision Baseline evaluations of NISE and Graduation Approach ProjectsThe major military operations in Iraq against Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) ended around December 2017. The Government of Iraq (GOI) re-established control of Telafar, located about 80 km away from west Mosul in April 2017. At the time of liberation, the area was reported to be almost entirely emptied of its inhabitants. Following the end of military operations, early reports of the humanitarian situation in Telafar showed that approximately 60,000 - 80,000 residents had returned, which local officials estimate to be 30 - 40% of the pre-2014 population. According to EL cluster, there are 844,552 people in need in Ninewa Governorate alone, of which 158,103 in Telafar. World Vision Iraq (WVI), in the scope of HRP, is proposing livelihoods intervention in Telafar District to meet the Emergency Livelihoods (EL) needs of the most vulnerable population residing in Telafar city and surrounding rural areas. The selection of location is backed by three major rationales. First, according to REACH comprehensive Multi-cluster needs assessment, supported by WV and other agencies, it was reaffirmed that Telafar remains among the top areas in need in Iraq in multiple sectors (Highest number among non-displaced ), while EL flagged as most severe. Secondly, WVI will leverage on its existing livelihoods program, which will last until September 2019, in Telafar to increase cost-efficiency and ensure effectiveness of this project. Thirdly, since needs of population is multilayer, WVI aims provide complementing programming in Telafar along Protection/Education intervention, so as to introduce a synergy of activities to bring long-term change by addressing multiple interconnected root causes of vulnerability. Based on MCNA assessment, 36% IDPs are in need of EL assistance. The needs of returnees and non-displaced population (Host Communities) are comparable to those of IDPs in most aspects, with 39% of Host Communities (HC) in need of EL assistance. The EL need is reflected in lack of income, increased vulnerability and food insecurity. This, in turn, amplifies negative coping strategies, such as reduced food consumption or frequency of meals, spend or depletion of savings, selling household property, etc. Compared to male, female, children and disabled people are more impacted. The reduced food intake due to lack of income has significant impact on growth and health of children, pregnant & lactating women and elderly, while depletion of savings also cause school drop out of children due to economic stresses. In many cases, dropped out children and youth have higher protection vulnerability due child labour. The school non-attendance rate among the IDPs and HC ranges from 23% to 38%. All target population flagged inability to afford education as the main cause for non-attendance. Livelihoods gaps also affect other sectorial outcomes, for example in both IDPs and HC, insufficient income is the main barrier of accessing services, such as healthcare. Thus, EL needs had received the highest severity scores compared to other sectors. Vulnerable women, such as female-headed household, find the situation even more difficult. In Iraq, it is not easy for women to engage in labour market. Due to cultural norms, it is harder for females to establish network or “westa” for seeking job or doing business, as most business owner or entrepreneur are male. Even for female business owner, they might find challenges in connecting to the market to sell produces, as they are less confident or equipped, since traditionally there were less women in this business industry. In the light of the above WV is proposed activities will respond to immediate needs of the affected population to restoring their livelihoods and contribute to their well-being through small asset transfers modalities, integrated with protection and WASH interventions which WV is also applying for HRP/IHF for Telefar.

Activities

World Vision’s (WV) proposed interventions will cover geographical areas of Telafar, by assisting in Emergency livelihood needs through small livelihoods asset replacement, to provide immediate support to the targeted 400 returnee and 100 Host Community (50% women, 50% men). A baseline will be conducted to evaluate the existing livelihoods strategies, community infrastructure and draw the profile of the vulnerable households. The assessment finding will further inform the selection criteria of the beneficiaries. However, priority will be given to female-headed household, families with children under 5, pregnant/lactating women and disabled members. WV will leverage on its existing LVCD (local value chain development) Project in Telafar to conduct market assessment, value chain analysis and identify areas of linkages of beneficiaries with private sector, NGOs and other stakeholders. In communities where mixed groups are not appropriate, both men and women groups will be formed, so as to ensure women will be represented and their needs are taken into account during participant selection and activity planning.The envisaged Livelihood replacement intervention will use established work norms (working standards, roles, tasks, etc) will be calculated on the technical requirements of the asset rehabilitation/creation needs. Each targeted households will participate 20 days in total. The Small Livelihood Assets replacement interventions will be conducted through grants for 400 Returnees and 100 Host Communities households (50% Female 50% Male) to support them in restarting their livelihoods activities. The intervention will support participants to restart their livelihoods activities that they engaged in before the conflict, which is not possible to continue due to lost and damaged productive assets. The asset replacement activity will be informed by the new situation of value chain and labour market, to ensure households can realistically and productively restart their livelihoods, and at the same time add-value to the community as whole. Local authorities will be consulted to avoid duplicating efforts on asset replacement, targeting and be informed of any regulations that can support or hinder activity. The inclusion of women and youth will be ensured since the targeting, when selection criteria are in the process of contextualization per community. The weight for each criterion will be balanced to ensuring that women and youth have equal access to participation.

Mitigation Measures

a. WVI in Iraq Security Manager/officer, in coordination with the project team and monitors will frequently re-assess security threats, with reference to updates provided by United Nations Assistance Mission for Iraq (UNAMI). The project team will be provided with appropriate security orientation. b. All staff members will undergo full mine awareness education from a certified NGO. The participants of asset replacement in the rural areas should present the reference that their land is demined in advance. c. The community members will be sensitized on project activities and objectives prior starting the activities, as well as selection criteria. Among the objectives and necessary preconditions, it would be agreed with community and local authorities to have vulnerable women and youth participants on the project. Community and religious leaders will be encouraged to attend the training on human right and women empowerment. The selection of beneficiaries will be transparent and led by community via TFG, minimizing bias assumptions that may lead to dispute among community members. d. Different strategies will be enhanced to ensure access to interventions. Logically, timing of the activities will take place during daytime and preferably not on national or religious holidays. Moreover, where required, different sites/groups for men and women will be established to minimize cross-gender exposure (which might deter beneficiaries from participation). In case a beneficiary is unable to participate in some activities, weaver can be made based on discussion with TFG, so that there is an option to send a next of kin/relative on behalf of the vulnerable beneficiary who cannot physically participate or for the vulnerable beneficiary to attain non-labour intensive tasks. e. Assets will be examined in line with related risks. Participants prior receiving the assets will be trained on safety on how to handle the assets well as risks associated with livelihoods activity. f. All participants in the small assets transfer activities will have proper safety training, while the nature of the work will be of a low risk with proper equipment or tools provided. Project personnel will be trained on the first aid and monitor the field on any obstacles/objects that can cause harm. The work plan will be shared with the local authorities and hospital for securing means of timely health assistance if needed. g. Project staff will closely monitor and screen the beneficiary for the small livelihood assets-transfer interventions. Age limit, will be included into one of the criteria of beneficiary selection, while awareness will be raised about the undesirability of deploying children to participate in the work. Suspected minors will be talked with to identify their age and/or their need to be in school. h. The agency will maintain the principle of impartiality, neutrality and independence by maintaining a clear communication with communities on selection criteria, background of agency, as well as involving communities in key decision of design and planning of intervention. The project will also pay attention to cultural sensitivity, and ensure both female and male staff will be deployed to work with different groups of beneficiaries. Also, feedback and complain mechanism will be in place to ensure a two-way communication is enabled to handle risk and issues at a timely manner. i. The project will coordinate and consult communities and local authority with the location, date and time of distribution, so as to minimize risks. Information and updates from security, and UNAMI will also be taken into consideration. Where locations are with high security risk, project team will be accompanied by security staff/team to ensure sufficient support.

How is your project accountable to affected population

To ensure accountability, the project will adopt WV’s Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) with reference to its “Communicating with Community” strategy, which has been mainstreamed in all other WV livelihoods and food assistance programming, so as to ensure two way communications between beneficiaries and the project. The AAP is guided by 4 pillars of framework as below:1. Information provision –WV will provide information of interventions using face-to-face engagements and banners. To raise community awareness on PSEA, related information will also be provided, including rights and reporting mechanisms. Likewise, community will be informed on available feedback and complaints mechanisms. Information will use appropriate language with pictorial aid. As per the sensitization and training section of the implementation plan, Project Staff will plan a range of meetings to ensure effective knowledge transfer to beneficiaries, especially in regards to modality for this project as well as their rights in the project.2. Consultation- WV is committed to the principle of informed consent and ensuring that communities are aware of, understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. WV will ensure to consult the local authorities and communities on the key decisions for the project before the implementation through FGD method.3. Participation - WV will ensure to collect inputs from the most vulnerable people (beneficiaries) to raise their issues. At the same time, the community representative will be actively involved throughout the project through Task Force Groups, using their knowledge and understanding of local context and needs.4. Suggestion and Feedback Mechanism - different mechanisms will be used to receive information, complaints, and feedback from people of different backgrounds, including face-to-face meetings with MEAL team, and Suggestions & complaints boxes at community centers. This information is classified 5 categories (Sensitive complaints, non-sensitive complaints, suggestions, inquiries, feedback), and all inputs received will be addressed and followed up appropriately. Monthly summary report of all the inputs will be shared with project staff.Age, Gender and Diversity Policy: The AGD policy is relevant to all humanitarian actors and all interventions in every emergency. When working with affected populations, WV Staff are instructed to first understand their specific needs and abilities. Communities are not homogeneous groups but are composed of individuals with specific backgrounds. Close engagement with persons of concern is critical to WVs understanding of their ability to understand and assess their situation and specific needs. This helps WV to design appropriate responses, determine what must be done to assist and protect them, and ensure that marginalization and vulnerability is not increased. The success of our work in emergencies depends on obtaining access to and engaging with persons of concern.
