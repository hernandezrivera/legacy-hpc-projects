Project Summary

This project is intended to support 10,800 beneficiaries (5,500 women, girls 2,500,1,000 boys and 1,800 men) through an integrated approach aimed at saving lives by ensuring GBV and Child Protection assistance is delivered to the intended beneficiaries in a timely, accessible and context sensitive manner. Women and girls will be prioritised for these services and will be identified from the 3 counties of Tonj East, North and Gogrial East as informed by the recent HNO 2020 through the different referral pathways existing and established to address GBV and CP cases. These have been prioritized because: during crisis, GBV cases among women and girls are high where vulnerability increases and access to services decreases, sexual and gender-based violence is widespread, sexually transmitted infections, unintended pregnancies, unsafe abortion, and maternal morbidity or death escalates (OXFAM, 2019). Noted also is that the daily threats women and girls face (as they seek necessities such as water, firewood, shelter and food), often go unreported and unaddressed, compounding the issues compromising their health and well-being.
The recent floods have caused rippling effects in most parts of South Sudan not sparing Tonj and Gogrial East Counties in Warrap State. UN OCHA reported that by October 15 2019, between 60,000 to 80,000 million people across South Sudan were affected by floods alone and few partners could access flooded areas (due to destruction of infrastructure) to deliver emergency food assistance. These floods on top of destroying shelters, farms, and infrastructure, thousands have been displaced to open higher ends as they wait for the water level to recede. In these times, the people more vulnerable to violence generally are even more at risk of abuse, assault and exploitation in search for survival in shared and congested places that even deny them privacy hence exposing them to abuse.
During emergency/crisis, shelter, food, water, and medical care are always prioritized by humanitarian partners since they are the ‘well-known needs’ and the core of emergency programming in the first days of response. However, for women and girls, displacement and destruction are not the only threats during emergencies but their unique needs should be identified and addressed for them to live a dignified life else are left vulnerable to abuse. For example, in times of crisis, women are at enormous risk of physical and sexual violence – from armed groups, strangers, neighbors and family members. When a woman has been raped, she has just three days to access care to prevent the potential transmission of HIV, five days to prevent unwanted pregnancy, and sometimes just a few hours to ensure that life-threatening injuries do not become fatal. However, in most of the targeted counties, there are neither such services nor public safe homes for GBV survivors which puts them at a great risk.
Greater Warrap State, a patriarchal society poses a unique context in addressing GBV. There are alternative dispute resolution mechanisms such as a social council of elders who resolve cases informally. However, the path for a female survivor is blocked with the hurdles of stigma, shame, fear and real threats to her security. A recent KAP study conducted by TOCH in Gogrial East and West Counties (August 2019) confirmed these gender biases.
TOCH is committed to ending GBV as part of its mandate that seeks to end harmful practices affecting women and children across South Sudan employing a rights-based, community-based and survivor-centred approach at the individual, family and community levels. In addition, TOCH has a contextual understanding of GBV of the targeted project sites with a strong presence and good working relationships with the local communities and authorities.
TOCH does coordination of GBV-sub cluster for Warrap and in addition, a beneficiary feedback mechanism will be established as a reporting mechanism for commitments and action

1 - Needs

Globally, women and girls face risks to their safety, mental and physical health, and sense of empowerment on a daily basis. This however does not mean that men and boys do not suffer from violence (UNFPA, 2019). The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence in South Sudan have destroyed livelihoods and left more than 7 million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection (HNO 2019). While the situation was no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the country has been hit by yet another calamity, ‘the floods’. The HNO 2020 estimates that out of 7.5 million South Sudanese in need of emergency assistance, 1.85 million (25%) require GBV support. Seasonal flooding that started in June 2019 have displaced millions of population worst of all destroying shelters, foods, livelihoods and reducing the already constrained coping capacity of the conflict affected persons. An estimated 600,000 to 800,000 people have been reported in 29 counties of South Sudan to have been affected by floods (UN OCHA, 2019). 364,600 affected people are in Warrap State in different locations including about 300,000 people in Gogrial West, Gogrial East and Tonj North counties. In September 2019, armed clashes were also reported in Tonj, Warrap, with civilian casualties, displacements and suspension of humanitarian activities. Furthermore, cattle-raiding in Tonj South recently displaced thousands to Tonj North. An estimated 46,000 people were displaced in Maiwut (33,000) and Tonj South (13,000) (UN OCHA, 2019). During emergencies, displaced populations are forced to live in overcrowded settlements and usually, women and children are even more at risk for abuse, assault and exploitation (UNFPA, 2019). There is a heightened sense of instability, insecurity and fear, a loss of autonomy, and dependency on others for aid. It is at this time that people become more vulnerable to violence. In Greater Tonj, over 22,000 people have been reported to be affected by floods while 6,250 families are reported in Gogrial East County and 70,000 people classified in Emergency and at risk of GBV, WASH and health. It is also reported that the raging floods have resulted into substantial destruction of houses, road networks and destruction of livelihoods of 10,892 households (according to the results of the Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) conducted by South Sudan Red Cross (SSRC) in collaboration with the State department for Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) in August 2019). The South Sudan GBV Sub Cluster recent needs analysis identified the following 14 counties as priority counties under Tier 1 for 2020 HRP; Aweil East and Aweil North, Ayod, Balliet, Bor South, Cueibet, Gogrial East, Ibba, Longochuk, Maridi, Tonj East and Tonj North, Twic and Wulu. Basically, three thematic areas for comprehensive GBV programming were prioritized: Prevention activities, Risk mitigation measures (in collaboration with other sectors) and Response services. TOCH’s planned activities are in line with the GBV SC recommendations. Cultural practices including child marriages and women’s inability to own resources are also a contributing factor to the severity of GBV in Greater Tonj and Gogrial East Counties. A KAP study by TOCH (August 2019) revealed that women do not seek legal help even when they are abused because it is culturally accepted that men have the right to harass and beat them. They are locked out of obtaining basic health and education services they need to survive due to socio-economic and gender related cultural barriers. Domestic violence is rife; wife beating is considered a normal practice by both men and some women as an appropriate way of ‘discipline’ or ‘teaching’. Traditional practices such as forced and early marriage and intimate partner violence including marital rape are rooted in social norms that not only condone violence against women, but may even encourage it.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

A KAP survey has already been conducted in Gogrial East by TOCH. However, one will be conducted in Tonj North and East Counties in order to understand and plan according to the local needs and context. Besides, awareness raising campaigns will be delivered in a gender-balanced team and culturally appropriate ways.Protection Mainstreaming through working with other cluster partners, Safety Audits, protective accompaniment, and boosting the income generating activities fuel energy stoves/lamps to mitigate any probable conflict and ensuring the intervention does not cause further harm to the beneficiaries or the wider community. Moreover, ensuring the safety and dignity of beneficiaries should be paramount and this can also be achieved through collaborating with formal protection systems and services in place through the referral pathways of public health, police and legal support. More so staff and all stakeholders will be trained on the importance of beneficiary safety to ensure confidentiality of information as well as prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). After the training, they will be made to sign an oath of confidentiality and a PSEA that is included in the Terms of Reference of all personnel working in referral pathways. A complaint feedback mechanism will be established in consultation with the community and TOCH staff will endeavor to sensitize the community on how to report abuse, who the focal point staff is and the available referral pathways.Ensuring equitable access to services for all beneficiaries will be considered at the project design stage to make sure the targeting criteria as well as the assistance is accessible to the vulnerable groups including women, girls and persons with special needs. 9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Ensuring accountability and transparency is one of the core values of TOCH. All staff before deployment will undergo a training on Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) including understanding HR systems, harassment procedures (PSEA) and codes of conduct. The community will be briefed on the same beforehand and required to provide feedback through an agreed and appropriate beneficiary feedback mechanism. This mechanism may be in the form of a feedback form, complaints box or contact details that are widely disseminated among the targeted population. Target communities will be engaged through the project cycle as service providers, beneficiaries, volunteers during project design, community awareness campaigns, monitoring and reporting (providing feedback). This is intended to facilitate their participation, ownership and sustainability of project activities. Lastly, gender and age disaggregated data will be collected and reported to ensure inclusion and equitable participation by vulnerable groups.
