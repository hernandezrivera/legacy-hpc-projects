Project Summary

Context: Almost 13 years of continuous conflict and two devastating earthquakes have given rise to alarming levels of food insecurity across NW Syria. According to the 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), of a total population in NW Syria of approximately 5 million, 3.65 million people are facing moderate or severe food insecurity. High inflation, currency depreciations and rapid commodity price rises continue to drive humanitarian needs, resulting in increasing vulnerabilities and poverty levels.
Impact: Agriculture is the backbone of the rural economy, providing the main livelihood and income source for the majority of HHs in the region. Climatic shocks, limited access to agricultural inputs, and water scarcity, have severely limited crop yields and livestock productivity. Small-scale poultry production and dairy processing – critical to the food security and nutrition of a sizeable proportion of HHs and one of few livelihood opportunities accessible to IDP HHs – are being decimated by the rising cost of inputs and high mortality rates resulting from epidemics.
Needs assessments: This project incorporates the findings/priorities identified by the Food Security and Livelihoods Assessment (2023) and assessments conducted by FAO including a Post-earthquake Rapid Needs Assessment on Agricultural Livelihoods and Production in the Northwest (2023), a Market Assessment of Selected Value Chains of Vegetables, Dairy and Poultry (2023) and a Participatory Rural Appraisal (2023). All indicators point towards a critical need for quality agricultural inputs, the protection of productive livestock assets and access to irrigation.
Strategic Alignment: The response is aligned with Strategic Objective 3 of the 2024 HNO. It is also aligned with FSLC’s SO2 and SO3. Implementation of this project will be duly coordinated with the FSL Cluster and its partners and, where appropriate, with the Early Recovery and Livelihoods (ERL) Cluster. Referrals to other clusters will be made when appropriate.
Beneficiaries and target areas: 196,100 HHs (1.2 million individuals) will be assisted in the most affected sub-districts prioritised in accordance with the findings of the 2024 HNO and will target HHs that are severely and moderately food insecure. Target areas have been selected based on accessibility as well as on the suitability of the proposed intervention. Beneficiary selection will be in accordance with FAO’s vulnerability criteria and the respective HH’s capacity to benefit from the assistance provided.
Interventions:
- 17,900 HHs to benefit from critical support towards the production of wheat (USD 475 voucher for 200kg TSP + 200kg urea per HH), forage crops (100kg seeds in-kind + voucher 100kg TSP), and summer (knapsack sprayer in-kind + voucher for NPK and DAP per HH) and winter vegetables (in-kind seeds + knapsack sprayer organic fertilizer + voucher for NPK and urea per HH).
- 12,400 HHs to benefit from seasonal cash-for-work for vegetable harvesting (USD 6 x 30 person days per HH).
- 19,500 HHs to benefit from animal feed distributions (600kg voucher/USD 400), treatment of critical veterinary diseases and support towards animal reproduction (USD 300 direct support per HH).
- 130,000 HHs to benefit from vaccinations against prevalent Transboundary Animal Diseases (ND/PPR/S-G pox/FMD/LSD; av. value USD 15 per HH)
- 6,000 women-headed HHs to benefit from a poultry production package (15 hens + 2 roosters + 200kg feed valued at USD 400 per HH)
- 10,300 HHs to benefit from cash-for-work for light rehabilitation of irrigation canals and communal irrigation stations (USD 8.25 x 52 person days per HH).
Protection Risk Analysis
Risk: Tensions emerge between host community (HC) members and IDPs over perceived inequitable distribution of assistance. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures (MM): 1. Community mobilization process to disseminate information on eligibility based on established vulnerability criteria.
