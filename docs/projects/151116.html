Project Summary

The project will focus on providing access to education opportunities for 3,500 learners (40% girls) from IDP settings in Dollow district as well as those from 22 schools that closed down due to attacks from AS through introduction of a forreign curriculum in Garbarrey district. The project will expand five existing schools (2 of which are IDP schools) in across three districts (Dollow, Luuq and Belet Hawa) to accomodate new enrolment of learners who will be supported to enjoy learning opportunities beyond the project period. The project will further enhance the quality of education service delivery in the 5 targeted schools through training of 95 teachers (50% female) on basic pedagogical skills, child safeguarding and school safety, while mainstreaming protection awareness as part of education activities in schools. The project will run for one year after which it will be integrated into the existing NCA education programme in Gedo region.

Needs

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is among the most complex and longstanding emergencies in the world. The HNO 2019 estimates 2.6M people are displaced out of which 1M are school aged children. More than one-third of those in need are internally displaced persons (IDPs). The increasing rates of displacement present significant protection challenges, particularly for women and girls, as well as school aged children. Many households have lost traditional socio-economic safety nets and frequently experience extremely poor living conditions in Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) sites. Dollow district hosts a large number of IDPs from various regions across the country, who continue to experience barriers towards access to basic services including education. Over the recent months the number of new IDPs has grown significantly amounting pressure to the already overstretched resources and basic services. Having no access to schools means that boys and girls get exposed to various risks that can be avoided if they were enrolled in school. NCA has been providing EiE for over 3,000 boys and girls from Qabasa and Qansahley IDP camps for the last six years. The two established schools have enabled IDP boys and girls enjoy their right to learn while serving as safe spaces from potential risks. NCA recent assessments indicate that there are over 1500 IDP children in need of education opportunities in these camps, majority of whom are new IDP children, yet the existing schools can not accommodate more enrollments. On the other hand the rate of school attacks in Somalia have continued to rise in the recent years. 22 NCA supported schools were shut down due to AS attack disrupting learning for over 2,000 children (40% girls). NCA will provide learning opportunities to 3,500 children (40% girls) out of which 1,500 (40% girls) will be IDP children.

Activities

NCA will implement a number of activities that will enable IDP and host community boys and girls without access to education to continue enjoying their right to education. NCA will expand the existing 2 IDP schools in Kabasa and Qansahley through consurtcion of 10 TLS (5 in each school) to accomodate the new IDP learners. In addition, a tracer study in Garbaharrey to establish the effects of the school attacks on the population. It will further seek to establish their location and the proportion of school children that remain out of school to date. NCA will then expand five host community schools in Belet Hawa, Dollow and Luuq districts through construction of 10 TLS (5 per school) to support 2,000 learners (40% girls) to re-enroll in these targeted schools. In total, 20 TLS will be established in the targeted IDPs and host schools. The new established TLS as well as existing classrooms in the targeted schools will be equipped with 500 desks (25 per classroom) to accommodate the new learners, who will be provided with learning materials to enable them learn effectively. NCA and partners will further train some of the teachers; 95 (40% female) previously teaching in the affected schools and 50 CECs (40%) female on school safety as well as pedagogical skills and re-employ them to continue providing quality service delivery. These teachers will be provided with a monthly incentive to sustain them in schools, while providing support to CECs to effectively manage schools. NCA will also work closely with the government to implement school safety framework towards protection of schools from attacks. Protection awareness will also be done in all the target schools to enhance safety.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Integration of affected boys and girls from IDP settings and Garbaharrey host community into the regular ongoing school programe supported by NCA will be one of the strategies that the project will put in place to enhance continuity of learning beyond the project period. NCA will work closely with the government to implement the school safety framework to enhance protection of schools from potential attacks. Teachers and CECs will be trained on school safety and supported to establish mechanisms to protect the school environment, while children clubs will be supported to promote protection awareness messages in the target schools. Both the government and community stakeholders will be engaged to identify durable solutions to various attacks in schools which will enhance ownership and sustainability.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The targeted learners (2,100 boys and 1,400 girls) from IDP and host communities do not have learning opportunities. Yet these children continue to miss out on their right to education, excluding them from opportunities being enjoyed by their peers in other districts. In addition being out of school predisposes them to further risks that could harm them such as early marriages and recruitment into militia groups among others. As such the project will focus on ensuring that these learners are traced and supported to re-enroll back to school in safe learning environment and enjoy their right to uninterrupted learning during and beyond the project period.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Boys and girls of school going age are affected in various ways during conflict and related emergencies, yet more often than not their needs are hardly met or taken into consideration during such situations. In the event of school attacks, children including their teachers and CECs are exposed to physical harm and are affected psychologically yet some of these challenges particularly in hard to reach areas go unaddressed. This is also the case for IDP children particularly girls who largely remain at home to engage in household chores while the male counterparts continue to learn. NCA through the project will ensure that the affected boys and girls as well as their teachers are provided with support and safe spaces in safe and accessible schools to continue learning and providing education support respectively.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Given the exposure that boys and girls from the affected schools are facing being out of schools, they continue to lag behind, having missed a whole academic year hence are likely to perform poorly after enrollment back to school. In addition, as indicated above, being out of school exposes to various risks such as early marriage (for girls) and recruitment into militia groups particularly for boys. NCA reports indicate that some boys have already enrolled as child soldiers in Garbaharrey which is harmful and denies them their right to progressive development, a healthy childhood life and the risk of death among others. In the IDP camps, the risk of engaging in criminal activities and exposure to harmful practices such as early marriage for girls remain high hence the need to provide learning opportunities to minimize the risk of exposures.
