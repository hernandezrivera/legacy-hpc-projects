Project Summary

Project Summary:
The intervention aims to provide wide-ranging support for household and community-based agricultural, livestock, and livelihood assistance in linkage with the value chains during 2024. The project will target the most affected people in NWS and engage them to effectively attempt to be self-reliant in the best possible way. The intervention plan anticipates that this will lead the families and their youth to think constructively and be self-reliant in the medium to long term. It will target all populations of IDPs, returnees, and host communities and take into consideration the most vulnerable people such as the women-headed families, elderly-headed families, and child-headed families. Moreover, the project will take into consideration the PWD. The project is designed to be implemented over 2024 to increase the self-reliance for both HH and communities through:
1. Distributing of agricultural inputs for wheat farmers (seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, irrigation cost, harvest cost) coupled with related training, good production practices for 1000 HH of wheat farmers during 2024. the selection criteria are: Technical/Eligibility criteria: 1) access to land maximum 4 hectares 2) has an irrigation source. Vulnerability: 1) Household is headed by a woman or/and elderly or/and child 2) At least 2 children less than 5 years old 3) family has people with Disability. 4) family has people has orphans 5) Big family - more than 5 children less than 18 years old.
2. QC will establish 4 livestock mobile clinics for 4 months in northern Aleppo and Idleb. The livestock mobile clinics will provide health and re-productive services for cows, sheep, and goats, together with the provision of guidance and consultation to local herders and breeders.
3. Provide health care services and vaccination for 10,000 animals in 2024.
4. Establishing one Cooperative Agricultural Mechanization Unit (CAMU), and supporting it with the necessary machines in Mare’. QC will establish a cooperative agricultural mechanization unit in collaboration with the Directorate of Agriculture in Mare'. This unit will be equipped with essential machinery and equipment, including a tractor, seeder, Disc plow, Moldboard plow, Ripper tiller cultivator, fertilizer spreader, Pest Control Spray Machine, and a hay pressing and packing machine), complete with all the required accessories.
5. Strengthen the local communities’ capacity for supporting the local population through rehabilitation of the second phase of the value chain on infrastructures (bakeries, flour mills, silos for storage, in addition to the irrigation channels), in addition to establishing early warning and DRR systems.
6.Establishing 2 workshops to manufacture the green wheat (Frekah/Syrian food) per workshop 40 workers by providing them with the opportunity to short-term work through cash for work activities in Mare’.
7. provision soil testing lab in northern Aleppo: QC will set up a soil analysis laboratory, as complementary approach to building resilience within wheat and vegetables value chains program.
8. Support wheat marketing through purchasing the wheat from the farmers. QC seeks to purchase 6350 MT form 1500 farmers during 2024. The total beneficiaries to be reached by the project over 2024 over 12 months are 162,445 The total budget for this project is 4,902,411 USD 60 % of the budget is secured.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The Protection Risk Analysis for the project implementation in NW Syria highlights potential risks and corresponding mitigation strategies. These include addressing sexual exploitation through establishing a complaint mechanism, training staff, and recruiting and training female workers. Violence during distributions is mitigated by selecting suitable sites and times, setting up multiple distribution points, and collaborating with partners. To prevent aid diversion, coordination meetings with community leaders and signing MOUs with local authorities are planned. Overspending on non-food items is addressed by providing vouchers or e-cards instead of cash and monitoring vendors.Regarding the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) strategy, compliance with QC PSEA policy and conducting awareness sessions for gatekeepers and beneficiaries are emphasized, with a focus on addressing complaints through the MEAL department.The Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures section recognizes stable security situations but highlights potential challenges such as increased demand due to operations in East Euphrates, delayed deliveries from border closures, and the risk of new conflicts in Idleb. Mitigation involves close coordination with local councils, advocating through the FSL cluster to address food demand gaps, and maintaining communication with the Turkish government for aid delivery. Continuous monitoring of security situations and updating safety plans by QC's safety and security officer are emphasized to manage unforeseen conflicts effectively.
