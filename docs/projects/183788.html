Project Summary

The multi-sectoral refugee response is targeting two geographic areas: (1) vulnerable South Sudanese refugees, both protracted and new arrivals, in West and South Kordofan States, and (2) refugees fleeing from Ethiopia in Gedaref States. This intervention links with and responds to the 2022 Refugee Response Strategy under the HRP to strengthen the protection environment for refugees, to enable access to timely protection and lifesaving assistance as well as to provide equitable access to basic services for refugees and strengthening opportunities for resilience and self-reliance.
The refugee response will provide support for 48,500 individuals. 39,967 in both South/West Kordofan are targeted across 9 localities. As part of the Tigray response we will target 8,533 vulnerable refugees in Gedaref state.
Concern’s intervention will provide integrated health, nutrition, WASH, and FSL/livelihoods services for vulnerable refugees in 1) host community settings in Kordofan through health/nutrition facilities and their catchment areas, and 2) Um Rakuba refugee camp setting and nearby catchment population in Gedaref state.
Under the health sector, the project will deliver primary health care services through medical consultations and treatment, reproductive health and outpatient services for refugee children, PLW and family members.
Under nutrition sector, through an integration with the health services in the catchment of the health intervention geographic locations, the program will respond to severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) treatment for refugee children under 5 years of age, and PLW through CMAM approach.
The proposed plan is integrating WASH services in the same catchment locations by renovating water facilities and constructing water points in health/ nutrition centers, and strengthen hygiene promotions. The project will also provide soap and sanitary kits for refugee women to improve personal hygiene for vulnerable refugees and emphases on COVID-19 sensitive WASH programming.
South Sudanese refugees in West and South Kordofan state will be provided FSL services such as cash programming and cross cutting environment protection support.

1 - Needs

The 2022 HNO estimates over 13.4 million people in need for humanitarian assistance, out of which 1 million people in need are refugees. The growing tensions in the Tigray region could lead once again to large scale displacement with large movements across the border into East Sudan. According to the latest September 2021 UNHCR data on Refugees and Asylum-seekers per Country of Origin, 786,524 come from South Sudan, and 70,246 from Ethiopia. Life-saving health, nutrition, WASH services are limited for these people, and particularly in Kordofan often time the lack of services with an increased number of people brings additional burden to the local communities to deliver services. Refugees and asylum seekers face the brunt of the current economic hardship of Sudan (423% devaluation of currency as of July 2021), sharing with local communities shortage of fuel, bread and water/electricity. Consultations with refugee beneficiaries during 2021 projects implementation in the targeted geographic locations confirm the increased need of life-saving services (PHC services, nutrition for CU5, potable water and sanitation services in both facility and community level, and food security) to a different degree but leading to an equal amount of sufferance and increasing vulnerability. Health: the currently running health facilities lack minimum PHC services for both refugees and host families and the state MoH has extremely limited resources (both human and financial resources) to run and further develop the existing services. In West Kordofan particularly Concern is operating in Meriam, Kharasana locations where the majority of the refugees settled within the host communities. In South Kordofan Leri, Talodi and Gadeer, apart from Kadugli are the focused areas of response. All these locations have limited PHC services (weak referral services, medicines, few medical expertise etc.) that requires Concern to continue PHC services. Nutrition: according to Concern's SMART survey conducted in West and South Kordofan operation areas (15 facilities) in 2021 indicates critical need for nutrition intervention e.g. GAM 17.4% in SK, 19.2% WK, SAM 3.8% in SK, 5.1 in WK, Child Min. Diet Diversity 60% in SK, 58% in WK. The Coverage Survey also indicated that only 49% of OTP services available, TSFP coverage is 41%. In Um Rakoba camp, Gadarif the situation is similar: the local government have no resources to support refugee services and completely rely on the support of NGOs. Concern is operating in Zone 1 and covering the needs for approx. 10,000 refugees. WASH: the health facility assessment in WK/SK that Concern conducted, including institution coverage, only 15.7% average score across the surveyed 14 health facilities capacities domain. Lack of potable water, latrines, incinerators, hygiene practices undermine the PHC minimum requirements. FSL: In 2021, the Food Security & Livelihoods Cluster estimated 9.8 persons are in need ofhumanitarian assistance, that forms more than 20% of the population of Sudan. The IPC Phase 3 worsened in North Kordofan (20%), South Kordofan (20%) and Gedarif (19%). The main areas identified for intervention for refugees are agricultural inputs support such as drought-tolerant seeds and farm tools, livestock support with adequate training and capacity building how to manage the given resources in a sustainable way, considering the climate change and resilience driven needs, particularly in the W/SKordofan where refugees live within the host communities and they have access to small plots for cultivation. Therefore the FSL intervention is focusing only in West and South Kordofan states.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. Health/nutrition: However, it is acknowledged that health and nutrition services that the project seeks to support can have negative impacts on the environment, especially in camp settings. Poor medical waste management might pose health risks. The project will ensure proper training and awareness raising for the health workers on environmental protection particularly related to proper waste management (segregation, storage and incinerating medical waste). The project will also provide basic facilities to support appropriate segregation and disposal of medical waste according to best practices. As per any rehabilitation planned, the use of locally available materials will be suggested to reduce the environmental footprint and increase cultural sensitivity. During the renovation of facilities, Concern will ensure that renovation debris are properly handled during and after the renovation. WASH: similar to the health/nutrition component, renovation work will be done with careful handling of construction debris. FSL: the distribution of drought tolerance seeds will contribute to the climate change driven needs to use significant water, capacity building training for refugees e.g. Climate Smart Agriculture (Concern signature training) will provide knowledge and improved practices on how to adopt to the new climate while cultivating the plots/land, particularly in Kordofan.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behavior change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where refugees settled, there will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.
