Project Summary

The project aims to provide the humanitarian community with regularly updated baseline on the presence of IDPs and returnees across the country complemented with a comprehensive facility and service mapping in areas of high return as to ensure informed planning and response.

1 - Needs

Despite the optimistic outlook encouraged by the increase in the number of returns in the months following the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in late 2018, the first half of 2019 has been marked with an increase in the average monthly number of IDPs who arrived at assessed locations. According to available DTM data, in June 2019 there were still 1,465,542 IDPs, of which some 169,979 arrived at locations of assessments in 2019. During the assessment, DTM mapped 1,271,487 returnees, almost a quarter of which returned to their areas of habitual residence in 2019. This snapshot illustrates the complexity of the current situation in South Sudan that requires a comprehensive analysis and data collection effort. Continued primary data collection on multi-sectoral needs, displacement and returns, intentions and perceptions, is thus essential. Reliable and timely information remains essential for the continued monitoring of new displacement, but also, for the creation of environments conducive to returns. Since 2017, DTM serves as an inter-cluster tool that provides actionable and relevant information to the humanitarian community and Government authorities. With a cross-country coverage, at location level, it figures as a common service to the humanitarian community. Together with other partners, DTM aims to respond to various data needs. The value of DTM methodology has been recognized by the leading UN coordination body, OCHA, and it resulted in a joint, updated baseline (at location level) of South Sudan’s IDP population. Combining data collection on stocks for pre-defined population categories (IDPs and returnees) and multi-sectoral village, neighborhood and site level assessments, DTM builds a holistic picture of the population estimates and needs on the ground. Internal and cross border movements due to different reasons are one of the factors shaping the overall population mobility in South Sudan. Dynamic, reasons and durations of intended migratory patterns are captured through flow monitoring registry surveys. So far, DTM hosts a unique database of few dozen thousands of surveys with South Sudanese nationals (interviews with approx. 70,000 individuals / month on group or household level), but also nationals from neighboring countries who cross the borders. Temporary or long-term movements to and from PoC sites and reasons thereof are important elements to indicate people’s intentions and needs regarding the conditions within displacement sites and those arising from intentions to move out of the Protection of Civilians sites. To reflect on these and movement trends, DTM is implementing Displacement Site Flow Monitoring (DSFM) in Bentiu, Wau and Malakal PoC sites as well as five collective centres in Wau. Understanding displacement site migration dynamics is key for strategic planning on durable solutions and to identify possibilities of safe returns. Since August 2018, DTM is supporting the provision of food assistance through biometric data management. Through regular authentication and verification of registration records, the systems ensure that assistance is provided in a targeted and accountable manner. Long years of war have taken their toll on the availability of services and infrastructure. In support of humanitarian, transition and recovery efforts and to facilitate evidence-based programming through efficient resource allocation, DTM has launched the Village Assessment Survey designed for Facility Infrastructure and Service (FIS) mapping. Collected information will provide the humanitarian community, partners and relevant government authorities with georeferenced information on the status and location of facilities (including education and health) along with information on the quality and current availability of basic services.

2 - Activities

In response to data and information needs in South Sudan, DTM proposes a continuation / scaling up of the following activities: Mobility Tracking, Flow Monitoring (including Displacement Site Flow Monitoring),Facility Infrastructure Mapping and Biometric Registration, providing the maintenance of the existing team structure composed of Juba-based team of data, analysis, research and reporting experts, as well as operations staff in the capital, sub-offices and focal points across the country. Mobility Tracking: location level (village / site / neighborhood) data collection of stock figures on the presence of population categories (IDPs, returnees) combined with the multi sectoral assessments. Since mid-2019, DTM has established a full country coverage, reaching a total of 2,312 locations in 470 payams of all 78 counties across South Sudan. Building up on the existing network of focal points, including 88 county and state RRC focal points and 863 enumerators across the country, DTM will continue providing the humanitarian community with updated figures on IDPs and returnees, aiming to adjust the methodology to be able to capture population changes at location level. This exercise requires more frequent baseline assessments (monthly) and upgrade of the existing model.Flow Monitoring activities in points of transit and entry / exit to / from the country, and specific surveys at main displacement sites, will provide a comprehensive picture on internal and international human mobility corridors, enabling partners to understand mobility dynamics. In the upcoming period, DTM is committed to undertake a stock-taking exercise and capitalize on the plethora of available data and information collected through flow monitoring so far in order to provide partners with an in-depth analysis of the mobility patterns.Facility Infrastructure Mapping (FIS) is a specific model of the Village Assessment Survey designed to generate information on gaps in services and infrastructure to guide transition and recovery programming enabling sustained return and integration. Information collected will be proactively shared with displaced communities to help address the information gap often reported by beneficiaries with regards to knowledge about the situation in their area of habitual residence prior to displacement.IOM’s biometric registration system has been active since 2014, with more than 860,000 beneficiaries active in its database at the end of the third quarter of 2019. DTM has been assisting partners in targeting affected populations and ensuring accountable distributions through beneficiary identification, authentication at food distribution and biometric maintenance activities.

Components such as Facility Infrastructure Mapping and Mobility Tracking provide detailed geographical information about assessed areas. They are implemented in close cooperation with authorities. In a longer term, the project aims to enhance the relationship with relevant authorities, and communities, and explore possibilities to utilize the data for future use of South Sudanese authorities.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

DTM does not directly provide humanitarian services to South Sudanese populations in need. Direct beneficiaries remain relief organizations and other data users rather than individuals. DTM pro-actively involves members of marginalized communities when conducting registration activities as well as youth and women and girls when conducting assessments in cooperation with service-providers such as WFP. The risk of these being left out is low and the impact would mean either a lack of registration or not being represented in the evidence base for various programming. A satisfaction survey conducted in June 2019 suggested that vulnerabilities are catered to during biometric authentication exercises at displacement sites and host community settings. Personal data gathered from biometric registration is stored and handled according to IOM’s data protection principles.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DTM engages community representatives of vulnerable groups when conducting assessments on group level and disaggregates household / individual level data collection by sex, age and vulnerability criteria. DTM furthermore pro-actively engages in data collection on selected vulnerable populations such as on disabilities in PoC sites in cooperation with Humanity and Inclusion. Interviews with key informants and direct observation are two main methods for data collection in DTM. While doing so, field teams composed of PSEA-trained enumerators engage with local communities to gather best estimates on the populations living on site during the assessment, including a demographic breakdown, vulnerabilities (pregnant, lactating women, etc.) and their needs. Further on, information pertinent to the needs of the most vulnerable members will be also captured through facility infrastructure mapping which gathers information on the status of infrastructure and quality / availability of services at location level (distances from boreholes, access to education, medical facilities, availability of medical services, etc.) and their accessibility.
