Project Summary

Food insecurity continues to adversely affect South Sudan with 7.10 million people, including refugees projected to experience severe food insecurity in South Sudan in 2024 during the peak hunger season. Comparing with the year 2023 there is not much of an in improvement improvement of the food security situation which remains worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. The protracted conflict and steady economic decline have eroded the limited provision of public services and exacerbated individual vulnerabilities. South Sudan faces several social challenges as it attempts to navigate a revitalized peace agreement and a transition leading to elections in 2024. The food insecurity situation in Unity, Ruweng Administrative Area and Upper Nile state remains poor. Localized tensions compelled by Intra and inter community clashes, revenge killings, cattle rustling, burglary, theft continue to pose a great threat towards community livelihoods. Targeted individuals as a result of revenge killings are threatened to pursue livelihoods and economic opportunities and hence continue to remain vulnerable. To address the flood & conflict induced acute food insecurity situation as well as livelihood need of most vulnerable community, DRC intends to achieve following results –
1. Food Assistance: Address immediate household food security needs through provision of food assistance and prevention of malnutrition initiatives.
2. Agriculture and Food Security: Flood and conflict affected Agro-pastoral IDP & Host communities’ food security and livelihoods are protected and restored through the provision of emergency and early recovery and flood adaptive agriculture and fisheries practice for most vulnerable households
3. Economic Recovery and Market System: To ensure improved access to income & livelihood opportunities through Cash / Market based Livelihood interventions vulnerable Households.
DRC's vision is to provide a dignified life for all people affected by displacement. As such, DRC Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) activities are sectorial inter-linked with Protection (including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and Peace building activities. DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming in all the project activities and assessment tools will also be adopted and specific criteria for inclusion will be developed to priorities the most vulnerable households in the targeted communities. DRC will target mainly Rubkona, Guit, Malakal Town, Malakal PoC, Kodok, Ajoung Thok, Jamjang and Pamir Counties and across South Sudan through mobile responses to address acute emergency needs. DRC’s programme will work torwards enhancing social cohesion through support to vulnerable households to create, rehabilitate and protect durable community assets in with a specific focus on women and youth as key agents of change. Additionally DRC will focus on ensuring conflict sensitivity and mainstream peace building activities to ensure addressing the some of the key drivers of food and nutrition insecurity linked to conflict and displacement. Access to livelihood opportunities is also affected by social cohesion which needs to be addressed prior to implementation of food security and livelihoods interventions. DRC will work towards improving the protective environment through mainstreaming protection and GBV issues across programming. DRC conducts regular Protection monitoring to inform programming and screen protection cases in all programming and ensure protection concerns are addressed and integrated into the planned action.

1. Needs

South Sudan is currently experiencing significant levels of human suffering, exacerbated by the unexpected influx of individuals fleeing conflict in Sudan. This has led to increased food insecurity, widespread violations of human rights, and hindered the ability of individuals to recover from shocks. The high levels of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain worrying, with the most severely acute food insecure populations continue to be significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. Latest IPC findings show that 7.10 million people which is 56% of the population surveyed people are projected to be facing high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between April and July 2024. Furthermore, 5.78 million are projected to face similar high levels of food insecurity between December 2023 and March 2024. The influx of returnees and refugees into South Sudan following the outbreak of conflict in Sudan on 15th April 2023 further compounded the already dire humanitarian situation straining resources available for food assistance. The continued fighting and influx meant the resources available remained stretched increasing humanitarian needs requiring additional funding to meet critical food and nutrition needs for the South Sudanese returnees and newly arrived refugees from May to October 2023.Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.16 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM). 870,000 pregnant or breastfeeding women are expected to suffer acute malnutrition in this period. An estimated 72 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. DRC conducted gendered conflict analysis has also shown that though some displacement in the target locations was initially caused by large scale violence, respondents indicated that more recent insecurities have caused movement restrictions, forcing people to remain close to their places of residence. This also prevents people from engaging in livelihoods activities, accessing services, as well as prevents displaced populations from returning to their areas of origin. The analysis further shows that male youth and men who have lost their properties and livestock due to conflict, has limited their ability to engage in traditional livelihoods activities. Low levels of education, limited marketable skills, and the lack of capital has made it difficult for these groups to get employment or start businesses. Currently, some men are engaged in other livelihoods activities such as burning and selling of charcoal. Some of the conflict-affected unemployed youths in the target locations have resorted to collecting items in the dumpsite to survive, while some are now part of gangs, reportedly engaging in criminality and causing insecurity. Women in many cases have become the primary breadwinners for their families, on top of their traditional domestic and child-care duties. Some women have started small-scale businesses in the target locations town. They sell tea and rent shops or land to sell assorted groceries. Premises are made of plastic sheeting provided by non-governmental organizations (NGOs). Women with less economic capacity – especially young women - collect firewood to sell. They are at risk of sexual harassment when moving through dangerous areas to do so .

2. Project activities

Under this project, DRC proposes to implement following activities; CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & some 3) Under partnership with WFP, DRC will provide Food for Assets will improve food security while improving community infrastructure such as flood dykes and swallow wells to channel the water for irrigation. These assets would be identified by communities based on their needs and provision of food assistance will also increase the food production and access to diversified food. DRC also anticipates additional funding that will cover Multi-purpose cash that will complement to meeting the above mentioned objective. DRC is aware that the complex conflict dynamics in South Sudan have the potential to both negatively impact or be negatively impacted by humanitarian activities. Proposed activities are designed in accordance with protection and Do No Harm principles and will follow a gendered conflict sensitive approach that actively seeks opportunities to promote social cohesion through decision-making processes. The conflict sensitivity and social cohesion component of this project will focus on strengthening community social cohesion through supporting local capacities and provision of durable community assets. A participatory conflict analysis shall be conducted and developed at the beginning of the implementation to ensure that conflict sensitivity is taken into consideration throughout the project cycle. DRC will work with community-based structures we have already established, on conflict analysis, training on conflict management and peacebuilding, and community dialogues. CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support; DRC will provide the emergency access to agricultural inputs to most vulnerable to increase the food production in Unity, Upper Nile and Ruweng Adminiustrative Area. Households will be prioritized who rely on the agriculture, have lost the agriculture tools due to the displacement and or don’t have self -capacity to buy the seeds and inputs. DRC will also provide the training on best agronomic practices to increase the food production, reduce the post -harvest damages and building on seed multiplication approaches. The intervention will ultimately increase Households self-reliance capacity and will reduce the dependency on humanitarian assistance in coming time. DRC will also encompass efforts to increase the local seeds demand in the respective counties to develop the market systems. DRC will also use the Cash & Voucher assistance through seed vouchers to enable the local procurements of seeds. Both crop and vegetable seeds would be provided through seed vouchers so that beneficiaries can buy seeds on their preferences. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks and & contextual changes; DRC will continue working with local agriculture extension workers to extend the services on promoting climate smart agriculture practices to increase the food production, promote local seeds and seed multiplications approaches, reduce post-harvest losses and increased crop diversification. As mentioned above in objective 02, DRC will promote to local procurement of seeds and support the seed fairs through seed vouchers enabling communities to buy their preferred seeds for both vegetables and crops.
