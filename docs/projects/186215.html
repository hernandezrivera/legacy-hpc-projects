Project Summary

For more than ten years, International Medical Corps (IMC), has been implementing GBV internvetions in the East, Adamawa and the North regions, specifically in the refugee sites of Ngam, Borgop, Gado-Badzere, Lolo, Mbile and Timangolo and in the off-site areas of Djohong, Mbai-Mboum, Meiganga, Garoua-Boulai, Mandjou, Kette, Gbiti, Kentzou and Yokadouma. Through these services, IMC has registered nearly 500 cases of GBV from 2017 to 2021, including all types of violence, such as rape, sexual assault, physical assault, forced marriage, denial of resources, opportunities and services and psychological violence. Survivors of GBV, children in need of protection and Persons with Specific Needs have benefited from various services provided by IMC, including psychosocial, medical, security and legal support. The creation and training of community-based structures such as women's committees, husband's school and gender clubs in 2018 allowed IMC to facilitate community participation in the fight against GBV through community awareness-raising on GBV issues and its consequences, available services and the importance of reporting perpetrators.
Despite the results obtained, women's participation in community self-management structures remains low. In the past years, communities have become more tolerant to having women as leaders. However, refugee women, because of their status, still face major difficulties in integrating into local associations in host communities. In addition to social and cultural barriers which are in large part the origin of protection risks, the drastic reduction in resources is serious problem faced by refugees in terms of their protection and empowerment. Due to the persistence of gender-based discrimination, both refugee and host community women who are victims of violence, are more likely to have physical, mental and reproductive health problems and, remain at a high risk of GBV. There is therefore a need to strengthen community participation in interventions related to protection, in order to improve access to various services including protection services, and to strengthen the coordination of actions in order to improve advocacy and support for persons of concern.
IMC UK has a strong capacity to raise funds from other donors to complement UNHCR funds, has gained acceptance from all stakeholders, including other implementing partners, representatives of line ministries, administrative authorities, and has a strong knowledge of the context. IMC UK has extensive experience in implementing activities in Cameroon in general and the Far North in particular. Similarly, IMC UK has gained the trust of the population benefiting from its services provided in all intervention sites. IMC UK has been present in the Eastern region since 2008, and in the Far North since 2010, and has the capacity to respond quickly and in a timely manner to sudden humanitarian emergencies, including IMC UK’s response to the Goura humanitarian crisis following the massive influx of Nigerian asylum seekers fleeing attacks of violence in Rann, Nigeria in February 2019 and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and 2021. With the support of its donors, such as ECHO, BPRM, START, AFENET, CDC, BHA, UNFPA and UNHCR, IMC UK is currently implementing health, nutrition and protection services in five regions: Far North, East, Adamaoua, North and North West.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Within the complex and dynamic context of massive movements of refugee populations fleeing violence in the Central African Republic (CAR) and the resurgence of COVID-19, there are currently more than 272,000 CAR refugees living in the Eastern, Adamawa and Northern regions, with more the 6,000 new refugees arriving in the Garoua-Boulaï area in December 2020 following the presidential elections in CAR. The situation has greatly weakened the already precarious socio-economic atmosphere in this region and at a time when humanitarian assistance is being drastically reduced. This situation negatively affects the whole protection system of the population of concern, including CAR refugees and vulnerable host communities. In the East, Adamawa and North regions, sexual violence and exploitation, especially against young girls, is a major risk and issue. Early and forced marriages are also a major problem, with 68% of girls in CAR married before the age of 18 and 29% before the age of 15. At the time when child protection stakeholders developed the strategy for the protection of Central African refugee children in the targeted regions for the 2018-2020 period, there were 590 children identified as survivors of forced and early marriages. In addition, women and girls are exposed to sexual violence when gathering firewood and water to meet their needs. Child labour in mining sites exposes children to abuse and survival sexual remains a major risk for girls. With regards to community engagement, participation of the population of concern and the inclusion of women, men, girls and boys in the humanitarian program cycle remains a weakness despite past interventions in community mobilization. There are limited opportunities for the crisis-affected population to seek durable solutions to their problems and to improve their self-reliance. The project will focus on improving community-based protection mechanisms for Central African refugees and affected populations in the East, Adamawa and North regions, by to strengthening the commitment of the various stakeholders (community members, local organizations, municipalities and government services) in prevention and response services. These structures will be empowered to facilitate the prevention and response to GBV and the empowerment of survivors through a harmonized and concerted strategy with line ministries and the community. In a short term, this will lead to the establishment of strong community protection mechanisms including Local Protection Committees (supervised and supported by the municipalities) that promote and ensure the protection of vulnerable people and their fundamental rights to education, health, nutrition, shelter and other services. As far as the management of GBV survivors is concerned, IMC UK will focus on improving the skills of CSOs/CBOs to prevent GBV and progressively build their capacity in case management and protection services so that, at the end of the five-year project, they will be able to effectively provide psychosocial care to GBV survivors. In addition, in order to improve the coordination of protection services for GBV, Furthermore, IMC UK will support the mapping of actors involved in the fight against GBV to increase knowledge of and access to available services under the leadership of MINPROFF and the mayors. Local organizations, decentralized state services and municipalities will be supported to jointly identify and seek sustainable solutions to the problems faced by vulnerable people and with their full participation. IMC UK will also strengthen the capacities of the government structures, namely MINAS, MINSANTE and MINPROFF and take into account the protection of people with specific needs to in empowerment activities empowerment and activities focusing on reducing the risks of violence, exploitation and sexual abuse.
