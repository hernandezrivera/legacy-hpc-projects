Project Summary

Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI), a national NGO proposes actions that will target 60,000 most vulnerable beneficiaries comprised of 10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 boys and 20000 girls. Targeted county/ beneficiaries continue to face massive flooding and cyclical waves of cattle raiding, inter/ intra communal skirmishes and subsequent multiple displacements, compounded by multiple needs, unprecedented food insecurity levels, high malnutrition levels (GAM prevalence of 20.7% and SAM prevalence 4.9%), protection concerns including gender based violence and abuse, inadequate health and water, sanitation and hygiene.
The project has been designed to respond to the 2020 HRP WASH Cluster strategy and prioritization criteria. This will focus on scaling up efforts to respond to acute WASH needs, targeting most vulnerable and address high levels of malnutrition through WASH and Nutrition integrated programming for famine prevention starting from the OTP centres, targeting mother/ caretaker-malnourished children, responding to livestock open defecation affecting children in addition to comprehensive water point management.
Proposed project will ensure protection mainstreaming via regular assessments which shall be conducted to mitigate GBV risks including sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring involvement of people living with disabilities, ensuring activities ensure Do No Harm, including linkages with GBV actors, Nutrition actors—JAM and Real Medicine Foundation; in addition to ensuring environmental protection.; ensuring Accountability to Affected Populations is mainstreamed throughout the intervention to guarantee maximum and meaningful participation of the affected men, women, girls and boys.
SPOCI has been operational in Pibor since 2015 and currently implementing UNICEF funded actions
that will sustain current programming momentum and scale up activities in 2020. SPOCI is a member of State level Cholera Preparedness Task Force, National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG, member of Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group and WASH Cluster Accountability to Affected Populations Technical Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has for the past three years supported over 72,000 vulnerable populations with WASH/Education interventions through SSHF, UNICEF among other partners support in Pibor. Through these, the organization has fostered very strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries of water and irrigation and counterpart agencies especially those it integrates Nutrition/ GBV/ Public health focus in previous and current programming. This is in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.

1 - Needs

Pibor is among other counties which are currently experiencing massive flooding and recent waves of active conflict which started in April, 2019, compounded by multiple needs, unprecedented food insecurity levels, high malnutrition levels, protection concerns including gender based violence and abuse, inadequate health and water, sanitation and hygiene. The county is among others in Jonglei which has the highest proportion of food insecure households (73 percent in IPC 3 or worse); contributing to the latest September 2019 IPC Update which projects 61.1% of the populations in Jonglei will be facing IPC Phase 3 or worse food insecurity with IPC AMN Phase 3 rating between January and April 2020 and projected to deteriorate further. Recent IMC SMART Survey Report in Pibor County indicated GAM prevalence of 20.7% and SAM prevalence 4.9% based on WHZ and the presence of bilateral oedema. Poor access to safe water, hygiene, sanitation, child healthcare and feeding practices in Pibor are likely to further aggravate high malnutrition levels (IPC June 2019 Update). SPOCI’s anecdotal evidence of current flooding which have affected over 5,000 people in Pibor and over 20 boreholes submerged under water, including over 33 latrines facilities further exacerbate vulnerable populations access to WASH. Recent Interagency assessments in Pibor indicate deficient key water supply infrastructure due to both the lack of maintenance/damage sustained during the recent months of conflict, compounded by current flooding in Pibor which has disrupted livelihoods and consequent displacements. Open defecation is widespread and risks exposure to water borne diseases is high; communities have reported an increase in cases of diarrhea and Acute Watery Diarrhoea. Coupled with poor sanitation coverage including schools and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation (IRNA Report, 2019; SPOCI Situation Reports, Pibor Oct 2019). Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) is currently implementing a UNICEF funded action in Pibor and proposes to scale up emergency WASH and Nutrition integrated programming for famine prevention starting from the OTP centres, targeting mother/ caretaker-malnourished children, responding to livestock open defecation affecting children in addition to comprehensive water point management. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 5 years programmatic presence in Pibor and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs. Activities will ensure integrated programming with WASH, Nutrition, Education, Health, Protection etc to address the high level of food insecurity and malnutrition, coordinate with RRC among other partners. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Targeted beneficiaries, men, women, girls and boys, who include people with special needs and the elderly, will be supported whilst taking into account of their rights and considerations on how each of these groups are affected by conflict and ensuring the project respects their rights and dignities. SPOCI will in addition involve key stakeholder groups and beneficiary representatives to ensure accountability and beneficiary voices are heard and incorporated to inform high quality programming/ response.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

SPOCI’s consultative and feedback approach will ensure targeted beneficiaries, mainly displaced people, their views and perspectives on implementation are taken into consideration to inform an two-way appropriate response. These will as well be registered and targeted to benefit from the various support packages during implementation.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

SPOCI’s project is linked to the government line ministry priorities in supporting the line ministry of Water and Irrigation and in line with the Global Action Plan for WASH. Key community leadership structures and RRC will in addition engaged and consulted including working hand in hand with line ministries, WUCs, hygiene/ nutrition volunteers and promoters. SPOCI will hence prioritize these interventions by closely working with line ministries, local stakeholders and beneficiaries including capacity building initiatives that will ensure active participation and sustainability.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project is aligned with the 2020 HRP WASH Strategy through WASH and Nutrition integrated programming for famine prevention starting from the OTP centres, targeting mother/ caretaker-malnourished children, responding to livestock open defecation affecting children in addition to comprehensive water point management. SPOCI’s proposed projects will work and preposition NFIs through Cluster/ IOM Pipeline. In addition, inter-cluster synergies will be explored in targeted by close working with partners in nutrition, health, FSL, Protection. These different categories will be consulted and involved in implementation of the project including monitoring and evaluation; while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Pibor is fragile with an unstable context that is likely to warrant insecurity and instability that could negatively impact the project. However, SPOCI has a dedicated Monitoring and Evaluation department that has in the past and will in the proposed project; carry our regular analyses, thorough analysis of conflict dynamics and risks with appropriate mitigation measures via Do No Harm principles to assess the necessity and feasibility of implementation without causing further harm among target beneficiaries.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

SPOCI’s sustainability and handover strategy will involve strengthening service delivery through ministry of water and irrigation extension workers in the targeted counties/ payams/ Bomas in addition to working with hygiene/ nutrition volunteers. These will be supported and trained during implementation to take over activities, with strengthened line ministry linkages and local leadership structures to support implementation, even upon project closure.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will mitigate key risks and protection concerns including congregation during implementation, by prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’ including advocacy and prevention of Gender Based Violence and Sexual Exploitation and abuse. SPOCI will ensure non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men. These will also in addition liaise with protection partners including putting in place appropriate referrals. SPOCI will focus on mainstreaming and also fully engage with the community among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities including advocacy on retrogressive cultural practices such as cattle raiding, abduction of children/ women and GBV; and coordinating with Mine Action Group in activities near/ environs of suspect mines/ UXOs/ ordinances.Project beneficiaries will be identified as host/ IDP/ returnee communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided. Household heads providing care to children below 5 years, and hosting pregnant and lactating mothers, the disabled and the elderly will specifically be identified as primary beneficiaries of inputs to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

SPOCI will ensure a two way communication and information sharing with all relevant groups and stakeholders. These will include men, women, girls, boys, youth, people with special needs and the elderly. These will be involved across all stages of planning, consultation, monitoring, implementation. Activities will be conducted in presence of beneficiaries trustees such as local leaders, RRC officials including various youth/ women groups. Areas of implementation and distribution lists will be shared and endorsed by community representatives; in coordination with counterpart agencies. Interventions will be done timely including during safe periods to avoid risks posed during late hours. Active two way feedback mechanisms will be employed including sharing staff contacts and beneficiary feedback desks at various locations in the targeted counties. Conflict sensitive programming will be ensured to ensure do no harm ethos.
