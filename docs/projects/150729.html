Project Summary

This initiative is a part of the project which is planned to be implemented by a consortium consisting of partner organisations Dorcas and SOS CV UA and possible participation of other implementing partners. Overall objective: To provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for the most vulnerable people affected by the humanitarian crisis in Luhansk and Donetsk regions of Ukraine (along the contact line and on NGCA). SOS CV UA works in the Luhansk region, focusing on child protection, with complementary provision of non-food items to the vulnerable families and wide range of PSS for children and adults. The main aim is to mitigate the impact of conflict on the vulnerable families with children living along the contact line and in Luhansk, NGCA and ensure that children are protected in family environment.

Needs

MAIN HUMANITARIAN NEEDS OF SOS TARGET AUDIENCE IN LUHANSK REGION1. Protection needs. Access to life-saving information and services, improvement in referrals and multi-sectorial coordination remain a key protection need. Needs in child protection are increasing. Nearly a sixth of school and kindergarten interviewees to UNICEF mentioned without direct prompting that many parents have paid much less attention to their children as a result of the stress of living in conflict and poverty. With low resilience among confict-affected people, parents/caregivers are overstretched and are increasingly adopting negative coping mechanisms such as alcohol abuse, as our state partners are reporting, which is leading to an increase in domestic violence and child neglect. Among the most burning needs are the needs in psycho-social support (PSS), especially along the contact line, with such age and gender differences: children and elderly people reportedly have more problems with night sleep and deterioration of psysiological functions, women are more often express psychosomatic reactions; more problems with aggressiveness in boys and men. Women are more willing to get help. Another problematic category in need of PSS is teenagers, both girls and boys, including increased number of suicide attempts. 2. Needs in NFIs. The conflict significantly affected provision of families with basic children's goods in districts along the contact line and on NGCA after outflow of suppliers and delivery limitations due to safety risks. The general impoverishment of the affected population, limited selection of goods and high prices on children goods have forced some parents to stop purchasing clothing and footwear, diapers, special hygiene products for children.3. Physical health needs increased due to the lack of quality secondary medical service and as a result of stress/PTSD, sever anxieties, psychosomatic disorders, decreased immunity, and cannot be treated well due to lack of finance. According to HNO, access to health for one third of households in the conflict area is hampered, in an environment where every second family reports having one or more family members suffering from a chronic illness. The biggest family health expenditures are usually purchasing of medicines, and the prices are continuously rising. From the social point of view, needs assessment has shown that one of the most vulnerable categories still are families with children under 3 living along the contact line. In case of post-delivery and breast-feeding connected complications there are no qualified medical specialists (gynecologists, mammologists, etc.), as well as psychologists or therapists to help in case of post-delivery depression. The need in PSS is bigger for parents with a baby under 1 and a pregnant women. These difficulties can result in deterioration of children’s and mothers’ health, even with a risk for life, severe post-delivery depression of mothers, early rejection from breast feeding (majority of mothers - according to observations of local doctors), hypothermia, malnutrition and underweight of children, their distressed psychological state, delays in child development.If a vulnerable family fails to satisfy the described needs, child protection risks increase: neglect and domestic violence against children leads to families breaking down, with parents sending their children to grandparents or other relatives/non-relatives (according to REACH assessment 19% of households reported caring for unrelated minors) or leaving them to be cared for by care institutions. Unfortunately, state services organizations cannot provide sufficient response due to a lack of financial and human resources. More children receive the status of suffered from the war, but no state services are available to help them. Local social workers, teachers, medical staff are lacking the knowledge and skills to work with children and families experienced war trauma.

Activities & outcomes

For the next year, SOS CV UA plans to provide comprehensive psycho-social support and emergency relief on the basis of community centers in Stanitsa Luhanska and Luhansk at least to 600 children (girls and boys), 220 adults (women and men), and at least 300 households. These children and families will receive comprehensible PSS, protection information (including on prevention of GBV), NFIs according to their individual needs. Work of mobile team on NGCA will enable provision of psycho-social support and protection information to at least to 3600 children (1800 girls and 1800 boys), and 1400 caregivers (1300 women and 100 men). These response is part of planned joint project with Dorcas, build on current implementation and cooperation within the Ukraine Joint Response program, a consortium program implemented for the Dutch Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The Accountability to Affected Population in the project will be ensured by:1) Functioning of beneficiaries’ committees gathering according to the need but at least 3 times through the project: in the beginning to share our plans with the community and ensure better adaptation of project activities to specific local needs, including gender- and age-based; during mid-term review to apply corrective measures; in the project finalization phase to give possibility to beneficiaries to evaluate the project results, assess quality of services received and define how much satisfaction of their needs increased due to the project (this information will be used also for informing the next planning cycle)2) Signing the acquaintance and strict following by all the project staff such key documents as the SOS Code of Conduct and SOS Care Promise, where accountability obligation is included 3) feedback and complaints system serving to adjust and change operational plans during the programme cycle so affected populations can comment on the quality, appropriateness and/or adequacy of programme interventions, and report violations of the law, SOS policies and Code of conduct 4) proper informing the beneficiaries whenever changes in the project are made.
