Project Summary

According to the IPC projection, in the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security (FS) situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69M people (57% of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53M people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5. The project aims at addressing the FSL of the most affected people with chronic vulnerabilities worsened by frequent climate-related shocks, the macro-economic crisis, conflict and insecurity, etc. Assistance will help affected people to meet their immediate, medium and long-term FSL needs of the target communities of IPC level 3,4 and 5. To ensure the target population recover from food insecurity and the disaster which occurred as a result of disasters, PAH will distribute agricultural inputs such as vegetables, crop and fishing kits and tools to the most vulnerable HHs as well as MPCA.To address the pressing needs of the most vulnerable populations, PAH will provide a community-led project to support livelihood activities. This will be developed with and prioritized by the community groups themselves such as income generating trainings and initiatives that will boost their empowerment through skills’ development. Microgrants initiatives and provision of training to community groups/CBOs will diversify livelihood and creating community resilience and will strengthen households' ability to absorb and adapt to shocks and contextual changes. During the analysis the AAP will be considered throughout the project cycle to ensure quality of the activities. PAH will ensure community engagement is the foundation of its projects, ensuring that the initiatives are responsive to the community's needs. Extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. This methodology ensures ownership and sustainability, as the knowledge remains in the community. PAH will introduce different irrigation methods including drip irrigation systems together with community training on their adequate and sustainable use to enhance the copying mechanism during the dry season. Special attention will be given to seed quality testing to ensure high quality of the seeds. Additionally, to traditional plots, PAH will encourage setting up small-scale vegetable gardens, which can be established at the Nutrition/Education sites. This facilitates planting and irrigation allows higher diet diversity and minimize SAM/MAM cases and support enrolling children to schools. Fishing has the potential to immediately address food security needs. It enables people to immediately harvest fish from the rivers and swamps in the areas. In the communities with access to functional markets and availability of cash programming, will be considered. The decision will be based on initial market and community assessments, considering cash vouchers/MPCA or cash for work. Food for asset (FFA) will be conducted in the areas where there are needs and local markets can support the project targeting the establishment of community structures and facilities like schools and infrastructures like community road projects and communal farms establishment. Food for education and school feeding programme will be considered in the project areas where PAH carries FSL activities with seeds distribution and vegetable and cereal crops production. A holistic approach to addressing FSL, building resilience and long-lasting effects, PAH will integrate interventions with other sectors such as WASH, Nutrition, Education, Protection and S/NFI through combined hygiene and nutrition promotion in addition to screening and referral to nutrition partners on the ground. Education and health will be integrated through training of SHCs and school gardening and awareness at the schools, Nut/health centers. Protection will be mainstreamed across all sectors and additional protection team will be supporting the communities.

1. Needs

The acute food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating as a result of the economic crisis, repeated climatic shocks – primarily widespread flooding – and conflict and insecurity. The inflow of returnees and refugees fleeing the conflict in Sudan is exacerbating the situation – putting additional pressure on an already fragile country. According to the IPC, In the second projection period of April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analysed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population analysed) will likely experience IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience Phase 5 (Catastrophe). During the lean season (April to June 2025), the severity of acute malnutrition is expected to deteriorate in 62 counties within South Sudan. Given the high levels of severe acute food insecurity in South Sudan, there is a need for immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, particularly those with a high proportion of populations in IPC Phase 4 and 5. The most food insecure populations are in locations that have been significantly affected by frequent climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the economic crisis (currency depreciation and high food prices), conflict and insecurity – including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan – causing forced displacement, low agricultural production and a reduction in humanitarian assistance. According to PAH multisectoral assessment of October 2024 in Pibor and Nasir counties, the overall food security and situation needs humanitarian assistance especially in GPAA, Pibor and Upper Nile, Nasir where both natural and man-made disasters resulted in destruction of farms resulting in low agricultural production and limited access to market as a result of routes closure. In addition, analysis of the needs-assessments showed that the combination of conflicts, political instability, flooding, and a dire economic situation due to currency depreciation and asset depletion has worsened living conditions, causing the community to lose assets and livelihoods. Unfortunately, the current economy due to the multiple factors depending on constant and unstable fluctuations of the local currency, insufficient financial systems such as access to financial institutions like banks and Savings And Credit Co-Operatives, lack of financial services that can grant appropriate loans or invest in other livelihood activities, limited knowledge of the community in terms of accountability and cash handling, negative cultural and gender norms, displacement, and the inability of the authorities at the different level to robustly improve the dire economic situation as well as the livelihoods of the people. These barriers are believed to have an impact on the livelihood and economic situation of the community.

2. Project activities

1. Provision of fishing kits: Fishery as an immediate source of food and livelihood: a) Fishing kits' distribution and demonstration on proper use. In case of access to market and availability – cash or vouchers modality will be used. b) Training on fishing techniques, environment preservation, risks mitigation while fishing. Linking trainees with traders to encourage market linkages between fisherfolk and the supply fisher men. c) Training on smoking and other forms of fish preservation.2. Provision of agricultural inputs (Seeds and tools): distribution of inputs for the main/off planting season including crops/vegetable kits depending on FSL cluster packaging (Core Pipeline) or market. Some of the distributed seeds are resilient to climate shocks like droughts/flood. The tools will include items such as Maloda, hoe and watering cans. Some new nutritive types of vegetables may be introduced to improve the nutritional status. Seasonal fruit trees will be part or the distributed seedlings for reclaiming degraded lands and improved agroforestry practices. CVA modality may be used. 3. Provision of training to reduce post-harvest losses and raising stability of crops: PAH aims to introduce various technologies (e.g., silos, granary from local materials) and processing equipment training in rural areas. PAH will support identified Artisan groups of farmers through vocational skills training on the production and maintenance of appropriate farm implements and storages equipment. 4. Capacity building: a) farming practices that limit negative impact on the environment, including avoiding cutting and burning trees and decomposition of the cut trees b) Training for extension workers and setting up demonstration plots Schools: i) Farmers to farmer’s exchange visits ii) Support in capacity on HH level – training in groups or on the household level on preparing nutritious meals with fish, vegetable, crop, and preservation of food. C) Access to organic pesticides and production D) Trainings on production of organic pesticides from locally available materials. PAH will also conduct cooking classes as training on the cooking (preparation) of the different vegetables. During the cooking classes, the different veggies are introduced, as some vegetables are not indigenous to the community. 5.Food for Education: a) PAH will consider school feeding programme after assessments of needs and the possibility of sustainability and ownership by the community. b) PAH will target education and sustainable health improvement by training school hygiene clubs and school gardening. 6. Cash programming will be based on the initial functional markets and community assessments, considering CVA or CFW. PAH will mainstream cross-cutting issues across all activities focusing on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, AAP and committing to improving the effectiveness of cooperation with communities. PAH will have an integrated approach with FSL and WASH, NFI, protection and nutrition. PAH will pay attention to community hygiene and nutrition promotion on food hygiene and safe water management, to break the chain of contamination at the household level, nutrition screening and referral of SAM/MAM cases to nutrition centres, protection of most vulnerable and decrease the risks of GBV. 7. Provision of Microgrants - The activity focuses on empowering CBOs through the provision of microgrants, community kits, and tailored training to enhance their capacity for self-sustained growth and development. A) Provision of Microgrants: After assessments, training sessions will be conducted to enhance their capacity, with a particular focus on record-keeping, financial transparency, etc. B) Distribution of Community Kits tailored to meet the needs identified during the engagement process, will be distributed to CBOs. C)To ensure the sustainability of microgrant activities, PAH will work closely with the CBOs to develop comprehensive business plans that include exit strategies.
