Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Counties of Wau, Jur River and Raga in Western Bhar el Ghazal, Budi and Terekeka from Eastern and central Equatoria respectively targeting total of 96,671 beneficiaries. Through early detection and case management, 31,468 boys and girls, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) will be reached (SAM 5,573, MAM 14,801, PLW 11,092,) and 65,204 shall be reached through preventive activities (MIYCN 25032, VAS 35,068 and BSFP 5103) in all the four Counties with estimated cost of $481,500.
AFOD will adopt combination of strategies for this nutrition response such as establishment of static services, mobile OTP and TSFP site, capacity building, and integration of nutrition services with Health (malaria screening and assessment of immunization status using child health card, distribution of LLITN), WASH (provision of safe water, soap for hand washing, education on hygiene and sanitation), FSL (vegetable gardening, cooking demonstration, and ), Education (establish ECD kits) and protection for complementarity and synergy and preposition of supplies for desired treatment outcome. Key stake holders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complaint desks, and community dialogues meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback.

1 - Needs

Although the implementation of revitalized peace agreement is for over two years, it has not reduced the humanitarian need in South Sudan. In 2021, 8.3M people need Humanitarian assistant comprising of 24% women, 54% children (0-17 years) and 15% people with disabilities (HNO January, 2021). It is estimated 7.7M people are expected to experience acute food insecurity (IPC 3, 4 and 5) in 2021 mostly children (54%) and women (50%) according to IPC result 2021. The nutrition situation in South Sudan remains a public health concern with high burden of acute malnutrition among children and women which is predicted to be above the emergency threshold of 15 percent in 2021. An estimated 1.9 million people will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021 mostly children (74%), pregnant and lactating 26%. Exclusive breastfeeding practices stands at 68.1% (HNO January 2021) which is below 75% target. Terekeka, Budi, Wau, Jur River and Raga Counties are classified with poor GAM rate (FSNM Round 25) and the major drivers of acute malnutrition in these Counties are: raising food insecurity resulting into inadequate dietary intake, insecurity (civil, tribal/clan conflict), economic crises with continuously rising commodity prices, climatic change (unpredictable rainfall pattern and floods), disasters such as disease outbreak (COVID-19) compromising the weak health and WASH system. AFOD will adopt static and mobile approach for delivering nutrition services for optimum coverage, robust monitoring, and utilization of data to inform decision making, integration of services for cost effectiveness, to prevent duplication and overlap. The need for management of acute malnutrition is still huge despite ongoing humanitarian assistance. Acute malnutrition has a direct undesirable impact on children under 5 years, women of childbearing age (15-49yrs) including Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). Therefore, the need for management of acute malnutrition in under five, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) in Terekeka, Budi, Wau, Jur River and Raga Counties is critical as indicated by poor GAM levels and is likely to worsen without intervention. It is on this basis that AFOD is submitting a proposal to implement management of acute malnutrition to prevent further deterioration in GAM prevalence and averting complications related to acute malnutrition and promote health in under 5 years.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AFOD will ensure through the complaint feedback mechanisms, relevant information on protection against sexual exploitation is disseminated to the beneficiaries to ensure exploitation of beneficiaries is reported on PSEA. Health facility staff and nutrition staff will also be trained on PSEA matters to be able to identify and report exploitation between the beneficiaries and any authority or partners. AFOD shall roll out its whistle blower policy or guidelines, conflict of interest policy and ensure all staff sign and adhere to the organization code of conduct. Hence, no tolerance to exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Transparent complain feedback mechanisms will be established and strengthened in all collective sites. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable persons to provide feedback on the services they receive AFOD shall ensure two-way feedback platforms will promote IDPs to share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries managed appropriately. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the beneficiaries, the project will promote harmony among the beneficiaries and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of race, tribe and ethnic dimensions. The nutrition volunteers will be empowered to monitor, report and refer cases to the health facilities and will participate in coordination mechanisms with other agencies through support from the nutrition team.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Transparent feedback and/or complaint mechanism system will be established and strengthened in all health facilities. AFOD shall ensure two-way feedback platforms are created/improved so that beneficiaries can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the current complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be analyzed and use for decision making. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization during the community outreaches and at service uptake, feedback shall be transmitted to the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AFOD will ensure feedback from beneficiaries is compiled and analysed to inform decision making. The analysis of the feedback from beneficiaries will be used to inform or guide any changes or improvement required. The beneficiaries will be continuously encouraged to use the feedback mechanism established to give feedback about the services provided and through their committees, appropriate solutions will be sought collectively. Project changes or modification will be done with reference to the feedback received from the beneficiaries.
