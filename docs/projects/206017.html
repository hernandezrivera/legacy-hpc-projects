Project Summary

South Sudan literate levels remains very low. According to UNICEF only 23% of the total population in the country have had formal education and this has affected the access to education to millions of young children. Its even worse for the children with disabilities who have no access to education services and schools are not accessible.
The capacity of teachers to teach in schools is very low availability of disability inclusive learning materials for learners with disabilities is also quite limited. This has left learners with huge learning gaps and especially learners with disabilities.
Nodding syndrome is a neurological disorder that affected the cognitive ability of the patients and its prevalent in in Western Equatoria state. The disease is a neglected degenerative condition with partly known aetiology. This syndrome manifests itself in children between 3 and 18 years and, over time, leads to physical deterioration, cognitive and intellectual impairment, and premature death. The condition has had negative effects on the mental health of young learners and has left many of them without education due to lack of disability inclusive training materials for the learners with disabilities.
This project is intended to support children with nodding syndrome in Western Equatoria state enrol in schools and other children with disabilities access education in emergency through identification and registration in the target counties of Mundri East, Mundri West Mvolo, Maridi and Wau.
Integrated capacity building of teachers in pedagogy, disability inclusion, PSS, protection/GBV would help them to give lifesaving skills support to learners so that they can be able to protect themselves and improve resilience by the youths.
Light for the world intents to support 2502 (1200 boys, 1200 girls) children with disabilities and 50 men, 52 Female ) who are teachers and PTA members in the target counties, returnees IDPs and host communities in Wau, Mundri East, Mundri West, Mvolo and Maridi Counties. This will be done through community-based rehabilitation programme where children with disabilities in the communities are supported by CBR field workers will offer rehabilitation, train them on sign language, community awareness on the need of persons with disabilities to access education services. Distribution of assistive devices that will enable children with disabilities to access education services will also be done.
Light for the world will also work closely with OPDs/organizations of persons and train them on advocacy for inclusive education in emergency this will ensure that children with disabilities are able to access education services and that education institutions are disability inclusive.
Light for the world takes seriously gender mainstreaming, Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse as well as accountability to affected population principles and all this will be put in place to ensure there is gender equity, cases of sexual exploitation and abuse and especially in schools are reduced with accountable practices to the beneficiaries by establishment of complaint and feedback channels
The project will establish disability inclusive temporary learning spaces (TLS) and classrooms, establish disability inclusive gender-segregated latrines with water points. Light for the world will give schools supplies, hygiene kits, and recreational materials as well as building the capacity of teachers and PTAs to offer disability inclusive education.
Light for the world has vast experience in disability inclusion and supported the ministry of education in drafting the disability inclusive education policy. This project will also ensure that the disability inclusive education policy is disseminated to the policy to key stakeholders in effort to realize disability inclusive education in South Sudan.

1. Needs

South Sudan has been in political conflict since 2013 and this greatly affected service delivery in the country. A lot of school infrastructure was destroyed during the fighting among the conflicting parties. Its estimated that over 2.2 million children were out of school by 2018 (GPE 2020) and with the county having one of the youngest populations in the world, this puts pressure on the limited education resources in the country.The countries literacy levels are one of the lowest in the country with UNICEF estimating that only 23% of the population are literate and this is even worse for persons with disabilities that have limited access to education services due to lack of disability inclusive learning environment and materials.The conflict in Sudan has greatly displaced populations and the country is experiencing influx of returnees with a total of 347242 individuals having crossed in to South Sudan as of 29th October 2023 (UNHCR Sudan Situation Report, Oct 2023). The returnees are majorly children and women including children with disabilities who are in need of urgent disability inclusive education in Emergency.COVID 19 pandemic has had negative impact on the education system. Before the pandemic a total of 2.2 million children where out of school but this has increased to over 2.7 million children. This includes children with disabilities who lack access to education services thus needing urgent provision of disability inclusive education.According to the IPC analysis November 2023, Wau county and Juba County are in IPC 3 and the situation is expected to get worse in the other counties and this has great impact on the access to education and especially for children with disabilities as parents prioritize food rather than education.Flooding is a major contributor to lack of access to education and according to IRNA report conducted in Mundri east and Mundri West Counties in November 2022, over 6 schools had been affected by flooding and over 1000 learners forced out of school. This also included children with disabilities.In Mundri east, Mundri West, and Mvolo Counties, 2 out of 5 children are affected by Nodding syndrome and out of those over 65% are not in school. Light for the world will strengthen referral to support enrolment and retention, hygiene ADL trainings, and first aid training for students and teachers during seizure.Lack of disability inclusive teaching materials for teachers and disability inclusive learning materials as well as lack of disability inclusive learning infrastructure have negatively impacted the enrolment of children with disabilities in learning institutions. This has led to low enrolment of children with disabilities in learning institutions.Lack of payment of salaries and delayed incentives to the teachers have led to low turnout and motivation of teachers to attend to learners. This has had detrimental effect on learning of children and even worse children with disabilities who need dedication and care in schools by teachers and other key stakeholders.Lack of segregated WASH facilities, menstrual hygiene management kits and hand washing facilities in the schools have also led to girls absenteeism in schools. Early and forced marriages among young girls and other protection concerns like child abductions have had a huge impact on education services for young girls that has led to relatively low number of girl enrolment in schools.This therefore calls for urgent intervention to ensure that children including those with disabilities have access to quality disability inclusive education that will be key in improving literacy levels in South Sudan as well as empowering the most vulnerable in the community.
