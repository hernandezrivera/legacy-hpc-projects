Project Summary

The HALO Trust will continue to deliver a comprehensive approach to Mine Action in NW Syria through three implementing partners, involving Enhanced Contamination Impact Survey (ECIS), Risk Education (RE), and Victim Assistance (VA). HALO will also extend the scope of its activities to include Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and the disposal and clearance of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) in NW Syria, and also aim to expand operations into areas controlled by the Government of Syria (GoS).
In late 2020, HALO will complete its ongoing ECIS work in NW Syria, surveying the remaining 225 accessible communities across Idleb and Aleppo. The continuation of this activity through 2020 will provide ERW contamination data that will immediately improve community ERW awareness and safety, as well as identify priority areas for RE and future NTS and disposal/clearance.
HALO will provide RE to 43,400 beneficiaries in 2020 (8,212 men, 6,538 women, 15,829 boys, 12,821 girls), with 1,372 ‘Training of Trainer’ (ToT) trainees (e.g. other NGO workers and Community Focal Points) being included as RE beneficiaries. A particular emphasis will be put on those who are most at risk of accidents (including children, young men, and IDPs). As part of this effort, RE ToT trainers will continue to support NGOs and Community Focal Points currently delivering RE to vulnerable communities and IDPs, improving the relevance, integration, and reach of RE messaging across all relevant humanitarian sectors.
HALO will continue to collect information about ERW accidents and victims through Victim Data Collection (VDC) to better understand how and why accidents occur and provide referrals to survivors in need. This will enable HALO and other actors to better tailor ECIS, RE, and VA activities to the needs of the beneficiaries.
In 2020, HALO and two of its implementing partners will continue to implement VA activities in NW Syria. HALO’s initial reach will be 4,542 beneficiaries through 3 x rehabilitation centres run by HALO’s partner, providing physiotherapy, psycho-social support (PSS), peer support counselling, orthopaedic services and speech therapy, 3 x mobile units, and multi-purpose assistance. Referrals to 3 x prosthetics centres run by other health organisations will also be provided. To promote workplace inclusion, up to 7 survivors will be recruited, trained, and employed by one of HALO’s partners, while inclusion training to other NGOs and a small apprenticeship pilot will also be run. Through another partner, HALO will provide inclusive livelihood opportunities and referrals to specialised services. 240 beneficiaries (185 men, 55 women) will participate in vocational training, workplace internships and be eligible for micro-business grants, or receive agricultural/livestock assistance. Case management staff will assist a further 240 beneficiaries by referring them to other specialised services. Overall, HALO and its implementing partners will reach 5,022 beneficiaries across its 2020 activities.
HALO will also introduce an NTS and ERW disposal capability in NW Syria, implemented through at least one of its partners. This capability will expand directly on the outcomes of HALO’s ECIS work and will be based on its experience in southern Syria in 2018. HALO, through its Turkish partner, has engaged with Turkish authorities at the governorate level and has received informal positive feedback, official approval will not occur until the project has been approved.
Additionally, subject to obtaining registration from GoS, HALO seeks to contribute to the introduction of a pilot ERW clearance capability in areas controlled by GoS. HALO will aim to prioritize areas highly impacted by the conflict where widespread ERW contamination is expected.

4 - Needs

The Syrian crisis has resulted in widespread ERW contamination, leaving approximately 50% of the population at risk of ERW accidents and close to 2,000 communities reporting contamination and explosive incidents (2019 HNO). As of 31 Dec 2019, HALO ECIS teams in NW Syria have surveyed nearly 85% of the 944 accessible communities across Turkish-controlled Aleppo and opposition-controlled Idleb. Contamination has been confirmed in just under 50% of communities surveyed to date. In GoS areas, there is currently no validated data regarding ERW contamination, but it is expected to be widespread and significant, based on the intensity of past conflicts. ERW contaminates urban and rural areas, causing injury or death, inhibiting livelihoods, and restricting access to land, basic services, and humanitarian aid. From Oct 2016 – Jan 2020, HALO identified 5,885 victims from 4,826 ERW accidents, including 2,207 in Aleppo and 1,497 in Idleb. In Syria overall, every ERW accident on average kills 1.5 people and injures 2 more. A third of the survivors suffer from at least one limb amputation (2019 HNO). Hence, the disability rate in Syria is high, with an estimated 3.7 million Syrians suffering from temporary and/or permanent physical impairments (2019 Disability: Prevalence and Impact). In Aleppo and Idleb, 30% of residents have some form of disability, twice the global average. Persons living with disabilities are disproportionately affected during situations of conflict and emergency, and women and girls with disabilities are particularly vulnerable. Alongside surveying, marking, disposal, and clearance to remove ERW threats, it is also critical for HALO to provide RE to conflict-affected populations to reduce ERW-related risk and prevent accidents. According to the 2019 HNO, 95% of ERW victims/survivors in Syria did not receive RE prior to their accidents. UNMAS has also indicated that less than 50% of the Aleppo population and less than 12% of Idleb received RE.

5 - Implementation of activities

In NW Syria, HALO will conduct its activities through implementing partners. In GoS areas, HALO is planning direct implementation.RE for At-Risk Groups: In NW Syria, RE ToT Trainers will deliver training to NGO workers and Community Focal Points. ECIS and NTS/Disposal teams will also conduct RE sessions whenever needed. In 2020, 43,400 individuals will directly benefit from RE.ERW Survey, Marking, Disposal and Clearance: In NW Syria, ECIS teams will visit communities to collect critical information about ERW contamination. Following ECIS, NTS/Disposal teams will identify Confirmed Hazardous Areas or Suspected Hazardous Areas, and undertake ERW disposal whenever possible. In GoS areas, HALO plans to contribute to a pilot ERW clearance capability, subject to registration. HALO will share all collected data with UNMAS.VDC/VA in Areas Affected by ERW: In 3 x rehabilitation centres across NW Syria run by HALO’s partner, physiotherapy, PSS, orthopaedic consultancies, speech therapy, and peer support counselling will be provided to 4,542 beneficiaries. Survivors in need of prosthetics will be referred to prosthetics centres, with the cost of prosthetics funded by HALO’s project. 120 beneficiaries will receive vocational training, workplace internships and be eligible for micro-business grants, while agricultural/livestock assistance in the form of conditional vouchers will be provided to another 120 beneficiaries. Case management will be provided to a further 240 beneficiaries in need of referral to other specialised services.MEAL Activities: HALO will conduct MEAL through two methods – through partners and independent field monitors. Partners’ MEAL staff will compile monitoring reports and feedback forms, while HALO’s field monitors will conduct regular spot checks to validate project implementation. They will attend all activities, including trainings, and complete QA forms. Key informant interviews and focus group discussions will also assess the impact of projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Teams perceived as having a biased affiliation, exposing them to harm (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Mitigation - partners are well known/trusted in their areas of operation, and coordination with local authorities will facilitate safe access. Only partners’ visibility/logos will be employed in the field. Escalated dependency on assistance by VA beneficiaries (Likelihood: medium; Impact: medium): Mitigation - early health intervention, referrals, and self-reliance promotion through peer support activities will increase beneficiary socio-economic participation and reduce humanitarian assistance dependence. Female beneficiaries not provided with equitable assistance (Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium): Mitigation – recruit sufficient female staff, who are better positioned to reach out to women/girls, gathering data on their requirements and ensuring their needs are met whenever possible. Lack of community acceptance, limiting successful project implementation (Likelihood: low; Impact: medium): Mitigation – employ lessons learnt from previous projects and undertake needs assessments to inform project design including activities, locations, and beneficiary selection. Teams will also conduct community liaison during implementation. Staff exposed to, or guilty of, GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse (Likelihood: low; Impact: high): Mitigation - all project staff are trained in PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and 'Do No Harm'. Activities are designed to consider the needs of men, women, boys, and girls. Each beneficiary has access to feedback and complaint mechanisms. Field MEAL will also occur. Injury to team members &/or local community members during survey/disposal tasks: (likelihood: low, Impact: high): Mitigation - teams will be supervised by experienced EOD Technical Officers, with attention paid to individual member safety, collective team safety, and safety of local populations. Teams will deploy with a vehicle, first aid equipment, and communications.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security situation deteriorates in NW Syria, exposing field staff to possible harm and preventing successful project implementation: Mitigation - HALO and its partners maintain threat contingency plans and security SOPs. Field supervisors, informed by field security staff, can adjust the daily/weekly work plan. All teams are deployed with mobile phone communications, a first aid kit, and knowledge of the nearest medical facilities. Partial or total suspension of activities (including fixed facilities and mobile teams) will be temporarily imposed if necessary. If activities cannot be resumed, HALO and its partners will assess the possibility to operate in other areas. Removal of UNSC cross-border resolution in NW Syria after Jul 2020 prevents ongoing UN intervention: Mitigation - Turkish-controlled areas and Idleb would remain accessible by HALO’s partners based in Turkey, and HALO will continue cross border operations under its non-UN funded projects. Alternative coordination bodies could take over if the UN ceases its coordination role, such as the NW Syria NGO Forum. Inability to obtain GoT approval to conduct NTS/disposal in NW Syria: Mitigation - HALO has successfully renewed its NGO registration in Turkey and has partnered with a Turkish NGO to conduct NTS/Disposal who is already conducting activities in NW Syria. Inability to obtain GoS registration to conduct clearance in GoS areas: If GoS registration is not successful, HALO will continue operations in NW Syria.
