Project Summary

With the proposed humanitarian responses, International Medical Corps (IMC UK) will provide integrated lifesaving nutrition service to conflict affected host communities and displaced population in Canal/Pigi, Panyjar, Malakal, Fashoda, Manyo, Panyikang and Juba IDP Counties of Jonglie, Unity, Upper Nile and CE states. To address the suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among vulnerable children and women, IMC will continue to increase equitable access and utilization of comprehensive quality preventative, promotive and curative nutrition services for children, adolescents and women in the targeted counties experiencing multiple deprivations, including high acute malnutrition rates above WHO emergency threshold, food insecurity, high morbidities, high GBV concerns and poor WASH conditions. The proposed response will include management of wasting, through the treatment severe acute malnutrition without medical complication at OTP sites, severe acute malnutrition with medical complication at SC sites, and Moderate acute malnutrition treatment for both under five children and PLW at TSFP sites. In addition, the project will support and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices through Maternal-Infant Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) activities focusing on the first 1,000 days of a child's life. IMC UK propose to deliver a comprehensive package of nutrition services provided through establishing and strengthening 60 OTP/TSFP static and outreach/mobile facilities and 4 SC sites across the project sites.
IMC UK will spearhead the integration of nutrition services in the health system, and promote the simplification of early detection, referral, and treatment of children with acute malnutrition including empowering families and communities by promoting the use of Family-MUAC, mother and father support groups, CNVs/BHW as a main vehicle for delivering multi-sectoral services at community, household, and facilities level. To continue boosting the existing coordination, knowledge and experience transfer and sustainability, IMC will work with SPEDP (Support for Peace and Education Development Program) the local partner active in UNS, Unity, Jonglei and CES to enhance the localization efforts and strengthen the capacities of communities and CHD staff at the county level to support preparedness and transition of provision of nutrition services from Implementing Partners.
IMC UK will monitor the nutrition status of the population and the nutrition response through routine nutrition program data, quarterly MUAC screening campaigns and population-based SMART Surveys. With its Nutrition Surveillance Team, IMC will undertake nutrition assessments in its operational locations and support to undertake in nutrition assessments in Priority counties as well to provide robust information on the nutrition situation of a given population, this information will be key in responding effectively to nutrition needs in the project areas.
IMC will employ a people-centered approach that mainstreams Accountability to Affected population (AAP), strengthen the community system and collaborate with FSL, WASH, Health, Education and Protection partners to target and ensure that children and women access a safe, equitable and dignified comprehensive package of essential services through the nutrition sites to prevent malnutrition. IMC is the leading health and GBV partner in Malakal, Panyjar, Pigi/Canal Counties and Juba IDP, this will present a unique opportunity for the integration of health, nutrition and GBV, thus reducing challenges associated with coordination, monitoring, and evaluation. The integration approach will also reduce operational costs by leveraging IMC’s operational presence and the established relationships with the authorities.

1. Needs

The nutrition status of children in South Sudan continues to deteriorate due to the multiple hazards the county is facing. According to the April - July 2023 integrated food security classification (IPC) projection, 7.76 million people were expected to face a Crisis status (IPC Phase 3) or acute food insecurity, with 43,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Between June and September 2023, over 60% of the population is expected to be acutely food insecure, leading to widespread Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and in 2023, about 1.4 million children under five years are expected to suffer from wasting of which 345,893 children will likely be suffering from severe wasting. According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. The prevalence of global acute malnutrition remains very high in all IMC operational areas. The measles outbreak continues to affect children. From Jan to June 2023, 2,546 suspected cases of measles and 32 measles-related deaths were reported. The influx of returnees due to the ongoing Sudan crisis reiterates the need to enhance surveillance and immunization services to reduce the number of cases. As of October 317,993, people have crossed into South Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. Findings of SMART survey conducted by IMC in November 2022 in Panyjar, estimated GAM prevalence at 20.2 % (15.7 - 25.6 95% C.I.). The crude death rate was of emergency threshold at 1.36 (0.80-2.31) deaths per 10,000 persons per day while under-five death rate was 1.39 (0.55-3.46) deaths per 10,000 under-fives per day. The SMART survey conducted by IMC in Malakal in September 2023, revealed the GAM 20.2 % (16.4 - 24.7 95% C.I) and SAM as 5.2 % (3.2 - 8.5 95% C.I.). Similarly, the SMART survey conducted in Canal/Pigi in March 2023 by IMC revealed GAM prevalence as 17.8 % (14.0 - 22.3 95% C. I) and SAM prevalence 3.8 % (2.7 - 5.4). The crude death rate was below emergency threshold at 0.60 (0.30-1.18) deaths per 10,000 persons per day while under-five death rate was 0.55 (0.18-1.66) deaths per 10,000 under-fives per day. As per 2023 FSNMS survey’s findings, GAM prevalence for Fashoda estimated as 32.8% and SAM as 8%, Manyo 27.5%, SAM as 5% and Panyikang 32.8% and SAM as 8% which is very high above the emergency threshold of WHO.In addition, the SMART survey conducted by CWW in Juba IDP on 2022, revealed GAM as 19.0% (15.2 - 23.6, 95% C.I), whereas SAM was 4.9% (3.6 -6.7, 95% C.I.) indicating a very high malnutrition rate. The crude mortality rate (CMR) was 0.49 (0.30-0.79 95%C.I.) deaths/10,000 people/day and under five mortality rate (U5MR) was reported at 0.22 (0.03-1.66, 95% C.I) deaths/10,000 people/day. Morbidity rate was at 47.7% of surveyed children in the two weeks prior to the assessment with fever (38.6%) and diarrhea (35%) being the most prevalent diseases in the camp.Analysis shows that the key drivers of malnutrition include high food insecurity, with an estimated 50% of people being food insecure; poor water and hygiene conditions over 50% of the population lacking access to safe water or sanitation; high child morbidity rates with an estimated 67 % suffering from diarrhea, malaria or upper respiratory infections; gender-based violence concerns; and poor child feeding and caring practices with only 4% children getting the minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well. The persistent inter-communal conflicts, widespread flooding, and the current Sudan crisis has also exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition in the targeted counties, hence, immediate response is needed to avert a possible further catastrophe and spike to IPC Phase 5 (famine).
