Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to continued access to safe drinking water, food and learning opportunities in safe and secure learning environments for 462,161 children and adolescents affected by humanitarian emergencies in 1,234 schools in 13 districts.UNICEF will provide teaching and learning materials, tents for classrooms, and Emergency School Improvement Grants to support the provision of clean drinking water and hygiene facilities; minor repairs to damaged infrastructure, food and non-food items. UNICEF will also provide School Health supplies, Information, Education and Communication materials, and support trainings to strengthen mental health and psychosocial support and safeguarding.

1 - Activities

Zimbabwe adopted the Education Amendment Act of 2020 to protect the right to education for all children. However, education was interrupted and learners lost out on learning time when all schools closed for more than 6 months due to the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19). Thousands of learners are more likely to drop out of school. The negative impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic are projected to continue well into 2021 and beyond. The combined effect of increased climate-induced hazards, and deepening economic challenges are likely to contribute to increased household poverty, food and water insecurity and diminished capacities of parents to meet the costs of education. Learners, teachers and school communities will be at greater risk of hunger and psychological stress. In response to the protection and learning needs of children and adolescents affected by the humanitarian emergency, UNICEF will undertake the following priority activities to increase access to continuous learning opportunities in safe learning environments for 462,161 children and adolescents in 1,234 schools across 13 districts: 1. Provide teaching and learning materials (School-in-a-box-kits, early childhood development kits, recreational kits, materials for teachers, and teachers’ kit, supplementary radios, textbooks and Non-Formal Education Modules accessible to learners with Disabilities);2. Provide Emergency School Improvement Grants for the procurement of inputs for emergency school feeding; the provision of improved drinking water; minor repairs to damaged infrastructure, including WASH facilities, and the planting of trees to increase tree cover in wind or storm-damaged schools; 3. Provide temporary safe learning spaces for affected learners (Tents, temporary, semi-permanent structures), including WASH facilities and furniture;4. Provide School Health supplies, including age and disability appropriate Water, Sanitation and Hygiene kits, Dignity Kits, water purification tablets, deworming prophylaxis, counselling and support, and hygiene materials such as soap and cleaning detergents; 5. Support the MoPSE to collect, analyse and disseminate real time data using an SMS-based survey (RapidPro) and conduct monitoring visits in affected districts to understand the status of affected populations, infrastructure and emergent needs;6. Support provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial support services by training and prepositioning a rapid response team of mental health and psychosocial support professionals in each of provinces and districts; 7. Conduct cluster-level trainings for teachers to respond to emergencies; 8. Support the provision of non-formal education programmes and skills training, including Accelerated Learning and Remedial Education to help with re-entry support to return to school after emergencies;9. Provide Information, Education and Communication materials designed to be accessible to diverse groups of people, including illiterate persons, persons with disabilities and those from minority language groups, in all affected districts; and 10. Support trainings of MoPSE personnel and EiE partners in all provinces on DRR, child safeguarding, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Disaster Risk Reduction and Resilience and other topics to help strengthen the sector’s capacity for coordination, integration and enhance efficient and effective delivery of quality education services.

2 - Indicators

UNICEF will track the following indicators as part of this project:• Number of children and adolescents benefiting from UNICEF-supported school feeding by age, gender and disability status;• Number of children and adolescents benefiting from UNICEF-supported safe drinking water sources by age, gender and disability status;• Number of schools supported with UNICEF-supported temporary learning spaces; • Number of schools and learners receiving UNICEF-supported supplies per year;• Number of persons (male and female) trained in DRR, PSEA and Child Safe Guarding;• Number of schools in humanitarian situations receiving UNICEF-supported Emergency School Improvement Grants;

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

While UNICEF did not consult communities to develop the project, the design of this response plan builds on lessons learned from past emergencies, including Cyclone Idai, floods, and the ongoing COVID-19 epidemic. Key among these lessons is that some of the much-needed resources, including temporary learning spaces take far too long to be delivered after the emergency, and that the coverage of psychosocial support (PSS), trauma and counselling support for learners and teachers is often below expectations. UNICEF has engaged the Department of Civil Protection and the Ministry of Health and Child Welfare on the training and preposition of mental health professionals, building on the community-based Civil Protection Committees. UNICEF will work to ensure that all affected populations supported through its humanitarian action as well as teachers and school communities are able to hold UNICEF to account for promoting and protecting the rights of individuals accessing humanitarian assistance. Specifically, UNICEF will provide information on various interventions through various media and in a form stakeholder can understand. Deliveries to schools will be pre-planned in consultations with heads of schools and representatives of communities, to enable them to provide feedback on the delivery of humanitarian assistance. UNICEF will also plan with and share information with the MoPSE, to enhance accountability. UNICEF will also create complaint and feedback mechanisms such as WhatsApp platforms for Ministry personnel, including heads of schools, U-Report and social media.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

While the project is designed to respond to the learning needs of children and adolescents in humanitarian situations, addressing those needs may have negative impacts on the environment. For example, there may be logistics-related emissions, waste and other environmental impacts along the supply chain of moving goods and services from their producers to populations affected by disasters. Recognizing this, where financially feasible, UNICEF will prioritize the procurement of locally-made goods and services to reduce the carbon footprint associated with transporting goods from offshore. The transportation of goods will be done using serviced trucks that are not only able to deliver bulk goods to reduce fuel consumption associated with making multiple trips, but also reduce inefficiencies. UNICEF will also ensure that transporters use serviced vehicles that do not emit as much fumes. To mitigate against the carbon footprint created by the project, UNICEF will procure indigenous trees and distribute them to storm-damaged schools to enhance tree cover and offset emissions. In addition, UNICEF will strive to procure environmentally friendly supplies. UNICEF will also work to ensure that reports are not printed on paper, to cut down on paper waste.
