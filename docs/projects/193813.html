Project Summary

In line with Shelter Cluster HRP Priority need #2, PUI is proposing to provide Essential rehabilitation of sub-standard Housing Units (HU) to 150 households (HH) (825 individuals) living in substandard HU, and Critical winterization repairs to 150 HHs (825 individuals) who are exposing to harsh weather conditions during the rainy season. In addition, PUI will provide tailored and essential protective NFI kits to 150 HHs (825 individuals) who are exposed to harsh weather conditions during the winter season. The action will protect the target group from harsh weather and provide them with adequate living space. Finally, PUI will provide adaptation works to HHs having at least 1 person with a disability.
The action is based on the technical assessment conducted in April 2021 by PUI to identify the detailed shelter repair needs of 240 substandard housing units. Besides, 5 focus groups discussions were conducted by PEF (Palestinian Environmental Friends association), one of PUI’s partners, to capture the challenges as well as the needs of the HHs affected by the harsh weather conditions and in a real need to implement winterization repairs to their houses.
PUI, with its main partner PEF and in close collaboration with its local CBO partners, will target 300 HHs, with a particular focus on acutely vulnerable households including people with disabilities (PwDs) and female-headed households (FHH) living in substandard HUs and exposed to extreme weather conditions, through shelter rehabilitation works. The beneficiaries’ selection will be done according to the social and technical vulnerabilities scoring system adopted by the Shelter Cluster. PUI and PEF will consult the final beneficiaries on their needs and preferences for the repairs and upgrading works, taking into consideration the specific needs of women, children, older people and Persons with Disabilities (PwD) or chronic-illnesses. Based on assessed needs and capacities, the proposed shelter upgrades and winterization repairs will be done through a self-driven approach with a focus on local communities’ skilled workers in each targeted community, Abasan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Bani Suhaila, Al Fukhari in Khan Yunis and Shokat al Soufi and Ouraiba/Al Bayuk in Rafah.
PUI will closely collaborate with Humanity and Inclusion (HI) for adaptation works targeting PwDs in order to be in line with technical specifications, and to provide tailored, accessible and inclusive assistance ensuring the dignity of the targeted group.
PUI and its partners have demonstrated effective capacities in rehabilitating substandard housing units in similar previous experiences and rely on strong local networking of local partner CBOs. The planned action will be implemented with PEF besides PUI local CBO partners: Al Walaa Association in Abasan Al Kabira, Future Home Association in Khuzaa, Qaa AL Qurain in Al Fukhari and Mariam Al Azra Association in Al Shouka. Their knowledge of the communities will be an added value for the inclusive approach of the project to ensure that the most vulnerable, in terms of age, gender and diversity (AGD) within the identified households will be part of the action, as well as to ensure the accountability of the project, so all feedback can be taken into account.
The action will be coordinated with the Shelter Cluster, the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH), the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and key actors working in the area of intervention.

1 - Needs

According to the MSNA 2022 findings under the shelter sector, across the oPt, it is estimated that in 2023, 663,000 Palestinians are in need of humanitarian assistance to access adequate shelter (including basic households supplies), and therefore lack minimum levels of privacy, protection and dignity. 89% of the people in need of shelter assistance are in the Gaza Strip. It is also indicated that there are over 68,000 female-headed households suffering from damages and defects to their shelters, 11,000 of whom live in severe and extreme shelter conditions that do not meet minimum standards for protection, privacy, covered living space, resistance to extreme weather conditions, or suffer from unrepaired damage or inadequate WASH facilities. Female-headed households were found more likely to remain in the remains of their damaged shelters than male-headed ones (7.5% to 2.8% - UN Women Gender Needs Assessment 2022), due mostly to concerns related to protection risks and dignity when being hosted in other people’s houses. Similarly, 86,000 persons with disabilities live in shelters with partially damaged or defected, of whom 17,000 individuals live in severe and extreme shelter conditions. HHs with PwDs are also disproportionately impacted: of households with completely or partially demolished homes, 12% report having a PwD, while households without any home damage report an 8% rate (UN Women 2022). Women and girls living in substandard, overcrowded, unsafe shelters with lack of privacy and limited access to hygiene facilities are more acutely exposed to risks of gender-based violence (GBV). These conditions are likely to increase protection related concerns and consequent negative coping mechanisms such as early marriages, and greater exposure to GBV-related risks. According to UN Women 2022 Gender Needs Assessment, inadequate shelter also impacts the mental health and wellbeing of families and disrupts children’s education. Damages to shelters causes a loss of privacy for children, forced to share rooms with other siblings or adults. In addition to the stress caused, this increases the risk of child abuse. This particularly affects girls, targeted disproportionately by child abuse due to its gendered nature. Moreover, for boys and girls, the loss of feeling of safety in the home, the experience of adult family members unable to ensure protection, as well as other factors sur as the loss of personal items (e.g. toys, photos) can be a heavily distressing and potentially traumatic experience. Data also reveals that younger head of households (HoH) are more severely affected, as they have fewer financial resources, smaller or less numerous living spaces, and own fewer household assets than older HoHs. They are therefore more likely to have to resort to displacement in collective shelters, when older HoH can cope with displacement more easily.Gaza is one of the most densely populated areas in the world, with 2.11 million Palestinians living in an area that is 365 km2, with a population density of 5,800 per square kilometer. Gaza suffers from a chronic housing shortage of 120,000 units, with an annual average of 14,000 housing units needed to meet natural population growth. While the occupation remains a major driver of shelter needs in Gaza, the housing crisis is impacted by a combination of different factors: escalations, the blockade and the high costs of construction, and limited economic opportunities as a result of over 15 years of Israeli restrictions on the import of building materials and equipment, the deteriorating socio-economic situation, and recurring waves of hostilities. 21,500 existing housing units across Gaza which are classified as substandard and do not meet the minimum requirements of living space, weather protection, hygiene facilities, and privacy, require different degrees of rehabilitation. It is also observed that poor housing conditions and damages caused by conflict disproportionately impact most socioeconomically vulnerable and marginalized households (UN Women 2022).According to the MSNA 2022, 8.9% of the assessed households are at risk of eviction. For 28.9% of those this is due to disputed ownership, for 27.2% to the lack of capacity of tenants to cover rental costs, and for 17.2% to inadequate shelter conditions. Additionally, the MSNA-WASH 2022 analysis revealed that 12% of Gaza households reported being affected by flooding in the 3 years prior to the MSNA data collection (of these households 41.2% reported their shelter being impacted by flooding and 87.1% reported floods disrupting their daily activities).

2 - Activities

In line with HRP 2022 strategic priorities (objective 2), the proposed activities below aim to provide adequate living space for the most vulnerable households, notably female-headed households, families with PwDs and acutely vulnerable HHs living in substandard housing units and at higher risk of being affected by harsh weather conditions. The project will target the communities of Abasan Al Kabira, Khuza’a, Bani Suhaila, Al Fukhari in Khan Yunis and Shokat al Soufi and Ouraiba/Al Bayuk in Rafah.• Selection of beneficiaries: The action will be coordinated with MoPWH, MoSD and the Shelter Cluster; therefore, the potential beneficiaries list will be obtained from MoPWH and MoSD databases, notably the list of “hardship cases” from the MoSD. Furthermore, PUI will conduct an in-depth assessment (including socio-economic and technical assessments) and apply the shelter cluster criteria and scoring system to ensure eligibility for assistance. In total, 1,650 direct beneficiaries will be supported through the action. PUI recognizes that people belonging to different Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) groups who live in substandard living conditions are exposed to risks and threats, whereas they are often stripped of opportunities to engage in positive coping strategies to tackle them. The project will prioritize households including PwDs, persons with chronic-illnesses and mental health issues/illnesses, older persons, FHH, HHs with younger HoHs, and other groups with specific needs, in the aid provision, while also ensuring that the aid itself is tailored to cater to their specific needs and capacities. Adaptations and adjustments in the provided interventions will be based on consultations with different AGD groups of people.PUI will conduct orientation meetings with the selected beneficiaries to provide detailed information on the project’s activities, timeline, selection criteria and feedback and complaints mechanism (FRM). Additionally, PUI protection officer will explain PUI’s PSEAH policies complaint mechanism. During these sessions, PUI will share the details of the contact persons for PSEAH, distribute the hand out FRM leaflets and cards which include the contact information in case of facing a misconduct/misbehavior from the humanitarian worker or the community. Moreover, protection staff will place PSEAH awareness printouts and the FRM mechanism posters on the local partner’s advertisement boards. • Activity 1: Essential rehabilitations of sub-standard HUs to 150 HHs (825 individuals, 407 females, 418 males, 393 children, 399 Adults, 33 elderly, 35 PwDs). This would entail rehabilitation of the roofs, windows, doors, kitchen, bathroom, etc., with an average of 2,500 USD for each target housing unit. The rehabilitation works will mainly focus on the safety of stairs, balconies, and roof connected to stairs, external and internal doors, windows, roof and external walls, internal walls and surfaces, water supply and safe storage, functioning shower/ toilet facilities, functioning kitchen facilities, sufficient space per person and privacy, improving gender separations, and electricity supply and lighting, as well as home adaptation for PWDs (handrails/grab rails, constructing small ramps, widening doors, replacing a bath with a level access shower, fitting lower work surfaces to make kitchen easier to use, and other internal modifications). A detailed BoQ will be developed for each targeted HU which will be shared and discussed with targeted families.• Activity 2: Critical winterization repairs and provision of essential protective NFI e-vouchers to 150 HHs (825 individuals, 407 females, 418 males, 33 elderly persons, 36 PwDs) exposed to harsh weather conditions and floods during the winter season. The repairs will include windows maintenance, installation of new windows, and/or external door/s if needed and sealing of the house’s roof within an average of 600 USD for each targeted housing unit. The rehabilitation activities will be implemented through a self-help approach. The work will be supervised by PUI’s technical manager, PEF’s civil engineers, and field monitors, with a technical support from HI for any required adaptation works for PwDs. PUI will be in charge of the overall management of the project. PUI’s main implementing partner, PEF will be responsible for data collection, updating the technical assessment and follow up of the civil engineers, participating in the beneficiary selection process, and reporting on the progress of activities. For households unable to conduct the works by themselves for any reason (no able persons in the HH), a contractor, selected by PUI, will conduct the works under the supervision of PUI and PEF. Beside the winterization repairs, PUI will provide tailored NFIs assistance to the same 150 households using PALPAY’s e-vouchers platform. The NFIs items allowed through the e-voucher include but not limited to clothes, heater, kitchen sets, basic furniture, emergency LED lighting system, wheelchairs and other assistive devices for PwDs, etc. The average value of the e-voucher will be of 200 USD per HH. The use of the e-voucher modality will give the beneficiaries the flexibility and freedom of choice when buying the needed non-food items. In addition, it contributes to boosting local economy.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

• Cash: Cash modality is recommended by the affected population and the humanitarian actors as long as the markets in the affected areas are functioning, the financial service providers are operating with an adequate capacity, and goods are available. According to the MSNA 2022, 91.8% of HHs reported a preference for cash and voucher assistance in any modality. In GS, goods are available in the local market, but the affected population has lost the means to buy them. This is why cash transfers can be securely provided to the targeted households who can choose how to use the money to cover their priority needs in a dignified manner. Evidences showed that cash transfers, in GS and other contexts, are often more efficient and effective than other forms of aid. The cash transfers modality provides affected people with the means and flexibility to decide and priorities their life-saving and recovery needs. Moreover, it also supports the local markets and contribute to boosting local economy. Cash transfers are effective in addressing negative coping strategies such as poor diets and debts. • Voucher: Evidences showed that beneficiaries of humanitarian aid projects generally prefer voucher assistance over in-kind distribution of NFIs. The modality gives the target BNFs greater flexibility and freedom of choice in the purchase of the needed NFIs according to their priorities and preferences. Also, it contributes to enhancing the sense of dignity. Moreover, the use of voucher modality in GS context, where goods are available, and markets are functioning, is important and plays a vital role in revitalizing and boosting the local economy especially in light of the chronic cash liquidity issues. The voucher also ensures the attainment of the intended objectives of humanitarian assistance through restricting to some degree what recipients can buy or where they can make their purchases.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s feedback and response mechanism (FRM) is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. For example, the idea of increasing the number of installments provided to the vulnerable households was a result of the collected feedback and recommendations raised by former beneficiaries in similar PUI interventions. In the same way, the choice of distributing cash rather than in-kind derives from beneficiary feedback, as it provides them the flexibility in the management of their expenditures. MEAL team will strive to ensure that feedback, whether positive or negative, will collected and followed up on by PUI representatives independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be shared by the MEAL team with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Officer and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises. Project modalities or objectives will be discussed with the program and technical team, in order to better respond to the needs and concerns of the served population.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

PUI uses various two-way communication channels: oral, written, easy to read, face to face, via email and through meetings; to ensure that feedback and complaints mechanism is easily accessible by all beneficiaries including people with specific needs such as PwDs and children. These communication channels include (but are not limited to) feedback boxes, hotline number, email, and the pages of the organization on social media. PUI designed a kids-friendly and interactive hand-out card including the hotline number, drawings, and painting exercises to increase their awareness regarding their rights, and to encourage them in complaining in case they faced a misconduct/misbehavior from humanitarian workers or the community. Additional publications were designed to the same purpose i.e. posters and leaflets During all the beneficiary selection processes, orientation meetings with the beneficiaries, and the field visits, PUI’s team systematically provides detailed information on the feedback and complaints mechanism, sharing the details of the contact person FRM leaflets are handed out and the process is also explained orally, to account for those who cannot read. Also, PUI tries to disseminate the information of the FRM through local partner CBOs and key community leaders, handing out cards, posters, and other means of information dissemination regarding PSEAH and the FRM mechanism, ensuring cultural appropriateness and inclusivity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may also be provided verbally in the field, through text messages, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). The MEAL team will be providing an initial feedback in less than 24 hrs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEAH is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. Moreover, PUI will provide targeted PSEAH sessions to project personnel, partner staff, service providers ahead of community engagement as well as during the implementation of the project. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed (i.e. the Protection Cluster Service Directory) and complemented by other resources identified by PUI if needed. Furthermore, all PUI field staff have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEAH and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partner, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies to the standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people; and conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs. As mentioned above, PUI will closely collaborate with Humanity and Inclusion (HI) for adaptation works targeting PwDs in order to be in line with technical specifications, and to provide tailored, accessible and inclusive assistance ensuring the dignity of the targeted group. Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, conducting inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
