Project Summary

Recurrent climatic shocks, including drought and flooding as well as ongoing conflict and insecurity contribute to growing internal displacement in Somalia. A total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. With an estimated 2.9 million women, men, girls and boys currently displaced. According to Shelter cluster an estimated 3.7 million people including displaced and non-displaced populations need shelter and NFI assistance as a result of poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. The IDPs are currently residing in urban and Peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions. Armed conflict and widespread insecurity, as well as recurrent climatic shocks, perpetuate high levels of humanitarian needs and protection concerns. The most vulnerable groups, who are exposed to the highest risks and are consistently socially excluded, include women, children, the elderly, child- and female-headed households, the physically and mentally disabled, people living in conflict zones, and marginalized clans.
Under the proposed intervention, SFH aims to address the immediate humanitarian needs of the newly displaced, the protracted IDPs and host communities living in a deplorable shelter conditions in Kismayo, Afmadow and Wajid districts by providing life-saving and life-sustaining Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance to the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls. The project will ensure affected population have protection, privacy and improve their living conditions through the provision of appropriate emergency shelter assistance, non-food items (NFI) while transitioning to durable solutions. The proposed intervention aims to provide NFIs/core relief items to 3000 HHs (10,800 women, 7,200 men) newly displaced and protracted IDPs through in-kind/voucher based on market assessment. Further, 1500HHs benefiting a total of 9000 individuals (5400 women & 3600 men) newly displaced IDPs will also receive Type 2 emergency shelter kits or $213 voucher to improve their shelter condition. The content and the composition of both the NFI and ESKs a will be in compliance with most recent standards provided in the latest Shelter Cluster strategy. SFH will conduct detailed market assessment prior to the rollout of the project to determine the most appropriate methodology of providing the assistance as either In-Kind/voucher. Where there is stable market functionality and traders with capacity such as Kismayo,-voucher will be used. In-kind distributions will be considered in hard to reach areas that doesn't support stable market. A total of 27,000 most vulnerable persons in the target region with have an improved shelter and Core relief items. While strict community and vulnerability based targeting will be applied in the registration and distribution of the assistance, female and child headed households, the elderly of above 65+, the chronically ill and person living with disability will be prioritized. SFH will undertake post distribution and post construction monitoring for each round of activity completed.
Responsive community response and feedback mechanism will be established. SFH will use age, gender and diversity approach to collect the data of the affected population. Different population groups including men, women, children, older people and disabled will be consulted and their specific needs will be considered in planning and implementation of the planned interventions.

1. - Projects activities

The following main activities will be undertaken to achieve the program objectives and desired outcome 1) Distribution of CRI/NFI Kits to 3000 HHs (10,800 women, 7,200 men) among the newly displaced and Protracted IDPs in lower Juba and Bakool as per the cluster standards. In-kind distribution will be considered in Wajid due to lack of functional market and vendor with capacity to supply. This is because the town has been under sieged or land locked and entry of goods has been difficult forcing the local traders use donkey carts to sneak limited goods-which often fall in the hand of the armed insurgent and got burnt. To avoid use of cash for unintended activities by the beneficiaries and thus compromising the desired goal of the project, SFH will prefer the use of voucher in Lower juba where there is stable market and vendor with robust capacity to supply the required items. This is will promote market based programming and strengthen the capacity of the local vendors by procuring locally available materials and items. The beneficiaries will have access NFI items worth $100 from the local vendors.2) Distribution of 1000 Type 2 Emergency Shelter Kits to 1500HHs benefiting a total of 9000 individuals (5400 women & 3600 men) newly displaced households through In-kind/voucher. SFH prefer voucher, because the use of the unrestricted cash to address emergency shelter and NFI needs may a) Result in some of the cash being spent on non-shelter or NFI items and therefore have a consequence in the project outcome b) Self-selection of items by the beneficiaries may lead to the use of sub-standard materials or their incorrect usage. 3) Conduct Post constructions monitoring and post distributions monitoring (PCM/PDM) for every activity completed as per the cluster standards. 4) Establish effective, responsive and accessible Community response and feedback mechanism (CRFM). SFH will target the most severely affected populations that demonstrate acute, life-saving need for either shelter materials or basic household items. Support will be provided on the basis of identified need regardless of a beneficiary’s “status,” such as IDP or returnee. At the same time, SFH recognizes that disability, gender, age, and status are often factors that enhance vulnerability and thus need to be taken into consideration when analyzing the degree of need. Persons who are exposed to a combination of risks and have limited ability to cope in the face of those risks will always be prioritized. Households who are characterized by: presence of members with psychological condition or physical disabilities, pregnant and lactating women, older person or chronically ill individuals, female or child headed household, large households and households with many young children, households with extremely vulnerable shelters, HHs belonging to marginalized groups or clans will be prioritized. The proposed intervention will be measured against the following indicators. Indicator 1: Number of Household/persons receiving Non-Food Items (NFIs) through in-kind/voucher. Target: 3000 HH (18000 Persons). Indicator 2: Number of Households/persons receiving Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) through in-kind/Voucher. Target: 1500 HH (9000 Persons). Indicator 3: Number of Shelter/NFI distributions undergone PCM/PDM exercise. Target: 5 Indicator 4: Number of Shelter/NFI distributions that have complaint and feedback mechanisms established. Target: 2

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. Mitigation measure:-a) Targeting will be guided by needs, access and vulnerability and the vulnerable groups include women headed household, child headed households b) Inclusive representation of the committees will be prioritized to ensure persons with disability or special needs are represented and their data made available. c) SFH will make special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits. they will be assisted and their safe arrivals ensured. d) SFH will establish accessible and functional response and feedback mechanism include real-time free hot lines b) Establish information desks at the distribution sites to receive timely complaints or compliments from the beneficiaries e) Prior sensitization of the target beneficiaries on how the feedback and response mechanism work. f) AAP committee member and local authorities including IDPs existing governance structures will be involved in the sensitization, registration and distribution process this will strengthened feedback mechanisms and ensure transparency. g) Prior sensitization on the targeting criteria, individual entitlements, distribution day and date will enhance transparency and accountability. h) Gender inclusive and Needs-based targeting regardless of displacement status will be prioritized. 4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. Mitigation: a) SFH will Identify central locations to ensure beneficiaries do not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. b) Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. c) NFI will be distributed in an appropriate NFI bag that can be carried to allow beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them. 4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area. Mitigation measure: a) In close coordination with the CCCM actors and particularly camp management agencies engaged in HLP and site development-SFH will ensure land tenure security guaranted by the local authorities for atleast 5 years prior to any shelter constructions. b) As a member of the CCCM and protection clusters SFH have been engaged in site planning and de-congestion processes before, we will harness this capacity to ensure shelter standards are meet.
