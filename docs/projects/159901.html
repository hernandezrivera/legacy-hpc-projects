Project Summary

In alignment with HRP Strategic Objective #1: Physical and mental well-being”, herewith is a summary of WHO interventions:
Intervention A: Support Emergency Preparedness and Response Capacities of the Somali Health Sector: The project will contribute to strengthening the health system’s ability to withstand and cope with recurrent shocks and disasters. Its purpose is to build preparedness and response capacities of health authorities at all levels, as well as health cluster partners who provide services to Somali communities. In this respect, this intervention will seek to: (i) finalize national action plan for health hazards; (ii) training of senior level managers from health authorities on public health risk assessment and development of contingency plans; (iii) train 100 health workers from health cluster partners on public health risk assessment and development contingency plans; (iv) provide epidemic prone disease case management training to health workers from selected health facilities; and (v) support establishment and functionality of health emergency units at federal and state levels.
Intervention B: Expand the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN) for Epidemic-Prone Diseases in Somalia: Somalia is prone to epidemic disease outbreaks due to protracted conflict which has led to a total breakdown of health systems and displacement. People in IDP (internally displaced persons) camps have limited access to safe water and proper sanitation, which predisposes them to disease outbreaks, especially cholera, measles and malaria. Early detection and notification of epidemic disease alerts which are supported by real time responses can lead to a reduction in morbidity and mortality. To this end, this intervention will (i) strengthen existing EWARN reporting facilities; and (ii) expand the number of reporting facilities which report through the EWARN system.
Intervention C: Health Emergency and Resilience Building in Somalia: In line with the objectives of the “Somalia Recovery and Resilience Framework”, this project aims to respond to immediate, lifesaving needs of vulnerable communities in Somalia, as well as strengthen resilience in health across the country by: (i) responding to immediate, lifesaving needs of vulnerable communities; (ii) strengthening health resilience through health education and promotion at community level; (iii) preventing and responding to outbreak-prone diseases, including vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks; and (iv) enhancing the capacity of local health workers to be equipped with the skills and knowledge necessary for providing relevant health services.
Intervention D: Improving Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) care for conflict affected persons in Somalia: MHPSS services are largely non-existent in Somalia. Moreover, in a health system where formal institutions are ill-equipped to deal with such problems, affected individuals often resort to harmful coping strategies, (e.g. self-medication, substance abuse), which worsen mental health and the psychosocial effects, and reinforce existing stigmas around mental illness. In alignment with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings, this intervention will seek to ensure that: (i) health professionals and community health workers in conflict-affected communities gain professional-level capacity to deliver MHPSS services (including youth-centered and gender-sensitive services); (ii) persons with mental illness and psychosocial issues in conflict-vulnerable communities are provided with socially inclusive MHPSS services through community-based psychosocial support approaches and health service delivery; and (iii) community awareness on mental health/substance abuse, stigmatization, harmful behaviors and negative coping mechanisms that drive conflict is increased, thereby empowering communities to effect positive change through peer sensitization/education.

Project Activities

Intervention A: (i) Support establishment and functionality of health emergency units at federal and state levels, including the development of an Emergency Operations Center (EOC); (ii) Develop a national action plan for health security; (iii) Develop contingency plans for key hazards identified in previous years; (iv) training of up to 100 health authorities staff from Federal and State levels on public health risk assessment and case management; (v) train up to 200 NGO health workers on public health risk assessment and case management; and (vi) provide training on case management to 200 health facility workers. (PROJECT TARGET: beneficiaries calculated as Direct (trainees) = 500; Indirect (host community) = 307,750; Indirect (displaced as 15% of host community) = 54,308). Intervention B: (i) Strengthen existing EWARN health facilities through refresher trainings; (ii) expand EWARN reporting to reach 90% of all health facilities across all regions (approximately 800 health facilities in total); (iii) establish and train district-level Rapid Response Teams (RRT) in 50% of all districts to support verification and investigation of alerts on epidemic-prone disease; and (iv) strengthen reference laboratories (Hargeisa, Garowe, Mogadishu) through training of staff and provision of reagents and equipment to analyze biological samples for infectious health hazards, as well as for the shipment of samples from all districts to the reference laboratories; (v) in addition to expanding the EWARN reporting sites covering the 14 priority diseases, include trauma cases/casualties from secondary and tertiary facilities in reporting. (PROJECT TARGET: beneficiaries calculated as Direct (trainees) = 484; Indirect (host community) = 1,000,000; Indirect (displaced) = 150,000 – please note, that estimates are only approximate). Intervention C: (i) Respond to immediate, lifesaving needs of vulnerable communities through deployment of RRTs and IERTs (integrated emergency response teams), as well as through the provision of life-saving medical supplies; (ii) strengthen health resilience through health education and promotion at community level for 50% of the population; (iii) prevent and respond to outbreak-prone diseases, including vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks, through alert verification, rapid response, case management, and vaccination campaigns; and (iv) enhance the capacity of up to 100 local health workers to be equipped with the skills and knowledge necessary for providing relevant health services; (v) establishment of an EMT (Emergency Medical Team) that provides a continuum of trauma care from triage and transportation, to trauma management in surgical facilities; (vi) expand the 53 nutrition stabilization centers by 50%; (vii) train 25 health workers, using WHO standard guidelines, on management of SAM with medical complications. (PROJECT TARGET: beneficiaries calculated as Direct (trainees) = 125; Indirect (host community) = 308,069; Indirect (displaced as 15% of host community) = 54,364). Intervention D: (i) Develop multidisciplinary training modules on MHPSS and integrate these modules into health training curricula (e.g. at Somalia National University); (ii) deliver trainings for up to 100 health facility nurses and community caregivers in conflict-affected communities; (iii) provide psychosocial support services for up to 10,000 persons in conflict-prone settlements at the individual, group, family, and community level, taking into consideration gender dynamics, stereotypes and gender norms, and using existing primary health facility as the entry point; and (iii) develop a peer-to-peer mental health/substance abuse awareness program with marginalized communities, and support implementation so as empower peers to become change agents in communities. (PROJECT TARGET: beneficiaries calculated as Direct (trainees) = 300; Indirect (host community) = 307,920; Indirect (displaced as 15% of host community) = 54,338).

Mitigation Measures

• 1b: Seek to (i) ensure the location, distance and access routes to the health facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups; (ii) identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence; (iii) avoid community tensions, by providing local/host communities with access to camp health facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities; (iv) consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether health interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities; and (v) ensure services can be accessed by persons with non-mobility related disabilities (e.g. persons with impaired hearing/vision/cognitive ability/mental health) by providing information about services in both verbal and written form. • 2a: Seek to (i) collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming; (ii) ensure assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable (identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in a community and prevent their discrimination or exclusion); (iii) ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services; (iv) Introduce special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities eg. mobile health services, transportation/taxi services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks); and (v) make infrastructure adaptions to the health facility so that it is accessible to persons with impaired mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, older persons, bed-ridden individuals). • 3a: Seek to (i) consider separate waiting areas for males/females; (ii) have a proportionate number of female health staff, including a female doctor rotating between locations if possible and adequately informing women on which days a female doctor will be available; (iii) employ health staff with skills and experiences working with children; (iv) separate examination rooms from public spaces/waiting area; and (v) keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use (i.e. effective data protection measures).
