Project Summary

"The proposed activities by UNICEF aim to improve the coverage and the quality nutrition services and their use by the beneficiaries for the prevention and treatment of malnutrition in camps and host communities, using three strategies: (i) strengthening of systems; (ii) promotion of community- and facility-based nutrition interventions and (iii) integration of complementary nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions. While the first strategy seeks to strengthen the existing systems by improving the human and institutional capacities, as well as strengthening partnerships with government and non-government organizations, the second strategy intends to improve community demand for services, outreach and participation in identifying and addressing causes of malnutrition and improve the quality of nutrition services at both community and facility level. Lastly, implementing complementary nutrition-sensitive and specific interventions will improve the impact, by contributing to addressing immediate, underlying and basic causes of malnutrition.
Following a life-cycle approach and to break the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition, the main targets of these interventions are children under-five years (with a special attention to children under-two), adolescents (with focus on adolescent girls) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and the elderly.
Key interventions will focus on screening and referral of malnourished children, in-patient and out-patient treatment of severe acute malnourished (SAM) children, growth monitoring and promotion (GMP), prevention and treatment of micronutrient deficiencies through micronutrient supplementation, promotion of optimum Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices (IYCF) and maternal and adolescent nutrition, through intensive social mobilization and behavior change communication. Support to livelihoods improvement, as part of resilience building (especially in host communities) will also be implemented.
Throughout the implementation, a special focus will be given to gender and equity, to ensure fulfillment of human rights for any kind of vulnerable populations. Strenghtening coordination and partnerships with other institutions involved in the Rohingya response, as well as improving information systems, supporting nutrition suveys and assessments will also be an important aspect of UNICEF's interventions."

Needs

After a notable decrease of malnutrition rates from 2017 to 2018, the situation appears to have stabilized in 2019 in makeshift camps, especially for acute malnutrition. The preliminary results of the SMART survey conducted in October 2019 (Round 4) show that in these areas, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate is unchanged, at 10.9% (compared to 11 in 2018) and is categorised as 'high' based on the new WHO/UNICEF prevalence thresholds, with a slight reduction of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rate (0.9% versus 1.1%). On the contrary, the situation has worsened in Nayapara registered camp, with an increase in GAM rate (13.3% in 2019 versus 11.2% in 2018). In both areas, micronutrient deficiencies and stunting prevalence remain very high, and constitute a major public health concern. In 2019, stunting prevalence and anemia in children under-five years in makeshift camps were respectively 32.6% and 37.1%, while in Nayapara, 39% of children under-five years were stunted and 39.4% were anemic. The existing situation is caused by insufficient use of services by beneficiaries despite improvement in coverage of services. In makeshift camps, only 59.3% of children less than five years have received vitamin A supplementation in the past 6 months prior the SMART survey, while only 60.9% of pregnant women and 15.3% of adolescent girls received Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) tablets according to guidelines. Similar trend is also observed in Nayapara. Additional causes are poor nutrition practices, especially insufficient dietary diversity among infant and young children, and insufficient use of improved WASH facilities. Diarrhea represents to second common cause of morbidity in makeshift camps and Nayapara (around 28 %), and according to SMART survey Round 2 almost 50% children in the makeshift camps and 26% in Nayapara registered camps are not exclusively breastfed. Besides 45% of infants in the makeshift camps and 24% in the Nayapara camp are not introduced to timely breast feeding at birth (within one hour of birth). The SMART survey conducted from February to March 2018 in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila shown that malnutrition is also a cause of concern in host communities. Overall GAM prevalence is 11.4% (11.6% in Ukhiya and 11.2% in Teknaf), with SAM prevalence at 2% in Ukhiya and 1.2% in Teknaf. Chronic malnutrition is very high, at 35.5% in Ukhiya and 31.3% in Teknaf based on the new WHO/UNICEF prevalence threshold requiring urgent action. In the initial phase of the response, the focus was not put on elderly population, while they are also highly vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition. The physiological changes put them at risk of malnutrition, and they usually face challenges to improve their livelihoods, therefore limiting their access to a healthy and nutritious diet. According to UNHCR demogaphic data, there were 30,205 population above 60 years in refugees camps in september 2019. While continuing to provide life-saving interventions through management of severe acute malnutrition, it is important to strengthen the preventive aspect of malnutrition. Additional efforts must be made to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable populations. To improve the knowledge and practices of the communities, and create demand for nutrition services, intensive social mobilization activities must be implemented. Creating linkages and improving coordination between health, WASH, social protection and nutrition, among others, is key to ensure better nutrition outcomes.
