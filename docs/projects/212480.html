Project Summary

Across all Oxfam’s interventions, it continues to identify protection risks & needs through regular protection analysis FGDs with community members in 2024. Needs: access to life-saving assistance, assistive devices, home & street lighting, basic home protections, psychological & psychosocial therapy, speech therapy for armed conflict affected children, & income-generation activities for individuals at heightened protection risk, etc.. Oxfam’s protection intervention is part of the General Protection intervention, & is not part of the GBV response, Child Protection or Mine Action. Oxfam's approach involves three main strategies: 1) supporting community coordination efforts to enhance self-protection capacities including supporting the establishment & capacity-building of community-based protection structures & later supporting community-led protection initiatives. Unlike other protection actors Syria, Oxfam does not prioritize community-led initiatives aimed at reducing socio-economic vulnerabilities or addressing health & education problems. Oxfam focuses on addressing man-made risks related to coercion, violence, or deliberate deprivation. 2) engaging with Protection-specialized actors in advocacy & policy work on protection issues, & 3) providing direct services, including distribution of dignity & winterization kits, & provision of IPA through cash, in-kind, or referral modalities. Oxfam opts to refer to them as "kits of personal items" instead of "dignity kits". Oxfam, while not a case management actor, understands the importance of partnering & coordinating with case management actors to deliver principled IPA. Oxfam independently identifies individuals at risk & extends assistance beyond referrals from case management actors. Oxfam's IPA (in-kind/through cash) encompasses a wide range of needs. It aims to prioritize individuals at protection risk undergoing case management services & those identified through various entry points, focusing on restoring lives & incomes, obtaining vital HLP or civil documentation, facilitating access to medical examinations & assistive devices, providing life-saving aid, & offering paid psychosocial/psychological therapy. Oxfam aims to prevent negative coping mechanisms, like child labor. In the absence of an IA referral mechanism, robust national social protection system, & sufficient sector integration, at-protection risk individuals under case management services often encounter unresolved action plans. Oxfam prioritizes protection cases referred by case management services, aiming to facilitate individuals' access to essential services & items, even if falling under different sectors as long as they are needed to enhance the overall protection outcome of the case.
IPA process: Oxfam requests case management actors to anonymously refer individuals at protection risk without sharing identifiable information or details about the nature of these cases. Subsequently, Oxfam Protection experts invite the case management actor to a Case Conference call to collectively, anonymously discuss & determine the feasibility of providing cash or in-kind assistance for the responses. During the call, detailed information about the protection risk or the perpetrator is not sought from Oxfam or shared by the case management actor. Oxfam's primary focus is understanding & addressing the requested protection remedy, need, or assistance, without unnecessary disclosures of the protection incident. Oxfam faces the challenge, especially with referrals from GBV case management actors, of cases that are experiencing socio-economic vulnerability rather than actual protection risk. According to referring actors, assumption made by case workers is that socio-economic vulnerabilities always lead to GBV risks for women & girls. However, Oxfam cannot rely on this general statement. During conferencing call, Oxfam frequently inquires if the individual is exposed to man-made risks such as coercion, violence, or deliberate deprivation, or if it is a case of socio-economic vulnerability. Once confirmed during the conferencing call that Oxfam will provide cash or in-kind assistance to the individual, case management actor contacts the individuals. To offer: a) referral to Oxfam (including sharing personal information with Oxfam after informed consent, or b) self-referral: providing information on how to directly contact Oxfam's Protection personnel to claim the assistance. If the individual agrees to the self-referral, case management actor provides a specific X code & shares the same code with Oxfam, for Oxfam to ensure the individual belongs to the pool of individuals decided entitled to Oxfam’s assistance. If the individual declines the referral after employing the risk matrix with the case manager's assistance, the referral to Oxfam can be disregarded. When Oxfam receives the identifiable information, no further assessments or questions related to the individual's protection experience are conducted. Oxfam only asks questions related to the preferred delivery method (in-hand or cash transfer) to help the individual make an informed decision about receiving assistance. Considering Oxfam's previous experiences with GBV case management actors & their limited technical capacity, Oxfam decided to prioritize referrals from CP, PSS, & disability-specialized case management in 2024. This prioritization ensures safe provision of IPA & avoids compromising the assessment quality & safety of cases.protection risk analysis: key protection risks identified through the HNO & Oxfam's community protection analysis: lack of access to civil documentation, increasing the risk of statelessness & exclusion from essential services, undisclosed risks of gender-based violence at domestic & community levels, worst forms of child labor, unaddressed trauma & psychosocial distress for children & caregivers leading to increased violence at various levels, & limited access to life-saving assistance. These risks are exacerbated by today’s realities, & many are left unaddressed due to limited capacity & resource constraints within protection sector & weak integration with other sectors which further restricts the ability to have a comprehensive approach rationalizing limited resources & maximizing impact while addressing risks. Oxfam’s distribution of individual dignity kits is blanket covering all women & girls aged 14+. Oxfam is exploring ways to contextualize the content of the dignity kit to meet the essential personal needs of men & boys in the communities targeted. While designing the content of kits, Oxfam strives to commit to sector minimum standard, and avoiding overlap with other contents winterization of MHM kits distributed by other actors in same location.
