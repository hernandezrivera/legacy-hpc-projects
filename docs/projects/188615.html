Project Summary

This project will target the most vulnerable small scale farmers, mainly IDPs, returnees and hosting communities in Syria which are per the HNO for 2021 have expressed most need for income-earning opportunities, fuel, food and health services. The thematic geographic focus of the project is based on the vulnerability of population, accessibility to basic services and livelihoods opportunities, the rate of displacement or returns, and the highest severity scale.
Interventions will include:
(1) Provision of training on agricultural practice with the aim of increasing knowledge and skills for better and environmentally, and economically sustainable agricultural production; (2) Provision of agriculture input and farming tools to restore agriculture productive capacity; (3) Support sustainable livestock husbandry practice; (4) Support food processing activities based on local agricultural production, coupled with support for market access (value chain development).
The type of assistance provided will be based on the beneficiaries' identified needs as well as their ability to participate in/benefit from the intervention.
Targeting and Vulnerability criteria: severely food unsecured; IDPs; HHs hosting IDPs; HHs with children under five years old and/or pregnant women and/or lactating women; FHHs; HHs with no productive assets, or functional means of income/ reliable source of income; Elderly headed HHs; HHs headed by chronically ill members or headed by physically challenged heads; 9- HHs not receiving adequate assistance from other sources; HHs meeting other vulnerability criteria as identified by the communities .
Specific criteria for livelihood assistance:
For the support of sustainable livestock husbandry practice proposed activities, UNDP will exclusively target HHs who have livestock and/or experience in livestock management, access to grazing land, availability of labor for livestock activities and willingness to maintain livestock.
For the provision of agriculture input and farming tools, UNDP will target HHs who have agriculture land and sources of water but must be the small holders.
For the support of food processing activities based on local agricultural production, UNDP will target beneficiaries with minimum required Know-How and have access to agriculture input and market. The estimated number of Households is 4000 distributed as follows: 800 HH will benefit from the provision of training on agricultural practice; 1200 HH will benefit from the provision of agriculture input and farming tools; 1500 HH will benefit from the support of sustainable livestock husbandry practice; 500 HH will benefit from the support of food processing activities.

4 - Needs

As per the HRP 2021, 13.8 per cent of the Syrian population has a poor Food Consumption Score (FCS), with 41.4 per cent just at the borderline. If no immediate assistance is provided, it is most likely that the majority of people will slide into the poor FCS category. In the targeted areas, the situation is expected to worsen throughout these areas because of the prevailing and protracted challenges. Moreover, as per the Situation Report #3 published by WFP in March 2021, the food security situation in Syria is at critical levels and continues to deteriorate, according to the latest WFP mobile Vulnerability Analysis and Mapping (mVAM) data. The national average rate of inadequate food consumption (poor and borderline combined) increased by 11 percent month on-month (from February to March 2021) and 72 percent year-on-year (from March 2020 to March 2021). UNDP started working in the rural areas where communities rely on agriculture and livestock as a source of food and income, identifying agriculture and livestock as live saving assistance and the most sustainable strategy. UNDP will respond to needs by delivering agricultural assistance while ensuring sustainable agricultural practices through community and resilience-based agricultural & livestock interventions. i.e. contribute effectively to increasing the resilience and livelihood opportunities.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNDP will provide technical support and oversight on the quality implementation of the project and will take the lead in the overall implementation of the project in close collaboration with the different stakeholders. The project team will consist of both programme management and technical teams on the ground that will develop a detailed plan based on the implementation strategies. Moreover, the UNDP field staff will share their expertise in community mobilization, beneficiary selection, data collection and data analysis, awareness raising activities. In addition, UNDP will follow its procurement protocols during project implementation. UNDP will setup a solid and transparent selection criterion of beneficiaries, although there may be variances in the vulnerability criteria in the different communities, UNDP will work with the local communities to prevent discrimination and ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable groups. To further ensure the participation of the most vulnerable groups, UNDP will follow-up with the selected beneficiaries who did not benefit from the project activities in order to ensure that the selection criteria has been implemented correctly. The project team will provide regular, impartial monitoring through direct field monitoring visits, occasional, remote telephone monitoring, and indicator data tracking mechanisms. This is intended to provide a regular assessment of the project for appropriate course corrective actions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Perform conflict analysis before planning the response; 2. Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent; 3. Make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, area reached equally; 4. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms; 5. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the communities concerned; 6. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to Affected Populations’, by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation; 7. Make sure some process of receiving complaints and of responses to complaints is put in place; 8. Have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1- Levels of support to targeted households are large enough to meet needs on the ground2- Activities supported by the UNDP are coherent and lessons can be consolidated to have an impact on policy and implementation.3- Approvals are granted by local government to start project implementation4- The exchange rate of local currency is stable. Risk: 1- Needs on the ground are higher than available resources.2- Factors of implementation change making a change in the policy or implementation plan a necessity 3- Delay in implementing project activities due to delay in receiving consent from related government bodies.4- Rapid fluctuation of local currency will affect negatively the procurement process where some supplier might withdraw their offers in case of currency depreciation.Mitigation measures:1- UNDP will apply solid selection criteria methods of beneficiaries. 2- UNDP will plan for different implementation methods to be able to adapt with abrupt changes.3- UNDP will advocate through different means to chase up the approval process. 4- UNDP will speed up the bidding process to reduce the risk accompanied to currency fluctuation.
