Project Summary

This intervention aims to provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity targeting two states Blue Nile and South Kordofan with high tensions to Implementation of the action plan of the protection of children in armed conflict. Re-integrating children associated with armed groups and addressing grave violations through the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism, Unaccompanied Separated children and other vulnerable children.
Through this intervention VSF-G intent to complement interventions of child protection and through this project will also ensure that social reintegration activities are fully coordinated with and complementary to other activities being implemented by VSF-G and partners in the targeted locations including education, health, nutrition, GBV/protection and WASH interventions (inclusion of youth as community sanitation motivators; WASH clubs at schools, linking children targeted by the program and their families to other health or nutrition services and to be represented in different community development committees, etc.).
VSF-G works in conflicted-affected areas and newly accessible areas, with increased chance to have released children who were recruited by armed forces. VSF-G has worked with unaccompanied children who were integrated with families within their communities as well as with child-headed households. VSF-G will work with the S/NCCW, line ministry, sector partners as well as CBOs to facilitate reaching those children, otherwise identified at-risk children in high risk areas/communities.
Through this intervention VSF-G will support child protection services which including Placing and supporting Unaccompanied and Separated children in Alternative care in Baw, Gissan and Damzin localities in Blue Nile and Abu Jebaha , Abassia and Kadogli localities in South Kordofan with introducing and Provide Case Management and referral services to 100vulnerable children through strengthen of CBCPNs and referral system with support to social workers and family tracing and reunification of those children. As well as build capacity of teachers on child protection, child protection reporting mechanisms
Through this action VSF-G aims to create awareness in the communities around children’s right to protection, preventative measures to minimize the risk of violence, abuse or exploitation of children in the target areas through creating a social, cultural, institutional and legal environment for them. This will be complemented by the existing centres, committees and networks in Blue Nile and south Kordofan State, in addition to establishment and training of community-based child protection networks (CBCPNs) as well as build capacity of government counterpart on child protection principles, mechanisms, prevention of recruitment, child rights and importance of cooperation between the government and the communities in implementing child protection.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

election of beneficiaries will be performed with due consideration of “Do No Harm” principles, ensuring fair representation of different vulnerable groups regardless of their ethnicity or gender. Criteria for selection of beneficiaries will be socialized with the communities and ample opportunity will be provided to raise concerns and/or complaints about the selection process. The involvement of the host communities to address their needs and promote peaceful coexistence will be ensured. The target beneficiaries will be empowered through awareness raising with regard to protection principles focusing on prevention and response to exploitation and abuse in project orientation sessions, and by involving them in decision making and participation in the project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established and the communities will be encouraged to provide their feedback through multiple channels. VSF-G will ensure to respond timely and appropriately through its program and project management to the communities’ feedback and/or complaints. This aims to improve programming, understand the targeted communities’ perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in early detection of problems such as targeting (exclusion/inclusion), misconduct including sexual exploitation and abuse, diversion and fraud. As well as VSF-G and partners staff will be oriented on PSEA. The safety and security of both beneficiaries and staff will be duly considered for each location/activity. Selected distribution points, and more importantly water points to be rehabilitated will be in safe and secure areas to promote protection of vulnerable groups such as girls and women who fetch water for household use. The safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be prioritized through assessing and identifying risks associated with assistance provided (e.g. livestock, vouchers, etc.); including analysis of the local environment such as ownership patterns, recent history of looting, husbandry practices and accessibility to livestock services. Ensuring that distribution sites are safe for all people involved, and that beneficiaries will not face problems with theft, intimidation and extortion. If beneficiaries must travel, it must be ensured that the road through which they travel is safe, they might be provided with transportation to ensure safety, otherwise distribution points will be moved closer to beneficiaries’ homes. For elderly people or persons with disabilities, special arrangements will be made through coordination with welfare departments and community-based organizations to identify those with limited mobility to include them in the project activities as appropriate.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

project will adopt Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. IASC commitments with regard to AAP are incorporated in this plan including governance and integration of feedback and accountability mechanisms into program design, maintaining transparency and dialogue between the partners and the affected population, participation and involvement of the target population in design, monitoring and evaluation of this project, establishment of strong communication systems to ensure feedback is received, processed and responded to timely and effectively. All the while mainstreaming protection and considering the safety and confidentiality of the target population. approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and or marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted throughout all the stages of the program; communities’ feedback was the most significant element for the needs assessments conducted before proposing this action- the proposed intervention is based on communities’ needs. Community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure that the project is well understood by target communities and raise the communities’ awareness about their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners toward them. Participation will be promoted through the involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. The community committees will be created to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries, participate in the development of beneficiary selection criteria, distribution of inputs and monitoring and evaluation of the intervention. Representatives from a cross-section (concerning age, gender, and diversity) will be ensured to participate as community committee members and in the monitoring and evaluation of the project. The consortium partners have intensive experience working with the target communities and established relationships and inclusive community structures which will facilitate the establishment of effective feedback mechanisms. The most appropriate mechanisms will be established in consultation with community members, taking into consideration literacy rates, accessibility to communication devices and the presence of suitable sites for establishment of complaint/suggestion boxes; to ensure accessibility of all community members.
