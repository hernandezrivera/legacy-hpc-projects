Project Summary

This proposal by Cosmic Nudge Foundation (CNF) seeks to address the life-threatening barriers to quality basic and vocational education for vulnerable school aged girls, boys and children with disabilities CwDs between the ages of 3 to 17 in conflict affected areas in targeted local government areas (LGAs) in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. The proposed project is targeted to reach 6 LGAs, targeting 970 direct beneficiaries from each of the LGAs with a total project target of 5,820 of the conflict-affected displaced, returnees, host communities, school-aged girls and boys in the BAY states. These children will be reached through both formal and non-formal education settings.
This proposed project by Cosmic Nudge Foundation seeks to provide and improve on the knowledge, of all the direct beneficiaries and focus will be placed on scaling up timely and equitable access to quality basic and vocational educational empowerment, through community mobilization, sensitization and advocacy, and capacity building.
The project will evoke a participatory approach by involving not only the beneficiaries, but State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), School-Based Management Committee (SBMC), Islamiyah School Associations and other stakeholders within the target states.
The project is designed for a 12 months’ time-frame intervention duration, with a proposed overall budget of $426,507.

1 - Needs

The protracted conflict within the northeast has severely impacted the already-weak school system in the North East, leaving the limited system unable to absorb the high numbers of displaced children looking to attend school. In addition to high numbers of out-of-school children, learning outcomes for children in-school (including foundational literacy and numeracy rates) are low and drop-out rates are high. An estimated 1,867,183 conflict-affected children across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states are estimated to be in need of education services. The crisis has caused large-scale displacement within Nigeria and across borders creating hundreds of thousands of IDPs and returnees. This displaced population is significantly less likely to participate in education and that is especially true for IDPs and returnees living in formal or non-formal camps, many of whom live far from government schools and have limited income to spend on school and school related costs. Among this group, girls are especially unlikely to participate in organized learning activities because of the greater domestic burden that family and community placed upon them, concerns about protection and interaction with unrelated males and perceptions that girls may not need education. Host community children, IDPs and returnees living in host communities may have better access to schools, but often also struggle in schools which do not have conducive learning conditions such as overcrowding or lack of supplies and qualified teachers.All the BAY states recorded poor enrolment of children in the 2018-2019 academic year, because of limited learning space and lack of resources. In Yobe State, children have access to adequate learning materials in only 24% of schools, and proportions are even lower in Borno (14%) and Adamawa (10%). School furniture is another identified gap: just 33% of schools in Borno have adequate furniture (chairs, desks, mats), 24% in Yobe and 20% in Adamawa (JENA, 2019). Moreover, children, especially girls, demonstrate a higher level of attendance in schooling when provided with full uniforms, including sandals and socks. Actors in education need to begin expanding the definition of educational supplies in order to meet this need for gender-responsive programme planning.Primary needs identified by the Education in Emergencies Working Group (EiEWG) include support for both alternative and formal education. Needs include development of alternative learning opportunities for children and youth not currently accessing education, supported by construction, rehabilitation and equipping of temporary learning centres, rehabilitation or renovation of classrooms, provision of furniture and WASH facilities, training of teachers, provision of teaching and learning materials, distribution of student kits and school uniforms. In order to ensure the quality of learning, lessons delivery methods need to be adapted to ensure the learning outcomes of radio education are attainable; including local language facilitation of lessons and expanded curriculum content.

Community sensitization through “back-to-school” campaigns to address parents’ reluctance to send their children to school, most of all for girls.5. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Throughout the project implementation, CNF will ensure to use proven mechanisms to engage community members and stakeholders; this is to ensure accountability and transparency. To this end, Cosmic Nudge Foundation (CNF) will set up functional and contextually appropriate feedback and accountability mechanisms in such a way that the beneficiaries can confidently and safely relay their complaints, dissatisfaction and suggestions of project implementation.These will be through the use of suggestion boxes that will be fixed at strategic locations within the affected communities, the use of complaint help desk and a toll-free line where beneficiaries may relay their complaints comfortably. These feedback mechanisms will ensure to provide the beneficiaries with timely and appropriate responses while maintaining the principle of confidentiality.
