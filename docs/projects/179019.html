Project Summary

Objective 1: Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people
• Supply safe and adequate water through trucking This intervention will secure safe access to clean, sufficient and adequate water for drink and hygiene purposes, improve proper hygiene practices of 86,061 IDPs (41,759 male, 44,302 female) living in 71 camps in Harim, Salqin, Dana, Kafr Takharim, Maaret Tamsrin, Qourqeena and Atareb sub districts throughout the entire project duration. Each individual will receive 35 liters per day. In Total 64,546 cubic meters.
• Water quality monitoring through testing at three levels: water sources, water trucks, and household to ensure water is according to standards.
• Installation of water tanks: IYD will procure and install 38 new galvanized water tanks between 71 sites to address a current shortage. Each tank is equipped with multiple taps to serve more than one person simultaneously
• Desludging of grey water, this grey water will be discharged from the septic tanks in 71 targeted camps from the latrines and showers which connected to those septic tanks
• Daily cleaning of 366 latrines blocks which constructed by pervious projects.
• Solid waste removal will be organized from each of the 71 camps .All waste will be disposed into a communal landfill after the consent of the respective local council
• Provision of 370 garbage bins: to improve the hygienic conditions in the targeted camps
• Distribution of essential hygiene materials;
• Hygiene promotion and awareness. Strengthen hygiene promotion activities and IEC materials with focus on COVID-19 preventive key messages and proper hygienic practices.
Objective 2: Support to water and sanitation systems to ensure regular services for affected people in NWS
• Improved operation and maintenance for water supply systems: This activity will have two parts one in the camps and another in the community as the following:
o In the camps: this project will include the expansion works for the existing water network to reach the camps around the well who are depending on the water trucking to ensure their daily water need, increase the pumping capacity by equipping the well with electrical and mechanical equipment such as generator, submersible pump and electrical control panel, installing a renewable energy system(solar power system)
o In the host community: Through the supporting of eight water stations in Idleb, Dana and Teftanaz subdistricts, and by operation costs (fuel, spare parts, lubricants oil, and supporting of the water stations operators, water networks operators’ salaries for three months.
• Improved access to sanitation services: Through support to sanitation systems with the rehabilitation of the existing sewerage lines In Idleb city and replacing the damaged and old parts of the sewerage network

4 - Needs

As the humanitarian situation for the 4.1 million people living in northwest Syria remains dire. The impacts of displacement, military operations, security hazards, and nine years of conflict are increasingly exacerbated by the rapid devaluation of the Syrian Pound (SYP) and the COVID-19 pandemic. In an area where some 2.8 million people already rely on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs such as food, water, shelter, healthcare, and education, the impact of these recent developments is entrenching the existing humanitarian needs and creating new ones. (OCHA, Situation Report No. 20/Sep-2020) In NWS (as of 3 November), a total of 7,059 confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported (4,265 from Idleb and 2,794 from Aleppo governorates), including 42 deaths. 2,728 people have reportedly recovered. In NWS (as of 3 November), a total of 7,059 confirmed cases of COVID-19 were reported (4,265 from Idleb and 2,794 from Aleppo governorates), including 42 deaths. 2,728 people have reportedly recovered. (OCHA, COVID-19 Response Update No. 12, 9 Nov.2020). Families in 50 percent of assessed communities reportedly have insufficient access to water. In the context of the COVID-19 outbreak, these conditions increase the risk of transmission among this highly vulnerable population. The dire conditions in these areas parallel the extreme needs prevalent across other parts of the northwest, where deteriorating conditions continue to increase reliance on humanitarian assistance.

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD’s response to the Syria crisis is led in Turkey and Syria by the Program Manager (PM) who is responsible for the operational and strategic management of the organization’s programmed in both countries. The Syria Project officer are responsible for programs. quality and provide support and technical advice for the implementation and management of the actions. The Quality and Program Development Coordinator leads IYD’s MEAL, CRM, program development and information management teams. The HR officer oversees the support functions of HR, logistics and administration. Finances are. overseen by the Finance Manager (FM), assisted by Finance officer and Assistants. In total IYD has over 60 staff based in Turkey in various roles. In Syria, IYD has a team of over 400 staff who are based in the 2 Governorates in Aleppo and Idleb. IYD’s Implementation plan will be as the following steps: • Needs Assessment: WASH rapid needs assessment conducted via our MEAL and WASH team in coordination with the local councils and the community representatives in the proposed areas • IYD will hire the required staff to coordinate with local authorities and to communicate the projects activities. • BOQ and Estimates: for the proposed activities rehabilitation of water station and other activities using standard tools/formats preparing detail estimates. • Conduct the procurement/Bidding Process as per IYD’s Procurement policy. • Review of agreements/Purchase order by project Coordinator or relevant staff and signing of agreements by supplier/IYD's Purchase order. • Site Layout/commencement of work: WASH officer and WASH assistant to perform site layout. • Sample of Preparation and approval• Conduct quality control visits

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

This project is designed to provide WASH services to some of the most vulnerable populations. services intended to reduce drudgery associated with securing daily domestic water. IYD adapted protection and gender equality in all activities. In this project, IYD will ensure all WASH facilities are free from all forms of discrimination, abuse, and friendly to be used. IYD ensures protecting the rights of beneficiaries and ensures that no harm is caused because of its activities. IYD will make sure that its staff signing the code of conduct. Any case recorded between the staff or beneficiaries will be reported to the Partners. The project will be implemented in coordination with the protection program supported by IYD. The protection staff in IYD will coordinate with WASH program staff to ensure maintenance of WASH facilities. The WASH staff will be provided orientation/training as may be necessary to remain sensitive and be responsive to protection concerns.IYD has dedicated and qualified protection staff to support WASH concerns. IYD will look to integrate suggestions from the local community that improve the implementation of the project and encourages ownership, to ensure that the presence of the organization is not causing harm within the community. These consultations include focus group discussions with different target beneficiaries and key informant interviews (KIIs) with community leaders, local authorities, and other key stakeholders. Through these FGDs and KIIs, IYD will identify needs as highlighted by the various groups and look at addressing these needs through adjustments to the project.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The border between Turkey and Syria closes sometimes which might affect the delivery of some required items into the proposed locations especially the capital equipment such as water pumps and electric generator sets and to mitigate this access restriction, IYD has increased local procurement of project supplies inside Syria, recruitment of staff from inside Syria further access controls on sending supplies to the project area. Therefore, this proposal presumes continued access to the project area or the availability of supplies and equipment in the local market. Sudden clashes or air attacks probably happen during the project period. To mitigate these risks, IYD staff have received mine awareness training which will be repeated on a regular basis during the project period. IYD cross-border security group provides a forum for IYD staff at different levels in the organization structure to analyze security risks and to update the security management plan. This proposal presumes that the local authorities in the targeted area will continue to allow NGOs to implement projects according to humanitarian principles without undue interference in the selection of beneficiaries and staff. IYD will not pay fees or taxes to armed parties to the conflict inside Syria or to ISIL or other sanctioned groups as per the Joint Operating Principles. beneficiaries are made aware that goods and services under this program are coming from IYD rather than from the local authorities. IYD is managing risk and potential fraud by ensuring compliance with and adherence to IYD’s approved and documented policies.
