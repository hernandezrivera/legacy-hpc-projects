Project Summary

Nearly 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through the end of the year, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. The key drivers of acute food insecurity in Somalia include the combined effects of poor and erratic rainfall distribution, flooding and conflict. Moreover, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. It is likely that sustained, large-scale humanitarian food assistance and government support since January 2021 have mitigated the magnitude and severity of food insecurity. Despite minimal damage in early to mid-2021, Desert Locust will continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture availability and crop production across Somalia. Available forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most of the country, which would adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes (FSNAU September 2021)
According to the results of 35 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2021, the overall median Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) in Somalia remained Serious (11.1% weight-for-height z-score). The drivers of acute malnutrition include high morbidity, low immunization, low vitamin-A supplementation, reduced access to milk, and food insecurity. Urgent treatment and nutrition support are required for approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), who will likely face acute malnutrition between August 2021 and July 2022, including 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation.
In response to this, READO aims to strengthen life-saving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on appropriate infant and young child feeding practices in emergency, micronutrient interventions and optimal maternal nutrition. This will be done through the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children 0-59months, multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women and children and infant and young child feeding promotion services among the IDPs in Baidoa, Hudur, Dinsoor, in Bay/Bakool region in Somalia.
In regards to areas and populations of concern, Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. The following are considered areas of concern and are in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions. They currently have or are projected to have a GAM prevalence of Critical, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9 percent or by a mid-upper arm circumference below 125 mm of 10-14.9 percent: Juba Cattle Pastoral, Juba Riverine, Southern Inland Pastoral of Elberde (Bakool), Bay Agropastoral, Baidoa IDPs (Bay).

1. - Projects activities

Provision of IMAM (Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition) with a combination of primary Health care service and out-patient (OTP) therapeutic feeding for the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children aged 0-59 month in the Bay and Bakool Region; the nutrition service will be done through a network of mobile clinics, static OTPs. The supplies will be supplied by pipeline managers' projects as READO will only ensure the frontline service delivery. The system of OTPs is structured on 10 static sites, 10 mobile.(3 fixed OTPs in Bay region (1 in Baidoa relocation center, (2 mobile in Baidoa Rural villages of Awdinle and Lowiile with the coverage Communities), with Integrate EPI services (immunization services delivery) in OTP services as part of the static and mobile nutrition program through health funding. • Provide and integrateNutrition, health and hygiene education (NHHP) education in all static and mobile OTP sites. • Ensure that children have access to vitamin A supplementations and deworming.For PLW, as recommended by WHO, supplementation with iron-folic acid and using singledoseof albendazole (400 mg) • Promote breastfeeding practices among mothers providing IYCF counseling services. • Link SAM case-management to MAM treatment • Ensure a community screening of malnutrition, provide by CHWs and mother-led MUAC for both children 6-59 months and PLW. • Capacity building for the staff with refreshertraining on management and prevention of acute malnutrition and training on IYCF.Community health workers (CHWs) will also be given refresher training on the detection and referral of acute malnutrition, IYCF and NHHP promotion. Finally, mothers would betrained on Mother lead MUAC Screening and provided with MUAC tapes for early detection and referral of malnourished children.Project Indicators• Number of boys/girls (6-59 months) treated for severe acute malnutrition (Target 4,000) •Number of boys/girls (6-59 months) treated for moderate acute malnutrition (Target4,000) • Number of pregnant/lactating women treated for moderate acute malnutrition(Target 4,000)

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exclusion and marginalization: READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups.READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which included who has access to nutrition care, who manages facilities, who is able to influence decisions on the provision of nutrition services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. This information will be considered when determining locations for nutrition interventions. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will provide the local/host community with access to camp nutrition facilities in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic health services and facilities. In addition, READO will consult both displaced and host communities about nutrition needs and assess whether nutrition interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. This will be done through introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access nutrition facilities eg. Mobile nutrition services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting theelderly/persons with disability; community networks). In addition, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to nutrition needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for nutrition center location, design and services. Moreover, READO will engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. READO will alsohave have a proportionate number of female nutrition staff. READO will also establish complaints and response mechanism. READO will use information boards to display Complaint Response Mechanism phone number during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection and at nutrition centers. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaint. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. In turn, the call center will be in charge of calling beneficiaries as part of the monitoring system. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence. READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to nutrition facilities and services are safe. This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installationsor threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. READO will also advocate for install lights outside and on the access route to the facility with the government of South West State Somalia. In addition, READO will ensure that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private. This will be done by establishing an information-sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts are made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided.
