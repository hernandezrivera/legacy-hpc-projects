Project Summary

Grassroots Relief and Development Agency (GREDA) intends to implement sustainable agriculture, fisheries, livestock and honey processing project for sustainable economic recovery and resilience in Western Equatoria State targeting IDPs/returnees in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties because these counties were badly affected by the conflict that erupted in Juba in 2013 and 2016 respectively. The targeted three (3) counties are currently hosting high numbers of IDPs and returnees. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report/records the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are as follows:-
(1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (14,000 people are at crisis level and 7,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure
(2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (29,000 people are at crisis level and
14,000 people at emergency level). 43,000 people are critically food insecure.
(3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760 (16,000 people are at crisis level and 5,000 people at emergency level). 21,000 people are critically food insecure. The average of 60% population in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties are farmers (FAO 2016). The crops grown successfully in these targeted counties include maize, sorghum, beans, pumpkins, okra, tomatoes, groundnuts, and sim-sim. The counties fall within the Green Belt in Western Equatoria State include some of the most fertile lands in the country and farmers in the area can reap two or three harvests per annum from the same plots. In November 2015, Western Equatoria State as a whole experienced one of its worst crop harvests in recent history. The level of food insecurity reached was historical lowest in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties that one person out of four being considered food insecure. The livelihoods inputs/equipment in the conflict affected parts of Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties were looted in several Payams including Bangolo, Medewu, Yeri, Lessi, Dari, Kotobi, Lui, Lozoh Witto Kediba, and Amadi Payams. The signing of the revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCISS) in September 2018 has relatively brought peace in South Sudan including Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. The IDPs and returnees are returning in their original home land. Unfortunately they lost all their agricultural, fisheries, livestock and honey processing inputs/equipment. There is high need to supply them with agricultural tools, improved seeds, and seed processing equipment, fisheries kits, veterinary drugs, cold chain, and honey processing equipment, sun-drying racks, and solar driers, smoking ovens, post-harvest technology trainings on food packing, handling, hygiene and storage to empower 10750 IDPs/returnees and host communities to produce their own food to reduce malnutrition, hunger and gain strength and resiliency against macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks and humanitarian access challenges.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021.Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. These unmet needs increased vulnerability for populations in 2022.More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). Approximately 80 per cent of the total population lives in rural areas and works in the agriculture sector. Poverty rates are very high and subsistence agriculture remains the main source of income for the vast majority of the population. Severe food insecurity is expected at the height of the lean season of 2022 due to the impact of conflict, climatic and economic shocks. Local currency depreciation, inflation, high import and fuel costs, and other factors, are sustaining high staple food prices and limiting economic activity, which in turn restricts household purchasing power. In accordance with Mid-2019 population (NBS) report the populations of the three (3) targeted counties are reported as follows: - (1) Mvolo County Total population is 71,586 (2) Mundri East County total population is 95,873 (3) Mundri West County total population is 46,760. Lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged people and people with disability are vulnerable groups severely affected by food insecurity in this Counties. In Western Equatoria State; Insecurity, poor roads, lack of basic services prevented the deliveries of lifesaving humanitarian relief assistance. Overall food insecurity worsened and some communities were facing catastrophic needs. More children were acutely malnourished than in the past three years. Lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged people and people with disability are vulnerable groups severely affected by food insecurity. Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psychosocial distress. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced Labor and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brinks of mortality especially in urban areas. The high levels of acute food insecurity in the country are driven by conflict, related displacement, low crop production, flooding, diseases and pests, the economic crisis, the effects of COVID-19 limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The continuing currency depreciation and associated impacts on commodity prices and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who rely on markets to purchase food and other basic needs. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2022 (with limited recovery from the previous floods) and humanitarian food assistance much lower than required in the face of increased needs and will certainly result in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods, especially during the lean season. In particular in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties malnutrition and hunger has become the primary causes of many deaths among the Lactating/pregnant mothers, children, old aged people and people with disability,IDPs/returnees and host communities in Western Equatoria State.

2 - Project activities

To ensure successful implementation of this project the following activities are essentially important for the implementation of this project and the activities are as follows: - 1. Recruit qualified 3 agricultural officers, 3 fishery officers, 3 livestock officers as field coordinators (three officer per county) in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties. 2. Carryout community based participatory planning mobilization to assess beneficiaries’ critical needs, priorities and choices through analyzing technical, financial, environmental and social feasibility to ensure beneficiaries understand the project and take ownership of the project to enhance successful implementation and sustainability of the project in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties 3. Procure and distribute assorted agricultural inputs (vegetable,crop,and fishing kits) to 10,650 beneficiaries in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. 4. Setup Kitchen gardens at Nutrition facilities in Mvolo, Mundri East and Mundri West Counties. 5. GREDA animal health workers to carryout vaccination and treatments to 9,300 livestock beneficiaries who are unable to access veterinary drugs to their livestock in Mvolo,Mundri East and West Counties 6. Conduct capacity building and training,asset building and market support targeting 500 beneficiaries in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo counties. 7. Conduct continuous project impact monitoring and evaluations targeting 20502 beneficiaries in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties.
