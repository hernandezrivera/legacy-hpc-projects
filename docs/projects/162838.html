Project Summary

The project will provide the access to basic water services, limited sanitation services and access to hygiene services community in Um Baru, Kornoi and Tina localities in North Darfur through targeted wash interventions.
This action will be guided by Sphere standards and will focus on:
- Increase the availability and the access of safe drinking water;
- increase the number of limited sanitation services;
- improve consciousness and practices thorough Solid waste management and hygiene campaigns.

1 - Needs

Key findings from COOPI KAP surveys conducted in Um Baru include that water availability is always below 15 liters per person per day (9.15 liters). During the dry season the average time to reach a water source is mostly between one and three hours and waiting time at the water source is regularly between one and three hours. Thus, ensuring the provision of water for human consumption and production is a key priority in the identified areas. In the period June – August 2019, 5.8 million individuals are estimated in need of food and livelihoods assistance to mitigate food insecurity. Major concerns exist for the States classified under Phase 3 and above, in particular, South Kordofan, Red Sea and the four Darfur States (Central, North, South and East) which reported a significant increase of food insecure people compared to previous analysis. Despite the satisfactory outcome of the 2018 cropping season, market availability is low, with traders reported to hoard their agricultural produce, regarded as a more reliable form of savings compared to the fast weakening local currency. In addition, foreign currency shortages are seriously constraining availabilities of imported food commodities, mainly wheat; 58% of the households cannot afford the local food basket due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high food prices. Prices of cereals, which started to surge in late 2017 driven by the removal of wheat subsidies, were in July at near-record levels in most markets across the country: prices of millet, mainly grown and consumed in western areas, in Nyala (South Darfur) and Al Fasher (North Darfur) markets, despite the bumper 2018 harvests in the area, were in July– 25% higher than one year earlier and more than twice their levels in October 2017. The current economic crises are projected to persist and will continue to reduce poor household’s purchasing power and limit their access to market foods.A general push towards Return, Recovery and Reintegration activities has been experienced at both local and federal government, as well as UN Agencies and major donors. Over the last few years over 36,966 people have returned to North Darfur, many more are expected to return from Chad (IOM, 2018). On the 31st of May 2017, Chad, Sudan and the UN have signed a tripartite agreement for the voluntary return of refugees from both countries. As of 1st of June 2018, there are over 350,000 Sudanese refugees registered in Chad and returns to North and West Darfur are currently on-going. Between 2014 and 2017, over 230,000 returnees from Chad have been recorded to have settled in Darfur. Since 2015, a total of 101,424 persons returned in North Darfur state. 82,722 are IDP and returnees, 72% of them live in Mellit and El Fasher localities. The remaining 18,792 are returnees and almost all (99%) are settling in Kornoi, Um Baru and Tina localities. As of October 2019, the Displacement Tracking Matrix has identified a total number of 483,790 returnees from internal displacement within 57 localities, pinpointed at 1,060 locations, across six states in Sudan. In terms of locations, North Darfur assessed the greatest number of sites – spanning 853 locations (80% of the total locations visited by IOM).

2 - Activities / Outputs

The project envisioned to support around 35.000 beneficiaries living in villages of Um Baru (25% of the total), 24.000 ben in Kornoi (25%) and 11.000 ben on Tina (25%) for a total of 70.000 beneficiaries; the interventions will target vulnerable people and it will be used with a participatory approach in communitarian environment like schools, health centers, women committees.Result 1: Increase the availability and the access of safe drinking waterActivities: 1) operation, maintenance and rehabilitation of handpumps, 2) mini water yards, 3) boreholes;4) haffirs;5) Promotion of hygiene and sanitation awareness

5 - Environment Marker

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Main project activities including maintenance of water points, installation of mini water yards, and construction of haffirs have minimum environmental risks. Solar pumping system will be piloted for powering the mini water yards instead of diesel generators thus providing a 'clean energy' alternative. COOPI and partners, through their FSL projects will integrate activities like tree planting around the water points to further protect the environment.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The design of project activities was based on needs assessment that reached representatives from all gender groups. Along side technical requirements, the selection of sites for water points will consider the needs of women and girls. The project will also deliberately target the participation of women in all the stages of the project implementation including involvement in decisions making structures like water user committees.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

COOPI and partners will create awareness to the affected population on their rights and potential risks of safeguarding concerns. In addition, all gender and age groups within the affected population will be sensitized on the need and channels for reporting safeguarding concerns. The project partners will position themselves as willing and ready to welcome concerns from beneficiaries through regular interaction and soliciting for feedback. Key project staff involved in the project and in contact with the affected populations will be sensitized on their obligations in ensuring safeguarding of the beneficiaries.
