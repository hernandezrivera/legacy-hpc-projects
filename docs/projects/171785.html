Project Summary

The floods and conflicts in most parts of the South-Central Somalia have triggered huge displacement in 2020, with the volatile protective environment coupled with negative coping strategies, leading to violations of rights.Up to 66 per cent of the population in need in Somalia are children who continue to be exposed to threats of recruitment, abuse, neglect, abduction, exploitation and violence. Hence, 1.88 million children including 10% of children with disabilities need immediate child protection services.
Boys and girls of all ages in Somalia continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights including family separation, sexual and physical violence, killing and maiming, psychosocial distress.
The child protection project will further improve prevention and response to separation of children from their families through identification, family tracing and reunification activities and provision of comprehensive case management and psycho-social support including emergency care management, and referral to foster families in Bardera and Belethawa district. SOVA organization will establish Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) in targeted flood affected areas to support children wellbeing, emotional needs through provision of psychosocial support and recreational activities. SOVA will enhance monitoring of GBV and child protection concerns in the displacement/evacuation areas through community based child protection focal points and local community structures. The local coordination mechanisms will be utilized with aim of improving standard/quality of services and ensuring systematic collection of GBV and Child Protection related data to inform better response and advocacy.
The key focus of this project will be providing Case management services for unaccompanied or separated children (UASC) through immediate response for child and family separation as well as provide equitable, timely and accessible GBV services for women, men, boys and girls through provision of live-saving medical services, and Identification and PSS Counselor training will be conducted to provide counseling training to traumatized women and girls affected by the Confict,floods and Covid-19 pandemic in the targeted project areas, Psychosocial support will be provided to children and caregivers to protect and promote children’s wellbeing and full participation. Psychosocial support will focus on engagement of children, caregivers and families through restoring, strengthening and mobilizing family and community systems by: reducing and preventing harm; strengthening resilience to recover from the impact of the drought and other disasters.
It’s the responsibility of SOVA organization to safeguard children and will make sure our staff, observe do no harm to children, and avoid to expose children to the risk of harm and abuse, and respond timely any concerns about children’s safety within the communities in which they work, are reported to the appropriate authorities and all staff will be signed child protection declaration letters.
SOVA project implementation is guided by Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC),and upholds the right of children and allows them to Participate project implemention,listen children’s views and allow them to fully participate child protection project in order for them feel sense of ownership.
All people with disability like physical and mental disabilities are allowed easily access SOVA protection project and will be given access to our facilities and eliminated barriers faced by persons with disabilities and enabled them full and meaningful participation of project. Community based prevention and response services that address the needs of vulnerable families and survivors of violence are a key priority to SOVA CP and GBV project implementation in 2021 and beyond and want to see the people with disabilities participating in every aspect of the social, professional, economic and political lives in Somalia.

Mitigation Measures

Mitigation measures to exclusion and marginalization based on status (2a): 1) Undertake direct identiﬁcation and registration of beneﬁciaries without use of middle men or gate keepers. 2) Develop and enforce an elaborate selection criteria with priority accorded to most vulnerable households including those from marginalized and minority groups. 3) Adapt the highest levels of transparency in beneﬁciary selection and provide target communities with with timely information on the selection criteria, organisational procedures and processes that affect them. 4) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving different fabrics of target communities thus creating a platform where marginalized communities can share their grievances, if any. 5) Operationalize a project-level hotline where target beneﬁciaries can share views and/or concerns. Mitigation measures for exclusion and marginalization based on gender and age: 1) Collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to inform beneﬁciary selection and maintain gender parity. 2) Ensure that activities are designed in a manner that address the unique needs of women, men, boys, girls and those with special needs. 3) Establish Joint project implementation committees involving youth and women groups from target communities thus creating a platform where youth and women can share their grievances, if any. 4) Undertake direct identiﬁcation and registration of beneﬁciaries without use of middle men or gate keepers to ensure inclusivity regardless of age and gender. 5) Develop and enforce an elaborate selection criteria with priority accorded to most vulnerable groups such as women, boys and girls. Mitigation measures for exposure of beneﬁciaries to violence: 1) Continuously assess local dynamics and make plans accordingly. 2) Ensure that access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly and safe distribution. 3) Undertake program activities during daylight hours, ensuring ample time for beneﬁciaries to go back home before nightfall. 4) Adapt and observe the Do-No-Harm principles at all times to prevent unintended negative consequences. 5) Provide maximum protection to beneﬁciary data and household information to prevent misuse and exposure of target persons to risk.

Please provide further explanation

This proposal is humanitarian in nature focusing on the provision of life-saving shelter assistance to people in humanitarian crisis. However, it also contributes to building the resilience of targeted communities in a number of ways. On the one hand, provision protection in an environment of humanitarian crisis protects beneﬁciaries from harsh climatic conditions, health hazards andSuffering which leads to improvement in their living conditions and chances to access livelihoods. Complementary multi-sectoral interventions such as food aid, WASH and protection are often more effective when combined with protection, leaving IDPs more resilient to future shocks and able to cope with the grim reality of displacement.
