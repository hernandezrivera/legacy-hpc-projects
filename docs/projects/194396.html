Project Summary

Eleven years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, the need for protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance in all challenging catastrophes of drought, flood, conflicts and violence initiated by the communities, land disputes, continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55 per cent of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighbouring countries.
According to assessment did by joint humanitarian unity state, the resulted war which is currently turned into conflicts have devastated all living style and disappeared peace, severe poverty, instability, local economic meltdowns, displacements, formation of too many groups that targets civilians in form of raping women, girls and children, recruiting child boys into military, child abduction, demolition of local educational facilities in the villages in the regions, especially the greater Upper Nile region(e.g. Unity State). As a result of this harsh crisis toward humanity and the innocent people across the country, many became homeless, refugees in the neighboring countries, IDPs fenced in POCs, victims due to loses of family members, fully depending on humanitarian Aids and fundings. This project will be community owned, and everybody will participate in the project cycle. GUNO's approach of operation normally focuses on a person as a whole by providing psychosocial/physical, emotional, economic and social support. There is normally accountability and feedback mechanism in place to ensure that the needs of the affected people are taken into consideration while developing and implementing a project. GUNO had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and the affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups) in the target county. The design of this project is in accordance with the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women, and men of different ages so that no activity will be carried out in isolation or fragmented manner as the proposed project aims to adopt a comprehensive approach based on the participation of all stakeholders. Once a project is approved and funding available, all necessary authorities will be informed, documents will be shared, and meeting will be conducted with community leaders and representatives of all age groups and sex from the affected population to be informed about the project and during implementation information will be passed continually during monthly review/feedback meetings.
