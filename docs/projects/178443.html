Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its 2021 ERL intervention based on the analysis of MSNA data in NW Syrian, need severity for ERL assistance, and key messages of HNO 2021, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and ERL Cluster. The project aims to strengthen access to livelihoods, improve access to basic services and infrastructure, and support efforts of local economy-recovery in addition to support social cohesion for the affected-population, for 33,330 INDV (15,635 M/ 17,695 F) in Aleppo and Idleb. The project will empower youth participation through establishing Youth Groups, whereas the project activities will fall into two main categories as follows:
** Improving access to income-generating opportunities, and to production and economic infrastructure as well as to basic services targeting 18,330 individuals (9,715 F/ 8,615 M), of which 75% are children and youth.
1. BO will rehabilitate 3 local economic infrastructures for 9,000 INDV (~4,200 M, ~4,800 F), and 15 schools (225 classroom) for 6,750 children, 6-17 years old (3,175 B/ 5,575 G). The rehabilitation will also include the drainage systems, access roads, and access to basic utilities targeting 9,000 INDV (~4,200 M, ~4,800 F). Cash for Work modality will be used in the rehabilitation to create 150 short-term income-generating opportunities. The workers will be selected based on clear eligibility and vulnerability criteria.
2. BO will provide 100 hours training on vocational skills, and 20 hours on management and finance skills to 80 youth on the most critical vocational/ skill gaps, that will be identified based on market need assessment, also, will deliver in-kind support to the youth so that the youth accessibility to livelihood opportunities will improved.
3. BO will support 100 entrepreneurship and 100 rural enterprises by grants and productive assets, in addition to provide training on basic marketing, management principles, and cost/ price estimation techniques; that will allow the entrepreneurs to initiate, rehabilitate or expand their enterprises.
** Improving resilience and cohesion for 6,000 INDV (3,180 F, 2,820 M)
4. Absence of banking or credit services is pointed as a major problem for many people created a significant gap in their ability to address their needs. So, BO will establish 3 “village micro-credit associations” to serve as a small window for micro-credit. Bonyan will constitute management committees, formulate rules of operation, establish the capital window of USD10,000, and provide advice in managing the associations.
BO will select the target communities based on scoring and prioritizing system in coordination with ERL Cluster, then will engage the selected community in all phases of the project. Also, BO will ensure that the support will be provided to the most vulnerable people through transparent process based on clear vulnerability criteria (HLP veriﬁcation will be included). Principles of Do No Harm and AAP as well as humanitarian standards are incorporated in the project design and will be ensured during the project timeline. Also, MEAL team of BO will conduct ongoing monitoring of project activities to verify achievement of indicators and to correct any deviation.
The PwD will be involved in all activities as direct beneficiaries in CfW or rehabilitation of infrastructures … etc. Workers with disabilities will be chosen so that they have a mild / moderate degree of disability, so that they can complete tasks that do not require much muscular effort. For example, these people can conduct periodic cleaning campaigns, wiring works for the lighting systems, data collection survey, or sensitization sessions for the community members. On the other hand, PwDs will benefit directly from the improved infrastructures/services including sewage and drainage systems, solar lighting, and rehabilitation of roads.

4 - Needs

HNO2021 indicates that poor economic conditions, physical destruction of infrastructure, and large-scale displacement have led to competition over scarce livelihood opportunities and overburdening available social services.26% of the PiN are PwD, of which 75% are without access to medical, social and economic support, with higher rates of incidence in some areas. The assessment showed that PwDs are vulnerable to exclusion from the humanitarian response, despite their specific or additional needs, due to many environmental factors such as physical and social barriers.The state of disrepair in which basic infrastructure lies after years of destruction continues to hinder the prospects of a resumption of economic activities and increase the poverty scale in which the affected population face. The poor state of basic infrastructure also represents a major impediment to the return of displaced persons to their places of origin, pushing them towards harmful coping strategies, in addition to increasing dependency on humanitarian assistance.The pandemic has been affecting the entire socio-economic system and has led to a dramatic loss of livelihoods across the country and presents an unprecedented challenge to socio-economic resilience. At the end of 2019 around 86% of Syrians lived below the poverty line, and just under 90 % as of mid-August 2020. Before the pandemic, 41.90% of HHs could cover their expenses, while currently only 9.7% can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. About two thirds HHs lost one or more sources of income, and about half lost between 75% to 100% of their previous income.

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about ERL needs of IDPs, Host Community and Returnees (with keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition to collect any other necessary data (i.e. market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries following transparent prosses based on clear vulnerability/ eligibility criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable affected population. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will verify project indicators. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Targeted locations are secure, safe and accessible during implementing the project.- In asset support activities, beneficiaries will not sell assets and they will use it to improve their livelihoods. Risk and Mitigation: Risk 1: Exposure to the risk of theft or kidnapping after receiving cash or asset assistance.Likelihood: low / impact: highMitigation:1- Choose key locations to distribute cash / asset that are safe for all beneficiaries, including women and people with disabilities. 2- Not to publish the information and dates of receipt in the community to people other than the beneficiaries. Risk 2: Selling asset after receiving it by the beneficiaries and not using it to generate income in small enterprises.Likelihood: low / impact: highMitigation:1- Evaluate the beneficiary's situation and ensure his ability to work with the provided equipment.2- Signing contracts with the beneficiaries in the presence of a committee from the local community / local authority to ensure that the asset will not be sold and will be used to generate income only. Risk 3: Escalation of the security situation.Likelihood: Medium / Impact: HighMitigating Measure:1. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment2. Targeted locations are included in de-escalation agreements. In case of unexpected military actions, possible solution is to conduct activities in another locations.
