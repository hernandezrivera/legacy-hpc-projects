Project Summary

This project aims at providing a disability inclusive humanitarian response in Cabo Delgado. People with disabilities are in urgent need of food support, MHPSS and rehabilitation services. Training of mainstream humanitarian actors and government institutions in ‘disability inclusive humanitarian action’ will further contribute to this objective.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The crisis in Cabo Delgado, Mozambique, has rapidly escalated in 2021. Attacks by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) expanded geographically and increased in intensity in 2021, significantly heightening protection risks, especially for people with disabilities. People with disabilities as a target group have been severely underserved in the crises response in Cabo Delgado, especially around technical support in disability mainstreaming and Disability Inclusive Community Development (DICD). The specific gap our proposed project will address is the inclusion of persons with disabilities across all the priority areas we have identified. For the time being, there is no reliable data about how many girls, boys, women and men with disabilities have been affected by the conflict and how they are being reached by humanitarian response. According to our assessment the main identified needs for people with disabilities are: food support, mental health and psychosocial support services, rehabilitation services and training of mainstream humanitarian actors and government institutions in ‘disability inclusive humanitarian action’ to ensure a disability inclusive humanitarian response.Key Target Groups:Girls and women with disabilitiesTotal: 140/ Sex: 100% female / Age: 56 (below 18), 84 (above 18)Boys and men with disabilitiesTotal: 60/ Sex: 100% male / Age: 24 (below 18), 36 (above 18)Humanitarian and government actorsTotal: 40 people/ Sex: 50%f and 50% m/ Age: above 18 (100%)

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Overall, we will apply a ‘Do No Harm’ (DNH) approach across all our project interventions in order to understand and mitigate any risks of harm for our target groups. Light for the World has also conducted a risk assessment to identify potential risks and develop mitigation measures accordingly. The main focus of this project is meaningful access to assistance for particularly vulnerable groups. For the limited scope of this project one of the most vulnerable groups, people with disabilities, have been identified as the main target. The project supports them in accessing different forms of assistance and empowers them to have their voices heard in clusters and also works with humanitarian and government actors to design their humanitarian response in a disability inclusive way.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project was designed based on previous assessments in the region including a Rapid Gender Analysis. During these assessments, both girls, women, boys and men with and without disabilities were consulted on their needs. In addition, the different needs of children and adults were taken into consideration.Light for the World will coordinate with different agencies to support vulnerable target groups in using Linha Verde and ensure accessibility for persons with disabilities. On the project level, we build on our experience from setting up beneficiary feedback loops in previous humanitarian projects. Hence for this project we will also provide for the following along the feedback loop: (1) create channels for solicited and unsolicited feedback (such as disability-friendly complaint boxes in the different women and child-friendly spaces, periodic FGDs/interviews, public/community meetings, coordination meetings with community leaders and government officials); (2) respond to the feedback in real time; and (3) communicate the response back to beneficiaries („closing the feedback loop“). We will work closely with other humanitarian actors and stakeholders to ensure effective referral pathways.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The main objective of the project is to support girls, women, boys and men with disabilities within the humanitarian response. People with disabilities will be identified through our work on Disability Inclusive Community Development, applying a community-based approach of identifying people with disabilities according to the Washington Group Questions and in addition provide further assessment to determine appropriate rehabilitation plans for their individual needs. People with disabilities will further receive food assistance, MHPSS and assistive devices. The project will further provide training and awareness raising to other humanitarian and government actors to achieve a broader disability inclusive humanitarian response.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Light for the World has an environmental policy, which will be taken into consideration throughout the project. No major negative environmental affects are foreseen based on the planned project activities. Nevertheless, we will take into consideration more environmentally-friendly options for transport and green procurement to reduce unnecessary waste.
