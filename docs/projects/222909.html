Project Summary

The proposed intervention is designed to tackle the prevailing protection challenges in four prioritized counties in two states, including Upper Nile (Malakal and Panyikang Counties) and Northern Bahar El Gazal states (Aweil South and Aweil East) through the provision of integrated multisectoral, emergency lifesaving GBV responses services.
ARDF will employ both the GBV Mobile Response Team (MRT) and Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) approaches in the delivery of lifesaving emergency GBV response services to achieve Protection Cluster/GBVAOR objectives PCO 1 & 3, aiming to reach 20,000 vulnerable beneficiaries including refugees, returnees, IDPs, and Host communities. Of this total target beneficiaries, 10,000 are women, 5,000 are Girls, 2,500 are men and 2,500 are boys. In this intervention, ARDF will employ
The efforts will ensure that survivors of sexual and GBV have access to a range of services and information through the following key activities:
1) provision of GBV case management.
2) specialized individual psychosocial support through Integrated Therapeutic Techniques;
3) Cash for protection under case management;
4) Distribution of dignity kits;
5) Support for survivors to access multisectoral services through safe referrals.
6) Updating and development of GBV referral pathways and service mapping in operational areas;
7) Awareness-raising sessions targeting women, men, boys, and girls on GBV risks, PSEA, and MHM together with WASH teams, available response services, and timely reporting;
8) Deployment of the GBV Mobile Response Team (MRT);
9) Prevention initiatives through the enhancing Girls’ Agency and Gender Equality (ENGAGE) program;
10) Safe motherhood programming;
11) capacity building for GBV service providers and government social welfare staff;
12) implementation of community feedback mechanisms.
The proposed intervention will also address the specific needs of adolescent girls and people with disabilities by ensuring they have access to GBV information and case management services. Direct beneficiaries will include women and girls most at risk of GBV. Community men and boys will also benefit from the project through GBV awareness and capacity-building initiatives, serving as allies to promote positive social norms against GBV in their communities, and supporting survivors in accessing GBV services. ARDF will regularly report the implementation of GBV interventions on 5W's and resources mobilized on Financial Tracking Services.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains challenging due to access constraints, inter-communal violence, armed group activity, cattle raiding, political instability, economic fragility, flooding, and poor infrastructure, and has mostly affected vulnerable groups, especially women and girls are at risk of SGBV and other forms of GBV. This situation is further exacerbated by the influx of refugees and returnees from the ongoing Sudan crisis. GBV remains widespread in South Sudan, due to entrenched harmful social norms and practices, systematic gender inequality as well and conflict-related sexual violence due to the prolonged nature of the conflict severely impacting the safety and protection of women and girls' rights.According to UNFPA- 2023 study the Sudd Institute indicated that 50-70% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months, while the GBVIMS 2024 half-year report noted physical violence as the most reported GBV incident, mainly connected with intimate partner violence which counting 36% of incidents, followed by sexual violence (rape 24% and sexual assault 6% which constitutes 30% of all reported incidents in the first half of 2024 were mainly reported in prioritized states. IOM’s 2023 Intersectoral Need Assessment findings suggested women and girls are at an increased risk of GBV, including rape with a significant number of respondents avoiding water points and firewood collection as a result. According to IRNA Reports 9th August 2024 and 12 December 2023 conducted in Aweil South and Aweil East respectively, women and girls are at risk of SGBV as they walk long distances to fetch water and firewood for domestic purposes. The report also highlighted a lack of comprehensive GBV response services due to access impediments, a lack of community awareness on GBV prevention and mitigation, and the community practices of harmful traditions such as early/forced marriage. The report further highlighted that GBV is more prevalent within households and mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. Many of the community members who participated in the key informant interviews and FGD acknowledged that the prevalence of GBV was high in their communities as they mentioned to have witnessed GBV-related incidents perpetrated almost daily. This is largely attributed to the deep-rooted cultural beliefs, attitudes, and perceptions about women and girls that contribute significantly to unequal power dynamics among men and women, hence resulting in normalizing violence against women within the communities. In addition, fear of retaliation through revenge killings was mentioned to be a major barrier to accessing the available services by survivors. This calls for emergency response intervention. In Upper Nile, especially in Malakal and Panyikang Counties, protection concerns remain significant due to the recurrent inter-communal conflict, flooding, and the influx of Returnees and Refugee from Sudan crises placing Women, girls, and children in extreme psychological distress, sexual gender-based violence (SGBV), and other abuses. Clinical management of rape (CMR) services remain a critical gap in the target locations due to limited access to health services and skilled providers. The recent inter-agency assessment conducted in Malakal County across the western bank in areas of Lelo Payam and Warajok Bom on 17th October 2024, highlighted that women and girls are at high risk of SGBV due to the presence of cantonment area for armed men.The Protection Cluster’s Monitoring Spotlight report (September 2024) indicated that in Upper Nile state and other states such as Western Bahr El-Ghazal, Unity, Jonglei, and Central Equatoria) 42% identified armed actors, 28% of business owners, and 28% of community members as main perpetrators of sexual violence.
