End Date

31/12/2020

Project Summary

This grant will address the priority needs of approximately 12,000 people in the targeted location both from host community , IDPs as well as returnees .The project will enhance access to quality, timely protection services for women ,men ,girls and boys , including provision of comprehensive case management for vulnerable children and UASC and those with protection concerns , it will as well provide psychosocial services to children whose psychosocial wellbeing is severely affected by emergencies and those at risk of gender based violence. Prevention and mitigation of risk to the affect community will be one of the top priorities .The project will also create a conducive environment which will enable the affected population to find durable solution to their day to day problems in other hand several assessment will be carry out to identify the gaps that need urgent interventions and will be shared with the clusters and other humanitarian partners. There will be regular monitoring to evaluate the project and share the progress and challenges with donors during the project review.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to be a protracted emergencies running into its sixth year and with the promising of peace that is witnessed in most part of the country many people have started coming back into their areas of origin, as per DRC Aburoc IDPs site movement trend tracking between 7 to 10 house holds weekly arrived in Aburoc from Sudan’s refugees camps, some people settled in the IDPs site Aburoc, while others were on transit to the villages surrounding the IDPs site some returnees proceeds direct to Kodok and Lul Payam of Fashoda County .The returnees are likely to face several challenges that include inadequate services for them to start their life since their shelters and livelihood were destroyed during the crisis, the destruction of social network and disruption of daily routine for children jeopardize their resilience and greatly increase the likelihood for them to experience prolonged psychosocial distress. WOCO has been implementing CPiE project supported by UNICEF 2018 to 2019 in Fashoda but due funding gap PSS activities have been left unsupported, Currently more 3000 children who were participating in WOCO’s CFSs are still in dire need of focused and non-focused PSS intervention.The population of Aburoc IDPs including the host community according to the last head county was approximately 8,672 individuals and these number has increased rapidly to17,860 due the influx of these returnees from Sudan.Kodok and Lul population according to the WFP food distribution registration is 10,817 individuals, Lul population including the host community is 8,704.and there is still increasing trend in spontaneous movement of returnees from refugees camps and those in the PoC to their areas of origin. Intention survey conducted in Malakal PoC in 2019 indicated that some IDPs are interested to return to their area of origin which include Fashoda . De-militarization of river side and cantonment exercise which is a key component in achieving a revitalized peace agreement is anticipated to attract large number of returnees. WOCO has been tracking the movement of children who traveled with their caregivers, cases of separated and missing children have been reported by the returnees. Case of missing children (cross border) have also been reported by the some IDPS in Aburoc and the possible location is Leeri refugee camp in Southern Khordofan (Sudan), WOCO’s current FTR case load contain 345 cases of UASC including missing children with some cases of high protection concern which are in need of urgent intervention to avoid lose of connection between the children and their primary care givers given the fact of fragility of the country. Community in Aburoc have raised a concern of the increasing instances of child labour mainly affecting adolescent girls exposing them to various form of GBV. Another protection concern which is on alarming rate is Child marriage and early pregnancy, despite the effort of protection partners in addressing some of these issues, the economical constraints and lack of information particular among the returnees families still remain a strong driving factor to exploitation and exposure of children to early marriage. Children continue to be forcibly recruited while also suffering from other grave child rights violations. Cases of CAAFAG were reported in Fashoda some of these children reintegrated themselves voluntarily into the community without undergoing through comprehensive reintegration process, though there was some changes observed in their behavior, much need to be done to change the mentality and provide a sustainable reintegration program for these children ,comprehensive approaches are required to identify and release those who are reported to be in the military IO barracks. In addition, cases of to the above mentioned protection concern the returnees are likely to be exposed to ERWs and UXOs which are prevalent in the locations they are planning to settle

2 - Activities

- Provide case management services which include Registration ,follow up and reunification ,referral to the services providers base on need.- Establishment of child friendly spaces ,training of the CP staff, animators and case workers on PSS, case management and CPiE and conduct out reach activities to reach children who can not access the CFS including caregivers support sessions.- Child protection awareness raising and prevention messaging .-Strengthening the existing community based child protection committees to mitigate child protection including child help desk.- Child protection monitoring ,coordination and assessment.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

As a humanitarian organization WOCO has the humanitarian imperative to support affected population’s in accordance with International humanitarian law and humanitarian principles. Several principles will underpin and guide our actions to ensure our rights are legally recognized. We will uphold the right of our beneficiaries to participate in all aspect of the project by setting up accountability mechanisms. We shall ensure the participation of women, older persons, adolescents and children as well as other persons with specific protection needs. We shall also communicate in a transparent manner about our activities and consult with communities and their leaders on decisions that affect them, while also respecting the confidentiality of personal information. To ensure equality and non-discrimination while supporting beneficiaries without discrimination of gender, tribe, gender sex or religion. In accordance to the principle of Centrality of Protection set out in the IASC Statement we recognize the complementary nature of the different mandates and roles of humanitarian actors and agencies. With a situation as complex in Fashoda County , we shall enhance collaboration among humanitarian, peacekeeping, human rights, political, and development actors.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The major beneficiaries of the project are the IDPs and the returnees who are in dire need of protection and other essential services .This project is designed in a way that all the vulnerable population are well covered and have access to services . WOCO will liaise with CBCPNs and local authorities to ensure that services are directed to the right beneficiaries . The project will ensure Points of service delivery that can be accessed by all. Child friendly and women friendly spaces are accessible for targeted children including children with disabilities. In terms of information sharing WOCO will use community members to translate in their local languages to ensure everybody gets the messages, on where and how to access services, services this be done through posters with pictures and translated to them in local language.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will focus on building the capacity of the local authority and community representative and network to continue advocating for services on behalf of their community and mitigate issues related to rights violation,the organization will work hand in hand with the line ministries to ensure that national laws and policies that protect children and women right are disseminated and implemented issues of children associated with arm forces will be coordinated with the government and the concern agencies and ensure that they are released and reintegrated into the community and enjoy their right of education.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is dedicated in responding to the protection needs of IDPS and Returnee children, adolescents and women affected by emergency in Upper Nile State Fashoda County. This is in line with the Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan and also in line with the South Sudan Regional Refugee Response Plan. Through our outlined activities, we seek enhance coordination within government institutions, at national, state and local levels. Secondly, we aim to provide conditions enabling safe, voluntary and durable Solutions to Displacement. We continue support the provision of critical protection related services and prevent risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience through provision of comprehensive case management including for UASC and use of CBI for vulnerable children. This can also include service mapping , establishing referral pathways strengthening the capacity and resilience of children and communities to prevent and respond to violence through community based mechanisms, ensuring increased capacity of communities to prevent separation of children from their families, to protect children from physical injuries, recruitment, and to provide psycho social support services. We also aim to work in close collaboration with other sectors by integrating child protection into education, WASH, FSL, Nutrition and Health while working in partnership with other local NGOs through case management referrals

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

With the political dimension and Revitalization Peace Agreement the movement of the returnees will continue to be fluid as they explore option for return, relocation of local integration. Especially at the onset, the returnees may return temporarily or seasonally to form and access assistance and to check the status of their properties or evaluate their options regarding housing access to livelihood services, schools to their children and medical services. The environment is sufficiently safe and stable for the IDP and the returnees who are coming for resettlement because of the presence of humanitarian services, UNMISS that is monitoring the peace progress and the proposed cantonment in these areas though they will be succumbed to the exposed or presence of UXOs and ERW in the area including destructive of shelters and livelihood which were destroyed to scratch. WOCO with the accredited team in place will continue raising awareness on Mine Risk Education to inform the returnees and the community on contaminated and hazardous.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

WOCO believes in ownership as a key approach to sustainability of projects based on acceptable community owned and supported sustainability plans. We work within the principle that “Exit is not about us”. Since the response is important to the community the way in which we communicate about exit is vital. We will ensure rigorous sustainable programming capacity development of local community structures and local stakeholders. Capacity development will not only focus on needs but also participatory engagement with beneficiaries, leadership, strategy, resource mobilization, and financial management. We believe in influencing wider stakeholders at community, district and national level. We believe that advocacy is part of the shift towards sustainability through building broader coalitions for change with wider stakeholders at community and district including families and children. It is these stakeholders who are left behind they are the legacy. Consequently, ensuring a systematic learning, documentation and sharing of good practice. As part of its healthy exit, WOCO have invested in analyzing and documenting project learning, documenting achievements, lessons and best practices that can be shared with communities to ensure a broader base of information once the project was closed. The final step towards an exit involves closure of activities as well as sharing off final reports. WOCO will develop several processes and forms to assist them exit well and capture learning (such as Partnership Completion Report; Letter of Recommendation; Partnership Completion Final Call, and Partnership Completion six-month Follow-up Call).

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Leadership/Governance:The organization will be committed and accountable to the affected populations by ensuring that there is an internal control mechanism (policies and bi-laws) to guide the project staff and ensure that they are empowered to implement activities in the projects coverage areas. Staff will also be briefed on the Humanitarian Code of Conduct to ensure commitment to standards and appropriate interaction with beneficiaries.Transparency, Feedback and complaints: The organization will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and its affected populations over information provision. The Organization will consult the beneficiaries (men and women; boys and girls) on a regular basis to seek their views on how to improve policies and practice in programming, ensuring information sharing, recognizing communication and reporting channels and that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined to express their views on the project implementation. Participation, design, monitoring and evaluation: The organization will engage affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes and will establish clear guidelines and practices toengage beneficiaries and ensure that the most marginalized population are represented and exercise influence. The projects monitoring and evaluation will involve men, women, boys and girls from among the beneficiaries in a creative way. Information sharing and complaint mechanism will be strengthened to enable them express their views on the project implementation.
