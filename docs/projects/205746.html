Project Summary

To respond to the needs, PAH will conduct short and long-term interventions across the country, both static and emergency especially in the areas where cross-sectoral needs are dire. Shelter/NFI projects may complement other responses and PAH will always work to ensure synergies with its own projects or other partners’ programs. PAH will use both in-kind (light and standard kits distribution) and CVA modalities to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the newly displaced persons and the ones with vulnerability, returnees, and others living in conditions of high vulnerability in coordination with the protection team. PAH will strengthen its CVA interventions in 2024 to approximately 30% of the project. It will be implemented in areas of returns with functional and accessible markets. In-kind responses will be conducted through mobile and static teams in close coordination with cluster partners. PAH will use the established humanitarian hubs for logistic coordination, information centres, and organizing responses to reach remote locations. Where such a modality is not available PAH will use its own procurement and transportation solutions, especially for last-mile transportation. PAH plans to implement both static and mobile responses. Concerning the beneficiaries breakdown, while planning the responses PAH will follow OCHA population aggregation.
PAH is an experienced organization with a broad portfolio of Shelter/NFI projects successfully implemented across the country. In the provision of transitional and essential shelter kits, PAH and protection partners will conduct in-depth HLP rights analysis using the unified cluster tools/questionnaires in the areas of shelter construction.
Land tenure consultation meetings and Community sensitization meetings on HLP rights will be organized throughout the project cycle. Such meetings will result in identification and agreements on land ownership for the shelter construction and provision of essential shelter kits. Through the support of HLP focal point, UNHCR, IOM, and NRC (protection and HLP partners), the beneficiaries will be supported to obtain communal land tenure documents in order to avoid the risk of being forcibly evicted.
PAH will consider the formation of shelter committees in the areas of project implementation. The committee will include representatives from local authorities, traditional chiefs/elders, women, and youth to ensure equal rights and representation to every age and gender group. These committees will work throughout the project cycle to monitor the implementation progress and give complaints/feedback. Before the construction of any transitional shelter, PAH will require the beneficiary to provide proof of land ownership with legal documents with close consultation from local chiefs and the Ministry of Land and Housing especially from urban settlements to avoid escalation of land-related disputes.
In areas/plots of collective centers, PAH will sign agreements (MoUs) with the Ministry of Water and physical Infrastructures, Housing and Land utilization, and Land tenure/local authorities to guarantee a minimum period of land use to avoid eviction of beneficiaries. Wherever possible, PAH will a set follow-up strategy to support in identifying land ownership.
PAH will continue to identify HLP-related issues throughout the project phases among the affected population and do refer them to the HLP focal point and other stakeholders.
As part of flood resilience and sustainability of the project, PAH plans the construction of transitional and flood resilience shelters for both returnees and flood-affected population.
PAH implements multisector responses to ensure covering the diversified needs of affected population targeting 41,667 individuals.

1. Needs

About 20 % of the population of South Sudan, i.e. 2.3 million people, are currently internally displaced, while over 2.3 million South Sudanese are still located in neighboring countries including Sudan. Continued conflict and instability combined with recurrent flooding are the main drivers of large-scale displacement within the country. The conflict in Sudan is generating an unprecedented wave of returns, with people crossing the border presenting critical immediate basic needs while stuck in congested transit places. It may also generate tensions over land tenure issues and a wave of economically weak people settling in urban centers.In 2023, approximately 8.9 million people, including 24% women, 15% people living with disabilities and 4.7 million children, need multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance. In 2023, approximately 3 million people in South Sudan will struggle to meet their basic needs in SNFI due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from the ongoing floods and conflict. The increment is attributed to the current economic crisis in the country, new displacements and ongoing floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity, part of Western Bahr el Ghazel and Western Equatoria States. Of the total population in need of SNFI assistance, 58%t are IDPs, 33% are returnees and 12% are vulnerable host community members.The continued displacement and increased movement of people, combined with sustained flooding and economic crisis, have further depleted natural resources such as timber and grass, leading to competition for the limited available resources. The competition usually generates conflict and localized violence, leading to multiple displacements. The markets lack essential items or have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required items for a safe and dignified life.A lack of shelter presents health and protection risks and poses a negative impact on the economic situation of the affected households. Exposure to extreme weather conditions is linked to the increased morbidity rates, mainly due to malaria, easy transmission of airborne diseases as a result of congestion, and other risks associated with the lack of proper shelter. The lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who usually go to the bushes to collect the increasingly scarce construction materials, exposes them to GBV. The lack of privacy also prevents individuals from living a dignified life and engaging in income-generating activities. Continued displacement and relocation of the communities from high-risk areas exacerbate conflict related to housing, land and property ownership among the displaced, returnees and host communities.As a result of these compounding factors, the affected people have limited coping strategies. Some adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as engaging in exploitative work, commercial sex, stealing and living under trees or in open shelters, exposing them to health and protection risks. Timely provision of SNFI to at least 3 million people across 79 counties remains critical to enable them to live a dignified life.Inadequate or lack of shelter negatively affects physical and mental health, education, livelihoods and food security, which can generate increased protection risks, particularly for women and children. People with inadequate or no shelter, particularly women, girls and PWD, experience greater levels of violence than those with minimal shelter, which expose them to negative coping strategies. Essential SNFI are required for people’s wellbeing, health and dignity – to have a restful night’s sleep, meet their personal hygiene needs, prepare and consume food and provide the necessary levels of thermal comfort and needed privacy.

2. Project activities

1. PAH will provide emergency shelter kits and critical NFIs materials in areas of newly displaced people due to localized outbreaks of sub-national violence, areas of returns and where there are protracted displaced populations with protection risks and new vulnerabilities especially people living with disabilities in the prioritized locations. PAH will request items from core pipeline for distribution through coordination with cluster and SFPs. PAH resources will be used to supplement in kind distributions through the use of local functional markets. 2. PAH will provide emergency shelter and critical NFIs kits to newly displaced population with floods. PAH designs a separate emergency shelter and live-saving NFIs kits targeting only newly floods affected population. The floods kits will be distributed based on cluster coordination team recommendation. Unified Cluster separate reporting templates will be used during floods responses in close collaboration with cluster partners and SFPs. 3. PAH will provide in-kind essential shelter and NFIs kits for replacement. to improve living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and host communities/affected non-displaced especially people living with disabilities, who are unable to meet their S/NFI needs. PAH will increase long-term responses in coordination with WASH, FSL and Protection clusters in delivering multi-sectoral responses. PAH will collaborate with CCCM partners to increase informal and formal settlement planning in 2024. All distributions will be coordinated and jointly conducted with other cluster sectors. PAH will establish CFMs in all the project locations to ensure AAP throughout the project cycle. Agreed and clear targeting criteria will be communicated to the leaders, beneficiaries. PAH team will disaggregate the population by, gender, age, vulnerability and disability. Where necessary, PAH will provide the transportation of items from core pipeline to the project side. 4. PAH will provide Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) modality to respond to emergency shelter and essential NFI needs for the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees, in coordinated manner: PAH will conduct rapid market assessment and protection risk analysis to determine the delivery mechanism before the provision of CVA; a) CVA interventions will be implemented in areas with functional and accessible markets b) JMMI reports published by REACH will be used for monthly price monitoring supported by IA-CWG partners. PAH plans to increase the CVA response modality to 30% where there will be multi-purpose cash assistance of unconditional with both restricted and unrestricted delivery mechanisms. CVA team will encourage the usage of local available materials for shelter construction and repair in traditional manner to increase resilience and coping mechanism. PAH is active member of IA-CWG and JMMI South Sudan. 5. PAH plans construction of transitional and flood resilience shelters for both returnees and flood affected population. PAH plans piloting construction of transitional and floods resilience shelters in the areas of returns, with involvement of women, men and people living with disabilities to promote community participation and empowerment. PAH will strongly strengthen and advocate for flood-resilient response where that is the main driver of displacement and vulnerability in order to decrease year over year humanitarian needs. 6. PAH will also follow HLP due diligence protocols and conduct area specific conflict analysis to undertake and determine ownership of land so as to avoid legitimizing illegal occupancy or marginalizing real owners of land and property which may exacerbate conflict. There will be community sensitization and awareness on HLP supported by protection cluster and HLP partners. MEAL plan will be developed for the project to determine the type of data that need to be collected to track project progress.
