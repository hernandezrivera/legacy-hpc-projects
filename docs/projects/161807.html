Project Name

Start Date

End Date

Project Summary

Through CfW CARE will identify beneficiaries and engage them to provide labour for various construction or rehabilitation of basic social infrastructures. Both skilled and unskilled labour will be provided by the beneficiaries, in a case where the beneficiaries do not have the necessary skills or expertise to engage, CARE will source key skilled labour within the community to complete the work. The project will target a minimum of 60% women and engage them in the various activities. Priority will be given to women and youths then men in all the activities.
CARE will also engage beneficiaries in various Income Generating Activities to revitalise either their lost livelihoods or support them to engage in new activities. Emphasis will be paid specially on activities that support women empowerment with skills and business management trainings provided to ensure that
Targets
Cash for Work – 5,000 HHs (Borno – 2,500, Yobe – 2,500) Targeting at least 50% Women to work for 15 days a month for a minimum of 4 hours/day on the community, social infrastructure. From the 5,000 HHs any adult HH member is eligible to take part in the monthly work or represent his/her family in completing their work.
VSLA – CARE will facilitate the set-up and training of 200 VSLA groups (Borno – 100, Yobe – 100) and strengthen these groups.
IGAs – a total of 2,000 HHs (Borno – 1000, Yobe – 1000) will be supported with kits for various activities such as Petty Trade, Food Processing and other small businesses which would be implemented either in groups or individually. Skills acquisition trainings will be conducted by CARE International to all the 2,000 HHs on the different IGAs.
Social Infrastructure – 1 School and 1 Community Abattoir in Dikwa will be renovated.

1 - Needs

Due to the insurgency social structures like the schools were destroyed which have resulted in denying children access to learning in conducive environments. These schools lack basic infrastructure such as furniture, dilapidated walls, doors and windows. As a priority concern raised by the community, CARE will rehabilitate the dilapidated school structures and ensure that completed sets of furniture are provided to these classrooms. Another area of concern is the lack of a community town hall which could serve as a safe or meeting space especially for women. Community meetings are usually held at the houses of the community leaders usually in open spaces which is quite normal for the men for women they require a level of privacy usually during meetings conducted specifically for them or other kinds of forums for women only. In the Community Based Participatory Planning (CBPP) conducted by CARE livelihoods team in Dikwa, it was discovered that the main source of income/livelihoods before the insurgency in Dikwa was agriculture (crop production). Lately, due to the increased attacks of insurgents there has been a lot of restrictions which has led to various restrictions of farming activities. These range from community members attacked or killed while trying to access their lands to the military digging trenches around the community and there by restricting movements. Women and the elderly are among the worst affected.In many cases women and men have resorted to engaging in various coping strategies such as daily work or petty trading to cover food gaps and other basic needs usually supported by NGOs or the government. In this situation Income Generating Activities presents a great opportunity to the identified beneficiaries to be trained and supported with start-up kits to engage in an activity they are comfortable with.The findings also show a lack of knowledge or skills as it relates income generating activities among beneficiaries to successfully start-up and manage businesses.

2 - Activities

Cash for Work (CfW)This activity will target 5,000 HHs (2,500 – Borno, 2,500 – Yobe) with women accounting to at least 50% of the project participants in which both skilled, semi-skilled and unskilled workers will be engaged or used to provide labour for the various community social infrastructures that have been identified. CARE will identify and engage a Financial Service Provided (FSP) to pay a direct cash transfer in envelope to the beneficiaries after each month’s work. This will include mapping-out the locations of the Community Infrastructure Maintenance/Rehabilitation School Renovation - Rehabilitation and renovation of schools in Dikwa is amongst one of the identified needs as it relates community social infrastructure assets rehabilitation. As a prioritised concern CARE will focus on one school to ensure that the class blocks, roofing, floors, doors, windows are repaired with full furniture installed in the classrooms for a conducive learning environment. Abattoir - Initially Dikwa had a Central abattoir close to the market area serving the entire communities within the Central area. The structure has been destroyed entirely during the invasion of the insurgents’ years ago. As a priority need CARE will provide materials required to construct/renovate the abattoir. Additionally, for hygiene purpose a wash borehole will be drilled at the site to ensure there is access to water for the various activities Income Generating Activities Tailoring: 100 HHs will be supported to engage in tailoring. The project will target potential women and youths from the household to be supported with various kits such as sewing materials, threads, needles and lubricating oil etc. for start-up. Women will constitute 60% of project participants Food Processing: CARE will identify 80 HHs women specifically in Dikwa and Yusufari to be supported with inputs and on various food processing activities which could range from: selling of beans cake (kosai), masa, chin-chin etc. Grinding Mills: 140 HHs will be grouped into 7 units to be supported with grinding mills as an activity to reinforce their household assets and capabilities collectively. With a communal location already in place for the grinding mills, CARE will renovate the facility and distribute the beneficiaries evenly to the groups and then provide grinding mills alongside other kits to ensure smooth running of mills by the selected beneficiaries. Women will constitute 50% of the project participants Meat Processing: 40 HHs will be paired in 20 groups and supported with start-up kits to commence various meat processing activities such as: Suya, Balangu, Kilishi etc. Restaurant: CARE will identify 60 HHs to be grouped in 10 and will support them with the required materials and inputs to start-up a small restaurant in Dikwa. Women will constitute over 80% of project participants Soap Making: CARE will identify, train and provide start-up kits to 80 women HHs for soap making. Petty Trade: CARE will identify 1,400 HHs to be supported with various inputs/materials in different petty trading activities and more than 80% of beneficiaries for the activity is expected to women. Training: CARE will pay careful attention on training beneficiaries in two different categories which will complement each other;1. Business Management Training: A business management training will be conducted to the beneficiaries on general business management and entrepreneurship skills 2. Skills Acquisition/Vocational Training: Beneficiaries will be paired and linked with already established/skilled workers in the various fields of IGAs to learn directly from them and receive subsequent mentorship support.3. The trainings are expected to empower both women, men, girls and boys VSLA: CARE will use its expertise to set-up and train 200 VSLA Groups (Borno – 100, Yobe – 100) and women will constitute more than 80% of VSLA groups.
