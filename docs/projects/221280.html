Project Summary

This project seeks to increase 5000 girls regular school attendance in the Northwest region of Cameroon by increasing their dignity, safety and rights in their MHM through improving the four pillars of MHM in the school enviroment which include (1) Social support through community mobilisation to change attitudes, beliefs and norms and end MHM stigma and discrimination (2) Knowledge and skills among girls, teachers, parents and community leaders to permit school girls learn to manage their menstruation (3) facilities and services by providing and ensuring genderresponsive WASH facilities (toilets, water, change space, disposal pits for used sanitary pads etc.) in schools and (4) materials and supplies for girls to access available MHM products supplied in schools, to permit school girls use appropriate and affordable menstrual materials. The achievement of these 4 pillars of MHM in schools through the following outcomes 1) Increased skills among teachers and engage in positive practices for menstrual hygiene in the school environment. 2) Increased uptake of quality MHM services and products among 5000 school adolescents. 3.) Increased participation of stakeholders (Students, national authorities, communities, including men / boys, religious and traditional leaders in demystifying the taboo on MHM with respect to gender and human rights). These will create a friendly MHM school environment that increase girls’ safety, dignity and rights hence encourage girls’ retention in schools and contributing to girls’ health and well being outcomes. The activities will focus on; capacity building, of stakeholders, awareness creation on MHM, lobbying and advocacy, procurement and distribution of MHM equipment and monitoring and evaluation. The project will work closely with UNFPA, UN Women, Ministry of Secondary Education, Ministry of Water and Energy Resources, Ministry Women Empowerment and Family, local NGOs, traditional/religious leaders, Municipal and regional councillors in the Southwest Cameroon.
The project duration is 1 year targeting 5000 girls in 10 school in the Northwest Region with a proposed budget of One hundred and nine million two hundred and fifty two thousands three hundred and fifty frs (198,641 USD)

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Statement of the problem or challenges to address the view described in the Reference Terms.The challenges of adolescent MHM in schools are center on ;- Limited knowledge by stakeholders (students, teachers, parents, religious/traditional leaders)- Poor MHM services in schools- Lack of MHM materials in school infirmairies- Deteriorated and ill equiped WASH facilitiesAdolescent Sexual and Reproductive Health Rights (ASRHR) continue to be a challenge following the taboos, misconceptions or myths associated with it as parents, teachers, traditional/religious settings including health facility personnel. They belief that ASRHR is all about sex. E.g. parents see it a taboo to talk about sex to their kids, religious institutions see it immoral to discuss sex with adolescents, teachers shy away or avoid the topic and often have limited knowledge about SRHR, Government entities particulalry local councils do not see it a development need and ignore it, deteriorated school toilets, no water, soap and schools that afford an infirmary barely have a nurse for its functioning. Health services are not adolescent friendly and always supicious of adolescent being pregnant who come there for services. These limitations leave adolescents to struggle alone with no accurate information on ASRHR challenges and the onset of menstruation (menarche) makes matters worst for adolescent girls as it is often a surprise and sometimes girls are scared or worried and think they are sick and many at times do not know who to ask for advice. They often resort to tear any material they find at home and use not caring about its poor hygenic conditions and often will experience blood leaks in schools and sometimes acompanied by stomach aches, cramps, nausea, etc. Many of the adolescents become withdrawn, stay out of classroom while in school or put their heads on the bench in class and will not respond to teachers trying to wake them up and out of fraustration the teachers often throw insults on them which cause some to absent from schools during period. Even when girls use good menstrual materials, they are worried about blood stains on their clothes while some permanently drop from school. This writer experienced all of the above « as an adolescent and during one of my period flow, the teacher said ‘surely you are pregnant’ and my mother asked me same particular when i experienced nusea and vomited. » The above challenges about adolescent MHM is in line with a joint WSSCC-UN Women research in Cameroon 2018 revealed 29.7% absentism among school girls during their menstruation. Also, the research showed that the types of toilets used by women and girls did not always guarantee their privacy and safety and did not allow proper menstrual hygiene management. The crisis in the Northwest and Southwest caused many schools to close down and those functional experienced slow investments in WASH activities and many toilets deteriorated terribly affecting girls to change during period flows and requires serious emergency intervention targeting the four pillars (Social support, Knowledge/skills, Facilities/services and materials/supplies through increased teachers skills and engage in positive practices for menstrual hygiene in the school environment, Increased uptake of quality MHM services and products among 5000 school adolescents and Increased access to information and participation among stakeholders to break taboos on MHM with respect to dignity and human rights by Students, national authorities, communities, including men / boys, religious and traditional leaders. The strategies to facilitatat achievement of results include Commuity based approached, Survivor centered approach, collaboration through MOUs, Advocacy/lobbying, Capacity building, procurement , awareness raising, Feedback and complaint mechanisms etc.
