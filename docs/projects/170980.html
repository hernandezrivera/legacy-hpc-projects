Project Summary

The proposed project intends to support access of marginalized and underserved communities in East Jerusalem, H2 Area, and Area C to specialized preventative and responsive CP mechanisms, despite occupation and COVID-19 hinderance. For this purpose, Tdh and PCC have joined in partnership to be able to deliver a comprehensive package of intervention to respond to the complexity of the targeted areas of intervention and high interlinked Child protection and GBV risks. The proposed interventions equip the targeted communities to have solid community protection mechanism that are functional and sustained, especially when formal protection mechanism is lacking due to security conditions in area C, halt in PA coordination, and/ or lockdown that is imposed due to COVID 19. The logic of intervention integrates responding to immediate and emergency needs of children, survivors and their communities affected by conflict and pandemic-related violation of their rights, enabling access to specialized child protection and GBV services, while creating conditions for sustainability and preparedness at community level. Specifically, the action strategically pursues three strategic axes of intervention with the following expected results:
1- The availability of qualitative preventative CP and GBV services at community level is expanded. The result will be sought by through providing technical support to community-based specialized and non-specialized child protection and GBV workforce; capacity building of CBOs and communities in awareness raising on CP and GBV concerns; providing mental health and psychosocial support in alignment with the partners’ MHPSS framework; prioritizing and mainstreaming among CBO service providers safeguarding and PSEA, Provision of orientations sessions on children’s legal rights and how to support families and children in conflict and contact with the law, with a focus on children in conflict with Israeli authorities.
2-The capacities of Community-based service providers (CBOs) to provide specialized, principled, and confidential CP and GBV response is strengthened and community-based preparedness to reduce the conflict-related protection and GBV risks is reinforced. This includes capacity building on case management processes, safe identification and referral of CP and GBV cases, on procedural guidelines for GBV cases in the justice spheres, increase preparedness through pre-deployment of emergency and dignity kits at community-level.
3-Specialized support to critical and high-risk children and survivors through specialized counselling and case management is remotely performed and available, despite lack of access and limited availability of services.
The action inscribes directly contributes to the achievement of the SRP Objective 1 of the Opt HRP and specifically to the Protection Cluster objective 2. It also corresponds to the needs identified ICCG action plan for H2 area.
In alignment with the recently conducted needs assessment and the Protection Cluster needs, the partners will target 8 highly affected communities in East Jerusalem, H2 area and Area C in Hebron and Bethlehem. The locations under this intervention have been selected based on consultations, assessed needs, and strategic and trustworthy relationship with the targeted CBOs in these areas.
Around 4764 individuals (1710 girls, 1710 boys, 994 women and 350 men) will directly benefit of the action. Children and survivors exposed to violations of their rights and limited access to services, this includes GBV survivors, children at psychological distress, child laborer’s, children facing or exposed to child marriage, marginalized children, children with disabilities (expected to be up to 5% of the case load), children at risk of violence, children at risk of drop out, and other vulnerabilities. Other 17,080 expected to indirectly benefit from the protective systems the project aims at developing and consolidate within the communities.

1 - Needs

The concept is based on the findings and analysis of a Rapid Need Assessment (RNA) conducted by the project partner; the Palestinian Counseling Center (PCC) in 14 Marginalized Areas in Jerusalem, West Bank and Area C during the COVID-19 Pandemic in May 2020 and it is systematically reassessed against the monitoring of the context and of pre-existing needs and protection concerns affecting hard to reach and underserved communities. The findings are corroborated by the preliminary findings of a need assessment that Tdh is conducting at global level, with a focus on OPT, with a child-participatory methodology (the report will be released in October). A more in-depth assessment is planned to be conducted at the start of the project, especially for H2 and Hebron area as it was not included in the RNA. The effect of the pandemic has exacerbated an ongoing chronic humanitarian crisis, shaped already by conflict and military occupation.; a situation brought about by a lack of compliance of IHL and IHRL that is progressively deteriorating, even more. Children continue to be exposed to high levels of violence, are denied their fundamental rights, and are unable to live in safety and dignity. Recurring violence and detentions, as well as the high risk of direct conflict exposure with Israeli Forces and settlers have a harsh impact on their psychological well-being and safe development. The daily impact of living in hot spot settings implies enormous levels of fear and anxiety affecting adult caregivers, child protection workers and, overall, the vulnerable population in the communities, leading to negative coping mechanism and reduced levels of resilience. During the period March-August 2020, Israeli Authorities increased the demolitions of Palestinian homes further exposing many to risks associated with the pandemic. (OCHA,2020). The RNA conducted highlights that the selected areas have higher levels of vulnerability particularly during the COVID 19 pandemic, the latter exacerbating pre-existing protection concerns. Moreover, UNOCHA Humanitarian dashboard ( Feb 2020) on H2 area shows that 69% of households have experienced settlers’ attacks or intimidation in the past 3 years, 75% of households have had their house raided by the Israeli Forces since October 2015. and 21% of households indicated that one or more of their children were arrested by the Israeli Forces since October 2015, half were arrested more than once. Indeed, children are constantly being exposed to chronic episodes of occupation-related violence, in addition to the general fear of contracting the virus. The NA identified the selected areas as highly suffering from service delivery gaps pertaining in particular to psychosocial services and from recurrent cases of violence and abuse, especially against children and women. Boys are the ones more exposed to contact with Israeli forces given, among others, the enactment of manhood and masculinity concepts to confront the occupying power. Girls are exposed to conflict-related violence (including conflict-shaped gender-based violence), psychosocial stressors to their wellbeing and social norms that perpetuate patterns of exposition to violence, lack of access to services, stigma and marginalization. According to recent data (PCBS 2019), 29 per cent of Palestinian women in Palestine, or nearly one in three, has reported psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic violence by their husbands at least once during the preceding 12 months. The pandemic has increased exponentially gender-based violence cases, and specifically Intimate Partner Violence and Domestic Violence, and for increased risk to psychosocial wellbeing and protection of most marginalized communities, further exposed to hardship, lack of service provision, restriction of movements and loss of livelihood means. UN Women rapid assessment (2020) on impact of COVID-19 Crisis on the access of women victims and survivors of violence to justice and security services in Palestine has highlighted that “service providers are struggling to meet the emerging needs of women at risk of violence due to limited resources…. [and] many civil society and governmental organizations are struggling to turn into virtual working mode due to lack of the necessary infrastructure, including equipment’s, and lack of the technical know-how”. At the same time, the Family and Juvenile Protection Unit at the Palestinian Civil Police and the Public Prosecutors Office have reported a dramatic decrease in cases related to domestic violence which can be attributed to movement restrictions which limit women’s ability to report abuse. Furthermore, the capacities of community-based mechanisms and service providers to cope with the overlapping stressors of the pandemic and the socio-political situation in Jerusalem, H2 area and Area C in the West Bank is scattered and weakened and can no longer fully benefit from the critical protection services delivered by the Family Protection Unit and other duty bearers even prior to the pandemic. This highlights the need to invest in community-based protection mechanisms to ensure enhanced access of children and women to protection services during the pandemic and beyond.

2 - Activities

Activity1: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programmes aimed at supporting vulnerable children and families who have been affected by conflict related violence and violations and violence in households ; Activity 2: Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support; Activity 3: Awareness raising for vulnerable communities on CP issues; Activity 4: capacity building for non-child protection professionals ; Activity 5: capacity building for CP service providers; Activity 6: Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS) for adults and youth; Activity 7: Awareness raising on accessing legal aid in context of IHL/IHRL violations and displacement; Activity 8: Protection Mainstreaming: Activities aimed at strengthening protection throughout the humanitarian response, including in the context of the COVID-19 emergency; Activity 9: Self-care for service providers; Activity 10: Mental health and psychosocial services for GBV survivors; Activity 11: Distribution of dignity kits; Activity 12: Support referrals for GBV cases; Activity 12: Awareness raising for persons at risk of GBV; Activity 13 : GBV: Capacity building for GBV service providers; Activity 14: GBV: Capacity building for humanitarian actors (non-GBV specialist); Activity 15: GBV: Advocacy on gaps in GBV prevention and response. The project will start with an in-depth assessment of the targeted communities, especially for H2 and Hebron communities. The project will support 8 CBOs located in East Jerusalem , H2 and Area C. (Madaa Creative Center in Silwan, Mt. of Olives Women Centre, One Hand Society in Issawiyieh, JCAN in Kufur Aqab, Beit Ommar and Masafer Yatta in Hebron and 2 CBO's in H2 area. The partners will work directly with CBOs management to steer their capacities to lead child protection and GBV response within their communities. Each capacity building package will mainstream inclusion of people with disabilities, women and girls. Providing concrete examples and analysis on how to ensure meaningful and equal access. Also, the partners will strategically focus on consolidating the capacities of specialized child protection and GBV workers based at community level (counsellors). Each counsellor (4) will supervise the service provision of 2 CBOs, by providing support to community-based volunteers (2 per CBOs) and technical assistance. Volunteers are selected on the basis of their capacities, commitment, relevant studies on social working and reputation/mobilization within the respective communities. The partners will support the CBOs in performing appropriate selection of the counsellors and volunteers, as per project’s rational that proactively invest on community-based resources and protection mechanisms. Following that, all along the project, the partners will ensure technical supervision, guidance and continuous capacity building on the job of counsellors and volunteers and will supply with specialized remote assistance with most at need children and survivors.The design and implementation of the above-mentioned activities will be carried out ensuring age, gender, diversity and cultural appropriate standards. A solid M&E plan, designed in alignment with Tdh’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Transparency, Quality and Accountability Policy, will ensure monitoring of equal access to proposed services, allow identification and addressing of protection concerns, enable effective and inclusive feedback and complaints mechanisms. The project lays on a strong community-based approach that works with and through communities to enhance relevancy of the action, accountability, participation and community’s agency all along the project cycle. The partners pursue a human right-based approach, integrating systematically in the action analysis of gender norms, power imbalances, patterns of discrimination for vulnerable groups and people with disabilities among others and actively seeking and assessing inclusive, gender-responsive and human-rights responsive outcomes and processes. Tdh and PCC will apply at all times its Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP) as well as its Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies and will ensure partners are applying them while carrying out all project activities. The project also aims to support the CBOs in developing their own safeguarding policies to ensure protection of their constituents beyond the project duration.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

During the project implementation, Tdh and PCC will guarantee with the targeted CBOs that project beneficiaries’ feedback, suggestions and recommendations are encouraged and collected, to ensure activities remain relevant and beneficial. Beneficiaries, including children will also be engaged in the action's regular monitoring through feedback sessions and project activities, as well as regular satisfaction surveys. Complaint/ feedback boxes will be accessible in different locations where the activities will take place. A phone number will be widely disseminated to target populations to ensure that they feel comfortable filing a complaint and that persons with limited mobility due to physical disability, COVID 19 movement restrictions, or any other reason, have equal access to feedback mechanisms. Therefore, at the beginning of project implementation, project partners and targeted CBOs will agree together on the process for complaint mechanism, taking into consideration the beneficiaries’ feedback in its design, to make it more child friendly, gender sensitive and inclusive . Complaints will be tracked and logged by dedicated and trained focal point in a confidential manner. All complaints will be handled efficiently and confidentially by Tdh accountability team, and lessons learned are generated regularly based on complaints received. Complaints and feedback channels are assessed on a periodic basic to remain relevant and accessible to all the affected population. Tdh has a very comprehensive Child Safeguarding policy (CSP), PSEA policy, Code of Conduct and confidential mechanisms in place for reporting and response. Every Tdh staff , partner and volunteers will be trained on the CSP, PSEA and the Code of Conduct and sign the policies. Satisfaction surveys are a part of the service delivery mechanism of the proposed project and periodical consultative meetings with beneficiaries will be organized to create a safe environment for them to express their feedback.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project design has considered the safety and the dignity of beneficiaries as a first priority, given the very specific group it covers children particularly and directly affected by conflict related violence and children and adults affected by GBV. Indeed, the project has been conceptualized according to the DNH framework looking at the intergroup relations and intergroup conflict, within a deep analysis of the community dynamics of the targeted communities and its very specific local context. Specific methodologies within the service provision delivery, mainly legal counselling and PSS services, have been integrated to avoid re-victimization of children and survivors, including capacity building, support activities and awareness to families, to community members and to child protection workers. All staff involved in the proposed project, including partner organizations’ staff, will be trained on protection and gender mainstreaming as well as Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Package (CSP, PSEA, DSMW and GCC) to promote best practices and to ensure that no harm is resulting from Tdh’s activities and staff Additionally, preventive and responsive protection mechanisms will be also applied following a multidimensional holistic approach. It will also include awareness-raising and community-based integration activities

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of the equity, under this project, boys, girls, women and men will equally participate and benefit from the proposed activities according to their assessed needs. Tdh will also provide equal access to services to all identified children and survivors, including persons with disabilities but also taking into account the gender differences (for instance girls exposed to conflict related violence, harassment and those dealing with concepts of safety and honor). The project will also target equally all people of concern without discrimination. The project will also use an AGD lens, to collect and analyze disaggregated data, and ensure that Tdh G&D Marker is implemented and is taken into account in the MEAL plans in compliance with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines. Gender balanced participation will be specifically monitored through M&E diversity, gender and age disaggregated tools and indicators in order to quickly identify possible participation and protection concerns related to activities and take consequent measures to promote safe and dignified access. Dedicated spaces and moments will allow free expression, participation and the possibility to provide feedbacks for adolescents’ girls and women and persons with disability.The partners align with Tdh Gender and Disability policy that informs the program in all its stages together with the IASC guidelines on inclusion of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian Action. In accordance with Tdh Gender and disability policy, the proposed action sits at level three of the G&D scale, namely Gender and Diversity responsive. Specifically, the implemented approaches aims at proactively overcome existing biases, discriminations, barriers, taking into account different needs, opportunities, interests for the targeted population in the program design. The partners expect reaching out The partners M&E is designed to analyze access, meaningful participation and accountability across diversity breakdowns. The findings systematically inform the action, that will be adjusted to remove potential barriers, enhance participation and interact with discriminatory dynamics, ultimately aiming at transformative change. Concretely, the partners will ensure that systematic inclusive needs assessment and needs observation is done, taking into consideration the voice and needs of people with disabilities and people with specific needs. The capacity building package on child protection, safeguarding PSEA mainstreams inclusion to ensure appropriate capacities are developed within the targeted communities and among community-based workers. Feedback and complaints mechanisms are also designed to be inclusive across gender, age and diversity. Priority will be given to children, caregivers, survivors marginalized and discriminated, disabled in accessing services and participate meaningfully to the proposed activities. Activity locations will be assessed against inclusivity and accessibility standards and remedial actions will be taken to enhance compliance with Tdh policies and IASC guidelines.
