Project Summary

Alignment with the Protection Sector Objective: 1,2 & 3, this project seeks to Strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to promote meaningful access to information, opportunities, and services for older persons, persons with disabilities, and women and children at heightened risk, including through enhanced participation of refugees in the humanitarian response.
As the Age & Disability specialized organization, CBM Global Disability Inclusion (CBMG) and it’s partner Centre for Disability in Development (CDD) will implement a comprehensive strategy to develop the advance level capacity of Protection Actors including field-level staff and Authorities which include training on accessibility and accessibility audit, removing barriers, ensuring meaningful participation of persons with disabilities, ensuring sex, age, disability, and diversity data (SADD), disability-inclusive GBV programming, Gender and Disability intersections, and Disability Inclusive Project Cycle Management while the basic level training have done by the last six years. The Inclusive Protection will practice by mainstreaming actors after the advance level capacity building.
Hosting roundtable meetings on the operationalization of IASC guidelines and Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older Persons and Persons with Disabilities, implementing four must-do actions, producing good practice documentation, IEC material, video publication, and conducting on-sight field level disability mainstreaming guidance for the Protection Sector as well as the Authorities (RRRC, CIC, District, Upazila administration and different sectors of ISCGs). Onsite visits will be conducted with protection actors to showcase accessible shelter models in Rohingya and Host communities to ensure accessibility and safety of camp shelters while mitigating injury risks. This project will also promote disability inclusion through establishing and empowering (10) disability Self Help Groups (SHGs) in the Ukhia host communities (Haldia Palong, Ratna Palong, Raja Palong) and (05) Disability Support Committees (DSCs) in five camps (8W, 8E,13, 18 and 19) through training on advocacy and rights, SHG/DSC concepts, leadership development Humanitarian architecture, accessibility, monitoring and evaluation and through conducting monthly group meetings and exposure visits. CBMG and partner CDD will actively work with SHG/DSCs to advocate for inclusive practices, claiming their rights to meaningful access to humanitarian services including protection services while brokering relationships between SHG/DSCs with mainstream actors to promote the participation of persons with disabilities in Humanitarian project design, implementation, monitoring and community disability awareness sessions. A Baseline study on the status of SHG/DSCs will be done.
Community awareness-raising initiatives will be done on Disability and Gender intersections, GBV targeting men, women, boys, and girls to address stigma against persons with disabilities while ensuring their inclusion in protection programs. Awareness raising will also be done through 16 days of activism against gender-based violence and the International Day of Persons with Disabilities commemoration.
The project implementation team will be comprised of Humanitarian Disability Inclusion Advisor (partial):1, Lead of the project (Partial):1, Disability Inclusion Coordinator :1, Disability Inclusion Officers (responsible for SHG/DSCs identification/formation of SHG/DSCs in camps and host communities, capacity building activities, provision of disability technical advisory support including onsite technical support): 2, Disability Rights Advocate Officer:1 (from an established Organization of Persons with Disabilities responsible for SHG/DSCs engagement), Humanitarian Financial Coordinator (Partial): 1 (responsible for maintaining project accounts): 1, and Support staff (monitoring and evaluation officer, capturing learnings) :1.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"In 2017, a significant influx of Rohingya refugees into Bangladesh occurred due to a violent and brutal crackdown by the Myanmar military in Rakhine State. The Rohingya, an ethnic and religious minority in Myanmar, faced persecution and atrocities, including mass killings, sexual violence, and widespread displacement from their homes. Fleeing for their lives, hundreds of thousands of Rohingya sought refuge in Bangladesh, crossing the border in search of safety and protection. Since 2017, a total of 962,416 Rohingya have been residing in various camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. Within the Rohingya refugee camps in Bangladesh, people with disabilities encountered significant challenges due to the camps' constrained infrastructure and resources. People with disabilities often had limited access to essential services such as healthcare, education, and livelihood opportunities. Many lacked proper rehabilitation care, which led to dependence on family members and restricted their participation in camp activities. While community awareness regarding the rights of people with disabilities in the camp has improved, there remains a need for inclusivity across most services. Regrettably, the engagement and active participation of individuals with disabilities remain restricted. The Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (ADINA) assessment in Rohingya camps by REACH found 12% with disabilities in the Rohingya camps. The rapid assistive technology assessment (rATA) study showed 1% met assistive device needs, 51% had unmet needs, and 48% lacked such products. The Rohingya crisis has strained resources and services in the host communities of Bangladesh, leading to competition for resources, environmental degradation, and economic challenges. The situation of people with disabilities in the host communities’ mirrors that of the Rohingya camps. However, the presence of organizations in the host community is notably scarce compared to the Rohingya camps. Rehabilitation services are severely lacking in the host communities, with only CBMG & and CDD actively addressing community-based Rehabilitation & Disability inclusion in the host communities. To meet the existing gaps CBMG-CDD has been implementing Rehabilitation and mainstreaming activities under the Rohingya Response Programme, this is an ongoing program since December 2017 which has been adapting to the changing context and needs, to which we now have two key strategic pillars (i) Rehabilitation (ii) Disability mainstreaming. There are, however, still significant gaps as revealed by a number of rapid assessments that have been conducted by different organizations. Taking into account the lessons learned over the past two years, CBMG and CDD have made some adjustments to the proposed interventions. In light of the transition of the ADWG, the project will collaborate closely with the Age, Gender, and Diversity Advisor of ISCG to ensure that the humanitarian response to the Rohingya crisis is inclusive for individuals of all genders, and ages, including those with disabilities and older persons. This approach aligns with the Protection Sector's Protection Mainstreaming and Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) Strategy. Over the past two years, CBMG & CDD have enhanced the capacity of SHGs & and DSCs. In this phase, the project will implement an advanced-level capacity development program for these groups and actively involve them in action-oriented activities. Moreover, this phase will utilize the community mobilizers more effectively in the Rehabilitation service delivery. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project is well aligned with the rationalization principles, in that it adopts a multi-sectorial approach which will entail active involvement with the ISCG engaging Humanitarian agencies operational in both Refugee and Host communities as well as the Local Authorities to mainstream disability. Project activities which include on-site technical support and capacity building of SHGs/DSCs and brokering of relationships with mainstream agencies will be well coordinated with the agencies in the camp and Host communities. The capacity building of SHGs/DSCs to advocate for their rights to meaningful participation in the response will influence disability inclusion in the long-term creating structures and systems to ensure representation of people with disabilities in Local government committees and various project committees to promote their rights by themselves for accessing different services. CBMG-CDD will work to ensure continuity of mainstreaming, influencing, advocacy, and technical support interventions. Through the project guidance notes, tip sheets, training modules, and IEC material will be developed and shared with sectors. CBMG-CDD will participate in ISCG coordination, PWG meetings, and Local Authority CiC meetings to ensure smooth coordination of activities in the host and refugee communities. The project will develop a risk matrix to identify, analyze, and mitigate against likely risks. The project will share monthly monitoring and project update reports with the Protection Sector Working Group.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"CBMG collaborated with Self-Help Groups and Disability Support Committees for insights during project design. Proposed interventions align with input from participants with disabilities. At least 12% of project participants will have disabilities, identified through prevalence surveys using WGSS-E. A conservative 2024 target was set, aiming to surpass it through improved data collection. A GEDSI action plan, using WGSS-E, will guide inclusivity.SHGs and DSCs will be strengthened, ensuring at least 50% persons with disabilities. Regular meetings, training, and linkages with CBMG/CDD-supported OPDs will empower these groups. Implementing partners will receive disability inclusion training for accessible activities and modified facilities.Persons with disabilities will be referred to CDD for rehabilitation and Assistive Technology. Information will be shared in accessible formats, and the project will use technology and accommodations for inclusivity. Rehabilitation services will be offered closer to communities.Awareness sessions on disability and prevention will combat attitudinal barriers at the community level, fostering an inclusive environment. The goal is active participation and contribution from people with disabilities to community development."

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

"CBMG-CDD will ensure accountability to affected populations by establishing inclusive complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms that proactively capture systematic feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly. The iFRM will follow the following five steps. Step 1: Embed inclusive feedback & and reporting (iFRM) mechanism in all new projects.Step 2: Analyze the community profile and local context.Step 3: Consult with communities.Step 4: Decide on the most appropriate feedback and reporting channels.Step 5: Develop a plan of action for handling feedback and training staff. CBMG-CDD will ensure that women, men, girls, and boys receiving humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of the project and that they have a basic right to participate in the decisions that affect their lives, receive the information they need to make informed decisions, and to complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or has unwelcomed consequences"" "
