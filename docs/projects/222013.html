Project Summary

Current WASH infrastructure in Leer and Mayendit north counties is unable to provide adequate WASH services (water, safe disposal of faeces and hygiene promotion) due to increase in population, risking increased outbreaks of water borne diseases including acute watery diarrhea, cholera, and malnutrition. Inadequate WASH is a major cause of diseases in Leer and Mayendit counties and improving sanitation through hygiene promotion is known to have a significant beneficial impact on health in households. In Leer and Mayendit counties there are IDPs and host communities without good sanitation because it was scanty from the beginning, or it was destroyed by conflicts and flooding. There is need for maintenance of hygienic conditions, through improved availability of latrines, safe water source, waste disposal, to raise awareness on water and sanitation related health/hygiene issues, especially to raise awareness of water quality in order to successfully distribute Homebase care kits, dignity kits to the deserving girls and women, and to reduce the risk of cholera outbreak, and other related diseases. The project aims to upgrade 7 -functioning boreholes to water yards, Drill and equip boreholes, Construction of 5 protected shallow wells and reparation of 3 mini-water yards in several Payams of Leer and Mayendit north Counties so that the IDPs, the returnees and un-displaced population can access water at an average of 20liters/person/per day which is average per the UNHCR and SPHERE standards but is far better than the current average usage 10lts/person/day from protected sources, and use of alternate unsafe surface sources according to IPC data (July to September 2024), IRNA September reports Leer & Mayendit, where on average 54% of the community are accessing safe water in some payams while others having surface water as the only source. Some of the displaced population has been forced to live within hosting population and IDP camps with very limited WASH infrastructure resulting to increased open defecation, overuse of functional boreholes and mini-water yards, institutional and shared family latrines etc. The project envisages to conduct hygiene promotion to influence behavior change from harmful practices that encourage spread of diseases, by involving women, men, boys and girls, and government line ministries personnel, through community hygiene promotion approaches, water and sanitation management committees, through involving all the stakeholders in the populations that includes people living with disabilities and the elderly. A total of 13,040 people (women, men, boys and girls) will benefit through provision of access to safe adequate water by upgrading the boreholes and reparation of mini water yards, construction of protected shallow wells that are flood resilience and improve sanitation through construction of flood resilience gender sensitive latrines and hygiene promotion awareness creation. UNIDOR has been able to demonstrate that hygiene and sanitation awareness coupled with the installation of improved water facilities can trigger an improvement in attitudes towards hygiene and sanitation, good health, among the communities in Pariang, Mayendit, Koch, Leer, Rubkona, and Panynjial Counties in partnership with UNICEF, IOM, SSHF. UNIDOR programing does conduct GBV safety audit before the construction and rehabilitation of water points, institutional latrines, creating awareness about hygiene and sanitation in advance of the implementation of distribution of WASH NFIs and construction of WASH infrastructure. This approach often guarantees a more enthusiastic participation of women, girls and boys, which leads to a higher level of acceptance and ownership by a larger group of community members and supports greater sustainability of the program by reinforcing the link between water, sanitation and hygiene.This project aims to enable the community access WASH services while prioritizing the safety of women, girls.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to be affected by flood and economic disasters in some areas and the threat of conflict in others. In Leer and Mayendit north counties Unity State, this situation coupled with high levels of poverty, high returns of refugees due to the Sudan conflict, destroyed infrastructure, food insecurity and flooding, has caused mass displacement of people (women, men, boys, girls) who are residing in IDP camps and within the receiving host communities. These vulnerable IDPs, returnees, host populations continue to suffer more burden due to inadequate and disproportional WASH infrastructure considering the SPHERE, WHO and UNHCR guidelines. This climatic, economic and political crisis has negatively affected the already poor to access basic water, sanitation and hygiene services. According to HNO 2024, communities were hit hard by the intensified conflict in Sudan and another 6th year of major flooding. An estimated 5.01 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2025 as a result of poor or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. According to (FSNMS round 30), IPC data( July to September 2024) and September 2024 Leer & Mayendit IRNA reports, , only 54% of the population reported having water from a protected source, however, the situation varies from one Payam to another, others having surface water as the only source, an issue that is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities and the elderly. Difficulty in securing safe water is only part of the issue; extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that in some Payams eg Dablual, Kuerdung 98% of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. Surface water is often untreated prior to consumption and this, along with the widespread practice of open defecation, creates a substantial public health risk, especially during floods. 18.3% of population affected by acute watery diarrhea. Limited access to water is also compounded by 18% households reporting having at least one container to safely store water (FSNMS Round 30), September 2024 IRNA reports and IPC data (July to September 2024). Protection concerns are also present when accessing water points, with 78% of households, reporting they felt unsafe while collecting water especially at the flooded hand pump sites. Some women and girls through FGD reported avoiding water points due to GBV concerns. 74% of household at national level practice open defecation, its more worse in flood affected areas. In certain states, such as Unity, some counties the percentage of households accessing a latrine is consistently below 10%, with open defecation being the norm (FSNMS Round 30) IPC data( July to September 2024), and September 2024 IRNA reports. Lack of access to soap is one of the main barriers hindering personal hygiene in Leer and Mayendit counties. Poor personal hygiene contributes to the spreading of water-related diseases, including diarrheal diseases. Poor access to WASH services and goods combined with high levels of food insecurity has a detrimental impact on the health of the most vulnerable people (women, men, boys, girls), as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition. According to IPC data ( July to September 2024). The nutrition situation will deteriorated more, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap

2. Project activities

Upgrading of boreholes to mini water yards, Drilling of boreholes and equiping, construction of flood resilience protected shallow wells, construction and rehabilitation of flood resilience gender sensitive latrines, Installation of handwashing facilities at key public facilities and areas (Nutritional facilities, entry places to IDPs settlements, densely populated areas, schools, health facilities etc.). Training of line ministries and handing over the facilities to the communities. Training of Community hygiene Promoters (CHPs) Training of community health worker/volunteers/Nutrition worker on WASH minimum package. Provision of Home Base Care kits, Hygiene standard kit items, dignity items distributed to women and girls as planned in the project. Hygiene promotion messaging and awareness creation campaigns. Equipping Nutrition/institutional facilities with WASH Minimum package for functional Water supply, sanitation system – waste management and disinfection of surfaces with HTH (Stabilization centers, OTP, TSFP).Equipping institutions i.e., Schools, and Nutrition facilities with WASH Minimum package for functional Water supply, sanitation system and hygiene promotion, waste management and disinfection of surfaces with HTH (Stabilization centers, OTP, TSFP), market places, densely populated areas, garbage sites etc, for prevention and protection against cholera. Access to sanitation facilities for IDPs, densely populated areas- markets. Access to safe, adequate water to (IDPs, Returnee’s areas, hosting communities)
