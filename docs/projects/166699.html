Project Summary

The COVID-19 addendum to the Drought Response Programme in Dry Regions of
Zimbabwe was necessitated by the emergence of cases of COVID-19 in the country
which has a probability of escalating as foreign based nationals trickle back, rates of
testing nationwide remain low, quarantine centers continue to register high porosity with
detainees with unknown statuses escaping and local transmissions begin to be
registered. As of 28 May 2020 Zimbabwe had registered a cumulative 149 cases of
COVID-19 with most of them among returning citizens undergoing mandatory testing on
return. However local transmissions have been recorded across provinces and contact
tracing is difficult due to the informal nature of the economy. Responses in the addendum
would aim at enlightening communities to ensure households and individuals take
necessary measures to minimize their risk of contracting the virus and in cases where
some are already affected they also take precautions to prevent spread through the
community. The government has recently made announcements pointing to a reopening
of the economy and loosening of lockdown measures which sees industries opening,
schools preparing for a phased opening and travel restrictions relaxed as authorisation
requirements for travel were relaxed. This increases the risk of local transmission and
would require individuals and families to be on high alert to prevent contracting the virus.
The response would seek to strengthen existing government structures through trainings
on prevention and handling of COVID-19 for district and community level stakeholders.
Community level trainings include village health workers, local clinic staff, local police
personnel, extension staff and community leaders who play influential roles in their
societies. Resources will also be availed to ensure there is adequate handwashing at
potential hotspot sites like townships, schools, health facilities, water sources and other
community service centers where gatherings are unavoidable. In addition to work by the
various community level stakeholders in educating communities, awareness campaigns
by mobile teams will be conducted and posters with messages in appropriate languages
will be used across all the districts. The deteriorating economic situation coupled with
restricted activity has potential to exacerbate cases of domestic violence as well as
sexual exploitation and abuse and the training packages will also specifically cover
issues to do with prevention and handling of the cases. For the quick flow of information,

1 - Activities

The project activities include: - Training for district level stakeholders. This focuses on the District Water Supply and Sanitation Sub-committee (DWSSC) which has representation from ministries covering thematic areas of health, education, local governance, water supply and public works. These would be involved in training for community level stakeholders as well as in the mobile awareness campaigns. - Training of community level stakeholders (village health workers, local health facility staff, school authorities,local police personnel, traditional and elected leaders). Trainings focus on capacitating them in terms of preventing spread of COVID-19, educating communities on prevention, monitoring of unavoidable gatherings like funerals, as well as reporting on activities conducted and suspicious cases - Installation of handwashing facilities and provision of detergents/sanitisers at identified hotspot areas (townships, water sources, schools, health facilities, meeting points). - Putting up posters in hotspot areas in appropriate languages -Awareness campaigns focussing on COVID-19, prevention of domestic violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Loudspeakers will be used by teams that will be driving through the communities emphasizing on need for self protection by individuals, avoidance of public gatherings and observing social distancing. - Monitoring and surveillance through a digital reporting system -Procurement and distribution of PPE for project stakeholders

2 - Indicators

Indicators for the project include the following: - Proportion of people reporting improved knowledge on COVID-19 disease. - Number of villages covered by mobile awareness campaigns. - Number of hotspot areas with installed handwashing facilities disaggregated by type of service center. - Number of people trained on COVID-19 and other cross cutting issues
