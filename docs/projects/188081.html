Project Summary

Oxfam will support the resilience building in the crisis-affected agricultural communities through rebuilding their absorptive, adaptive and transformative capacities to address future risks, fragilities and uncertainties. While the project will help the communities to address their immediate and short-run needs through the project duration for 2 years, it will help them to recover their resilience pathways. In other words, building the links between emergency and development phases. The project will rely mainly on the existing systems, knowledge and capacities to make positive change that will foster positive transition. In full community participatory way, Oxfam will identify the critical risks and concerns and the main causes of vulnerabilities in the agricultural communities and the existing capacities and plans to address them immediately, both in the short and long terms.
While Oxfam will target 15,000 most vulnerable HHs, by providing them with the needed assets like agricultural and livestock kits, it will also help enhancing the community’s capacity as a whole. This will be done through rehabilitation of irrigation system and bakeries. Also, by building the community mechanism and advocating with different stakeholders to change the market environment to be more suitable for women assess and participation, and rehabilitation of the irrigation channels through labour-intensive schemes when feasible.
Besides, the project will link the most vulnerable and willing families with the market systems in a sustainable way by supporting market driven income-generating activities (IGAs), including well-adapted vocational training. This will come after performing a thorough market and livelihood assessments in order to identify the existing opportunities and capacities in the local market systems.
The project will be implemented in full gender and climate sensitive manner; therefore, gender aspects will be mainstreamed into all the project aspects in order to encourage women economic empowerment and participation in the communities decisions. Risks and vulnerabilities related to climate change will be prioritized and addressed.
The geographical targeting will be done after close coordination with other humanitarian actors in order to ensure the highest possible level of complementarity and avoid overlapping.
Oxfam’s resilience approach is more central to our humanitarian and recovery work to be used as an overarching framework in addressing the scope of varied needs that now span from lifesaving to early recovery. The resilience approach framed around building people’s and their communities’ absorptive, adaptive, and transformative capacities are meant to be utilized to bring more cohesion of our activities across different social processes. This has resulted in the integration of our WASH and Food and Economic Security programming under Country Goal.
Resilience programming can only be delivered through a one programme approach that enables us to address the immediate impacts of shocks and crises as well as the systemic causes that generate acute vulnerability.
Oxfam pursues a One Program Approach, a comprehensive, integrated framework that brings together our humanitarian, development and influencing work to make a joined-up system capable of addressing current crises while delivering deep, systemic and long-term change at scale.

4 - Needs

Based on the upcoming years 22-23,the FSA Sector has seen an increase in the severity of needs, especially regarding the lack of food& agriculture-based livelihoods.It also promotes enhanced livelihoods and resilience programming and advocates for a smooth and area-based transition from pure emergency to early recovery and livelihoods to ensure integration with ongoing resilience actions. Oxfam targeted areas under the SS 3-4. As per the HNO,Aleppo PiN 2,826,869, RD 1,828,302,Hama 1,103,817, DZ 653,174, Dar'a 736,169.The key needs of the areas are an agricultural business,irrigation canal rehabilitation,Income generating activities,skill development training, bakeries rehabilitation, small scale food production.Oxfam assessments performed in Aleppo, RD&DZ,Oxfam found that farmers have lost their capacities to restart and sustain their agricultural activities due to the destruction of the irrigation systems, very high prices of farming inputs; livestock and fodder, high prices&shortage of fuel, suspension of the government subsidies programs due to lack of resources,insufficient access to lands which in many cases are controlled by military groups.Therefore, farmers have relied on several negative coping strategies that will worsen the agriculture sector more in the near future, like drilling wells close to the Euphrates the matter that cause salination, selling of their livestock, and migrating to urban areas looking for livelihood opportunities and joining military groups.

5 - Implementation of activities

- Context analysis to identify the main causes of risks and vulnerabilities, the existing capacities and the potential pathways for resilience building.- Vocational and skills building of the most vulnerable farmers and livestock breeders- Provision of agricultural kits (seeds, fertilizers, livestock, fodders ..) to the most vulnerable HHs through the appropriate modality - Provision of food production (horticulture, poultry-egg laying hens, market gardens) to the most vulnerable HHs through the appropriate modality - Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the performance of the established businesses, their sales growth, existing problems, and difficulties.- Support establishing micro and small businesses after ensuring enough linkages with the market system, special focus on women economic empowerment- Rehabilitation of existing irrigation systems and bakeries, supporting the communities to build gender sensitive water management.- Post Distribution Monitoring to monitor the functionality of the rehabilitated irrigation systems and bakeries and whether women and PWDs have equal and dignified access to the irrigation water and produced bread.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: - Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and transactions delays prevent the implementation of project activities. - The USD to SYP Exchange rate fluctuations could result in either big gain at end of project period with no time to appropriate the additional funds, or not enough funds to fulfil commitments and project objectives. - Potential of fraud and aid diversion, amongst Oxfam staff, suppliers and partners - COVID19 spread puts at risk the health and wellbeing of affected communities and of humanitarian workers delivering assistance. - Assumptions: - No interference by conflicting parties in the implementation putting at risk independence, and impartiality. the local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the rehabilitated bakeries. the community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries sufficiently. The social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities’ decision-making process.Mitigation: Oxfam will perform rapid market assessment to check the availability of the required items and their prices. All Oxfam and partner’s staff will be inducted on Oxfam’s standards on safeguarding and accountability. Very clear accountability mechanism will be put in place prior to the implementation. Oxfam will go for NCEs to absorb the surpluses that it might have as a result to an increase in the exchange rate.
