Project Summary

Objective 1: Refugees have access to lifesaving primary health care and reproductive health services with a particular focus on children under five pregnant and lactating women.
RI will support this through the delivery of primary health care services to refugees through mobile and static clinics. The activities will include (but are not limited to) direct outpatient consultations (focus on women and children); diagnosis and treatment of communicable diseases; provision of reproductive health care including antenatal care, safe delivery, postnatal care, family planning, and medical response to sexual abuse cases; provision of immunization to children under five and reproductive-age women; identification and training of community health volunteers (CHVs) for risk communication and community engagement on COVID-19 and other infectious diseases
Objective 2: Acutely malnourished refugee children and PLW have access to lifesaving nutrition services.
RI will support direct management of SAM and MAM cases (targeting girls and boys under five and pregnant and lactating women experiencing wasting); micronutrient supplementation; targeted nutrition surveillance and monitoring; and local capacity development with respect to nutrition counseling and education for affected communities and their caregivers.

1 - Needs

The spillover of refugees into East Sudan continues to generate a growing humanitarian crisis with the population having significant health, nutrition, water, and sanitation needs. Ri anticipates high need for refugee response in Blue Nile and Khartoum where RI operates and has operational presence. Based on the recent clashes and airstrikes in Makele presents an estimated refugee influx into East Sudan between now and December 2021, with an estaimte figure of 62,000 as the UNHCR assessments.Relief International (RI) conducted a rapid needs assessment (final report to be shared) between 22-28 November 2020 at the main border crossing point Hamdayet in Kassala State as well as in Um Rakuba camp, Gedaref State. RI is targeting Gedaref State as it is where many refugees are being relocated in order to ease the pressure at the crossing points in Kassala, which is increasingly overwhelmed. . Hamdayet is a small and remote border town, located about 240 KM from Kassala and 296KM from Gedaref with a previous population of 6,000. According to the UNHCR report, there are now an estimated 20, 000 refugees in Hamadayet. The rate of arrivals at Hamdayet has slowed in recent days with only 649 arrivals on November 24, 2020. In Um Rakuba, the camp is approximately 75 Km South of Gedaref town and can be mostly reached via a paved road except for a small section of about 6 km of the dirt road leading directly to the camp from Dardoug town junction. As of 27 November, Um Rakuba has reached its current capacity of 10,000 persons. Additional sites are being identified by the Government to extend the camp and accommodate expected arrivals following ongoing discussions between UNHCR and COR. Based on observations, there are a number of pregnant women and women with young children among the arrivals. In addition, a sizeable number of youths have been observed. While conclusive data on the occupational profile of refugees has not been collected yet, most of the refugees interviewed are farmers who reported that they have not harvested their fields yet. According to UNOCHA, about 45% of refugees are children under 18 years, who often suffer disproportionate levels of morbidity and mortality during emergencies, and an estimated 43% are female. UNFPA also reports 7,500 refugee women of reproductive age (15-49 years) while 700 women currently pregnant with an estimated 75 deliveries within the coming month, requiring ANC and skilled birth attendance. In addition, shortages in health staff, medicines, and medical supplies, as well as beds and ambulances have been reported, hindering the ability of health partners to respond to the increasing needs. The assessment confirms that the refugees are in dire need and a difficult situation in need of urgent lifesaving assistance such as access to safe water and sanitation, health care and nutrition, non-food items (NFI), food, education, and protection. In RI’s experience, It is important to note also that Tigray State, where the majority of refugees are traveling from, has a high level of COVID-19 transmission, and cases in the state account for 7% of all cases in Ethiopia and an urgent response is necessary to ensure that this does not further escalate the COVID-19 crisis in Sudan. Health and Nutrition: According to RI’s rapid need assessment Um Rakuba, within Gedaref State, has one health facility run by MSF which was observed to be overcrowded with more than 25 people in the waiting area. The most common reported illnesses and needs are respiratory infection, minor wounds, maternal health, and chronic diseases such as diabetes, HBP, and malaria and sanitation needs.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The scenario accounts for health and protection concerns similar to those in previous waves of arrivals, namely persons with special needs (PWSN), sexual gender-based violence (SGBV), malnutrition, and possibly tuberculosis (TB) and HIV. Large numbers of unaccompanied or separated children (UASC) are also anticipated, along with persons in need of psycho-social support. The very limited number of latrines, with showers and washing facilities, is of particular concern, and ensuring gender separation will be critical as well as the provision of security lighting for use at night and for protection of women and girls.RI recognizes that women are disproportionately affected by shocks and stressors during displacement and at the same time are often expected to contribute disproportionately towards coping strategies and recovery. Gender considerations will be mainstreamed throughout project planning and implementation with a focus on women’s roles. Promoting equitable access to health services will ensure that the project reaches the most vulnerable members of the targeted communities. Nearly all health indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers and PLWs through reproductive health services. Privacy and confidentiality of patients will be respected and ensured at all times of service delivery. RI will deploy health workers of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of both women and men in a comfortable, private, and confidential way. Examination bed areas will be covered with medical screens to maintain privacy while the examination is being conducted by the health workers (male and female). Also, language can be a barrier, so RI will focus on finding bilingual (Tigray/Arabic) people to help with the communication needed to provide the services. COVID-19 precautionary measures will be taken into consideration when defining the response plans, particularly in high concentration zones like reception centers. RI will work with other service providers to establish referral pathways for S/GBV and CP survivors as necessary and will ensure follow up of all cases.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries, including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices, complementarity in complaint responses such as referrals of GBV cases; and strengthening capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedbacks to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and, enhance program recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle, etc. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program designing and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
