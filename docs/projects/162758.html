Project Summary

The long-lasting conflict in Darfur has put immense pressure on the existing health service delivery. The situation has been Exaggerated by the continuous influx of the IDPs and returnees in various states of Darfur, especially in Central, West and Central Darfur and the health system became over whelm with the case load of the patients. The communicable diseases like malaria, ARI and diarrhea are the major cause of morbidity and mortality among children Under 5. Similar situation has been noticed in other states including Red Sea state. The State of Word Survey S3M in 2018 revealed that Sudan conflict has affected more than 3 million children, with approximately 67% of the children under the age of 5 (U5) undernourished and 50% suffering from severe acute malnutrition. CRS have been providing support to the MoH in Darfur states and Red sea through its health & nutrition programing with support from different donors and will continue this support in the year 2020 and beyond. These services include medical consultations to the vulnerable population in targeted localities of Central, West and East Darfur and Red Sea. In addition, health and nutrition awareness among male and female will be enhanced through community sensitization and mobilization among the communities. The capacity of the project, MoH staff and volunteers will also be enhanced through formalized trainings on health-related topics and major killing diseases in the children U5 years of age.

1 - Needs

Sudan is facing humanitarian crises since the end of Darfur conflict and according to the recently conducted survey and sharing the result with humanitarian organization in September 2018, the State of Word Survey S3M revealed that Sudan conflict has affected more than 3 million children, with approximately 67% of the children under the age of 5 (U5) undernourished and 50% suffering from severe acute malnutrition. Since the onset of the conflict in 2003 in Sudan and end of the crises, Health & Nutrition situation has deteriorated, and the health care system is scarce of resources to meet the health needs of the communities in Darfur as a whole and in Central, Western and East Darfur in particular. The communicable diseases like malaria, ARI and diarrhea are the major cause of morbidity and mortality among children Under 5. The influx of returnees and IDPs over the years has put pressure on the already scarce resources and it is difficult for SMoH to provide the essential health care services to all affected communities in both states. Using the UNICEF State of the world children report 2018 an estimated 45% of children U5 mortality is caused by different health problems including malnutrition. On the other hand, the malnutrition rates have remained the same for the last 30 years and these health related issues are further contributing in increase of high malnutrition rates. Some health partners have managed to achieve some progress in some areas but still there is a dire need to provide basic health services to the affected population. The conflict has particularly affected already fragile health system in Central, Western and East Darfur, which has left a decrease in the availability of qualified health workers and facility staff, and critical shortages of urgently needed medications, medical supplies, and equipment. Almost, similar situation has been revealed in Red Sea state as per the S3M report 2018. There are significant gaps found in staffing, resources and capacity of the existing staff working with MoH. In 60% of health facilities in CD and WD are not fully functioning since the end of Darfur conflict. The percentage of health facilities providing Basic Essential Obstetric Care is particularly low in West and Central Darfur, merely 8.8% and 8.7% of facilities respectively provide BEmOC. There were outbreaks of acute watery diarrhea in near past and more than 200 cases were reported but for certain outbreaks to be responded in time, SMOH is almost lacking the capacities due scarce resources. The recent outbreak of Dengue and Chikungunya declared by West Darfur health authorities revealed that the capacity of the health system is not enough to respond to the outbreak. Same situation has been seen during the Dengue and Measles outbreak in 2018. Infections, malnutrition and lack of water and sanitation facilities are all closely linked as they negatively affect morbidity and mortality rates. Eye infections are the one of the top causes of morbidity in Sudan. CRS is working in the area since 2011 and providing support to SMoH to provide treatment for children through its existing health infrastructure in both states. Under this support, CRS intends to extend its services to provide the integrated health & nutrition services to the affected communities in Central, Western Darfur with the extension of services to its newly established field office in East Darfur and to continue these services in Red Sea state as well. The proposed project continues to support the health system to respond to certain outbreaks through formalized trainings on Acute Water Diarrhoea, Dengue and measles protocols and case management. CRS will also support to provide basic pharmaceutical support to SMoH for common ailments. In addition, an integrated social behavior change communication strategy will be adopted to complement the project activities for better health of the affected population in the target localities of all targeted states.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CRS always ensure that during the project design the gender equality and women empowerment in the project interventions shall always be addressed across all sectors including health & nutrition. The project will strengthen the capacities of partners and community-based structures for gender analysis, targeting, collection of sex and age disaggregated data, and promotion of access to basic services for vulnerable women, PLW, adolescent girls & boys, girls with disabilities and ethnic minorities, which will increase their participation and influence decision-making in all stages of the project. The project will work with the Volunteers, partner organization to identify and address any harmful traditional/cultural practices and norms (e.g. FGM/C, early/forced child marriages, etc.) that are barriers to gender equality and women empowerment.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CRS always ensure that all projects are aligned with its MEAL policies. Therefore, CRS utilizes a list of MEAL Policies and Procedures to ensure compliance with CRS polices regarding the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning core functions of implementing our programs.
