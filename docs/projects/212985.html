Project Summary

According to the 2024 Syria HNO, 13.6M PiN of WASH assistance. Of this, 6.8M (51%) are female & 2.3M (17%) are PWDs.
Moreover, according to the MSNA conducted by WH in Dec 2023 in NWS:
- WASH was reported among the top three priority needs in more than a third of assessed camps.
-Most commonly reported reasons IDPs cannot access sufficient water were high prices of water trucking (89%) and the main network is completely or partially damaged (51%).
-Most commonly reported public sanitation issues were no sewage system in the camps (56%), garbage on the site (36%), and sewage flowing onto the site (33%).
- Most commonly reported problems with access to latrines were not enough latrines (78%), no available latrines for PWDs (51%), and latrines having no privacy (62%).
- In 69% of assessed camps, HHs reportedly had no access to a functioning main water network, leading to informal water trucking being the most commonly reported source of drinking water (87%).
- Most commonly reported coping strategies for lack of water used by HHs are modifying hygiene practices (43%), relying on personal water stocks (38%), drinking less (30%), and drinking water used for other purposes (14%).
This program intends to provide an integrated emergency and sustainable WASH intervention package to HC and IDPs in the communities and the camps and informal settlements in NWS.
The program formulation has been based on needs identified by the WASH cluster, supported by NA conducted by WH in Dec 2023 and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
WH’s WASH program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of WASH interventions.
1. In-camp, WH’s intervention will focus on life-saving WASH support and delivery of basic services for 21,000 IDPs residing in 20 camps across Idleb, Harim, Ariha, and Jisr-Ash-Shugur districts. This will include:
- Supply safe and adequate water through trucking (40 liters/person/day).
- Water quality monitoring through testing at three levels: water sources, trucks, and households.
- Expansion of water distribution networks and sewerage systems in the targeted camps where applicable, including installing a renewable energy system (PV Solar energy systems) to minimize the water system operating costs & to ensure sustainability & long-term impact.
- Installation of water tanks to address a current shortage.
- Regular emptying of septic tanks.
- Solid waste removal will be organized from each of the 20 camps.
- Provision of garbage bins to improve the hygienic conditions in the targeted camps.
- Addressing the gap in latrine numbers in the targeted camps by constructing new latrine blocks, making them gender-sensitive, and addressing the needs of the elderly & PWDs.
- Consumable hygiene kits and Cholera Kits will be distributed to displaced families in all target locations.
- Hygiene awareness sessions.
2. Off-camp, WH’s intervention will integrate a more sustainable component, with a focus on long-term improvement of access to safe water sources, through:
- Rehabilitation of 4 water stations & supporting them with the operational costs for 6 months located in Afrin district to serve 55,000 BNFs.
- Rehabilitation of 4 sewage systems in 8 communities in Afrin district to serve 44,000 BNFs.
- Develop tariff-based system management in line with the WASH cluster recommendations & coordination with relevant stakeholders: fees will be collected monthly from BNFs under the supervision of LC to secure operation and maintenance costs required to sustain the project.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female BNFs and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
This intervention will benefit a total of approximately 120,000 INDIVs (2700 men, 28200 women, 30600 boys, and 34200 girls) across NWS. No secured fund yet.
