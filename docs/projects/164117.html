Project Summary

The project aims to provide protection, child protection and gender-based violence prevention and response services to the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls through community-based interventions and protection emergency response mobile teams in Northern Syria (North Aleppo) and Northwest Syria (West Aleppo, Idlib,)
The project will cover 9 WGSS, 6 CFS, 31 Mobile teams (21 of those teams are emergency response protection mobile teams to response for the new displacement movement and to conduct the assessment needed to respond for those IDPs) covering most of Idlib governance, west and north of Aleppo.
The main objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through:
* supporting the protective environment in Syria.
* provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, psychosocial assistance.
* Individual Protection Assistance targeting people with protection, CP, GBV concerns
*support other community initiatives through community centres and outreach mechanisms to address particular protection issues/concerns as identified through protection monitoring.
Provision of PFA support and structures PSS support, including case management.
*Provide legal advice/counselling and legal representation on civil status documentation /registration as well as on housing/land/property issues.
*Expand the availability and quality of protection" CP and GBV services" in targeted location accordance to strategic priorities of the 2020 GP, CP, MA and GBV. As well to ensure the protection of the population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and advocacy with duty bearers,
Increased access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the child protection minimum standards in Humanitarian Action " by providing Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys.
Provide quality specialized GBV services for survivors to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV, e.g., case management (1000 GBV survivors), psychosocial support and establish referral pathways, IRD will provide GBV awareness raising activities, YMC and specialized programming for adolescent girls, conduct GBV training including GBV Awareness Raising toolkit for male employees, inclusion of disability and elderly in GBV Programming (online disability inclusion training developed by the GBV SC and WVI), training about life skills program for adolescent girls. IRD plan to Support to women-led organization inside Syria to expand their GBV technical knowledge and GBV programming. IhsanRD will invite 90 humanitarian actors (non-specialist) from other sectors (Health; Camp Coordination and Camp Management; Education; Food Security and Agriculture; Livelihood; Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) to be trained on GBV guidelines to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialized services. IRD aim to reach 70000 BNFs through GBV services.
Technical support will include delivery of trainings and follow-up (follow-up workshops or practical trainings) to ensure best practices taught are practiced in day-to-day encounters with beneficiaries. Also as part of its direct technical supervision, the GBV officer will conduct the following: biweekly case study sessions with case managers
Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on adolescent girls and to reduce the impact of explosive hazards.
The project aims to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and duty-bearers at the national and community levels to assess, analyze and address protection risks and needs and to respond to child protection concerns in Syria and Build the capacity of GBV specialist and non-specialist to respond, prevent and mitigate GBV.

4 - Needs

Several needs of protection continue to be widespread and severe in Syria. They are driven by a range of situations including the impacts of active hostilities, new displacements, crisis cumulative long-term consequences and dynamics linked to return increasing the stress on the communities. According to UN-led needs assessment initiatives, the occurrence of multiple protection issues in the same community is common. This environment requires a sustained response by humanitarian actors to provide specialized services to respond to the most urgent needs, to mitigate risks, to build a favorable protection environment, with particular attention to groups with specific needs and who are at risk.As per latest OCHA reports, since Dec 2019, more than 50% of the new IDPs are women and children which increases the needs of protection services.According to voices 2020, various types of GBV continue to pervade the lives of women and girls. Deep-rooted social norms, compounded by the length of the crisis and mounting lawlessness are normalizing this violence, severely affecting the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of their rights.Children are exposed to many protection risks and severe rights violations. The crisis impact continues to foster an unfavorable environment where the psychosocial needs are unmet leading to distress. Child labor is a major protection risk, including its most dangerous forms such as recruitment, begging and scavenging for scrap metal. Children, including CWDs, who face the risk of other forms of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation–CPM Dashboard and Emergency Response Report.According to several needs’ assessment carried by IRD during 2019, women and girls are still facing the challenges of early marriage, prevention from education, sexual abuse and the unjust work opportunities. As for child protection needs the main problems facing children are fleeing from school, physical and emotional violence, and child labor.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRD will implement the mentioned activities by provision of CP, GBV, MA, GP services as follow: Provision of CP services including (structured ,recreational PSS, CM for vulnerable children, IPA, awareness raising sessions, parenting skills, distribution of recreational kits &bracelets) through establishing 6 CFS aim to engage children in activities designed to support positive PSS wellbeing & coping strategies, each CFs will be accompanied with MT to provide outreach activities &target the community with CP services.Provision of GBV services including (life skills, Vocational trainings, PSS, awareness raising sessions, CM for GBV survivors, DKs , baby kits) through establishing of 9 WGSSs to provide access to safe environment for women & girls,Establishing of 33 MTs providing Protection emergency response package in emergency situation, including (PFA, information sharing, referrals, IPA & cash for vulnerable cases, recreational PSS, awareness raising ,surveys & assessments in the communities in coordination with the protection cluster). IRD is protection emergency response FP in Jebel Seman district in coordination with protection actors, monthly meetings will be conducted in both Syria & turkey level to share information,needs, services, gaps, challenges. CB component is a main part of the project, all staff will be trained on protection specialized (CP, GBV, GP) topics with targeting 75 humanitarian actors with safe Identification&referrals training.All protection facilities will be rendered to be accessible for PWDs along with provision of transportation service to PWDs & elderlies & target them with specialized services(CM & IPA).MEAL will use & develop M&E tools in adherence to M&E toolkit that is published by GBV cluster. That includes but not limited to (outreach monitoring & evaluation, DKs distribution monitoring, evaluation of PSS structured activities, IPA evaluation, beneficiaries’ satisfaction, WGSS checklist, managing learning process and managing CRM

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

IhsanRD will follow the risk analysis and mitigation measures that conducted and developed by the cluster and sub-cluster and will highlight the top issuesProtection risk:1- Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging. Ihsan will Engage Community throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders and Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture. 2- Other risks associated with the project are difficulties in GBV mainstreaming with other humanitarian sectors projects, Therefore, Ihsan will train the other sectors on the principles of gender-based violence and Identifying the risks of gender-based violence in each sector and the referral pathway. Ihsan will ensure that all teams are trained on GBV basics, and for GBV service providers 3- Lack of commitment in integrating and enforcing risk mitigation measures; lack of accountability; Sectors may put survivors at risk if they don’t follow correct procedures. Likelihood Medium Impact, High Mitigating Measure. 4- GBV services are not prioritized in emergency response.1. Risk of diversion of resources for emergency response from the ongoing programming. Likelihood: High Impact: Medium Mitigating Measure. 2.Dignity kits distribution as a safe entry point in the community. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor-centered services CP risk: 1.Risk of doing harm to girls and boys through the provision of specialized child protection services. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Ihsan will work on ensure training packages are in line with CPMS 2.Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles. Medium Impact: Medium Mitigating Measure. IhsanRD will Design exit plans from the start for the project and Identify other service providers and establish referral pathways in order to follow the ethical closure measurement

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery to IDPs and Host communities are not served likelihood: high, impact: medium, mitigation measures: Before a decision is made to establish a new service, IhsanRD will use the existing tools/ mapping and coordinate at local level with other protection actors to ensure balanced coverage responding to needs; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities if possible and unless motivated by needs; consider focusing on underserved/rural areas as well as projected return areas. IhsanRD will ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be informed. 2- The population targeted by the intervention is put at risk as a result of the location of the project; lack of consideration for the accessibility to services in terms of freedom of movement, safety and security for men, women, boys, girls, elderly and persons with disabilities. IhsanRD will conduct safety audit to ensure safe access (route and location) by men, women, boys, girls, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, psychological or other impediments). Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community (to ensure diverse perspective and perceptions are considered). Select staff for all activities (especially outreach volunteers) with adequate knowledge of the community, but also respecting balance in gender, age, social profile. 3- Different vulnerable groups might not have the same access to services and assistance. IhsanRD will Ensure information sharing with community members on services available and regular communication to the community about the nature and the target of the services. IhsanRD will activate the mobile approach of the project to target most vulnerable people including PwDs and elderlies
