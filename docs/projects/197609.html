Project Summary

Children and youths are the most vulnerable groups affected by the ongoing Anglophone armed conflict in the South West and North West Regions of Cameroon. As estimated 1, 033, 000 (463,000 from SW) school aged children have been out of school due to the dual emergencies (conflict and covid-19). The situation is even worse for girls who are victims of gender-based violence (e.g. pregnant girls, girls forced to early marriage, victims of sexual violence, etc) as they face difficulties in accessing learning, and psychosocial care in schools and learning spaces. Children with disabilities are at higher risk of not having access to learning services and exhibit low enrolment rates at primary school levels coupled with the fact that inclusive education is not yet formally anchored in the national system of Cameroon.
This Sports and recreational games are played to motivate learning, as opposed to professional. The primary purpose is equal participation and involvement of conflict/Covid-19 affected children aged 4-17, with the goal of improved educational preparedness, resilience ,social integration, stress relief, tolerance , physical wellbeing and fun, through Positive Coping Mechanisms. It is strategic and also seeks to impart other life skills; Safety, how to say no, Self Care, and Coping with stress. It will also motivate learning and counter trauma faced by conflict affected children experiencing Rape, exploitation, neglect, sexual abuse, physical violence, and psychological violence.
HADY’s mission for 2022 is to increase and improve access to safe and continuous learning , for 7000 (4500+ girls and 2500+ boys, aged 4 to 17 disabilities included) conflict affected children using recreational activities which is innovative and conflict sensitive. HADY has developed a recreational toolkit with over twenty indoor and outdoor conflict sensitive games, replicated from UNICEF. This will guide the designing, implementation, monitoring, evaluation and reporting of this project. Recreational Activities will help children manage stress and provides a sense of self-esteem, which will directly reduce anxiety and depression. It will increase motivation to learn.
This project will target and train 80 learning facilitators, primary school teachers, and other education actors on how to use recreational activities to improve and motivate learning, and counter trauma of conflict/ covid-19 affected children. HADY will also build their capacity on Child Safeguarding, Gender Based Violence guidelines, Child protection, physical First Aid and CPR Skills. We will distribute recreational games items using the HADY Recreational kit Bag, targeting safe learning spaces and vulnerable community schools. We will monitor trained learning facilitators in these safe learning spaces and community schools daily to collectively engage conflict/ Covid-19 affected children with recreational games using the “DO NO HARM “ and INCLUSIVE approach where safety of the affected children and other stakeholders will prevail. Protection has been mainstreamed to ensure that the all stakeholders are safe at all levels of the project.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Children and youths are the most vulnerable groups affected by the ongoing Anglophone armed conflict in the South West and North West Regions of Cameroon. As the conflict continues, education continues to be at the center of the conflict and the dire situation has been exacerbated by the emergency of Covid-19 pandemic which left 6,369 schools (2, 677 from SW and 3,692 in NW) about 4,200 Community Learning spaces (1,764 in SW and 2,436 in NW) closed to contain the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic. As estimated 1, 033, 000 (463,000 from SW and 570,000 from NW) school aged children have been out of school due to the dual emergencies (conflict and covid-19). A total of 28,866 (12,124 from SW) teachers are not in schools (Safe Back to Learning, EC, NWSW, 9/1/20). During the 2019/2020 school year, schools at preschool and primary levels were closed due to insecurity in the North West and South West Regions affecting learners and education actors from these areas. The resulting movement of conflict affected children from these has exerted significant pressure on the already limited learning structures in the host areas, exacerbating pre-existing vulnerabilities (MYRP, ECW, and Cameroon). The situation is even worse for girls who are victims of gender-based violence (e.g. pregnant girls, girls forced to early marriage, victims of sexual violence, etc) as they face difficulties in accessing learning, and psychosocial care in schools and learning spaces. Children with disabilities are at higher risk of not having access to learning services and exhibit low enrolment rates at both primary school levels coupled with the fact that inclusive education is not yet formally anchored in the national system of Cameroon. Schools and universities also experienced closures as of March 2020 that were announced by the government to reduce the spread of Covid-19, thus affecting the schooling of 7.2 million enrolled in public and private institutions. HADY targeted 7,000 (4,500 girls, 2,500 boys, special needs included) children in 2022, but could only engaged 2,521 (1,404 girls, 1,117 boys, 29% IDP, 71% Host, and 41 persons with disabilities) in recreational and sports games. This significant gap was due to limited resources given that Education has been under funded. In 2023 HADY is targeting 7,000+ (4500+ girls, and 2500+ boys, special needs included) conflict affected children. During our time in the field children and education actors have shown great interest through appreciating the impact of sports and recreational games on vulnerable children. During our impact evaluation question titled “What Do You Love The Most?” 98% of the children expressed their love for recreational games and sports and a tool for their motivation to learn and stay in this learning environment. A Teacher said “conflict affected children; especially IDPs have been more active in class after engaging in our sports and recreational games”. These results have informed our strategy to scale to other vulnerable learning environment in South West Region of Cameroon.
