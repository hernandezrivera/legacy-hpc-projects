Project Summary

Through this proposed intervention, DCA will provide support to women and girls through the provision of services as well as provide economic empowerment and continue to engage men and boys in transformational behaviors to work as allies with women. Therefore, the project will enhance the wellbeing and dignity of the host and Rohingya refugee communities, empower women and girls to access services, and make positive life choices. The project will furthermore mitigate negative coping mechanisms, enhance wellbeing in families, and support women and girls living in host and Rohingya Refugee communities to better lives in an intermediary situation. Individual health, psychological and social consequences of GBV will be reduced, ensuring that survivors will have improved their psychosocial abilities and minimize health impacts as well as be economically and socially empowered with skills introduction.

1 - Identified Needs

"Protection risks remain of grave concern in the Rohingya refugee crisis, which has left 1.2 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. The refugee population in Cox’s Bazar has reached more than 900,000, of which 52% are women and girls, and 54% are under the age of 18. The congested nature of the camps poses serious safety, security, and well-being risks, which are particularly high for women and girls who continue to be at disproportionate risk of S/GBV. Further, men are the household level decision-makers, reducing the economic power of women and girls. This, combined with conservative religious beliefs, reduces women’s mobility in the community and even leadership engagement. According to the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, GBV is one of the most pervasive forms of human rights violations to which Bangladeshi women and girls are exposed.DCA has also conducted several FGDs and In-depth interviews covering women and girls as well as PwDs, older people, lactating mothers, single mothers, and so on for identifying the need of the community.The overall risks for women and girls protection and are part of the conditions sustaining unequal power dynamics, which contribute to the high rates of IPV in the camps. The 2020 JRP reported incidences of multidimensional SGBV that include physical assault, sexual violence, rape, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Considering that SGBV is generally under-reported, the fact that 75% of all reported SGBV cases are IPV points to the heightened vulnerability of women and it is assumed that the actual rate of IPV is likely to be much higher. Adolescent girls are at particular risk of early marriage, sexual violence, and trafficking. Adolescent girls experience challenges to access services because of concerns expressed by parents or guardians for their safety and security.The 2020 JRP also noted that despite the presence of government services and GBV service entry points established by NGOs in host communities, gaps remain in ensuring adequate provision of safe and quality GBV services. Besides, low awareness of available services and deep-rooted socio-cultural barriers limit survivors' ability to access services.Women’s leadership and active engagement is essential, in particular under-represented and marginalized groups such as persons with disabilities, older people, adolescent girls, in all aspects of the COVID-19 response.The COVID-19 crisis has further exacerbated risks for women and girls as isolation, and lack of access to SRH has led to increased reports of GBV within families due to growing movement restrictions, fear, stress, and financial insecurity. "
