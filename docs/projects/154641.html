Project Summary

INTERSOS’ strategy for 2019 is to strengthen and scale-up the education interventions in the areas where it is present with other sectors, i.e. Protection and Health. For what concern the education sector this geographically translates into Ninewa governorate (Tel Afar, Mosul IDP camps), Kirkuk (Hawiga and Kirkuk), KRI and Salah ad-Din governorate.
Drawing from lessons learned and best practices, the education interventions will continue to go hand-in-hand with child protection, while reinforcing the child protection component through the expertise gained in general protection case management. Following the change in the Iraqi context and the new challenges posed by the current stage of the crisis, INTERSOS will focus on supporting the formal schools in the retaken areas and only intervening with its own structured Temporary Learning Spaces (TLSs) or in the community centers (CC) when no other option is available to children. Support to formal schools does not mean a disengagement from non-formal education (NFE) services, but in fact it entails an additional effort to improve the interaction and the complementary between NFE and the formal system in coordination with other NGOs and with the government. In non- camp setting the school becomes the central space to restore the well-being of the child. The engagement of the community in all the stages of the process enhances the creation of child friendly protective environments along with promoting social cohesion and ownership of the beneficiaries.
The proposed intervention ensures that the targeted conflict-affected children and adolescent returnees in the retaken areas and IDPs living in/outside camps are guaranteed access to quality education, by supporting the rehabilitation of schools, capacity building to teachers and PTAs members, provision of equipment and material, delivery of NFE activities, PSS and child protection mainstreaming through awareness raising in the communities. In the retaken areas the action targets returnees and vulnerable children supporting the quality of formal education and promoting the enrolment of out of schools’ children through outreaching and individual paths of re-enrolment basing on individual needs’ assessed. The innovative element, core of the integrated action, is the establishment of a school based CP system, where teachers become focal points for case management internal referral. A community based approach is followed throughout the all action ensuring the participation of teachers, parents, community representatives and children in the design of the activities and in the implementation phase. In the camp setting the intervention targets school-aged children with a focus on extremely vulnerable girls and boys. Thanks to the cooperation with the Directorate of Education, the children with more difficulties in catching up with the curriculum will be referred to the TLS or CC and will be supported in their learning process. In the camp where formal school is not an option, the TLS will be the only way to provide access to education and avoid to widen the gap of education of a whole generation of children. Thanks to a holistic approach, these children will receive also emotional and psychological support during recreational and PSS activities

Needs

Between late 2013 and 2017, intensification of conflict in north and central Iraq has resulted in large scale displacement with 1.9 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) identified across Iraq as of August 2018. With the starting of the violence in 2014 Iraqi education system has been completely disrupted and the governorates of Ninewa, Kirkuk and Salah Al Din have strongly been affected in terms of violence, interruption of services, damages and destruction of infrastructures. Tel Afar district has been mostly affected by the conflict, being under ISIS for about 3 years. During this time many of the schools’ facilities have been closed down. Many schools have been used as prisons (to keep Yazidies prisoners) and many have been used as storage. Almost all of the schools have been eventually plundered. Very few schools remained open under ISIS promoting a different curriculum linked to ISIS ideology.Ninewa governorate’s data from UNICEF show the impact of the crisis on educational infrastructures: Out of 175 operating schools in Tel Afar district before ISIS only 69 schools are operating in 2018, with overcrowded classes, lack of teachers and equipment. The currently operating schools have been highly damaged during the conflict, left with mortar shots on walls and signs of shelling. Teachers themselves, report signs of psychological distress being subject of displacement or having witnessed violence. In order to restore the quality of education, capacity building is needed in terms TiCC, class management, positive behaviour, PSEA and safe identification and referral. Education and Protection clusters have underlined the need of following an integrated approach in order to properly respond to the needs of the children returnees in the retaken areas. This approach will help in promoting positive coping mechanisms along with enhancing the resilience of the child reducing their vulnerability (source: Minimum Child Protection Activities Package for Urban Retaken Areas). In the IDPs camps of Mosul the TLSs run by INTERSOS are still working at their full capacities, but can reach only a part of the children in need (in Salamya the TLS hosts around 3,000 children and Nimrod’s one around 1,800 children). The absence of other actors operating in non-formal education in these camps put the children at high risk of engaging in armed groups and/ or child labor. Based on INTERSOS’ assessment on children participating in non-formal education and child protection services, there still is a need for psycho-social support. A sense of instability is pervasive among families and their children, especially considering that the people remaining in the camps or going back to the camp because secondary displaced are those facing the biggest challenge in rebuilding a life after the conflict. For refugees in KRI,the main barrier to access education are the language of instruction,curriculum, the lack of textbook and secondary schools, and financial constraints.In 2018, a high prevalence of poverty-induced child protection risks such as child labour (6% of households has at least one child aged 6-17 working) and child marriage (3% of households with at least one married child aged 12-17 inside camps, 2% outside) were observed both among IDPs and returnee children, compared to past years when grave violations such as indiscriminately targeting of children during active conflict, and family separation, were more prevalent. There are more than 15 districts across Iraq where 30-50% of children show signs of psycho-social distress.According to a recent study conducted by War Child UK and Mercy Hand in East Mosul a change of attitude caused by psychological distress has been identified among girls and boys. While girls tend more likely to experience sadness and antisocial behaviors, boys tend prevalently to adopt aggressive behaviors with a high risk of joining armed groups. Prolonged displacement and lack of access to livelihoods opportunities in the areas of return significantly increased the incidence of certain child protection issues, i.e. child labour, including the recruitment into pro-government armed groups, as well as child marriage and other forms of sexual violence and exploitation. Furthermore, women and girls with perceived affiliation to extremists are at high risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Girls and boys living in and out of camps are increasingly out of school (26%), mainly due to the lack of financial means, exposing them to higher child protection risks, while children with disabilities experience physical and social barriers in accessing essential services and education, and are more vulnerable to violence, exploitation and abuse. Finally, the need for explosive hazard risk education is imperative for children who are the most at risk.Sources: 2019 HNO, ROAR, RASP, MCNA VI, Education cluster and Child Protection cluster DATA, TDH - Child Protection and Education Need Assessment, Feb 2018

Activities

Target activities for IDPs, refugees, returnees and host communities in IraqSO1-Rehabilitation of schools: Primary and secondary schools will be rehabilitated in Ninewa, Kirkuk,Salah al Din. A set of indicators is taken under consideration in defining the target schools: a) safety and accessibility of the area, b) level of damages of the school building (25% - 30%) c) number of children enrolled in schools and possibility of welcoming new students d) capacity of DoE of supporting the school in terms of resources i.e. salaries to teachers. Among the main works foreseen for each schools: rehabilitation of windows, doors, electric system, rehabilitation and painting of walls. Special attention is given to works enhancing the accessibility of the buildings in order to promote the inclusion of CwD. Main elements: installation of ramps, establishment of common areas at the first floor, enhancing the lighting system of the classrooms, installing accessible toilets for girls and boys.-Provision of equipment to schools: Equipment such as furniture, water tanks, laptops and printer will be distributed to the schools. A list of the items will be developed in cooperation with the DoE according to the specific needs of the schools.-Provision of material: educational material, books and stationary will be delivered to schools in the retaken areas and to the TLCs in the camps. A list of material will be developed with the support of the school personnel, to meet the specific needs of each school/ TLC.SO2-Training to teachers and non-formal education’s personnel: formal teachers (MoE) will benefit from training modules on: TiCC, class management, inclusive education, PSEA, CP, and elements of safe identification & referral (integrated CP). Training modules will be followed by in class supervision and coaching. Capacity building will be provided to facilitators, education personnel and support staff identified among the targeted population in/out IDPs camps. Main topics will be related to principles of NFE, participatory approach, PSS activities. A special coaching will be carried out with the facilitators engaged with catch-up classes.5. Establishment and restoration of PTAs: PTAs will be restored and/ or established in the schools. PTAs play a fundamental role in promoting the relation school– family enhancing a participative and safe environment for the children. Roles and responsibilities of the PTAs are defined through clear guidelines. For the TLCs the PTAs committees will supervise the running of the TLS itself, promoting community participation in children’s education. -Training for parents and community members: parents and community members, elected to be part of the PTAs will receive specific training on Child Protection, PSEA and elements of safe identification & referral. This activity is meant to actively engage aware community members in positive behaviours enhancing a community safety net for the children.-Provision of non-formal education to returnees out of school children, in/out camp IDPs/refugees and vulnerable children: A) catch-up classes, for children who missed years of schooling B) Basic literacy and numeracy for younger children are left out of the school system. Through this activity they will be able to integrate in the school C) Remedial classes, for children who are already enrolled in formal schools and need some additional support and tutoring.-Conduction of recreational activities: recreational activities are implemented in the retaken areas and in the TLSs located in the camps. Volunteer teachers from the community are in charge of the activity which target boys and girls aged between 4 and 17 years old among IDPs/ refugees, returnees and host communities. -Establishment of school based protection teams and delivery of comprehensive case management (integrated CP): protection teams will be established in the areas addressed by the action. Management of CP cases and proper referrals to specialised services (internally or externally). For the most vulnerable cases transportation will be provided if service not available in the area. -PSS: structured individual and group psychological support activities for boys and girls (integrated CP): ensuring safety and confidentiality; provision of individual psychological counselling for cases under case management and community-based psycho-social support activities. Existing PSS programs for children will be enhanced through the adoption of the No Strings International (NSI) methodology. Children’s desk will be established in each school such as a safe environment for supporting boys and girls. -Community based awareness campaigns (integrated CP):Awareness sessions are an effective way to stimulate community participation and to spread messages that serve the overall project’s purpose.The awareness campaigns will focus on sensitise the community on right of education, CP, mine prevention, child labor and early marriage prevention

How is your project accountable to affected population

Throughout the implementation, meaningful participation of beneficiaries will be also ensured. A two-ways communication with affected populations will be guaranteed to: • respect the dignity of affected communities in the areas of operation; • have better control over the organization's internal processes; • improve monitoring and evaluation and better understand children needs and communities’ needs, including tracking them as they change over time; • make education intervention more effective, by allowing a more efficient reporting of cases of abuse and exploitation, if any; • avoid unintended and damaging consequences of INTERSOS work. Similarly to what has been done so far, since the start of the Mosul crisis response, INTERSOS staff will continue to consult beneficiaries in order to adapt the proposed intervention to their changing needs, as well as to receive their suggestions on how to better adapt the response throughout the project implementation. A comprehensive feedback/complaint mechanism, inclusive of feedback/complaint boxes, feedback/complaint forms in Arabic and feedback/complaint Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for detailed response is currently operational in INTERSOS ongoing projects. Other stakeholders/actors working in cooperation with INTERSOS (i.e. in case of referral services) will be subjected to the same system to guarantee quality implementation of activities and accountability. Information about child protection services gathered through monitoring activities will be shared with other relevant stakeholders and the IDPs Information Centre that is operational in the whole of Iraq as a way to ensure wider accountability towards beneficiaries and humanitarian actors. Other accountability mechanisms will include SOPs for programming developed and used by the organization, as well as signs posted in the semi-permanent spaces, specifying that all services are provided free of charge. PSEA policy is going to be one of the core trainings for all of the core and field staff (see paragraph “Protection Mainstreaming”). As described in the background, this intervention takes into account the changed situation in the camps where INTERSOS has been present since the beginning. In order to keep the intervention relevant, INTERSOS’ core staff has been consulting the community and the facilitators/support staff who are recruited from the community among returnees and IDP camp population. These consultations have informed the project proposal and are the key for the success of the community-based approach. The community-based approach, already present in INTERSOS’s interventions, will be the full core of one output of this program. The identification of beneficiaries for INTERSOS’ continual intervention will be conducted through different modalities and lessons learned, based on existing information, collected and shared by existing protection and education partners/community leaders operating in the targeted retaken areas and camps. An advanced community based approach will permeate all the activities. PTAs will have a strong role in the creation of a safe community network. The committees will be restored and/ or established in each school in order to enhance a protective environment for vulnerable children. Through this action teachers and parents, who will be trained on CP elements, safe identification and referral will have a major role in the process of identifying the needs of boys and girls both inside and out of school. The involvement of aware and trained members of the community will be an added value in reaching out of school children and in developing awareness campaigns able to address specific topic sensitive to the community. Teachers who play an essential role in the development of the child and are key members of the community will be promoted as main actors of positive change
