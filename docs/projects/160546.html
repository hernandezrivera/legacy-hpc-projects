Project Summary

This project is specifically meant to address WASH Cluster Objective (CO4): Mitigate WASH-related gender based violence. WASH Cluster Objective (CO1): and WASH Cluster objective CL11/CO3 Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees. Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package; through provision of timely sustainable and equitable lifesaving access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees. The proposed project will target IDP, host communities and returnees, school going children, women, vulnerable groups including HIV victims and youth in Kol Payam, Chahari Payam and Choro Payam in Ikotos County
In collaboration with other WASH partners on the ground, eg AVI; the project will focus to address water, sanitation and hygiene needs of displaced populations and host communities and provision of GBV intervention in Ikotos County.
Integrated WASH and GBV-TARGET Association will prioritize WASH GBV in communities and schools to improve access to safe water for conflict affected IDPs and host communities in Ikotos County through rehabilitation of boreholes/hand pumps, MHM kits to women and school girls and rehabilitation/construction of latrines in schools/health centers.
TARGET Association will establish Water User Committees at all borehole/hand pump rehabilitated that will be trained to strengthen their capacity to provide maintenance of the water point at local level for sustainability. The rehabilitation will be conducted through existing local hand pump mechanics. TAEGET Association will request the essential tool from core pipelines, and where available the local existing spare parts will be used, however the project will supply those spare parts missing considering the variability on the ground. The projects will also focus to achieving hygiene behavior changes at household level and in schools through hygiene and sanitation promotion with much emphasis on behavior changes communication (BCC).
TARGET Association will supply and install hand washing facility (tanks) in schools to mitigate GBV at schools, and promote sanitary practices and hygiene behavior changes in schools. Dignity kits will be distributed to the affected Mothers. At schools, dignity kits will be supplied and distributed to girls as menstrual management tool and mitigate BGV at schools.
Integrated WASH and Nutrition: The food insecurity in Ikotos still remains crucial. TARGET Association will prioritize that SAM children and their families have access to safe drinking water and sanitation. The idea is to improve clean hand washing at critical times and safe water storage at households and reduce spread of diseases or chance of contracting diahhoeal disease in children which results to malnutrition. TARGET Association will target training mothers of SAM/MAM patients on correct uses of water treatment. TARGET Association will supply and distribute washing soap, bucket, water purifier and water filter to each SAM/MAM house hold. At the health facilities, TARGET Association will supply and install, hand washing facility (tank), Waste Bin in health facilities including nutrition centers.

1 - Needs

Ikotos County is situated in Eastern Equatoria, in Southern Sudan. After decades of war it is hard to determine the population of any county in the region with any accuracy. In 2002 the Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Association—the humanitarian wing of the Sudan People’s Liberation Movement—determined the population of the County, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), to be 67,3401. The County is the ancestral home of the Nilotic Lango tribe, who are thought to have a population of around 25,000-30,0002 people, many of whom live outside the County. The County is administratively divided into six payams and further sub-divided into bomas. The Lango tribe has six distinct sub-tribes—the Lokwa, Dongotono, Ketebo, Logir, Lorwama and Imotong. The Lango’s neighbours are the Didinga and Buya in Budi county, the Lotuko in Torit county, and the Acholi and Karimojong across the border in Uganda. Ikotos was a part of Torit County until 2004 when it was made a new County. The Lango tribesmen are both pastoralist and agriculturalists. Cattle are integral to their day- to-day life, central to contracting of marriages, performance of rituals, and exchanges of gifts. Cattle raids have always occurred, especially during the dry seasons because cattle move faster in short grass than during the rainy season when the grass in the plains is very tall and makes it harder for raiders to speedily move them In Ikotos, one of the main causes of conflicts in the past was cattle raiding. With the recurring cycles of conflicts in Sudan, most Lango clans lost most of their cattle and fled to Uganda. They then resorted to cultivation to make a living and used money (instead of cattle) to pay bride wealth. Now cash is often accepted for marriages but when challenged to pay the bride price in cattle, raiding may be the only option for youth in an environment where small arms are in abundant supply. Meanwhile, most of the population have started to return to have access to their original land for cultivation and most of the return villages lack adequate WASH services as many were vandalized during conflict, hand pumps need rehabilitation, most community practice pen deification and there is limited access by women to WASH supplies for household and personal hygiene. These includes water containers, soap, sanitary pads, etc. The most affected payams that TARGET Association would want to intervene being, Kol, Chahari, Choro payams . This WASH saving response will be implemented in coordination with the Directorate of Water and Sanitation and the static WASH partners AVSI, with operational presence in Ikotos. Moreover, according to the IPC mapping of July 2019, current GAP/ PIN: 70,513 (55%); Most affected Eastern agro pastoral zone more isolated with poor road connection to market: affected by the dry spell (cereal gap/ availability); high food prices and declining terms of trade for cereals in exchange for livestock (financial/ physical access. Western/ central green belt produced a surplus but with limited access to local markets resulting in surplus exported to Uganda or Hatire. GAM rate 13.5% Serious (phase3). P1 PIN: 76,923 (60%); 60% 16. population in central & western highlands utilizing their surplus production and with income earning casual labour opportunities with the logging concessions; 40% eastern region are expected to face a continued deterioration in their food security: stocks run out; livestock moved to the cattle camps; cattle raiding over scarce resources; heavy reliance on markets for cereals; declining terms of trade and high prices; livestock diseases and poor pasture further reducing milk yields. Net effect then is a minor deterioration overall but not significant for a phase 4 area classification. P2 PIN: 76,923 (60%): Western & central highlands reach peak lean season; cereal stocks expected to run out; greater reliance on markets with high prices; greater importance on wild food gathering, hunting, and sale

2 - Activities

TARGET Association will intervene in Water supply, Sanitation, Hygiene promotion activities involving trainings and awareness campaigns in the community• Water supply including borehole rehabilitation/repair will include water quality analysis, hand pump mechanics training and Water Sources management committees for the rehabilitated/repaired water sources. Hygiene sessions for pupils, menstrual hygiene management sessions for school going females above 12 years old, provision of dignity kits to schools girls of menstruating age. • Community WASH will target host communities and include community targeted hygiene promotion activities, household water treatment sessions for vulnerable groups and media campaign. Much of the hygiene promotion activities will have more focus on hygiene education than promotional activities with many of the target beneficiaries sensitized on the importance of good hygiene practices, such as hand washing and prevention of diarrhea. • Hygiene promotion activities will continue with more focus on promotional and demonstration activities like latrine use and maintenance, safe water chain, and households’ visits. The program will support activities that will ensure its sustainability such as intensifying information campaigns, education and promotional activities. TARGET Association will use community-based approach to implement these activities, continued collaboration with host community and schools using life skill education and strengthening committees and hygiene promoters to support follow-up activities. To ensure that the intervention is suitable to the current situation in the areas of concern, water sources will be assessed and included in the project on a rolling basis, in close collaboration with DWSS. Water point’s repairs, rehabilitation will be outsourced to the outside supplier, which will be closely monitored and supervised by TARGET Association WASH Technician and DWSS staff. Formation and training of Water User Committees will provide the sustainability and strengthening of the Operation & Maintenance system of water sources, as a community-led approach to increase resilience to adequately deal with emergencies and with the aim to create a strategy for reduction of borehole spare parts subsidy, taking into special consideration the active participation of women in the decision making processes. A Water User Committee (WUC) will be formed in all locations where TARGET Association will take care of water sources to ensure maintenance and sustainability of the water sources. TARGET Association will also organize three trainings for hand pump mechanics in the three payams of operation. The workshops will concern hand pump repair and maintenance, and go more in-depth on the technical issues.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

TARGET Association has national staff living and working among the beneficiary populations in Greater Equatoria region. After more than four years of programming, TARGET Association is known and always trusted by beneficiary population in South Sudan. Beneficiaries will be involved and consulted in the different stages of the project cycle. Meetings will be held with community leaders, community groups, local authorities, and stakeholders as part of needs assessment at the beginning of project to ensure collective responsibilities. Participatory focus group discussions will be used to inform the nature of needs, ideas and preferences of beneficiaries in addressing their needs and equal access to services. During project implementation, regular meetings will be held with community to get their feedback to improve project delivery. In addition, intentional information-gathering will be conducted through the Cascade Groups network such as mothers/women to elicit feedback so as to inform management decisions and programmatic designs. TARGET Association will install various complaint’s mechanisms that are safe and accessible to all groups including the most vulnerable groups considering cultural, geographical, protection and safety and power dynamics. The complaint’s mechanism will be monitored regularly to ensure its effectiveness and improvement. Further, mixed approach will be employed to collect feedback such as through direct contact with beneficiaries, exit interviews, focus group discussions and in meetings of the Cascade Groups. The CGs are particularly valuable the large number of women engaged in every two-week cycle and the informal way that beneficiaries can receive feedback. Complaints will be properly investigated and handled as well as confidentiality, in line with TARGET Association’s standard procedures which are outlined in the policy documents including TARGET Association’s Code of Ethics, Child Protection Policy and Policy on PSE and GBV.
