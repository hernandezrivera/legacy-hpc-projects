Project Summary

According to HNO 2022, 14.3M people need humanitarian assistance, among which 9.1M need emergency assistance. Moreover, 10.9M are people in need of FSL services, and 3.2 people are in need of CP services.
The 2021 IPC report , showed that 7.3 million people in Sudan (16% of the population analysed) are in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above). Of these, around 5.5 million people are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), while about 1.8 million are critically food insecure classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An increase in localised conflicts triggered population displacement, which, combined with the deterioration of the economy, led to higher than usual levels of acute food insecurity. As a result, the highest prevalence of population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse are observed in several states, including Central Darfur state ranging from 17-18%.
According to Sudan 2022 HNO, The United Nations verified 292 grave violations against 274 children (143 boys, 131 girls); including killing, maiming, sexual violence, recruitment and use by armed groups and forces, abduction, and denial of humanitarian assistance mostly in the Darfur region. The economic crisis and increasing deprivation continue to fuel harmful coping mechanisms, particularly among adolescent girls and boys. This highly affects adolescent boys to be more likely to be killed and injured, detained, recruited, or exploited, while adolescent girls are particularly at risk of child marriage, harassment, exploitation, and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. MSNA 2021 reports reflect that 38% of girls married before the age of 18, and 12% of them married before their 15th birthday, with no access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services for both girls and boys.
Khartoum State (7 Localities) has one of the highest range of protection insecured situation among most populations in the refugees and the urban poorest most affected population exacerbated by the political situation, lack of livelihood opportunities, economic deterioration which is not expected to improve and the most vulnerable will not be able to meet their basic needs,
BPWO designed this project aligned to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) - Sudan (2022) Strategic , Strategic Objective 3 (Mitigate protection risks and respond to protection needs through humanitarian action). This project also aligned with the child protection activities targeting children and adolescents among them IDPs, and vulnerable children from the host communities. All activities aim to enhance care and protective environment for children through strengthening community-based protection safety nets; provision of direct individual case management support to the most vulnerable children, strengthening of community‐based child protection mechanisms and referral structures, provision of psychosocial and mental health services for children through MPC and mobile CFS targeting both children and caregivers, as well as strengthening the response mechanisms through linkages between formal and informal structures to work together to improve the system
This intervention contributes to cluster objective 1 and 2 and contribute to humanitarian response plan objective 3. Moreover; BPWOI will coordinate with all different INGOs, NGOs, relevant government ministries and local authorities in Khartoum State , this will be through ensuring information sharing based on need, the aim is ensured relevant and related actors are informed and maximize the use of resources and sharing of best practices.

1 - Needs

BPWO has strong humanitarian and development operations in Khartoum State science 2015, which align with the 2022 HRP alongside the BPWO strategy's 2022-2024. The primary purpose of the interventions under this project is to extend and expand the lifesaving child protection and Helth interventions to meet the critical humanitarian needs of conflict-affected people in targeted areas of the host community at the four localities in Khartoum state . BPWO will work to strengthen the caring and protective environment of children through training and holding meetings with parents and community members to strengthen the mainstream protection and child safeguarding through all interventions by humanitarian actors CP working . Multi-purpose Centers facilitators and Mobile CFS Facilitators will be identified within the communities, and the local community workforce will be selected and trained on main child protection concepts focusing on child rights/ protection, PSS, and Caring for Child Survivors on Sexual abuse to operationalize the multipurpose centre and mobile CFS. Community leaders will be targeted to disuss the negative impact of physical and humiliating punishment and promote possible positive dicipline approaches in their learning with a focus on BPWO’s models “parenting without violence and safe childhood” common approaches. Cross-cutting issues, like gender, are priority concerns during humanitarian responses, and BPWOI will mainstream them across its programs. BPWOI mainstreams gender activities in all its program work by including males and females among staff and volunteers, getting and providing feedback to different groups on the project's performance, and finding ways to improve it in a consultative manner. Save the Children is accountable to the population affected and beneficiaries of the project. Throughout its operation in this area, BPWOI has shown continued solid performance and created good working relations with the local authorities and the community, thus making sense of ownership for the programs and active participation in program activities. Through the technical and operational team, BPWOI will continue replicating the efforts & coordinate with other partners in the areas with a solid commitment to its core vision, mission, and values to serve the community as to project implementation strategies. In summary, this project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risks associated with the current Health and Child protection issues in the proposed area

2 - Activities/Outputs

-Establishment of 4 child-friendly inclusive Multipurpose Community Centers (MPC) targeting a total of 4,590 children (2,745 girls, 1,845 boys) and 1 mobile Child-Friendly Space targeting a total of 2,700 children (1,620 girls, 1,080 boys) at (Jabalawlia,Umbada<sharg Elnel and Hag yousif) in Khartoum, to provide structured psycho-social services, life skills, and protective behavior, social counseling, child to child interaction, awareness sessions on protection and rights, age and gender-segregated youth and adoleBPWOents clubs. The MPC spaces will work as protection hubs and service referral points and also work as meeting points for CBCPNs when required. A special focus will be on adolescent BPWO engagement through peer-to-peer activities linked to Helth and parenting programming- Procure and equip 4 MPCs with child-friendly, and age-appropriate indoor and outdoor play/ recreational materials/equipment to support PSS and center activities for children ages 3 - 18years. FGD will be conducted with children in the target locations, to identify culturally appropriate play materials, which will include and not only restricted to slides, swings, drawing books/papers, crayons, balls, music speakers, and traditional music instruments). Child-friendly messages shall be designed and developed on child protection concerns in Khartoum State in the four selected localities area and it will also include information on the Helth support available to children, caregivers and families in child protection messaging, with clear information’s on referral printed out to economic recovery, cash and voucher assistance and/ or livelihoods support provided services.- Establishment of 3 youth and adolescent BPWOe groups with a total of 90 children (45 girls, 45 boys), 30 children per group aged between 13 to 18 years old, to provide peer-to-peer activities to other vulnerable children in the MPCs and in the community as well. The groups will be trained on child protection, child rights, mobilization, and public speaking to strengthen their capabilities in participation and community engagement. In addition, they will be equipped with materials and items that will support them with monthly activity delivery to children and parents targeting a total of 3,000 individuals (1,541 girls, 730 boys, 405 women, 324 men)

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this intervention, BPWO is committed safeguard Right holders from violence and discrimination and is aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals. BPWO will implement the project in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance during assessment, design of project and delivery of services. The project team will ensure all elements of accountability to affected population to be captured through extensive engagement and consultation with the community. Project is designed to inform all displaced about the availability and location of services. The project ensures safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory provision of services. The intervention will also carefully apply Do No Harm approach to avoid any negative impact that it might bring to the area or the targeted communities. Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. The assistance and protection measures will not undermine local capacities for self-protection as well as support the efforts of the affected population and local communities to find security and restore dignity. Project intends to respond to the root causes of the protection concerns in the area. To achieve this, BPWO embraces community based protection approach, for building capacity of local communities, service providers and government officials. Their capacity will be enhanced in analyzing & finding solutions to protection problems, inclusive of reducing threats& risks, reducing vulnerability of affected population and increasing their capacity to cope with protection problems following exposure to shock and disaster.
