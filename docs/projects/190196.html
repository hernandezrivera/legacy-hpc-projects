Project Summary

Drought in East Bale zone worsens the Water availability, sanitation and hygiene practice and the livelihoods of both host and IDPs. In the zone 277,533 (38.6% of the zone population) of the people were aid dependent in continuous manner in 2021. The drought is affecting five woredas (Dawe Kachen, Dawe Serer, Rayitu, Sewena and Lega Hidha) very intensively . Fortunately, LWF has presence in four of them and selected Dawe Kachen, Dawe Serer, Rayitu and Sewena for intervention.
The project will mainly focus on WASH and Agriculture to benefit 63,805 people directly in four woredas of East Bale. To address the gaps LWF is planning to rehabilitate four ponds in four woredas to benefit 8,720 people through Cash for work and from the pond. 280 people per each woreda will be benefited from cash for work for 70days. 740 HHs (140FH HH and 600MH HH) will get Teff seed for the next sowing season. The farmers will also be supported through capacity building training on improved agronomic practices and post-harvest technology. In addition, 400HHs (50% Female Headed) Will be supported through unconditional CASH transfer through bank.
WASH NFI will be distributed in four Woredas and will benefit 4000HHs to improve hygiene level of the community. Hygiene campaign will also be done to supplement the WASH NFI distribution and will benefit 32,685 People from Host and IDPs.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

LWF coordinates with the national, regional, zonal and woreda level coordination platforms. LWF is actively working with DRM-ATF, WASH, Shelter cluster, protection cluster, Inter Agency Accountability Working Group (IAAWG), cash working group and nutrition clusters. It completes and reports 4Ws and 5Ws in the respective clusters. The participation in the clusters ensures to avoid overlaps in responses and enhances synergy and complementarity. At the Zone level, LWF actively engages in the Go-NGO forum where implementing partners and the relevant sector office heads meet regularly usually once per month to discuss progresses, overlap issues and challenges and seek solutions. Similarly, LWF participates in the woreda level Go-NGO forums on a regular basis. LWF will continue to work with all clusters and steering committee members and report regularly

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

LWF will monitor the working context and also the possibility of GBV for example if girls and women are exposed to such situations due to their actions and unaddressed needs. LWF will respond to GBV cases based on the organizational principles and guidelines. The project will ensure women and girls are participating and their voices are heard from the project inception throughout the project implementation, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM), project evaluation and project close out. Women and girls will have a say on important aspects of the project such as during selection of beneficiaries (identifying beneficiaries’ selection criteria), verification, distribution site selection, distribution day/timing selection, and who (from the HH member) should receive the assistance. At least 50 per cent of the community Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) committees will be women and the project will ensure people with disabilities and other special needs are well represented. In addition, out of nine WASHCOs five will be women
