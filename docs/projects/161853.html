Project Summary

As part of its mandate to provide protection and solutions, UNHCR through the Protection Safety Nets Project will initiate measures through livelihoods programming activities that will provide opportunities for returnees, IDPs and affected populations to recover their livelihood and, acquire skills and capacities that enable them engage in profitable income generating activities. Vulnerable households and individuals are provided a pathway to economic empowerment, recovery and durable solutions. The project aims to ensure that all persons of concern are able to make a safe and sustainable living that meets their basic needs, contributes to their dignity, and provides for the full enjoyment of their rights.
UNHCR will implement activities in support of this project in the following locations:
• Borno (Ngala, Mobbar, Maiduguri, Jere, Bama and Gwoza)
• Adamawa (Yola North, Yola South, Mubi North, Mubi South, Michika and Madagali)
• Yobe (Damaturu and Gujba)
The Project will provide opportunities for Women and Men, Girls and Boys – using the Age Gender and Diversity approach. UNHCR will ensure the achievement of this objective through the following means
I: vocational training programmes in different crafts and Financial Literacy and Business Development training (FLT &BD).
II: Facilitate Access to Self-Employment / Businesses: Promote business-oriented community-based cooperative units. Beneficiaries in such cooperatives will be provided necessary training – vocational skills, business management, financial literacy as well as startup kits to undertake collective production and marketing of the produce.
The Protection Safety Net measures implemented through livelihoods programming will reduce needs, vulnerabilities, and heightened protection risk exposures - including sexual and gender-based violence risk exposures. It will also contribute to conducive conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and returnees, enhance community resilience and boost the provision of (or access to) sustainable livelihoods. These actions align with the Strategic Objectives of the 2019 – 2021 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Strategy, the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy (Nigeria, 2017), and the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria (2017).

1 - Needs

Now in its 10th year, the conflict in North-Eastern Nigeria continues to have a devastating impact on civilians in the affected communities. It is projected that the affected states and Nigeria as a whole have lost over nine years of human development progress since the crisis began. According to the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 28, about 2 million peoples are internally displaced in the Northeast and the returnee figures as at August 2019, stand at over 1.6 million persons in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) States. In being forcibly displaced from their places of usual residence, the displaced populations have lost their livelihoods and livelihoods assets. The poor and most vulnerable members of affected communities – being the most affected by the loss of livelihoods. The continuing insurgency and counter-insurgency operations limits their ability to access their fields and farm lands due to the fragile security environment which exposes them to the risks of abductions, kidnappings, rape, killing and other forms of violence perpetrated by the insurgents. This further pauperizes the affected populations, exerting an extremely high toll on their sense of dignity and ability to attain self-reliance in meeting their basic needs. The loss of livelihood assets often results in dependence on Humanitarian aid as is currently being witnessed in most displacement sites within the Northeast. As a consequence of the severe economic hardship that has been caused by the insurgency, most of the affected households have limited or zero opportunities to support themselves and their families. The long-term loss of skills, livelihoods and economic independence is known to be a driving factor for negative coping mechanisms which impacts negatively on the protection environment, and exposes these vulnerable individuals to heightened protection risks. The UNHCR Protection Safety Nets project will ensure that vulnerable households and individuals are re-tooled with the capabilities, assets and strategies that they could use to make a living – as an integral part of solutions for the displaced population in the Northeast. In alignment with 2019 – 2021 Nigeria Humanitarian Response Strategy, the HCT Centrality of Protection Strategy (Nigeria, 2017), the Strategy on Protection, Return and Recovery for Northeast Nigeria (2017) and the Nigerian Governments development plans, UNHCR continues to lead and coordinate efforts to deliver Protection and life-saving assistance while remaining focused on the search for solutions. Improving livelihoods through economic inclusion is a key component of UNHCR’s strategy to achieve protection and solutions outcomes for displaced populations in the Northeast. UNHCR Protection Safety Net measures implemented through livelihoods programming – will empower vulnerable individuals and households to access employment opportunities, entrepreneurship and small-scale business opportunities and business financing. These actions promote self-reliance, restoration of dignity and the sustainability of returns as a durable solution. . Increased returns have been witnessed across affected communities in the Northeast, including the voluntary repatriation of refugees (from Cameroon) and spontaneous returns. The increased returns creates an urgent need to support the returnee households with the skills and assets they require to meet their basic needs. There are existing gaps in structures and facilities for the provision of technical and vocational training in the return communities and LGAs. Limited number of actors implementing self-reliance activities in return communities creates a gap and limits access to opportunities for sustainable livelihoods engagement for a large percentage of the displaced population.

2 - Activities

Access to self-employment/business facilitated: Some of the activities to be carried out will include: • Beneficiary selection • Capacity assessments • Market assessments • Linkages to micro finance opportunities Access to Self-Employment / Businesses; additional activities that will be embarked upon are: • Entrepreneurship / business training • Financial literacy training • Business development training • Facilitate linkages and access to small business loans Additionally, Cooperative units will be formed and supported with training and business start-up kits (operational/start-up materials) to commence their businesses. The cooperative units will receive technical support and mentoring through the project year, to stabilize their businesses. The project will employ cooperative monitors to provide relevant guidance and closely monitor the units to ensure that all cooperatives run their respective businesses effectively. Vocational Skills training: The project will provide opportunities to support local integration / reintegration IDPs and returnees - women, men, female and male adolescents, as well as vulnerable host community members through vocational skills. The project will design and roll-out market-driven skills to strengthen livelihoods and the local economy. Returnee, IDP and host community men, women and adolescents will be trained to acquire skills in carpentry, electrical works, soap making, tailoring, brick making, briquette making (as cooking fuel) amongst others. All beneficiaries of vocational training programmes will benefit from Financial Literacy and Business Development training (FLT &BD) in all three States. The FLT enables the beneficiaries (individuals or households) gain the basic understanding of managing finances, savings, and good credit lines for business. It increases the likelihood that the beneficiaries will utilize the banking/local thrift services and access more finances to support their business. Peaceful Coexistence with Local communities Promoted: As a cross cutting measure – the project will support sustained advocacy and engagement with communities about the vulnerabilities and risks of specific groups through engagement with religious, traditional and community leaders - local champions who can influence others in the community to accept and support returnees. This initiative will create a platform for overcoming barriers to community reintegration.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected populations will be strengthened through the development of a community-based feedback mechanism; supporting partners to the inclusion of functional and accessible complaints and reporting mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries and focus group discussions. UNHCR is an active member of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and has community-based structures known as Protection Action Groups (PAG) within Maiduguri and LGAs where UNHCR is present such as Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Damaturu, Dikwa, Gudjba, Gwoza, Jere, Gwoza, Madagali, Michika, MMC, Monguno, Ngala, Rann, Yola North, Yola South. Through these structures, UNHCR ensures that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of UNHCR and its partners for intervention. UNHCR shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk. In previous projects, some key concerns raised include beneficiary selection, the support provided, community representatives and payment process. All issues reported will be systematically investigated and followed up in a timely manner. Received complaints shall be categorized according to the level of sensitivity and severity and responded in accordance with protection principles.
