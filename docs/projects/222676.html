Project Summary

Inter-sectoral synergies and integrated programming is planned for the locations of Tombura, Yirol East, Lafon, Magwi, Juba, Kapoeta East providing WASH services to mainly IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities; over 65% of these female. Outreach Initiative will greatly link planned nutrition, health, FSL education and protection services to the WASH program. WASH needs are strongly linked to <5 mortality and malnutrition, high disease burdens, SGBV and food insecurity. The project will reach a total of 40,000 persons in the locations afore mentioned through the provision of timely, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees, Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package and mitigation of WASH-related gender-based violence. The project will ensure communities in Tombura, Yirol East, Lafon, Magwi, Juba, Kapoeta East have adequate water supply through hand pump rehabilitations – working with existing community structures while upholding required protection protocols culminating into risk assessments to assess safety and access for mainly women and girls, since they are the primary users of water points. Water supply will further be enhanced through a sustainable approach of household water treatment, through the provision of water treatment inputs, while empowering communities to adopt self-reliant mechanisms of maintaining clean water supply at household and communal level. Since women and girls are primarily responsible for water collection for household and domestic use, the project will mainstream protection and gender programming, through a project collaborative mechanism with other projects implemented by Outreach Initiative, and with other stakeholders in Tombura, Yirol East, Cueibet, Lafon, Magwi, Juba, Kapoeta East. The project will engage men for accountable practices, reaching boys and youths too with awareness services since these are the main perpetrators of SGBV against women and girls. Sanitation services will be gender based, with latrines segregated by sex, clearly marked, lockable on the inside, well lit, and setup in locations deemed safe by women and girls after an appropriate risk analysis. Outreach Initiative will merge planned FSL, nutrition, protection programming with WASH services in order to provide a complete package to communities in these locations. Minimal packages will be provided for highly malnourished (100% HHs with SAM will be targeted) and food insecure households, these being prioritized for response to avoid a recurrence of vulnerability. Distribution of WASH NFIs will focus on HHs with identified multiple vulnerabilities such as malnutrition, food insecurity, disease burdens and those that female/child headed. Messaging will be coordinated with other sectors (Health, Nutrition, Protection, FSL) to deliver comprehensive and fully packaged information to the intended beneficiaries. The WASH project will enhance girl child education through the provision of MHM and Hygiene Kits to adolescent girls to enable continuation of learning, thereby inhibiting early marriages, while empowering them to be more resilient.

1. Needs

Chronic needs continue to manifest amongst Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in parts of Greater Equatorial and Lakes states, with women and children mostly affected. Cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict in counties in targeted have further led to chronic vulnerabilities due to weak delivery systems for essential services. Central Equatorial and Western Equatorial States play host to over 200,000 IDPs with returnee figures close to 100, 000 (58% female). Assessments in the locations targeted indicate a grim picture of the humanitarian situation. Latest Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) data lies within Phase 3/4 food insecurity mainly attributed to displacement, insecurity, floods, poor crop yields as well as lack of livelihood solutions. Over 70% of the most vulnerable persons per Outreach Initiative’s led assessments are women and children. A similar trend is observed in Lakes State. With over 70% of the populations assessed, over 51% of the populations lack an improved water source hence limited access to water. Many of the populations indicated safety concerns prevent them from accessing their preferred water source, and that SGBV is the main protection concern for women and girls. 46% of the population reported the main source of drinking water is an unimproved water source (river, swamp. Unprotected well), needing over 30 minutes to access and return from their preferred drinking water source. (REACH, 2024). Latrine use is low, with WASH based mortality and health concerns over 75% amongst the assessed communities. WASH related diseases contribute heavily to high malnutrition rates amongst under 5s and pregnant and lactating women with GAM rates above the WHO thresholds. Limited availability of nutrition services and lack of access to health services is exacerbated by the lack of adequate WASH services, thereby affecting mainly children, pregnant and lactating women, elderly persons and persons with special needs. Protection concerns remain significant, and are highly fuelled by inadequate WASH services, especially amongst women and girls, who bear the rigor of availing water for domestic use, with majority trekking long distances and spending time at water points, leaving less time for other livelihood activities and other core responsibilities. Girl child school drop outs are strongly linked to lack of MHM kits and adequate hygiene and sanitation services at schools, with adolescent girls absconding from school due to fear of reprisal as a result of menstrual experiences. Drop outs of adolescent girls further fuel early child marriages, leading to cycles of vulnerability especially amongst the female population. Provision of multisectoral services will help tackle the underlying factors identified as needs amongst the communities. Outreach Initiative plans an integrated cluster programming aimed at stimulating efficiencies and a convergence of sectoral responses inline with the strategy. Provision of WASH services, while addressing issues related to malnutrition, disease, food insecurity and protection will be at the forefront of the project. Multi – sectoral synergies create significant milestones in the humanitarian response, with provision of adequate WASH services necessary to mitigate under 5 malnutrition aggravated by the severity of AWDs, which pave way for co infections and stunting amongst children. Outbreaks of Cholera and MPOx are currently ongoing necessitating improvements in WASH standards in the counties targeted.

2. Project activities

Outreach Initiative proposes the following activities in the locations targeted: Provision of safe, clean, adequate and equitable water to the vulnerable population through rehabilitation/repairs of the non-functional water supply facilities such as boreholes, hand-dug wells and setup of food resistant sanitation facilities; Distribution of household water treatment tablets including PuR and Aquatabs on a case-by-case basis to HHs and communities while ensuring proper usage, sustainable solutions to water treatment and adequate supply for communities without adequate access to clean and safe water; Distribution of WASH NFIs including buckets, soap to enhance WASH conditions of communities. These will include MHM/Hygiene kits for women and adolescent girls; Provision of appropriate and safe and flood resistant sanitation services while considering people with special needs including people with disabilities, women, and girls through construction/rehabilitation of gender-appropriate communal latrines at displacement sites, supporting and promoting household latrine constructions, construction of institutional latrines for schools, nutrition (OTP/SC) centers and health facilities; Training of community WASH stakeholders comprising of hygiene promoters, pump mechanics, water management committees with at least 55% female representation. Training packages will range from safe water handling techniques at HH and communal level, sanitation practices, hygiene promotion, WASH in Nutrition, health, protection packages, WASH minimum standards, as well as cross-cutting issues such as environmental protection, HIV/AIDS, and SEA; Carry out hygiene and sanitation awareness promotion in the locations targeted, in a language the communities understand using several approaches comprising of house to house visits, focus group discussions, mass campaigns at water points, and public gatherings, school hygiene clubs, special needs associations; Strengthen community feedback mechanisms and coordination in the locations targeted through the establishment of community feedback structures and reporting desks for protection related issues connected to the WASH services provided.
