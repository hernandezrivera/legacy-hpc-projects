Project Summary

Humanitarian needs within proposed project locations of Masyaf and Salamieh Districts of Hama governorate continue to be high and complex. A key driver of vulnerability for both displaced and resident households, as well as returnees, is limited and stalled income levels leading to negative coping mechanisms and tenuous food security levels. These economic impacts exacerbate hardships and increase humanitarian needs in the food sector; thousands of new families have been rendered vulnerable and need aid. Loss of hope, dignity and social cohesion are emerging risks that threaten any future stability. Youth, women and children face acute vulnerability due to a reduction in basic social services, and negative coping strategies include: reducing spendings on NFIs to meet food needs, taking on debts/loans, skipping payments of debts/rent to support other needs expenditures and selling productive assets.
Humanitarian programming needs to actively balance between meeting immediate humanitarian needs, while simultaneously contributing to local resilience and transitioning off exclusively humanitarian assistance in order to obtain a decent standard of living. Helping community members earn a living is therefore a key pillar of any intervention in the current Syrian context.
Community-based Cash for Work (CB-CfW) programming represents an innovative modality for cash programming inside Syria. First piloted by AKF in 2017, CB-CfW is a joined-up approach that places community organizations at the centre of design and implementation of humanitarian programming while at the same time providing temporary livelihood opportunities to vulnerable HHs able to work.
AKF has been implementing Community-Based Cash for Work (CB-C4W) projects for six years. Through this project, AKF’s extensive experience with various CB-C4W projects will be expanded to address required social services in Masyaf and Salamieh districts. The main objective is to sustain and recover the economic livelihoods of crisis-affected people especially women and youth through:
- Providing 3-month emergency employment through CB-C4W activities for 2000 individuals, of which 60% will be female, 80% youth and 4% PwDs. PwDs will be provided with job opportunities near their homes, a supervisor will follow up with them carefully and they will handle tasks that do not go against their disabilities.
- Implementing 10 CB-C4W projects; (Public parks, collecting solid waste ...etc.) which aim at improving infrastructure and basic social services delivered to the population within Masyaf and Salamieh Districts. Each project will be implemented over the course of 3 months during which AKF will provide management and oversight. The types of projects will be determined based on the needs of the communities and will be identified through the local community and consultation committee.
- Strengthening the civic capacity in the delivery of public services by involving local communities and Local Non-Governmental Organizations (LNGOs) in planning, implementing and monitoring the CB-C4W projects.
Each direct beneficiary household will receive monthly cash estimated at 100,000 SYP. Cash transfers will be provided through AKF’s cash transfer platform via the First Micro-Finance Institution (FMFI). CB-C4W projects and lists of beneficiaries will be informed by the Project Steering Committee (PSC) and community organizations (e.g. LNGOs).

4 - Needs

Almost 10 years of crisis in Syria continue to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrians, in parallel to the fallout from the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, dramatic inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic led to extra stress on social and basic services while depriving Syrians of jobs and livelihoods. The effects of these concurrent crises contributed to a significant contraction of the economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access social services and essential infrastructure, and further undermine social cohesion. (HNO 2021). ERL needs remain large in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people (51% Female) needing dignifying early recovery and livelihood support. Before the pandemic, 41.9% of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7% can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital (UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment). About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost 75% to 100% of their income.13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance – a 21% increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. Over 90% of the population is estimated to live below the poverty line (2021 Needs and Response Summary Report).While there is a lack of in-depth information on Masyaf district, the 2021 HNO statistics indicate that negative coping strategies are a major problem across the country.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide wage-earning employment over the course of 3 months to 2,000 vulnerable individuals, of which 60% females, 80% youth, 4% PWDs, and 30% IDPs and returnees. Each beneficiary will receive monthly cash estimated at 100,000 SYP through a bank account opened at AKF’s financial service provider the First MicroFinance Institution (FMFI).LNGOs and CBOs will implement the project primarily by assessing communities’ needs, designing corresponding CB-C4W activities, identifying target groups, and implementing CB-C4W projects. AKF will provide training to LNGOs and CBOs on identifying community needs, designing quick impact project, gender equality, and management of CB-C4W projects.AKF will provide management and oversight during the implementation of CB-C4W projects in the target districts. Direct beneficiaries will be identified in coordination with LNGOs/CBOs who will generate the list of beneficiaries to be verified by an elected Project Steering Committee (PSC) based on clear criteria with a focus on women, youth, PwDs and IDPs and returnees. Process and outcome monitoring activities will be implemented by AKF directly and include baseline and end-line assessments, labour market price analysis, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring and FGDs with project stakeholders. The project adopts a gender and protection-sensitive approach based on AKF’s Do No Harm and safeguarding policies. AKF’s local, community-based approach, integrates conflict-sensitive programming to identify and mitigate unintended results of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure inclusive community participation for vulnerable groups of women, youth, PwD, and elderly.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Workers may be subject to discrimination by residents of areas targeted by CB-CFW due to negative cultural perception of some work types.Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Low.Community organizations will expand awareness of work type among targeted communities to identify vulnerable individuals who are willing to undertake such work type. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help identify cases of exploitations and take actions against them.B: Gender-related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit women participation in some types of work such as solid waste collection.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium.Female trainees will be asked preference of specific work/task to ensure their comfort. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution and an active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness-raising to facilitate positive interaction with female participants. Teams will be established with mixed genders and female leaders. C: Increased risk of inter-community tension where CBOs members might be accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries.Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium.AKF will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria.D: GBV and job associated hazards.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium.Workers will be given protective equipment. Women will be assigned to work in locations close to their residence enabling them to be familiar with the surrounding environment, and ensure easy access; consequently, minimize the risk of GBV, reduces time burdens, and facilitate family care responsibilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement.Mitigation: In program areas that are deemed high risk, AKF will delay implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel. Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties in procurement.AKF has extensive experience undertaking programmes in extremely difficult places. AKF will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement to within the Governorates. Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the program and communicate with the donor as needed. The transfer of funds to implementing unit delayed due to modification in banking regulations.Mitigation: AKF in collaboration with AKF’s head office in Geneva regularly monitor transfers, bridging financial resources to ensure minimal disruption to program implementation when needed. Syrian Pound (SYP) has experienced high rates of inflation.Mitigation: Inflation risk is mitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF manage reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation. A significant influx of IDPs and/or the vulnerability of resident populations increasing.AKF’s Humanitarian programme continually adjusts its delivery plan to cope with the IDP movements and villages circumstances change. WFP supplements food baskets when IDP flows are large, to ensure a social safety net for IDPs and host families.
