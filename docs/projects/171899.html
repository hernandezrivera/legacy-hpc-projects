Project Summary

The action will contribute to improving resilience of conflict and COVID-affected people through the provision of MHPSS services at the community level.
The project will be comprised of multiple components including direct MHPSS support to affected communities; training to key community support workers, members, medical staff and relevant local and regional authority employees; establishment of mobile MHPSS units and youth engagement and training to combat isolation of the most vulnerable population in the targeted oblasts
IOM will expand their existing MHPSS hotline services, to increase outreach to affected populations. The hotline will be operated by psychologists who are able to provide immediate psychological support and schedule follow up sessions with a team of IOM psychologists based in eastern conflict area (ECA) 7 days a week.
To enhance the capacity of local community leaders, authorities and social workers to manage increased psychological stress, in particular in the most isolated areas, IOM will roll out a series of trainings on resilience building and positive coping mechanisms. These trainings will be held across Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts by IOM psychologists with the aim of creating multi disciplinary teams at local level to sustain MHPSS support to the community beyond the project.
Recognising the recurrent risk of community stigmatisation associated to MHPSS assistance, which can restrict access, IOM will run an awareness raising campaign to combat the negative connotations associated with recognising mental health issues and reaching out to related social services. The campaign will be delivered through a variety of media at the regional and local level to maximise its reach to the intended audience.
In response to the concerns raised by the protection cluster and regional administration regarding accessibility of services, IOM will establish mobile MHPSS units to ensure that also remote communities in hard to reach and underserved areas will have access to MHPSS services. Mobile MHPSS units will combine both psychological assistance and outreach to ensure that communities/households currently unable to access service providers will eventually be able to do so. The Mobile Units will also engage all community leaders, local authorities’ officials and social workers who received MHPSS training in their service delivery. The purpose would be to familiarise them with the concerns of local communities, as well as to ensure continued MHPSS support to these communities beyond project duration.
Mobile theatre units will also be piloted as a means to engage affected communities through greater interactive methods, while bearing in mind physical distancing and other COVID-19 risk mitigation measures.
Lastly, in efforts to engage and empower youth and combat social isolation of the most vulnerable population in the targeted oblasts, IOM will conduct out reach to higher education facilities specialising in psychology and social work to identify candidates for communication skills training. The trainings are part of an effort to scale up communication skills of targeted youth but also to address the intergenerational communication gaps that leave elderly people feeling isolated and increasingly lonely. Through engagement with local authorities and social stakeholders, IOM will seek to identify elderly individuals who feel they would benefit from more frequent interaction with other community members. The youth will then be linked to elderly participants in the hope that weekly calls will help reduce the anxieties and fears that come with isolation and work to improve the overall wellbeing of the elderly participants. IOM will engage with higher education facilities to pilot this initiative as a social component of their education in psychology/social work for longevity of the activity.

1 - Needs

Communities in ECA, particularly those close to the contact line, have been exposed to 6 years of compounded stress and fear over physical threat presented by the ongoing conflict. With the outbreak of COVID 19 in early 2020, reports of psychological issues spiked as communities struggle with uncertainty of COVID 19’s effect on existing vulnerabilities. Vulnerable families have had their food security impacted (over 50% of respondents reduced food quality and over 20% cut back on other essentials to afford their food needs – IOM RIA, April 2020) with the latest NMS stating 70% of female IDP single headed households (HH) reported having barely enough money to cover food needs. Food security and lack of access to adequate health and social services have been repeatedly highlighted by partners working in hard to reach areas. With livelihoods also impacted (10% unemployment recorded in 2nd quarter compared to 8.7% in 2019 as sited in HNO) HH have reduced capacity to meet their basic needs in already difficult circumstances. This multitude of difficulties compound already mentally exhausted populations, putting HH at risk of negative coping strategies. This is all set against a backdrop of a struggling health and social support system – that lacks the capacity to manage the physical, mental and emotional strain the situation has placed on HH and individuals – as well as of a society that traditionally tends to stigmatise mental health and psychosocial support. As in many countries, COVID 19 has disproportionately affected vulnerable populations within ECA due to loss of movement, access to safe spaces and essential care services. These vulnerable groups account for many of the over 15,000 ‘lonely people’, registered with the Luhansk regional Ministry of Social Policy (MoSP) prior to COVID 19. The MoSP reported to IOM that this number increased substantially as a result of COVID as quarantine measures forced more people into isolation. The risk of marginalisation is of concern for social/medical workers who, due to risk of contracting the virus, are at risk of social stigmatisation and increased social isolation and stress (HNO). A May 2020 report by Help the Aged found that 100% of elderly people surveyed experienced COVID 19 anxiety from amongst population of which 94% had already indicated conflict related mental health issues. With the elderly representing over 36% of population in Donetsk and Luhansk oblast – and an even larger share of those most in need of humanitarian support – they are amongst the most isolated residing in remote settlements with little to no family present and limited communication networks. As a high-risk group, the elderly are likely to become increasingly isolated as a result of COVID 19, compounding their existing loneliness. Among the elderly, women are disproportionately affected by isolation (86% of respondents to Help the Aged survey living alone were women). However, this gender-based disproportionality is represented across other female age groups. HNO cited women as more vulnerable to COVID 19 due to their accounting for 82% of health/social workers in Ukraine. Additionally, women are also the ones who often have to take on additional care roles within family, which has been exacerbated by the closure of schools and social institutions. The impact on women’s physical and mental health has had even more serious consequences, with the Office of the Attorney General reporting that cases of domestic violence have almost doubled last year (795 reported May 2019, 1,511 in May 2020). Specifically, ECA has seen a 50% increase in GBV as reported in 2020 HNO. All these compounded stress factors, at the individual, family and social services level, highlight the need for broader MHPSS across communities. This need has been voiced by protection and health actors as well as government and community stakeholders at both individual and institutional levels and confirmed by IOM field offices.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Outcome 1: Persons, affected by the conflict and, exacerbated by COVID-19 enjoy quality essential MHPSS services that respond to their needs. Output 1.1: Increased availability of MPHSS services in conflict area Activities: 1.1: Scale up of IOM MHPSS hotline to provide psychological support and referrals to affected population and medical/social workers that will operate 7 days a week 1.2: Conduct MHPSS trainings on psychological first aid, burnout prevention, stress resilience techniques and other essential topics as identified by psychologists across Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. Trainings will target a wide range of beneficiaries including, amongst the others: social and medical workers; teachers; village council employees; community leaders and vulnerable households and communities. 1.3: Develop and implement an information campaign targeting communities of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts with the objective of normalising the seeking of psychological support and combating stigma associated to mental health. Carry out awareness-raising workshops (taking into consideration physical distancing and other COVID-19 risk mitigation measures) and distribution of awareness raising material in targeted communities. 1.4: Based on the findings of assessments to be conducted at local level isolated communities will be identified and, MHPSS mobile units will be established in those communities to provide psychological support in remote locations who do not have access to telecommunications or to main awareness raising initiatives. The Unit will include psychologists and a community outreach worker who will disseminate information about IOM MHPSS services hotline and conduct training sessions for the community at large. The Mobile Units will also engage all community leaders, local authorities’ officials and social workers who received MHPSS training in their service delivery. The purpose would be to familiarise them with the concerns of local communities, as well as to ensure continued MHPSS support to these communities beyond project duration. 1.5: Conduct workshops for young volunteers from social work and psychology educational backgrounds to train them in communication, peer support and empathy skills with the intention to connect them with isolated elderly men and women. An experienced psychologist will be invited to take part in the selection process to make sure the candidates meet the requirements. IOM staff will conduct monitoring calls to elderly participants to make sure they are satisfied with the services. IOM actively seeks for the views of affected population to improve project implementation and to identify existing gaps that should immediately addressed. For the urgent response to the complains and suggestion on how to improve project implementation, feedback mechanism thought the Internet and telephone will be established during the project implementation. IOM will regularly inform beneficiaries on how the provided inputs were used. Beneficiaries of the current project will be provided with the phone numbers and links to leave their comments on project activities. IOM staff that works with feedback mechanism will be trained how complaints and response mechanism works. IOM will conduct regular monitoring and evaluation activities in all project locations to assess progress against set project indicators, to collect sex and age disaggregated data and to allow for necessary adjustments that might arise as lessons learnt. Phone surveys, individual interviews and monitoring on-sight visits will be done by IOM to collect both quantitative and qualitative data to assess the level of satisfaction and how beneficiaries are impacted by project activities. Drafting of regular and ad hoc progress reports, infographics, briefs and other documents for donors and broader humanitarian community, as well as a final evaluation report meant to assess performance and inform future similar programming will also be carried out.
