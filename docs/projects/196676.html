Project Summary

Resulting in ADRA’s presence in the targeted areas of Homs and Hama, severe protection concerns and gaps in the humanitarian response have been identified, particularly regarding people with special needs, who face severe difficulties for accessing safe food and basic services. Based on these identified needs, ADRA will implement a variety of protection activities in line with the cash working group, targeting a total of 1,612 HHs. The Multipurpose Cash Assistance for PWD Project will provide approximately 6,379 people in need with multipurpose cash assistance in Hama and Homs to cover basic lifesaving and life-sustaining needs of food items, WASH and NFIs. ADRA will provide monthly assistance to the targeted families for five months through local financial service providers, aiming to reduce their reliance on negative coping mechanisms. ADRA has the required approvals for proceeding to the intervention and has already signed a MOU with FSPs companies. The MEAL team will conduct surveys before and after the provision of the cash, and will ensure the adequacy of the intervention, taking into account the beneficiaries’ needs.

3 - Needs

After more than 10 years of conflict, the humanitarian situation in Syria remains complex. People’s access to basic services across Syria continues to decline, hampered by damaged infrastructure, lack of critical supplies, and a decreasing purchasing power due to inflation and high unemployment rates. The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has reached 15.3 million, of which 70% are in severe, extreme or catastrophic need (HNO 2023). Food insecurity remains extremely high: with an estimated 15 million severely food insecure people (2 more million people that in 2022), Syria ranks amongst the ten most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021. The food insecurity particularly impacts vulnerable and marginalised groups such as pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and persons with disabilities (PwD), whose additional medical expenses and nutritional requirements are often overlooked. Across Syria, households are reverting to negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, child marriage and the sale of productive assets, which impacts the households’ long-term resilience. In Homs, more than 90% reported high limitations in meeting their basic needs with PwD particularly impacted due to additional health and hygiene expenses.

4 - Implementation of activities

ADRA Syria will identify the most vulnerable and in-need persons aiming to contribute to the SO1 target (9.1 million) and reach 1.9 million people at risk. ADRA will undertake a rapid market assessment ensuring access for all targeted beneficiaries including men, women, the elderly, and PWDs. Necessary approvals are obtained and the distribution process will be through a FSP in the targeted areas. ADRA will communicate with the beneficiaries through telephone/ text messages informing them about the objective of the cash provided, the selection criteria, the amount of money they will receive, an explanation of the process and the documents needed to receive the money, the organization and the donor providing the assistance, a telephone number for any inquiries and how to raise a complaint or feedback to ADRA. ADRA will attempt to follow the MEB and CWG recommendations at the implementation time for the transfer value, currently 440,000 SYP. Approximately one month following the distribution, a PDM survey will occur that will address topics related to the distribution, accessibility, knowledge about the project, information and complaints hotlines, satisfaction, selection process, protection measures, and the impact of the MPC transfers, if the assistance corresponded to their needs, safety and appropriateness of the intervention.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Project beneficiaries are at risk of violence at collection points, family and gender-based violence at home, an increase in community tensions, as well as the spread of disease in overcrowded spaces. All project beneficiaries are selected with protection mainstreaming and the ‘Do No Harm’ principles in mind, based on specific criteria including people with disabilities, Female-Headed Households, families containing elderly family members, and other vulnerability criteria such as people with health concerns. This reduces the possibility of tension with other community members who were not selected for assistance. If any other beneficiaries who fall within the criteria are identified during the life of the project, they will be either considered to be added to the project or will be added to the list of complementary projects. Established protection measures include:- Staff sign and undergo training on the ADRA Protection Policy and code of conduct which outline the responsibilities and appropriate behavior.- Staff use of PPE / social distancing and prevention measures against COVID-19 and cholera.- Presence of ADRA staff at FSP offices where possible during cash distributions - Collect cash at their most convenient time that they feel safe to do so. - Standard amount for CWG. ADRA has selected trustworthy service providers (SP) from which detailed reports will be requested from the SP and verified with the beneficiaries during the PDMs, in order to avoid fraud.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ADRA will ensure that issues related to the protection of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) are addressed throughout the project life cycle through the ADRA Syria protection policy. The project will be guided by IASC GBV gender guidelines and ADRA protection principles. ADRA will also conduct additional staff training in gender and protection mainstreaming, especially PSEA. All ADRA staff and volunteers undergo mandatory PSEA training at the beginning of their assignment and refreshment training annually. The complaints and feedback mechanism is also available and equipped to handle sensitive complaints. Moreover, in the cash distribution points, ADRA staff will be present for monitoring the process and ensuring the safety of the beneficiaries. In this way, ADRA guarantees that the implementation of the activity does not pose any risk of sexual abuse and exploitation for the beneficiaries of the project.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Community/family tensions/jealousy arises as the result of the intervention. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium.Mitigation: ADRA will ensure the beneficiaries, community leaders and local authorities are aware of the selection criteria of the project and are involved in the process of the design of the activity, ensuring that the most vulnerable will be selected. In addition, the use of the service provider for cash transfers is a low-profile modality which does not attract attention to beneficiaries, allowing beneficiaries to remain anonymous. Payment days will vary and relevant authorities will be engaged. Depreciation of the Syrian pound resulting in lower purchasing power. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium.Mitigation: Negotiations with the donor, MoSAL and the CWG to adjust the transfer amount Funds are not used for the intended purpose/fiduciary risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: mediumMitigation: the service provider will be carefully chosen and checked for presence on any terrorists or terrorist affiliated lists. Vendor and community monitoring systems including several levels of approvals will be in place. Cash assistance has a negative impact on local markets: Likelihood: low. Impact: medium.Mitigation: ADRA will conduct market monitoring in the targeted areas and in areas with a large targeted population stagger the transfers to minimize the impact on the local market.
