Project Summary

Needs: More than thirteen years of one of the worst crises and emergencies in modern history have resulted in the forced displacement of over 11 million people and the large-scale destruction of homes, public infrastructure, and services, including health care and water and sanitation systems in Syria. According to the HNO 2024, 16.7 million people are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria. Of them, 5.5 million are displaced. According to the SMART Survey in NWS (August 2023), The prevalence of acute malnutrition based on WHZ in both Aleppo and Idleb governorates is 4.4% (3.1-6.1% C.I). The prevalence of underweight recorded in the survey is poor at 11.8% (9.5-14.5% C.I) in Aleppo and acceptable in Idleb at 9.1% (7.3-11.3% C>I). The two survey areas recorded high stunting rates of 22.5% (19.7-25.7% C.I) and 22.2% (19.3-25.3% C.I) in Aleppo and Idleb, respectively. The highest need is in Aleppo governorate, with 909,511 CU5 and 360,552 PLWs in need of nutrition services.
Project Summary: The project aims to deliver comprehensive integrated nutrition, child protection, health, and hygiene services tailored to meet the critical nutritional needs of CU5 and PLWs within targeted areas across Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. This project will establish and manage 14 OTPs integrated within 14 PHC centers and link to them 14 RRTs. Additionally, to operate 3 SCs within 3 pediatric and maternity hospitals. The integrated nutrition program provides various essential services, including:
1. CMAM: Through the OTPs and RRTs.
2. Inpatient Nutrition Services for CU5 suffer from SAM with medical complications within the SCs.
3. IYCF-E: awareness sessions, counseling sessions, and social mobilization.
4. MUAC Screening among CU5 and PLWs for early malnutrition detection.
5. Referrals: SAM cases with medical complications, PLWs with medical problems, and disabled beneficiaries will be referred to the appropriate treatment centers for further evaluation and care.
6. Distribution of Micronutrients and nutrition supplements to prevent anemia and malnutrition among CU5 and PLWs.
7. Hygiene Promotion to improve sanitation and reduce disease transmission
8. Identification and referral of abuse survivors for comprehensive support.
9. ANC and PNC Referrals: PLWs will be screened and referred to ANC and PNC.
Protection Risk Analysis: Displaced populations, especially IDP women, may struggle to access nutrition services due to various barriers. Mobilization activities target IDP women to address information gaps. Security disruptions can impede referrals, countered by regular meetings with service providers and staff training. Coordination and communication efforts mitigate service duplication. Staff are trained to respond swiftly to emergencies despite security risks. Disabled individuals receive designated transportation. Privacy concerns in mobile nutrition services prompt safe space considerations. Female staff handle PLW concerns. The project employs a clear MEAL plan to monitor and improve activities, incorporating beneficiary feedback and satisfaction.
Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Security threats such as airstrikes and violence can hinder RRTs and affect beneficiaries. RRTs receive training for better incident response. Equitable access to nutritional services for women, girls, and GBV survivors may be limited in certain areas; RRTs target vulnerable groups and ensure private spaces for breastfeeding mothers. Staff are trained to engage with GBV survivors, and referral mechanisms for specialized support are established. Child safeguarding policies are in place to protect children from harm by staff and volunteers.
PSEA: SRD has PSEA policies in place, which include safe and effective response reporting systems and a survivor-centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected. Staff are trained to identify signs of abuse and follow PSEA policies, ensuring survivor safety.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Due to the pressing need for nutrition support in NWS and the high number of PLWs and children U5 requiring urgent life-saving nutrition services, SRD will operate 14 active extended OTPs in targeted areas, each linked to 14 RRTs. These centers and teams will be integrated into SRD-supported health facilities to provide a comprehensive package of nutrition and health services. Additionally, 3 Stabilization Centers (SCs) will be established in pediatric and maternity hospitals to provide specialized care for severe cases.This program will involve implementing CMAM services, including: (Screening of all Children 6–59 months and PLWs in the targeted location. Treatment and follow-up of SAM cases according to OTP guidelines by using the RUTF supplies. Referral the SAM cases with complications to an inpatient treatment program (SC), along with IYCF-E counseling for pregnant women and caregivers of children under two years. The counseling focus will be placed on optimal maternal nutrition and care during pregnancy, timely initiation of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months, and pregnant, lactating women, alongside skilled counseling and support, timely and safe weaning, MAD among children aged below 2 years and continued breastfeeding until 2 years of age. Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children. Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E (infant and young child feeding in emergencies) recommended feeding practices. Monitor and report on unsolicited and untargeted distribution and use of breast milk substitutes Support health/nutrition education and promotion of Other nutrition Supplements distribution). Integration with health, Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and Child Protection sectors will enhance the effectiveness of these programs. Additionally, immunization and nutrition supplies will be ensured.Stabilization Centers provide inpatient care and continuous monitoring of SAM children, treating medical complications, providing therapeutic feeding according to protocol, offering psychosocial support to families, counseling on nutrition, care, and hygiene, demonstrating energy-efficient baby food preparation, and referring stable children to OTPs for continued treatment and recovery.
