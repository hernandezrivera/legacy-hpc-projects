Project Summary

The overall objective of the programme in Bangladesh is to ensure that displacement affected Rohingya and host community adolescents and youth can claim their right to continued learning in an inclusive and protective environment leading to increased resilience, individual and community well-being, social and economic progress, and enhanced empowerment and agency for all.
Youth need assessment will be conducted with ACAPS by the beginning of the project to identify adolescent and youth (15 to 24 years) needs and adapt to ensure access to learning for female learners, learners with disabilities, and at protection risks. Design and monitoring of services will include human centred design approach. Inputs will be taken from labour market assessments and market analysis as well.
Rohingya refugee and host community youth will be supported to access relevant, quality learning through personal, social, and emotional development opportunities with access to livelihood opportunities. This will be achieved by providing basic skills: including functional literacy and numeracy, skills for life: including a stand-alone tailored life skills programme and transferable 21st-century skills, skills for work: including labor skills training, short vocational training, and long vocational training plus coaching and mentoring to gain relevant competencies and skills to have better access to the labour market. This will be achieved through networking, job placements, small business start-ups, and apprenticeships.
Basic foundational literacy and numeracy, life skills and skills work capacity building will be focusing on the areas of Food Processing, Food Safety, Food Utilization; Block Printing, Handicrafts, ICT, Mobile Phone Repairs, Solar lamp production/maintenance, Tailoring (General), Homestead gardening/school or centre gardening, provision of start-up grants and start up assets, 21st-century transferable skills, capacity building for business/soft skills (business strategy development support), provision to internship and apprenticeship opportunities with local business, support with appropriate market linkage for income generating activities, start-up kits support. Disaster Risk Reduction messaging and awareness will be added. Creation of community practice groups for social cohesion (within and between communities), women’s groups (cooking demonstrations and basic nutrition classes) will be added. Trainings/vocational trainings in the community level to build more resilience on Livelihood security and to build more bondage and social connectivity and stability among host and Refugee community. Protection/diversification of assets, improve household economy will be considered for longer term outcomes through these activities.
Capacity building on inclusive and gender-responsive education practices, crisis-sensitive planning, management, and coordination for quality provision will be ensured. Curricula/guidance developed throughout the project will be shared with the sector and partners.
NRC will continue to be an active member in the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) working group. In the event of an emergency, NRC’s operational areas in camps and host communities will have opportunities to integrate functional DRR plans, with emergency teams. Included services will be Better Learning Programme (BLP) psychosocial support, recreation activities, and temporary learning classes, delivering life-saving messages and conducting community outreach and engagement. Mobile teams will also be enabled to distribute essential learning materials to children. Needs-based approach will be used in case of a need.

1 - Identified Needs

The fifth year of the humanitarian response in Bangladesh had faced the COVID 19 pandemic, and only by the end of September 2021 was it able to operate under reduced restrictions of COVID-19 guidelines. Although, different modalities were used for providing basic assistance during this time and progress were made, Rohingya refugees remain at perilous situation for having uncertain future, primary target of climate change impacts and disaster risks. Continuous challenges remain with ever increasing learning gaps, gender based violence, inadequate WASH facilities, minimal education opportunities, and restriction of participating in livelihood activities. This ultimately means that they remain highly aid dependent. Access to quality, inclusive, diverse and age appropriate education for both Rohingya and host community children and youth is limited. According to 2022 JRP PiN, there are 534,245 children, adolescent and youth age 3 to 24 in need of support. The efforts to provide education services to refugees have been constrained by a number of factors, including a lack of space for facilities in camps that are already heavily congested, the lack of qualified teaching personnel specially females and the governments constraints pre and during COVID. The Government of Bangladesh does not allow refugees to enrol in formal education or partake in livelihood activities outside of the camps. From J-MSNA 2022, 41% of households reported food items not lasting until the next distribution, and 70% of households reported having adopted livelihoods based coping strategies due to a lack of money to meet basic needs in the 30 days prior to data collection, the adoption of strategies, which shows that sustainable coping mechanism needs to be in place for camp context for better quality of life. In the same report, 89% of households reported engaging in coping mechanisms due to a lack of money to meet basic needs in the 30 days prior to data collection. Trends have been discussed such as - Increasing proportions of households reported access to food , as well as access to income generating activities , among their top 3 priority needs, and reported levels of adoption of livelihoods based coping strategies remained comparable to 2020 J MSNA findings; this may point at households not having recovered from the COVID 19 outbreak and its secondary impacts on food security and livelihoods, with a potential for further deterioration; and in-line with these trends, and possibly also linked to reduced purchasing power and increased food prices compared to last year ( FAO WFP Market Monitor, August 2021 ), results further indicate reduced proportions of households with acceptable food consumption scores compared to 2020 J-MSNA findings. These shows a high current need for the overall context for livelihoods activities. During community consultation meetings with the authorities, they indicated the gap in response to the high dropout rate among adolescent and youth due to income generation activities, early marriage, and parents’ disinterest in education, lack of alternative modalities which accommodates access to livelihood opportunities. Across Bangladesh, the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation contribute to more frequent extreme weather events and severe disasters. This year, the humanitarian context was also impacted by massive fire in Noyapara RC camp, and in camp 8E, 9 and 10 in separate occasions. The humanitarian emergency worsened at the onset of the monsoon season, with heavy rain, high winds, flooding and landslides damaging not only the refugees’ shelters but also a reality for millions living along Bangladesh’s coast to displacement and forced migration.
