Project Summary

According to FSNAU/FEWSNET an estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and will face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, due to the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall.
An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. Among these are an estimated 2.6 million Somalis across Somalia who remain displaced primarily due to conflict, lack of livelihood opportunities and drought.
For hard to reach rural populations food security status remains poor due to the residual impact from the 2016/17 drought and drought like conditions coupled with the recent natural calamities which continue to undermine opportunities for improvement in food security status. Further, some residents from rural area who lost their livelihood through drought like climate, conflict and insurgent control, have ended up in already overcrowded IDP camps or hosted by relatives who already have limited access to food.
This is the state in Hobyo where the main livelihood is pastoral. Despite this, the district has access to a great resource; the ocean. This readily provides fish which is a food resource that has not been fully explored due to cultural beliefs and norms. Most of the residents of this areas do not consume fish, neither do they work in the opportune fish industry (OCHA, 2016) which could have provided job which in turn would improve food access. The proposed plan seeks to assist residents explore the livelihoods in the lucrative fish industry, and initiate behavior change to fish consumption through activities as enumerated in the plan.
Besides Hobyo, Kismayo also havs the privilage of being next to the ocean with the fishing industry as a livelihood source, and available fish for diet diversification. This district has a huge IDP population which will be a key target of proposed interventions. The response plan seeks also assist the residents of this district access the benefits of the fish industry. This will be done through training of youth and men in fishing skills and fishing equipment and material maintenance in a bid to improve their livelihood through the fish industry. Women will not be left behind but a selected group will be equipped with knowledge and enabling factors in food processing and preservation also in a bid to improve their livelihood. The response plan acknowledges that those disabled in the population are mostly stigmatized and a result do not see themselves as contributors to the workforce and the country’s economy. The plan therefore will seek to include this group by equipping them with skills and enabling factors for them to earn a living and hopefully improve the food security status of their families.
Malnutrition in these districts is at critical levels with exception of Banadir where the levels are very critical.The response plan seeks to contribute to reduction of prevalence through addressing the underlying cause of acute malnutrition through improvement of household food security. The proposed interventions towards this is improvement of food availability, through provision of fish and improvement of food access through improving livelihoods by creating jobs and/or expanding employment opportunities for households , through income created by the fish industry.
Government officials will take lead in most key decisions and will be involved through the
cycle for sustainability. The programs will adhere to set government strategies and policies related to interventions, and will also contribute to achievement of cluster 2020 HRP working closely with government entities and the cluster.

Mitigation Measures

For IDP in Mogadisho the main risk is displacement. for this the organisation hasmaintained a database for all beneficiaries with telephone contacts and that ofrelatives. For those in rural Mudug the risk is spontenious clan clashes. While it isdifficult to deal with this, we have ensured that services are provided for the differentclans in their areas equitably. Interclan clashes therefore have minimal effect ourprograms. Victims of GBV will also be provided with an organisational GBVfocal point who will link survivors to appropriate assistance. Programs will also ensureIDP have access to basic services in an equitable and dignified manner with equalityalso observed in job allocation in projects serving IDP population. Among IDP the riskof exclusion based on tribe is real. Most IDPs in Kismayo are from a certaincommunity who found it hard to access services in the recent past based on their tribeand skin color. The organisations' policy does not allow exclusion and actuallyreaches out to all community members to receive services. Recruitment of staff is notbased on tribe but professional competence. This has produced staff from a hybrid ofcultures who have learned to work together well. Some positions are also reserved tothe target communities and community groups that are marginalized like IDPs andwomen so that equity is achieved. All gender will be awarded opportunity to benefit inequitably. Women and youth who are mostly left out in recruitment will be consideredin all projects and positions will be awarded equitably. Needs assessment will includeall community groups so that an all conclusive, agreeable and fair assessment isreached. both men and women leaders with an additional youth representative will bepart of community health board as per organisational policy. This will ensuredecisions made are all inclusive. SAF UK policies have sought to put men at the helmof needs affecting their wives, daughters and female relatives. The organisation's AAPframework seeks to ensure men participate through affirmative action for menensuring that men are not excluded from interventions targeting women beneficiaries.Children who are often left out will not only be seen as beneficiaries but their views onservice delivery ad need will occasionally be sought by a professional child specialistso that the whole community participates in program design and implementation.Community diagnosis, community dialogues, evaluations and assessment will seek toinclude all community groups to ensure all are reached. Dialogues, evaluations andconversations will include women, children and youth with their own forum so as toensure their views are considered and used to programing. 2020 programs will seek toimprove access to women and girls , who are key beneficiaries of proposedinterventions by scaling up services to be within a few kilometers walk. Far and hard toreach areas in rural settings will be served by mobile teams so as to reduce risks andhazards faced by these groups enroute to accessing services.

In all proposed areas the environment is sufficiently safe to enableimplementation of resilience activities. All our projects have a good working relationship with the government and other relevant stakeholders. Some of our facilities were handed to the organisation by the government and work under strict supervision of the same. Others run by the organisation are community owned but supervised by MoH, possessing long term agreements witnessedand endorsed by government. In all proposed districts, project involve government entities in all activities and receive several monitoring and supportive supervision visits. The organisation is also not only registered by government to operate in the locations but also have Memorandum ofunderstanding with clear terms of engagement including relationship with government, commitment to serve and deliver, allegiance to total transparency and accountability, and commitment to deliver positive outputs and outcomes. The organisation also has strong working relationship with communities we serve working closely with all age groups who are involved right from needs assessment. In all areas, community health boards have been formed not onlyto represent the community in matters health but also provide leadership and guidance. Sustainability continues as our focus area in 2020 and the same is being entrachanced in our policies. As part of our policy and AAP framework, beneficiaries are informed of their entitlement to quality service delivery and access to utilities as per project requirement. This aspect is currentlyoperational with a strong, non- bias and functional feedback mechanism in place.
