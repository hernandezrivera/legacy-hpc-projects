Project Summary

The war in Syria continues to have a profound impact on people across the country and at least half of the people affected by the crisis are children. The humanitarian situation in the country remains severe as the decline of the economic situation exacerbates the effects of the conflict. The education sector is impacted by low availability of education services, few functional schools, shortage of teachers, and especially qualified teachers. The targeted areas are expecting large numbers of returnees to further stretch the education sector’s capacity. The impact of the COVID19, rapid devaluation of the Syrian Pound and the international sanctions are putting additional strain on the population and local administrations.
There are 133 schools in Hama and Homs, out of which only 62 are currently in service. Most of the schools that are operational are overcrowded. 47% of the schools totally miss furniture, while 53% have partial availability of furniture. In the event of an increase in the number of returnees to the target areas, it is expected that the schools will not be able to accommodate children unless additional classes are opened. All assessed schools are only partially accessible and most of the toilets are unusable. The recent Needs Assessment suggests that there are currently approximately 104,000 children of school age in Hama and Homs, and it is estimated that 60% - 70% of them are at risk of dropping out, as the areas have high numbers of returnees.
All of the areas that FCA program is covering area are marked as 3 or above on the severity scale. Most of the target location are newly opened returnees areas with the high demand for education services and often presence of operational schools is a motivating factor for the families to return to the area.
The project directly contributes to HRP priorities both in terms of geographical prioritization as well as by combining soft (non-formal education, protection etc) and hard components (school rehabilitation, school furniture, students kits etc) in education focusing on quality education.
The project will address beneficiaries' need by implementing the following activities:
Access to education related activities:
1.1. Rehabilitation or establishing of ECE rooms in schools
1.2. Provision of Non Formal Education (NFE) through remedial and catch-up classes
1.2. Rehabilitation of the school building including WASH facilities (for boys and girls)
1.3. Distribution of Student's kits and winterization items
1.4. Back to learning campaign
1.5. Setting up prefabricated classes in the locations where schools are damaged and cannot be rehabilitated.
Protection related activities:
2.1. Protection events in schools
2.2. Establishing and supporting school based protection committees
2.3. Recreational activities in schools
2.4. Community led initiatives
2.5. Psychological support for teacher (in and outside the classroom)
Quality of Education related activities
3.1. Equipping schools with furniture and teaching and learning materials.
3.2 Teachers' training
3.3. Support for children with disabilities
3.4. Setting up and supporting Parents Teachers Associations (PTA)
WASH related activities
4.1 Rehabilitation of water supply
4.2. Water trucking to schools
4.3. Provision of personal hygiene kits (especially for girls) and school hygiene supplies
M&E Framework is used to ensure that stakeholder opinions are heard. Activities will be directly monitored by FCA field staff under the supervision of the Education Project Manager. 2 M&E staff will be responsible for the monitoring of results, activities and indicators as proposed in the action. The data have been collected during the NA conducted in Oct 21-Jan 22; in the beginning of the project FCA team will collect baseline data for each indicator as per the LF. Different M&E tools have been created: activity report, school attendance sheets, child wellbeing questionnaire, entrance/exit tests, school rehab. checklist.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1: Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.) or from public health risks including Covid-19. M1: FCA will make sure that schools buildings and grounds are safe and protected. This will include stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, safe electric installation, water, etc. All School grounds should be fenced. All construction works will be supervised and monitored by technical staff (engineers and MEAL officer). FCA will ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place before work start. R2: Tensions within the community or between the organization and community due to feeling of being unfairly treated, insufficiently consulted or concerns with the project. M2: Consultation with beneficiaries and local structures, transparent process to be followed. FCA will advocate with sector/donor that that donor conditions do not undermine the ability to response adequately according to do no harm principles. R3: Delay or non-approvals of planned activities or planned processes M3: FCA works closely with local education and other relevant authorities and community members to develop a positive relationship and discuss project interventions before the project starts.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R1. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving, particularly children. The risk may be heightened for children with disabilities, those unaccompanied, orphans and younger children. M1.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practised and monitored. Polices will cover standards of behaviour for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level. Trainings and refreshers on PSEA should be done systematically. M2. FCA staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. M3. Targeted communities and staff of the learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures. M4. All FCA staff sign PESA as a part of the job contract.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1: Risks related to implementation and approvals. M1: MoU with the MoE has been signed on Jan 13, 2021. It creates background for smooth cooperation with DoEs in Hama, Homs, RD and Idleb. R2: Delays for national staff to access selected areas of interventions. Access restrictions on international staff. M2: In case FCA is not able to reach the target population, and related delay in start-up and implementation of activities a closer coordination and engagement with SARC and MoE will take place. R3: Risk of insecurity, conflict and violence. M3: FCA will regularly security SOPs and provide refresher training for all staff. In the event that the conflict breaks out, security contingency plans already in place will be followed. Evacuation to safer areas will be evoked when deemed necessary. In case of inaccessibility as a result of insecurity, activities will be moved to safer areas while trying follow beneficiaries to their areas of refuge. R4: High inflation rate. M4: In case the price of goods or services will increase, FCA will inform the donor on the difficulties to follow the agreed plan and proposes revisions. FCA will closely monitor the exchange rate of SYP and will make sure that impact of the exchange rate on the project activities is minimal. Joint action through DINGO. R5: Transfer of funds to Syria. M5: Request donors to send funds directly to Syria. Consultations through DINGO. R6. Increased returnees/IDP caseload. M6: close monitoring of the population movement
