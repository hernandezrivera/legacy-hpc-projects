Project Summary

In Somali, Gender based Violence (GBV) is deeply rooted in unequal power relations between men and women. There is very little GBV service provision particularly amongst displaced populations. Existing social, cultural and customary practices reinforce unequal power relations which also undermine the reporting of incidences of GBV therefore limiting survivors’ access to services. The consequences of this are increase in the risks of complications such as STIs, HIV, unwanted Pregnancy, trauma, and repeat offences from perpetrators. There is a gap in delivery of preventative measures as well as direct and support services for survivors. This has resulted in most survivors left without adequate medical and psychosocial security. The update of assistance is also low due to low reporting. The COVID19 event has further reduced services provision and access.
The GBV/FGM COVID19 rapid assessment reveals that accessibility of GBV services in the community was below average. Only 47% of the respondents indicate they had access to GBV services while 53% do not have access to GBV services.
A CCCM assessment and a joint Humanitarian Gap Analysis (July 2020) in IDP camps uncovered that distant water points, markets, health facilities/schools, poor lightning, lack of doors on toilets and lack of disaggregation of sanitary facilities are some of major factors that increase GBV exposure. ; Women and girls of IDPs and host communities continue to express the need for dignity protection and material support such as dignity and hygiene kits, solar lanterns, torches and mattresses to meet both basic and protection needs .Women and adolescent girls from 18 – 50 years old comprising those living with disabilities are exposed to Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse. The anecdotal reporting of de-prioritization of CMR services for rape and other survivors of GBV is a major concern for GBV service provision
A July 2020 “GBV/FGM COVID19 assessment reveal that 38 percent respondents confirm increase in GBV incidents. The number of GBV survivors call to GBV hotlines grew by 283% in FMS and 767%in Somaliland. Similarly, GBVIMS 2020 data show increase in intimate partner violence (55%); Physical Assault and rape at 61% and 12% respectively in comparison with Physical Assault and rape at 56% and 14% of 2019.
Given ADA’s expertise in community engagement and dialogue. GBV programming will be integrated into the existing health and nutrition service in Galgaduud and lower Juba. Other interventions will include Capacity building and sensitizing the target communities on issues of human rights and GBV preventive and response measures by improving skills and knowledge through trainings and focus group discussions thus helping the community to prevent, cope and respond to GBV violations. Provision of medical/referral, psychosocial services as coping and response mechanism. Training the community elders on camp management and coordination to improve coordination and foster peaceful co-existence between the IDPs and the host community an Improved settlement facility.

Mitigation Measures

Our mitigation strategies will include Community engagement of all groups of people especially marginalized groups to ensure a joint transparent process in the response mobilization phase. ADA will engage local disability and women groups in the process to ensure these groups are not left out. Strengthening of gender inclusive community-based protection mechanisms through Information campaigns will be boosted to ensure impartial and timely access to GBV protection services, especially in situations of displacement. Protection monitoring will be used for both referrals and evidence-based advocacy, especially with authorities responsible for the implementation of durable solutions for displacement affected communities. ADA will build trust in the efficiency of our Complain and feedback mechanism to identify any situation of exclusion, and to condition implementation to be an equal and ensure direct access to all communities. We will ensure that complaints are followed up immediately and corrective measures are implemented on time. In site planning by provision of solar lights around water and sanitation facilities will reduce risk in camps.
