1 - Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict in Libya continues to severely impacts on children’s wellbeing and their families’ livelihoods. Since 2011, dozens of children have been killed and injured by airstrikes, shelling, small arms fire, improvised explosive devices and explosive remnants of war (ERW). As a result of armed conﬂict in 2011 and renewed conﬂict since 2014, explosive remnants of war contamination continue to be a reality and major concern in numerous cities across Libya, impacting on public infrastructures such as schools, roads and hospitals. The ERW threat is exacerbated by the mines and ERW left from previous conﬂicts.The threat from explosive hazards has been further exacerbated since the escalation of conflict in Tripoli since April 2019, clashes in Murzuq in August 2019 and recent waves of intensified clashes around Tripoli, which have resulted in increased civilian casualties and further displacement. Communities in southern and eastern Libya suffer from similar conditions, such as Mizda, Sebha, Benghazi, Derna, Tawergha, and Kufra.The intensified conflict in Tarhuna, Sirte, and southern Tripoli increased IDPs from from 373,709 IDPs to 425,714 IDPs due to the intensified conflict in western Libya. Children and adolescents are highly exposed and particularly at risk of ERW injury because of their inherent curiosity, mobility and love of play. Possessing a narrower field of vision than adults, they sometimes do not see the danger until it is too late. Children caught in a blast are less likely to survive because their small bodies are more vulnerable. Those who survive are likely to experience serious physical, psychological and social problems and may have their life chances affected by the consequences of the incidents. Children, including migrants and refugees, and IDPs returning home are in particular exposed to the threats from landmines and ERW because they are often unaware of the dangers of playing in or traversing hazardous areas. Landmines and ERW prevent access to reconstruct homes, roads, schools, health facilities and other essential services. They deny access.As in Tripoli and other parts of Libya continues to intensify, as well as in other parts of Libya, it is crucial to ensure that conflict-affected communities and particularly children/adolescents and their caregivers receive Mine/ERW Risk Education to reduce the likelihood of incidents and injuries from mines, ERW, and other explosive hazards and enhance reporting mechanisms including through mass media. Children affected by armed conflict and their caregivers (including displaced, returnee, non-displaced, migrant and refugee). The objective is to equip boys and girls with knowledge and skills on how to protect themselves from the risk posed by mines, explosive remnants of war and other explosive devises in order to reduce the likelihood of incidents and injuries and enhance reporting mechanism.

2 - Activities

1. Conduct training of community workers, teachers and NGO workers (using Training of Trainers delivery modality) to deliver Explosive Ordnance Risk Education for children and caregivers, ensuring gender considerations; 2. Undertake baseline/knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) assessments with children in priority locations to determine the level of awareness and save behaviors on mines, ERWs.3. Implement pre and post behavioural tracking tests to determine the level of awareness and safe behaviours on mines and ERWs.4. Implement community and school-based mine and ERW risk-education sessions for con-flict-affected children and their caregivers, including internally displaced persons (IDP), migrants and returnees in the affected areas, ensuring promotion of gender participation; 5. Develop, produce and disseminate different public information and messages through focused mine and ERW risk education campaigns and advocacy, under leadership of LibMac; 6. Ensure that all information reported on Mines and ERW related incidents is referred to LibMac and UNMAS to enable a timely and appropriate survivor’s assistance response and vice-versa when incidents affecting children require immediate protection and MHPSS support.
