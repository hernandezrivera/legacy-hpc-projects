Project Summary

Through this intervention, Ihsan Relief & Development (IRD) seeks to be part of the solution as the education system in Syria has been greatly impacted by the 13-year crisis, which has also caused immense suffering to the civilian population. It is currently fractured & under-resourced, making it difficult for children to access education services.
According to 2024 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO); the protracted crisis in Syria, further compounded by the aftermath of the February earthquakes continues to negatively impact the education system. There has been a 4% increase in PiN, compared to last year. This emphasizes the urgent need for emergency education services to support up to 7.2 M school aged children and teachers, including Children with Disabilities (CwD). The PiN comprises 6.9 M children 5–17 years & includes 230 K education personnel. There are more than 2.45 M Out of School Children (OoSC) and over a million who are at risk of dropping out. Based on the IDP Sites Integrated Monitoring Matrix (ISIMM Plus), 57% of IDP sites in NW Syria do not have access to primary schools. It is crucial to provide teachers and other education personnel with adequate support, including training & incentives considering the critical nature of their role. It is worth noting that by the end of last year, the Education Cluster serving NW Syria was the 3rd most underfunded cluster after CCCM and WASH. Given these challenges, funding for education remains a critical gap. In reference to to the OCHA factsheet as of Dec 22, 2023;
- The total number of school-aged children in NW Syria is 2.2 M.
- The number of children in need with severity from 3 to 5 is 1.9 M in NW Syria.
- The percentage of schools that are partially or fully damaged (due to conflict/earthquake) is 29%.
The proposed intervention's overall objective for 2024 is to improve equitable access for school-aged children to safe, inclusive, & quality learning environments to foster their resilience. This will be achieved through implementing a comprehensive package of education activities to 40 schools in which are located in high-needs areas of NW Syria (in addition to rehabilitation school buildings).
The proposed project's locations will be within the following 16 sub-distracts (in camps and off-camps based on needs):
- For non-formal education comprehensive support: Atareb, Daret Azza, Teftnaz, Maaret Tamsrin, Harim, Dana, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Qourqeena, Armanaz, Darkosh, and Ariha sub-distracts. 23 schools 17600 children, and 1195 teachers & education personnel.
- For formal education comprehensive support: Azaz sub-distract. 2 schools 400 children, and 40 teachers & education personnel.
- For rehabilitation: Afrin, Sheikh El-Hadid, and Ma'btali sub-distracts. 15 schools, 9000 children, and 525 teachers & education personnel.
The total direct BNFs is: 28,760 (27000 children "14850 G, 12150 B 'including ~2% CwD' " and 1760 teachers and education personnel "966 W, 794 M")
All proposed activities are in line with the Education Sector’s HRP objectives. The project is going to address education needs through a comprehensive package of support in addition, to conducting school rehabilitation in Olive Branch area. IRD seeks to increase children's enrollment in the targeted schools by supporting a quality learning environment.
IRD can't significantly influence the security situation but will provide additional safety & security training for teachers to handle security concerns with students. IRD has chosen relatively safer locations where displaced people move to rather than from. To prevent any violations of Child Safeguarding Guidelines (CSG) by education workers, IRD will provide CSG & Code of Conduct (CoC) training for all staff. If a violation occurs, a committee consisting of the field education team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, and education supervisor will investigate & communicate with the EDs for appropriate disciplinary action based on IRD's CSG policy.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The proposed project activities and interventions are as outlined below:IRD will conduct digital Back to Learning campaigns In order to minimize gathering; BTL will be implemented in digital ways 'low-cost with widespread' by posting the BTL brochure campaign on the front page of local internet network providers. The BTL materials will appear on the mobile screens once the community members want to log-in to the internet. The enrolled students will receive formal (SiG) / non-formal (SLP) education services in addition to textbooks distribution. All enrolled students will receive recreational activities. All teachers & education personnel will be provided with monthly incentives to increase their motivation and ensure their retention in schools (the incentives will be based on the 'Teacher’s Incentive Harmonization Gaziantep Hub'). The project will cover the schools’ monthly operational costs, including recreational materials, refreshment, cleaning, hygiene materials, and stationery, and the provision of students' and teachers' kits. The quality of education will be improved through contextualized teacher training and further support to their professional development by delivering (CSG, TiCC, SNAP, & PSEA). Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be a component in the activities of this project. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held. Due to Cholera & COVID-19, there will be comprehensive precautionary measures in these schools, including periodic sterilization processes for classrooms and WASH facilities, hygiene awareness sessions for students, training for teachers & educational staff, and hygiene provision materials for all schools. A tree planting activity will be conducted in the non-formal education targeted schools through children’s participation. This activity will enhance student leadership and community engagement. It’ll raise awareness about care and preservation of the environment as well. Also, students will gain a sense of real achievement. In addition to provision PSS and MHPSS activities for children and adults. To enhance children's access to safe learning environments in EQ-affected areas of Afrin area; another 15 schools (due to conflict or earthquakes) will be rehabilitated & provided with furniture.MEAL plan is aligned with project's log frame. Surveys will collect feedback on the implemented activities. Monthly updates will be maintained via IPTT for progress tracking. IRD ensures accountability via transparent communication: email, complaint boxes, WhatsApp.IRD will conduct awareness-raising sessions PSEA for children, delivered by teachers. Complaints related to PSEA are received through IRD channels & addressed directly by IRD focal point.IRD's risk management approach includes assessing potential hazards and mitigation measures. Given the unpredictable nature of the conflict, like shelling or displacements, IRD monitors the project areas closely and conducts weekly security reports.
