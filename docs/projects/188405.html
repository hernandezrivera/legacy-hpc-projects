Project Summary

WVSR with its partners will be operating in Idlib (Harim district), Aleppo (Afrin and Azaz districts), Raqqa (Tel Abyad) and Al Hasakeh (Ras al Ain) 18 locations, supporting 10 PHC and 5 Hospitals. The current 3 MMU will be transitioned to 2 static clinics and a mobile emergency team. This will include support with salaries, operating costs (electricity, fuel for generators, water supply, equipment, medical supplies – pharmaceuticals and consumables) and transport.
WVSR’s health program will align programs to implement the EHSP with partners for primary health services, ensuring local communities receive the best possible care. This is contingent on adequate funding, staff capacity, supply chain and pipeline continuity, and security that does not result in damage or closure of services. WVSR and partners will be supported to adapt to changing circumstances to continue to offer services. WVSR will work with hold regular meetings with donors, local governorate health authorities, partners (on a monthly basis or as needed) and other actors to meet these basic needs in conjunction with the health cluster, of which they will actively participate.
Essential lifesaving primary health care according to the cluster guidelines, this will include child health, reproductive health SGBV prevention and response. Treatment of communicable and non-communicable disease including basic mental health services. All health programming will take into consideration COVID-19 prevention and IPC measures in all health facilities. WV with partners will support 10 PHC, and 5 hospitals and 3 MMU and 1 Rapid response team with free medical treatment. Where the health facility is unable to meet the needs of the patient, referrals will be provided with follow up, and in Atmeh will support an ambulance service for patient transfer and referrals during medical emergencies All health programs will focus on contributing to responding to the COVID-19 pandemic with provision of PPE, infection prevention and control (IPC) protocols in health facilities and with staff including provision of oxygen concentrators at the PHC.
Capacity building of health staff and community health workers linked to health facilities' on topics such as prevention and management of communicable disease (CD), non-communicable (NCD) diseases, IPC, reproductive health (RH) including family planning, SGBV, STI management, and Integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), based on capacity needs
Community health outreach with trained CHW, social workers and SGBV specialists to support home- based care, outbreak prevention, immunization awareness, PSS, SRHR and SGBV awareness and response. COVID-19 prevention, homebased care and vaccine awareness.
Extended Program of Immunization (EPI), centers supported with a mobile outreach team to cover remote areas and communities that are unable to attend the EPI centers. These centers will be in civil buildings for ease of access for children and their caregivers. Each EPI center will have 3 vaccinators and 2 social mobilizers. Social mobilizers will also be engaged in vaccination campaigns on the importance of vaccination and follow up for children that have not returned for subsequent doses
Support to EWARNS-Covid-19, covering salaries, transport and operations in the 4 labs that can manage PCR tests within NWS and NCS for all COVID-19 related surveillance and response. This project will have the support of WHO for technical guidance and, a reference lab in Turkey to ensure accuracy of testing protocols.
Monthly reporting from health facilities on patient services, staffing, medication consumption etc. in conjunction with the technical, MEAL and accountability teams, assessing outputs, reach and acceptability, with third party monitoring on a 6-monthly basis. It includes feedback from questionnaires and FII from staff, patients and community/health leaders.

4 - Needs

In conflict, economics and COVID-19 remain the key underlying needs in NWS. The number of people in need of health services is over 2.5 million and expected to increase as reduced funding means a number of services have to close down. Thus challenging organisations to ensure the provision of integrated primary health care services according to the EHCP as well as lifesaving and secondary health care services (NWS HC situation report June 2021). Chronic shortages of health care workers in North Syria have disrupted the functioning of and access to essential services, particularly reproductive and maternal health. Shortfalls of safe essential medicines and limited services means availability and access remains a challenge, with just 58% of hospitals and 53% of PHCs in Syria being fully functional (HNO 2021) There is a risk of outbreaks due to low EPI and COVID-19 vaccine coverage will overwhelm the health system. For Covid-19 only 8.5% have 1 dose and 4.6% fully vaccinated. (WHO: WoS bi-weekly situation report 1-15 Jan 2022). COVID-19 infection will remain a significant risk for people in Syria in the foreseeable future, and vulnerable groups remain at high-risk for severe disease and death, and will overload the already stretched services. EPI coverage is still low. For example, in NWS, a vaccination coverage cluster survey conducted by ACU noted a low coverage of most essential antigens with coverage for BCG 65%, HepB 45%, Penta1 63%, Penta3 38%, MMR1 40% and TT5+ 18

5 - Implementation of activities

WVSR and partners will work with local communities to ensure appropriate health services are available that meets community’s standards. This includes operating primary health care centers with free services to all people in the local catchment area. The centers will negotiate with other providers to offer referrals for services that they cannot offer. All staff will be hired locally so that the training and support will benefit the whole community. They will also be trained in safeguarding, PSEA, and accountability. Community outreach by locally trained CHW, social workers, MHPSS specialists, and nutrition officers to households and through community meetings to provide information, training, advice and referrals. This will be done in a culturally sensitive way using male and female facilitators. WV will support the partners in building their capacity in project management, financial management and reporting and grant management etc. This will build capacity for the future and enable them to be more self-reliant. The EPI centers will operate in community centers to give wider access to the service, with health and nutrition education conducted to support caregivers. The EWARNS system will be run and managed by ACU our implementing partner. They will provide reporting back to WV and the donor of all activities. The planned budget for 2022 is 16.4 million

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WVSR will undertake contextual and security risk assessments to decide on location of service provision, including safe access. WV will engage with local authorities to ensure safety of community, medical staff, services and commodities. Through community engagement WV will manage expectations and the effect if health services may need to be curtailed. They will build relationships with neighboring services and set up referral mechanisms to enable communities to access services if needed. Mitigation of Protection risks for children, SGBV, adolescent pregnancies, barriers for minorities and those with disabilities to access services. WV will develop clear communication to manage community expectations including updated service mapping, operating hours, roles of staff. For safety of health workers, WV security focal points will engage with WHO the cluster and community to ensure that health staff are informed of changes in context. WV will report any attacks on health workers to ensure that all cases are tracked. The program will also focus on prevention and response to GBV to mitigate stigmatization, family resistance to girls’ mobility, and lack of trust in services through community education, and referral f survivors for any services they may wish to access. Staff will be trained in clinical management of rape, and how to counsel survivors.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA. And it is committed to ensure that all people –women, girls, boys and men –are treated with dignity and respect, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. All staff will be trained in their responsibilities related to PSEA. WVSR has mechanisms in place to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers. including policies that all staff sign, character checks of all new staff, and policy review with partners. WVSR will train first line workers, assign field focal points with partners. WV field and national focal points will raise the awareness in the community of their rights, and how they should expect to be treated with special focus on child friendly material. WV will provide regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with clusters and the PSEA network led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists by WV field staff and is constantly monitored through the project lifecycle. WVSR has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents. WVSR also has an internal online platform called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely anonymous reporting with impartial response.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There is a risk of health worker shortage as staff may leave Syria due to deteriorating of social and economic conditions, which will impact the ability to maintain health services. This is a medium risk and WVSR will provide approved salary and benefits for staff, and PSS services as they need. Increased cases of Covid-19 or other outbreaks may result with health staff absence to treat patients. This is highly likely over the project period and will highly impact the program. The project will monitor staffing and plan to ensure adequately trained staff are available or provide referrals and where necessary assistance for patients to attend another facility. All staff will be encouraged and supported to get vaccinated and provided appropriate PPE with training on use, and refresher training on prevention and treatment. Health facilities will set up isolation or separate treatment facilities for patients. There is a moderate risk of attacks on health workers, and the project security staff will monitor the context and provide regular updates and training to ensure that health staff are aware of security risks and how to mitigate them. Health facilities will have evacuation or hibernation facilities in place. Stockouts of medical supplies are a moderate risk and the project has approved suppliers of quality medications and will keep at least 1 month buffer stock to reduce the risk of stockouts. The project will liaise with donors and WHO about contingency stocks.
