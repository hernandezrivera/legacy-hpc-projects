Project Summary

With this project IOM aims to provide critical shelter and non-food item assistance to households affected by natural disasters or other shocks, improve living conditions, social cohesion and resilience as well as contribute to reducing environmental impacts of the displacement.

Needs

Camps and sites remain congested, seriously impacting physical and psychological well-being, especially of children, adolescents, women, and persons with disabilities. The round 12 Need and Population Monitoring (NPM) report indicates an average area of 18.76 m2 per person, significantly lower than the sphere standard recommended for site planning of 45 m2 per person. Accordingly, most shelters do not adhere to the covered living space of 3.5 m2 per person. While there is a clear need to take measures to increase covered living space across the camp, the severely limited availability of new space means that the majority of households will have to remain in their existing sheltetrs on their existing sites through 2019. The lifetime of the bamboo under local climate and conditions, however, is only estimated at 6 to 18 months, and many of the existing shelters are therefore unlikely to withstand the 2019 monsoon season. There is therefore a need to increase the durability of shelters in the short-term whilst prioritising the move to new sites to increase covered living space as and when new and decongested sites become available. Protection issues also present a major concern, particularly for young children, women and older adults. The lack of adequate lighting in shelters and public spaces was reported as a top safety concern followed by danger or violence when collecting firewood. Both male and female key informants highlighted that the recent monsoon season had weakened the shelters. Bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, resulting in a situation in which shelters can easily be broken into. Several such cases resulting in thefts were reported. Female key informants also emphasized the discomfort in existing shelters due to humidity, especially during the hottest portions of the day.

Activities or outputs

Shelter Sector CoordinationIOM, as lead-agency of the Shelter Sector, will deploy and maintain a coordination team composed of a sector coordinator, IM and technical capacities to coordinate shelter activities. Shelter/NFI activities1. Contingency stockpiling: IOM will procure and stockpile 60,000 Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs) in the IOM-managed Shelter/NFI Common Pipeline, and replenish supplies based on the stock levels. IOM will maintain the Common Pipeline system with a streamlined application process and logistic capacities which enable the release of supplies in the immediate onset of disasters or other shocks.2. Emergency Distribution: IOM will distribute emergency shelter and essential non-food items for affected populations in instances of severe weather events, natural disasters or other shocks. This will include the collection and verification of field reports in conjunction with site management, distribution of materials and the provision of transportation, site preparation, and shelter set-up support through Cash-for-Work for Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVIs). Supplies will be released from the Shelter/NFI common Pipeline.3. Transitional Shelter Assistance: To respond to the need to increase the durability of the existing shelters and move towards more robust temporary/semi-permanent shelter solutions that will last for years, IOM and partners will support 60,000 Rohingya households to upgrade their shelters through Transitional Shelter Assistance. Through this beneficiary-driven process, Rohingya families will be supported to carry out shelter improvements and regular maintenance activities by providing (i) durable shelter materials: treated borak and metal footings, (ii) shelter construction and maintenance training, (iii) technical assistance, and (iv) monetary inputs: voucher system. Support will include provision of continuous monitoring and technical support to facilitate the proper application of in-kind materials through IOM and partner teams on the ground in various camps.4. Mid-Term Shelter: Where sufficient space and a site plan are available, IOM will design and construct 2,000 mid-term shelters in sites identified by site management. These households will benefit from training and durable solutions (treated borak and metal footings) and designs will be tailor made based on the specific needs of each case. The most vulnerable households will be targeted, prioritizing those at most risk of flood and landslides, female-headed households, youth-headed households and families with chronically ill members and/or special protection needs.5. Lighting and NFIs: IOM will provide lighting and other NFI support to 60,000 refugee HHs to increase safety and reduce protection concerns. Improved solar light home systems will reduce safety concerns in areas with limited light, while complementary NFI support will be provided in the form of cash assistance to transfer decision-making power to the refugees, provide livelihood opportunities and stimulate the local economy. 6. Alternative fuel and cooking options (LPG): To address the lack of access to cooking fuel of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar while simultaneously reducing health and protection risks and curtailing environmental degradation, IOM will distribute stove kits and gas refills. All items in the Common Pipeline will be appropriate for the needs of women, men, girls, and boys, ensuring that the specific and increased needs of the most vulnerable are met. IOM will work with beneficiary communities throughout the project duration to ensure greater community ownership and an understanding of specific needs. For example, the shelter will specifically facilitate the inclusion of female family members into the shelter upgrade training programme and work the protection team to develop tools to quickly identify specific needs and provide tailored support or cash where necessary to ensure shelter is adequate.
