Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to improve access to housing, land and property rights for returnees, displaced persons, refugees and host communities in Mayo-Sava, Mayo-Tsanaga and Logone-et-Chari divisions in Far North region of Cameroon.
Violence perpetrated by armed groups in the Far North of Cameroon has led to forced displacement of populations and caused many human rights concerns. In Round 22 (March 2021) of the DTM , 82,586 households of 514,747 individuals (43% male, 57% female) were identified, including 341,535 IDPs (68%), 48,902 out-of-camp refugees (10%) and 124,310 returnees (22%). According to Round 22 report, returns have continued to increase at a slower pace since 2019 but were high between January and March 2021 (7,869 households) for only three months, compared with 10,927 for the whole of 2020. Reasons for 40% of return are access to arable land.
During 2021, 1287 HLP cases (50,97% of women) were followed up in the Far North in 20 villages by the NRC’s team. The cases identified related to the lack of property documents, forced eviction, secondary occupation, inadequate houses, insufficient arable land, access deprivation. Another HLP issue in the Far North is related to military operations conducted by the Cameroon army against NSAG. Those military operations affected around 7500 persons who were either evacuated from their homes or whose access to their farming lands was blocked. It is also important to note the rise in inter-community conflicts over the insufficiency of natural resources, which have caused, for example, the forced displacement people in the Logone-et-Chari in 2021.
NRC will reinforce its response and provide support to displaced populations and vulnerable host communities to access their HLP rights. NRC will deliver information, awareness and counsellings sessions to IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host communities on HLP issues. .
Additionally, NRC will provide legal assistance to conflict-affected population (IDPs, refugees) in need of securing their HLP rights. NRC will assist vulnerable individuals to obtain signed agreements from local authorities and traditional leaders (chief of villages) for beneficiaries at risk of forced eviction . Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. This process will contribute to the prevention of forced evictions.
Furthermore, NRC will build the capacity of local and traditional authorities on HLP issues, including through the provision of training and coaching on HLP rights and CDR (Collaborative Dispute Resolution), to strengthen their HLP response. NRC will also train humanitarian actors operating in sectors such as shelter, WASH, education, and food security. This will help ensure that these sectors prioritize land tenure agreements instead of the direct and customary land transfer in their interventions and prevent or solve land-related conflicts. For due diligence approach, NRC will work with beneficiaries of Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), Shelter, Wash and Education programmes to secure access to land. In order to avoid or reduce forced evictions, NRC will assist Shelter, Wash, LFS beneficiaries to access land with increased security of tenure.
In order to strengthen the coordination and give further visibility to the needs of affected populations with regard to access to land, housing and property, NRC will lead the HLP Area of Responsibility. This will also contribute to an integrated and multi-sectoral response to HLP from national and local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors.
NRC will advocate for the HLP rights of conflict-affected populations to be respected. Workshops with relevant government officials and humanitarian actors at national and regional levels will be held in order to enhance the access to HLP rights for forced-displaced persons.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Whereas security of tenure and improved access to HLP rights are essential to protection and dignity of people affected by displacement, forced displacement has resulted in the loss of HLP rights. The most common livelihood activity among IDPs, out of camp refugees and returnees is agriculture. However, there is a decline in agricultural activity following displacement due to the loss of property, including land and/or livestock and other means of income generation. In general, IDPs and refugees arrange informal agreements to access land or rent houses and farms. As tenure is insecure, this can result in forced evictions. Most land transactions in rural areas are based on customary law and certified by a ‘certificat d'abandon de droits coutumiers’. Based on customary practices, and when available, traditional authorities give plots of land to displaced populations for housing and farming purposes. Unfortunately, the HLP rights of these displaced communities are not secured/ respected. These customary agreements need to be strengthened to prevent forced evictions and conflicts. In the Far North Region, a steady return movement is observed with an estimated 124,310 returnees as of March 2021 . As IDPs return to their place of origin, various HLP issues arise including secondary occupation, destroyed/inhabitable homes, missing property ownership documentation, scarcity of agro-pastoral land, and difficulty accessing land for female heads of household. Challenges in relation to HLP rights during displacement and upon return can result in major conflicts among displaced and host communities, leading to disruption in social cohesion and escalating tensions. The community as a whole stated that COVID 19 would not have had an impact on obtaining HLP rights, however, a problem of stigmatization and social cohesion with the proximity of the borders could be envisaged.Capacity building in HLP rights and CDR: NRC will build the capacity of local and traditional authorities on HLP issues, including through the provision of training and coaching on HLP rights and collaborative dispute resolution (CDR). Local actors play a key role in this process especially local government agencies involved in land related dispute resolution. NRC will also train humanitarian actors operating in sectors such as shelter, WASH, education, and food security. This will help ensure that these sectors prioritize land tenure agreements instead of the direct and customary land transfer in their interventions and prevent or solve land-related conflicts. Information and counselling sessions on HLP rights: ICLA will deliver information and awareness sessions to IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host communities on HLP issues. NRC mobile teams will also provide orientation and counselling in displacement and return areas, including IDP sites and refugee camps.Legal assistance on HLP: Additionally, NRC will provide legal assistance to conflict-affected population (IDPs, refugees, returnees) in need of securing their HLP rights. It will assist vulnerable individuals to obtain signed agreements from local authorities and traditional leaders (chief of villages) for beneficiaries at risk of forced eviction. Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. Advocacy for the promotion of the rights of displaced people to HLP rights: Advocacy initiatives will be organised to reduce any other barriers to access HLP rights. NRC will reinforce its role in HLP Area of Responsibility (HLP AoR) coordination both at regional and national level.
