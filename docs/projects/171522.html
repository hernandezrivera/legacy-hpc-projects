Project Summary

From the 2019 HRP report, access to education in Somalia has reached a historic low, with more than three million children out of schools. The national Gross Enrollment Rate (GER) is 30% and 26% for primary and secondary education respectively. The situation has further been compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic which has led to noteworthy disruption of schooling in Somalia with over three million children being enable to attend school even as the government makes efforts to contain the pandemic. Outcomes of a multi-sector joint assessment on "IDP influx" conducted in Berdale District in September 2020 revealed that there we no IDP schools in the IDP sites in the district apart from one school in Berdale town as confirmed by 85 per cent of the respondents. Only 30 per cent of the respondents mentioned that school aged children attend school in their village, whilst no children have reportedly attended school since displacement. The core reason for not attending school was stated as not being able to pay school fees and inability to buy other educational materials. The schools in Berdale are 1.5 to 3 km away from the IDP sites, making it extremely difficult for IDP children to access them. In reference to the education cluster PiN for 2021, 59,116 school-age children are in total lack of humanitarian assistance in education i.e.12,076 in Jowhar, 9,291, in Berdale, 36,555 in Baidoa and 1,194 in Diinsor. However, INTERSOS will target a total of 5,321 children (2,754 girls and 2,567 boys) in Jowhar, Diinsor, Baidoa & Berdale Districts collectively with the aim of ensuring access to continuous education, in line with Somalia Education Cluster framework and objectives.
In Jowhar District, 1,400 students (721 girls, 679 boys) in 11 schools will be targeted by this project. The proposed project will support 7 already established schools managed by the community namely: Shaikh Hanfi Primary and Secondary School, Imaamu Shaafici Primary and Secondary School, Buulo-Play Primary School, Shaikh Hussein Ade Primary and Secondary School, Horseed Primary and Secondary School, Shaikh Ooyaaye Primary school, Shaikh Hassan Barsane Primary and Secondary School and 4 new IDP camps namely: Airport IDP camp, SNAIPIASA IDP camp, Shaikh Omar IDP camp and Jiliyale IDP camp. In Diinsor District, INTERSOS will support 4 public schools with a targeted student population of 1,218 (560 girls and 658 boys). INTERSOS also targets 950 children (550 girls, 400 boys) in 8 IDP camps in Berdale district namely: Tawakal (310 HH), Kayow (331 HH), Towfiq (300 HH), Tosilow (351 HH), Bulo Idow (340 HH), Wargarasle (306 HH), Baqey (260 HH) and Koror (245 HH). In Baidoa District, INTERSOS will target total of 960 children (520 girls and 440 boys). Additionally, INTERSOS will also support 793 children (403 girls and 390 boys) in 5 IDP schools in the areas of Wadajir and Horseed. The project envisages to provide the immediate needs of the school going children including scholastic materials i.e. learning kits, curriculum books, school furniture, and recreational materials. From the total target of 5,321 children, 532 children (301 girls and 229 boys) with disabilities and other special protection needs will be supported through a robust referral system in order for them to access equal education and will also be linked to INTERSOS' protection sector. To foster gender inclusivity and protection in its interventions, INTERSOS will ensure both girls and boys receive services equitably and 500 girls receive dignity kits and sanitary pads. The most critical gender concerns include access to quality education and protection from sexual violence. The project will also provide emergency school feeding, water and construction of gender segregated wash facilities. Using the reflective circles approach, INTERSOS will train 160 teachers (80 female and 80 male), CECs and students in "Education in Emergency Contents" and "Life Skills Development."

Mitigation Measures

To reduce the risk of exclusion of marginalized community’s/ minority groups fromaccessing humanitarian services/assistance; INTERSOS will adhere to thehumanitarian principle of non-discriminatory with respect to neutrality and will ensureto promote right based targeting linked to criteria-based selection to access services.INTERSOS will also give special consideration to people living with disabilities andother minority groups as they have special cases for assistance unlike others.INTERSOS will advocate for more resources by giving adequate data and informationof people in need so that humanitarian and development actors can synergize forsustainable solution to the overall problem of displacement. INTERSOS will engagewith all community members including the marginalized, the disable, women, men,children, boys and girls to ascertain their needs, risks, protection concerns, preferenceand as well understand their coping mechanisms. This information will be collected,analyzed and monitored to ensure holistic discovery of the situation hence reduce therisk of exclusion of marginalized communities to access humanitarian services.INTERSOS will also build the capacity of the marginalized/minority groups to adopt tosituations in emergencies, disaster mitigation, and build their resilience portfolio.Secondly, to reduce the risk of women, girls and boys excluded from consultations anddecision-making processes; INTERSOS will endeavor to support to build the individualself-protection capacity through training so as to contribute to high self-esteem amonggirls and women. Adolescent girls in schools will be trained on life skills development,decision making and protection to build their capacity to develop positive copingmechanism. INTERSOS will have a spectrum of meetings and community forums to have an active role of women and girls in decision making and bringing solution totheir problems. This will also enhance the relevance of the project as well inducessustainability. Furthermore, INTERSOS will continuous build rapport by having anappropriate, relevant and suitable feedback and complaint mechanism in place.Besides complaint and feedback box which will be placed in convenient locationswithin the community to improve communication and contribute to decision making,INTERSOS will provide beneficiaries with a hotline and complaints number thusensuring inclusion of the largest possible segment of beneficiaries. INTERSOS willcommit to promote internal whistle blowing/reporting of SGBV cases and any reportedcase of SGBV incident will be kept with utmost confidentially and proper proceedingfollowed to ensure justice is prevailed to the victim. INTERSOS will form womencommittees will also be used to address women issues including access of girls toeducation. Thirdly, to address protection concerns stemming from displacement;INTERSOS will conduct community sensitization forums to allow local acceptance and reduce community hostilities during crises and displacement as communities areasked to consider fundamental human rights with respect to gender, age and diversity.Moreover, during crises, family composition changes as children and other familymembers are traced nowhere, in this respect, INTERSOS will employ the expertise ofthe protection department to allow families who don’t know the whereabouts of theirloved extended assistance. INTERSOS will also build the capacity of the displacedcommunities to cope with impacts of the displacement as asset mapping and servicesreferrals are maintained to ensure steady positive coping mechanisms. Staff fromINTERSOS will ensure to have distribution locations that are easily reachable to allwith specific attention to women and children with protection concerns receivespecialized treatment and services. INTERSOS will ensure to have supplies in time soas to induce speedy access of humanitarian services to avoid further damage.

Please provide further explanation

This project proposes to support IDP's children within the Inter Agency StandingCommittee (IASC) framework and principles on durable solutions for internallydisplaced populations, which recommends that IDP's should enjoy withoutdiscrimination, a minimum access to basic education among other rights, alsoas part of building their resilience in the face of the challenges they face. In thisregard, this project aims at supporting IDP children not only access school, butalso transit to public schools that are in the host communities to bene􀀀t fromquality education within the national education system. In the search to findlocalized and durable solutions to the education problem, INTERSOS has foryears worked on promoting the “school Cluster approach” where children fromIDP schools who attained higher than Grade 3 are transitioned to a nearbypublic school to continue learning. The transitioned children will equally besupported with transport fare, fees, learning kits, and other educationalmaterials to achieve uninterrupted access of learning. Most importantly, theproject activities are aligned to the delivery of the national education outcomes,strategies, approaches and systems in place for the country as outlined in theNational Development Plan. Explicitly, INTERSOS will promote the use of theapproved curriculum books of the Federal Government of Somalia. This willensure that the IDP children are not marginalized from the mainstreameducation system and are able to easily transit into the public-school system.Incorporation of WASH, gender, child rights and protection, safe hygiene andsanitation practices like washing hands after toilet use, among other lifesavinginformation and life skills education will enable the learners and communitiesto build their resilience during disease outbreak and/or crises. As interventionsare intended to begin to shift to formal learning, the project will also respond tothe educational needs of school dropouts from the IDP camps and the host communities through accelerated learning program to overcome challenges ofinterrupted schooling linked to floods, poverty, and over-age or disadvantagedand vulnerability. INTERSOS considers reaching out to 200 (100 female) for theaccelerated learning program with learners transitioning to the mainstreamformal education. In this project; INTERSOS seeks to promotecomplementarities with other ongoing projects and therefore the VTC center inBaidoa currently funded by UNICEF will be used to enrollover age children, youthand adolescents to access marketable professional skills. Professional coursessuch as tailoring, tie and die, electrical skills and computer will be provided atthe center. During activities implementation, particularly trainings, INTERSOSwill use the reflective circle approach to inspire community-based solutions tobarriers to education in the 5 targeted districts, thus ultimately contributing to amore sustainable and lasting impact.
