Project Summary

UOSSM is proposing to directly implement child protection programming aims to address the CP concerns through the following interventions:
In order to enhance the capacity of CP staff to respond adequately to the identified CP concerns NW Syria, Training will be done on CPMS, case management, family tracing and reunification FTR and PFA. Moreover, the child protection networks (CPNs) will be established and received basic training on child rights, community mobilization, awareness raising and follow up support. Women, men, youth and adolescent boys and girls will be members of these CPNs. They will be equipped with the right skills through training, coaching and mentoring to operate as entry point for CP awareness raising and referral mechanism in the targeted communities and to enhance community mobilization on CP rights which will improve reinforcing the social structures for child protection. The project provides focused psycho-social support services through child friendly spaces (CFS). caregivers, youths and community leaders encouraged to become involved. Generally, girls and boys including adolescents in the CFS will participate in different kinds of activities to develop their personal and social skills such as cultural, life skills, and recreation activities. Similarly, parents and caregivers will be targeted with structured activities through parenting programmes to help them understand, prevent and respond to protection risks for children. In conjunction with the CFS, mobile teams will also be established to conduct recreational PSS activities as well as focus on mobilizing communities to better protect their children. Moreover, they will conduct service mapping and coordinate with the humanitarian actors in the targeted areas to establish and strengthen of localized referral mechanisms to specialized child protection services and other services; Case workers will also link with the CFS and mobile teams to ensure provision of specialized child protection services through mobile outreach, counselling and case management services addressing children and caregivers’ vulnerabilities that are related to disability, physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of child labour, child recruitment and child marriage aiming to support children to reach their full potential . UOSSM will support Child Protection Networks (CPNs) in identifying entry points to access Child protection cases. CAFAAG cases will be assisted to access formal and informal education, and livelihoods opportunities that are commensurate to local economy. Active family tracing will be required for cases that need reunification and follow up visits conducted to ensure reintegration is going according to plan. Children with disabilities especially children with intellectual disabilities will be provided with special needs rehabilitation programme to support their integration with their peers. USSOM will use the harmonized massages developed by the CP sub-cluster to raise awareness on some of the issues (family separation, physical harm, child labour) as well as sharing information with the affected communities on the available services in the targeted areas.

Needs

Children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killing and injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to the conflict. The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psycho social well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. Many are without civil documentation to prove their identity, access basic services and safeguard their rights.According to Protection Sector DRAFT HNO 2019-chapter (2019) v.3 – 09-12.2018: It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations. Respondents in those assessed communities where discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance emerged, identified unaccompanied girls as the most likely group to face such discrimination. The conflict and its impact on children and families have fueled negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children. According to child-protection-situation-monitoring-dashboard October 2018 conducted by the child protection monitoring task force which is an initiative by the child protection sub-cluster- Syria (turkey) consisting of Syrian and international NGOs (UOSSM is a cofounder member) supported by UNICEF: After interviewing 278 key information KIs providers in 80 communities in Aleppo and Idlib by child protection monitoring teams working in the non-governmental organizations: 87.05 % of KIs reported children in need for focused non-specialized psycho social services.58 % of KIs who reported children engaged in type of work that prevents them from going to school. 74.46% of KIs that reported children living in households with persons with specific needs (PWSN) engaged in child labour. 58.99% of KIs who reported children being separated from their usual caregivers.

Implementation of activities

Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to child protection concerns in Syria:UOSSM will conduct the following training for the staff and CP networks to enhance the quality of the project’s services. •PSS and Psychological First Aid (PFA). •Communication skills. •Group facilitation skills.•GBV core concept.•child protection minimum standards CPMS.•Family Tracing and Reunification .•CP case managementSupervision mechanism for working staff will be put in place by deploying qualified supervisors and team leaders.Community-based child protection interventions are available for girls and boys in targeted locations -By establishing the 3 CP centres in Idlib and Aleppo, after hiring gender balanced staff who will be chosen from the IDPs and host community in the served area with a good reputation and act as key persons in their surroundings. Each CP centre will be equipped with CFS to provide safe space where children will receive focused psycho social activities. All the required actions to enhance the meaningful access will be taken such as consultation with children and parents on the activities’ timing, the appropriate location, and safe infrastructure specially to enhance access for children with disability. The staff will be trained on CP safeguarding policy and sensitisation sessions on code of conduct will be held for the staff, children and parents. Each CFS will receive 10 groups (4 groups under 10 ,3 adolescent girl’s groups and 3 adolescent boy’s groups) each week for 8 weeks. the curricula will be used to be age- appropriate and aims to Create a safe space and focus on fostering resilience, self-esteem and dignity. Pre-post evaluation to measure the impact of the intervention. -Parenting skills programs will be conducted through the CFS and outreach activities and targeted IDPs parents and vulnerable parents from host community by trained and gender balanced staff. -CPNs will be established from local community .youth will be engaged in the CPNs to increase leadership skills and meaningful engagement of youth in the project activities. Following CPNs training, UOSSM will mentor CPNs, supporting them to facilitate their own meetings, and ensuring that meetings are regular and help them identify the proper focal points for advocacy messages -survey communities in the areas which covered by our -services with coordination with CPMTF in monthly basis, -awareness raising campaigns in the community through home, camp and schools’ visits for all age groups will be conducted by the outreach team with engagement of the CPNs in facilitation the sessions.Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in prioritized locations Provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centres and outreach mechanisms.The case management team will be linked to each center with coordination with outreach teams and will respond to children with or at risk of CP concerns thorough assessment tool the needs, risks, and protective factors for each child will be identified.The team will use the documentation forms developed by the CP sub cluster and will be trained on the case management SOPs. supervision mechanisms will be put in place. Case workers will be equipped with updated localized service mapping. trained and supervised special education facilitators will provide children with special needs with rehabilitation program based on assessment of each case and with follow up plan for parents .the M&E Plan aims to achieve the following objectives:•Monitor the changes in the numbers of Beneficiaries of the project per month.•Monitor and assess the quality of services provided.*Establish clear data flow channels between the different stakeholders.
