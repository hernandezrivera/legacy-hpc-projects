01/01/2022

Project Summary

The goal of Community Aid for Fisheries and Agriculture Development (CAFAD) FSL HRP 2022 appeal is to enhance food production and reduce over-dependence on food aid through livelihood support and capacity building initiatives. These will support the FSL cluster aim to reduce the number of people in IPC 3, 4 and 5 in the selected counties.
CAFAD plans to provide conditional food assistance through FFA mechanism and conditional cash transfers (social skills and safety nets) to 10,000 vulnerable households (4000 women, 3000 men, 1000 boys, and 2000 girls with at least 5% of these being PLWD) in Nyirol, Uror and Longechuk Counties in alignment with 2022 FSL Cluster objective 1 that to “Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3) in a way that prevents the spread and transmission of COVID 19”. Community development and asset committees will identify the most crucial asset like dykes, roads, small-scale irrigation systems etc. that needs rehabilitation or building to improve long-term food security and resilience.
In alignment with FSL Cluster objective 2, CAFAD plans to support 30,000 HH (12,000 women, 9000 men, 3000 boys and 6000 girls with at least 5% of these being PLWD) in all the target counties with main season crop, vegetable, and fishing equipment. Also, CAFAD plans to conduct livestock disease surveillance, vaccination and treatment campaigns targeting 160,000 heads of livestock against worm infestation, diarrhea, pneumonia, trypanosomiasis and ectoparasites in Nyirol, Uror and Canal/Pigi Counties. A total of 125 CAHWs (36 women and 89 men) will undergo refresher training in conjunction with the County department of livestock and fish resources. One cold chain facility will be rehabilitated and maintained in Nyirol, Uror and Canal/Pigi County.
In order to establish a link between humanitarian intervention, ongoing peacebuilding efforts in South Sudan and capacity development, CAFAD proposes training on proper safe management of food supply chain, adaptive food production, income generation skills targeting farmer field schools, small grants for petty trade such as village savings and loan schemes. These activities are aligned to FSL cluster objective 3 and will target 4000 HH (1600 women, 1200 men, 400 boys and 800 girls) in all the counties above.
Women’s needs, rights, prevention of GBV, and their voices will be taken into consideration in all the interventions above ensuring integration and inclusion of youths in reconciliation and peacebuilding. Accountability to the Affected Persons (AAP) will be considered by the formation/training of gender-inclusive committees with complaint and feedback mechanisms including regular monitoring/review meetings in all interventions. All the activities proposed here-in will be conducted in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 through maintenance of social distance, use of masks, frequent sanitization of hands and compulsory use of other MoH SOPs and guidelines.

1 - Needs

From HNO 2022 data, there are 187659, 167344, 128646, 90901 and 44888 IPC+3 persons in Fangak, Uror, Nyirol, Canal/Pigi, and Longechuk Counties respectively. This number represents severe food insecurity for 95%, 85%, 85%, 85%, and 60% of the entire population in the respective counties. Of major concern is Fangak and Canal/Pigi Counties where the population suffered devastating effects of flooding as from May 2021. UNFAO (report Jan 2022) on the impact of the flooding on livestock and crops shows that 251,000 livestock in Jonglei alone drowned on floodwaters and others died due to lack of forage. Shoats (sheep and goats), typically owned by poorer and more vulnerable households were more affected. An IRNA assessment conducted in August 2021 by CAFAD and other organizations operating in Fangak (Phom and Manajang) found that a population of 64,395 persons have been displaced by flooding across the area. The communities were in dire need of humanitarian assistance and are mostly surviving on water lilies and other wild foods, especially in Keew, Juaibor and Phom Payams. Most of the farms had been submerged by the floods before the harvests with 100% destruction of crops especially maize and sorghum which had been planted earlier in the season. Preliminary FAO analysis indicates that about 65107 Ha of cultivated land has been damaged due to floods, with an estimated loss of 37 624T of cereals, which is expected to have negative consequences on the food security of the affected populations.FSNMS round 27 (Feb 2022) indicates that the main drivers for the high food insecurity experienced in the Counties is from compounding effects of flooding, intercommunal conflicts disrupting livelihoods and high food prices. Other household-level idiosyncratic stressors including GBV, high morbidity, and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of essential basic services like health and WASH facilities make the situation unbearable.About 70% of people in Longechuk County are farmers according to UNFAO with main staple cereal being maize contrary to other parts of South Sudan. The major problem faced by farmers is pests and disease, lack of quality seeds and extension services. Other factors leading to the high food insecurity in the county are insecurity due to interclan feuds recent one being on February 2022 leading to displacements and constant tension.An IRNA assessment conducted in August 2021 by CAFAD and other organizations operating in Canal/Pigi found that a population of 35,462 persons have been displaced by flooding across the area. The communities were in dire need of humanitarian assistance and are mostly surviving on water lilies and other wild foods especially in Diel, Kurwai and Alam Payams. Most of the farms had been submerged by the floods before the harvests with 100% destruction of crops especially maize and sorghum which had been planted earlier in the season.IPC Acute Food Insecurity situation in Uror County for April-July 2021 was projected at phase 4 (Emergency) while the IPC for Acute Malnutrition for children below age 5 years is projected to be critical for the same period with the likelihood to deteriorate without the current levels of humanitarian response in the County. The main triggers are high prevalence of diseases, poor quality and diversity of food. The County has an estimated population of 182,016 persons with more than 61% of these living below poverty line according to World Bank classification while 73% depend on humanitarian food assistance as per REACH assessment findings.

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) gifts the beneficiary an opportunity to use cash in a manner that they deem fit and is appropriate in meeting their range of needs, that the use of cash and or vouchers enables to stimulate markets and provides the beneficiaries with an assistance that is accessible via a local market system but restricts to a certain extent what recipients can buy or where they can make their purchases. Cash transfer programming also enables households to take care of their priority needs, is cost-effective, affords beneficiaries their dignity, supports livelihoods, strengths local markets and promotes financial inclusion.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project has been designed in such a way as to stimulate and promote market functionality. CAFAD understands that markets contribute to both food security and livelihoods and are critical features for a community’s social-economic survival and progress. The design of the project is such that it stimulates market functionality by ensuring affected target groups access food, essential household items, fuel and other types of relief or services; in addition, beneficiaries may be able to access additional and urgently required agricultural inputs and services and afford them opportunities for wage labor or link them to buyers for their produce.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The patterns of relations between men and women are influenced by the marked inequality within the community. CAFAD’s Gender approach (2020-2024) aims at ways of increasing the impact of the program and is committed to gender-sensitive indicators to assess the impact of actions on the situation of men and women and extend of power relations between them. CAFAD acknowledges that gender inequalities impact negatively project outcomes and hence the need for strategies that enhance gender equality. So, the organization endeavors to take account of differences, experiences, concerns and needs specific to both sexes so that the projects it implements benefit both men and women to the same extent. This has increased the effectiveness and improvement of project quality. Gender equality empowerment will be integrated into the entire awareness program. This will be targeted to women and girls to ensure that they understand their rights, particularly on matters related to food security and how to access those rights. Men will be targeted with the same in order to re-socialize young boys on the government’s commitments to gender equality as a constitutional right and the reduction of gender-based violence. CAFAD has continuously encouraged women leadership in the entire project site through involvement in the Boma Project Management Committee. CAFAD Policy seeks to mainstream gender throughout all programming, ensuring that indicators reflect the importance of different roles that men and women play in sustaining their household’s livelihoods. CAFAD will seek to increase women’s involvement in decision-making and promote equal power relations in all processes. Tackling the issue of gender-based violence (GBV) in communities is a key component of CAFAD’s approach to gender equality. CAFAD Country Office and the Field Offices will adopt a “do no harm” policy to ensure that Project approaches do not give rise to gender-based violence or other forms of intimidation on women. Where land is required for purposes such as group farms and demonstration plots, written consent from the owners if private or local authorities if communal will be sought before use of such lands. Beneficiaries’ risk to violence during registration and distribution will be minimized by having teams assisting women and men on separate desks. The distribution will also be restricted to between 8am and 5pm to allow the beneficiaries to reach home in time- for the women to perform their family roles and avoid GBV caused by delays at the distribution sites; the timing also helps in safeguarding against abduction (of boys and girls) as these perpetrators fear daylight. The number of Distribution points will also be increased to make sure beneficiaries do not travel over long distances to access our services. This will also be helpful in this era of COVID 19 as it goes a long way into making workflow faster.
