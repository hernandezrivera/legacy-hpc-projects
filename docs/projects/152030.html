Project Summary

WFP will provide unconditional food-assistance to the most food-insecure, crisis-affected populations.
Life-saving general food assistance (in-kind food and cash-based transfers) will be delivered to IDPs, returnees and host populations in North-East Nigeria (Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States).
Early recovery and resilience to shocks will be promoted through asset creation and preservation, increased livelihoods opportunities, and enhanced agricultural value chains.
Joint multi-sectoral assessments and Emergency Food Security Analysis will be conducted regularly to monitor the nutrition, food security, and markets situation in crisis-affected states.
A gender-transformative approach that reflects the needs of women, men, girls and boys will be adopted. WFP will mainstream protection throughout its work, supported by a solid understanding of the operational context and protection analysis. Training and capacity building of staff and partners in protection and humanitarian principles will include on-the-job coaching. WFP will ensure that affected populations (women and men) will be consulted throughout the programme cycle; beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be tailored to their preferred means of communication. Beneficiaries will continue to be empowered with knowledge on their food assistance entitlements and their rights, including WFP’s zero tolerance policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) will be embedded in M&E tools, complaint feedback mechanisms and training of partners and staff.
WFP will focus on concrete operational solutions to avoid situations in which people assisted by WFP could be exposed to SEA. Along these lines WFP will enhance its support to income generation activities targeted to women, expand their engagement in the production and use of fuel efficient stoves, deploy women as beneficiary registrars, monitors and security personnel. The referral system and monitoring system will be reviewed to maximize user friendliness and access by assisted populations as well as operational adjustments for problem solving. Particular attention will be given to supervise the retailers and operators in the distribution of cash based transfers. WFP will support milling activities for the grain commodities it provides and will implement effective solutions for the provision of condiments and cash. Deep field presence through the hubs and proximity to affected populations will be enhanced for these purposes. Three experienced international and six Nigerian protection officers will be deployed. Reporting will be done on a monthly basis through standard WFP reporting and meetings. Regular oversight and evaluations will be conducted.
WFP will continue the collection of key information on beneficiaries and assistance provided as part of its M&E activities. Monthly monitoring will include distribution and activity site management; beneficiary and entitlement verification; and ensuring adequate protection and gender measures. Lessons will be regularly drawn from monthly process and biannual outcome monitoring and reporting, and periodic reviews and evaluation to inform strategic and operational decisions.
WFP will further consolidate partnerships to enhance national and state-level emergency preparedness and response systems, including in food security and nutrition assessment and monitoring, early warning, protection, gender analysis, targeting and registration processes, safety nets, cash-based transfer capacities and supply chain.
Where security and access to land allows, increasing livelihood activities will complement food assistance to support access to productive assets, income generating activities and self-reliance in the long-term. Assistance will be coordinated with government distributions, to ensure food assistance is complementary and fills gaps. Through an integrated approach to analyzing and addressing food and nutrition security, integrated, multi-sectoral interventions will be encouraged, for example through collaborations with the Nutrition, Health, WASH and Protection Sectors, among others. WFP will also continue to proactively engage with a wide range of federal and state actors, including the Ministries of Agriculture and Rural Development, Health, Budget and National Planning, Finance, the National Social Investment Office (NSIO), Women Affairs and Social Development, PCNI as well as other federal and state institutions. Furthermore, WFP and the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) will continue to strengthen cooperation between the two agencies. WFP will continue to partner with a number of local and international NGOs and will work with civil society organizations to continue strengthening the capacities of national actors.
The project addresses the underlying causes of the protracted crisis by further integrating humanitarian and development assistance and improving disaster risk reduction in alignment with WFP’s dual mandate and the international policy debate on bridging and operationalizing the humanitarian-development nexus, thus using a development lens in its humanitarian responses, and aligning early recovery and development interventions accordingly.

Needs

The persistent and widespread humanitarian crisis in the northeast of Nigeria continues to cause repeated displacement, protection concerns and unrelenting food and nutrition insecurity. Millions of children, women and men bear the brunt of the conflict, which has decimated livelihoods, crops, livestock, infrastructure, education and basic services. Of the 1.76 million people that remain displaced in the three most affected states (Adamawa, Borno and Yobe), nearly 80 percent are women and children. In recent months, thousands of people in dire need of assistance have newly arrived at areas of relative safety, placing increasing pressure on humanitarian actors to meet their most basic food, nutrition and other needs. The Cadre Harmonise (CH) report released in March 2018 indicated an improvement in the food security situation when compared to the previous report released in November 2017. The positive trend in the food security situation can be attributed to somewhat improved access to affected populations coupled with the scale up of humanitarian assistance by the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community to deliver food and nutrition assistance as well as livelihoods support, along with favorable agro-meteorological conditions during the recent growing season and slight recovery of markets. Despite an improvement in the food security situation, about 3 million people were projected to be experiencing food insecurity in June-August 2018, according to the CH (March 2018). In spite of some improvement compared to last year, access to farming/grazing land, labor market, productive assets and livelihood activities remains limited. Given the current levels of food and nutrition insecurity, below-average crop production, disrupted livelihoods, and high staple food prices, food security and nutrition situation remains extremely fragile. Large populations in the northeast remain dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their food and nutrition needs.

Activities

(1) Provide unconditional food assistance and income-generating activities to food-insecure IDPs, returnees and host communities affected by crises [contributes to Food Security Sector Objective 1]. In 2019, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 650,000 beneficiaries with general food assistance (in-kind food, cash-based transfers) in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States. Food assistance will be maximized from the beginning of the year up to the lean season and will decrease during the final quarter of the year (post-lean season and harvest period). (2) Provide conditional transfers to food-insecure persons, including women, young people and smallholders [contributes to Food Security Sector Objective 2]. In 2019, it is planned under this activity to reach up to 150,000 beneficiaries with conditional food assistance, increased livelihoods opportunities, asset creation and preservation, and natural resource management in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa States. In 2020 and 2021, it is planned a gradual increase in the number of people assisted to reach 200,000 and 250,000 beneficiaries, respectively. (3) Support the technical capacity of the federal, state and local actors in information management systems, vulnerability assessment and mapping, monitoring and evaluation, safety net management, food technology and fortification, supply chains, nutrition and emergency preparedness and response, integrating gender [contributes to Food Security sector Objective 1, 2, 3].
