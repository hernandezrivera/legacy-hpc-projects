Project Summary

The proposed action will improve the living conditions of displacement and conflict individuals in Borno and Adamawa states across Maiduguri, Monguno, Gwoza/Pulka, Madagali through a multi-sectoral area based response. The affected populations in need of assistance will receive through Food Security, WASH, Shelter, Education General Protection and ICLA (Information Counselling and Legal Assistance) services building on NRC’s response under the 2022 HIP and ongoing interventions within the same targeted
localities to maximize the impact. Aligned to the HIP priorities and NRC’s view that the current emergency is protection centred all activities will be focused on reducing protection risks like exploitation, physical risks of movement, and transactional sex, among other risks and negative coping mechanisms caused by the lack of access to basic services. The action will equally focus on the displacement and conflict affected children provide and maintain access to safe, inclusive and quality Education to out of school
children. A comprehensive approach will be adopted to address identified barriers to education which include structural and economic barriers that hindered children access to education. The action will also build on the protection sector efforts towards the centrality of protection to ensure that NRC and other partners are taking a protection-focused lens in the Northeast response. Aligned to the
HIP2023 strategic priority, NRC will seek to address the growing and unexpected needs caused by floods and communal clashes through the use of the “Crisis Modifier”/”Disaster Preparedness” to ensure a timely response with Borno and Adamawa. Lastly, to contribute to better humanitarian access and protection, NRC through in-country, regional and global advocacy teams, will build on advocacy efforts to bring attention to the crisis in Nigeria.

1 - Needs

An estimated 23,5 million across Nigeria are expected to be in Crisis (CH Phase 3) by June -August 2023 with an estimated 3 million people in the BAY states facing poor food security outcomes( CH Phase 3-4), 276,107 in CH Phase 4 whilst 2,975 in famine like conditions (Cadre Harmonise Oct-Dec, Annex 11). As the insecurity remains, flooding and high prices of agricultural inputs and a depreciation of the local currency persists, about 3,4 million people in people in Adamawa (1 million) and Borno (2,4 million) Statesare projected to be in CH Phase 3 (Crisis) in 2023. According to the NRC MSNA (December 2022, Annex 2), about 90% of the assessed households were food insecure with 64% having "Poor" Food Consumption Score (FCS), 26% relatively food insecure at "Borderline" FCS and only 9% food secure in "Acceptable" FCS category. Comparing the two states, Borno had the worst food security outcomes with 74% of the population in "Poor" FCS category whilst Adamawa state had 55%. About 64% of the respondentsin both states indicated food as their main and immediate priority, with 9% reporting that they had not received any form of assistance. All LGAs in Borno state had more than half of their population food insecure with Hawul LGA having 82% of the population food insecure, Biu (80%), Jere (76%), Gwoza (72%), MMC (69%) and Monguno (62%). In Adamawa state, Madagali is the worst affected with 66% of the population food insecure followed by Girei 62%, Yola South and North 54% being food insecure andForfure 57%. Additionally, 94% reported that their food stocks would only sustain them for 3 months. Food commodities were reported to be available in the local markets as confirmed by 86% of the respondents. However, key market access constraints included physical market access at preferred times due to conflict related risks, eroded purchasing power and food commodities prices increases. Poor access to agricultural inputs was another key food security factor highlighted with 57% reporting high prices as themain deterrent. As high as 93% of the assessed population expected poor agricultural productivity the following season. The proposed locations of intervention for this Action are showing an CH Phase 3 and the food security outcomes are expected to deteriorate during the 2023 (Cadre Harmonise, Oct-Dec 2022).
