Project Summary

Providing access to free and quality nutritional treatment for children under five at ATFC and ITFC level, but also free and quality mental, pediatric and maternal health care (including assistance to GBV survivors) for children under five as well as pregnant and lactating women;
Providing access to health promotion activities at community level, including sensitizations (i.e., IYCF, reproductive and mental health, hygiene and COVID-19), training (MUAC for Parents, cooking demonstrations etc.) conducted by CHWs and matrones;
Improving the quality of care in the supported facilities through better care conditions (i.e., minor WASH rehabilitation, capacity-building and skill transfers, availability of nutritional and medical inputs) and organizational services in the supported facilities (i.e., free of charge referral and counter-referrals, patients monitoring and follow-up, supply chain management); and,
Strengthening the capacity of the targeted health facilities to prepare for and respond to emergencies (including disease surveillance at facility and community level, preparation of contingency plans and deployment of mobile clinics). In that perspective, this action aligns with USAID/BHA’s goal to contribute to empowering conflict-affected communities by reinforcing their resilience and giving them the tools to respond to crises while preparing them for the transition to recovery.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Within Mokolo HD, ALIMA’s data analysis shows that more and more people living outside of supported areas come to seek treatment in ALIMA’s supported facilities, as the other non-supported health facilities are either dysfunctional (Tourou) or non-functional (Toufou). For instance, between January and October 2021, the number of OPD admissions at the 5 health facilities covered by 2019-2020 and 2020-2022 BHA grants represented an increase of 27% compared to the same period in 2020. Recurrent shortages of medical inputs in non-supported health facilities witnessed in 2021 have also contributed to exacerbate this disparity. Besides, the presence of the refugee camp of Minawao further impacts the local health system. Children under five from Minawao accounted for more than 10% of the admissions into the ITFC and pediatric unit of the Mokolo Regional Annex Hospital (ALIMA, Activity Report 2021). When it comes to reproductive and maternal health care, data available at HD level shows that the maternal mortality rate amounts to 86/100,000 live births (DHIS2, 2021). This could be explained by widespread socio-cultural practices favoring at-home deliveries with the support of traditional birth attendants rather than assisted deliveries in health facilities. Against this backdrop, it seems necessary to intensify sensitization activities at community level through the existing network of CHWs and the implementation of ALIMA’s matrones strategy. As regards to mental health care, the health system is partially functioning at HRAM level. Nonetheless, a rapid needs assessment carried out by ALIMA in 2022 shows that mental health care remains largely overlooked, underdiagnosed and understaffed. Only the most severe cases are identified as mental health cases at community level and referred for treatment. The rest of the cases are treated by traditional healers. Within the facilities, the MoH staff has not been trained yet about mental health diagnosis and case management. The crisis also continued to exacerbate the lack of qualified health workers (i.e., management of primary health units by health auxiliaries), resulting in a decrease in quality of care provided in the area. For instance, there are currently only 2 doctors and 3 nurses working in Makary and Fotokol HDs, which is equivalent to 1 doctor for 103,133 inhabitants and 1 nurse for 68,755 inhabitants. As to Mada HD, only 3 doctors and 3 nurses are operating in this HD, which represents 1 doctor or nurse for 56,079 inhabitants. Yet, WHO establishes the following thresholds: 1/10,000 inhabitants for doctors and 1/5,000 inhabitants for nurses. Besides, the districts are located in a malaria endemic zone and face a peak of malaria every year (between July and December). Today, malaria remains the main cause of morbidity among children under five, despite regular rounds of SMC. In 2021, the districts also faced several outbreaks of cholera, measles and Covid-19, among others. Yet, the roll out of advanced vaccination strategies is complicated by the lack of means, the high insecurity and remoteness of certain health areas. These various factors led IPD admissions to increase by 29% in 2021, compared to the same period in 2020. With regard to reproductive and maternal health care, only 41% of deliveries are assisted while the regional average is 29% (IOM Survey, July 2021). Due to the cultural context in the Far North region, most of the deliveries usually happen at home, leading to a higher risk for the health of both mothers and infants (i.e., postpartum hemorrhages and infections).The nutritional screening carried out in 18 health areas of Mokolo HD in July 2021 by UNICEF anticipated a deterioration of the nutritional status of the population. Another nutritional screening, carried out in March 2022 by ALIMA, found that the prevalence rates of SAM and GAM amount to respectively 0.50% and 2.69% in ALIMA’s supported areas.
