Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to empower women and girls and work closely with men and boys GBV advocates/ambassadors, so that they are more able to identify solutions and strategies to address concerns and to act and advocate for themselves in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar Counties. The project will directly target 900 men 4,400 women, 1,300 boys and 2,900 girls from returnees, host community, and IDP population. This project will also strengthen prevention and mitigation of GBV in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar counties. All these are in conformity with the GBV Sub Cluster and Protection Cluster strategic objectives. For GBV survivors, Women and Girls Friendly Spaces will offer direct access to case management, PSS, referral, counseling and other GBV specific prevention and response. Project activities will include but not limited to: conduct training to 30 (10 per county) community based caseworkers, front-line workers and focal points on case management; conduct training to 30 (10 per county) community based caseworkers, front-line workers and focal points on psycho-social support; conduct 45 (15 per county) community legal awareness, assistance and counseling sessions; conduct 12 (4 per county) training and support on income generating and livelihood activities such as embroidery, crocheting, bead-work, tea sessions, weaving, basket making etc; construct of 3 women and girls friendly space (one in Mayom, one in Leer and another one in Panyijiar) for individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services); establish or strengthen 3 (1 per county) referral pathways for GBV survivors to access multi-sectorial support and create awareness on the existing referral pathways in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar; train 30 (10 per county) community mobilizers on protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning; conduct monthly men engagement and dialogue sessions in each county as part of awareness raising and prevention messaging that will also integrate Health such as Ebola or HIV to communicate prevention messages. This will involve SGBV, Health education material printing and distribution to strategic locations; create 6 (2 per county) women protection teams, among others to provide protective accompaniment in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar; conduct quarterly SGBV advocacy and awareness creation sessions with the hope of imparting skills on how to express anger without interpersonal violence; conduct monthly community protection awareness and dignity kits distribution in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar; and conduct 15 (5 in each county) training and support to women and girls with Energy Efficient Stoves as part of SGBV risk mitigation measure in Mayom, Leer and Panyijiar.
The project documentation will utilize GBV case intake forms that will be clearly labeled and stored in lockable shelves to ensure confidentiality. The project implementation strategies will include: capacity development for sustainability; protection programming; area-based development approach; Do no harm, conflict sensitivity planning, and gender and youth mainstreaming and empowerment. This project will also be integrated with other cluster projects such as FSL, CCCM, WASH and Shelter/NFIs, child protection for joint missions and other projects that HRSS are undertaking in the same location such as Protection and Returnee Monitoring in Leer and Panyijiar, CCCM in Panyijiar and Leer, among others. The project will be guided by the following principles and in relation to AAP: leadership and empowerment of women and girls; women and girls engaged in all aspects of the space; safety and accessibility; integrated in community and contextually relevant; inclusivity - all women and girls consulted – especially marginalized; coordination for multi-sectorial support and services – and referral; outreach to communities to encourage women and girls to participate; and sustainability and feedback mechanisms.

1 - Needs

While armed conflict has declined in South Sudan since the warring parties signed a peace agreement in September 2018, implementation of the deal is lagging, the political situation remains fragile, and millions remain displaced both inside and outside of the country. Meanwhile, instability in neighboring countries has driven nearly 300,000 refugees into South Sudan. Poor rule of law and easy access to arms has resulted in an increase in inter– and intra-communal violence that continues to force people from their homes. Still, the South Sudanese remain hopeful. Despite UNHCR’s non-return advisory for refugees (updated in April 2019), more displaced persons are showing interest in heading home. The recent revitalized peace process promises to offer new opportunities in 2020 for South Sudan’s women, men and children. However, the cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than 7 million people or about 2 thirds of the population in dire need of Humanitarian assistance and protection in 2019. The same proportion as in 2018 while the situation is no longer escalating at a rapid speed, the country remains in the grip of a serious Humanitarian crisis with a number of protection challenges related to general protection and especially Sexual and Gender Based Violence. Sexual and gender based violence is a persistent problem in South Sudan. There is no doubt that with the recent state of mass displacement in Unity State, current high returnee influx, and the complete lack of rule of law and respect to human rights, the situation has only deteriorated. Indeed a 2018 UNMISS Human Rights report explains that all parties to conflict have committed acts of sexual violence against women. UNFPA estimates that 24,500 South Sudanese women and girls are at risk of sexual violence. A recent study conducted by International Rescue Committee (IRC) and Care International revealed that 65 percent of women and girls in South Sudan have experienced some type of GBV in their lives. This project will support case management and PSS for survivors of sexual and gender-based violence and link, awareness, group counselling and redress for individuals with efforts to eliminate economic and social marginalization, addressing some of the root causes of violence against women Leer, Panyijiar and Mayom. In Unity State, discriminatory social norms and power inequalities between men and women affect all aspects of social economic and political life. In Leer, Mayom and Panyijiar, women and girls face increased levels of sexual and gender-based violence within and out of their own homes yet their voices are not heard. This is coupled by patriarchal attitudes and beliefs that still persist about men’s superiority to women and the naturalness of segregated gender roles that see women mainly as caregivers and men as bread-winners. The same gender-biased attitudes also underlie the high social tolerance or even justification of domestic violence in Leer, Mayom and Panyijiar. Moreover, women in these communities have experienced decades of conflicts and displacement. Women and girls lack opportunity to move freely or safely partly because Leer, Panyijiar and Mayom are still threatened by insecurity and partly because public spaces culturally and traditionally belong to men. There are few opportunities for women and girls to gather together and support one another. Opportunity to generate income has reduced as the economic situation has been more difficult. The creation of friendly spaces for women and girls has been a key strategy for HRSS in the protection and empowerment of women. However, these opportunities still lack in many locations in Leer, Panyijiar and Mayom and therefore presenting a need to be addressed by Hope Restoration through the support of this project.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. HRSS is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. HRSS is a lead national NGO on GBV in Unity State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. HRSS with other partners have developed a referral pathway in Leer (that includes Panyijiar), Mayendit and Rubkona, and will develop another in Koch and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools HRSS will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in-charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. HRSS staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project and awareness of PSEA created to everyone engaged in the project including beneficiaries.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

HRSS will participatory implement this project in a conflict sensitive manner, ensuring that inclusive participation of all stakeholders and in particular the beneficiaries at all stages of this project cycle. Participation will be enhanced through conducting participatory needs assessment in an inclusive and participatory manner and frequent consultations of key beneficiary groups in decision making particularly women and girls. HRSS will spend at least two weeks in the field to engage and work closely with beneficiaries to set up a project implementation committee that will last throughout the project, nominated by the target beneficiaries and composed of the beneficiaries. This committee will share with all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, the elderly, those who are illiterate, women and girls, issues agreed upon during their meetings such as types of assistance needed, safety audits, meeting and distribution locations and sites. HRSS will also set up a distribution committee composed of protection specialists, beneficiaries and local leadership to monitor any distribution process, and to provide feedback to HRSS on issues concerning protection such as SEA, livelihoods, the distribution process and the whole intervention process from the beginning of the project. HRSS will also use the presence of beneficiaries to pass messages such as health, livelihoods, SGBV among others through messaging and awareness creation. The feedback team will be composed of the project implementation committee representatives, representatives of various constituencies of beneficiaries and HRSS’s protection advisor. Conducting both pre and post distribution awareness and monitoring respectively will provide feedback from the various groups of beneficiaries. Reports developed by HRSS will be available for community, partners and even government authorities to access for references. Participation and feedback will address key service issues and gaps identified.
