Project Summary

The programme provides life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support, and contributes towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure. Targeting both IDPs (70%) and host communities (30%) involves activities; 3,000 standard NFI kits distribution (3,000 HHs) and winterization components such as distributing (500,000 L) heating fuel to 1,667 HHs, and (2,000) stoves to 2,000 HHs and (25,000) winter clothes kits for 25,000 children both gender . As well as providing restricted cash for house rent services (1,000 units) for those IDP vulnerable groups and prioritizing female or child headed house holds and elderly people and considering people with disabilities (1,000 HHs; 5,000 individuals). This programme includes 2,500 house rehabilitation activities which will target both IDPs and host communities (2,500 HHs ; 12,500 individuals), this activity includes rehabilitating partially damaged units through ceiling repair, walls buildings and holes fixing, doors and windows, infrastructure, electricity network and solar panels, plastering, plumping and providing the water tank. This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensuring that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils. The programme will target those IDPs living in deteriorated collective shelter through providing shelter rehabilitation for 50 collective centers (school buildings) targeting about 5,000 individuals. The programme also considers contributing towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and households by constructing 200 compressed earth blocks CEB units (200 HHs; 1,000 individuals).
The programme will seek feedback from both male and female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access and equal opportunities.

Needs

According to SNFI HNO 2019, there are 4.7 million people in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. And 4.4 million people require NFI assistance, a 6 per cent decline from the 2018 HNO, and the second year in a row that the total number of people in need of NFI assistance has reduced. The number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000, indicating that the affordability of items is affecting stable areas as well as those more directly impacted by the hostilities. The number of people in need of shelter assistance has increased in Idleb and Aleppo in North Syria. Overall, from the 2018 HNO, the severity of need has increased in only 21 per cent of sub-districts. Overall 300,000 more people now living in severity 4 and 5 areas over the last year with an overall increase in the number of people in need of 570,000. There has also been a significant worsening in overall shelter arrangements in Syria, with 36 per cent of sub-districts reporting an increase in the proportion of communities living in damaged or temporary shelters. Approximately 680,000 households cannot carry out necessary repairs to their homes, most frequently because they cannot afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation, and also due to a lack of skilled labor. As in previous years, the primary providers of shelter assistance and support are host households with an estimated 520,000 households hosting others. In Syria, households traditionally listed the male spouse (i.e. not female) in HLP documentation. As a result of the crisis, Syria has an increased number of female heads of household and separated households. Given the above-mentioned protection risks related to HLP and the specific need for HLP documentation as a prerequisite to receive certain shelter assistance and rights, women face an additional burden in accessing their rights and this support. According to the HNO 2019 the largest numbers of PIN are in Aleppo (1,130,815) and the Idleb (847,579) governorates. The programme is considering targeting areas of severity scale number 4-5 in both Aleppo and Idleb and aims at addressing humanitarian needs.Communities reported different NFI needs among different groups based on gender and age. For instance boys and girls were reported as being in greater need of clothing and shoes; men as in need of heating fuel, light sources, and water containers; women and girls as in need of sanitary pads; women as needing kitchen-related items and; the elderly as requiring blankets, energy and light sources. These findings from HNO 2019 demonstrate the wide range of items that households continue to struggle to access.

Providing 200 housing units to the 200 HHs (1,000 individuals). (The units will be constructed with Compressed Earth Block CEB). Providing emergency cash for rent for the most vulnerable HHs (1,000 HHs for three/four months), this involves 1,000 benefiting families (5,000 individuals) according to set criteria with focus on women-headed families and the most vulnerable families and the IDPs HHs lost their shelter considering the forced evacuees HHs.5 "50" school buildings (Collective centers) rehabilitated. Protection Risk Analysis

The programme has considered the Do-No-Harm principle in that it will use local field staff that will evaluate and assess the security situation, so that mitigation and response measures can be taken where and when needed. We are committed towards mainstreaming protection principles into all of our projects and do so through the use of several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff on protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation, protection cluster involvement and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. Collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries. Our documentation and media activities, including photography and videography, incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity.The programme involves the following protection risks:Protection Risk1: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls.Likelihood: HighMitigation: 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2. Train staff on code of conduct, 3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts, 4. Address any PSEA complaints, 5. Recruiting female field staff, 6. Incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity, 7. Committing towards mainstreaming protection, capacity building of all staff on protection and mainstreaming.Monitoring: Monitoring staff report.Protection Risk 2: Beneficiaries might be exposed to risks if attending badly planned, organized and conducted distributions in connection with poor safety and security planning and/or lack of adequate consideration for vulnerable groups.Likelihood: Medium, Mitigation: 1. Carry out thorough safety assessments on the areas where the distribution sites are planned, 2. Ensure that NFIs distribution are completed in time for beneficiaries to reach their homes during daylight, 3. Consider specific risks to women and seek community opinion on how to best avoid and mitigate risk, 4. Communicate intensively with national and local authorities, stakeholders and community leaders, 5. Establish priority lines for vulnerable persons, such as disabled, elderly and pregnant/lactating women, 6. Set-up a door-to-door distribution for the very vulnerable HHs.Monitoring: 1. Complaints raised are addressed, 6. Hiring female staff to work during distributions. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Access restrictions to geo area due to active fighting and/or shifting of front lines would prevent the humanitarian actors to provide timely emergency relief to the affected population.Likelihood: HighMitigation: 1. Constantly monitor the security situation on the ground, 2. Develop contingency planning, 3. Set-up multiple back-up distribution centers, 4. Liaison with the local authorities, 5. Explore different distribution modalities, 6. Elaborate different plans for flexible deliveries in areas that are inaccessible.Monitoring: 1. Monitor the security situation on the ground & hire security staff and specialists, 2. Liaise with local authorities and focal point in the field, 3. Keep communication channel open with civilian authorities, stakeholders and NGOs, 4. Monitor and identify as needs arise new distribution points, 5. Develop and maintain monitored contingency plans. Risk 2: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation for the shelter rehabilitatedLikelihood: HighMitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document that was developed by the SNFI Cluster, 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP, 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed, 4. Have an HLP section in the M&E assessment form and verify the documents available.Monitoring: 1. Monitor the existence of the documentation and conduct periodic visits to the rehabilitated Shelters in order to assess the condition of the beneficiaries and verify that they have not been unlawfully evicted, 2. Have HLP element monitored and verified during M&E. Risk 3: Risk of conducting rehabilitation of building with poor structural integrity and/or not fully cleared from land-mines and explosive remnants of war (ERWs).Likelihood: HighMitigation: 1. Conduct a thorough shelter assessment and infrastructural technical assessment of the buildings that are aimed to be rehabilitated, 2. Coordinate the interventions with local authorities that might be aware of security issues in the neighborhood, 3. Ensure that ERWs clearance is conducted if needs be, 4. Check with other actors working in the same area to understand what kind of issues they encounteredMonitoring: 1. Conduct safety monitoring of the shelters, 2. Conduct detailed technical assessment of the infrastructural integrity of the building.
