Project Summary

An estimated 2.1 million Somalis are in Crisis and Emergency (IPC Phases 3 & 4) and face significant consumption gaps through December 2019, an extended hunger season following the poor performance of the 2019 Gu season rainfall. An additional 4.2 million Somalis are in IPC 2 (Stressed) and engage in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs. In total, 6.3 million Somalis face acute food insecurity through the end of 2019. The food security situation reflect the cumulative effects of below-average rains experienced across most parts of Somalia during the 2018 Deyr (October-December), followed by harsh weather conditions during the dry 2019 Jilaal (January–March) season and the delayed and poor performance of the 2019 Gu (April-June). These consecutive seasonal poor performances have been exacerbated by limited recovery from the extended impacts of the severe 2016/2017 drought.
The Bay-Bakool low potential agro-pastoralist and Guban pastoral livelihood zones are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and represent hot spot rural population for priority response interventions. Most parts of northern and central Somalia are also classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and require urgent humanitarian assistance. Rural livelihoods that are classified in IPC Phase 2 during the current projection period but have been in worse phases in the recent past also considered priority in terms of response.
According to FSNAU report agricultural and livestock production in riverine, rain-fed, agropastoral and pastoral area in Somalia have been severely affected due to consecutive unfavorable rainfall seasons and associated impacts. The Agro-pastoral and Riverine farmers in Hiran, Bay, and Bakool, Lower and Middle Shebelle have suffered successive below average and poor harvests for most of the seasons over the past three consecutive years. Unfortunately, the 2018 Gu that halted the protracted drought in Somalia was not as favorable for some of the Agro-pastoral and Riverine farmers (e.g. Hiran) as compared to other livelihoods in Somalia. The delayed onset of the 2019 Gu season and prolonged dry spells negatively affected the main agricultural production season. Low river levels in Shabelle and Juba rivers has also contributed to very low 2019 Gu season cereal production. Successive poor harvests have also contributed to the depletion of agricultural seed stock. The 2019 Gu cereal production in southern Somalia is estimated at 41 000 tons, including 6 900 tons of off-season harvests of late August/September. The total cereal harvest in southern Somalia is the lowest GU harvest since 1995 and 68 percent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2018. Decrease of cereal stocks are triggering increases in the price of sorghum. As such, livestock-to-cereal and wage labor-tocereal terms of trade are exhibit a downward trend. Therefore, this project will focus on target population of 9030 (1,290 HHs) comprising agro-pastoral communities and vulnerable population who need immediate assistance within the Bay region. The project will reduce the percentage of people in sever acute food insecurity phases (crisis and worse), and support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets, to enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness.

Project Activities

The activities that the organization will be involved is as follows: 1) Kick off project in Bay region in January 20202) Quarterly review of the project in Bay in April, July and October 2020. 3) Identify suitable CfW projects for rapid drought response & recovery (rehabilitate productive livelihood assets e.g. water pans, shallow wells, irrigation canal and natural resources) 4) Support community led identification and registration of CfW beneficiaries 5) Support community led identification and registration of unconditional cash transfers beneficiaries 6) Provide simcards to the beneficiaries with correct registration details 7) Provide tools for CfW activities 8) Implement CfW projects for drought response & recovery 9) Selection of the money vendors 10) Sharing of beneficiary mobile numbers with the money vendors 11) Cash transfers to the beneficiaries 12) Post Distribution Monitoring by PASOS M&E staff 13) Money vendor provides the electronic statement to PASOS as proof of transfer of cash to beneficiaries. 14) Verification of the the electronic statements 15) Reimbursement of the money to the vendor 16) Selection of the water management committees 17) Training of the water management committees 18) Handing over of the rehabilitated assets to the relevant local authorities and water management committees

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement The returnees targeted population risk of exclusion is medium since PASOS will ensure that data is collected among the beneficiaries with the help of already community structures and effective approaches such as outreach services so that all the vulnerable populations will be reached. Lack of documentation among the returnees does not mean that they are excluded but rather their vulnerability analysis. PASOS will also ensure that all the affected population understand the process of obtaining food assistance and livelihoods opportunities at the appropriate time, date and safe location.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age The risks of exclusion based on gender and age is medium. The project’s gender focus creates new challenges for women and/or HH and community conflict by promoting women’s and age equality. PASOS will mitigate this risk by ensuring that activities targeting women include men in appropriate ways; male leaders engaged to support women’s involvement in decision making; gender impact of project (positive & negative) closely monitored; implementing agencies trained to refer GBV cases. Issues related to age exclusion will also be mitigated. There is a risk of excluding child headed household in the project. Minors will not be considered in restricted cash interventions but rather PASOS will involve the relevant local agencies to ensure that the child-headed households benefit from the unrestricted cash interventions. The risk of beneficiaries experience intimidation, extortion or theft of resources following distributions is medium and PASOS will Inform beneficiaries of potential risks, ensure distributions are conducted in secure areas and allow beneficiaries ample time to travel during daylight hours, ensure robust complaints mechanism and post distribution monitoring is in placeMitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence The main risks include: a) Inaccessibility of project locations due to security concerns. The risk is medium and PASOS will ensure that all the project locations are in both secure and potentially insecure areas to ensure continuous implementation of activities; and will also regularly review security status with local authorities and institutions. 2) There is also the risk of taxation by local authorities/power holders on distributed resources that the beneficiaries will face. The risk is medium and PASOS will mitigate the above mentioned risk by work with local authorities and other stakeholders to create awareness that these resources and assets are not subject to taxation, monitor and take immediate action on reports of taxation through accountability and feedback mechanisms

Please provide further explanation

Involving the marginalised populations, including women and girls, minorities and IDPs will provide a stable and safe environment that will increase community, development partners and government in terms of collaboration, inclusiveness, capacity building and effective implementation of the project. The quality of participation will ensure that respect and attention is given to opinions, ideas and perspectives of the community in planning and implementation. The result of broad-based consultation and engagement is expected to lead to greater ownership of the programme by communities, government and other stakeholders. The programme will be built upon relationships the PASOS already have with local communities, leaders, and government representatives, and complemented by long-term programming and other sectoral projects each agency is implementing in the area. This will considerably mitigate negative impacts of the programme’s closure further helping to enhance the target beneficiaries’ resilience. Sustainability is exceptionally important to build resilience and depends heavily on two key conditions: 1) communities’ willingness and ability to properly manage the project beyond the agencies’ intervention period; 2) the appropriateness and adaptability of the project itself. Community-led vulnerability assessment and analysis provide understand on local conditions, resources, needs, skills and knowledge. Ongoing engagement and communication with local and regional authorities, cluster partners as well as government officials will be critical for the success of the programme to ensure buy-in, ownership and sustainability.
