Needs

The humanitarian consequence of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine is critical with 5.2 million people affected by the crippling and deepening impacts of the now protracted crisis. Of these, 3.5 million people need humanitarian assistance and protection, which is a 3 per cent increase from 2018 due to mine contamination, escalating psychological trauma and the degrading impact of the lack of access to basic services. Most people in need live in the two conflict-affected oblasts of Donetska and Luhanska – which has a 420 km-long ‘contact line’ dividing the theatre of active hostilities since 2014. With active shelling and landmine contamination, civilians continue to be killed and maimed: over 3,000 killed and almost 9,000 injured since 2014. To date, 181 humanitarian partners are involved in humanitarian assistance provision. While systems are in place, coordination requires constant revision to ensure it is adapted to the context to ensure an effective and efficient emergency response to cover the most critical needs of the affected population. Ensuring the distinct needs of women and girls, boys and men are met in the areas of protection, emergency water, food, health, shelter and access to critical basic services/markets is crucial.

Activities & outcomes

• Conduct regular monitoring and analysis of events impacting the humanitarian response, including reporting, advocacy, needs/gaps and response analysis, to better support the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in its traditional role as secretariat. • Coordinate and lead joint assessment processes, including missions. Ensure regular reporting on results to better inform decision making processes. • Develop and maintain information products, ensuring the sex and age disaggregated data is collected and used to enhance gender sensitive analysis and action. • Lead and provide coordination platform for efficient multi cluster coordination, including assessments, response and monitoring. • Ensure regular monitoring on funding, including identifying and reporting on funding gaps.• Strengthened donor engagement through regular meetings on priorities and gaps; • Increase awareness and support for the humanitarian community to increase operational capacity and guarantee unimpeded humanitarian access.• Support capacity of agencies to effectively mainstream gender into needs assessments and overall project activities in order to ensure positive gender equality outcome.• Conduct and put in place emergency preparedness measures in support of humanitarian operations.• Sharing and circulating of timely analytical and situational reports to increase awareness and identify pitfalls for humanitarian operations.• Perform civil military coordination with the de facto and Ukrainian Government authorities for the UN and humanitarian partners, including facilitation of humanitarian notification system. • Work with development actors hand in hand to better align recovery/development and humanitarian activities, through the humanitarian and development nexus working groups and the work on the collective outcomes. • Increase awareness of the crisis through regular public information and communication engagement (campaigns, audio-visual products, media and social media) through the existing Humanitarian Communication Sub-Group

• Production of quality reports, advocacy and communication documents shared with stakeholders – Regular • Data updated and shared with stakeholders, and disaggregated by age and gender – Regularly • Joint assessment missions occur on a regular basis – As needed• Increased number of assessments reflecting gender issues and concerns – Regularly • Humanitarian information products are updated regularly – Monthly• Multi cluster assessments are regularly used as a tool for assessments – As needed• Regular humanitarian overview reports include humanitarian funding status – Monthly• Donor meetings occur on a regular basis (Kyiv and field levels) – Monthly/Quarterly• UN Agencies and other humanitarian partners cross “contact line” and freely access conflict affected areas to deliver humanitarian assistance – As per need• Situational and analytical reports are regularly submitted and circulated to humanitarian partners – Daily, monthly and as needed• Preparedness and resilience aspects of emergency response are regularly reviewed and planning processes are in place, including the Inter Agency Contingency Plan, UN Partnership Framework (UNPF), MPTF, RPA etc. The Inter Agency Contingency Plan for Ukraine is regularly updated – Monthly/Annually• Sustaining civil military coordination and follow up for movement of humanitarian goods and personnel conducted – daily, monthly and as required ad hoc.• Improving and maintenance of working relationship with the de facto and Ukrainian authorities to enable humanitarian operations – daily, monthly.• Regular meetings of the Humanitarian-Development Nexus Working Group and its collective outcome groups to ensure alignment of humanitarian and development interventions - Regularly• Release, dissemination and widespread implementation of joint public information and communication campaigns, media and social media engagement and human interest stories (audio-visual) – daily, weekly, monthly and ad-hoc as required.
