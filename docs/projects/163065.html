Project Summary

The country health delivery system is currently weak and compromised mainly due to the debilitating economic environment, which has resulted in stock outs of essential medicines and supplies. This is compounded by demotivated work force working for two days a week, with doctors having been on strike for more than three months. Health services are currently mainly available in the private sector beyond the reach of the majority in the country. It is also important to note that, there is an increase in out of pocket payment for health services, considering even for those who used to be on medical aid the shortfalls are now significantly high because of the inflation. These challenges have seriously compromised access to health services with vulnerable groups like, people living with disability, PLWHIV, elderly, children under 5 years and women most affected. The current drought induced food insecurity is exposing the majority of the vulnerable populations in the target districts to risk of malnutrition, with Global Acute Malnutrition rates on an increase. These prevailing challenges in the country predispose the communities to risk due to diseases outbreaks (37124 diarhoea case load in the 13 districts targeted by this project), inadequate case management and poor access to health services (25% of women who delivered were not attended to by a skilled birth attended in the 13 target districts). There also exist capacity gaps for MOHCC and weak routine disease surveillance systems compromising timely detection and reporting of disease outbreaks and public health events.
The project will feed into the cluster response, working in collaboration with WHO, local NGOs and MOHCC to sustain access to health services and timely response to endemics. To ensure appropriate responses in the different target districts, the project will facilitate assessments to map risks and hazards, vulnerabilities and response capacities, ensuring involvement of the communities and local stakeholders. Through capacity building trainings for MOHCC staff on IDSR and logistical support, the project will strengthen routine surveillance and strengthening of early warning systems to ensure timely detection of disease outbreaks and health events. This will be coupled with strengthening the capacity of local laboratories at district hospitals through support with reagents, trainings and logistics for sample collection and transportation.
In light of the case management challenges usually evident in emergencies, the project will strengthen case management and referral pathways through capacity building trainings for MOHCC staff and VHWs complemented with equipping with provision of job aids. This will improve management of medical consequences of malnutrition, common communicable diseases like diarrhea, typhoid fever, malaria and measles. For the capacity, building to yield fruits there is need for adequate stocks of essential medicines and medical supplies; the project will support distribution of essential medicines (procured by the cluster lead through NatPharm) to health facilities in the target districts. To ensure access to health services in hard to reach areas, outreach services for PHC services will be strengthened in target districts
In an endeavor to enhance coordination, reporting and data use in the target districts, monthly review meetings will be supported in the target districts.
