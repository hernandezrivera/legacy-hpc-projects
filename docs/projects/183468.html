Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to PHC L 2 and the provision of integrated sexual and reproductive health services (SRH) in area C in the west Bank to ensure the lifesaving care for the residents of the most vulnerable communities.
This will be achieved through the following main activities:
1. Provision of health services that are needed due to the humanitarian crisis by supporting Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS), and Al-Islah Social Charity Society- Jericho (ICS) to run primary health care level 2 mobile clinics including provision of SRH services for women, girls, young people including people with disabilities.
2. Strengthening the capacity of SRH providers in the targeted locations on GBV detection, and referral and training on SRH topics including Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to sustain SRH services during emergencies.
3. Enhance accessibility of PHC and SRH services for persons with disabilities.
4. Community outreach on essential SRH and COVID19 information, i.e. danger signs in pregnancy, family planning, etc. COVID19 will be integrated into the information and community outreach in regards to how it impacts SRH.

1 - Needs

The occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) is best described by the chronic political crises which has long been impacting the health care system in the country and has caused a severe crisis in the development and capacity of the system to grow and sustain essential services, especially for those living in Area C, H2, and East Jerusalem. In 2021, the COVID-19 outbreak continued to put further strain on an already fragile healthcare system in the oPt. The sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRH) services including interventions targeting GBV survivors have been disrupted, particularly during the times of intense. Access to basic healthcare, including family planning, that prevents maternal and new-born morbidity and mortality has been relatively improved during 2021 in the country, however, the SRH services were sometimes overlooked, and some were provided with low quality in particular in Area C. According to MoH 2020 Annual Report, there are about 130,000 adolescent girls, 90,000 vulnerable women and 225,000 children, including 30,000 neonates in need of the provision of sexual and reproductive healthcare, gender-based violence, obstetric and neonatal care, and emergency nutrition services . PMRS with the support from the UNFPA conducted mobile clinic needs assessment in April 2021, the results indicated that there is major dependency of the residents of area C on the mobile clinic services including SRH and GBV services whereas no alternative modalities have been identified to fill the gap. furthermore, the study revealed that there is a huge need to sustain the SRH services including GBV, and adolescence and consultation especially for pregnant women and those with high risk pregnancy as well as awareness raising for community . Through the UNFPA monitoring and field visits observation to the mobile clinics it was recommended to diverse the health care service delivery modalities in the mobile clinic such as home visits which can help reach the most vulnerable groups such as; women who are at high risk pregnancy and persons with disabilities. About 180,000 Palestinians living in communities located in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the closed area behind the separation wall, continue to suffer from limited or no access to primary healthcare and are therefore in need of mobile clinics services (HNO 2022). Based on the data from the Mobile Clinics Working Group, about 150,000 Palestinians in marginalized locations in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the closed area behind the separation wall, continue to suffer from limited, or no, access to fixed healthcare services and are therefore in need of mobile clinic service provision. Due to the occupation`s restrictive planning system and its associated demolition orders, Palestinians living in those area face huge obstacles in accessing health services in these communities. This indicates a strong need for mobile clinic services, especially for SRH care.Beside the alternative modalities of SRH service provision, it is recommended to support the MoH PHC services and the PHC care in general in nearby areas of area C where there is an apparent lack and inability to sustain the SRH and GBV services due to COVID 19 outbreak, the disruption of the lockdown, and the change in priorities which channeled the majority of resources to combat COVID19 and its consequences. Even when services are available, there is a decrease in overall demand due to; movement restrictions; lack of public transportation; and loss of wages and inability to afford transportation to services. The COVID 19 pandemic has clearly pointed out the need to improve the coordination among service providers and jointly establish unified referral pathways for emergency situations especially on the local level with the aim to link it with the national level as well. This will positively impact the quality, availability, and accessibility of health care services including the SRH services.

2 - Activities

Activity 1. Support the continuation of mobile clinics services provision for PMRS and ICS in area C of the West Bank to provide L 2 PHC based on the minimum service package of the Mobile Clinic Working Group (MCWG) to ensure universal coverage and the continuum of SRH lifesaving care services for women, girls, and youth by integrating the GBV, and youth services within the PHC. Furthermore, the project will support five mobile clinics; four clinics run by PMRS, and one run by the ICS to enable these clinics to continue delivering services in the areas where MoH has limited access to. The PMRS and ICS have extensive experience in providing mobile clinic services in area C and both are well known in these communities. The UNFPA will support the formation of a steering committee which will have representatives from PMRS and ICS in addition to UNFPA, the committee will be responsible for running and monitoring the overall implementation of the project. The steering committee will also help to improve coordination and collaboration among the two partners to provide quality SRH services and facilitate referrals through establishment of unified and clear referral pathways. The project will enhance the role of the midwives in providing continuum of lifesaving SRH services through midwifery home visits led counseling sessions in those areas targeted by the mobile clinics as to reach more women, and girls especially those with limited access. The midwifery led counselling home visits will help mitigate the burden on the pregnant and lactating women, and support relieving symptoms of postpartum depression by providing psychosocial support to those women in need. Activity 2. Strengthening the capacity of SRH providers in the targeted locations on GBV detection, and referral and training on SRH topics including Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to sustain SRH services during emergencies. The training module will be determined and prepared based on the needs of the mobile clinic teams and will be linked with the MCWG to invite more SRH providers to attend the trainings which will improve coordination and referral pathways among SRH stakeholders during emergencies. Furthermore, the mobile clinic will have close collaboration and coordination with MoH to facilitate the COVID19 vaccination process for the residents of those communities in the targeted areas where the mobile clinics operate. This will be done through; a.) Referral of people to nearby MoH COVID19 vaccine centers b.) Host the MoH COVID19 vaccine personnel in the mobile clinic premises and support in gathering people and organizing the event. Activity 3. Enhance accessibility of PHC and SRH services for persons with disabilities: The project will ensure that services will be accessible for persons with disabilities. Therefore, the project will contribute in the adaptations of small-scale physical and information accessibility for a number of health care facilities under the MoH and those premises that usually host the mobile clinics. In parallel; technical support for MoH health care staff and the PMRS mobile teams will be provided by sensitization sessions and technical trainings to ensure that they have the ability to interact and provide quality SRH services for persons with disabilities. This will be done in partnership with organization of persons with disabilities (OPD) who will be consulted on the capacity building process and will be responsible for implementing the trainings as well as follow up on practices. • Activity 4. Community outreach and awareness raising. This will include dissemination of SRH service provider’s information to all beneficiaries, and the production of accessible awareness messages on the available services and hotlines to access SRH consultations in case of emergency in the targeted areas. Conduct educational sessions for women and pregnant women incoordination and collaboration with all SRH service providers in the targeted areas on the high risk pregnancies and post-natal care among other SRH issues. The project will support the community awareness sessions through selected grassroots CBOs for pregnant mothers and women on protections measures of COVID-19 and other SRHR topics, such as family planning. Some planned sessions with groups of adolescents’ girls in those targeted locations will also be organized, those vulnerable girls will be reached with awareness messages specifically on menstrual hygiene, early marriage, adolescence health and psychosocial issues as they deem appropriate.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Overall, the feedback will be generated through field visits in addition to the partner’s regular reporting. A dedicated survey will be designed and implemented to collect and analyze the perspectives of the beneficiaries throughout the span of the project implementation. This information will be utilized in two ways. First, data collected will inform the ongoing activity implementation where feedback will be used to improve the services being provided and ensure a good match between beneficiaries needs and the services. Second, data collected will also inform the lessons learned and will help us share best practices. Specific community feedback on mobile clinics will be gathered through engagement with local committees, set up to guide and ensure responsiveness to local demand, and to promote sustainability of the services. The committees are currently being established and will seek to be representative of the local community with regards to gender, age, and disability status. UNFPA and its IPs will consult with the committees for guidance on needs and priorities and to receive feedback on quality and timeliness of services and any other feedback.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Due to the ongoing pandemic, it is crucial that the project integrates COVID-19 protection and prevention measures, such as PSEA, risk mitigation measures and adapts interventions accordingly. UNFPA will act upon the gained understanding with the priority to consider and uphold the rights and \ not to cause any further negative impact and harm. Safety and security will be guaranteed by upholding dignity and protection in the provision of services focusing on privacy, acceptance and person’s choices. Partners and other actors of the project will be trained and mentored to deliver non-judgmental, disability-sensitive and high-quality integrated PHC/SRH services ensuring confidentiality, privacy and respecting person’s choices about the care of those seeking help, including persons with disabilities. Safeguards are to be put in place in ensuring confidentiality to not be compromised when mobility is restricted. At the same time, the specialized personnel need to be supported in delivering services and upholding their right and dignity through being treated equitably and fairly. It is especially important to train service providers on COVID-19 response guidelines both to protect the patient and the service provider. In relation to health facilities, safety and protection will be reached by identifying and securing surrounding areas, minimizing threats and vulnerabilities, especially following and implementing COVID-19 IPC guidelines. Reasonable accessible infrastructure adaptations will be made when needed e.g. ramps, railings, as well as information and communication means like braille sign language interpretation to partners’ health facilities in order to make services more inclusive for all.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project design takes into consideration needs based on gender, age and vulnerabilities as well as the disabilities since the need for SRH and accessibility to services are different. Primarily, the project ensures that selected communities receive SRH services to improve their quality of life. This project is designed to provide equal and impartial accessibility without barriers and discrimination through all planned activities. Impartiality is upheld through striving for a large outreach and accessibility by coordinating with other operating actors along with continuously conducting needs assessments. It is of importance to highlight and promote women, girls, and young people’s rights to health care and how/where to obtain it through comprehensive awareness and education sessions e.g. utilizing existing referral systems.The project is based on the needs of women, girls, young people, in addition to other potential differences in urgent needs, faced risks and faced traumatic experiences, which are gender, age and disability -specific, and do also vary depending on the location of residence. It will primarily target identified vulnerable women, girls, young people for crucial SRH including considering the arrangement of peoples’ access and availability to services including adapting services and information (developing a specific alternative communication strategy in order to ensure reaching everyone, especially those at risk of exclusion). That is achieved by offering different alternatives in order to be able to widen the reach and fulfil the needs, through existing health clinics (fixed and mobile) in addition to home visits when applicable and enhance services and interventions through other channels such as phone and virtual platforms. This will allow barriers to be faced such as movement restrictions that prevent people from seeking help, for instance, making sure all persons with disabilities who are in need for SRH services are considered, and those who cannot access services due to for instance restrictions related to COVID-19 are also reached. Other restrictions such as; lack of internet access, socio-cultural norms and restrictions can also be addressed through tackling stigma and misconceptions at all levels. Mobile clinics and virtual platforms will especially ensure services, information and community-based support interventions reach people in the most vulnerable communities.
