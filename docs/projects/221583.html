Project Summary

The project seeks to improve the nutrition status and Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) practices among the vulnerable households in the county. AFSS intends to improve the nutrition status of children 6-59 months and the Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) through integrated approach (Nutrition specific and sensitive). The nutrition-specific activities will seek to increase access to and coverage of appropriate treatment of moderately and severely malnourished individuals as per the national Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) guidelines. These will include community mobilization and sensitization, active case finding by CNVs and mothers using the family MAUC approach, passive/onsite nutrition assessment by the nutrition and health workers, enrollment of identified MAM10000 (5200F,4800M) ,SAM(3000; 1560F & 1440M) and PLW (11000) cases into the nutrition programme for appropriate care, capacity strengthening of staff, health workers, (48; 12F & 36M) CNVs (106;38F & 68M) and MtMSGs,(46). Dispensation of Specialized Nutritious Foods and therapeutic foods (CSB++, RUSF and RUTF). The Preventive activities will be geared towards addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition such as; poor child care practices and sub-optimal MIYCN practices. These will include; MIYCN counseling for both 10000 mothers/caregivers of children 6-23months and 5000 pregnant women. Vitamin A supplementation of children 6-59 months (35000; 18200F &16800M), De-worming for children 12-59 months (30000; 15600 F &14400M) . Promotion of the production and consumption of diverse micro nutrient-rich foods such as dark green leafy vegetables (120) using contextualized climate-smart techniques both at the community and Health facility levels ; Rearing of small animals like guinea pigs and rabbits to increase the consumption of high biological value animal proteins.
AFSS will conduct malaria testing using the Rapid Diagnostic Kit (RDT) at all standalone facilities for all children who will present fever and positive cases will be provided with the anti-malarial drugs and in facilities with health services, all children will be referred to the health partner for testing and management. All children presenting with diarrhea will be provided with anti-diarrheal drugs and those with respiratory tract infections will be referred to the nearest Health facility for management.

1. Needs

Food insecurity and malnutrition have prevailed in South Sudan, due to persistent drought and insecurity fueled by inter-communal violence and now the ongoing Sudan conflict that continues to drive up humanitarian needs in South Sudan (UNICEF.2022). South Sudan faces significant challenges related to malnutrition. According to the IPC September 2024- July 2025 report indicated that an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months are suffering or expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and will be in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2024 to June 2025. The report also shown a 20.4 percent deterioration of acute malnutrition compared to 1.66 million registered in 2023. The total number of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 650,000 and 1.43 million, respectively. Additionally the FSNMS R30 also shown the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remained above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%. Today, one in five children in South Sudan suffers from acute malnutrition and this worrying situation has not changed since 2010. Despite the existence of guidelines and strategy for delivering Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) programs in South Sudan, the indicators for IYCF are still sub optimal especially on complementary feeding practices where only 16.2% of children 6-23 months received minimum dietary diversity (IPC 2024-2025) in Eastern Equatoria and 16.5% at national level. In Kapoeta East county, less than half (45.45%) of infants of 6-8 months of age were reported to have introduced solid, semi-solid or soft foods in a timely manner (SMART survey 2022). Generally, the Minimum Acceptable Diet (MAD) among the children was poor (4%). According to the 2023 FSNM survey, the prevalence of GAM was 10.5% in Eastern Equatoria State and the nutritional status of the younger children (6-29 months) seemed to be more affected than older (30-59 months) (FSNMS,2024) while it was 17.7% at the national level. Periodic nutrition assessments conducted in KEC and KSC have indicated that the high prevalence of acute malnutrition is cyclic and chronic in nature implying that the cycle will not end unless the key drivers are adequately addressed. The major factors contributing are; high prevalence of diseases, poor access to improved drinking water & sanitation, and poor infant and young child feeding practices. In this regard, the management and prevention of acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women (PLW) becomes paramount. Concerted efforts from the government of South Sudan and nutrition partners through the National Nutrition cluster (NNC) are in action to reduce the prevalence of acute malnutrition and consequently prevent malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality among children under five years of age and PLW. AFSS has been at the forefront of this in the counties of Kapoeta East, South, Maridi and Ibba, Mundri West and Mundri East through the implementation of nutrition programs using protocols stipulated in the national CMAM guidelines along with the nutrition sensitive approaches. Despite the interventions, under-nutrition among children has remained prevalent and there is still need for bold actions to address the situation. Therefore, AFSS proposes to continue with the provision of integrated quality nutrition services for children 6-59months, pregnant and lactating women in KE County. The integrated approaches will be utilized with the aim of addressing the malnutrition situation in the county. The nutrition-specific activities will seek to increase access to and coverage of appropriate treatment of moderately and severely malnourished individuals as per the national CMAM guidelines. Nutrition-sensitive activities will be geared towards addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition such as; poor childcare practices, sub optimal MIYCN and WASH practices through SBCC, and practical approaches.

2. Project activities

The nutrition interventions will target all children aged 6-59 months, pregnant women in the second trimester and Lactating Women with children less than 6 months of age. These will be identified from the communities and at the health facilities with the support of the Community Nutrition Volunteers (CNVs), MtMSGs and Nutrition Assistants with the aid of MUAC tapes, WH/L Z-scores and assessment of Oedema. Beneficiary monitoring will be carried out on a bi-weekly basis. Management of the individuals enrolled into the programme will be in accordance with the South Sudan CMAM protocols. Community mobilization and sensitization will be carried out routinely alongside the active nutrition screening by the CNVs. Beneficiary nutrition and demographic data will be captured in the OTP/TSFP Ministry of Health Nutrition Registers that will be stationed at the health facilities providing nutrition services. Nutrition specific activities will include community mobilization and sensitization, active case finding by CNVs, passive/onsite nutrition assessment by the nutrition and health workers, enrollment of identified MAM/SAM cases for appropriate care, capacity strengthening of frontline staff on GBV, MIYCN, CMA ,and optimal WASH and MtMSGs on family MUAC and MIYCN, Dispensation of Specialized Nutritious Foods (CSB++, RUSF and RUTF) for both TSFP & OTP. While the Nutrition sensitive activities will include; Promotion of the production and consumption of diverse micronutrient-rich foods such as dark green leafy vegetables using contextualized climate-smart techniques; Rearing of small animals like guinea pigs and rabbits to increase the consumption of high biological value animal proteins. This will be done through collaboration between AFSS and the Food Security and Livelihoods implementing partners. AFSS will also continue to promote optimal WASH practices through collaboration with WASH implementing partners that will provide WASH kits and water purification tablets, conduct capacity building of MtMSGs and school going children on liquid soap making. MIYCN practices through Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches such as onsite Nutrition and Health Education (NHE), food and cooking demonstrations and conducting community nutrition dialogues as well as awareness campaigns. AFSS will conduct malaria testing using the Rapid Diagnostic Kit (RDT) at all standalone facilities for all children who will present fever and positive cases will be provided with the anti-malarial drugs and in facilities with health services, all children will be referred to the health partner for testing and management. All children presenting with diarrhea will be provided with anti-diarrheal drugs and those with respiratory tract infections will be referred to the nearest Health facility for management. To ensure protection among the targeted beneficiaries, AFSS will liaise with the protection department within the county to provide a safe referral pathway for the identified victims. Additionally, through the gender and protection department, AFSS will put into place a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) that will be situated within the communities and at the nutrition sites. To promote sustainability and continuity of the project, AFSS will work in collaboration with the government stakeholders during the planning phase, capacity strengthening, community mobilization, routine project monitoring as well as review of the project progress.
