Project Summary

In line with the UNICEF Humanitarian Action Appeal and the Health Cluster Humanitarian Response Plan For The 2023 Humanitarian Program, the project is aimed at achieving equitable health outcomes for the vulnerable, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children. This will be achieved through addressing humanitarian health needs while building the resilience of local communities against health emergencies and linking with development interventions. UNICEF will respond to emerging health emergencies in accordance to the
Core Commitments for Children in Humanitarian Action. The key modes of interventions in this project are service provision, availing medical supplies and vaccines, national and state level capacity building, community mobilization and advocacy.

1 - Needs

Sudan is facing many challenges in ensuring its children’s survival including finance and security. Moreover, the rapid-onset emergencies such as floods, conflicts in the west (Darfur) and the south (South Kordofan/Blue Nile States), and epidemics (cholera, measles, viral hemorrhagic fevers, etc.) continue to affect the country. Fighting between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) began on 15th April 2023 in Khartoum and swiftly expanded to neighboring cities and states, especially in Darfur, and some other parts of Sudan. Despite a number of cease-fire agreements, the fighting and air bombardment continue, mostly in Khartoum and West Darfur, with sporadic clashes in North Kordofan, North Darfur and South Darfur. The conflict between RSF and SAF has rekindled intercommunal hostilities in Geneina, West Darfur, displacing the entire town.Humanitarian needs in Sudan had already reached historic levels before the crisis erupted. The Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) was revised due to the escalation of violence across the country that erupted on 15 April 2023. A staggering total of 24.7 million people, 13.6 million of whom are children, now require humanitarian assistance. For Health interventions UNICEF is targeting 11 mln population includes 3,163,728 children and women who can receive primary healthcare in UNICEF-supported facilities and 1,655,308 children to be vaccinated against measles as a proxy indicator of immunization interventions. UNICEF will need US$837.6 million to reach the most vulnerable children and families with life-saving child protection, education, health, nutrition, social protection, and WASH programmes. The increase in funding requirements reflects the magnitude and devastation of this new conflict, on top of the country's already dire humanitarian state. As a priority, UNICEF will ensure gender responsive humanitarian action, with disability considerations incorporated into all efforts. The financial requirement also includes $67 million in order to significantly expand the access and provision lifesaving health services to children and women. In addition US$100 million is required for cash support to 250,000 families under the mother and child cash transfer plus programme. This effort uses a resilience lens to provide cash and access to various services to vulnerable children, women, and their families in order to minimise malnutrition and maternal and infant mortality. Sudan is going through a massive humanitarian crisis. Without an immediate and extensive response, the consequences of displacements, a lack of basic social services and protection would have devastating and long-lasting ramifications for Sudanese and refugee children residing in the country. Timely and flexible funding are essential for UNICEF to immediately reach the most vulnerable, crisis-affected children and ensure cost-efficient interventions for them to access services and prevent harm, which will pay off in a longer-term. ave been injured, over 1 million have been forced to flee within Sudan, and at least 319,000 have fled into bordering countries. Many more vulnerable children and families are trapped in conflict zones, with no means to leave and little or no access to essential services and necessities such as health care, water, protection, and food. According to WHO, only 16% of the hospitals in Khartoum are fully operational, with 61% not functioning and 23% operating at reduced capacity.

2 - Activities/Outputs

TThe following are the key activities to be implemented through this project: 1Deliver health and nutrition supplies to newly displaced people in “safer” parts of the country.• Procure and preposition medical equipment, IMCI, PHC, midwifery and obstetric kits, trauma kits, inter-agency health kits and consumables, including vaccines with maintaining cold chain.• Ensure undisrupted nutrition supplies pipeline including RUTF, therapeutic milk, OTP medicine, and micro-nutrient supplementations. UNICEF is managing pipeline for core supplies for SAM treatment for all the partners.• Ensure continuity of lifesaving interventions at community and primary healthcare levels focusing on pregnant and lactating women and children under 5.• Support to SMOH for mobile clinics/OTPs for vaccination, SAM management, IYCF-E, IMCI: malaria and AWD, considering the rainy season, needs in displacement sites, and areas with no functional fixed sites.• Strengthen outbreak preparedness and response interventions at PHC/community level with early warning on risks of communicable diseases (Cholera, Measles, Malaria, Dengue); training on detection and case management of infections among children and pregnant women.• Ensure provision of PPE supply along with training on Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) in health facilities/community health level• Risk communication and community engagement: RCCE, use of PPE and washing hands while caring for the sick, cooking all animal products before eating. Implementation of community-based and outreach/mobile health services to vulnerable populations including the IMCI community component, ICCM, and outreach vaccination; Printing and distribution of health-related protocols, guidelines, training modules, recording forms, and registry books; Strengthen monitoring and evaluation.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate change is one of the causes of the increased vulnerability of the population of Sudan to natural disasters including floods, droughts and their consequences such as eruption of conflicts related to competition for resources causing displacements, increase in vector-borne diseases outbreak and others. This project aims to strengthen the capacity of the health system in Sudan to make it more resilient to emergencies and to be able to detect early signs of shocks before they happen to minimize their risks. Vulnerable communities related to the impact of climate change are targeted through this project. The project’s support at the community level will particularly contribute to community’s resilience to and ownership of their own health as part of the mitigation measures.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection is one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing (both online and class room trainings) its staffs and implementing partners to raise awareness on Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence not only those may be happening within its staffs bit also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at organizational level to put in place this mechanism. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: -The children, women, men and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone or tolerate behavior that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH - An Organizational Culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero tolerance for SEA and SH, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented. - Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights. - Swift and Credible Investigation and Sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations. - Survivor-Centered Response: Provide survivor-centered assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced. - Engaging Partners in the fight against SEA and SH.
