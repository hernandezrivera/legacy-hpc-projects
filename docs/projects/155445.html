Project Summary

To restore and improve livelihoods among vulnerable communities in targeted rural and urban districts. Improve agricultural outcomes, nutrition, health, gender equality and economic resilience through Village/Community Savings and Lending Associations and Cash for Assets interventions

Needs

• From December 2018 through March 2019, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) food security outcomes are expected to prevail in typical cereal deficit areas. This will mainly be due to depleted own-produced food stocks, constrained livelihoods, as well as high food prices. Stressed (IPC Phase 2) outcomes will be expected in the northern and other surplus production areas. • Economic challenges are expected to continue in 2019 and will have direct and indirect impacts on livelihoods and food security. Shortages of foreign currency on formal markets will remain as one of the main drivers of the parallel market for foreign currency, despite some government measures to curb it. Shortages of most basic food commodities like cooking oil, sugar, heat flour, and bread on the formal markets began in October and continue, especially in remote areas.• The economy is characterized by widespread increases in the prices of food and non-food commodities alike. Since the introduction of new tax and currency measures in October, most commodity prices have more than doubled. These measures also spurred speculative pricing in the economy. Most goods and services are increasingly being charged in scarce US Dollars or at highly inflated parallel market prices in bond notes, RTGS, or mobile transfers, making them inaccessible to a large section of the population.

Activities

• Village/Community Savings and Lending Associations (VSLA) formation, which promotes small changes and investments households can make to dramatically improve agricultural outcomes, nutrition, health, gender equality and economic resilience. • Capacity building of agro-dealers and traders to improve VSLA members’ access to high-quality, affordable inputs and output markets for surplus production. • The targeted Cash For Assets (CFA) interventions are designed to create, protect or rehabilitate productive assets and WASH infrastructure (repair/rehabilitation of institutions toilets and repair/rehabilitation of public toilets) that directly impact improved household food and income security, diversified livelihood opportunities and improved resilience. • The finalised prioritisation of projects will be determined by the final budget available and a more in-depth feasibility assessment of the potential rehabilitation and construction works. Priority will be given to ensuring that the infrastructure projects achieve the greatest possible scale in terms of the reach (number of people) and scope (number of wards targeted) for the greatest level of impact at ward and district level.
