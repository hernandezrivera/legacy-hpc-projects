Project Summary

Given the increasing number of persons suffering from injuries and disabilities caused by conflict situation, the physical rehabilitation interventions through static centers and mobile unit will have a visible and immediate impact, through providing quality rehabilitation services along with care giver training, PSS services, provision of assistive devices, training of service providers and awareness for non-rehabilitation service providers.
This intervention will help people with injuries or disability to increase their mobility and decrease their dependency on others for day to day activities. It will help them to come out from the corner at home to outside to integrate more in community. The project will insure the availability of mobility and orthoses devices in the proposed areas for immediate donation to the patient.
The mobile unit visits clients in their settlement developing a care plan, providing rehabilitation sessions outlined in the individual plan, and training caregivers to support in client mastery of tasks of daily living. The mobile unit is equipped with basic supplies like bands, balls mobility devices like wheelchairs, crutches, and toilet chairs. Mobile unit personnel assess, fit, deliver, and train clients in the use of assistive devices.
PSS services will be provided to disabled patients in the centers and sometime throughout mobile teams when needed.
Furthermore, the centers and mobile unites will create referral pathway with the governments and private hospitals in the proposed areas to insure the delivery of Rehab services to the patients after the surgeries and the continuation of having medical services.
The target group are:
• People with disabilities/injuries.
• Care givers of people with disabilities.
• Physical therapy Service providers.
• Non-rehabilitation service providers.
The NGO will Support 5 Physical Rehabilitation Centres and 9 Rehab Mobile Units, the INGO will support the centres and the mobile units in terms of running cost, equipment, assistive devices, incentives and training of the staff and caregivers and mobile units providing outreach services.
• Idleb City Static Rehab Center and four mobile units: covering Idleb City, Nayrab, Bennsh, Foah, Toum, Batenta, Taltuneh, Zardana Mashehad, Ram Hamdan, Kafr – Kafrehmul, Kafr Jales
• Al Dana Static Rehab Centre and mobile unit: in northern Idleb, covering Al Dana town and surrounding IDP camps and Deir Hassan - Darhashan
• Al Madiq Castle Static Rehab Centre and mobile unit: covering north Hama countryside and Hawash
• BAS Rehab Center and mobile unit: in north Aleppo covering Azaz, Shmarekh/ Salama/ Shamarin/ Kafr Kalbein/ Talil Elsham
• Al Bab Rehab Center and two mobile units: in north east of Aleppo covering Al Bab city

Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria is one of the world’s largest humanitarian crises since World War II. The conflict has significantly affected the demographic structure of the country, causing large displacements within and outside the country. Explosive weapons represent the most significant threat to civilians in Syria, accounting for over 8 out of 10 of recorded incidents, many of the explosive devices used have steel cases that cause shrapnel or have been designed to incapacitate, injure or kill people, Blasts from explosive devises cause unique patterns of injury, normally only seen in combat, with penetration wounds from shrapnel and blunt trauma resulting in complex fractures being the predominant forms of injury. 89 per cent of cases involving explosive weapons resulted in permanent or temporary physical impairments (Handicap International). Explosive weapons used on such a large scale will continue to be a leading cause of death, disability and injury long after the conflict in Syria is over. Many survivors suffered from severe multiple injuries, including complex fractures, penetration or trauma wounds, having been struck by ballistic fragments or crushed under collapsed buildings. Despite the very serious nature of these injuries, including the loss of limbs, permanent paralysis caused by spinal cord injuries or sensory impairment, many of the wounded have been unable to access adequate medical care. Almost a quarter of those facing these life changing injuries are children under the age of 18. The rehabilitation needs of those injured remain largely unmet, with an overwhelming demand for rehabilitation and prosthetics, in addition to the desperate need for mental health and psychosocial support. In the absence of appropriate care, untreated injuries will potentially lead to permanent impairment and a more significant degree of disability for many. Out of 2000 survivors, 60 percent of them have received no ongoing assistance, with 35 percent of those injured having access to either rehabilitation services or to an assistive devices but only 5 percent having access to both rehabilitation services and an assistive devices. 32 percent of survivors are very dependent in some or all aspects of daily life, with 37 of those injured totally dependent on others to carry out tasks such as toileting, washing, eating or sitting up in bed, 39 percent are very dependent in one dimension or more of daily living, (AAR Japan) Rehabilitation services are an urgent priority throughout static centres and outreach activities conduct by mobile teams to facilitate more positive outcomes for those injured, for example, in improving joint and limb function, pain management, wound healing, and psychosocial well-being. Similarly, assistive devices such as a wheelchair or mobility aid are needed to prevent impairments becoming more severe and allowing survivors with disabilities to access services. There is a need for training on effective physiotherapy and other strategies to improve functionality, to regain mobility and prevent further deterioration post injury. Training programs need to be established to facilitate this technical input. In addition, inclusive community response plans are needed, with awareness training sessions to reduce stigma and combat the loss of dignity felt particularly by the injured who are no longer able to carry out daily functions independently.

Implementation of activities

As part of this project the NGO will support Support 5 Physical Rehabilitation Centres and 9 Rehab Mobile Units in terms of running cost, equipment, assistive devices, incentives and training of the staff and caregivers and mobile units providing outreach services and provide the following services through each of facilities: Provide physical rehabilitation services and follow up sessions throughout static centers and mobile teams. In cooperation with health directorates, the INGO will support 5 static centers in terms of running cost, equipment, assistive devices, incentives and training of the staff with 9 mobile units conducting outreach activities. The mobile unit visits clients in their settlement developing a care plan, providing rehabilitation sessions outlined in the individual plan, and training caregivers to support in client mastery of tasks of daily living. The mobile unit is equipped with basic supplies like bands, balls mobility devices like wheelchairs, crutches, and toilet chairs. Mobile unit personnel assess, fit, deliver, and train clients in the use of assistive devices. In coordination with MHPSS department, PSS services will be provided to disabled patients in the centres and sometime throughout mobile teams when needed. The centres will create referral pathway with the governments and privet hospitals in the areas to insure the delivery of Rehab services to the patients after the surgeries and the continuation of having medical services. Service providers training on how to support people with disabilities and war related injuriesTraining, support on self-care, and technical guidance including occupational techniques and Activities of Daily Living therapy offered by seasoned professionals will lessen staff turnover and improve overall results. Activities of Daily Living therapy addresses persons with disability and aims to facilitate daily self-care and promote independence at home. The NGO will train physiotherapists and rehabilitation workers to acquire background understanding of pathophysiology, understanding of assessment tools, fitting of assistive devices, treatment advice for basic and complex cases, case management tools, the training will be conducted by Doctor of Physiotherapy, however ongoing trainings and supervision is necessary due to the complex nature of physiotherapy cases in conflict areas. Training of caregiversCare giver trainings will be provided by physiotherapist (PT) and Rehabilitation Workers (RWs) during technical follow up visits at home or at the rehabilitation centers. The training content is adapted as per the needs of each beneficiary and his/her care givers. It includes mainly:- Activity of daily living (bathing, brushing, dressing and undressing, clothing and unclothing etc.) for caregivers of those clients having severe limitation in hand function and body movements.- Use and adjustment of mobility devices (wheelchairs, axillary crutches, braces, splint etc.)- Basic exercises that requires minimum supervision by PTs and RWs.- Positioning and handling for beneficiaries having developmental delay or spinal cord injuries.- Transfer techniques for beneficiary having spinal cord injuries. Training of service providers on disability awareness.The INGO will conduct awareness sessions for Community volunteers/ workers and frontline health workers within the INGO's other departments as well as from other organizations. It has observed that community level volunteers have basic skills to identify children/people with severe disabilities only as it is easy to identify due to limitation in functions and body shape.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks• High levels of violence and criminality is a risk to safety of staff, particularly in Idleb. Beneficiaries and humanitarians alike face a real risk to their physical safety and security due to ongoing violence, targeting by different groups and criminal elements and being caught up in conflict related clashes.• Working with children, women and girls encompasses risk of potential sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), physical abuse and harassment, gender based violence and trafficking from a range of actors including their own family members, persons in positions of power including local officials, service providers and NGO's own staff and staff of other NGOs.• The lack of quality services and referral increases risk of further stigmatization and harm, lack of trust in available services and fewer disclosures.• Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services.• Frontline service providers risk developing post-traumatic stress for dealing with violence in their daily lives and supporting complex cases Mitigation Measures• The organization has developed an extensive security management structure, encompassing a security manager, as well as security officers, assistants, analysts and guards who establish valuable community based contacts enabling acceptance and a steady flow of information. The organization undertakes full Security Risk Assessments (SRAs) of all activity locations identify threats, estimate impact, and assess risks. Strategies developed from the SRAs, take the form of recommendations, security directives, and are incorporated into location specific Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). • Staff have emergency contacts and safe houses in Syria in case of emergency; receive regular security updates; are able to receive treatment at NGO supported clinics and medical evacuation in organizations supported ambulances; and only enter an area if they feel comfortable doing so.• The organization maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and follows internationally established protocols and standards for child safeguarding. The organizations staff and partner staff are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children. The organization has an established community based feedback and response mechanism to including a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases. All community centres and facilities have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback including reporting child protection concerns anonymously. The organization considers maintaining a gender balance in its outreach teams and at static centre, in order to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable with the services provided.• The organization provides staff wellbeing activities to its field staff, in order to help them cope with stress at the workplace and in their daily lives.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions• The NGO continues to have the approvals from local authorities to continue to provide services in targeted locations• The NGO can maintain access to targeted locations to deliver the proposes assistance• The security situation remains stable enough to implement the programs on a day to day basis• Non-interference by groups in control of geographic locations in delivery of proposes assistance. Risks:• Security situation remains volatile where inter armed group violence, criminality, military operations by major political actors could lead to destabilization of the current status negatively impacting safety and security of staff and beneficiaries• Delays in procurement and delivery of large shipments of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables, medical and non-medical equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicines• Program locations are taken over by Government of Turkey or Government of Syria or allied forces; leading to restrictions in implementation of activities without specific approvals (in the case of GoT) or through cross border operations (in the case of GoS) Mitigation Measures:• The physical security of the organizations facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded when needed. Close security monitoring, the end result of which prompts movement restrictions. Security training for staff which would hopefully limit/lessen exposure. The organization will support evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations with to continue managing program or revise targeting as per changing context. The organization will consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation and support for the treatment of injured staff and compensation for families of staff that may be killed as a result of the conflict activity and severance pay if staff can no longer continue with the organization.• The NGO uses joint efforts of procurement and legal staff to clarify all details of hosting countries regulations to comply. The NGO applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to be granted with authorization to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistance under all donor funded projects.• The NGO will explore options to continue support the program through local partner NGOs registered in Turkey in the case of Turkish takeover to maintain support to targeted facilities. The organization will withdraw support in case of GoS takeover in order to protect the safety of the staff as cross border operations are deemed illegal by GoS
