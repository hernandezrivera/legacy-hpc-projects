Project Summary

Acute malnutrition has been a persistent problem in south Sudan noted to be among the countries with the highest malnutrition rates in the world. According to South Sudan Humanitarian Nutrition Overview report 2019, the national and internal conflicts and associated economic decline have eroded the Government’s ability to provide consistent basic services to its people a gap that has exacerbated the prevalence of acute malnutrition due to the reduced ability of children under five years of age, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), and chronically ill persons to meet their nutritional needs because of displacement and increased morbidity caused by disease outbreaks, lack of access to clean water, sanitation facilities and basic health services. This has been further worsened by short and erratic rainfall which results in crop failure, lack of food in the markets, lack of capacity to produce food or earn income to purchase food and lack of nutritional knowledge. According to the integrated food security phase classification report September 2019, Jur River county has been categorized in IPC phase three (crisis) with high levels of malnutrition that needs urgent intervention. Therefore, provision of equitable and quality preventive and lifesaving integrated nutrition interventions to children 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition in these locations can significantly reduce the prevalence of malnutrition. Unity Cultural and Development Centre (UCDC) is requesting for financial support from the 2020 South Sudan Humanitarian Fund to implement an integrated lifesaving nutrition services in Jur river county of South Sudan. Implementation of UCDC’s integrated nutrition program will be in line with the critical needs prioritized by the Nutrition Cluster in the SSHF for 2020 using Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) approach. This will be complemented by prevention approaches to malnutrition including use of group model approach like formation of mother support groups, setting up of kitchen demonstration gardens at nutrition sites to facilitate dissemination of key Maternal Infant and Young Child Nutrition (MIYCN) messages at community level in the Jur river county. These services will target children aged 6 – 59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in remote and hard to access areas of Jur River County. In a way to contribute to improvement in nutrition status of children 6-59 months and PLWs in Jur River County, UCDC proposes to reach to a total population of 10,500 vulnerable people in Jur River county. Of this population, UCDC will provide life-saving nutrition interventions to a total of 900 children (480 girls and 420 boys) with severe acute malnutrition, 2500 children (1300 girls and 1200 boys) with moderate acute malnutrition and 450 pregnant and lactating women. Children 6-59 months with SAM without medical complications as well as children 6-59 months and PLW with MAM will be admitted and treated in Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) respectively in 6 outreach and mobile OTP/TSFP sites. Children under five with SAM with medical complications will be referred for treatment in Stabilization Centers.
UCDC will work in close collaboration with nutrition partners operating in static health facilities for accurate referrals and to avoid duplication of nutrition services. Additionally UCDC will coordinate with UNICEF and WFP for core supply pipeline support for RUTF, RUTF, CSB++and other routine medications and therapeutic rations for the beneficiaries in both SAM and MAM programs. The proposed mobile and outreach activities under this grant will therefore complement the already ongoing activities supported by UNICEF and WFP.
As a malnutrition preventive measure, UCDC will form 6 MSGs through which women of reproductive age and men will share experiences and educate each other on key health and nutrition issues.

1 - Needs

According to the IPC report of September 2019, high levels of acute food insecurity still persist affecting mainly children and women. According to the IPC September (2019) projections, an estimated 4.54 million people (39% of the population) will likely face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity from September to December 2019 and a further likely deterioration from January to April 2020 affecting an estimated 5.5 million people (47% of the total population) who are likely to face crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. In South Sudan, the nutrition situation remains critical with a GAM rate at 11.6% and SAM rate at 2.3% and this is attributed to a combination of factors mainly, poor harvest, dry spells and past conflict (FSNMS round 23 report of August 2019).According to IPC report September 2019, Jur River county (Western Bahr el Ghazal state) have been classified in IPC phase three (crisis) due to conflict and environmental factors with serious implications on food security and livelihood of the most vulnerable communities which has forced many communities to unfavorable food insecurity and negative livelihood coping mechanisms that has worsened the levels of acute malnutrition. Increased flooding and dry spells could contribute to limited crop production and a further deterioration of the nutrition situation, despite efforts of partners, the food security and nutrition situation remains critical in the state of Western Bahr el Ghazal (WBerG) particularly Jur Liver County. According to the REACH report published in June 2019 confirms that population movements coupled with poor harvests and high market prices in Jur River county among others have led to increased vulnerability with many settlements reported relying on food aid or other negative coping strategies to meet their dietary needs. These affected populations lack immediate food sources to meet their daily caloric needs, lack fast maturing seed varieties and farm inputs and livelihood sources which increases the likelihood of acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women and therefore, this project comes at a critical moment when the high prevalence of acute malnutrition among children, pregnant and lactating women in this location need to be addressed.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct mass community mobilization and awareness on malnutrition through nutrition campaigns and stakeholders workshops reaching 1200 households Activity 1.2: Identify, establish and train 6 mother support groups to target women and men. Activity 1.3: Identify, establish and train 30 CNVs five per mobile site to provide MIYCN counselling services and community screening and referral as per national standards. Activity 1.4: Provide individual and group counseling at the nutrition centers. Activity1.5: Provide CMAM, MIYCN and WASH refresher training for nutrition staffs including CHD as per national standards. Activity 1.6: Conduct biweekly community dialogue meetings to target MSGs on behavioral change and cultural barriers to MIYCN with key messages facilitated by CNVs Activity 1.7: Mobilize, support integration of Vitamin A and Deworming during NIDs and Child health days through social mobilization and distributions.Activity 2.2: Identify, Recruit and train 42 Nutrition staffs (12 Nutrition Assistants and 30 CNVs to provide mobile outreach CMAM services in hard to reach areas as per national standards. Activity 2.3: Provide timely and quality Treatments for SAM and MAM boys and girls 6-59 months and PLW with MAM at 6 OTP/TSFP mobile sites. Activity 2.4: Ensure timely transportation and delivery of nutrition supplies to all 6 mobile OTP/TSFP sites/outreach sites in Jur River county. Activity 2.5: Provide all reference guidelines and job aids at all mobile OTP/TSFP sites. Activity 2.6: Establish and ensure all 6 nutrition sites have Minimum WASH facilities. Activity 3.1: Provide GBV and CP training for Nutrition staffs (Nutrition Assistants). Activity 3.2: Establish referral system between nutrition program and other sectors including FSL, Health and protection. Activity 3.3: Establish compliant and feedback mechanism at all 6 nutrition mobile sites. Activity 3.4: Provide Hygiene and sanitation promotion and WASH NFIs through Mother Support Groups. Activity 3.5: Provide RDT for malaria for SAM children and provided appropriate treatments for Positive children in adherence with the national guidelines.Activity 3.6 Establish kitchen gardens at each mobile nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers on establishment and managing a kitchen garden at home.Activity 3.7 Conducting of safety audits with the mothers to establish the safety of the nutrition sites and other gender issues affecting them.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

UCDC has a legal framework that elaborates the recognition of the rights of beneficiaries in all the project locations. All project staffs are bound to operate under these protocols and a monitoring team constituted by community members shall be put in place to make sure there is no violation of the beneficiaries rights.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project implementation strategy shall make sure displaced people are included in the beneficiaries receiving screening services, nutrition education sessions and treatment of malnutrition and they will be involved as part of the community nutrition volunteers, lead mothers and mother support groups. They will also participate in the planning, implementation and monitoring the project progress and give feedback on the quality of the services offered.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

UCDC will ensure that the project will be introduced to county health department and make sure they understand the objectives and targets of the project. The national nutrition guidelines, registers, reporting tools from South Sudan Ministry of Health shall be used during implementation and monitoring. The nutrition sites supervision shall be carried out with the county health department according to the protocols provided by the South Sudan ministry of health and reports shall be shared with the County health department.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

UCDC shall make sure that the project mainstreams protection according the protection mainstreaming protocols across all the activities to be implemented. The project is aligned with the national objectives since it will be implemented according to national nutrition guidelines and all the reporting and monitoring frameworks shall be aligned with the existing nutrition implementation structures as per the nutrition cluster and the line ministries.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project environment is sufficiently stable and UCDC has a good geographical experience and presence in the those areas since we already have presence in these areas implementing food security project.UCDC shall continue to make sure there is complete sensitization of the authorities and beneficiaries to make sure that the project implementation doesn't lead to conflict.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project design is participatory in nature since the community beneficiaries and stakeholders will be part and partial of the implementation as community nutrition volunteers, nutrition assistants and lead mothers and the rest of the beneficiaries will be involved in daily maternal infant and young child nutrition activities at community level as mother support groups. This will promote sustainability since the community beneficiaries and stake holders will have acquired knowledge and skills in nutrition services delivery which they will continue to extend to the rest of the households after the end of the project. To ensure sustained functionality of the nutrition intervention activities, mother support groups shall be formed in the locations and trained and equipped with MIYCN knowledge so that they can continue sensitizing the community with maternal infant and young child nutrition knowledge. UCDC will ensure proper exit strategy procedures are followed by holding meetings with the County Officials and the implementing partners operating in Jur River County to share the success, lessons learnt and recommendations.
