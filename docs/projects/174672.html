Project Summary

Strengthened self-reliance of 14345 CAR refugees and 3605 host community households in East region of Cameroon
This project will target new beneficiaries in new zones and it introduces activities such as connecting smallholder farmers to markets, strengthening household and community assets, improving post-harvest handling, supporting women farmers and pastoralists, strengthening government ownership and building resilience amongst refugees and vulnerable host population for long-term development.
In line with PRM and UNHCR livelihood strategies, the project takes a multi-year, incremental approach to build sustainable livelihoods for refugees and host communities. It facilitates improved livelihoods skills and opportunities for increasing income through agro-pastoral production.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In line with PRM and UNHCR livelihood strategies, the project takes a multi-year, incremental approach to build sustainable livelihoods for refugees and host communities. It facilitates improved livelihoods skills and opportunities for increasing income through agro-pastoral production. In Year 1, the project identifies 2,300 beneficiaries for agropastoral activities (2,000 agriculture beneficiaries and 300 pastoralists). Agriculture beneficiaries will be provided with 500 ha of land, seeds and tools, and will attend training on modern agricultural techniques. Pastoralists will receive 3 small ruminants each, veterinary monitoring and they will be trained in zero grazing technics and marketing of small livestock produce. In Year 2, the project identifies an additional 2,000 agriculture beneficiaries and 300 additional pastoralists. 800 ha of land (500 for new and 300 for Y1 beneficiaries) will be secured and more inputs provided. It also extends the capacity of Year 1 agriculture beneficiaries in post-harvest management, value chain addition, marketing and transformation of specific agricultural products through trainings, technical support and the construction of drying areas. Committees are established, and members will be trained and supported to manage these facilities. The additional pastoralists will receive 3 small ruminants each, training and veterinary support.In Year 3, additional ha of land will be provided to Y2 famers (300 ha) who will also receive training on post-harvest management, market days, value chain addition, marketing and transformation of specific agricultural products. All the 4,000 agricultural beneficiaries will receive training on advanced agricultural technics and climate change adaptation and supported with transformation facilities. It provides refresher training and technical support to the 4,00 farmers in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development. Annual vaccination campaign in collaboration with the Ministry of Livestock, Fisheries and Animals Industries will be conducted in the zone of intervention. In year 3, annual general vaccination campaign will be held with the ministry of livestock, fisheries and animal industry. It will take in account Y1, Y2 pastoralist beneficiaries and other community members. The pastoralists will benefit from monitoring by veterinaries as well as refresher training on zero-grazing, livestock rearing and marketing of livestock produce. The project also establishes 260 VSLA groups in Year 1 and 2, of 20 members each for the farmers and 10 members for the pastoralists, for the total 4,600 participants of objective. In Year 2, it is legalizing the Year 1 VSLA groups and connecting them to microfinance structures. The VSLA groups established in Year will be legalized in Year 3. The purpose of the VSLA is to help community members access micro-finance at local community level. Year 3 sees refresher trainings for all entrepreneurial groups and links groups to national microfinance structures to help community members wishing to acquire bigger credits than what they can receive from their VSLA group. This will also help them safeguard their funds. Objective 2: Facilitate access of youth and women Central African refugees and vulnerable national communities to self-employment in the East Region of CameroonIn addition, the project identifies 150 women and structures them into groups of 10 women each in Year 1. Women represent 53% of the population according to UNHCR statistics, they are most subject to food insecurity and malnutrition than men. These women will be trained in specific IGA of their choice and will receive start up kits to start their own business. In Year 2, 150 new women will be identified, structured, trained and provided with startup kits. Meanwhile, Y1 beneficiaries will be supported to be registered by competent authorities.
