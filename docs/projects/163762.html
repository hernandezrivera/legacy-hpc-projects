Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to, and quality of education at both formal and informal learning environments through a combination of school infrastructure rehabilitation and activities that promote the psychosocial well-being of boys and girls in conflict-affected communities. With no end to the current conflict in sight, it is critical that affected populations, specifically youth, are provided with adequate educational opportunities and psycho-social support to enhance resilience and reduce negative coping mechanisms. This is particularly necessary for vulnerable populations including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, non-displaced conflict affected people, host communities, migrants.
IOM Libya’s Community Stabilization approach empowers communities to take ownership of programming that addresses underlying drivers of instability, including tensions between migrants and local communities, from identification and prioritization of needs through implementation. Thus, programs to restore critical services and community infrastructure, improve access to opportunities for conflict-affected communities and also foster communication and collaboration even among segregated communities. IOM has seen through this participatory approach that communities consistently prioritize education and psychosocial services that will contribute to the well-being of children, youth, and adults, critical to broader community cohesion and stability.
IOM Community Stabilization Unit will utilize preexisting local committees and community stakeholders to engage the community in identifying priority locations for rehabilitation in each city, while also working with schools, teachers, and civil society organizations to implement psychosocial activities appropriate to each location; including programs tailored to children in conflict and post-conflict environments.

1 - Needs

The education sector has suffered dramatically over the past six years, as teachers employed in public schools have lost their jobs amid the breakdown of government services, and Libyan and migrant students have had their education interrupted and constricted by the volatile security situation in the country. Over 30 % of the young Libyan population is affected by the weakened education system and among the most affected students are IDPs and migrants and those with special educational needs. This calls for an urgent response by national and international actors to avoid knowledge gaps and to lay the educational framework for the next generation. Compounding this, many conflict-affected and displaced children are suffering from psychosocial distress or trauma, which can affect their ability to learn and develop to their full potential. Access to education remains one of the key priorities for returnees in order to reestablish a sense of normalcy in children and youth lives. Migrants and refugee children particularly from Sub-Saharan African face the most difficulty in accessing education, as they prevented from enrolling due to issues around legal status and documentation. Those children that do attend the schools are often discriminated against and face additional language and cultural barriers. Yet education, along with other emergency responses such as shelter, water and sanitation, health and food aid, has been shown to improve the morale, physical and psychological wellbeing of children affected by conflict, displacement and disasters. In the short term, education plays a role in meeting children’s basic needs; in the long term, it will help them to reduce their vulnerability to disaster and will endow them with the tools to build new lives.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Although the context in each of the four locations is different, each city is challenged by tensions and conflict; some of which become violent. Community Stabilization applies a conflict sensitive lens to its programming, and is aware of several consistent risks, including tribal and ethnic conflicts, narratives of marginalization, and general insecurity. Continuous analysis of local context is needed in order to be aware of changing protection needs.

5 - Mitigation measures

While directly implementing various activities to address protection concerns in Libya, the project will mainstream protection as an integral part of its overall intervention. IOM will prioritize assistance to persons in situation of vulnerability. The project will identify specific protection needs of vulnerable groups, utilizing DTM’s Mobility Tracking with protection enhanced indicators. The findings will be considered throughout the project cycle including beneficiary selection, service modality selection, and delivery of assistance. Whenever cases with protection concerns are identified by the teams providing basic services, they will be referred to IOM’s Protection Team for further assistance or referral. Additionally, the project will be implemented in line with the international humanitarian principles and protection values, including ‘do no harm’, non-discrimination, meaningful access, safety, dignity, participation, empowerment and accountability.
