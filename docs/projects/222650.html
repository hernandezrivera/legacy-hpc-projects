Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced a worsening humanitarian situation since the outbreak of Sudan war in April 2023 that triggered the arrival of 880,348 returnees and refugees in the country. This situation highly increased the pressure on an already very fragile context where humanitarian needs are dire due to the combined effect of displacement related to inter-communal violence, successive flooding episodes and economic and political instability leaving 9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024—a figure anticipated to remain consistent with the 2025 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) projections.
This project aims to support the most vulnerable people located in the priority area where SI has operations namely in Upper Nile, Unity and Northern Bahr El Ghazal States.
The lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices, lack of shelter, food insecurity and livelihood insecurities puts at risk the most vulnerable.

Since 2006, Solidarités International has become a key humanitarian actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency
lifesaving responses across the country and providing integrated WASH and FSL services to crisis-affected populations in areas of static presence in Upper Nile and Unity States. SI plans to broaden its operations in Northern Bahr El Ghazal in 2025 to strengthen its assistance in response to the Sudan crisis at the border crossing sites. Since 2012, SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-
affected populations all over the country. Since 2022, SI is a member of the ERRM consortium and was able to deploy emergency response teams countrywide following conflicts, displacements or epidemic alerts.
In 2025, SI will continue to provide emergency live-saving WASH, FSL and Shelter responses across South Sudan to restore basic access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation while ensuring
equitable access, safety, and accountability to supported populations. Through its Emergency Rapid Response Mechanism team, SI will deploy to emergency-affected locations to provide immediate life-saving responses. It will include WaSH and FSL quick response under cash or in-kind distribution.
At the same time, SI is reinforcing its presence in Upper Nile and Unity as well as starting activities and Northern Bar El Gazal to provide basic WASH and FSL services that target the most vulnerable, including those suffering malnutrition, food insecurity, disease outbreak and protection threats.

1. Needs

In Unity and Upper Nile, where SI has a static presence, flooding and conflict in Sudan result in displacement of thousands of households, it reduces their access to basic WASH services, food access as well as increased protection risks. Water points and sanitationinfrastructure have been submerged, contaminated, and damaged. Even in areas with normally better water supply, flooding and displacement have had significant effects.Approximately 327,000 individuals have been displaced across 18 counties. This situation in addition of the increased pressure over resources (e.g. water, land, etc.)since April 2023 in link with the influx of migrants from Sudan will further impact the already affected-population. For instance, Unity State, one the county thatCLOSE huge loss in land for farming and livestock. In November 2024,47% of the overall population are facing high levels of acute food insecurity. Among children, 2.1 million are likely to suffer from acute malnutrition situation through June 2025. IPC projections for the first part of 2025 expect to have 7.69 million people facing acute food insecurity with a peak of vulnerability for the population during the lean season. On top of the needs identified, epidemics have been also reported in the country, from Hepatitis E to cholera outbreak reaching an epidemic situation officially declared in October 2024. WASH and food security related needs are huge, as newly displaced people are completely lacking access to basic services. This lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene and continuing shocks has increased the exposure of the vulnerable populations to water- borne diseases, in turn influencing rates of malnutrition (affecting especially children under 5and pregnant and lactating women). People with specific vulnerabilities, such as People with Disability, also face many additional challenges to accessing basicservices.

2. Project activities

SI’s 2025 strategy will focus on 3 Axes: 1) Short-term response to reduce mortality and morbidity for the most vulnerable population countrywide through static and roving multisecorial ERRM emergency responses (corresponding to WASH objectives CO1,CO2, CO3) 2) Ensuring medium term safe, equitable and dignified access to essential WASH services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of WSC 4&5; (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2CO3 and CO4) 3)Identify and implement long term solutions for sustainable community-based WASH services through piloting innovative solutions and engagement with communities and local markets, and increased flood resilience of infrastructure (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2,CO3,CO4) Axis 1: SI will implement the following activities in places affected by shocks countrywide for a duration of maximum 3 months:- water supply to communities and health and nutrition facilities: chlorination of water sources, distribution of water treatment kits at HH level, minor rehabilitations of existing water systems, training of Water management committees and provision of toolkits. SWAT or water trucking will be deployed if no other options are available. - Emergency sanitation solutions will be implemented in locations with public health risks. Mobile Sanitation Teams (MSTs) will be deployed for feces picking and removal. Rehabilitation of existing latrines in communities and health and nutrition facilities. Emergency latrines or defecation fields can also be done.- Hygiene promotion messages through community hygiene promoters.- WASH NFI and MHM items will be distributed prioritizing SAM children, vulnerable households including female headed households, unaccompanied elders, person with disabilities and PLW. Axis2: SI will implement the following activities in Priority 1 areas, and WSC 4 and 5 counties, Renk, Malakal, Rubkona and Aweil East: - Water supply in communities and TC through SWAT systems and the rehabilitation of existing water systems or water trucking.- Support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water in the facility to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections transmission - Construction and maintenance of latrines in health and nutrition facilities and TC. MST for feces and solid waste picking in TC and communities. Construction of waste management areas in health and nutrition facilities.- Vector control activities such as drainage, spraying and grass cutting in transit centers- Hygiene promotion through household visits and mass hygiene campaigns in communities, health and nutrition facilities.- WASH NFIs distribution to children under 5 and PLW suffering from malnutrition through referral from nutrition centers and in TCs.- MHM kits distribution following referral from Protection services. Using CVA modalities where possible.Axis 3: SI will implement the following activities in relevant priority 1 counties and WSC 4 and 5 locations, Renk, Malakal, Rubkona and Aweil East: - Pilot alternative means of localized and sustainable safe water supply with communities that provide improvements in access to safe water, e.g. adapted shallow wells, sand filters, and non-chemical household water treatment. - Rehabilitate and construct water points- Capacity building for WMCs with community engagement on contributions to WASH service management - Identify low tech methodologies for construction of community-led household latrines using only local materials targeting areas with high density of population with public health risk.- Assessment and support for local markets to supply WASH items and materials for activities. Transversal to all activities: Incorporate flood resilient designs for all construction of communal and institutional latrines. WASH safety audits and AAP consultations will be conducted before all interventions to implement inclusive activities that contribute to GBV mitigation.
