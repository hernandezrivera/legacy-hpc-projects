Project Summary

There is no solution at sight for the big displacement of Rohingya from Myanmar to Bangladesh. More than 900.000 people continue living mostly from relief in the camps. Vegetable gardening has been promoted by different organizations including HEKS/EPER over the past years and is an opportunity for Rohingya to make use of their farming experiences from their home villages and diversify their food intake.
HEKS/EPER has closely analyzed options for vegetable gardening in the camps and can build on previous experience. HEKS/EPER will directly implement the project by its experienced and capable staff . 7’000 households will be supported by training on vegetable cultivation and receive inputs during 7 seasons. The homestead gardening project primarily aims at supporting women in homestead production of vegetables and provides women a means to productive activities in an environment. The project will be selecting women beneficiaries with priority based on the scope and promote the practice of equal treatment in terms of women empowerment. Pregnant women and lactating mothers need more nutritional food and will be addressed and prioritized them in the Project accordingly. Boys and girls(adolescent) will be able to meet their nutrition from vegetable gardening result in more presence in the classes regularly. A minimum of 30:70 women to men ratio will be ensured for community mobilizers. A total 32'900 people; among them children female(<=17) 7'665, children male(<=17) 10'496, adult female(>=18) 7'204, adult male(>=18) 7'536, will be benefitted from this project. The Swiss Red Cross will provide compost that they produced in the SwS funded solid waste management plant, hence a crucial input that will benefit those people who have originally collected the waste.

1 - Identified Needs

AssessmentHEKS/EPER has built previous experience on vegetable gardening in the Rohingya Camps and implemented two projects in 2018 and 2019. Achievements and challenges of different approaches were analyzed. The project will be implemented in Camp 15 where HEKS/EPER is working since 2017 and neighboring camps 14 and 16, to which HEKS/EPER expanded already in early 2020 with Shelter Emergency activities and recently also with Transitional Shelter programming. HEKS/EPER has detailed experience on these camps, which strongly benefit the assessment, in times when field access and conducting surveys in the fields is minimized due to COVID-19. In January 2020, HEKS/EPER has hosted an experience sharing workshop between Swiss NGOs (Solidar with partner GUK, Tdh, and HEKS) on vegetable cultivation. For the preparation of this project, HEKS/EPER has coordinated with various NGOs implementing similar projects (GUK, Solidar, UNHCR, Dhaka Ahsania Mission, Helvetas, and CNRS) and discussed different approaches and challenges. Additionally, discussions were held with shelter experts at the shelter cluster, UNHCR, and IOM, as the limited cultivable land makes the roofs a relevant space for cultivation. Needs and Response Camp 14, 15, and 16 form a cluster of three camps. With 21.500 families it is the second-largest cluster after the mega camp Kutupalong with its 23 subcamps, which is only 1 km away. The three camps have been established in October 2017 on hills previously covered by forest adjacent to the main road linking all camps in Ukhia.The camps are highly overpopulated, which is a consequence of the allocation of land by the Bangladesh Government. Camp 15 has a population density of 18.8m2 land per person, which is significantly lower than the SPHERE standard recommendation of 45m2 per person . Camp 14 and 16 are slightly less densely populated. In all three camps, open space for vegetable cultivation is rare. Access to Food and Nutrition The Rohingya arrived in a setting where they are highly dependent on food support in the absence of legal opportunities to earn income in the local economy. People’s basic food requirements are covered by regular food distributions mainly under the lead of WFP providing food items equivalent to 2100 kcal. In the first years of the response, households received general food assistance consisting of rice, lentils, and fortified cooking oil which was not allowing a diversified consumption without households purchasing additional food items. Since early 2019, WFP gradually shifted the distribution approach to e-voucher systems, and since June 2020 reaches 86% of households by this methodology . Households have now access to 20 different food items which includes also fresh vegetables (2 varieties of spinach, potatoes, onion, garlic, green and red chili, lemon, and pumpkin). The shift in the distribution approach is a crucial element in diversifying food intake of people, which finally translates into improved nutrition status. Hoddinott examined associations between the type of food assistance and the anthropometric status of children in the Rohingya camps and found that children in households receiving general food support had poorer height-for-age z scores and were more likely to be stunted. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) decreased from 19.3 percent when the Rohingya arrived in 2017 to 10.9 percent in October 2019 , and is now below the WHO defined emergency threshold of 15%. However, it is still classified as serious. Following the Food Security Sectoral Response Strategy, the shift in the distribution modality should still be accompanied by promoting the establishment of micro-gardens. As these gardens are expected to have a further positive impact on diversifying food intakes, not only as people will have access to more food and vegetables, but also as people that cultivate their own vegetables, will be more likely to use their e-vouchers for other important food groups.
