Project Summary

VSO Bangladesh in partnership with Mukti Cox’s Bazar is implementing Education in Emergency (EiE) programme in Cox’s Bazar to provide vital education and safe space for learning and to help Rohingya children to reclaim their childhood. The programme aims to provide accessible Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) and learning opportunities for Rohingya children in a safe environment. VSO programme also include life skills training with focus on Sexual Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) education in the Rohingya camp for adolescent girls and boys. The project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. The different needs of girls and boys have been analyzed and integrated well in the activities and objectives. Sex and Age Disaggregated Data was collected understand the different needs of the groups. Providing SRHR services to girl and boy based on their different needs and will also be conducting awareness on child protection, children’s health, hygiene, WASH, SRHR and education related issues.
The project will promote inclusive teaching methods, ensuring participation of girls and boys by building the capacity teachers on using gender-sensitive methodologies Children living with disabilities are not enrolled in Home Based Centres in Rohingya Jamtoli Refugee camp 15. The project will identify Identification of the children with the disabilities(impairment) and other special needs who can access the ECCE in-home based centers

Needs

Recent Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment Report mentions indicates the necessity of community-based education which will ensure greater participation of school age child (5-17) attending to the learning opportunities. Also gap analysis suggest several girls and boys (15-24) are still to be reached. VSO appreciate these needs and designed programme so that targeted beneficiaries are reached through innovative approach. The project considers the child protection issues as high importance as well as cross cutting components towards all interventions. even through the different session massages are disseminated to ensure child protection. Even during the coordination sessions with the different actors, the child procreation issues are prioritized, for example perpetuate sexual abuse against girls. Big Sisters (community mobilisers) will be engaging Little Sisters to understand issues they are facing. Adult Champions are check in with Big Sisters to get reports about such incidents. Due to lack of awareness and some Traditional Harmful Practice both in the Rohingya the children particularly girls are supported to continue their education. SRHR are sensitive issue and have many stigmas and myths attached to it. Girls are not confident to share their concerns. In addition, educators may not feel comfortable discussing SRHR due to lack of knowledge or cultural reasons. Institutions may also not be prepared to include SRHR in their program due to the negative attitude attached to it. For example, in the Rohingya camp parents are less interested in sending their children to TLCs. Regular stakeholder meetings and discussions will be held by project staff with educators to assess progress and hear concerns. VSO has developed a sensitive approach based on involvement of relevant stakeholders to address misconceptions and stigma attached to SRHR. We believe that for adolescents and youth to realize their SRH rights, change must occur at multiple levels in society. That means empowered young people being supported by their families and communities to demand and access better quality SRH services and policies within an enabling policy environment. VSO’s pro-poor, rights-based approach to adolescent and youth sexual and reproductive health (AYSRHR) combines proven methods both for creating demand for SRH services and for building capacity of key stakeholders. VSO is planning to coordinate with health sector for the SRHR activities to include this as mainstream health program as this service can be included in health services, i.e, mobile clinics that other organizations are running in the camps. VSO are working with several community clinics in different districts to improve AYSRHR delivery services. There is limited space in camp and VSO will use the existing learning centers for reaching to more students through convert some of the centers to two storied ones in consultation with CIC, and sector.

Activities or outputs

Implementation strategy and approach:Enabling teachers and peers/learning facilitators (mother/big sisters/aunties):Building on learning and evidence from VSO’s Sisters for Sisters mentoring approach to educating out of school children, we will continue mobilise youth and adults from Rohingya communities to increase access to inclusive learning opportunities in safe environment. Children, including children with disabilities will be supported with community based ECCE support by the big sisters/mothers/ bothers who will be taking care of them to teach at the household level (5 -10 households by one big sister/ Aunt). VSO will work with other partners through sector for overall capacity building through this proven Sisters for Sisters mentoring approach. This approach is proven to be successful globally as this improved significantly the quality and inclusion of education for out-of-reach children from the most marginalized and disadvantaged communities.Implementing Partner:Mukti Cox’s Bazar will continue work as implementing partner for VSO and will be responsible primarily for implementing the project activities as per details implementation plan; organizing training, workshop, seminar, day observance, different meeting, education fair and any other event ensuring community engagement; provide age and level wise teaching learning materials and prepare programmatic and financial report and responsible for compliance issue. Enabling and empowering individual learners: Building on evidence and learning from other VSO countries and in the sector, VSO will pilot the use of mobile technology to deliver education services directly to learners. The lessons from “Unlocking Talent through Technology”, “TESTGhana” and “BELL App” developed by VSO Cambodia will be contextualized and piloted. In partnership with USTAD mobile VSO had developed a “School App” , which uses the contents like songs, poems, stories, teaching method from Rohingya community where Rohingya children, parents and community members participate in recording those. This App will be used offline; Contents would be downloaded before it would be used in camp. Addressing Disaster Risk Reduction and Environmental Aspects:The proposed program integrates VSO’s Organizational Disaster Preparedness Plan to understand the Natural Hazards and Climate Change Impact in Bangladesh in general and in particular in the areas VSO Bangladesh is working, to identify the current Capacity of VSO as Organization to deal with Emergency Situation due to Natural Hazards in working areas and to recognize Resource and Areas to strengthen for Adopting Emergency Situation. Feedback and accountability for the project:Like other projects, VSO will maintain a channel of accountability and will address the feedback from various stakeholders. VSO have a dedicated project management team in Cox’s Bazar who ensure that all activities are running while maintaining the required safeguarding issues in place. The Learning Center Management Committee (LCMC) visit the center provide feedbacks and VSO carefully address those, while discussing these issues with sectors or even with CIC. Involvement with present coordination structure:VSO have active participation in different sector meeting both in camp and in Cox’s Bazar. VSO submit 5W report to the sectors regularly. VSO have also close interaction with the office of the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commission (RRC).
