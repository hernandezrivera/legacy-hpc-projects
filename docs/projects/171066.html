Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen the protection and social safety net and resilience of children, caregivers, and communities in the face of chronic exposure to conflict-related violence by: (1) providing community-based and specialized child protection case management and psychosocial and mental health services to children and their families, (2) providing specialized package of services to GBV survivors and girls and women at risk, (3) surge emergency capacity of community-based organizations on psychosocial and child protection interventions and specialized MHPSS services, including online modalities, and (4) addressing IHL and IHRL violations through advocacy initiatives targeting all duty bearers and based on documentation of grave violations. This project targets Palestinian children directly and indirectly exposed to violence and abuse related to the Israeli Occupation, including settler violence, unsafe school environments, home and school demolitions, and displacement. War Child Holland (WCH) and partners the Psycho-Social Counseling Center for Women (PSCCW) and the Palestinian Counselling Center (PCC) will be targeting acutely affected children and families living in the most marginalized areas of Area C in the West Bank with specific focus on the Northern West Bank and Bedouin Communities located in the below:
- Area C of Nablus governorate covering Duma, Qaryout, Beita communities,
- Area C of Salfit Governorate Bourqin, Rafat, Hares, Kfil Hares and Kufer Al Deek
- Area C and Bedouin communities of the Jordan Valley under Tubas and Jericho governorates, including Kardalla, Bardalla, Ain El Beida, Al Aqaba, Al Jiftlik with special focus on Al Musaffah and Ash-Shuneh, Gharb al Muthallath, Abu al Ajaj, Fasayel including Fasayil al Wasta and Fasayil Al Fauqa, Marj Naji, Zbeidat, and Al Auja with focus on Ras Ein al ‘Auja. Outreach CP and PSS activities will be also be provided to Khirbet Tell el Himma, Ein al Hilwa – Um al Jmal,Hammammat al Males- Al Burj, Hammamat al Males- Al Meiteh, An Nuwei’ma al Fauqa Bedouin Communities.
- Area C and Bedouin communities of Bethlehem Governorate, Beit Skaria Job Al Theb, Al Taquo including the surrounding Bedouin communities like Rashaydeh and Kisan, Al Maasara, Al Walajeh
- Area C and Bedouin communities of Hebron: Marginalized communities of Masafer Yatta
The project partners will target 23 CBOs distributed among the six above-mentioned Governorates. The areas were selected based on previous experience of consortium partners in those areas and needs identified as well as on the OCHA Vulnerability Profile of Palestinian Communities in Area C.
In addition to specific Protection interventions, War Child and partners will mainstream PSEA and Protection in Covid19 response efforts, in line with local infection and prevention control (IPC) measures and global guidance notes produced by the Alliance for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (e.g. CPHA Note on the Protection of Children during Covid19 outbreak and annexes), and IASC PSEA during COVID-19 Response Technical Guidance Note.

1 - Needs

This project addresses MHPSS, GBV and protection risks stemming from occupation-related violence and complements and fills gaps in community-based CP, GBV and MHPSS interventions in Area C. The gaps and needs were identified through analysis of complementary assessments: WCH’s 2020 rapid needs assessment of the MHPSS and CP needs of communities in Area C in COVID-19, 2019 MHPSS, CP and education needs assessment of children living in Area C of the Tubas governorate, and 2018 PSS and CP needs assessment for Area C of the Jericho governorate; WCH’s 2019 HF ongoing project including WCH case management database; PSCCW assessment on GBV; PCC’s 2020 C19 assessment; UNFPA’s 2020 C19 assessment on GBV, in addition to UNICEF’s review of the humanitarian MHPSS needs/gaps in oPt. The project design was informed by consultations with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), Ministry of Women Affairs (MoWA), CP/MHPSS partners and GBV cluster; YMCA, MDM, UNRWA, MSF, World Vision, PRCS and direct input from communities. Area C communities included in this project are some of the highest ranked vulnerable communities according to the Vulnerability Profile Project (OCHA) and Protection Vulnerability Index.The targeted communities face regular incidents of settler violence (weekly), military drills, home raids, and demolition and displacement orders- which according to War Child’s assessments are the main causes of stress and distress for children. According to War Child Case Management database, in the Jordan Valley and Hebron Governorate, between 2019 and 2020, out of 148 cases, 69 of neglect, 23 were domestic violence, 32 emotional abuse, and 2 child labour. 40% of the cases were boys and the rest were girls.The majority of the cases are between the age of 6-11. While boys and men tend to be more directly exposed to occupation- related violence, girls and women are also affected. Based on an assessment conducted by UNFPA, C19 crisis has exacerbated women and children’s vulnerabilities.This has placed them at increased risk of various forms of violence, including intimate partner violence. Fears about the virus, economic stress and quarantine measures, have increased household tension, compounding domestic violence. Due to movement restrictions and lockdown measures imposed since the C19 crisis, life-saving care and support to GBV survivors have been curtailed in the oPt, creating a response gap both for survivors and those at risk of GBV. Face-to-face interaction and access to GBV services in safe spaces have been significantly reduced or, in most cases, completely suspended, increasing the need for online specialized MH counselling services. The HNO 2020 confirms that the GBV system is not sufficient in meeting the needs of GBV survivors. The system is fragmented with different quality and services depending on different areas. Among the most pressing issues are the lack of available, acceptable and accessible services, lack of quality GBV care and lack of functioning referral system. According to the information system of the GBV Sub-Cluster, around 52,000 GBV survivors were detected over the period 2017-2019 in the oPt-only around 78% received services, 7.7% were externally referred and more than 24% of the cases did not receive any services. SAWA recorded 9 cases of rape of women/girls aged 16-35 and 12 cases of attempted suicide among adolescents in August in the WB. With the ongoing C19 outbreak, PCC’s assessment confirms that in the majority of areas, cases of violence and abuse were reported, especially against children and women. The results indicate that psychosocial services are not available in most regions or families had to travel long distances in order to obtain psychological support services even in normal non-emergency conditions; there are some areas where there are no specialists or psychological counselors at all. Results showed that there is violence and abuse of a particularly high level against women. Tragically a girl died as a result of the violence two weeks before participating in PCC assessment. While the need for child protection, GBV and psychosocial support is increasing, the current care system is overstretched and cannot respond to the protection and psychosocial needs of girls, boys and their caregivers. The MOSD has expressed to War Child the need for more support needed for the CPN in the field, as the CPN only responds to high-risk cases and do not have the capacity to follow-up on low-medium risk cases. Access to the remote villages and Bedouin communities targeted under this project is also a challenge due to the MOSD’s capacity. Through this project, War Child will identify community case workers within targeted CBOs to increase surge preparedness at community level to detect children at risk, provide basic assessments and safely refer. WCH will provide case management services and referrals for low and medium risk cases, which are currently not covered by the MOSD or other organizations, preventing them from becoming high-risk cases. In addition, the project will strengthen the capacity of CBOs to provide community-based preventative CP and PSS interventions for children and families. DCIP will also closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, for greater accountability on IHL and IHRL by duty bearers.War Child and partners will also support urgent efforts required to scale up current services to prevent GBV and support its survivors. PSCCW and PCC will use their specialized expertise to provide an integrated package of services to survivors of GBV and awareness raising activities within communities on availability of services and referrals, and PCC will provide MHPSS services and telecounselling for children and families.

2 - Activities

HRP Objective 1: Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations HRP Standard Activity: Monitoring and documentation of IHL and IHRL violations and related trends. Capitalizing on their cohesive network of sources including local media, health workers, CBOs, and community leaders and members, DCIP field workers will closely monitor, document, and rapidly report grave violations against children, including child killings and injuries; settler violence; and recruitment. HRP Standard Activity 1 CHILD PROTECTION: Provision of emergency child protection responses, including case management, psychosocial support and programmes: (1) Case management services for 120 boys and girls. WCH community caseworkers in Area C will provide basic counseling, psychosocial support and individual case management for low to medium risk cases, and refer high-risk cases to the CPN. Detected children will receive integrated quality services, through appropriate safe referral to specialized mental health services provided by PCC under this project and external mental health and other service providers, in coordination with CPWG and the CPN at governorate level; (2) Protection groups for 300 children focusing on child rights, self-protection, and self-affirmation, and increasing awareness of Covid19 and safety tips to protect themselves and others from infection; (3) Tele-counselling sessions for 40 boys and girls who demonstrate indicators of psychological distress and need for clinical support (PCC); (4) Structured community based PSS sessions using WCH’s TeamUP methodology for 255 children. HRP Standard Activity 4: CHILD PROTECTION: capacity building for non-child protection professionals: (1) WCH will capitalize on its internal expertise to build the capacity of 10 PSCCW social workers and 14 CBOs in Area C on child protection case management. (2) PCC will train 10 CBO counselors on their community-based Child Protection methodology, and on identification and referral of children at risk. (3) WCH will train PSCCW to deliver quality structured PSS activities for children using the TeamUp methodology.HRP Standard Activity 2: CHILD PROTECTION AND MHPSS: Child Protection workers are trained on the guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support: Training by PCC for 10 CBO counselors and 5 PSCCW counselors in remote Psychological First Aid (PFA). HRP Standard Activity 3: CHILD PROTECTION: Awareness raising for vulnerable communities on CP issues: WCH, PCC and PSCCW, through 23 CBOs, will disseminate information on CP and MHPSS service provision and availability, ensuring CP/GBV mapping and directories are up to date. PCC will produce 2 publications and 3 CP animation videos for children and their parents on child protection, and will co-create episodes with children on child protection and rights. HRP Standard Activity MHPSS: Provision of MHPSS for adults and youth: (1) Positive Discipline in Everyday Parenting training for 300 parents of children affected by crisis. PCC will complement the PDEP sessions with WCH’S Caregivers Support intervention, which has been adapted to remote modality and includes further stress-release tips for caregivers. (2) PCC counselors will provide debriefing sessions to 100 families affected by violence or are under C-19 quarantine to help them deal with stress and possible stigmatization. (3) PCC will provide individual telecounseling and phone counseling to adults and youth who demonstrate indicators of psychological distress including as a result of GBV risks. HRP Standard Activity: GBV: Mental health and psychosocial services for GBV survivors (1) Group counselling for 255 GBV survivors. The groups will include 10% of women and girls with disability. Should access continue to be restricted due to Covid19, PSCCW will deliver these sessions through mobile teams thus ensuring continuity of service provisions throughout the project.HRP Standard Activity: GBV: Legal aid (1) PSCCW will provide 50 GBV survivors with legal aid before courts, including 5% of women with disability.Standard Activity : GBV: Awareness raising for persons at risk of GBV: PSCCW will implement 28 awareness raising workshops on women’s rights as stated in national and international legislative frameworks and how to access legal and protection services, and PSEA reaching 255 women and girls at risk of GBV. Standard Activity : GBV: Economic enablement: (1) PSCCW will provide 101 women (10% women with disabilities), with in-job training and vocational training to increase employment opportunities and enter the job market. Selection criteria will include: young women head of households, GBV survivor, living in marginalized areas, lack of income generation/livelihood. Standard Activity : GBV: Dignity kits: Distribution of 200 Dignity kits designed in partnership with affected communities to ensure the most appropriate items are included. Standard GBV: Capacity building for GBV service providers (1) PCC will train 8 PSCCW counsellors on MHPSS techniques for GBV survivors, focusing on identifying quick solutions for the client’s problems, short-term client-centered therapy and mental status examination for survival of GBV. These trainings will help PSCCW to expand its capacity in specialized MHPSS support for GBV survivors. HRP Activity Protection - Self-care for service providers: PCC will provide monthly professional supervision for 10 CBO counselors working with children and parents, 8 PSCCW counselors and 3 WCH case managers, including training on self care and stress management. PSEA Training: All project staff will be trained in PSEA by War Child's PSEA trainers.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

War Child considers the specific needs and vulnerabilities of boys and girls of varying ages, as well of men and women, recognizing their roles in the communities, while trying to empower those who tend to be left out of power. War Child aims at being accountable to the children and families of this project and has taken a systematic approach to ensure all its interventions do not harm. Practical applications of the Do No Harm in the proposed project will encompass: • Identifying vulnerable groups across communities to foster social stability, avoid exacerbating tensions within the community and promote the equitable distribution of services across identity groups;• Reaching out and including vulnerable children within War Child intervention as per locally contextualized vulnerability criteria;• Analyzing existing community power dynamics to ensure that the needs of individuals and groups at risk of marginalization are voiced (also through the feedback mechanism);• Do No Harm principles included as a component of child protection and child safeguarding competency development of partners and communities;• A strong focus and commitment on child safeguarding and confidentiality in all interventions• With regards to case management services, case workers consult with the child and caregiver as to where they feel most comfortable to receive the service – for some they prefer in their homes, for others they prefer in the case worker’s office• Timely referral to specialized referrals to MHPSS, CP, and GBV professionals

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure meaningful access and participation, vulnerable children and caregivers (including people with disabilities) will be identified through outreach (including home visits), awareness raising sessions in the community, structured PSS programs, then referred to case management for assessment. The case workers are based in the community where we work, making it easier to identify vulnerable children and caregivers. Following a comprehensive assessment, case workers will refer children who are under 18 years old and their caregivers to the needed internal services or external services. Access to external MHPSS services in the targeted communities are limited, therefore requiring children and their caregivers to travel to the nearest city which may be several hours away. As the travel and transportation costs may at times be a barrier to access, War Child in these instances will provide transportation support and case workers will accompany the children (including children with disabilities) to the specialized services to ensure access to care. In addition, given the overall and high need of children and families affected by conflict- related violence, selection of participants in community-based PSS activities and case management services will be based on specific vulnerability criteria (such as being acutely affected by or at high-risk of conflict- related violence, family or community violence/abuse/neglect, disabilities, child labor, COVID-19,etc), which will also be well publicized through community meetings.PSCCW will guarantee outreach to women and girls with disability in their GBV activities. CBOs and community facilitators will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys, including with disabilities, to tailor the community-based PSS and CP interventions specifically to their needs. Also recognizing that the participation of children with disabilities can be a challenge due to social-cultural norms, the project will actively identify person with disabilities through awareness raising in the community and coordinating with the CPN network and Protection Cluster partners such as the PRCS and YMCA who provide services for children with disabilities in the targeted areas if needed.CP Case workers will also coordinate with the CPN officers in their governorates who oversee protection cases for children with disabilities to ensure that children with disabilities are referred to relevant PSS activities as needed and coordination will be closely done with the available services provided by Protection Cluster actors such as the YMCA and the PRCS for children with disabilities. The non- specialized activities are provided by community members themselves, trained and supported as part of the project. Activities and interventions will be further refined and adapted to meet the specific needs of the communities through engagement of CBOs and community facilitators who will regularly engage women, men, girls and boys (including persons with disabilities) in defining their needs and tailoring the community-based PSS interventions. Case workers and community facilitators will also receive an orientation training by War Child staff on inclusion principles in order to skillfully adapt PSS interventions to ensure inclusivity, including for children with disabilities.
