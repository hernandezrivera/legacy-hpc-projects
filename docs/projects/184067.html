Project Summary

Sudan continues to face a number of shocks, complex socio-economic crises and complex transitional political challenges that threaten the lives and livelihoods of the population. A combination of political and economic crisis, conflict, natural disasters and COVID-19 have left millions of people vulnerable, at risk of food insecurity, malnutrition, disease, morbidity and therefore requiring urgent humanitarian support to save lives, protect assets and livelihoods. According to the Humanitarian Needs of Overview (HNO), at least some 9.8 Million people will need food assistance within the coming year.
This project will respond to critical life sustaining, resilience and early recovery needs by restoring food and livelihood security of over 100,000 most vulnerable populations including IDPs, returnees, vulnerable host community members and impacting other groups of concern including refugees. The overall objective of the project is to restore food and livelihood security of both displaced population and vulnerable host communities across 11 States in Sudan.
It will focus on agricultural support, livestock Support, livelihood cash and voucher support using Cash for Work modality for the restoration of critical community-based and productive assets whilst providing temporary and urgent labor-intensive employment opportunities. It will also support the provision of livelihood and income generation kits as well as vocational, skills and entrepreneurship training.
UNDP will work in close partnership with FAO, using area-based approach to deliver assistance by harnessing the combined collaborative technical advantage and the wide reach of the two organizations across Sudan.

1 - Needs

According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) 2021, some 9.8 Million people will need food and livelihood security support during 2022. This is being driven by combination of factors - political situation, macro-economic instability, hyperinflation, localized conflicts and violence with attendant displacement and high food. These have further been compounded by the lingering effects of the COVID-19 pandemic and recent floods that have seriously and disproportionately affected the most vulnerable groups. The Government removed subsidies on fuel and wheat with a knock-on effect on the prices of food and non-food items. Rising prices are now well beyond the purchasing power of poor households. According to the UN SDG 2021 report, almost 79% of Sudanese live below the poverty line, and 28 percent of total households are using negative food-based coping strategies. Up to 51% of households have resorted to harmful coping strategies, focusing on immediate food needs and depleting their assets. Fuel price increase has affected the cost of farming activities due to the high cost of mechanical and human labour and agricultural inputs. In addition, depreciation of the currency, and the prevailing hyper-inflation have resulted in high food and transport costs, as well as shortage and high prices of medicine, energy, and imported food and non-food items. National inflation rose around 44 points from May to July (422.78 percent) due to the fiscal deficits, huge monetization, which has been exacerbated by shortages of food, medicine, and fuel. While the macro-economic and fiscal reforms had produced their first results lowering the inflation rate to 367.5 per cent in September, the current political situation may reverse this progress and further heightening social and macro-economic grievances and vulnerabilities. Flooding has dealt a blow on the food security of the vulnerable with grim effect on small-scale farmers. According to OCHA, as of August 22, over 61,800 people were affected, with around 12,000 damaged or destroyed homes, along with damage or destruction to public infrastructures and farmland. Over 70 sq km of land was flooded; affecting agricultural activities likely to deprive the poor of their harvest. Inter-communal conflict incidents significantly increased across Sudan, particularly in Darfur, with the start of the rainy season and cultivation in July and August. The increased conflict incidents have equally increased displacement and resulted in the loss of lives and livelihood assets and disruptions to the agricultural season. Between June and August 2021, tribal conflicts in West Darfur, North Darfur, South Darfur, and White Nile State have displaced over 43,000 people. Most IDPs are located in surrounding villages or existing IDP camps in nearby main towns relying on humanitarian aid. Apart from floods, the impact of COVID-19 and the pandemic measures continue to affect the socio-economic prospects of the populations; increasing humanitarian needs.Sudan is also enduring the impact of the influx of Ethiopian refugees into already vulnerable communities. Since November 2020, over 70,000 Ethiopian refugees have arrived in Sudan, fleeing the Tigray conflict. This influx is creating tensions and putting pressures on the already vulnerable communities.The 25 October military takeover led to the arrest and detention the Prime Minister Abdalla and some of senior government figures; the dissolution of the Sovereignty Council and a state of emergency put in place. This new political development will no doubt have serious socio-economic consequences for the country and likely to further increase humanitarian needs. The incident will disrupt and undo the momentum and the various reforms in place to revamp the economy, maintain peace and affect donor support to the country.

2 - Activities/Outputs

This project contributes to the following strategic objectives of the of Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector. 1. FSL:SO2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives 2. FSL:SO3: Improve communities' capacity to sustain household livelihoods through the rehabilitation/ building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services. It will focus on the following components and activities: 1. Agricultural Support (Seeds, Tools): UNDP will work in partnership with FAO and the Ministry of Production in 11 States to provide emergency Agriculture Support to 25,000 vulnerable households in restoring agriculture production by ensuring they have access to high quality of locally adapted seeds and improved tools on timely basis. This will also include irrigation kits, gardening kits etc. to enable them to restore and stabilize their food production capacities. UNDP will initially work with FAO to do seed distribution using a combination of direct distribution, seed vouchers and seed fairs. UNDP will later initiate local seed multiplication initiatives to restore the local seed security systems working with the Agriculture Research Stations in the various States. Working with FAO, the Ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources, UNDP will provide community-based extension services benefiting over 25,000 households. 2. Livestock Support (Distribution, Treatments) UNDP will work with FAO to support livestock restocking to the 10,000 most vulnerable households and to ensure the improved livestock health. UNDP will focus on identifying, procuring and distribution of high quality breeds whilst FAO will work in partnership to provide vaccination and treatment services including the establishment of emergency Community-based livestock support. FAO will establish and train a network of Community-based Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) and equip them to provide animal health services to communities. 3. Livelihood Cash/Voucher Support: The project will establish/rehabilitate community productive infrastructure using Cash for Work benefiting over 40,000 people creating emergency employment. These may include infrastructure affected by conflict and recent floods such as markets; rural roads; dams, irrigation canals and embankments; water yards, community centers etc. They may also include community and group farms; providing essential labor for vulnerable households to enable them to cultivate in the next farming season. UNDP will contribute financially and materially to the intervention. Dubbed ‘’Community Stabilization brigade System’’ a set of machinery and heavy equipment will be provided by UNDP to be managed by a private sector entity who will provide skilled labor. Unskilled labor will then be provided by community members through temporal labor-intensive employment arrangements. 4. Livelihood Kits Support UNDP will provide livelihoods kits to support emergency employment and income generation schemes to enable affected households restore their income-generation capacities. This will include; provision of agricultural machinery such as tractors, solar pumps etc. that may increase production, as well as low-cost appropriate that may reduce post-harvest losses and maximize farm incomes through value addition and market access. It will also involve the provision of small business kits in cash and in-kind to enable communities recover enterprises and livelihoods. 5. Vocational Training: UNDP will work closely with UNIDO and the State Vocational Institutes to provide emergency vocational, skills and entrepreneurship training for 5000 vulnerable and crises-affected youth including refugees and nomadic groups. This includes skills training, entrepreneurship training and the provision of start-up kits to all trainees to enable them restart income generation and employment creation.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This Project will mainstream environmental protection in all activities to ensure that production and utilization of food and the pathways to household livelihood restoration have no impact on the environment. UNDP will promote climate-smart and self-restoring agricultural methods that has minimum impact on the environment through provision of solar pumps and high quality extension services and farmer field schools. UNDP will also apply a conflict sensitive approach to planning and implementing the project, ensuring the expansion of agriculture production and other livelihood opportunities do not create and or exacerbate existing natural resource-based conflicts.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention will mainstream protection concerns in food security and livelihood activities in order to address, mitigate and reduce risk to particular vulnerable groups especially women. UNDP will prioritize safety, dignity of vulnerable groups and avoid causing harm. UNDP will also ensure a transparent selection criteria and participatory processes, promote equitable and impartial access for all groups, enhance accountability. UNDP will also specially target and promote the participation and empowerment of the most vulnerable groups especially the Youth and women and take steps to address structural obstacles to their meaningful participation.
