Project Summary

UNICEF in close coordination with Ministry of Health, National Center for Diseases Control, WHO, UNFPA, IOM and UNHCR will provide essential and lifesaving health and nutrition services with focus on vulnerable children and their families in undeserved and remote areas of the country and in the detention centers. The target population include children under five children especially newborns, Pregnant and Lactating women. Due to protracted emergency in Libya most of the population has limited access to functioning public services and utilities exposing vulnerable groups such as mothers, newborns and children to preventable and curable disease. The health care delivery network and infrastructure in most of the districts is unable to provide needed health services to the population due to major service gaps and inaccessibility. According to SARA 2017 report the average general service readiness based on amenities, equipment, and medicine is 69 percent in hospitals and 37 percent in PHC centers leaving 31 percent of the health facilities without essential health services. The same report identified lack of essential medicines and supplies in health facilities across Libya with high risk of communicable diseases (polio, measles and others). The immunization supply chain system has dramatically suffered from the conflict and severe shortage of vaccines have been reported at the national level. In July 2018, The Ministry of Health declared a measles outbreak as a result of the increasing number of cases reaching a total of 702 (Report October 2018) with two deaths already reported. The national health authorities could not mobilize local resources to carry out an outbreak response due to the political fragmentation, ongoing conflict, insecurity, and weaken health system. UNICEF and WHO are supporting National Centre for Diseases Control/ministry of health to undertake a nationwide measles outbreak response immunization. In addition, the highly mobile population, as well as Libya being a transit country for a large number of migrants from the African continent, the risks for a broader outbreak for number of vaccine preventable and communicable diseases are high. This long and large-scale measles outbreak with 40 percent of suspected children without vaccination reflects very low routine immunization coverage. More than 50% of the under five children die within the first 28 days of life, most of these deaths are prevented through provision of Integrated Management of Newborn and Childhood Illnesses. In the given situation there is urgent need for strengthening health system for proving preventive and curative lifesaving and essential services to most vulnerable population in the country including all Detentions centers. This project is expected to contribute significantly in prevention of maternal, neonatal and child morbidity and mortality.

Needs

There are huge gaps in human and institutional capacity and quality related to child (including Newborn) health and nutrition services; a) Provision of essential medicines and equipment to health facilities in low performing and under-served areas. b) provision of essential newborn services to reduce neonatal mortality c) scaling up Integrated management of childhood Illnesses to prevent and treat childhood diseases d) screening and treatment of children with Severe Acute malnutrition and micro nutrient deficiencies.

Activities

1. Provision of essential medicines and equipment (resuscitator, oxygen concentrators, incubators)-enabling health facilities to provide responsive lifesaving health care) 2. Provision of cold chain equipment (Ice-line refrigerator, solar refrigerators, cold boxes, quality monitoring devices) to ensure quality of vaccine. 3. Training of staff of essential Newborn care and Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses to ensure care per global standards 4. Integration and scaling up Infant and Young Child Feeding practices, identification, treatment and prevention of malnutrition with IMCI strategy. 5. Evidence generation on nutrition status of children and women through SMART Nutrition Survey 6. Provision of tools and computer for data collection and also training of staff to ensure data quality

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Access, safety and dignity are basic considerations, the project activities incorporate a comprehensive social mobilization plan to ensure that communities are active participants in the project instead of being passive recipients of the services. The communities need and priorities are reflected to addressed through the planned interventions. A feedback mechanism will be established and functional to constantly review comments from the communities to improve service provision and management. Health facility management communities will be established to ensure quality and utilization of the services. Exit interview will be conducted to assess parents/client satisfaction and feedback on the services. The basic objective is to ensure that communities actively participate, accept and demand for quality and continued services. A monitoring framework will be in place to monitored, assess and evaluate the program implementation, quality and effectiveness and overall impact on the child survival and development. This Party Monitoring is also envisaged the monitoring plan to interact with beneficiaries. Active community participation, exit interview in health facilities, third party monitors and undertaking e interviews with the affected population to assess their participation and satisfaction will be key areas.
