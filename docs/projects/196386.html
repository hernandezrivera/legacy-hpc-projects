Project Summary

To strengthen resilience of the Rohingya /FDMN population, WFP will be supporting most vulnerable peoples through engaging participatory DRR activities which are able to meet basic food support and part of emergency preparedness. Through this project WFP will cover 33 camps including 02 Register camps targetting around 79,537 beneficiaries.
WFP cash for work activities will engage at least 25% women and 5% PwD within safeguards and a do no harm approach. The proposed projects will consider Structural interventions (Access, drainage, slope stabilization etc), Reforestation/Plantation, Waste Management/Drain Cleaning, Emergency Response/Cash for Workers/Porters.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WFP incorporate protection principles in designing & developing program interventions to ensure all activities are targeted to most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote & protect the rights of relocated refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. WFP is targeting crisis affected people including women, men, boys, girls, elderly people, person with disability as well as people with special need. WFP carried out various assessments and analysis, i.e., stakeholder analysis, gender analysis, vulnerability assessment, protection assessment and assessment of social and economic factor. WFP provides support through inter agency protection cluster and UNHCR to provide faster service to the affected population. WFP has followed PSEA guideline and inter agency referral pathway. Sectoral recommendations regarding protection are adopted by WFP to enable a safe, secured services.

3.2 - Gender

WFP has three key objectives to address the gender inequalities that continue to exacerbate hunger and nutrition around the globe: 1. Achieve equitable access to and control over food security and nutrition; 2. Address the root causes of gender inequalities that affect food security and nutrition; 3. Advance the economic empowerment of women and girls in food security and nutrition. To achieve these objectives, WFP addresses gender equality and women’s empowerment through a twin-track approach. This project will encourage the participation of women in all. Women engaged in several activities like plant watering, curing, carrying light construction materials, brick chip making, and delivery of portable water to construction sites.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Recognizing that children are often more vulnerable to experiencing protection risks, and often lack the means and opportunities to seek support when needed, WFP is committed to integrating protection strategies and actions into its programmes to reduce exposure to risk and to support a more protective environment where children can grow and learn. To mainstream child protection into WFP key activities, WFP has clear guideline and never engages child labor in its activities. WFP has mitigation measure to deal with child safeguarding issues which provides related orientation sessions for its staff and cooperating partners along with sharing simplified awareness raising materials. Child protection focal points remains present during the distribution of entitlements to prevent possible safeguard-related issues. Assistive and flexible nutrition services are provided to both parents with disabilities and children with disabilities.

3.4 - Diversity

WFP works to streamline gender, disability inclusion, elderly and other diversified people (including LGBTQI if found) to support the participation of all beneficiaries across its interventions, and to ensure their rights and safety from the diversity perspective. Trainings and capacity building exercises are conducted with WFP staff and cooperating partners on basic concepts, frameworks, and programmatic applications of diversity approaches. A number of information and communication materials are in use to ensure adequate awareness raising of the frontline workers, key staffs and ensure meaningful engagement of the diversified beneficiaries including LGBTQI. Every distribution site has segregated waiting areas, toilet facilities, and help desks managed by at least one female staff member/volunteer. At nutrition sites, priority is given to pregnant and nursing women with disabilities. Person with disabilities will be identified by using the WGSSQ tools.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP is committed to ensure accountability to affected populations (AAP) through adoption of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Commitments to AAP (C-AAP). WFP exercise active processes and actions that meaningfully place affected populations at the center of humanitarian response with the aim of aiding that meets people’s needs, elevates existing capacities and dignity, and helps strengthen long-term resilience. WFP’s priorities and approach to AAP is outlined in the Protection and Accountability Policy (2020), elaborated in the Community Engagement for Accountability Strategy (2021 – 2026) and mutually reinforces the Gender Policy (2022) and the Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020). WFP has established a Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) which includes a Hotline, help desk, and complaint boxes, for receiving feedback and complaints from the targeted populations. WFP do capacity development training for partner to enhance understanding on protection and AAP.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project has DRR elements and environmental issues, and climate change mitigation measures. There are reforestation activities including land stabilization in the whole camp to mitigate the impact of the heat wave, landslides, siltation, and desertification. It will create a positive impact on forests and wildlife, ecosystems, and natural components (air, soil, and water) of the environment. Drainage cleaning activity will help to manage the healthy watershed.
