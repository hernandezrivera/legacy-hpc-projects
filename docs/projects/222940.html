Project Summary

On 10 September, the Alau Dam started to fracture and eventually burst its banks, triggering flash floods that inundated, submerged and devastated low-lying communities in the state. Damaged septic tanks and flooded graveyards also spurred fears of a rapid spread of infectious diseases worsening the already existing hardship caused by crisis in the past decade.
The proposed project is essential in the aftermath of floods, focusing on sanitation and hygiene to protect public health and promote recovery. By ensuring access to clean water and proper sanitation facilities, this initiative will help communities rebuild and thrive in crisis and flood situation. The primary goal of this proposed WASH project is to provide timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services for affected populations especially the internally displaced persons (IDPs) who are resident in camps and host communities.
Ensuring that communities have reliable access to safe drinking water, providing adequate sanitation facilities to reduce the risk of waterborne diseases, educating the Mala Sheriff and Isa Gamai communities in Jere and Rann in Kalabalge LGA about hygiene practices to prevent illness and promote health, building and maintaining water supply systems, sanitation facilities, and hygiene stations, involving local communities in planning and implementation to ensure sustainability and relevance, training local authorities and community members to manage and maintain WASH facilities effectively, reducing the incidence of waterborne diseases and improving overall community health, enhancing productivity by reducing time spent collecting water and improving health outcomes. improving handwashing practice through awareness creation and provision of handwashing stations, especially among women, girls and other vulnerable groups, by providing safe sanitation facilities in communities, focused on providing technical assistance to local governments and developing local community standards for WASH infrastructure, aimed at improving WASH services to 4000 individuals in IDP community neighbourhoods, enhancing sanitation and hygiene services. For Care First Community Initiative, this WASH project is vital for creating resilient communities and ensuring that target vulnerable persons have access to basic Water Sanitation and Hygiene services in the above mentioned locations.

1 - Needs

Poor access to improved water sanitation in amongst the displaced persons in Jere, Kalabalge and Maiduguri Metropolitan Council remains a major contributing factor to high morbidity and mortality rates among children and adults. As a result of the recent flood, the use of contaminated drinking water and poor sanitation conditions result in increased vulnerability to water-borne diseases, including diarrhoeas which leads to death. 73% of diarrhoea and enteric diseases is associated with poor access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene, and is disproportionately borne by poorer children contribute to absenteeism in school and malnutrition. Only 26.5% of the displaced population use improved drinking water sources and sanitation facilities and 23.5% defecate in the open. As a result of ongoing protracted conflict causing unpredictable and continuous large-scale of displacement, recurrent large-scale cholera due to flooding, north-east Nigeria continues to experience multifaceted humanitarian crisis in the water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) sector. The main drivers of vulnerabilities related to WASH are closely linked to the flood and conflict-induced recurrent population displacement, and are also a result of pre-existing low level of WASH services in the Northeast. An estimated 2.8 million people across northeast Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained and equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation. According to an IOM report, 1.02 million people receive less than 15 litters of safe clean water per person per day while about 1.06 million people have intermittent water access with over 500 meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queuing time at water points. Care First Community Initiative will ensure the delivery of appropriate WASH intervention by both direct provision of water, sanitation, and hygiene promotion and through coordination with local and international partners. Through coordination with the Health Sector Working Group in Maiduguri, we will align our activities and the areas of interventions and Sector-specific objectives to reduce gaps in services and avoid service duplication while focusing on nutrition, water, sanitation and hygiene.

2 - Activities

Prior to the activity start, Care First Community Initiative will ensure local community leaders, elders, women, men, different group representatives and Ward administrators are actively involved in the site selection for rehabilitation work. Water quality will be given due attention with regular monitoring at source and distribution points. The level of residual chlorines (on average 0.5mg/l at drop-off points and 0.2mg/l at HH level) will be tested regularly using the pool tester. To break the potential of communicable disease outbreak due to unsanitary conditions, Care First Community will construct 3 blocks of latrines (containing 4 drop holes each) in each location of Jere, MMC and Kalabalge. Prior to the construction activity, CFCI will consult local community on the design and location of latrine facilities. Each block of the sanitation facility will have four stances where one of them shall be accessible for the people with disability. Disability inclusion, GBV and other protection risk mitigations will be considered during the planning, site-selection and construction phases of the sanitation facility. The sanitation facilities will be sex segregated and ensure safety and privacy of users. The latrines shall be constructed having cleanable slab and have internal locks. Based on the National WASH Cluster and SPHERE Minimum Standard, each stance of the toilet will be for 30 individuals use. Care First Community Initiative will procure and provide latrine cleaning kits to the latrine attendants to ensure the cleanliness of the facility. For this, CFCI will establish a local voluntary committee who will be facilitating the cleaning of communal latrines. 40 sets of cleaning kits will be procured and provided for this committees to facilitate the proper cleaning of the sanitation facilities. The latrine cleaning kit will contain; plastic brooms, different detergents for cleaning, buckets, jerricans, pairs of heavy-duty gloves, aprons, pairs of gumboots, mask and laundry soaps. Hygiene Promoters will monitor and supervise operations. When the latrines get full, they will be safely decommissioned so that it will not cause any further potential risk for the environment. The hygiene promotion interventions will focus to improve the bad behaviours associated with poor knowledge, attitude and practice among the targeted flood affected communities.CFCI will engage in rehabilitating 2 damaged water systems in each LGA which will provide drinking water, we will provide home water-treatment capabilities through sensitization and trainings, implementing rainwater harvesting systems, and providing filters, monitoring water quality, conducting initial testing, sampling and analyzing water quality, educating IDPs on the importance of water hygiene, and engaging them in the care and maintenance of water infrastructure.Three days training will be organized for the deployed HPs and the training will focus on promoting key positive behaviours like hand washing with soap at critical times, properly using latrines and sanitation facilities, and safe water storage. The trained hygiene promoters will conduct awareness creation, campaigning and community mobilization among flood affected people.Groups with a specific need that will be prioritized for assistance include people with disability, women and girls at risk, including unaccompanied and separated children, persons with serious health needs, persons with special legal or physical protection needs, single women, women-headed households. Psychological first aid, individual and group counselling, peer-support group and facilitate referral to specialized mental health care to the IDPs, including victims or potential victims of trafficking and at-risk groups. The above mentioned activities will target a total number of 4000 persons between January to December 2025.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Care First Community Initiative will set up complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM), a system that receives, processes and responds to concerns from the community on humanitarian services, assistance or behaviour. This will help us to correct mistakes and learn more about our work. In consultation with the camp leadership, we will set up the following medium; In person, Suggestion boxes, Voice recorders, Hotlines or toll-free lines, Community consultations, Complaint committees, Locked boxes, Direct complaints to project staff. To be sure these mediums are functional, we will do the following; • Make it accessible: Ensure that the mediums are accessible to everyone, including women, minorities, and people with disabilities. • Be culturally and politically appropriate: Consider the cultural and political context when setting up the CFM. • Be confidential: Ensure that complaints and feedback are kept confidential and that personal information is not shared. • Respond promptly: Respond to valid complaints and feedback in a timely manner. • Document the process: Document the investigation process, including evidence gathering, interviews, and findings. • Keep complainants informed: Keep complainants informed throughout the process. • Build on existing systems: If possible, build the CFM on existing local systems. • Inform the community: Fully inform the community about the CFM, its purpose, and how to use it.
