Project Summary

"The project aims to reduce the morbidity and mortality from COVID-19 through rapid detection, diagnosis, and prevention of the spread of the novel coronavirus (SARS-CoV-2), caring for those affected and breaking the chain of human-to-human transmission.
IOM will establish 4 SARI isolation and treatment facilities with the total bed capacity of 250 with the provision of isolation and treatment services to forecasted 6,000 cases in 9 months. Initially, existing 4 PHCs will be upgraded for limited scale isolations (total 11 bed capacity) to enable the isolation and referral of initial suspected cases and while infrastructural development for the larger facilities will continue. Existing health posts will be upgraded for appropriate patient screening and triage. 100 mid-term shelters at camp 20E will be repurposed for quarantine of contacts of COVID-19 cases during low incidence phase with the view to early containment of the outbreak. In the high incidence phase, the facility will be converted into a 200 bed isolation facility for mild cases. Additionally, IOM will support government health facilities in Ukhiya, Teknaf, Cox's Bazar Sadar and when needed in broader Cox's Bazar (including Maheshkhali, Ramu and Chakaria) with structural support, human resource and logistics for case management and infection prevention and control.
To establish a prompt and effective referral system for COVID-19 cases and contacts, IOM will lead a dispatch and referral unit along with designate ambulances and vehicles. Three ambulance disinfection points will be established for safe cleaning and decontamination of ambulances carrying COVID-19 cases. IOM will also support effective surveillance through participation of its skilled staff in rapid investigation teams and community health workers in contact tracing.
In parallel, IOM will undertake the procurement of necessary items, including prepositioning of commodities, PPE and medicines for patient management at dedicated isolation facilities, Rapid Investigation Teams and dispatch and referral unit including ambulances. The operation of the four Isolation and Treatment Centers (ITC) will require continous supply chain management for appropriate medical and other logistic supplies as well as the recruitment of large number of additional medical and support personnel as per WHO guidlines. Ensuring that all personal have the required amount of PPE to minimize risks of contamination and that ITCs are equipped with the necessary equipment and medicines will be a key focus of our procurement and supply chain teams. Additional human resources, including medical officers, nurses and cleaners will be engaged to strengthen IOM existing medical teams.
IOM will set-up mini laboratories in the isolation and treatment centers for common laboratory investigations including X-ray with portable machines. Laboratory testing facility for novel corona virus, when feasible, will be established at IOM ITCs. IOM considers using GeneXpert testing for COVID 19 in the proposed laboratories.
To ensure Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) procedures, protocols and systems will be established and monitored for effective response to COVID-19. Notably IOM will subject all supported 35 existing health facilities and the four ITCs to the IOM IPC Checklist to monitor medical team compliance with IPC measures. Through the network of Community Health workers of the implementing partners, IOM will ensure health risk communication and community engagement.
During preparedness mode, MHPSS activities will focus on increasing the capacity of IOM team members including volunteers to deliver basic psychosocial support (e.g. psychological first aid (PFA), basic counselling skills, and stress management skills)

Objectives

1. Reduce to the extent possible the morbidity, mortality and spread of COVID-19 by rapid identification and isolation of all cases, including most vulnerable providing them with appropriate care, and tracing, quarantining, and supporting all contacts. 2. Prevent transmission and amplification of COVID-19, by enhancing infection prevention and control in community and health care settings and effectively communicate critical risks (counter misinformation) to men, women, boys and girls. 3. Provide technical leadership, coordination, collaboration and information management support to all partners responding to the COVID-19 pandemic. 4. Ensure continuity of equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with a special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases while adapting services to the current context.

Needs

"Since the declration of COVID-19 pandemic by WHO on March 11, 2020 as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC), as of 28 April 2020, more than 2,995,758 confirmed cases and 204,978 deaths have been reported globally across 185 countries and territories that signifies it's speed of spreading, case fatlity rate and potentials of causing devastating impact throughout the globe. New cases and countries are emerging on a daily basis, and the situation is far from stabilizing. Many countries have seen their health systems overwhelmed, while others remain in a state of preparedness. The pandemic of COVID-19 poses an alarming threat for Bangladesh as well. Like many other countries, Bangladesh is experiencing growing number of infections through community transmission and the potetial threat of wider transmission is very high especially considering its massive density of population, high population mobility, lower socioneconomic and educational status and inequitable healthcare systems though, the international and internal movement of populations have been restricted significantly since 25th of March 2020. As of 28 April 2020, there are 7,103 confirmed cases COVID-19 and 163 deaths in Bangladesh. In Cox’s Bazar district 22 confirmed cases have been reported so far among the host communities and the number is in growing trend. There are a great number of risks and threats to this most vulnerable District in Bangladesh that hosts the Refugee populations from Myanmar in the camps in Ukhia and Teknaf upazilas. Cox Bazar hosts today around 859,000 Rohingya refugees, in an area of approximately 24 square kilometers, with an uncertain health profile. Furthermore, limited resources and infrastructures have the potential to exacerbate the overwhelming spread of the virus in a very short period within these communities. The overall health service delivery system of Cox’s Bazar is already strained to meet the current demand of the community, and it is insufficient in terms of infrastructure, human resources and supplies. The health facilities are not prepared for managing suspected and confirmed COVID-19 cases, which further increases the risks of overwhelming health systems early in the pandemic, leaving patients in severe or critical condition from COVID-19 in addition to other conditions without access to adequate health care facilities. Given the insufficient availability of infection prevention and control measures (both administrative and engineering), waste management and personal protection equipment—there is also an increased risk of hospital acquired transmission and exposure of health care workers. An IOM Cox’s Bazar rapid analysis has identified the following camp level threats and challenges: - Strained human resources, lack of infrastructure, medicine and commodities for rapid detection, isolation, treatment and referral of suspected and confirmed patients with COVID 19;- Inadequate isolation and management capacity may lead to increased disease transmission and hospital acquired infections- Insufficient awareness on COVID-19 among the community may lead to increased disease transmission as well as panicked behavior;- Increased risks of transmission of COVID-19 among the staff, particularly health facility staff, CHWs, hygiene promotion and cleaning volunteers and CwC / SMSD staff & volunteers;- Stigmatization of the disease, and by extension, those who are suspected to be sick;- Stigmatization of the humanitarian front-line workers, notably the ones working within health facilities. - Increased stressors from fear, panic, mobility limitation, diminished self-reliance opportunities, and so on because of COVID 19 may manifest into incidents of violence, including gender-based violence, child abuse and neglect, and exploitation; theft and damage to personal items, quarreling and fighting.

Output 4.1: Continuation of Primary Health Care services through the existing facilities and community health workers network. Output 4.2. MHPSS service delivery continuously offered to beneficiaries including alternative modes of support
