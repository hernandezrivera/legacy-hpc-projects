Project Summary

INTERSOS will provide lifesaving shelter and NFIs in response to specific needs of 40,000 IDPs and conflict affected populations assessed and verified to be in need. Deep field locations with recent displacements having populations in dire need will be the priority of INTERSOS in the distribution of life saving ES/NFI commodities. IDPs, returnees and conflict affected populations assessed and verified as most in need will be targeted in the counties of Nyirol, Pibor and Uror (Jonglei state), Tambura, Ezo, and Nzara, (Western Equatoria State), Leer, Koch, (Unity State) with mobile front line services.
INTERSOS will provide support to 10000 beneficiaries (protracted IDPs, vulnerable HC and returnees) in the counties of Bor South and Magwi through static response and CTP interventions. INTERSOS has gained significant experience in CTP interventions in South Sudan, piloting and implementing CTP programs in Torit, Magwi and Kapoeta South where a combined 1400 Households and more than 8,600 individuals have been served, and is currently implementing CTP activities through voucher and unconditional cash transfer in Magwi and Torit counties for a total of 1965 HHs.
INTERSOS will consider the specific needs, concerns and priorities of women and girls, men and boys of different age and with specific needs such as people with disability, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerabilities so incorporating protection principles during project implementation.
As a short-term intervention, the impact on the population and the contextual dynamics is sensitive and delicate balances are at play, for this reason the team will ensure that all accountability processes to the affected population are carefully followed. The intervention must be clearly defined and protection concerns taken into consideration so that distribution respects community dynamics, does no harm and is done in a dignified meaningful way. Contextual feedback mechanisms will be established in line with the distribution method and the location dynamics. Beneficiaries will be identified using approved South Sudan SNFIs cluster criteria of selection. In addition, INTERSOS will endeavor to give priority to child headed household, women, persons with disability, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the community who will benefit from Emergency Shelter and NFI distribution. Currently, the counties of Nyirol, Pibor, Uror, Leer, Koch, Tambura, Ezo, Nzaraa and Bor South need emergency shelter and NFIs assistance due to dire needs, where their fulfilment is crucial in saving lives; Magwi and Bor south are also in need of Emergency Shelter and NFIs assistance here the proposed project intends to deliver this assistance through CTP.
In Bor south INTERSOS will work closely with Protection and GBV partners.
This CTP intervention will enable most vulnerable groups such as unaccompanied elders, persons with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women and child headed families to receive Shelter/NFIs support as per their needs to restore their dignity and improve living standards. Project team will also comprise of Cash Transfer officer who will lead implementation and monitoring of cash Transfer programming activities in the proposed locations. INTERSOS proposes to use a mix of vouchers and unconditional cash transfer in order to meet the varying needs of the beneficiaries.
INTERSOS' CBI officer will be responsible for CTP interventions in Magwi and Bor South. and he may conduct, in coordination with the Cash Working Group and other partners market assessment in other location to explore the feasibility of CTP S/NFIs interventions.

Needs

The IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) (July 2018) highlighted that there is a potential of 922,181 individuals who had been displaced from their areas of origin to return. The DTM noted that the majority (83%) of these are reported to be abroad, namely in Uganda (56%), Sudan (17%), Kenya (7%), Ethiopia (0.6%) and other countries (2.4%). Areas that reported highest numbers of people who had been displaced and not returned include Kajo-Keji, Magwi, Lainya, Bor South,Juba and Twic East and Aweil North.Magwi county in Eastern Equatoria has also been reported to have immense needs for Shelter and NFI assistance. The INTERSOS assessment of June 2018 revealed that there were more than 7,000 individuals, most of whom are returnees from Uganda in need of S/NFI assistance.According to REACH Assessment of Hard-to-Reach Areas on S/NFIs (August 2018) in 40% of the IDPs settlements in Torit county IDPs are primarily living in temporary structures or in the open. INTERSOS is positioning herself to respond to the potential needs of these returnees in the areas of Magwi and Bor South.In addition, fighting has continued in many parts of South Sudan. For instance, in April and May 2018, there was heavy fighting in Namutina and Nagero in which forced many people to move into Tambura and surrounding areas. An estimated 18,500 people, mainly women were displaced into Tambura town and surrounding areas. These people are now in need of urgent need of humanitarian assistance including NFIs, emergency shelter, among other needs (OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin, July 2018). Much as the IDPs expressed desire to return home when fighting stops, before then, they are need of shelter and other items to preserve their life and dignity.Furthermore, areas of Motot and Pieri in Uror county have continued to experience huge influx of IDPs since January 2018. This was as a result of armed attacks leading to an influx of IDPs from the neighbouring Payams of Padiak and Yuai into the areas of Pieri and Motot. An INTERSOS needs assessment report of March 2018 revealed that there were approximately 13,529 IDPs in these locations, with projected influx as the areas of Yuai, Padiak, Waat, Pathai, Khorphulus remained highly volatile (INTERSOS, March 2018).Unity State have experienced in 2018 widespread insecurity and violence, and saw a notable increase in reports of new arrivals of IDPs, especially in Southern Unity, A May joint humanitarian assessment estimated that about 8,000 persons had been displaced to Leer town, Mer, Koch town, Mayendit town and Nyal. The population of the Temporary Protection Area in Leer increased from 575 in early April to 1,995 by mid-May. In the same period, more than 3,000 newly displaced persons arrived at the Bentiu PoC site (REACH, Situation Overview: Unity State).There remains a concern among humanitarians regarding needs in Nyirol, Pibor, Nagero, Tambura, Ezo, Nzara and Yambio as well as Magwi where IDPs and returnees are in dire need of life saving shelter and NFIs. Displacement trends have worsened in recent months, where approximately 350,000 individuals were reported to be fleeing from the Equatorias. Some of these have also started to return to the country, exuberating need for life saving emergency shelter and non-fooditems. (South Sudan Humanitarian needs Overview, 2018)

Activities

INTERSOS will provide access to emergency shelter and/or life-saving NFIs to 40,000 of people. Mobile interventions will target most-vulnerable IDPs, newly displaced and in secondary displacement populations. 30,000 beneficiaries will receive in kind emergency shelter and life saving NFIs in response to assessed and verified needs. In Magwi and Bor South INTERSOS will support 10,000 beneficiaries of most vulnerable returnees, host communities and IDPs with cash based interventions. INTERSOS will conduct four rapid monitoring missions (PDM) and four standard monitoring missions. Beneficiaries feedback and complaint suggestion mechanism will be in place while distribution of SNFI materials. This should be done in each location and activity. At each stage of response, INTERSOS team will be as much gender-balanced as possible, with at least one woman to be included. INTERSOS will ensure that at least 30% of ERT members will be women.INTERSOS will have static presence in Bor and Torit with two staff deployed as State Focal Point in Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States. INTERSOS has been serving as SNFIs State Focal Point for the Cluster in Jonglei State since June 2012 and has been the State Focal Point for Eastern Equatoria since 2016. Over the past five years, INTERSOS has conducted 57 distributions of NFIs and Emergency Shelter material, with a total beneficiary of almost 420,000 conflict affected IDPs throughout the country (mainly in Jonglei, but also in Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria and Western Equatoria areas of South Sudan). The above experience implies that INTERSOS has adequate response capacity in S/NFIs. INTERSOS has a deep understanding of Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria and western Equatoria contexts, with replicable good practices and lessons learnt on “how to work” in opposition and SPLA areas. As indicated above, INTERSOS is in a good position to mainstream gender and protection into NFI programs in Jonglei, Eastern Equatoria and Western Equatoria States. INTERSOS currently holds the role of GBV focal point for Jonglei, together with UNHCR and UNFPA, and acts as protection focal point in many counties such as Pibor, Waat, Lankien and Yuai. Currently INTERSOS is implementing 2 GBV projects covering Pibor, Nyirol, Akobo and Bor (funded by UNHCR, UNICEF and UNFPA). INTERSOS is one of the organizations with considerable experience in S/NFIs project implementation in South Sudan. INTERSOS intends to continue S/NFIs response and build on the successful pilot cash based intervention and continue to support the Cluster.Furthermore, INTERSOS is currently implementing an IPMT (Integrated Protection Mobile Team) project, this gives INTERSOS the ability to conduct timely and integrated joint S/NFIs and protection assessment to newly displaced or conflict affected population. The Coordination between S/NFI and IPMT teams may continue in providing joint services and improve protection sensitivities and CFMs in S/NFIs intervention.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

INTERSOS strives to incorporate participation of stakeholders in all interventions that it conducts. Throughout the assessment, verification and distribution processes, it will involve stakeholders meaningfully respecting decision making abilities regarding their needs. Arrangements that facilitate the involvement of stakeholders at all stages of intervention (assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation).INTERSOS will seek to carefully involve youth and women directly in the roll out of activities. Their direct support for assessments, verification and distribution and registration, aims to strengthen the participation and the knowledge among the beneficiaries of the activities to be conducted. INTERSOS team will always involve local authorities and community leaders to share design and objectives of the interventions. During CTP interventions INTERSOS will conduct sensitization activities for beneficiaries and stakeholders. Moreover INTERSOS will form a "CTP committee" to have an oversight on the CTP targeting and selection process (both vendors and beneficiaries). The committee include beneficiaries, marginalized/minority groups, RRC and/or local authorities, community leaders and traders representatives.
