Project Summary

A safe space for children is one of the most important things that helps the child to stay in a normal state, keeping him away from the atmosphere of wars and helping him to recover from the psychological trauma that he is exposed to under the current circumstances.
A safe space will be established to accommodate more than 200 children who have recently fled the war and air strikes, who have dropped out of their schools and have been traumatized by the bombing of their areas.
A safe space will be provided for children, in addition to providing life skills courses and awareness raising sessions.
Eight classrooms will also be provided for children, as there will be a classroom for every twenty-five students, and they will be provided with UNICEF curricula B, only to make them in the school atmosphere no more.
Motivational gifts will be distributed to them, and there will also be psychological specialists in order to supervise and operate the safe space.

4 - Needs

11,827 people recently arrived in Afrin, due to the systematic violent bombing, the children dropping out of their education and their safe space centers, which they used to go to, in addition to the psychological trauma of their adult parents due to the fearful fear.1) There is a great need to provide a safe space and we will work to provide psychological support in its early stages for children.2) There is a great need to provide psycho social support to parents to help them and help their children outside the centers.3) There is a need to sort out the children displaced to Afrin by specialists and refer them to specialized centers at higher levels.4) The lack of a safe haven for these children. Our sources are:1) Local councils,2) CCCM Reports.3) Humanitarian Response Team (Response coordinators).4) Our field team.

5 - Implementation of activities

200 children, twenty fathers and twenty mothers will be accommodated in our centre, The UNICEF B curriculum books for children, as well as stationery and motivational toys, will be provided to children.Children will be kept up to five hours a day, with the exception of the two weekly holidays, in addition to that weekly sessions will be received for parents to help them in awareness raising and psychological support sessions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Our field team deliberately held sessions and discussions with the displaced and local communities in Afrin through the local councils, and they participated in the most important needs of the families and students. Where our field team also made field visits to the homes of parents (Fathers and Mothers) and was able to conduct short-term interviews with the parents to see what are the most important needs that parents want their children. The situation of beneficiaries with special needs (children - parents) will be taken by preparing the safe space to receive them and to facilitate their access to the center. Through visiting parents, the focus was on how important it is for their girls to be enrolled in the center, as it is of great importance to their future. There are two young employees and a girl from our field team to help children and parents with special needs and facilitate their entry into the center. There will be a bus to transport children and girls to help them reach the center and to prevent harassment or any harm that may occur.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1) Fear of bombing the center = choosing an area near the border, which is considered somewhat safe.2) Children and girls may be subjected to verbal or sexual harassment through the road to the center = Renting a private bus to transport young students and girls to the center and then deliver them to their homes. 3) Children, girls and parents with special needs = Dedicate two employees to help them and facilitate their access to the center.
