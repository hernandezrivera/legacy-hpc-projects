Project Summary

This project aims at providing access to temporary and sustained water supply, safe, sanitary facilities, and increased awareness on safe hygiene practices to IDPs, host communities, returnees, and persons with disability affected by drought, floods, and conflict in Middle Shabelle, Hiraan, Kismayo, Baidoa and IDP settlements in Banadir. Temporary and sustained access to water will be delivered by providing water vouchers and rehabilitation of strategic water points. Hygiene kits that include soap, aqua tab, bucket, Jerrycan, and sanitary pads will be distributed to targeted high-risk families, i.e., families with malnutrition children, affected by AWD cholera, and persons living with disability. The project will also ensure that distribution points are close and can be accessed by people with disability. To promote good hygiene practices, hygiene promotion campaigns will be done in schools, water points, and health/nutrition centers in disaster-affected areas. The design of hygeine campaign materials such as IEC materials will consider the needs of visually and hearing impaired people. Disability disaggregated data will be collected throughout the project.To strengthen community ownership and ensure the sustainability of WASH assets, Water management committees (WMC) will be established; women, marginalized people, or those living with disabilities will have equal representation in these committees. Shock chlorination and dewatering of wells in floodaffected areas will be done to ensure water is safe for use again and to prevent further spread of AWD outbreaks. Gender-sensitive latrines with hand-washing facilities raised to avoid flooding and fitted with locks from the inside to curb GBV will also be constructed for the IDPs/returnees and dislodged. Siting of WASH facilities will be discussed with community members, particularly women and girls, to ensure optimum use and safety for vulnerable groups and people with disabilities. The design of WASH facilities will take into consideration the needs of disabled persons and the elderly. CLTS approach will be introduced where no latrines will be constructed to encourage good hygiene and sanitation behaviors aimed at achieving ODF free villages. Due to recurring floods in the Middle Shabelle region, WOCCA will use early warning systems to provide early warning messages in case of flood risk and create an effective response. The project will ensure that people living with disabilities will be included in every phase of the project. All WASH facilities will be located at accessible distances to women, girls, people with disabilities and vulnerable people in order to reduce time spent waiting and collecting water and to mitigate incidences of violence. The project will also ensure that women, men, girls, boys and people living with disabilities will participate equally in the design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation phases of the project.To ensure an integrated approach to WASH programming and GBV risk reduction, the project staff, and WASH committees will be trained on basic issues related to gender and GBV, including where survivors can report risk, access services, and referral mechanisms. WOCCA will hold sensitisation meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders, including groups at risk of exclusion, to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles. Accountability to the affected population will be enhanced by providing a feedback hotline to beneficiaries to provide feedback and complaints. To ensure quality in the project outputs, the project staff will continuously monitor and report on project indicators. Signing off and handing over WASH structures to beneficiaries upon completion will be done through community elders, government officials, and religious leaders. WOCCA will also adhere to the principles of "Do no harm.”

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

At WOCCA we believe a market based approach in the delivery of water is more cost efficient than doing it ourselves. It is less labor intense in terms of organizing while at the same time the risk of loss of water will be borne by the supplier. Generally we will access the situation beforehand and specifically look at: How does the target population normally access water during the dry season? Are there existingcoping mechanisms that could be supported/reinforced?If vouchers are to focus on settled pastoral communities, if there a risk of causing over-grazing of wet-season grazing areas (especially around water distribution points)?Will vouchers provide support to temporary mobile communities through the provision water todistant grazing areas in order to distribute pressure on scarce natural resources?Are the yields of boreholes sufficient to meet the water needs of water trucks in addition to theeveryday users?Will vouchers impact migration patterns of pastoralist populations?Will the most vulnerable populations and communities be insufficiently targeted due to roadaccess problems?If other than water, are there sufficient supplies available in target area?Will we do no harm?

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The supply of water will be done by asking around the normal suppliers on the availability. The assessment will determine if there is enough availability of the item that will be put on the voucher. If there is enough, a competitive tender will be put out to shortlist the suppliers that can be part of the supply in order to ensure that sufficient amount will be available to the beneficiaries. In case we expect a shortage, the commodities have to come from further away. This means a tender from actors that are able to access the commodities from outside the target district (even possibly from outside the country). The price of the voucher will include the cost it takes to move the commodity to the target area.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

To reduce the risk of violence during distribution, WOCCA will avoid centralized distribution of hygiene kits to minimize queuing time and risk of looting. Distribution areas will be close to the settlements to reduce travel distance for beneficiaries To ensure marginalized groups are not excluded, WOCCA will sensitize and engage all community groups during the response mobilization phase. WOCCA will also carry out community consultation to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability, and clan representation WOCCA will ensure that land tenure security, certified by relevant local authorities, is provided and guaranteed for a significant amount of time (at least 5 years) before any construction activities
