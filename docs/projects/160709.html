Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address immediate food and livelihood needs of 696,347 people facing severe acute food insecurity in several states across South Sudan including Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Unity and Warrap. This intervention will combine the direct delivery of food assistance to affected people with provision of emergency livelihood inputs targeted at revitalizing local food production as well as resilience building measures. The consumption stability achieved through the food assistance will safeguard livelihoods and assets and provide households leeway to engage in additional productive livelihood activities. This measured result, along with the project’s support for appropriate emergency livelihood inputs, community participation in value added actions, skill building and technical assistance will lead to recovery of agricultural and pastoralist livelihoods and the development of durable and productive assets. This will in the long term enhance food security, restore livelihoods, and build resilience to future shocks.
The project’s target is 696,347 people, anticipated to be in crisis or worse acute food insecurity in 2020. They include; 221,132 in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, 92,040 in Warrap, 173,685 in Jonglei and 209,490 people in Unity. Project participants will be selected through a rigorous community-based process with deliberate emphasis on vulnerable groups such as female and child headed households, households with children in curative centres, people with disability, the elderly and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs).
Specific interventions include: a) General food distribution with value added activities meant to ensure access to adequate and nutritious food to meet immediate hunger needs and address ongoing food insecurity b) Cash For Assets - that will be used as a resource for increasing community capacity to organize and engage in value-added and asset creation activities such as restoration of crop lands, flood control and natural resource management actions; c) emergency agricultural livelihood support consisting of crop, vegetable kits and fishing inputs in conjunction with agricultural skill trainings will help vulnerable households enhance own food production. The increased availability of vegetable and fishing produce will promote diet diversification. The adoption of better farming and fishing methods will contribute towards food self-sufficiency and reduced dependency on food assistance in the long run.
To attain optimal food security results, the project will promote complementary programming approaches, using the opportunity to interact with target groups beyond the activity of food and livelihood inputs transfers. The project will take advantage of all community mobilization platforms to disseminate key messages and actions on nutrition, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), gender and protection especially during targeting, community based participatory planning meetings, asset creation discussions and during food distribution.
JAM will ensure participation of women at all levels of the project. The project will ensure that at least 50% of the beneficiaries are female. To achieve this, female headed households will be prioritized during beneficiary registration and efforts will be made to ensure that during distributions, food is placed directly in the custody of women. Distribution sites selection and timing will take into consideration location centrality and protection concerns of women and children. The project will support the equal representation of females in local community institutions such as Project Management Committee (PMC) both at membership and leadership position levels.
The project will invest in capacity building of local actors including community members such as PMCs to ensure they have sufficient technical and managerial skills to plan and implement similar programs beyond the project period.

1 - Needs

The latest Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) findings estimate that over 6.35 million or 54 percent of the country’s population experienced severe acute food insecurity in August 2019. The crisis is further anticipated to continue in the coming year with 5.50 million people or 47% of the population estimated to face severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) in early periods of the year. The number of people who require humanitarian assistance remains high and women and children continue to be most affected. Climatic shocks and erratic weather patterns, pests and diseases, poor market functionality, inter-communal conflict, hyperinflation are some of attendant factors contributing to the food insecurity situation. The figures for malnutrition rates are also alarming. An estimated 1.3 million children aged 6-59 months and 352,000 pregnant and lactating women are currently experiencing acute malnutrition. Poor food security combined with poor feeding practices and limited access to health and WASH are continuing to fuel malnutrition Most counties in Unity, Warrap, and Jonglei state are experiencing critical level of acute malnutrition with GAM levels of 15%-29%. Over 1.2 million people in the proposed JAM operation areas are expected to face severe acute food insecurity (IPC phase 3 or worse) in the first quarter of 2020. Of these, 286,000 people will be in emergency acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4). The proposed counties include Guit and Rubkona in the former Unity state; Tonj East, Tonj North, Tonj South and Gogrial West in Warrap state; Aweil Center, Aweil South and Aweil West in Northern Bahr el Ghazal; and Pibor, Bor South and Twic East in former Jonglei States. A significant proportion of the population in the respective counties will be affected by crisis or worse food insecurity ranging from 40 percent for Tonj East up to 75 percent for Guit county. These projections have factored in the likelihood of availability of humanitarian food assistance and yet the numbers remain alarming. Underlying the worsening food insecurity situation in all counties are pockets of insecurity, cattle raiding, high food prices, flood related destruction of crops and disruption to markets. Severe environmental shocks (flood) experienced in the recent production season is expected to result in below average harvest and will reduce general food availability in most of the counties. Market shocks associated with high level food prices and reduced market access due to worsening road condition, will lower households’ purchasing power and access to food. Hence, alongside food assistance, efforts to curtail vulnerabilities to environmental shocks such as flood hazards will be imperative to safeguard food production and improve market access. The proposed counties are characterized by agro pastoral livelihoods where both livestock and crop production have important role in households’ food security. Despite its importance, crop production and productivity remains low due to limited size of cultivated land associated with labour shortage, unreliable rainfall and limited availability and access to agricultural inputs including seeds and tools. Fishing, hunting and gathering uncultivated foods complements annual food needs as well as serve as important coping strategies during lean periods. Capitalizing on these existing livelihood opportunities in farming as well as fishing by improving existing agricultural practices through expanding cultivable lands, enhancing access to inputs and skills will be crucial in stimulating local food production and contribute towards addressing the ongoing food insecurity crisis. This will improve food self-sufficiency, reduce food aid dependency and in the long run increase resilience of affected communities.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project will be implemented in close partnership with the government and community institutions and hence all the services and assistance provided by the project will be recognized by the respective local stakeholders. During stakeholder consultation and community sensitization, the project will provide clear information on the specific services and entitlements so as to build common understanding and facilitate the stage for local accountability.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Internally displaced persons (IDPs) are among the most vulnerable groups facing food and nutrition insecurity and will be one of the priority target beneficiaries for this project. IDPs will be given priority for food assistance as well as livelihood inputs such as vegetable and fishing kits. Some of the proposed counties such as Rubkona and Bor South are home to one of the largest IDP population in South Sudan and hence the project will give important emphasis to ensure their participation and benefits.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project will be implemented in close partnership with local government and community institutions. Government agencies such as Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) and Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry will have key role in the implementation of the project including targeting, providing technical assistance and monitoring and evaluation as well as ensuring security. At community level, the project will work closely with local Chiefs and Project Management Committees which are organized at Boma levels. The project will enhance the capacity of community structures and government counter parts to increase their ability to effectively organize and manage similar tasks into the long run. Capacity building of local actors and community groups will form a major component of the project’s sustainability and exit strategy.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The proposed project seeks to provide immediate food and livelihood assistance to affected population to save lives and restore livelihoods while helping households to develop resilience to future shocks. This action directly aligns with policies of the government of Republic of South Sudan, in particular, with government’s Humanitarian Response Plan which aims to save lives and alleviate suffering by providing multi-sectoral assistance to people in need. The proposed intervention for livelihood assistance through the distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits will further contribute to the government’s agriculture policy-Comprehensive Agriculture Master Plan-which aims to increase production and productivity of the agriculture across four sub-sector including crop, livestock, forestry and fishery. Hence, the project will have significant contribution to the achievement of food security related policies of the government of South Sudan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Following the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, security situation have relatively improved in many areas. The latest IPC report indicated that as a result of reduced insecurity in the country, access to livelihoods and market is gradually improving. The improving protection environment has also led to better humanitarian access further contributing to the increased access to food, nutrition and livelihood support, as well as service delivery. With the ongoing positive progress in the peace process, the security situation is anticipated to improve further in the coming year; creating conducive environment for humanitarian interventions to promote resilience focused long term development measures.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project incorporates sustainability plans. Sustainability of project outcomes including the creation of durable productive assets will be ensured through four approaches: a) Community participation, b) galvanizing attitudinal changes to promote self-help, c) building local institutional capacities, and d) strengthening linkages with other interventions. Through conducting proper community based participatory planning, JAM will deliberately work to instill strong sense of local ownership from the start. The project will invest in capacity building of local actors including community members (e.g PMC) to ensure they have sufficient technical and managerial skills to plan and implement similar programs beyond the program period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

1. To mitigate the risk of non-inclusion of women and girls in consultations, the project will strictly ensure vulnerable groups are equally represented in community mobilization events and local committees. Ensuring gender balance in such local level community institutions will empower women to influence local decision making on resource allocation. 2. To mitigate risk of exposure to sexual violence, the project will: a) train its staff on PSEA to enhance awareness b) conduct risk analysis to identify factors that expose women and girls to sexual violence and take appropriate protection measures such as setting up distribution sites taking into consideration timing, security and centrality of the sites. c) put in place complaint and feedback mechanisms, in particular, help desk, at distribution sites to provide safe and secure avenue for women and girls to encourage them to report on any act of abuse and seek for assistance.3. Targeting and beneficiary selection risk is highly likely, given the overall weak governance system in the targeted areas and the large size of the proposed beneficiary caseload. The project will ensure: a) targeting process is sufficiently decentralized down to Boma level and that targeting committees are established with fair representation across the community; b) targeting criteria are jointly developed with the community taking into account poverty based indicators or wealth ranking and that the community is sufficiently sensitized on the criteria; c) the project conduct verification of the targeting process before approving the final list. 4. To mitigate non usage of complaint and feedback mechanisms risk, the project will a) conduct sensitization so beneficiaries are aware of the availability, location and reporting of complaint and feedback mechanisms, b) establish safe, secure, clear and simple feedback mechanism and also how they can get can get response to their complaints on timely basis so that they have trust in the system.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed project has mechanisms by which target community members can voice their needs and preferences and demand inclusive processes. The project will facilitate community feedback mechanisms including setting up of help desks at distribution centers, AAP contact person and conducting post distribution monitoring and post planting assessments to gather community views and concerns regarding project services and procedures. Community members will be encouraged to give feedback on various aspect of the project including beneficiary selection procedures, access to information, distribution sites and time, entitlements and conditions of livelihood assistances, distribution procedures as well as on issues related to sexual abuses. The complaints and feedback mechanism gives community a safe and effective avenue to express their views and find solutions for issues that affect their interest. The project will ensure all information communicated to the target community is appropriate and tailored to local specific circumstances. The information will be delivered in local languages and will be culturally and gender sensitive. Printed information will take into account the high level of illiteracy rate and will be presented in pictorial forms.
