Project Summary

OBJECTIVE: Ensure 7,000 vulnerable affected households (31,500 individuals) have appropriate access to durable shelter and household items that provide increased safety, security, privacy, dignity, comfort and resilience to shocks.
The overall aim of this multi-sectoral project is to support vulnerable households and individuals (especially women and girls, children, persons with disabilities, and the elderly) to have access to safe and dignified shelter, settlement living conditions and communal WASH infrastructure, with access to basic items that meet household needs and provide safety and dignity. The overall objective of the Shelter-NFI component is ensure vulnerable affected households have appropriate access to shelter and household items that provide increased safety, security, privacy, dignity, comfort and resilience to shocks (such as high winds/storms), while meeting immediate and longer-term specific needs.
Throughout 2018 and in early 2019 (through already-secured funding), CARE Bangladesh will meet the needs of around 700 households (approximately 3,200 individuals) in Camp 16, through the construction of Mid-Term Shelters and Transitional Shelters, coordinated through CARE's role as Site Management Agency and in complementarity with Gender/GBV teams and WASH activities. This leaves around 4,000 households (18,000 individuals) in Camp 16 without access to improved shelter, many of whom continue to live in precarious site conditions at risk of health issues, fire and other safety risks, most notably for women and girls for whom GBV continues to be a real threat. This project aims to strengthen the integration of CARE's response, tying together settlement-level activities in shelter, WASH, site management and site development, while increasingly targeting towards self-identified needs in line with vulnerability, with a particular focus on the specific needs of women and girls to prevent gender-based violence. Furthermore, as CARE's presence expands (likely to include Camp 13 in 2019), additional households in need are likely to be identified (Camp 13 has a total population of around 9,900 households/43,000 individuals).

Needs

On 25 August 2017, violence erupted in Rakhine State, Myanmar, triggering a massive refugee influx into Bangladesh, 728,000 refugees have since arrived, bringing the total refugee population to 921,000 (ISCG 2018). Of this, almost 864,000 (almost 94%) of refugees are living in camps characterised by extreme congestion, overcrowding (62% of households reported sharing their shelter with other households; UNHCR-REACH MSNA, July 2018) and the risks associated with inappropriate shelter and settlement conditions - hazards of landslide, mudslide and flooding, risk of fire outbreak, threats to health, wellbeing and safety, and serious protection risks, particularly related to gender and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Due to this congestion, and linked to the proximity of the refugee settlements amongst or close to host community households and agricultural lands, there is no room to expand many of the camps further. This requires a carefully choreographed process (which may or may not involve temporary relocation) that permits incremental upgrades shelters to increase durability and safety, alongside parallel measures to improve the overall settlement conditions, including increasing effective access to water, latrines and hygiene information, information and guidance on how to maintain safe shelters and healthy homes, household items, and specific measures to improve safety, such as solar-powered lighting for which 53% of households reporting solar lamps as a priority need (UNHCR-REACH MSNA, July 2018). Where earlier in 2018, 35% of households reported having a part of their shelter (wall, roof) damaged or destroyed in the days prior to the UNHCR-REACH MSNA (July 2018), the aim for 2019 is to ensure all shelters are upgraded thorugh a variety of community-led, beneficiary-driven activities that ensure increased participation and appropriate targeting of assistance towards the most vulnerable, those with specific needs, and groups / areas at most risk. Across the whole refugee settlement in Ukhiya Sub-District, over 900,000 people are in need of Shelter and NFI assistance, out of which 100,000 (11%) remain in grave danger of landslide and flood risk hazard and require relocation to new areas or within the neighbourhoods where they currently live, concurrently strengthening their current sites to mitigate floods and landslide risks. A gender analysis published by the Inter-Sector Coordination Group (ISCG) indicates approximately 52% of the refugee population are women and girls. Among the working age population (age 18-59), 55% are female. While availability of basic services and facilities in camps have improved, women and girls’ risk to GBV – particularly to sexual violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, trafficking, and long-standing issues of intimate partner violence, early marriage, and economic violence from polygamy - are exacerbated by the culture of impunity, centralized power in male leaders in communities and lack of uniform and quality services to GBV. Despite improving living conditions, stigma and shame, limited mobility and poor access to resources of women and girls to resources and opportunities are not enabling women and men, girls and boys to gain equitably to the benefits of humanitarian aid. Humanitarian responses often miss opportunities to transform gender relations by empowering women and girls in their roles as decision makers, first responders and economic actors. Thus, integrating gender equality measures into the refugee crisis response is critical to ensuring that women, girl, boys, and men — particularly the most vulnerable and marginalized — have equitable access to (and benefit from) relief, services and information.

Activities or outputs

Across these two sites, in coordination with Site Management and other actors (including existing shelter actors), CARE therefore proposes the following outputs in Shelter and NFI: 1. Provision of Wall Bracing Kits to 3000 households with comprehensive technical guidance (13,500 individuals) The technical assistance component of this intervention will also provide technical guidance to non-recipients of this assistance (e.g. in inspections of beneficiaries of Tie-Down Kits from 2018). A specific approach will be developed to ensuring effective communication with women and girls. 2. Construction of 2000 Transitional Shelters (2000 households - 9,000 individuals), using the Transitional Shelter Approach where feasible (e.g. where partial cash-based interventions are permitted), composed of in-kind material distributions, cash/vouchers for self-purchase of materials or labour, labour support to vulnerable households, and comprehensive technical guidance to self-upgrade/construct shelters. A specific approach will be developed to ensuring effective communication with women and girls. 3. Provision of household Non-Food Items to 2000 households (2000 households - 9,000 individuals) through market-based approaches (where permitted; in-kind assistance may be necessary if the modality of delivery is restricted, but will be designed and targeted to ensure maximum participation, dignity and choice). Assistance will be in line with guidance from the Shelter Sector, WASH Sector and Cash Working Group, and targeted where appropriate to address the concerns and needs of women and girls, particularly in addressing risks of GBV. Due to exceptionally tight space constraints in established camps, prioritising the construction of Transitional Shelter over Mid-Term Shelters will allow existing, temporary shelters to be replaced with more durable, safer and resistant shelter. All interventions will be in line with Sector guidance and agreed standards. Cross-cutting activities will include environmental mitigation and mainstreaming Gender and GBV-prevention across all aspects of the project. This may include:- Environmental mitigation through responsible sourcing of bamboo and other materials- Re-greening and other environmental projects (through site development activities coordinated at shelter/household level as part of technical assistance)- Use of treated bamboo from sustainable sources that demonstrates proper disposal of waste materials (supply chain control) - Targeting of NFIs and other assistance based on need, with a specific focus on GBV-prevention (e.g. items supporting safety and dignity of women and girls, such as torches/solar lights, menstural hygiene materials, underwear etc)- Conflict mitigation and humanitarian mediation between refugees & host communities through provision of tailored assistance to host communities residing in or on the borders of the camp areas (up to 15% of total assistance)
