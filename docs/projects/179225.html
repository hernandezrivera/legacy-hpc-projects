Project Summary

The already fragile health system in NWS suffered from more than 110 attacks on health care facilities during 2019 and 2020 and has been negatively impacted by large-scale displacements, a shortage of health professionals, and the emergence of the COVID-19. IOM will enhance the health outcomes in northwest Syria by supporting the current health referral system, continuing the operation of three tuberculosis (TB) centres, and continuing two primary health care facilities.
To strengthen COVID-19 preparedness and response pillars and to enable a safe and effective COVID-19 response, IOM will support the current health referral system by transporting COVID-19 suspected patients to health centres. Additionally, all travellers crossing points of entry (PoE) into NWS from both directions will be screened for COVID-19 and referred and transported as needed. As it is necessary to limit disease transmission by reducing secondary infections among close contacts and health personnel in NWS, vehicles and staff must be disinfected after each transport of COVID-19 suspected cases to mitigate the risk of further infection in NWS. The COVID-19 referral system ensures that vehicles only transport suspected cases, and non-COVID-19 patients are directed to ambulances under the already existing health referral system.
IOM will also improve access to quality, lifesaving and life-sustaining health services in response to growing TB case numbers by operating three TB centres in NWS through experienced implementing partners (IPs). Each centre will provide a package of comprehensive TB services including TB diagnostics, TB treatments, food for patients, case finding, and screening of vulnerable populations. Since the start of the project in 2019 (previously funded by another organization), thousands of people have been tested, more than 800 TB patients have received services, and thousands of IDPs were prevented from catching the disease. Additionally, mobile teams run out of the centres will go into the community to screen and refer suspected TB cases to enable early detection of TB and counter misinformation.
To address critical gaps in health care services and provide essential health services to vulnerable populations, IOM aims to sustain primary healthcare services to the populations in two communities in northwest Syria. The two proposed facilities would provide adequate health response to IDPs and implement essential medical services as outlined by the Health Cluster in the standard package of primary health care services, including basic trauma and injury care, communicable disease and EWARN (disease surveillance system in NWS) reporting, sexual and Reproductive health, child health, basic nutrition, basic mental health and PSS, non-communicable disease management, and curative services.
All IOM staff will receive personal protective equipment (PPE) and all new staff will receive COVID-19 training on IPC guidelines and protocols as well as SOPs developed by WHO and the COVID-19 task force to ensure patient and staff safety.

4 - Needs

Medical services and health facility capacities remain critically insufficient across the vast majority of Syria’s sub-districts, with only half of the health care facilities in northwest Syria currently functioning. According to a rapid needs assessment conducted in November, COVID-19-related medical services also remain insufficient, with severe shortages in quarantine and isolation spaces, testing capacity, and health facility capacity across the vast majority of NWS’s sub-districts. The HNAP-COVID-19 rapid assessment published in Jan 2021 highlighted that NWS lacks COVID-19 medical services, with 20% of the key informants stating difficulties in accessing private health services, and 25% stated they could not access PSS. To limit the spread of infection, suspected cases must be isolated for transport and health services. The population of NWS has also been affected by TB. According to the WHO TB data for 2019, it is estimated that there are nearly 850 TB cases across the region; this could be an underestimate. Investment in TB case finding is needed to identity missing TB cases at an early stage. Essential health services are necessary to having a resilient health system, yet only 58% of hospitals and 53% of primary health care centers (PHCs) in Syria are fully functional. Abi Zar PHC is located in Al Shamali Neighborhood of Idleb city which faces insufficient health services and critical gaps in covering the needs of 250,000 people, while the Barisha facility is the only healthcare facility in the community and the surrounding areas. Additionally, in the last 6 months the two PHC’s averaged 10,124 consultations (4524 in Barisha, 5600 in Abi Zar) per month, highlighting the high needs in the region.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will strengthen COVID-19 preparedness and response pillars by operating a COVID-19 transportation screening and referral system, providing personal protective equipment (PPE) to staff, and by training new staff on IPC guidelines and protocols. The COVID-19 transportation referral system will be operated out of 20 locations in NWS and seven POEs and will provide transportation of suspected cases from non-COVID-19 health facilities to CCTCs and COVID-19 designated hospitals, ensuring the timely referral and continuity of care for patients. At the POEs staff will screen all of the travellers passing through to detect and refer COVID-19 suspected positive cases to be transported. All staff will receive two PPE kits which will cover 6 months’ worth of supplies, and any new staff will be appropriately trained on IPC guidelines and protocols as well as SOPs developed by WHO and the COVID-19 task force to ensure patient and staff safety. To face the critical shortage of health care facilities and improve access to quality, lifesaving and life-sustaining health services, IOM will operate through two Health Facilities in Abi Zar and Barisha which will provide the essential health services package (EHSP). To limit the spread of disease and improve health outcomes for TB patients, IOM will Operate three TB centers in Azaz, Afrin and Idleb which will provide comprehensive services including TB case-finding, diagnostics based on rapid detection of infection, screening of vulnerable population groups, improving adherence to treatment through treatment supporters and provision of food incentives (through inter-sectoral efforts with the FS Cluster), and treatment with high-quality anti-TB drugs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against health facilities and healthcare providers remains a key protection risk in NWS. IOM will ensure that health partners have required support to establish their duty of care policies and will engage with donors on the financial aspects of “duty of care” in the early stages of project design to guarantee flexibility in cases of unforeseen termination of contracts or re-location of the programs resulting from attacks on healthcare or change in control scenarios. IOM will also provide preparedness training for health providers in case of incidents, as well as on how to report any incidents through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM), as well as Health Cluster Flash Updates. Advocacy efforts against attacks on health care facilities will continue. Health services are often the first, and, on many occasions, the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance and are therefore an entry point for survivors to receive life-saving care. Health providers will receive training on how to offer survivor-centred, rights-based, non-discriminatory, trauma-sensitive first line support and health services and provide referrals to specialized GBV support services. Additionally, beneficiaries will have access to reproductive health services as part of the essential health package available at the PHCs. All centres will incorporate Cluster guidance on how to offer safe and confidential health services.

PSEA risks exist across all sectors of the humanitarian response. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

IOM will coordinate with the Health Cluster, WHO, COVID-19 taskforce and any other relevant partners to ensure that activities are not duplicated with other actors. Whenever possible, IOM will offer multi-sectoral support (such as FS support in TB centres) and referral pathways to specialized services for vulnerable populations. Additionally, IOM will involve all stakeholders throughout the project cycle including project design, implementation, and monitoring. IOM will communicate selection criteria to beneficiaries, provide services according to verified needs, and establish and communicate complaint and feedback mechanisms for all project activities. Additional risks include access restrictions due to the shifting of frontlines, non-renewal of the UNSC resolution, and the spread of COVID-19. In case of sustained insecurity and inaccessibility, IOM will be in regular communication with IPs to report any security incidents and conduct needs assessments to determine alternative locations. IOM also has a dedicated access unit that monitors and facilitates principled access for partners within Syria, and IOM will continue to participate in OCHA’s access working group. In case of non-renewal of the resolution affecting WHO’s supply line, IOM can preposition PPE materials and consumables for the PHCs inside Syria before the resolution is up for renewal, and consider moving to local procurement options for available materials. The risk of COVID-19 infection to beneficiaries and staff will be mitigated through IPC measures during transport and the isolation of COVID-19 suspected cases.
