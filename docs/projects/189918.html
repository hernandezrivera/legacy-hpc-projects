Project Summary

Ensure access to and continuity of essential primary health and MHPSS services, emergency referrals and outbreak response for vulnerable displaced people and host communities. This will be implemented through an integrated community-based approach in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces and through the deployment of mobile clinics and mobile brigades.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Through this project IOM proposes to deliver community-based support, supplement primary health care service delivery to hard-to-reach populations and improve access to mental health care in Cabo Delgado and Nampula provinces, which continue to be affected by pre-existing social issues and ongoing violence and displacement. IOM will provide primary health care services through the deployment of integrated mobile brigades, and support trainings for healthcare workers, and the deployment of mobile medical teams and mobile clinics to hard-to-reach areas, to meet the needs of displaced persons and host communities. Further, IOM integrated teams, in coordination with local authorities, will ensure community-based support for mental health and psychosocial support services, TB/HIV care and referral to specialized services. Community health workers will also conduct community engagement, health promotion and community based surveillance activities in resettlements sites and among host communities to identify and respond to cases of communicable diseases and chronic illnesses, including HIV, TB, COVID-19, diarrheal diseases, and more broadly public health events with epidemic potential. IOM will ensure coordination with DPS, SPS, SDSMAS, MISAU, INS and the health cluster partners at national level and Cabo Delgado level. 2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

- Mobile medical teams through mobile clinics and integrated mobile brigades deliver primary heath care and mental health services to vulnerable displaced populations- Community awareness-raising by community activists to promote health and well-being, including on, access to HIV and TB services, sexual and reproductive health rights, COVID-19 infection prevention and control measures, hygiene promotion and mental health - Active case finding and referrals of HIV/TB patients, and persons in need of mental health services, and potential cases of epidemic-prone diseases (COVID-19, diarrheal disease, etc) by community health workers- Support outreach health care services including integration of chronic conditions care (HIV, TB, and COVID-19 related co-morbidities), in the package of outreach services and effective delivery of quality services responsive to people’s needs. - Emergency medical and specialized referrals of patients to next level of care- Capacity development support to government mental health experts, including trainings, joint field missions (on-the-job training), and technical supervision to mental health personnel of the government, including psychiatric technicians, psychologists, and occupational psychologists, and integration of mental health experts (either IOM or government) into mobile brigades targeting the most vulnerable in hard-to-reach areas- Community engagement on COVID-19/communicable diseases, support to uptake of prevention & control measures, support to integrated community-based disease surveillance; monitoring of disease-related rumours to tailor response (initially COVID-19, the monitoring system will be adjusted and tailored to relevant disease in case of outbreak, e.g. cholera).- Improve systematic risk communication and community engagement, Procure PPE contingency stock and monitor vaccination coverage, improve uptake and ensure access to vaccination services while addressing psychosocial consequences of COVID-19 and other communicable diseases- Provision of community based psychosocial activities incorporated in above mentioned activities with particular attention to adolescent and young people, direct MHPSS services and referrals of cases, as well as capacity building of humanitarian and Government partners on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and Psychological First Aid (PFA) to ensure standards as established by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and the IOM Manual on Community-based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergencies and Displacement.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

IOM has an integrated protection-MHPSS approach which ensures intersectional considerations of protection and MHPSS needs as well linkages with health programming in a whole-of-community approach. IOM Health Teams who support mobile brigades and COVID-19 response are trained on protection principles. IOM identifies individual and structural-level protection risks by conducting the protection/safety audits in the districts. Also, the individual cases are identified and referred to specialised protection services in order not to cause further harm. IOM follows strict data protection rules and assistance standards in order to ensure meaningful access, not to cause further harm and empower individuals and communities assisted.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

IOM conducts community based rapid assessments in targeted communities and settlements, to map and assess populations main needs, vulnerabilities, resources, expectations and capacities, including key informant interviews and focus group discussions. This approach ensures that the project activities are tailored and respond to the needs of the communities and that community members can participate in project design and implementation. IOM also conducts satisfaction surveys among beneficiaries and regularly assesses knowledge of community activists and health workers, who are typically identified and recruited from among target populations. IOM also conducts mapping of available services for MHPSS, Protection and Health essential services, their respective capacities and gaps, to ensure that the project strengthens overall capacity of services providers to ensure continuity and sustainability of services.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM Mobile Teams identify people with special needs including individuals with physical and mental disabilities under the integrated health-protection-MHPSS activities. Also, IOM uses a diverse range of COVID-19 information dissemination materials and channels (including door-to-door, radio, and other community-based channels) to reach individuals with impairments.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed project does not include any construction work or other activities with significant environmental impact. In IOM’s community-based health and MHPSS activities strive to use environmentally friendly, recycled or reusable materials, and respect for the environment is included in some awareness-raising sessions with communities.
