Project Summary

WHO will continue supporting primary health care activities in Syria through both mobile teams and fixed primary health care facilities to ensure the delivery of the integrated Essential Health Service Packages (EHSP) to the population of Syria, while ensuring last mile coverage through integrated PHC network and access to healthcare to those in need who live in hard-to-reach areas. Estimated number of health facilities to be covered 500 fixed and MMUs along the country. Yet, the direct distribution is not applicable for PHCs, as the shipments are dispatched directly to MOH central level, and distribution tables are prepared by MOH based on their priorities.
It is imperative to enhance national health resilience and to reactivate public health canters’ by supporting the rehabilitation of non-functioning public canters, to repair extensive damage to crucial infrastructure, support ongoing medical training for human resource for health ,sustain availability of EML (medicines/equipment) and to undertake ongoing needs’ assessments to fulfill the gaps on the mid- and long term.
Objectives:
To ensure system strengthening the following programmatic areas will be in focus of the implementation of this project:
- Ensuring Access to Essential Primary Health Care Services
- Integrated Health Service Delivery/ PHC network
- Referral System
- Capacity building - trainings on a range of topics, such infection prevention and control, facility cleanliness and patient safety, pharmacy & warehouse management, facility management and standardized reporting through DHIS2.
- Health Information Systems - trainings on a range of topics, such infection prevention and control, facility cleanliness and patient safety, pharmacy & warehouse management, facility management and standardized reporting through DHIS 2.
- Quality of service provision.
- NCD integration
- TB integration into the delivery of EHSP
- Community health integration into the delivery of EHSP.
-Nutrition linkage: The provision of EHSP inclines Nutrition services, with focus on prevention of malnutrition among PLWs (Pregnant & Lactating Women) and U-5, health promotion acclivities, community-based interventions.
-GBV linkage: GBV first line support is part of the integratory services provided at the level of PHCs and community, after supporting health and community workers with capacity building, in collaboration with MHPSS programme and RH, basically through integrating with psychological first aid and psycho-social skills for COVDI19 responders.
It is important to mention here that due to SYP devaluation, the cost of rehabilitation and furniture from local markets was tripled, and thus the targeted PHC is reduced; still this is crucial support on the long term towards UHC and free of charge PHC services. Also to mention here that the number of operational mobile medical units, including medical teams supported is reduced compared to 2020 , as the final list of NGOs to be supported by WHO is under assessment by NGO unit along the country, the reassessment of qualified NGOs to be ready by end of July 2021. The planned MOUs in the 2nd half of 2021 is 20 , and this will bring the total supported NGOs to 30.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, estimated 12.43 Million are PIN for health with 3% increase compared to 2020.This necessitate to increase efforts to protect vulnerable groups, particularly in access-constrained areas, by ensuring the availability of lifesaving and life-sustaining essential health services in situation where about 50% of health care workers fled the country during the crisis and high staff turnover. The health system remains heavily disrupted; the COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with economic downturn in Syria, has pushed the health system to the brink. By mid-2020 more than 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below three critical emergency health standards; with just 58 per cent of hospitals and 53 per cent of Primary Healthcare Centres are fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. WHO continues to advocate for and support interventions to improve equitable access to quality health care for all affected populations including girls, boys, women and men. It is imperative to enhance primary health care services through increased number of PHC consultations across the country, including the management of NCDs, other common diseases, adolescents, reproductive health and diagnostic laboratory services, child and maternal health care. The implementation of the essential health care service package must be addressed and followed by improvement of the referral system. Due to shortage of medical staff, WHO will advocate the capacity building training for all medical staff at MOH, NGOs, SARC and health partners. The outreach health care through NGO is becoming more needed in affected locations with low health coverage. in 2020 WHO signed agreement with 29 NSAs

5 - Implementation of activities

1. Procure, distribute, and preposition essential lifesaving and sustaining medicines, medical equipment, and supplies including IPC/PPE supplies including IPC/PPE supplies to protect HCWs in the context of COVID-19. 2. Develop and distribute standardized guidelines with focus on non-communicable diseases, adolescent’s health, new borne care, Reproductive health, Elderly Health and other PHC programs 3. Capacity building on Essential Health Service Package at PHC and community levels 4. Support basic rehabilitation works, essential medical equipment and basic furniture for priority PHCs based on needs’ assessment and health coverage.5. Improving access to outreach primary health and referral services by supporting national NGOs through fixed & mobile clinics 6. Printing of PHC daily and monthly records to enhance timely and quality reporting.7. Community awareness raising activities.8. Expanding the IMCI program through the adaptation of the IMCI guidelines to the Syria context, building capacity of public health workers in health centres & supporting health facilities with required medicines and medical equipment. 9. Expanding the Caring of new borne at home program through building capacity of public health workers under the home visits programme at public health and community workers, setting up a referral system for cases requiring medical attention, and monitoring and reporting on visited families to strengthen the feedback mechanisms. 10. M & E: Coordination activities implemented with different partners to monitor progress and fulfill gaps (meetings, workshops); regular monitoring visits conducted at both central and district levels

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. Lack of access of basic health care services for all PIN among targeted population, due to non-functionality of PHCs, which in turn contribute to an increased burden of diseases (morbidity & mortality rates).2. Violence against health facilities and health care providers. Mitigation measures:1. Advocacy for rehabilitation of health facilities, strengthening the capacity building of existing health workers and promoting partnerships. Moreover, reporting on, advocating and coordinating with all stakeholders on the ground to enable smooth access.2. Continue reporting on attacks via SSA & MRM and health Custer flash updates.3. Provide preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case on incidents; this is supported under trauma projects ( Incident Management Training, HAZMAT courses,... ).

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1. Non-functioning health centres which hinders accessibility to basic PHC services & improving universal health coverage.2. Inaccessibility to basic health services imposes health threatening impacts /increased morbidity & mortality rates (diseases burden). 3. Turnover of staff and the need to keep on frequent training activities.4.Ending of UNSCR: UN Security Resolution will negatively affect access to humanitarian aid , especially for X-border shipments for NES.5.Lack of approvals for deliveries/ distributions of medicines; negatively affects the timely response.6.Long global procurement process, for PHC EML.7.Devaluation of SYP, negativity affects local suppliers' agreement and commitment to meet deadlines and completion of rehabilitation projects on timely manner / operational cost and consequently affects the value of money. Mitigation measures: 1. Support the minor rehabilitation & equipment for partially damaged health centres to increase health coverage with basic PHC and other health care services. 2. Expand partnership by involving stakeholders to provide basic PHC care services and to strengthen referral services. In addition to support outreach basic health services via mobile clinics. 3. Maintain capacity building activities for existing staff 4.Prepositioning of health aid at NES/NW5.Provide 50-75 % in advance payment for local suppliers to avoid SYP devaluation, stockpiling of constructions materials to meet deadlines with acceptable increased budget, long-term agreement.
