Project Summary

This project is going to provide an emergency education assistance to 10000 beneficiaries including 9500 Syrian children(4800 boys and 4700 girls) in formal and non-formal education , 330 (190 males , 140 females) education personnel and supporting staff in non-formal education in about 25 schools and in 25 back to learning campaigns(BTL) in 20 camps (10 of these camps are registered with local authority) in Northeast Syria in Ar Raqqa and Deir Azzor Governorates through prevention of children from dropping out , returning children back to school and providing an immediate access to formal and non-formal education.The proposed project is going to improve the access of 9670 Syrian children(4891 boys and 4779 girls), in 25 schools to formal and non-formal education by preparing safe learning spaces ,providing school furniture, teaching/learning supplies, textbooks and curriculum B , alleviate the suffering of during winter through provision of winterization support(fuel – plastic sheets – heaters) , and provide remedial classes to the low achievers and the children at the risk of dropping out and provide catch-up classes to the children out of schools , in addition to improving the quality of education in the 25 camps by providing incentives to non-formal education teachers , provide light professional development package including training on (Child Protection – Classroom Management – Literacy & Numeracy) to 330 non-formal education teachers and supervisors and providing PSS materials and activities and recreational materials to 9670 children(4891 boys and 4779 girls) in 25 schools located in 20 camps and in 9 different sub-districts in both governments.

Needs

Education was one of the features of life that stopped in Raqqa for more about 4 years between 2014 and 2017, large schools were destroyed, as most public facilities, students were cut oﬀ from schools, this catastrophic situation has deprived students from attending the educational process during that period. Accoding to an assessment done by Al Meen fo Humanitarian response –july 2018 : 1. Children & schools: 82 % of the families in AR-Raqqa have Children from age (6-17 years), 75 % of those children receive education in local schools.2. The main problems to send & keep children in your family (6-17 years) to school: The low quality of education, the inappropriate education environment and the lack of educational resources are the top 3 main reasons to send & keep children in families to schools.3. The top solutions for children to go and stay in school: Improving the learning environment, schools building enhancements and rehabilitation and support the education resources are the main 3 solutions for the majority of the children (6-17 years) to go to school and stay in school4. The top Priorities in the education sector: Respondents were asked to identify their top 3 priorities in the education sector as per their needs. Cash Assistance, Education supplies & resources, schools rehabilitation are the top priorities in the education sector across the governorate In addition to above, the response and current interventions are not meeting the actual increasing needs of education Northeast Syria and that makes absorbing new students is difficult because of the space, supplies and teachers who are under prepared to teach in a very difficult teaching environment. Multi-level and multi-aged learners who suffer the stress of war, in the same classrooms make it difficult for teachers to teach effectively and for lower level students to grasp concepts especially those who have re-joined schools after being out of schools or have dropped out for years. Despite this increase in primary education enrolment, intermediary and secondary level education services continue to be extremely limited. KIs indicated intermediary and secondary education was available to either less than half or to only a few children aged 13 to 17 in the majority of neighbourhoods, and to more than half of children in only six neighbourhoods

Protection Risk Analysis

SR will go beyond “Do No Harm” by designing and implementing activities of this project, that will promote collaboration and solidarity and avoid any predicted tension. Furthermore, SR will develop a set of protection activities to conduct during the project activities. The provision of education and psychosocial services to this community will help beneficiaries deal with the impact of conflict trauma and stress. It will build resilience within the communities and with the training to be included, whether around contingency/security planning or emergency drills, should improve the community engagement around self-reliance.As SR , we hope that the input of further functional educational facilities will make perceivable progress in the strife against the recruitment of young people to armed groups and early child marriage. It has long been a development indicator in the humanitarian community that young female students kept in school have a lower chance of being married at an early age. The longer educational opportunities can be provided, especially in a country such as Syria with such a strong and balanced educational background, the less likely both recruitment to armed groups and child marriage/ labour take place, We will be targeting both host and IDP communities, trying to ensure that community relations are not harmed but the most vulnerable and needy are targeted. Some priority will be given the IDPs depending on the location and whether another school is available in the area. We will try to ensure that protection principles are upheld in all our facilities and will be ensuring that there is training provided for Child Protection and all staff sign a Child safeguarding agreement as part of their contracts.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1 : Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas. Changes could increase the risks faced, displace or restrict movements of the population, local partners and others relevant to the project Mitigation Measures:1- Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation.2- Ensure flexible context-informed approaches and mechanisms to deliver the project outputs/outcomes, so that the projects activities remain relevant and can be implemented in a changing context3- Ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the projects beneficiaries and sufficient resources to enact those plans. Commit partners to ensure a minimum duty of care to their staff including a right of refusal.4- Ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security, including mine risk education.5- As needed/possible, minimise the distance travelled to/from learning spaces and the total number of students at any given centre by diversifying the locations where learning is carried out and keeping classroom sizes small, etc. 6- Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners, civil authorities and relevant security and access stakeholders. 7- All critical documents will be saved as soft copies, so that they will not get lost due to an attack. Noting that at times soft copies or computers may also need to be destroyed, partners will be responsible for frequently sending copies of critical documents to a safe location for archiving. Risk 2 : The perceived or real risk of the bombardment of schools, mines, cross-fire and kidnapping, etc. could make the targeted population deprioritize sending their children to learning spaces or schools Mitigation 1: In consultation with the community, partners will take into account risks to students, teachers and education personnel when designing their projects and work towards minimising risks. Mitigation 2: Key messages for back-to-learning (BTL) campaigns will take into account the concerns of parents and other community members.
