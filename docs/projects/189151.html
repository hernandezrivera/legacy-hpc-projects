Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Central and Eastern Equatorial, Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile and Western Equatorial states in the highlighted counties. This aim to improve access to comprehensive CP services including GBV and linkage to multisectoral services to benefit children, adolescents and caregivers. Plan has presence in the proposed locations. 2 counties (Pibor and Kapoeta South) are ranked severity 4 while the rest are under level 3. 65,000 beneficiaries (18,550 girls, 16,450 boys, 15,900 women, 14100 men). Out of 35,000 children targeted, 10000 (6000 girls, 4000 boys) are adolescescents while 15% of beneficiaries are persons living with disability.
Beneficiaries profile includes those living in remote, insecure and hard to reach areas, IDPs, Retunees, host community , pastoralists, vulnerable adolescents including girl mothers, elderly caregivers, female headed households, UASC among others.
Key protection risks addressed are school drop out, early and forced marriage and GBV, child labor, family separation and neglect, prevention of recruitments, psychosocial distress and other MHPSS issues, support for persons living with disability, dangers and injuries, access to justice and access to multisectoral services.
Plan strategies to address the issues includes provision of age gender responsive comprehensive case management including FTR, alternative family based care, cash assistance, NFI and dignity kits to address complex needs, PSS-MHPSS for children/caregivers, positive parenting and life skills education, CP/GBV capacity building for staff, volunteers, community structures and stakeholders, tailored community awareness and dialogues on harmful cultural practices, GBV prevention and CP monitoring and coordination with actors, mainstreaming diverse feedback mechanisms including integrated helpdesks to address CP risk PSEA/safeguarding. Plan will collaborate with Government, community structures, UNICEF, Protection cluster, CPAOR, CPWG, CMTF, UASC, PSS/MHPSS, GBV and CAAFAG WG. Others includes WASH, Education, PSEATF, shelter, WASH Etc. for service deliver.
Mixed static and mobile programming approaches including face to face and remote modalities will be integrated to allow adopting to changing needs and context especially deep field locations to support beneficiaries. Key interagency guidelines and Plan manual will be utilized for quality programming which includes revised CPMS standards, IA CM/CP/FTR/ SOPs, IA PSS working group PSS manual, CFS standards among other guidelines.
Plan will also adopt gender transformative approach, rights based, child/survivor centered approaches to programming to address root causes of child violation and GBV to shift community attitudes and negative behaviors that harm children. Gender/disability inclusion trainings will enhance access for persons with disability. Activities integration will be prioritized in Nutrition, Education, GBV, Food Assistance, WASH to holistically address diverse needs of beneficiaries including elderly.
COVID-19 measures will be observed per Ministry of Health/MHPSS guidelines to keep beneficiaries/ staff safe. AAP and feedback mechanisms will be integrated to address issues affecting boys, girls, men, women and enhance women and girls access to information through integrated and child friendly desks in CFS, schools, Nutrition sites etc., use of phones, girls symposiums, community feedback sessions.
safety audits, safeguarding risk assessment, community risk mapping and safe planning will strengthen centrality of protection/Protection monitoring to mitigate risks for beneficiaries and strengthen protective environment of children. Sustainability strategies includes capacity strengthening of community structures on CPIE creating pool of change agents and linking to development programs for resilience and working with NNGOs IPCS, GREDO, CHADO,SSADO and MOGCSW (Government) for planning handover and transitioning beyond this project.

1 - Needs

South Sudan face deteriorating humanitarian conditions and situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints, COVID-19, flooding and localized drought impacting livelihoods. Protection concerns are high, HNO/ HRP 2022 indicate 8.9M people are in need, 24% women, 53% children and 15% persons with disability. 3.5 million require CP services (2.9 million vulnerable children and adolescents) and 17% vulnerable caregivers . Government, with support of humanitarian agencies facilitated returnees hence 90,000 remained displaced within country with limited reintegration. A deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing with negative coping expected in 2022. UNICEF 2021 reports estimates over 1 million children need MHPSS. HNO/HRP 2022 indicate, over 50% of girls are married before 18 years, 2.4 million children were out of school in 2020, 34 % female own a phone compared to 56% male hence need for strategies to enhancing access to information. 60% of roads are affected during rainy season limiting humanitarian services especially for children, adolescents, persons living with disability. Government has developed robust legal framework to protect women and children but there is weak reinforcement. Customary courts cover early marriage, divorce, childcare and property rights and hear 90% of cases. However, chiefs are predominantly older men, deeply ingrained patriarchal views favoring men. Capacity building of key stakeholders and community structures including the chiefs is necessary to address issues affecting women and girls. Food insecurity and malnutrition have also exacerbated protection risks resulting to negative coping mechanisms (GBV), child labor, child marriage, and human trafficking in 2021, (SSD 2022, HNO/HRP report February 2022). The Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting verified 196 grave violations, recruitment/use of 129 children (five girls) in 2021. GBVIMS 2021 report indicate 6,237 GBV incidents reported between January and September 2021; 28% were Child survivors below 18 hence increase from 21% in 2020 to 28% in 2021. The 2021 analysis also revealed, 97% of survivors were women and girls.158 Of the reported GBV incidents; physical assault accounted for 36 % sexual violence for 24 %, and emotional abuse being 23%, (South Sudan 2022, HNO report; February 2022). State of World’s Children Report, Plan International 2021) reports indicate,13% of adolescents aged 10-19 live with MHPSS. 2021 FSNMS+findings recommend MHPSS needs, positive parenting life-skills education. 7% of children dropped out of school due to teenage pregnancy, early marriage, while boys engage in child labor, including risks of recruitment. AAP has to be strengthened and enhance safe reporting on PSEA. Friendlier and alternative mechanism can be incorporated. Violence against women and girls is triggered by negative and harmful cultural norms. Over 30000 and 4000 UASC need reintegration services (SCI and UNICEF 2021 reports). Plan has presence in proposed locations with active and open caseloads hence vulnerable children need Child Protection services; Kapoeta (84 boys 96 girls)Yei(154 boys, 143 girls)Torit (108 boys,140 girls)Pibor(110 boys 77 girls)Rumbek(61 boys,94girls) etc. Proposed project will be implemented in Yei, Kajokeji, Lainya, Morobo, Magwi, Torit, Kapoeta South, North, Greater Pibor, Rumbek Center, Rumbek North, Wulu, Cueibet, Malakal, Mundri East and Mundri West counties. Pibor & Kapoeta are ranked Severity4, others classified as severity level3. Protection risks to be addressed are child labor, neglect, distress, CAAFAG, early marriage, COVID-19 effects, neglect, school drop out, abduction among other. Interventions proposed are comprehensive CM, foster care, MHPSS, capacity building of staff volunteers and stakeholders, dialogues/awareness, feedback etc. These interventions are inline with HRP 2022, Protection cluster/CPAOR 2022 cluster objectives SO1, SO2 SO3 and CPAOR 2 objectives.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Efforts must be made to ensure AAP, as well as safe and accessible reporting on CP and GBV issues and need to enhance quality and accessible survivor assistance, use of GBV/CP referral pathways for safe referrals and enhancing community engagement, participation and accountability. Challenges in establishing traditional AAP mechanisms as complaint boxes, lack of access to hot lines due to low literacy level, lack or absence of mobile network in many parts of the country, (South Sudan 2022, HNO report; February 2022). Therefore, friendlier and alternative mechanism can be incorporated in programming to enhance access to information, utilization of services and ensure beneficiaries are engaged and participate in key programme activities. Key data and information from multiple complaints and feedback mechanisms through support from Plan International MEAL team, will be regularly analyzed to inform key issues, trends and recommendations from men, women, girls, women and persons living with disability. The information and data will therefore inform weather beneficiaries are satisfied with specific interventions or dissatisfied. Plan will therefore review each complaint and coordinate immediate feedback or indicate that the complaint is being followed up. The feedback and complaints will then be analyzed to inform key decisions such as change of targeting of beneficiaries, inclusion of specific groups who might have been left out during design of the project, change timing of delivery of activities, inclusion of community structures among others. The beneficiaries decisions can also inform activities to be adopted to meet the beneficiaries expectations. Management will also utilize the feedback and complaints to inform and make key decisions for the benefit of the project. where feasible, Plan will also integrate additional strategies to support vulnerable beneficiaries in hard to reach locations such as decentralizing services or introduction of Mobile CPIE services (PSS, case management, Trainings etc.) to ensure they adequately benefit from the project and mitigate risks related to walking long distance to access services. Lastly, based on the feedback provided through FGDs with children or CBCPNs, members or community review meetings, Plan will integrate them into project activities to ensure effective project delivery. For instance, Plan will lake effort to facilitate the community structures, provide technical support and guidance to effectively deliver their work with ease. Special attention will be given to vulnerable women, adolescent girls including girl mothers, elderly and persons with disability through adapting a friendly, confidential and safe mechanism to encourage reporting and airing of views. As long term strategy, Plan will explore opportunities for linking beneficiaries to longer term interventions such as peace building and peaceful co existence, livelihood support among others for resilience building and promoting self reliance. Community based structures will also be encouraged to gather views, feedback and information from beneficiaries and advice Plan on suggestions to continuously adapt programing to enhance beneficiaries satisfaction.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Plan international will adopt all project activities to ensure persons living with disability, individuals who are not literate, women and girls benefit from the project. For instance, recruitment of staff and volunteers will consider those from the local community, speaking same language and who understand the local context. They will be trained and oriented on gender and disability inclusion to be able to support the vulnerable individuals. Materials such as IEC, messages, banners, T-shirts among others will be developed with communities and translated into local languages while awareness sessions in communities and through radio will be delivered in both English and local languages. Plan International will collaborate with Humanity and Inclusion among other agencies and health providers to refer and support persons with diverse disabilities. They will also be included through the project phase to ensure their views and suggestions are taken into consideration. PSS and other play materials procured for PSS activities will consider brighter, bigger and safe materials that can be used by both well abled and children with diverse disabilities. In collaboration with Disability and Gender Inclusion organizations such as Humanity and Inclusion, Plan will conduct safety audits in CFSs, schools and community centers to identify disks and implement mitigation measures to keep children and young people safe. Additionally Plan will facilitate risk assessment for all project activities to identify risks and develop mitigation measures prior to engagements of children and young people in all activities. Joint assessments with community structures and partners will also be conducted in CFSs and schools to review access and mobility needs for adaptations to support the vulnerable individuals. Plan International intends to support marginalized and vulnerable groups including persons living with disability comprising of 15% of cumulative beneficiaries targeted in this project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Violence against women and girls has been one of the most neglected casualties of the current crisis, this is being triggered by the persistent negative and harmful cultural norms. Humanitarian response to the crisis affected population was previously affected by inter communal conflicted that has been recurring for the last 7- 8 years. Plan International is a member of the inter agency PSEA and GBV working groups and has a robust internal mechanism to follow up and investigate PSEA related issues. Plan has zero tolerance on PSEA and sexual harassments, has safeguarding children and young people policy and code of conduct which are signed by all staff, volunteers and associates. As per the established reporting mechanisms, cases can be reported through the focal persons confidentially, through safe calls and through the integrated help desks among other mechanisms. Plan will integrate safeguarding and PSEA in all project activities to reinforce preventative measures. Key information dissemination forums includes community meetings, forums, children and adolescent forums at CFS and schools, training and awareness activities to raise awareness, highlight reporting channels and encourage communities to report. Additionally, Harmful and gender discriminatory cultural and traditional practices continue to drive and exacerbate these CP issues including risks of GBV and PSEA. Lack of participation in decision-making affects girls and other excluded groups such as children with disabilities, denying them opportunities to grow, develop and fulfill their rights and full potential. Patriarchy, discrimination and exclusion, children’s low status and other drivers of unequal gender and power dynamics hinder efforts to change community attitudes and practices to protect children. All SGBV related cases will be supported through existing referral mechanism and in line with 72-hour rule, and coordinate linkage for clinical care and support by the lead GBV actors per location. Plan will also provide age and gender appropriate interventions by considering specific needs of boys, girls, men, women, children and caregivers with special needs and same will be reflected in all assessments and interviews. Plan will also strive to achieve a 53:43 ratio of women to men, girls and boys per location since majority of those at risk are vulnerable girls and women. In areas where Plan International is the lead Child Protection agency such as Greater Yei and Pibor, Plan will coordinate with other CP actors, CPWG and Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare to train and sensitize beneficiaries, staff, community structures and volunteers on prevention and response of PSEA and child safeguarding concerns. Other mechanism to be used to disseminate key information on prevention and reporting of PSEA and GBV incudes through printed IEC materials and posters printed in local language, printing hanging banners in Plan International offices, CFS and schools, printing relevant messages on T-shirts during calendar events, integrating messing in community awareness campaigns including community radio, discussion with Children clubs in schools among others. Plan will also work with other sectors such as Nutrition, Food assistance, education, Health among others to integrate PSEA messaging including staff and volunteers. Linkage and referral of at risk families and adolescents for food assistance and livelihood will also mitigate negative coping such as survival sex, early marriage among others. Adolescent girls and caregivers will also be trained on life skills and positive parenting to enhance the protective capacity, support and protection of vulnerable girls and women. Lastly, Plan International will work with other actors for joint training, awareness and sensitization on risks of PSEA during COVID-19, confidential report and support for affected children, adolescents and caregivers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Mode of information dissemination: Based on the feedback provided through , help desks, phone calls, suggestion boxes, FGDs with children or CBCPNs, members or community review meetings, Plan will integrate them into project activities to provide information and feedback to communities. Key information on project activities and schedule will be shared with community leaders and community structures for participation, engagement and support. Plan will also integrate the feedback to inform key decisions on actions to strengthen quality programming, and areas for adaptation to meet needs. For instance, if the CBCPNs suggest that their need megaphones to enhance their work, Plan will consider to procuring the megaphones. Through community meetings and FGDs, beneficiaries who cannot write, will be able to provide their feedback as they will be allowed to talk and express themselves freely. Also, persons with disability will be intentionally targeted as part of the feedback mechanism. The FGDs will be gender-disaggregated, men and women separately and also boys and girls separately. Plan will adapt favorable and friendly women and reporting mechanisms such as use of phones etc. Plan will also strive to benefit 53:47 beneficiary ratio between women/girls or male/boys including elderly &PWD. Plan will also strengthen the child friendly feedback mechanism and Integrated CP help desk, equip them with child friendly materials in Nutrition, Health, Food assistance, Schools and other key community points. Training will be done for CP community structures on key CP topics such as communicating with children, PFA, PSEA, Child safeguarding reporting procedures, referral pathway and support community information meetings. This will be done by Plan staff in collaboration with the community structures. Calendar events such as Day of African Child,International girls day, World children's' day will be utilized to disseminate key information to boys, girls, men, women, persons leaving with disabilities and use the events to amplify girls and children voices on issues affecting them. The key information and messaging will be developed through community consultative process, validated by community structures and CPWG and tailored for children, adolescents and adult audience. Where feasible, the information and messages will be translated in local language and delivered in first language the beneficiaries can understand. community structures comprising of adults, adolescents and adults in each location will be facilitated and supported to develop community actions plan inline with the key issues affecting child and girls which includes child labor, early marriage, importance of school among others. . Additional excluded and vulnerable beneficiaries, including child survivors of GBV, will be identified through community-based structures, families, child focused and help desks located, referral from other partners and other sectors in the referral pathways. Returnees will be identified by Plan International in collaboration with RRC and Government stakeholders. Further, children with low risk levels and not necessarily in need of case management or specialized support will be linked with child, youth, and community structures for support and follow-up. MEAL Officers per location will also monitor project implementation with regular visits to the project sites and be responsible for the monitoring and evaluation (M&E) framework, including procedures, timelines, and roles and responsibilities for data collection and reporting. The meal officers per location will also support in strengthening child friendly help desks and ensure timely reporting while supporting existing community feedback mechanisms. They will log in all complaints, analyze them and coordinate feedback to beneficiaries in collaboration with CP focal persons per field. Any safe guarding or PSEA related issues will be channeled to the safeguarding focal persons per location .

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

The project activities proposed above will be integrated with GBV prevention strategies such as community awareness on CP, GBV and COVID-19 risks, community dialogue to address harmful cultural practices that affect women and girls, engagement of community structures to renounce violence against women and girls as well as role model positive changes among others.Case management team will identify GBV and SGBV cases and coordinate referrals as per the location based referral pathways and ensure the survivors are supported inline with 72 hour rule. Adolescents participating in life skills and positive parenting sessions will also benefit from GBV awareness sessions while extremely vulnerable adolescent girls will benefit from MHM/dignity kits. child survivors of SGBV will be monitored and supported through comprehensive case management services and Plan will coordinate confidential referrals to other sectors as per assessed needs while observing information sharing and data protection protocol. locations for activities such as trainings and awareness activities will be carefully selected and the activities conducted in safe spaces such as CFS, schools, community youth centers among others. Timing of the activities will also be considered to ensure adequate participation of men, women, boys and girls. For instance, awareness activities including CFS/PSS activities will be conducted in the afternoon to allow adequate participation of women and children and to avoid affecting normal school activities. Where feasible, PSS activities will also be conducted within schools and CFS to allow more participation of vulnerable girls. all trainings for staff, volunteers, community structures and stakeholder will integrate sessions on GBV, referral pathway, safeguarding and prevention of PSEA. Case workers will benefit from ongoing coaching, supervision and technical support including case conferences to resolve complex GBV cases and receive guidance. Plan will also collaborate with CPAOR and GBV working group to roll out GBV packages, tools and relevant guidelines to enhance safety and quality support of child survivors and referral of caregivers to access timely support. Plan through participation in the relevant clusters and working groups, will amplify girls and women voices by highlighting issues affecting them and ensure women and girls participate in key calendar events such as international women's day, 16 days of Gender Activism, International day of the girl for joint advocacy on women issues.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Plan will regularly update internal contingency plans for flood response and collaborate with CPAOR for early warning mechanism and preposition support for possible response. Plan will support community awareness initiatives jointly with other actors and Government stakeholders to mitigate risks and impact of flooding. Communities residing in areas periodically affected by floods will be encouraged to move to higher ground assessment and identified by Government and Authorities to mitigate impact. As a long term strategy to mitigate climate change impacts, communities, schools including CFSs will be planted with trees and flowers and community members will be encouraged to plant and grow trees. Demonstration sessions will be integrated in CFS and school activities to role model and reinforce positive behaviors of growing trees. Additionally, resilience building preprogramme activities have been integrated in the project activities including peace building and peaceful co existence.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Cash assistance has been integrated in the key interventions such as case management to achieve protection outcome. Plan International implements the biggest WFP supported food assistance projects hence identification and referrals of vulnerable families will be done to food assistance focal persons. Additionally, the vulnerable families will be linked to livelihood and skills building organizations for support and resilience building activities.
