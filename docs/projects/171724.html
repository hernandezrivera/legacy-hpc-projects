Project Activities

Organization of community dialogue sessions on child protection and GBV and harmful traditional practices using “In Her She” toolkit as well as monthly focus group discussions with religious leaders on GBV, Human Rights, and PSEA in addition to awareness creation activities during calendar events, Provision of clinical management of rape (CMR) services, GBV case management including psychosocial support and referrals, training of GBV steering committees, community volunteers, and community members on gender GBV basics, prevention and response, human rights and PSEA in addition to the provision of material assistance (dignity kits, solar lantern, and Energy Saving Jikos) for women and girls at risk and GBV survivors. Objective 3: To improve operational response capacity through capacity development, strategic advocacy, and humanitarian dialogues (Protection Cluster Objective 1.Activity and outputs. improving the quality of CP services for boys and girls most at-risk and survivors of violence, exploitation and abuse in particular the child survivors of GBV, child labour, unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG). and Increasing the capacity of community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM) to prevent and mitigate CP risks through multi-layered, community based psychosocial support interventions to reduce vulnerabilities and risks to children by building a protective environment at family and community levels. Peer to peer awareness raising will be led by CBCPMs, youth and community volunteers’ networks to prevent child protection risks, identify vulnerable children in need of support such as case management and emergency child protection care (i.e. alternative care or basic PSS activities) and Protecting and supporting to children at risk and child survivors including unaccompanied and separated through providing case management services that meets their unique needs, which include family tracing and reunification services, alternative care based on global learning and field evidence. The capacity of community volunteers will be strengthened to support the case management process, namely through identification of children at risk, making home monitoring visits and offering peer support to caregivers.Provision of inclusive community reintegration services for children associated with armed forces and groups, or those at-risk categories. Provision of structured MHPSS activities including individual and group counseling. The Child Protection AoR also considers developing standardized tools/modules in structured PSS services, enhancing capacity of different stakeholders in PSS, strengthening MHPSS with more focus on children in need of additional more intensive support and Supporting to caregivers and families (MHPSS, positive parenting and other kind of support) of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. Awareness raising activities targeting community wide on prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, MRE and Children’s rights and Strengthening Child Protection information management(CPIMS+)for regular Child Protection trends analysis and evidence-based responses through inter-agency collaboration and CPIMS+ reports, Systematizing efforts to develop sustainable local capacities for child protection, Strengthen child safeguarding measures including PSEA and code of conduct to ensure that all interventions are safe for children. CP partners that have at least one staff trained as a PSEA and child safeguarding focal point, Child Protection safety audits/walks/mapping exercise will be used to identify safety and protection issues for children in camps and host communities and which allows children’s participation in identifying risks that concern them an
