Project Summary

Project Goal:
To ensure education to the deprived Rohingya community children who are forcefully displaced from their ancestral land.
Core objectives:
1. Providing quality education to the FDMN children residing in 7 different camps as per the Education Sector’s guidelines and materials;
2. Provide teaching and learning through digital methods;
3. Capacity building of the teachers, staff, and volunteers in order to cope with the Education Sector standard;
4. Rehabilitation of the learning centers in the camp areas;
5. Providing a child-friendly environment inside the learning centers;
6. DRR contingency planning for the monsoon.
Project Overview:
The project aims to deliver structured, high-quality education to 5,000 Rohingya children in seven camps, ensuring gender parity with a 50% female participation target.
Infrastructure and Resources:
Education is delivered through 50 learning centers built in compliance with Education Sector guidelines for construction, curriculum implementation, and teaching methodologies.
The Myanmar Curriculum is scaled up in all learning centers to align with sector priorities.
Innovative Approaches:
To overcome challenges like pandemics, the project integrates multimedia-based, animated, and interactive lessons, enhancing engagement and enabling remote learning.
Beyond Academics:
The project incorporates life-oriented lessons to equip children with skills that promote community improvement and social cohesion.
Commitment to Collaboration and Compliance:
As a long-standing Education Sector member, Mukti Cox's Bazar actively participates in coordination and camp focal point meetings. Mukti complies with all sectoral policies, emphasizing protection through:
A robust complaint and feedback mechanism.
Dedicated roles for Gender Focal, PSEA Focal, and Protection Officer to address safeguarding issues.
Community Engagement and DRR:
Community Involvement: The project engages parents, Majhis, religious leaders, and CiCs through regular meetings and orientations. Parent meetings encourage participation, building trust and cooperation.
Disaster Preparedness: A DRR budget supports annual learning center repairs during monsoons, per sector guidelines.
Social Cohesion Activities: Key events like Genocide Day and annual sports and education fairs foster unity and peacebuilding within the community.
Mukti Cox’s Bazar has undertaken a pioneering initiative to address the challenges Rohingya children face in learning and using the Burmese language, which is critical for navigating the Myanmar Curriculum. Recognizing these difficulties, Mukti implemented a six-month pilot program to incorporate the Hanifi language script as a supplementary learning tool alongside the Burmese curriculum.
The pilot, conducted from January to June, involved five Learning Centers and aimed to:
Simplify the transition to Burmese for Rohingya learners.
Leverage the Hanifi script as a culturally familiar bridge to ease language acquisition.
Enhance comprehension and practical usage of the Burmese language in daily life.
Outcomes and Recognition:
The pilot proved highly effective, with noticeable student engagement and language proficiency improvements. Following the successful conclusion of the pilot, Mukti shared its findings and outcomes with key stakeholders, including the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), Camp-in-Charge (CiC) officials, and program partners like UNICEF and Save the Children.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya community has endured decades of systematic disenfranchisement, discrimination, and persecution in Myanmar’s Rakhine State, leading to multiple waves of displacement. The largest refugee influx into Bangladesh began in August 2017, with approximately 1,010,298 Rohingya refugees currently residing in 33 congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas and on Bhasan Char Island, as per 2024 UNHCR data. Children account for 51% of the refugee population, totaling 498,855 (243,476 girls and 255,379 boys), with women, girls, and boys constituting over 75% of the total population. These groups remain particularly vulnerable to abuse, exploitation, and gender-based violence. Children have been disproportionately affected by the crisis, grappling with physical, psychological, and social challenges. Educational Needs and InterventionsWhile initial humanitarian efforts addressed emergency needs, the focus has shifted to education as a fundamental right. Leading organizations like UNICEF and UNHCR have supported educational initiatives, but a significant number of children remain out of school. Recognizing this gap, Mukti Cox’s Bazar launched a project targeting 5,000 learners through 50 Learning Centers (LCs) across various camps. Challenges Addressed: Lack of internet connectivity in camps hindered access to digital learning.Education materials remained static, lacking interactive elements. Innovative Solutions:Mukti Cox’s Bazar has introduced a digital platform called “Moja Kids” where the team works to enhance the creativity of the learners by encouraging them to take part in different extra-curricular activities which are presented in different social networks like facebook and youtube (https://www.youtube.com/@mojakids4321/videos) with the consent of the learners and families. These activities are not directly related to the education curriculum but it helps the learners to grow psychologically which boosts their confidence that directly affects their education in a positive way. To make sure these extra-curricular activities are not interfering with regular class routine, the recording sessions are usually taken place in off hours and on weekends. Child Protection and SafetyMukti emphasizes the safety and well-being of children within its Learning Centers: UNFPA Partnership: Gender-Based Violence (GBV) support for victimized females.PSEA Wing: A dedicated Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) unit ensures robust reporting, justice, and support mechanisms for victims and their families.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project aims to support the education of 5,000 FDMN Children and 1,000 Host Community children who are under 18 years.Mukti will prioritize inclusiveness to enroll girls and children with disabilities in LCs under this Project. Mukti will support women’s empowerment by ensuring their active participation in parents’ meetings, teacher training and staff training so that they can share their learning with their family as well as respective community. In order to do so Mukti will train it’s teachers and staffs to help the program dealing with disability and inclusion. The disability trainings will help the field teams to find out children with disabilities during the surveys and screenings. Children who responded to the Child Functioning Module in a way which indicates they may have a disability will be referred to the partners who works with inclusion in the selected camps. They will technically guide and support Mukti if they require a specific assistive device, which is measured and is tailored to that learner. To support equity in teaching and learning and improve data administration education technology will be deployed, including classroom radios and teacher tablets, for interactive learning, teacher’s professional development and strengthened record keeping on attendance, learning and assessment results.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Overall, an alarming 33% of parents only report attending parents’ meetings, partly due to the fact that these interactions largely appear to be structured as a one-way flow of information from staff to parents, with less space left for the community members themselves to take any initiatives. In order to increase the community awareness, Mukti will involve vulnerable communities, mainly by parent meetings to elicit their views and perspectives on programme priorities. Besides, Mukti will work in a similar manner with the community leaders like Majhis, religious leaders and CiCs through different meetings & orientations. Mukti plans to come up with a new mechanism with the target to bring social & behavioral changes through well planned and target oriented community engagement. Major activities related to the community engagement to ensure AAP are given below:• Parents group discussion (4 sessions per learning centre in a month)• CESG meeting in learning centers for better community engagement in a higher level• Community Dialogs under each learning centre (community engagement & Feedback mechanism)• Information Sharing Session in all the learning centres.• Introducing reporting mechanism in all the learning centres for the affected population in need.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Non-formal education at our learning center: The education system implemented in these schools is non-formal, operating outside the formal school system. Under this system, a batch of learners is enrolled and nurtured until they complete their studies up to the fifth grade. Following the completion of five years, a new batch is enrolled for another five-year cycle. Upon finishing their studies at this non-formal school, students have the opportunity to continue their education at a government school. The program aims to educate these children while accommodating their work responsibilities. To ensure flexibility, our schools operate in two shifts, allowing learners to attend school in one shift and work in another to support their families. Currently, we have 10 learning centers with 20 classrooms located in five different areas of Cox's Bazar, with the capacity to educate 1000 children.
