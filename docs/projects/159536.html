Project Summary

The Specific Objectives of the project are as follows:
• Support the productive capacity of farmers by rehabilitating open field land and providing access to water through functioning water systems (irrigation, water collection ponds) )
• Recover and sustain the livelihoods of farmers by providing cash for work activities
The project will address Country Strategic Objective 3 and FSS’s Specific Objective 1 which aims to enhance the productive capacity of farmers by improving their livelihoods and enhancing their access to water for agricultural purposes which improves the general food security situation and has a positive impact on overall nutrition and health status.The project will be implemented in partnership with UAWC who has a strong presence and history of intervention in the OPT including the Gaza Strip. Oxfam’s partnership approach is holistic and rights based. The project includes activities in Gaza Strip targeting Deir El Balah, East Gaza Governorate and the Northern Gaza Governorates. Activities will include land rehabilitation, planting of field crops and intercropping vegetables, water collecting ponds, total expected beneficiaries will be 867 farmers (4942 ind). The project aims to employ 170 agricultural workers in land rehabilitation activities. In addition, Oxfam will provide evidence-based policy recommendations, to specified targets, with relation to identified constrains and vulnerabilities connected to increasing the statues of food Insecurity in Gaza Strip. Proposed activities are based on Oxfam Influencing Strategy with the aim to influence acts that will challenge and reverse the Israeli government separation policy imposed on Gaza Strip. Oxfam will ensure mainstreaming protection throughout the project cycle, starting from assessment through the implementation and monitoring. This will be represented through beneficiaries selection through tackling protection based criteria in addition to the livelihood vulnerability. It will also address protection risks in a referral mechanism in liaison with protection cluster members.
The project is based on Oxfam’s resilience framework; ultimately aiming to increase beneficiaries absorptive and adaptive capacities.The project aims to also address aspects of gender injustices between men and women in the agricultural sector through training and analysis of women and men gender roles in the agricultural productive activities. The farmers pointed out that although women help their husbands, they are not considered farmers. Despite the fact that they believe that women play a fundamental role in farming activities by spending long hours of diverse work in the farm and that it has to be valued given that as it constitutes additional work on top of the household and reproductive responsibilities. Although women and men have complementary yet different roles in agriculture, men have more access to the benefit resulting from those activities; since most of the women don’t receive any financial return for their work in agriculture.

1 - Needs

The poverty level in Gaza is four times higher than in the West Bank and female headed HH’s in Gaza experience a poverty rate of 1.3 times higher than those of males (PCBS, 2017). Adding to the already deteriorating situation in Gaza, the cuts in funding imposed on the PA and the UNRWA by the US Government is affecting the delivery of emergency assistance to vulnerable families . The Great March of Return demonstrations taking place in Gaza have also contributed to higher food insecurity due to the damage affecting hundreds of dunums and assets since the crisis, directly impacting 300 farmers and disrupting the livelihoods of 3000 farmers according to the HNO 2019. To understand more the impact of the crisis on farmers, UAWC has conducted five focus group discussions (FGDs) in August and September of 2019 that cover farmers from all five Gaza governorates in which 125 farmers participated, the cross finding of these FGD can be summarized by the following points: Agricultural activities are becoming less profitable and unable to cover farmers’ HH basic needs, which resulted from:• The increasing agricultural inputs’ costs such as the cost for irrigation through Gaza’s power shortage crisis in addition to old inefficient irrigation system used by farmers.• Access restriction and bulldozing the agricultural lands by Israeli Security Forces in the ARA.• Losses of crops resulting from spraying herbicides by Israeli Security Forces in the ARA• Small and medium farmers in all the areas reached by the FGD indicated having accumulated debts to cover HH needs and agricultural inputs, where debts are reaching an average of 5000 NIS which exceeds the cost of planting 1 donum of land which is around 3000 NIS. • Many farmers have no or low access to credit as a coping mechanism for not paying their debts which endangers next season’s land planting. • The inability of farmers’ to hire laborers which increased family (women and children) labour in the land to reduce labour cost. This has contributed to reducing the seasonal agricultural workers employment opportunities which affects a large number of agricultural workers having no alternative work opportunities. This has further increased the school drop out due to work in the land and due to the financial burden of school.

2 - Activities

Activities focus on vulnerable farmers within a multi-layered and vulnerable context and take gender disparities into serious consideration. Support to land and irrigation systems will address agricultural land that has been affected either by abandonment or the GMR. Moreover, improving the perception of female work in the agriculture sector is a major focus of the activities to raise awareness of both men and women (especially young) working within the sector on labour divisions and agricultural rights of women. The project will also provide women with tailored CfW activities that provide them with cash through a gender sensitive approach. Activity 1: Beneficiary selection will take place through open applications in the communities targeted and cross-checking with MoA and the FSS. Activity 2: Rehabilitation of 300 agriculture dunums (field crops and intercropping vegetables) targeting small scale farmers (women & men) in East of Gaza , North Gaza and the middle area including provision of inputs for farmers and promotion of technical capacities. Outputs: 2.1 300 dunums rehabilitated (Field crops and inter cropping vegetables). 2.2 300 dunums planted, 2,000 vegetable seedlings per dunum. 2.3 organic fertilizers distributed for 300 dunums, 250 kg/dunum. 2.4 Metal fences installed for 300 dunums and Irrigation networks installed. Activity 3: Establishing 1500 m3 of water storage ponds to improve farmers’ (women & men) access to water in east of Gaza, North Gaza and the Middle Area. Output 3.1: 1500 m3 of Water storage ponds rehabilitated and constructed. Activity 4: Provision of necessary cash injections through Cash for Work targeting 170 agricultural workers 969 individuals (CFW (women & men)) to meet their basic food needs and capacity building for farmers Outputs: 4.1 170 farmers (40% women) engaged in CFW activities. Women for CfW will be selected based on the timing of the activity, length of working hours, and the skilled or unskilled schemes to ensure that the skills of women beneficiaries are being utilized and match the work required. (Age groups for men and women to be selected within the range of 18-49). 4.2 300 of women and men (between the ages of 20 49) participated in awareness raising on labour divisions and women rights. The project is planned to be implemented for one year that would be potential for extension. This year activities would focus on essential needs of farmers to restore and revive their livelihoods and protect them from increase of usage of negative coping s strategies. UAWC’s role will be liaising with the community and the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) in the beneficiary selection process that will involve conducting HH visits, coordinating the activities implementation, distribution of inputs to BNFs, and monitoring the day to day implementation on the ground. The list of beneficiaries will be partly provided by MoA through UAWC and the final eligible list will be cross checked with FSS to avoid duplication of assistance. Activity 5: Influencing activity that will include producing a policy paper looking into main factors causing food insecurity in the Gaza Strip, and the impact of Imposed Israeli measures. The policy paper will aim to demonstrate the fundamental contradiction that people in Gaza are unnecessarily food insecure due to a man-made crisis, despite living on very fertile land. Furthermore, the policy paper will be supported with case study to support the policy asks and recommendation. Output 5.1: Policy paper on food insecurity addressing the root causes

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The FCRM system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in-line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting, thus will not be recorded by the system. However, all received feedbacks and complaints will be reported and managed by using the ICT tool. It is worth mentioning minor and major issues will be addressed through programmatic actions including response to complaints providers. Field visits and satisfaction surveys will be capturing implementation and progress feedback rom beneficiaries which will allow for responsive and corrective actions whenever applicable. Moreover, feedback involves requests actions or changes that will not be feasible to accommodate in the project duration will be taken as lessons learnt for future projects.

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify

Feedback and Complaints Mechanisms and adequate information sharing: In September 2018, Oxfam launched its Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (FCRM) which provides wider access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive feedback either online, through verbal feedback or recording complaints received through other channels such as hotlines and feedback boxes. Oxfam and UAWC will establish the project feedback mechanism after consultation with targeted families/HHs to ensure use of most effective channels accessible by vulnerable groups. From previous experiences, it is expected that complaint boxes at distribution points and free hotlines will be the main tools for feedback collection. Media and communication tools will be used to raise the awareness of targeted groups towards safe and responsive feedback tools.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection of at risk and affected persons is one of the governing factors of Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions, informing humanitarian decision-making and response. It is central to preparedness efforts, as well as part of immediate and life-saving activities. Oxfam and UAWC will conduct risk analysis aiming to identify and mitigate any possible risk associated with the actions of the project , the good feedback and communication channels with the community will play a crucial role of the project action by modifying and improving the project action during the implementation Oxfam introduced a new guidance on safe programming which is implementing in all projects and programmes. the safe programming guidance include implementing measures that identifies, analyzes and mitigates risks along the project implementation. Oxfam carries out safe programming training with project partners to ensure joint and consistent safe programming along the project cycle.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Selection of beneficiaries will be carried out through open applications in the areas targeted to ensure registration of all potentially eligible farmers in the area in coordination with MoA. The HH application or assessment to inform selection will include livelihood vulnerability elements and HH vulnerability elements. Criteria measuring vulnerability of the livelihood will include parameters such as livelihood assets, potential, financial and social networks and accessible support, debt linked to livelihood, and the exposure of livelihood to physical threats. HH related vulnerability will include parameters related to the demographic information, members of the HH facing protection concerns such as illnesses, special needs, physical threats, sever coping strategies, accumulated debts, lack of access to basic needs. Oxfam and UAWC will announce the criteria for nomination and selection through community based organizations and committees. A feedback mechanism for selection of beneficiaries will be activated and made accessible and operationalized to ensure proper responses to beneficiaries on their inquiries and appeals in case of non-selection due to expected high interest and need.
