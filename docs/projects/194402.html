Project Summary

More than 3.5 million people, of whom 2.9 million are children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation and need critical child protection services. Child protection needs are highest in 65 counties, and high in severity in nine counties, including Tambura and Ezo. Beginning in late June 2021, sub-national violence in Tambura County, Western Equatoria displaced an estimated 119,000 people with reports of civilians killed and wounded. Many counties were affected by the violence, including Ezo where most of the IDPs have sought refuge.
CMMB is proposing to deliver comprehensive child protection and GBV services targeting 16000 (8000 for CP and 8000 for GBV) vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Tambura and Ezo Counties. The aim of this response is to reduce the suffering of boys, girls, women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk of experience violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect including GBV through provision of specialized/lifesaving child protection and multi sectoral services.
Activities include: identify and register unaccompanied and separated children; follow up on the reunification with the support of the cluster and other actors; establish Child Friendly Spaces as part of the psychosocial support for children including children living with disability; offer psychosocial support to unaccompanied and separated children as well as Sexual Gender Based Violence (SGBV) survivors; form a protection committee in the community and create protection awareness; train Health care providers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR); establish women friendly space (WFS); distribute dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age; strengthen existing referral pathways for GBV survivors; income Generating Activities (IGA) will be conducted for women, men, boys and girls on basic business skills to help enhance the coping mechanism. SGBV survivor will be supported through service accessibility and livelihood project.

1 - Needs

A total of 8.9 million women, men, girls and boys are expected to be in need in 2022 including 328,610 refugees across all of South Sudan’s 78 counties. This is an increase in absolute numbers from the 8.3 million people estimated to be in need in the 2021 as per the Humanitarian Needs Overview. Child protection needs are highest in country and high in severity in counties Particularly Tambura and Ezo in Western Equatoria. An estimated 2.6 million people risk facing gender-based violence in 2022, The increased risk of GBV and gaps in response services, as revealed by the assessments, highlight an urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and scaling up of specialized GBV response services. Specifically, there is a need to strengthen GBV case management, medical care, psychosocial support, safety and security, legal service, safe houses, livelihood, economic support, and GBV referral pathways. Primary GBV prevention interventions such as EMAP, SASA, and the Community Cares program, integration of GBV into other sectoral responses including safety audits, strengthening of GBV coordination, and provision of dignity kits are high priority interventions for the GBV Area of Responsibility.Sexual violence remains pervasive during conflict among rival communities. Violence in Tambura County of Western Equatoria in 2021 saw significant human rights violations and abuses, including targeting civilians based on ethnicity and gender. At least 440 people were killed according to the UNMISS Human Rights Division, and over 80,000 people were displaced by the Tambura conflict. Provision of both mobile and static structured community based psychosocial support services will be enhanced working closely with targeted communities and child protection networks to improve mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of children. Special attention and focus will be given to children living with disabilities to enable them access required care/services. Awareness raising will address prevailing protection issues and prevent/reduce risk of recruitment of children by armed groups and family separation. Child protection in emergencies training will build capacity of staff and community structures including prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. 10 CFS animators and volunteers (5 men and 5 women) will be equipped with relevant PSS knowledge and skills through training including GBV case management skills. Age and sex appropriate PSS activities will target 500 boys and 500 girls among IDPS across the two counties. The child protection team will work closely with the nutrition teams through nutrition centers to address nutrition needs as necessary. Adolescent peer to peer support groups will strengthen their coping skills towards life issues and build strong social networks reaching 200 youths (100 girls,100 boys). The UNICEF adolescent kits, life skills sessions including soft skills will be a key guide. A strengthened case management system will ensure vulnerable children receive specialized and individualized care and support to address their protection concerns based on individual needs and vulnerability including appropriate referrals. A total of 200 children (100 boys and 100 girls) will be targeted for comprehensive case management services out of which 40 extremely vulnerable children (20 boys and 20 girls) will be supported through cash-based assistance or nonfood items to meet identified critical protection needs. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

PSEA Focal Points will be identified and trained across project sites. They will conduct awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men of communities of concern ensuring that they are aware of their rights and entitlements through posters, videos, community meetings, focus group discussions and information pamphlets. Assessment and mapping out potential risk areas for children and recommending protective actions to combat SEA as well as having appropriate feedback mechanisms are key. All such complaints should be immediately reported to the Program Director by beneﬁciaries/survivors/PSEA focal points. Community feedback mechanisms will be enhanced through volunteers, meetings with children and community, focus group discussions, displaying available complaint options/suggestion boxes placed in strategic locations at the CFS.The project places emphasis on affected population’s ability to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Community child protection committees are empowered to enhance protection of affected and at-risk children working with caregivers. At risk populations have been identiﬁed, how and why they are at risk considering their speciﬁc vulnerabilities including those experienced by girls and boys and groups such as internally displaced persons, persons with disabilities and other minority groups. The project will endeavor to respond to their needs based on their level of vulnerability. The project identiﬁes and addresses barriers to access faced by various population groups. CFS are within safe and central locations to ensure children do not walk long distances nor get exposed to unsafe locations. Approved information sharing protocols e.g. CPIMS tools will minimize exposure of sensitive information. Limited access to project sites by children living with disabilities will be addressed through engaging community volunteers who might identify and refer such children. Adolescents will be supported to organize activities with children.
