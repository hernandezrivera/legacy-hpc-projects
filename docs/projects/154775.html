Project Summary

Iraq is experiencing ongoing displacement, with more than 450,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) in formal camp locations, and more than 120,000 in informal settlements and collective centres throughout the country. According to IOM Iraq Mission DMT documents a total of 1,866,594 internally displaced Persons (IDP) and 4,113,624 returnees, there has been mass return with over 4.1 million displaced Iraqis returning back to their areas of origin within Anbar, Ninewa and Salah al-Din governorate. This positive development has however been derailed by considerable gaps in accessibility of basic service. ROAR assessment of September 2018, conducted in Ninawa governorate shows that residence returning to Telafar city, Ba’aj and Sinjar towns are facing both protection concerns such as fear of attack by armed actors, disruption of social cohesion, explosive hazards, loss of civil documentation and inability to request or renew these documents and lack of livelihood opportunities.
REACH intentions survey conducted between December 2017 and January 2018 on National IDP camps highlighted the household profile in Najar, Ninewa and Salah-al-Din having high percentage of 18%, 14% and 19% respectively of widows and also having higher percentage of People Living with Disability with 20%, 26% and 28%. These target groups are usually highly exposed to gender based violence and other protection concerns, they therefore need specialized protection and assistance for safe and dignified returns. In addition, as a result of increased vulnerability and depletion of resources available, returnees may adopt other negative coping mechanism such as transactional sex, survival sex, trafficking, forced marriage and forced labor in order to provide for their families. Moreover, lack of basic necessities, access to services limited resources and poor economic environment due to mines, abandoned homes; people are forced into other coping mechanisms such as eating less or poor quality food, drinking unsanitary water, selling any remaining personal assets and discontinuing the use of essential daily items, this further exposes Person of Concern to economic, physical and sexual exploitation and abuse.
The war has impacted Ninewa, most especially Telafar district area thereby living persons of concern especially women and girls are highly exposed to gender based violence and other protection concerns; they therefore need specialized protection and assistance for safe and dignified returns. In addition, as a result of increased vulnerability and depletion of resources available, returnees may adopt other negative coping mechanism such as transactional sex, survival sex, and trafficking, forced marriage. There is need therefore to scale up the preventive, responsive, & remedial GBV interventions that are geared towards addressing the both the immediate and long-term needs of survivors while also addressing the root contributing factors of GBV that relates to gender inequalities and power relations. Harikar will establish one community centre to provide comprehensive and timely case management to survivors of GBV and also conduct GBV prevention and awareness through male engagement, monthly safety audits, service mapping and focused groups and monthly feedback mechanism. To facilitate the rebuilding of lives and militate against further risk of exploitation and abuse, Harikar, will support in the formation of livelihoods more so for women is of paramount importance, which currently is at a very minimal level. Harikar will therefore work closely with protection partners to provide life saving, timely services to the most at risks and vulnerable persons of concerns in Telafar and its environs.

Mitigation Measures

1. Conduct community sensitive outreach and community mobilization throughout the project. Engage on regular basis with Community leaders; engage with men and with boys for greater acceptance of the project allowing for participation of women. Negotiate with families to allow women's participation in the project activities, services.2. Harikar will actively sensitize the entire community on their key priority responsibility of responding and prevention of GBV. Most at risks persons of concerns will be linked to recreational activities and other referred to livelihood for self reliance. Harikar, will continue to have a complaint's mechanisms and will conduct regular beneficiary satisfaction surveys in order to continuously monitor and ensure quality of services and community receptive to the services3. Harikar and the parteners will reduce this risk by aligning its security assessments with other international and local organisations operating in Iraq, including the International NGO Safety Organization (INSO).

How is your project accountable to affected population

At every stage of this project, Harikar seeks to ensure GBV response is life saving, timely and responsive to the needs, rights and dignity of survivors and those at risks by involving all the beneficiaries in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation for maximum and tangible impact. Harikar activities will be inclusive of Age Gender and Diversity, thereby working closely with men and women, community leaders, people living with disability, youths, minority clans and religion through enhanced coordination and referral mechanism with other protection actors. Harikar will also ensure all staffs are capacity built in relevant topics for effective GBV programming such as case management skills, engaging men through accountable practices (EMAP), caring for child survivors, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and codes of conducts and the information management system among related topics to equipped them with the necessary skills and provide appropriate support to beneficiaries. Harikar will periodically conduct feedback sessions with randomly selected community members in the form of Focused group discussions, listening sessions and set up client feedback mechanism at the community centres in order to receive timely, direct and open feedback on effectiveness of services being offered. In additional, Harikar will work closely with all protection coordinating partners to enhance multi-sectoral spirit and sharing of information during monthly coordination meetings and any adhoc meetings called for.
