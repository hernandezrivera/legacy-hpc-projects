Project Summary

The project goal is to enable children affected by conflict and displacement in Rural Damascus and Dara’a governorates to meet their basic needs and benefit from community-focused initiatives, through a multi-sectoral approach.
Terre des hommes - Lausanne, Switzerland (Tdh) will contribute to this objective through the direct provision of an integrated package of public services oriented assistance to building resilience of children and communities, especially conflict-affected children (vulnerable returnees, host, displaced and non-displaced households living out of camps) in four communities in Rural Damascus as well as Dara’a governorate.
The project revolves around two complementary and integrated set of activities:
1. WASH in schools. This includes rehabilitation and repairs of 4 educational facilities as well as the provision of furniture and equipment.
2. A comprehensive package of in-school as well as community-focused interventions benefitting grade 1-6 students - vulnerable and Out-Of-School Children (OSSCs):
• Recreational and psycho-social (PSS) activities
• Non-Formal Education (NFE) and Back-to-Learning (BLT) initiatives
The project will also support training of teachers as well as the Parents and Teachers Associations (PTAs) through sustainable project awarding. The latter includes community outreach activities for OOSCs with a specific focus on increasing income generation as well as the availability of food production and in-take for children enrolled in grade 1-6.
Two sub-districts have already been identified and directly accessed by Tdh - Lausanne, namely Duma and Markaz Daraya. The project aims to further expand in other sub-districts such as Madaya, Zabadani, Rehiba, etc. A close and agile coordination with Education, WASH and Food Security partners is envisaged at sectorial and geographical level in order to maximise impact and collective outcomes while avoid over-lapping.
In 2022, the proposed intervention will target 5,000 children and 160 teachers by adopting an area-based and integrated approach addressing Education and Child Protection immediate needs through increased access to and availability of water, educational and child protection services provided to communities.
In 2023, the proposed intervention will be scaled up and replicated in Rural Damascus and in Dara’a - one additional governorate for a total of 10 target areas.

Implementing Partners

The project will be implemented directly by Tdh – Lausanne. Nevertheless, Syria Trust for Development (STD) as well as national and local authorities, will be key partners in relation to access-related issues. .
Emergency construction and rehabilitation-related activities will be managed and supervised by local contractors and monitored by Tdh. Local private contractors will not carry responsibility for programmatic decision-making.
In order to guarantee sustainability, ownership and quality control-, community-based organisations will be involved in every step of the project - whenever possible and applicable.

4 - Needs

Dates of the Tdh assessments: September 2021 and January 2022 Tdh assessment methodology involves a mix of needs assessment conducted formally and informally and through the review of secondary data. Primary data: Interviews with local key factors such as authorities, community members and local civil society members, Focus groups; Rapid technical assessment of the physical and social conditions on the ground, through Tdh staff and community-based organisations/civil society groups; Participation in the Damascus- based International Non-Governmental Organisation (DINGO) forum, the coordination platform for INGOs working in Syria; The INGO sectoral working and thematic groups; Bilateral and multi-lateral coordination with other INGOs based in Damascus. Secondary Data: Syria Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment (OCHA, REACH); Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (OCHA); Population and spontaneous return trends (OCHA); key Findings from Whole of Syria Protection Sector Key Informants Assessment Nov 2021. As per wider sector needs analysis, the schools and communities assessed by Tdh in Rural Damascus (see comments section for further details) confirm the following needs: - School Rehabilitation (electricity to operate water pumps and physical protection measures) - Provision of furniture, equipment and material - Lack of standard sanitation facilities - Lack of drinkable water availability - Lack of a conducive learning environment (absence of recreational and PSS opportunities)

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities will be implemented directly by Tdh. Tdh staff will design, implement and monitor recreational and PSS-related trainings to school personnel while supporting and supervising their execution within the educational facilities.Tdh staff will also provide school material and furniture while the infrastructure works will be completed by private sector contractors. The following is a step-by-step summary of the process for the hardware (school general repairs): - Exisiting Tdh's preliminary assessments and coordination indicate school and community needs in each site - Preliminary designs are developed in collaboration with representatives from the authorities and local communities - Consultative process is completed - Distribution lists and detailed technical designs are finalised in consultation with local authorities and communities - Submission of distribution lists, technical drawings, BoQs, Scopes of Work, Operation and Maintenance Plans - Local Private sector contractors are appointed based on TdH's agreed procurement processes - The works are implemented by local private sector contractors; non-skilled labor are drawn from local populations and in accordance with Tdh's CoC, and accompanied by robust monitoring. - Tdh construction monitoring visits to check progress against work plans and certify completion against the designs and BoQs. - Tdh performs after- distribution/completion/installation monitoring.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The main risk identified is that population, including victims, do not report allegations of SEA, which results in the organisation being unable to refer the allegation to investigation, hold personnel to account and assist victims. Therefore, Tdh will regularly train staff on PSEA policy and will sensitise community and relevant stakeholders about PSEA and how to respond to misconduct. Additionally –and in also in accordance to do-ho-harm principles-, Tdh will ensure that monitoring and coordination mechanisms with beneficiaries, civil society members and local authorities are in place and abide by the AAP framework. A specific complaint and feedback response mechanism will be implemented by also identifying a dedicated PSEA Focal point. In accordance with the facilities and centers where the project will be implemented, information and awareness material will be provided and displayed. The project will ensure that no individual can use the equipment and material as a mean of pressure or exploitation against beneficiaries, especially children, girls and women. Therefore, every recipient will be identified and final destination of the equipment will be clearly stated in the donation document and presented to the different stakeholders.
