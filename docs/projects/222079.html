Project Summary

South Sudan is grappling with a critical education crisis, driven by ongoing conflict, economic instability, and climate-induced shocks that disrupt learning opportunities. According to the 2024 humanitarian needs assessment, over two million children, including displaced persons and returnees, face significant challenges such as destroyed schools, displacement, and insufficient learning resources. The crisis is exacerbated by a severe shortage of qualified teachers, high pupil-to-teacher ratios (93:1), and inadequate infrastructure that fails to support gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive learning environments.
In response, the proposed project seeks to provide safe, inclusive, and quality education for 14,922(7,416male, 7506female) at a per capita cost of $42.89. The initiative includes training 320 teachers (225 men and 95 women) and 187 stakeholders (92 men and 92 women) to promote inclusive education. Additionally, community awareness campaigns will mobilize school enrolment, particularly for marginalized children.
A key component of the project is addressing limited access to education caused by insufficient infrastructure and barriers to enrolment in conflict-affected areas. To tackle these challenges, the project will mobilize communities through awareness campaigns that encourage enrolment, especially for girls and children with disabilities.
The project also emphasizes the provision of gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive infrastructure. Currently, the lack of safe learning spaces and inadequate WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) facilities hinders access to education. The proposed solution involves constructing five gender-sensitive, disability-inclusive WASH facilities, including latrines, handwashing stations, and girls’ washrooms. Additionally, assistive devices and sanitation kits will be provided to support hygiene and dignity.
To improve teaching and learning quality, the project will address the acute shortage of trained teachers and inadequate learning materials. Teachers will receive training on Education in Emergencies (EiE), inclusion and psychosocial support to help children cope with trauma. Essential learning materials will be distributed to 13,940 most vulnerable learners, aiming to improve the learner retention and completion.
Recognizing the high dropout rates among adolescent girls due to unmet menstrual hygiene needs, the project will distribute dignity kits to enhance menstrual hygiene management and support school attendance. Child safeguarding systems will also be strengthened, and stakeholders will be trained on gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation to ensure dignity and protection for vulnerable groups.
Limited community ownership and weak governance structures hinder effective education delivery. The project plans to strengthen 220 (120male, 100female)Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) and Ministry of General Education and Instruction to enhance governance, community involvement, ownership and sustainable education service delivery.
The anticipated impact of the project includes increased resilience in South Sudan’s education sector by enabling 14,922 children to access safe, quality education and providing targeted support to marginalized groups. Enhanced learning environments, teacher training, and stakeholder engagement are expected to foster long-term educational outcomes. These efforts will contribute to national goals of inclusivity, gender equality, and child protection.
This cost-effective approach ensures that every dollar directly benefits South Sudan’s most vulnerable children. The total project budget is $640,069
Finally, the project aligns with the 2025 Humanitarian Response Plan by addressing critical gaps in education and contributing to broader education outcomes. By prioritizing accountability, gender inclusivity, and protection mechanisms, the project ensures sustainability and long-term impact.

1. Needs

South Sudan faces severe education sector challenges, worsened by ongoing conflict, economic instability, and climate-related disasters. The 2025 needs assessment highlights critical gaps requiring urgent attention to ensure vulnerable children can access quality education.Approximately 2 million children, including displaced persons and returnees, urgently need education support due to barriers such as school destruction, insecurity, and displacement. Many children in conflict-affected areas are unable to attend school due to the lack of functioning infrastructure and the impact of ongoing violence, especially in remote regions.South Sudan faces a severe shortage of qualified teachers. About one-third of teachers lack formal training, resulting in a pupil-to-teacher ratio of 93:1, far above global standards. This shortage reduces education quality as teachers are overburdened and ill-equipped to manage classrooms effectively or provide sufficient learning support.The availability of learning materials is inadequate, with a textbook-to-student ratio of 8:1 instead of the global standard of 1:1. This shortage limits students' engagement with the curriculum, affecting their academic progress and overall learning outcomes.Schools require gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive facilities to ensure safe, equitable, and dignified learning spaces. Children with disabilities face major barriers, including inadequate infrastructure and a lack of trained teachers to support them. Segregated WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) facilities are essential to ensure privacy and safety, particularly for adolescent girls.Insufficient teaching incentives and emergency measures strain the education system. Support is required to maintain educational continuity amid crises. Psychosocial support and mental health services are vital for children affected by violence, displacement, and trauma, helping them recover emotionally and continue their education.The education response strategy will prioritize providing safe and inclusive learning spaces for 14,922 children at a cost of $42.89 per child. Teacher training, provision of essential learning materials, and support for marginalized groups are key components. The strategy also includes engaging 843 teachers (467 male, 376 female) and 187 stakeholders (92 male, 92 female) in capacity building, coordination, and promoting inclusive education in five counties. These efforts will require strong coordination between the government, humanitarian agencies, and donors to ensure a more resilient education system in South Sudan by 2025.The project aims to enhance resilience in South Sudan’s education sector, enabling 14,922 children to access safe, quality education. It will promote inclusivity by supporting marginalized groups and strengthening learning environments, teacher capacity, and stakeholder engagement. These efforts align with national goals of inclusivity, gender equality, and child protection, fostering long-term educational outcomes.This cost-effective approach ensures that every dollar directly benefits the most vulnerable children. The total project budget is $640,069, aimed at maximizing the impact of each dollar spent on improving education access and quality.The project aligns with the 2025 HRP, addressing critical gaps in education while contributing to broader education outcomes. By prioritizing accountability, gender inclusivity, and protection mechanisms, the project supports sustainable and lasting impact for children across South Sudan.
