Project Summary

Data from the NWSW regional delegations of basic and secondary education for 2022/2023 revealed that a total of 4,195 schools are operational, out of the expected 6,557 schools, leaving a total 2,365 schools (36 per cent) still not operational. This is an improvement over 18 per cent compared to the preceding school year where 54 per cent of schools were still non-functional. However, this situation continues to deprive around 298,000 children of access to education.
This increase in the number of formal schools open confirms the gradual improvement in the security situation in certain areas, the interest of communities in sending their children to school and the growing needs. A total of 632,457 pupils (54 per cent girls) are enrolled out of the 930,335 expected, and only 2 per cent of the pupils enrolled for the 2022/2023 school year were children living with a disability. At least one inclusive primary school has been opened in each division, but there is still a lack of material, financial and technical resources available to school communities and education actors. Other children are left behind simply because they live with a disability and either their parents do not want to send them to school, or the available schools are not prepared to receive them.
Despite continued attacks, there has been a comparative improvement from the previous years in terms of access to formal education within the Northwest and Southwest regions. However, the risks for children, youth and education personnel remains high, especially in rural areas and zones under full control of the NSAG. The only possible opportunity for these children remains the non-formal education.
To address this, UNICEF has been supporting 60 communities in eleven divisions where schools are closed to implement a non-formal radio education program easily accessible in rural areas that have poor internet and cellular connectivity and has provided learning continuity while reducing the risk of attacks in communities.
The overall objective of the project is to facilitate access to learning opportunities as well as the protective learning environment for crises-affected children in and out of schools including IDPs children in the Northwest and Southwest regions.
The aim of the project is to meet the educational needs of 120,000 out-of-school children who are unable to attend school because of the risks involved, but also because schools in their area have been closed for several years due to the crisis. The project will have the advantage of taking a longer-term view, to ensure the link between non-formal and formal education, so that when the security situation allows, children who have participated in the radio education program can enroll in formal schools in the higher grades. 600 teachers will equally benefit from the training in Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Conflict/Disaster Risk Reduction (CDRR) to supervise 36.000 children from targeted schools.
This project will help provide a child-friendly and child-led space, offering a variety of activities for children and youth. The learning spaces will be gender-sensitive and inclusive, where children and young people will feel they can learn and express their opinions. This will help to strengthen links with their community and improve their self-confidence and the development of their cognitive skills. The aim is for children to develop a better sense of self-esteem and self-confidence to improve their social and emotional well-being.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

This is an improvement over 18 per cent compared to the preceding school year where 54 per cent of schools were still non-functional. However, the risks for children, youth and education personnel remains high, especially in rural areas and zones under full control of the NSAG. The only possible opportunity for these children remains the non-formal education. To address this, UNICEF has been supporting 60 communities in eleven divisions where schools are closed to implement a non-formal radio education program easily accessible in rural areas that have poor internet and cellular connectivity and has provided learning continuity while reducing the risk of attacks in communities. Education was reported to be the second priority humanitarian need in the North-West and South-West regions, and the first in the neighboring West and Littoral regions which hosts the displaced communities. These regions, as well as the urban areas of the North-West and South-West regions, host the largest number of displaced pupils, coming from the areas most affected by the crisis and where schools are not operational for fear of safety of children, teachers, and other education personnel. Most parents who cannot afford to pay the cost of resettlement prefer to stay in rural areas while sending their children to urban areas so that they can have access to formal education. However, due to the limited resources they must provide for their children's education, most of them end up dropping out of school, which increases their level of exposure to protection risks. In rural areas without functional schools, some children take part in some of the opportunities offered to them, but with great needs and insufficient resources, most children remain without any support. Most of the children who have been out of school for over 7 years are over the required school age, which is a major obstacle to their access to formal education, and the non-formal education opportunities available cannot facilitate the integration of these children into formal education. Training has been provided for teachers to enable them to rise to the task of looking after children while strengthening their resilience, but given the magnitude of the needs, the small number of trained teachers is not enough to fill all the gaps.
