Project Summary

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, ACTED will utilize in-kind and cash-based modalities to cover SNFI gaps for a total of 59,365 individuals from IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and people with special needs (PSNs). The activities will include distribution of in-kind NFI and shelter kits, cash/voucher assistance for emergency shelter and NFIs, cash for work activities for shelter construction, and construction of communal shelters to respond to large scale emergency influx of displaced populations namely in Renk and Aweil Counties, supporting households located in sporadic informal sites and settlements in peri-urban settings.
The interventions will contribute to the following cluster objectives:
1. Ensure provision of timely and adequate access to safe and dignified emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities.
2. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable returnee, and protracted IDPs and host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs.
3. Support the most vulnerable returnees, host communities and IDPs access adequate durable housing options that will enable them self-recover and build resilience of affected population to recover from shocks.
Interventions will also include community engagement, and engagement with local authorities to ensure community-wide buy in and support for activities. Moreover, ACTED will work in close coordination with resident communities, humanitarian partners and agencies, RRC and local authorities to ensure any temporary or semi-permanent shelter solutions adhere to HLP standards and are accepted and permitted by the relevant stakeholders to ensure a durable, safe and equitable response.

1. Needs

South Sudan is characterized by long-lasting conflicts, economic instability and frequent and increasing climate change-related hazards/shocks, leading to chronic food insecurity, disruptions of livelihoods and displacements. The latest Integrated Rapid Needs Assessment produced by SSHF stresses the enormous needs of 4.2 million people for emergency shelter and non-food items assistance (with 1.37 million prioritized) with over 700,000 returnees who have displaced to South Sudan as a result of the Sudan crisis. With further influx projected into 2025, this figure will continue to increase. Moreover, inter-communal violence, poverty, food insecurity or climate-change, every year tens of thousands of people are forcibly displaced, leaving behind their home and belongings. This situation exacerbates the vulnerability for women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities: in 2024, 1.9 million were IDPs, more than 700,000 returnees and 1 million host communities, with 13% persons with disabilities and 50% of females. Thus, people urgently need the support from humanitarian actors to provide a safe environment and to prevent the further deterioration of their living conditions and exposure to protection risks and food insecurity. Needs vary significantly depending on the location, availability of resources and proximity to a conflict zone, which can generate a ripple effect: such as damage to natural resources used for construction work such as timber and grass, damage to roads and bridges, jeopardizing humanitarian accessibility and leading to delays in the provision of emergency assistance etc. Hence, the limited resources and precarity of the situation may cause competition between communities and ultimately conflict, precipitating further crises and displacements. Figures for internally displaced peoples have been steadily increasing over the past years as environmental shocks and political instability continue to worsen. Therefore, in 2024, ACTED will continue implementing S/NFI interventions in line with the following Cluster’s objectives and geographical prioritization. In 2025, Acted will support at least 59,365 persons, out of the 1.37 million people targeted by the SNFI Cluster. Therefore, Acted will align its interventions and activities with the following Cluster's goals for 2024: Ensure provision of timely and adequate access to safe and dignified emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced people or populations with new vulnerabilities. Improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees, and the host communities who are unable to meet their SNFI needs. Support the most vulnerable returnees, host communities and IDPs access adequate durable housing options that will enable them self-recover and build resilience of affected population to recover from shocks.

2. Project activities

In line with the SNFI cluster strategy, ACTED will support coverage of SNFI gaps for a total of 59,365 individuals from IDPs, returnees, and host communities, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups and PSNs. This will be done in areas where ACTED has a static presence and through mobile interventions in areas of displacement, targeting highly vulnerable returnee, IDP and HC largely residing in makeshift shelters in surrounding urban and peri-urban areas. ACTED has an established emergency response mechanism to ensure a timely response and effective coordination as part of its mobile responses which include SNFI, WASH, CCCM and protection components. A shock is flagged through clusters, working groups, ICCGs, and/or humanitarian coordination fora. Tools such as site profiles and site monitoring matrix 3Ws are shared with partners. In line with pipeline availability, ACTED will immediately launch a multisectoral response as per the identified needs of the targeted communities. The intervention duration will usually last between 4-6 weeks. ACTED will coordinate with all the clusters, working groups and humanitarian coordination fora as well as reaching out to ECHO and RRM partners to avoid duplication. ACTED will also coordinate with RRC, local partners, mobile partners, and cluster state focal points. In addition to activities surrounding service monitoring, ACTED will train local partners on coordination mechanisms to avoid duplication of response. Apprx. 2-3 months after an intervention is complete, ACTED will return to the location to conduct a post intervention/distribution monitoring and will share the results with the relevant clusters. The activities for mobile and static will include without being restricted to: distribution of Shelter kits and Emergency Shelter Kits, NFI kits, and cash for work activities for shelter construction/repairs. Cash for work activities will be conducted following market assessments and in coordination with relevant area cash working groups and the national cash working group. ACTED mobile responses will entail service monitoring and will, where possible, establish an advisable pathway through which community can make referrals to mitigate cases, HLP issues, and legal assistance. ACTED will prioritize to minimize any HLP issues arising from its shelter interventions, including eviction. At the start of each intervention, ACTED will conduct FGDs and KIIs with boma chiefs and local authorities to discuss any issues related to HLP. Moreover, the assessment period looks at HLP issues and informs the design of the intervention.
