Project Summary

The project activities and objectives are aligned with the Country and Cluster objectives. Cluster Objective 1: Fulfilment of fundamental human right to water by ensuring equitable, sufficient and sustainable access to basic quality WASH services, available when needed in accordance with safety and dignity of the unserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank. The project aims to ensure access to basic WASH services and avoid displacement of water-scarce communities in the West Bank and Strengthen their WASH preparedness and response capacity. The construction and rehabilitation of water networks and transmission pipeline will reduce water leakages and improve the water pressure, thereby increasing the quantity of water delivered to households which will improve their access to safe and clean water.
The project will target the most vulnerable communities in Area C with humanitarian interventions aimed at alleviating the challenging living conditions of the target population. These vulnerable communities suffer from limited access to water services due to the Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area. The proposed interventions will target unserved and underserved communities with essential WASH services, that will also protect the most vulnerable families under threat of forcible transfer.
The main targeted areas of the project are the farming and herding families who depend on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods. The most urgent need for these communities is the lack of access to safe water, at the required quality, availability, and affordability at the household level. Families have developed coping mechanisms to respond to the limited access to water - mainly through rainwater harvesting and the collection in cisterns to deal with the water shortages they are suffering from. This has also forced families to reduce their water consumption to less than 30 l/c/d and to reduce their expenditure on basic needs like health and education to cover high water costs.
The intervention will also target unconnected households in Area C communities to increase their capacity to treat and safely store water at the household level and to increase the quality of water consumed. This will be ensured through the installation of water filters; hygiene practices will also be promoted and improved through awareness sessions especially for those practicing open defecation.
• The project will target 8,991 people (2,043 women, 2,151 men, 2,285 girls, 2,405 boys, 52 elderly women, and 55 elderly men) in the humanitarian situation have improved/increased access to drinking water of at least 30 l/c/d of high-quality water for a period of 3 months.
• 1,460 students (748 girls, 712 boys) have improved/increased their access to drinking water of at least 5 l/c/d of high-quality water for a period of 3 months.
• 1,153 people (262 women, 276 men, 293 girls, 308 boys, 7 elderly women, 7 elderly men) in the humanitarian situation have improved their access to drinking water through safe drinking water through HHs connections.

Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2018, 156,000 people across the WB are either unconnected to a water network or receive water only once a week or less. Area C communities in the WB (especially in Southern Hebron, East Jerusalem periphery and the Jordan valley) are affected by this water problem. Around 270,000 Palestinians in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure. The assessments conducted by the UNICEF led WASH Cluster, and in particular by Action Against Hunger (AAH) and GVC in 2017, led to the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) approved Water Master Plan for Area C. This Water Master Plan for Area C details the water needs of 247 communities living in Area C, indicating that most of the targeted communities consume less than 30 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms for the water shortage. Further assessments conduct by AAH during June and August 2018 in Area C indicated that water scarcity is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. This is considered a context in which urgent humanitarian action is required to remedy, mitigate or avert an immediate and direct humanitarian risk. A further assessment conducted by GVC in March 2018, show that 51 communities in the WB depend on the purchase of water (main source for 90% of people), have a daily per capita consumption below 60 liters, and rely exclusively on water trucking for 84% of the summer period. Over 90% of them are in Area C where access constraints pose challenges for the trucks to reach remote localities. This has increased in the cost of water, up to 50 NIS/m³, depending on the location of the community and the season as well as the risks to truck damage and confiscation. Constrained access to drinking water affects children, elders and people with disabilities, who are most vulnerable, as well as women and girls in three different dimensions: 1) Quality of life due to coercive access to drinking water and and access to hygiene materials and safe menstrual hygiene management; 2) Feeling of Protection, due to long distance to fetch water exposing to settlers and IF violence; 3) Division of duties, due to the fact that the no access to safe water affects the roles of women, children and men reducing the positive effect of the division of labour between family members. All family members in the target communities are considered vulnerable to water scarcity, but it is a different burden for the women in herding communities who are responsible for water related issues in terms of health, hygiene and the management of daily household water needs. The difficult living conditions in Area C make women’s water management duties even more demanding.Moreover, access to adequate sanitation and personal hygiene is a concern in communities facing water shortages in WB. A water quality surveillance undertaken in 2018 by CESVI proved that–regardless water source and quality at the point of delivery–water for human consumption is contaminated in intervention due to cross-contamination during collection and storage. children under 5 are vulnerable to waterborne diseases, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding women who could expose the fetus to impair growth or contribute to stunting.In September-October 2017, 175 households were assessed in Area C of Hebron governorate. The assessment confirmed that 14.2% of people had no latrines, 64.1% very basic latrines. 69.3% of the people reported not to have hand-washing in the toilet, confirming a hygienic risk for public health. It is assumed that the practice of Open Defecation is common for 10% of the population, who do not have a connection to the piped water network and are in isolated rural areas, an assumption confirmed by WASH Cluster partners active in this location.

Activities

Under UNICEF’s overall leadership and in coordination with implementing partners as well as WASH Sector partners including PWA, WBWD, and local water providers, this project will comprehensively and sustainably support local water authorities to address the water supply needs of vulnerable Palestinian hhs through the complementary set of activities detailed below. UNICEF will facilitate coordination meetings with PWA, MoE, as well other international agencies to coordinate the activities and locations to avoid gaps and overlapping. UNICEF with partners have had joint coordination meetings to ensure and enhance synergies and programmatic coherence. Municipalities and village councils in the targeted locations have been identified as local partners responsible for collection of date related to the baseline and end line. UNICEF will also lead the advocacy efforts with the key stakeholders from the Palestinian and Israeli Authorities as well as the International Community, linked with the provision of the following life-saving WASH interventions in Area C: 1.Rehabilitation/construction of water networks for communities: Construction of pipelines and filling points for Bedouin and herding communities according to Area C Water Master Plan. 12 communities will have access to water though construction and rehabilitation of transmission pipelines and networks; this will reduce HHs dependency on expensive tankered water and poor water quality in cisterns and enhance their ownership. The installation will be implemented by contractors or/and village council members and beneficiaries, and will build upon their knowledge of the area and of the potential risks involved. 2.Construction/rehabilitation of hh cisterns for vulnerable families, with special attention to female-headed hhs and families with disabled family members in Area C and seam zones: 15 household cisterns will be constructed in Hebron governorate as per Master plan. A socio-economic baseline survey will be conducted includingfemale headed hhs and families with disabled members. The activities will be implemented by HHs to reduce the Area C construction associated risks. this will include chlorination campaign and distribution of chlorine tablets to the community to improve the quality of new storage capacity. 3. Improving access to safe drinking water through water trucking and development of national/regional/local governance mechanisms. This will prevent forcible transfer of targeted communities by improving their access to WASH services with an integrated protection approach by working at three levels: i. distributing trucked water; ii. developing capacities of water distribution chain stakeholder at local, regional and national level; and iii. development of the governance system to manage water distribution. The project will increase the conditions of unserved HHs, targeting 8,991 people and 1,460 students in 51 communities and 10 schools for 3 months. 4. Improving access to adequate sanitation and safe hh/personal hygiene: This will include: 1. Conducting baseline assessment to evaluate the access to WASH, especially those potentially at risk of open defecation and poor hygienic conditions including women and children; 2. Installing 400 hhs with Gravity Driven Membrane (GDM) technology to minimize the risk of cross contamination before consumption and training in 400 hh including women; 3. Collecting and analyzing water samples in hhs from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use (at the cup), and analyzing samples to assess water quality per biological and physical parameters; 4. Promoting hygiene and sanitation practices with kit distribution, promotional activities with a behavior change approach (RANAS) will be carried out in a culturally appropriate manner to analyze and tackle open defecation and practices contaminating water. sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers, children (5 18Y)

Indicators

The project activities will be continuously monitored by UNICEF and its implementing partners to ensure that the project is delivered on time and on budget. The project will also be monitored and evaluated within the UNICEF Integrated Monitoring and Evaluation Plan applied across all UNICEF programs. Project implementation will be reviewed as part of the Annual Programme Review jointly conducted by UNICEF and project implementing partners. Regular field visits to the locations and quarterly progress reports produced by partners and submitted to UNICEF constitute an integral part of the project monitoring mechanism.UNICEF programme officers will conduct regular field visits to the project sites as well as review progress reports from implementing partners. Quality checklists will be used to systematically ensure quality of the interventions during Programmatic visits. Staff will monitor progress against agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. The programmatic visits will also assess bottlenecks and barriers, challenges and lessons learned. This is in accordance with UNICEF’s requirement for the Harmonized Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT). Financial spot checks will also be conducted in line with HACT requirements. The findings of the monitoring visits will be the basis for adjusting the level of technical / administrative engagement to ensure optimal achievement of the project. UNICEF will have regular meetings with the main stakeholders (such as PWA and the WBWD), beneficiary communities and implementing partners to discuss the findings of field monitoring and adopt solutions in case of constraints. The implementing partners will establish and maintain a system for monitoring progress of the implementation of their specific Project Document using the defined results, including outcomes, outputs, indicators and targets set out in the logical framework. Indicator 3.2 (Activity 3.2): National/regional/local governance mechanisms strengthened to respond to water access/scarcity emergencies.
