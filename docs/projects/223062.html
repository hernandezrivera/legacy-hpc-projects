Project Summary

CARE is dedicated to enhancing the living conditions of refugees and host communities in Camps 13 and 16. Our strategy prioritizes community participation and ownership, improves accountability mechanisms, and mitigates safety risks, all while ensuring consistent access to essential humanitarian assistance and protection services. We focus on the most vulnerable households, specifically women-headed families and individuals with disabilities, to ensure they have easy and equitable access to our services.
Given the pressing need for disaster management and risk mitigation, our project will implement initiatives to strengthen shelters and develop camp infrastructure. These efforts address the risks posed by cyclones and the monsoon season. We will work closely with the Site Coordination and Camp Management (SCCCM) Sector, the Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner (RRRC), and the Camp-in-Charge (CiC), facilitating comprehensive service delivery and information exchange among camp actors to identify and bridge critical gaps.
Key components of the project include shelter support and site development activities aimed at fostering community resilience. We will actively engage youth and women throughout the project, ensuring meaningful involvement and empowerment. Our integrated programming model brings together our Shelter & Site Development, Resilience, and Gender Protection teams to deliver holistic support to both refugee and host communities. By prioritizing protection mainstreaming and adopting a gender-sensitive approach, we place a special emphasis on involving women and girls, strengthening social cohesion, and ensuring that women's voices are integral to the project’s success. Additionally, we will include women in Cash-for-Work opportunities, paired with skill development and awareness initiatives to empower them economically.
The project will address critical gaps in the early warning system and enhance the affected community’s capacity for emergency preparedness and response. Collaborating with the SMS agency, we will improve early warning systems to ensure timely alerts for cyclones, heavy rain, and landslides. By equipping the community with essential materials and knowledge, we aim to bolster their disaster management capabilities, fostering greater resilience. Risk mitigation will also involve sustainable construction projects, including reinforcing access pathways and stabilizing slopes.
To further address community needs, we will standardize and improve cooking areas, benefiting women who typically manage kitchen activities. Enhanced cooking facilities are expected to positively impact women's mental health and overall well-being.
Lastly, the project will focus on repairing and reinforcing shelters to make them more resilient and hazard-proof, ensuring a safer living environment for all residents.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Women in the camps face significant barriers to participating in Cash-for-Work programs, primarily due to social norms that limit their mobility and economic participation (Ref: Assessment of Economic Opportunities for Young Rohingyas in Bangladesh by Population Council). This project will focus on engaging women in CARE’s Cash-for-Work activities, promoting their active involvement in field operations. The camps are also located in high-risk areas prone to various hazards, further increasing the community’s vulnerability. The (ISNA) identified key gaps, including concerns raised by 4.7% of the community about inadequate or non-functional cooking areas in their shelters. Limited cooking equipment and overcrowded spaces force families to share resources, leading to delays in meal preparation. To address this, the project will coordinate with the RRRC, SMSA, and other shelter stakeholders to identify and implement effective solutions. Additionally, some community expressed safety concerns due to the use of substandard shelter materials, which are vulnerable to damage from natural disasters. These challenges highlight a broader coordination gap in meeting the community's needs, particularly among vulnerable groups such as women and children. To bridge this gap, effective service monitoring and a more inclusive approach are essential, ensuring that interventions are responsive and tailored to the specific needs of these populations. Greater participation from these vulnerable groups in decision-making processes is critical to ensure that interventions are sustainable, relevant, and impactful.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is dedicated to integrating protection principles into every activity and service provided to the affected community. By establishing effective monitoring mechanisms and functional referral pathways, we aim to ensure that protection is not only prioritized but systematically incorporated into all aspects of our interventions.Protection mainstreaming involves embedding protective measures into all programming areas, addressing the unique vulnerabilities and risks faced by community members, particularly women, children, and individuals with disabilities. This project will adopt a holistic approach to mainstreaming protection, ensuring that every service offered contributes to the overall protection of the community. Moreover, the project will adhere to minimum Sphere standards, which provide internationally recognized guidelines for humanitarian response. By following these standards, we ensure that our interventions meet the basic needs of the affected community in a manner that is safe, dignified, and respectful.Protection principles will be followed while initiating any distribution or selection and implementation of Site Development activities.In summary, this project’s commitment to mainstreaming protection principles across all activities and services is essential for fostering a safe and supportive environment for the affected community.

2.3 - Age & Disability

This project is designed to address the diverse needs of various age groups, including youth, adolescents, and the elderly, ensuring that all interventions are tailored to meet their specific requirements. By taking a targeted approach, we aim to foster inclusivity and prevent overlap with other sectors, thereby maximizing the impact of our efforts. For youth and adolescents, the project will focus on skill development and opportunities for community engagement. These initiatives will empower younger community members to become active participants in their own development, providing them with the tools they need for future success. For the elderly, we will prioritize programs that promote social inclusion and opportunities for meaningful participation, acknowledging their valuable contributions to the community.Importantly, the project is committed to ensuring the inclusion of persons with disabilities in all aspects of our initiatives. This commitment will be reflected in the development of community-led initiatives, where individuals with disabilities will have a voice in decision-making processes and project design. By actively involving them, we can create solutions that address their unique challenges and enhance their participation in community life.In addition to project development, we will implement various means of participation to ensure that persons with disabilities are not marginalized. This may include accessible information dissemination, specialized training programs, and adjustments to physical spaces to accommodate their needs. By fostering an inclusive environment, we aim to promote equal opportunities for all community members, ensuring that everyone has the chance to contribute to and benefit from the project’s outcomes. This holistic approach will strengthen community resilience and cohesion, ultimately leading to a more equitable and supportive environment for all.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This project is committed to establishing a robust Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework by implementing a comprehensive Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM). The CFRM will address a wide spectrum of concerns, reflecting a holistic approach that encompasses all project activities, including Shelter and SD initiatives. This will ensure accountability at every stage of the project cycle, from planning through to implementation and evaluation.Community members will have multiple channels to submit complaints—whether in-person consultations, phone calls, or written submissions. Upon receipt, complaints will be promptly acknowledged, and feedback will be provided within a specified timeframe, demonstrating our commitment to responsive service delivery and ongoing improvement.To ensure the effective management of the CFRM, project staff will undergo training in communication, active listening, and complaint handling. This training will empower staff to support and respect community members throughout the process. From the needs assessment phase to project closure, the affected community will be actively engaged and informed about all project activities targeting them. This transparent communication ensures that the community is well-aware of ongoing efforts and contributes to shaping the project at every stage.The project will prioritize community participation in identifying needs, prioritizing solutions, and monitoring implementation. This collaborative approach will address a broad range of community concerns, strengthen accountability, and ensure that the voices of those most affected are heard and acted upon.By fostering community involvement, accountability, and responsiveness, this project aims to cultivate a culture of engagement that leads to higher satisfaction, better outcomes, and a more resilient, empowered community.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

This project will actively mainstream the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles through comprehensive community engagement initiatives. We will conduct targeted training sessions for staffs & volunteers, equipping them with the knowledge and skills necessary to recognize, prevent, and respond to incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. These volunteers will serve as crucial advocates within their communities, promoting awareness and fostering a culture of respect and safety.Additionally, we will implement block-level awareness-raising activities to reach a broader audience. These initiatives will include workshops, informational sessions, and community discussions aimed at educating residents about PSEA principles and encouraging open dialogue about safety and protection. By engaging the community at multiple levels, we aim to build a strong network of informed individuals who can actively contribute to a safer environment, ensuring that everyone understands their rights and knows how to report concerns effectively. To effectively integrate PSEA into the project, CARE will ensure that any SEA allegations are properly investigated, and necessary actions will be taken according to standard operating procedures (SOPs) for SEA complaint referrals within the refugee response framework. Reports and investigations will be conducted in a timely, fair, safe, confidential, and survivor-centered manner. CARE will uphold a mandatory reporting system for all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse, in alignment with the CARE Safeguarding Policy and PSEA Network SOPs. The project will work to drive transformational change within social structures, reinforcing a zero-tolerance policy for sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project will adopt a climate-adaptive approach by utilizing environmentally sustainable materials for implementation. A key component includes activities focused on plantation and ongoing care and maintenance, promoting ecological health within the community. Additionally, the project prioritizes disaster risk management (DRM) initiatives in the camps by constructing sustainable access ways and implementing slope stabilization measures which widely have a positive impect in landslide mitigation. These efforts aim to enhance community resilience to climate-related challenges while ensuring safer living conditions. By integrating climate adaptability and sustainability into our strategies, we aim to support long-term environmental stewardship and reduce vulnerability to natural hazards.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

This project will play a crucial role in emergency preparedness and response. Under the shelter component, we will focus on providing tie-down kits to enhance the resilience of the affected community against hazards. These kits will empower residents to secure their shelters effectively. Additionally, we will organize various training sessions aimed at building community resilience. To reinforce learning and ensure practical understanding, a series of mock drills will be conducted, facilitating the visual transfer of emergency preparedness and response information to community members. Apart from this this project will support safety unit volunteers in emergency preparedness and response as needed in coordination with SMS agency. This hands-on approach will help strengthen the community’s ability to respond to emergencies effectively, fostering a culture of preparedness and self-reliance.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

This project will prioritize youth engagement by establishing block-level youth groups that will actively participate in project activities, including Cash-for-Work (CfW) initiatives. These youth groups will act as community representatives, serving as a vital link between CARE and affected community members. Through their involvement in CfW activities, youth will not only gain practical work experience but also take on leadership roles, advocating for the needs of their peers and helping to implement community-led initiatives. The youth groups will facilitate communication, gather feedback, and ensure that interventions are tailored to the specific needs of the community. This approach fosters a sense of ownership among young people and strengthens collaboration between CARE and the community, ensuring that all voices are heard and that solutions are both relevant and effective. By integrating Cash-for-Work opportunities, the project will provide youth with a pathway to economic empowerment, while also encouraging them to lead community-driven initiatives. Through their active participation, we aim to build a more resilient, engaged, and self-reliant community, leveraging the energy, creativity, and leadership potential of youth to drive positive change.
