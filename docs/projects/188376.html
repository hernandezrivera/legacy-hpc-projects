Project Summary

Under the strategic Objective 3, and the sector Objective 1 “Support water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria”, Rescate is resubmitting this project due to population dire need to have functional services to maintain their dignity and prevent GBV high risks while carrying water. The project WASH facilities rehabilitation in Ahmadieh & Em Al-Mara, near Dayr Hafir, covers fresh water and sewage networks extension to the houses.
According to the local community reps, the main water and the sewage networks has been laid by Water and Sanitation Est. at the beginning of 2011; pipelines reached to the villages entrance. However, due to the conflict, the project was suspended without the sub-networks’ extensions. Hence HHs cannot receive fresh water at homes neither linked to main sewage system. As a result, people are depending on jerry cans to secure their daily drinking water needs, other domestic water needs are secured via water trucking. It is worthy to mention that inhabitants carrying clean water from the near villages, or the nearest clean water source (one tap in 2 KM distance from both villages), this increases the exposure of women and children to Sexual exploitation and abuse, and GBV on their way to carry water.
Since there is no functional sewage network, the Ahamadieh inhabitants depend on pits’ soak, or they re-direct the wastewater outlets to the streets. These bad practices caused puddles in the streets, which limits the inhabitants’ accessibility, especially in winter, and causes skin diseases like leishmaniasis, and other water-borne diseases like diarrheas and gastrointestinal symptoms. This project is a lifesaving genuine effort to guarantee health, hygiene, and safety for women and children not to mentioned dignity when having clean street with sound environment, and potable water at home. ONG Rescate is present in the area since 2020 and is meeting with local communities who recurrently asked for the rehabilitation, Rescate team did field assessments and results showed how urgent need is especially to save people of water borne diseases and infections due to contaminated environment because people are using bad disposal measures whereby they increases the risks. Rescate, knowing the fund limitation is planning to start with phase one in both villages to rehabilitate and repair the most vulnerable areas in Ahmadieyh and Em al Mara. Next year, subject to fund availability, Rescate will submit another proposal with more in depth insights of the actual situation because the team would have been working on the phase one rehabilitation. 2023 plan is to finalize the whole networks in both areas to respond to the dire need of the affect population.

4 - Needs

Rescate worked in Dayr Hafir market, therefore had access to conduct field assessment in 2021, the team discussed with the council and communities the urgent needs of the affected population. Lack of water and sewage in Ahmadieh, and no sewage in Em Al-Mara. Inhabitants in Ahmadieh depend on soak pits for wastes disposal, they collect waste water and direct it to the streets. These bad practices of waste disposal caused puddles in the streets, this limits the street accessibility for the inhabitants, especially in the winter, and causes some skin diseases like leishmaniasis, besides rodents and insects. It is urgent need for WASH to support environment, health & control water borne diseases. The proposed intervention is targeting 400 HHs, 2,500 beneficiaries 70% of them are females, children & elderlies in dire need for functional sanitation to ensure their hygiene because under the current situation, people are obliged to discharge sanitation residues using costly private man/day workers and sewer discharging despite the limited results of such work modality. Rescate will commit to adopt a context sensitive approach to delivering humanitarian assistance in a way that considers the impact of assistance on the context & vice versa. We will review our interventions with stakeholders & community leaders to assess & monitor the impact (& take corrective measures if needed) on the broader environment through intervention design, implementation, monitoring & evaluation.

5 - Implementation of activities

Under this proposed 2022 intervention, start with maintenance works, then link old pipelines with the newly installed, tests of the new network operation, sewer jet to open blockages, clean tunnel, re-cover and deliver project works.Rescate will check the Lines via probing, scan, & examine; results will identify needs for replacement. Rehabilitation of undamaged pipes using sewage suction truck equipped with tanks, sewer jet vacuum & high-pressure water jet equipment to open the blockage of waste water pipelines to be operational after connection with the newly replaced pipes to ensure optimal performance. Clean manholes, repair leakages in the existing networks, to deliver the WASH services to the beneficiaries with best Rescate expertise within the available fund. Rescate is planning in 2022 to install the first phase of water and sewage networks, and deliver the sub network to the households by laying about 4,000 L.M of Poly Propylene pipes for sewage network in Em Al-Mara, and 4,000 L.M in Ahmadieh, about 850 L.M of Poly Ethylene pipes for drinking water network in Em Al-Mara, and 3,800 L.M in Ahmadieh, with all the needed accessories for the installed networks. in 2023, Rescate team is planning to target the areas to complete the lines. In 2022 to enhance awareness and water-borne diseases prevention measures and to address environmental pollution especially due to waste pollution. The works will include paving the roads where the works will be done.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: high– Impact: High, During women & children carrying water but water pipe will be tangible mitigation for this risk via project implementation: however worth to note that all Rescate staff, partners, and contractors, are required to sign the Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, Rescate is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its project to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on Rescate work, as well as the contractors’ work, and to rely any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues. All PSEA and/or GBV issues will be addressed first by the Project coordinator, who will share the concern with the Head of Mission based in Damascus, in order to establish a solution or way to proceed to enhance the protection of the PSEA/GBV survivor. When needed, the Head of Mission will scale the issue to ONG Rescate Headquarters in Spain, where the Director, in consultation with the gender specialists’ team, will look for potential solutions and/or an appropriate way to proceed.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Operational, one of the possible risks is miscommunication with different stakeholders’ groups. This event could have major consequences. Resources and response capacities being limited and far below known needs, there will always be perception of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension. Risk of increased prices due to heavy inflation. Mitigation: already accounted for the increased inflation in the budget, Contractor shall have 30% down payment to purchase materials under Rescate observation to quality, quantity and availability Fuel shortage and procurement supply delays are the Contractors’ responsibility and mitigated to ensure immediate storage of the purchased materials. Approval delays and Covid-19 restrictions are calculated in the project duration; hence schedule works in a manner to avoid mixing workers. Rescate will be working in strong coordination with ST, stakeholders & communities to facilitate focused response with strong need-based approach. Rescate had reviewed reports by OCHA, and the Sector on 1 conflict analysis before planning the response, ensured that a comprehensive needs analysis is conducted with stakeholders & is documented and made transparent, 2 during implementation, make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas, are reached equally, 3 keeping stakeholders & communities regularly informed of the project and its implementation progress.
