Project Summary

As the economic situation worsens, family savings have rapidly dried up. Families are selling valuable assets such as homes, tools, and jewellery, with financial reserves are now depleted, and worse, they are left without a base to grow from during the reconstruction period. Cumulatively, these economic impacts exacerbate hardships and increase humanitarian needs in the food sector; thousands of new families have been rendered vulnerable and need aid. Loss of hope, dignity, and social cohesion are emerging risks that threaten any future stability. And the worst of the economic crisis is yet to come. Furthermore, youths, women and children face acute vulnerability due to a reduction in basic services.
AKF-Syr have been implementing cash for work projects for five years. Through this project, AKF Syr’s extensive experience with various cash for work projects will be expanded into other required social services works within Salamieh district. The main objective is to increase access to resources to sustain and recover economic livelihoods of crisis-affected people especially women in Salamieh District through establishing cash for work activities. The specific objectives are to:
- Provide emergency employment through cash for work activities for 900 individuals, of which 400 will be females.
- Improve basic social services provided for the population within Salamieh District
- Enhance the municipal capacity in delivering effective public services.
- Contribute to improved social cohesion especially among community members involved in the community-based cash for work initiatives
- Strengthen the civic capacity in the delivery of public services by involving the local community and LNGOs (Salamieh Friends and Al-Adiat Associations) in planning, implementation and monitoring cash for work projects.
Public Works-Based Cash for Work
The action will implement cash for work initiative to provide conditional cash transfers to vulnerable HHs in Salamieh town. It is expected that 500 individuals, of which 250 will be women, will benefit from public works projects implemented in city of Salamieh. Cash for work will be implemented in partnership with municipal authorities and community associations within Salamieh town. The selection of all public works projects will be informed by a Steering Committee of community actors and take into consideration gender inclusive projects and nature of work. The selection of beneficiaries will target vulnerable households which are able to work, with a variety of assignments that will ensure high levels of inclusion for women and the disabled.

Needs

Salamieh District is located in Hama governorate of the Central Region in Syria with a population that increased from 240,000 (49% female) prior to the crisis, to 400,000 in 2013, while currently are accounted to 300,000 with an estimate 33% IDPs (Household Survey, AKF 2017). Besides, over the years of 2012 and 2013 the population of Salamieh City rose from 70,000 to 280,000, and is currently accounted to around 200,000. The District rural population currently represents 37% of the district’s total population in comparison to 50% prior to the crisis. The significant increase in population is resulting in a strain on local resources and a reduction in basic services. Due to the multiple layers of challenges placing pressure on local communities, there are gaps in some social services especially municipal work (e.g. solid waste removal, sewage system repair, etc.). ERL sector estimated that 50% of population is in acute need to access basic and social services, based on the 2017 ERL sector severity analysis. During 2018, AKF-Syr conducted primary assessments on immediate needs of households covering health, food, livelihood, household economy, education, vulnerability, and market prices. In December 2018 AKF-Syr compared all possible primary and secondary data to look for change over time in needs. To date, 33,256 households are registered in AKF-Syr cumulative vulnerability assessment database, up 6% compared to 2017 records. Out of which 28,222 households (85%) are vulnerable accounting for 123,951 individuals (55,760 F 45%). 55,931 households (HHs) live in Salamieh District out of which (28,222/50%) HHs were classified as vulnerable, up 5% from 2017. Of total vulnerable HHs, (6,770/24%) are IDPs HHs, (21,131/75%) are resident HHs, and (321/1%) are returnee HHs. Whereas (6,158/22%) of vulnerable HHs are female headed, (5,109/18%) HHs with at least one PwD, (13,585/48%) of HHs with at least one person with chronic illness. (6,151/22%) are HHs who have at least one elderly individual over 65 years old, and (6,260/22%) of HHs have at least one child under 5 years. 94% of the district population are food insecure (HFIAS), whereby 24% of the district population have either poor or borderline food consumption (FCS). Economic vulnerable households have worse. food consumption scores with 36% considered to have poor or borderline food consumption. Assessments indicate that average monthly income is USD 112/month, inclusive of humanitarian assistance. Yet, monthly household expenditure is avg. USD 177/month for a family of four, of which, food (67 USD/month), and health (28 USD/month) are considered major expense categories. Shortfalls between income and expenditure is largely met by social safety nets and humanitarian assistance (71% HHs) and debts/loans (78% HHs). In Nov 2018, the average SMEB for a HH of five members is 195 USD/month (11% decrease from Jan 2018). Unemployment contributes to declining in HH economic status especially among vulnerable households (82%F: 54%M). 39% of vulnerable households do not have workers, out of which 62% are female headed, and 38% are male headed households. Negative coping strategies include: reducing spending on NFIs to meet food needs (90% of vulnerable households), taking-on debts/loans (71%), skipping payments of debts/rent to support other needs expenditures (45%), selling productive assets (32%). Extreme coping strategies such as child labour for under 16 years (11% of vulnerable households), and child drop-out of school (8% of vulnerable households) are also observed and largely within rural areas. Based on HHs' assessments carried out in Salamieh district, 17000 vulnerable HHs were identified through a primary assessment conducted. 8000 HHs were selected to meet specific criteria to further verified by focus group discussions (FGD) and rapid assessments with CSOs. MPCT (including cash for work) are considered appropriate for this segment of HHs given the diversity of needs.

Implementation of activities

Community participation is instrumental in enhancing families’ ability to make decisions at a local level, improving their access to services and increasing their opportunities to recover assets. This action will be executed in collaboration with LNGOs in Salamieh town, and the Community Based Organizations (CBOs) in Rural Salamieh. AKFS has established and equipped 13 community management groups that are representative of the ethnic, social and gender composition of their villages in the district. AKFS is providing intensive training to the CBOs, including a dedicated module on project planning, management, implementation, and monitoring. As well, inclusive capacity building programme has been executed to the LNGOs to help them identify local strengths and weaknesses and create an understanding of families in need amongst neighbors in order to improve services and provide humanitarian assistances accordingly. Assisted by AKFS, LNGOs and CBOs will be responsible for addressing common development issues faced by the community, identifying cash for work activities and target groups, participating in designing and implementing cash for work projects, and carrying out relevant assessments as needed. - In some cases when the identified cash for work projects are under municipal authorities and need specific coordination and permits, the project will bring communities together in a civic private partnership where public services are enhanced. The project will engage community representatives in discussions with the municipal authorities of Salamieh district to identify available resources needed to provide the public services determined by the local communities. In terms of this agreement, the project will provide needs which are required to meet the objectives within a specific timeframe. The municipal authorities will commit to provide all needed data, maps and information, monitor the work progress and provide vehicles, and technical assistance as required. - As for the public works-based cash for work, a small Steering Committee will be established in order to preserve the partnership spirit. Members will be from AKFS, Municipal Authorities, LNGOs and CBOs. The Steering Committee will be meeting every 2 weeks. Its main tasks will be: 1- Reviewing and approving the detailed work plan. 2- Helping to achieve the project outcomes. 3- Monitoring the project risks and timelines. 4- Facilitating and assisting in obtaining supports and permissions from local and public organizations. - The action will support vulnerable communities by providing 900 short term employment opportunities for a period of 3 months in cash for work projects including but not limited to; solid waste management, public spaces, elderly care, drinking water and specific social projects. 900 community labourers (400 females) will be carefully identified. They are likely not highly qualified and desperately seeking job opportunities. Each labourer will commit to work for 4 hours a day and 20 days a month with a monthly cash/wages estimated at 45,000 SYP (around 100 USD). Project Steering Committee will be responsible to ensure that wages are fixed and about 10% lower than the market rate to ensure that cash for work projects attract the most economically disadvantaged individuals. Each cash for work project will seek also to recruit a project coordinator with 2 technicians and supervisors who will be evenly distributed between the 14 neighbourhoods of Salamieh Town and the targeted rural areas. - In order to identify the 900 beneficiaries, a list of these names will be generated by community organizations and verified by the Steering Committee based on clear criteria.Solid waste management is one of the main community concerns, therefore, at least 500 workers will be involved in garbage collection, each worker is able to collect up to 10 Kg daily. Consequently the total amount of garbage collected during the project would be 600 m3.

Protection Risk Analysis

A: Workers may be subject to discrimination from neighbourhoods as some types of work maybe consider unfavourable. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Low. Therefore, community organizations which have been established in communities, along with AKF household assessment database, will identify individuals who are willing and economic vulnerable, and able to complete this type of work.B: Gender constructs may limit ability of women to participate. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Prevailing gender constructs with respect to municipal work may result in limited recruitment of women to engage in this type of work or discrimination when completing this type of work within the community. Female workers which are recruited will be asked preference of specific tasks in order to ensure their comfort. Teams will also be established with mixed genders, with female leaders for each team. It has been found that gender dynamics are often more positive within team environments and especially ones that are led by women.C: CBOs members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement.Mitigation: In program areas is deemed a high risk, AKF Syr will delay implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel. Risk2: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in procurement.AKF Syr has extensive experience undertaking programmes in extremely difficult places. AKF Syr will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement to within the Governorates. Risk3: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level.Mitigation: AKF Syr regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the program and communicate with donor as needed. Risk4: The transfer of funds to implementing unit delayed due to modification in banking regulations.Mitigation: AKF Syr in collaboration with AKF’s head office in Geneva regularly monitor transfers. The impact of prolonged transfers will be mitigated by using bridge financial resources to ensure minimal disruption to program implementation. Risk5: Syrian Pound (SYP) has experiencing high rates of inflation.Mitigation: Inflation risk is mitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF Syr manage reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation. Risk6: Significant influx of IDPs and/or the vulnerability of resident populations increasingAKF Syr’s Humanitarian programme continually adjusts its delivery plan to cope with the ebbs and flows of IDP movements, and when the circumstances of particular villages change. WFP supplements food baskets when IDP flows are large, to ensure a social safety net for IDPs and host families.
