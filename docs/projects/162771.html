Project Summary

Maintain and strengthen the system of data collection in the Far North region of
Cameroon through IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) to support the
coordination of humanitarian activities. The DTM aims to provide reliable and up-to-date information on the numbers, locations of displaced populations, displacement trends and
reasons for displacement to better inform humanitarian response. The assessed groups consist of internally displaced persons, out-of-camp refugees and returnees. The data will be disaggregated by
age and sex. The DTM has the potential to be expanded and/or modified to take into account changes in the regional context.

Besoins / Needs

Since the beginning of 2014, Northeastern Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence conducted by the Boko Haram insurgency, leading to widespread displacements in the country with spillover effect in neighboring countries incuding Niger, Chad and Cameroon. The government of Cameroon, through a Note Verbale sent by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs to UN Agencies and the Ministry of Interior during the fifth steering committee on refugees and IDPs held on 25 February 2015 has called for efforts to respond quickly in order to address the needs of the displaced populations. This recommendation was reinforced by the signature by Cameroon of the convention of Kampala related to displaced population, made official by the presidential decree published on 31 December 2014. In 2015, IOM rolled out its Displacement Tracking Matrix as no data was yet available to allow the humanitarian community to have a clear picture of the extent of populations displacements. Latest figures from the August 2019 report of the ongoing DTM exercise indicate the presence of 270,870 IDPs, 110,574 returnees and 46,845 out of camp refugees present in the six departments of the Far North region. The DTM is a key tool for Cameroon and has become the main source of information on internal displacements, widely used by the humanitarian community. Providing a more accurate and detailed picture of the displacement crisis is essential in order to mobilize and direct resources to reach the most vulnerable populations. Therefore, the continuation of DTM activities is vital and will serve all humanitarian actors involved in improving the ongoing situation in the region.
