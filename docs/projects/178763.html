Project Summary

The proposed intervention's overall objective is contributing directly to the education sector 2021 HRP objectives by improving equitable access for school-aged children to safe, inclusive, and quality learning environments to foster their resilience. This will be achieved through implementing a package of education activities to 25 schools in NW Syria which are located in underserved and high-needs areas, where 15000 children (6000 boys and 9000 girls 'including 300 CwD ~2.5%', 5-17 years old) are enrolled (70% IDPs). By supporting a quality learning environment, IRD seeks to increase children's enrollment in the targeted schools. To achieve this, IRD will conduct digital Back
To Learning (BTL) campaign 'to minimize gathering and as a precautionary measure for COVID-19'. The enrolled students will receive formal/non-formal education services & undergo ASER test. To improve the students’ well-being during the project lifetime, all enrolled students will receive recreational activities. All teachers and education personnel (550: 300 men & 250 women) will be provided with monthly incentives to increase their motivation and ensure their retention in schools. The project will cover the schools’ monthly operational costs, including recreational materials, refreshment, cleaning, hygiene materials, and stationery, and the provision of students' and teachers' kits. Adding, conducting small-scale rehabilitation works. The quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training and further support to their professional development within each school by delivering (CSG, TiCC, PSEA, COVID-19) trainings. Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be a component in the activities of this project. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held to discuss what was achieved in the last period and set with IRD’s staff plans for the upcoming period. Due to COVID-19, there will be comprehensive precautionary measures in the 25 schools, including periodic sterilization process for classrooms and WASH facilities, awareness sessions for students, training for teachers and educational staff, and sterilization provision materials for all schools.
The project secured fund for 2021 is 700,000 USD

4 - Needs

According to OCHA Situation Report No. 26 - As of 26 March 2021, the provision of continued learning in a safe environment and the provision of support to education personnel & teacher incentives, and training for teaching and learning in northwest Syria remain the key priorities for the education sector, especially due to the impacts of the security situations and COVID-19.The demographic map in Syria has faced drastic changes constantly fluid and subject to change on a daily basis. According to WVI's report "The cost of conflict for Syria's children"- As of 4 March 2021; the highest level of attendance for the displaced children in NW Syria is 32 percent. This is despite the volatile situation, where attacks on schools have been pervasive since the start of the conflict in 2011. Boys tended to be more impacted by challenges in attending school than girls. Today, Syria is one of the worst places in the world to be a child. Close to 600,000 people, including 55,000 children, have been killed, half the population displaced, and a child’s life expectancy has been reduced by 13 years.Adding, and as the 2021 draft HNO; in 2020 an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of OOSC, and children who are at risk of dropping out, almost certain to increase. 71% of children in need of humanitarian education assistance live in areas with extreme and catastrophic needs. The highest concentration of these children is found in Aleppo & Idleb governorates.

5 - Implementation of activities

IRD aims to discourage drop-out and increase meaningful quality learning as well as increase enrollment of out-of-school children (OOSC), also, to secure the functioning of schools through:-Promote child safeguarding (CSG) & education quality in 25 schools in NW Syria by delivering training for Teachers on will (CSG), PSEA, & COVID-19 by the IRD field team, receiving three guides included a guidebook for education personnel working in emergencies, CSG guide, and Code of Conduct (CoC). Additionally, teachers & edu personnel will sign IRD’s CSG Policy & CoC.-Increase the awareness among parents and local communities on the importance of education and how to be protected from the COVID-19 pandemic by conducting digital Back to Learning campaigns within the targeted communities. IRD will go with this method only in order to avoid crowd gathering and achieve the widespread of the intended messages.-Determine children's educational levels and enable the teachers to plan and focus on the needed educational activities for each student by undergoing an ASER test.-Improve children's well-being and learning capacity through recreational activities that will be carried out by the classroom teachers who will be provided with recreational materials.-Ensure safe and quality learning environments by the provision of monthly required operational supplies.-Conducting small-scale rehabilitation works.-Provision of Education and COVID-19 prevention materials to schools, teachers, and children,-Conduct periodic sterilization process.-PTAs establishment per school. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held to set plans for the upcoming period.-Conducting MEAL activities: field visits, PDM, MEAL report, feedback mechanism.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The number one risk is security, as schools have been one of the main targets in the conflict. Although the situation has slightly improved due to the cessation of hostilities, it is unknown how long it will last. The security situation is not one that IRD can play a major role in mitigating, besides providing additional safety and security training for teachers to use with their students if security concerns arise. IRD has chosen relatively safer locations and has historically been locations where IDPs flee to, not where people are fleeing from. In order to avoid any CSG violation by any education worker, IRD will deliver training on CSG & CoC for all educational staff. In case any education worker violates the CoC/CSG; IRD will assign a committee including ‘field education team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, and education supervisor’ to verify the incident of violation and communicate with the ED in order to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on IRD’s CSG policy.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions1: The targeted areas are stable, and no large-scale military operations are anticipated, procurement and shipment roads/lines are stable, and relations with the Education Directorates and local authorities are accessible and supportive.Risk 1.1: A new wave of displacement to the project locations. L: Medium, I: High.Mitigation 1.1: Follow-up the security situation in the project locations, and integration with other stakeholders to provide emergency services.Risk 1.2: Deterioration of the general security situation in the project areas. L: Low, I: High.Mitigation1.2: Closely monitor and follow up security situations and coordinate with the education cluster and follow the instructions issued to maintain the project's safety and employees.Risk 1.3: Schools’ lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic. L: Medium, I: High.Mitigation 1.3: In case COVID-19 outbreak in project locations, and as a mitigation measure: (A) schools might be suspended fully. The project will adapt the distance learning modality tested during 2020 through 4 education projects implemented by IRD. Children will be provided with internet vouchers. WhatsApp groups will be created per subject and per class. Teachers will record the lessons to be shared with students.(B) If the closure was 'partial' then, the blended education approach will be applied, a mix of physical attendance and distance learning methods.
