Project Summary

This project seeks to:
o improve access to clean water through sustaining the water infrastructure and supply to meet minimum SPHERE standards both in the target communities as well as in ARC-run primary health care centres (PHCCs) and nutrition centers, emphasizing transition from the fully subsidized operation and maintenance to partial cost-recovery and water user tariff collection approaches.
o Improve the hygiene and sanitation conditions for IDPs and host community members, whose coping mechanisms have been affected due to conflict and influx of large numbers of displaced persons. This will be done through promotion and support of construction of emergency latrines and improved household shared latrines with hand washing points. Where feasible the CLTS approach will be used.
o Promotion of good health, nutrition and hygiene practices in targeted IDP and host communities through the timed targeted counselling approach (ttC), which is a methodology promoting key health, nutrition and hygiene behaviors and practices with the pregnant women and new mothers and their intimate partners and family members to ensure their health and well-being. In the WASH context, the focus will be on improving the chances of survival and normal development/growth of children during the first 1,000 days of life, as per the Baby WASH approach and in accordance with the UN Every Woman, Every Child Strategy. At the same time, ARC teams will work with communities, volunteers and Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDCs) on reducing the incidence of communicable diseases through curbing the risks associated with environmental factors by ensuring cleanliness of water points, shared latrines and functionality and maintenance of the drainage canals and the solid waste management systems and disposal sites, as well as ensuring the water quality through testing and treatment
o Mainstream protection in all WASH activities by paying particular attention to protection needs of different users in the design and use of infrastructure and WASH services by gender, age and diversity to ensure effective access, safety, and dignity of beneficiaries. Through community discussions and analysis of protection needs all WASH activities will be designed to mitigate the protection risks of the beneficiaries.

Needs

As per the Sudan HNO (2018), it was estimated that 4.9 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance in Sudan. Of these 4.4 million do not have access to adequate drinking water and safe means of defecation, and that their hygiene practices do not improve their health status. 70% of the people in need are in the five states of Darfur, South Kordofan and Blue Nile and comprise mostly of protracted IDPs, their host communities and refugees.According to HNO (2018) there are more than 1.97million IDPs residing in more than 66 IDP camps and settlements across Darfur. The protracted Darfur crisis and insecurity outside the camps has restricted access to land as well livelihood opportunities. As a result, IDPs are largely reliant on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs such as WASH and food. Together with overcrowded camps, shortage of WASH services provide perfect conditions for rapid spread of WASH related and other communicable diseases. Although AWD is under control in IDP camps, it remains a potent threat, pointing to the need for WASH services and AWD emergency preparedness and contingency plans. Through this comprehensive approach, ARC expects to achieve a reduction of morbidity and mortality associated with diseases and hazards resulting from poor sanitation conditions, lack of access to safe potable water and poor living conditions.The demand for water continues to grow each year. In high-density areas of displaced people, the water supply does not match the demand. The high population density in the camps increases the rate of water use and the frequency of breakdowns of water and sanitation systems, triggering a need for frequent repair and maintenance as well as continuous expansion and upgrading of WASH services to ensure uninterrupted services.In Adila in East Darfur, according to the October 2017 inter-agency assessment report, five out of 31 boreholes are not functioning. In Abujabra locality, also in East Darfur, there are 23 boreholes out of 62 that are not functioning, primarily due to overuse resulting from increased water demand generated by arrival of IDPs and refugees putting an unsustainable burden on the inadequate infrastructure. The host community is particularly in danger due to water insecurity caused by huge numbers of displaced persons and refugees. In addition, a number of boreholes produce brackish water that is not suitable for human use. ARC will seek alternative solutions to provide palatable water.The price of water at about USD $1 per 200-liter barrel is prohibitively expensive. The largely poorly resourced IDPs cannot afford to meet household needs for drinking, food preparation and other basic personal hygiene needs. The latrine coverage for both IDPs and host communities is very low and open defecation is a common practice.

Activities

Provide emergency wash services through the following activities: Upgrading 2 diesel pump to solar system in Adilla 1, Yassin 1.Daily chlorination ,Operation and maintenance of 9 mini water yards in Kalma camp.Dissemination of hygiene promotion massages through Drama,mass-media and hygiene sessions Daily water quality monitoring and surveillance .Operation and maintenance of 25 PHCs water supply system in East and South Darfur states.Ground water Monitoring through 3 observations wellRehabilitation of 10 Hand pumps in Kalma camp and rural areas.Adapt social sanitation marketing in Elneem, Gereida and Kalma IDPs camps.Distribution of cleaning tools and mobilize targeted community for conducting weekly clean up campaign Implement CLTS approaches in 4 rural areas in South and East Darfur states.Construction of 3 VIP school latrines in South and East Darfur.Replacement of 200 house hold latrines through cash base transfer for most vulnerable people in Kalma and Gereida IDPs camp.Using cash base program ARC is going to distribute 400 gender-sensitive hygiene kits to the women and girls in reproductive age. Formation & Strengthening of 6 Community Relief and Development Committees (CRDCs).Train 150 community volunteers as TOT in South and East Darfur using PHAST approach to mobilize their community in improvement of hygiene promotion practices . Formation and training of 4 schools' health clubs Printing and distribution of IEC materials to the community through HNW volunteers.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Frequent droughts because of climate change is expected to lead to reduced average rainfall. It is expected to have an impact on already at risk underground water levels. ARC has and continues monitor ground water levels in all operation areas through drilling of observation wells and use digital loggers to monitor fluctuations in the aquifer and quality of water. In addition ARC is carrying out water quality research on the brackish salty water in East Darfur with a view to design solutions based on research evidence. ARC will continue its use environmental friendly approaches such as use of solar energy instead of fossil fuels to pump water. ARC has converted a number of water systems into hybrid solar/ diesel pumping systems with diesel pump being used only as standby alternative in the event of a breakdown in solar system. This has lowered the use of carbon fuels like diesel and reduced the cost of operation and maintenance by 50%. ARC has developed latrine designs which use concrete slabs and beams instead of wooden poles to minimize cutting down trees. For super structure bamboo sticks will be used because bamboo is easily renewable and has less environmental impact .
