Project Summary

Reports from humanitarian actors indicate that financial insecurity, caused by an increasing unemployment rate and unstable, low wages, has had a detrimental effect on purchasing power. Furthermore, droughts, water scarcity, and limited energy supply have complicated people's ability to meet immediate food needs and sustain their livelihoods. The decline in agricultural production over the past two seasons has made it imperative to prioritize support for the agriculture and agri-food sectors, aiming to enhance food production and improve food security for the population.
Additionally, the situation is exacerbated by climate change, as the country has experienced record-high droughts resulting in a significant decrease in cereals' biomass. Wheat, in particular, has been severely impacted, with estimates suggesting that the drought will leave Syria 1.5-1.7 million tonnes short of its domestic wheat needs in 2022. Certain practices, such as subsidizing irrigation for strategic crops and supporting fuel prices, have also contributed negatively to the water crisis by promoting excessive water pumping and groundwater depletion issues.
The linkage of input subsidies to fixed centralized purchase prices and the energy crisis have also had detrimental effects on crop production, resulting in inefficiencies, reduced returns for farmers, and the emergence of informal market dynamics. The energy crisis has escalated production costs, impacted food storage and transportation, and hindered agricultural productivity.
All of these factors have severely hindered the sustainable functioning of food systems and value chains throughout the country. Producers are unable to restore their full productive capacity as they are compelled to prioritize immediate daily needs, leading to unsustainable practices and the utilization of saline underground water, exacerbating land salinization and agricultural land loss. Sanctions, import restrictions, exchange rates, and high inflation have forced producers to rely on limited availability and high-priced local agriculture inputs of low quality, which often prove unsuitable for the new climate conditions.
Market functionality is low, and market demand is affected by high food prices, inactive credit institutions, and a heavily regulated environment. In areas with returning populations, the absence of social cohesion and mutual trust makes it difficult to establish local trade networks, share information, or engage in collective action for livelihoods. Employment and income-generating opportunities are limited, and income levels fail to meet expenditure needs. While the number of women working as daily laborers has increased, the gender pay gap persists in both urban and rural contexts.
To address the identified needs based on the recommendations of the early recovery sector, Oxfam has decided to focus on providing livelihoods and job opportunities for the most vulnerable households affected by the crisis. Supporting the resumption of economic activity should involve assisting local shops and businesses by linking them with value chain assessments for different agricultural goods and conducting labor market assessments related to the selected value chains. Furthermore, vocational training, business support, smart grants, and on-the-job opportunities should be provided along the selected value chain to enhance skills and improve economic prospects for the affected population.
Oxfam has skilled staff to implement the project, increase the skills of target beneficiaries, and provide the necessary capacity building for the target group to enable an increase in their income and economic security, based on market and value chain assessments. A risk assessment will be conducted before the start of the project to understand the preferences of the beneficiaries, delivery mechanisms, potential risks, and mitigation measures. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

A participatory community engagement approach will be adopted to ensure the active participation of the target population. A total of 1,890 HH will benefit from this intervention, with 25% of them being female-headed HH.The action will also conduct price monitoring of basic items in critical markets and assess the impact of climate change to understand changes in price trends and adjust aid and interventions accordingly. PDM and evaluations of the applicants' practices will be conducted to measure the impact and beneficiaries' satisfaction.Oxfam plans to enable vulnerable and affected families in the targeted subdistricts to have access to essential basic needs and production inputs, along with the required skills, particularly in rural areas where livelihood diversification is less common than urban areas, and where agriculture activities are the main focus. Oxfam will ensure coordination among various partners to reduce duplication and promote complementarity.Oxfam will adopt a community engagement approach to enhance the active participation of the target communities in the entire cycle of the action and to develop vulnerability and eligibility criteria for the selection process (beneficiary-centered approach).SEA risks triggered by unequal power dynamics between staff responsible for beneficiary selection and the communities in need of assistance: Oxfam's PSEA and Child Safeguarding policy; safeguarding focal points in all hubs; training and refreshers on the topic delivered to staff and partners; CFM; reporting lines in place; manager's responsibility to create a working environment conducive to reporting SEA-related concerns; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries against Oxfam's vulnerability criteria and MoVs; in-house capacity to operationalize Oxfam's reporting and investigation routine.Risks: Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions, and transaction delays, which could lead to delays. Fluctuations in the USD to SYP exchange rate could impact project budget. There is a potential for fraud and aid diversion among Oxfam staff, suppliers, and partners. Assumptions: No interference by conflicting parties, which would jeopardize impartiality. The local authorities will allocate enough production inputs for the rehabilitated bakeries. The community will have the capacity to manage the work in the rehabilitated bakeries effectively. Social norms and traditions will enable women to participate in the communities' decision-making process.Mitigation: Oxfam will conduct a rapid market assessment to check the availability and prices of the required items. All Oxfam and partner staff will be trained on Oxfam's standards regarding safeguarding and accountability. Accountability mechanisms will be put in place prior to implementation. Oxfam will opt for NCEs to absorb any surpluses that may arise due to an increase in the exchange rate.
