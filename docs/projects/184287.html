Project Summary

Acute malnutrition remains a challenge and a major public health problem for children 6-59 months, as well as Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs)
2021 Post Gu FSNAU nutrition analysis shows that at the national level the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has still remained at a serious level of 11.1%. According FSNAU Post Gu technical release released on 9th September 2021 in the absence of humanitarian assistance nearly 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of the year. The key drivers of acute food insecurity in Somalia include the combined effects of poor and erratic rainfall distribution, flooding and conflict. Moreover, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 295,515 who are likely to be severely malnourished 463,204 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance
Among the various underlying factors, food insecurity remains be a major driver of malnutrition in Somalia. Based on the latest projections by FSNAU, from October to December 2021, food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr season rainfall, continued insecurity and other food security related risk factors, including rising food prices and cost of living, declining availability of milk for both consumption and sale, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season. It is therefore estimated that around 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3)
According to Post Gu nutrition assessment in target regions projects that from October to December 2021 that 1,090,820 people are stressed (IPC 2), 603,330 persons are in crisis (IPC 3) while 41,000 persons are projected to be emergency (IPC 4).
In light of the persistent high rates of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) coupled with the recent floods and desert locust upsurge there is need to continue delivery of lifesaving nutrition interventions targeting vulnerable populations in IDP camps and hard to reach communities. Mogadishu remains a priority since it is hosting larger number of IDPs as well as, Galgaduud and South Mudug regions where many pastoral communities lost their livelihoods and are in vulnerable state.
The project targets a total of 44,583 children 6-59 months with lifesaving nutrition interventions; 6688 severely malnourished children with appropriate treatment facilities in 6 Mobile, 5 Static OTP facilities with those manifesting complicated SAM admitted for treatment in the nearest stabilization centers and 20,063 pregnant and lactating women. In districts where CISP is the primary or the secondary IP, the intervention foreseen will be implemented using mobile OTPs facilities linked to existing static health facilities for better coverage. CISP is working with Education and Protection programmes in Banadir and Galmudug in the proposed areas. CISP will coordinate with the nutrition cluster to ensure that there is no duplication while ensuring maximum impact of interventions

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The target population faces continuous displacements due to armed conflict between SNA/AMISOM on one side and the Militant group on the other. The target population also faces forceful recruitment of young boys to the militia of the militant groups, although some local communities fought back and resisted they could not sustain and were ultimately defeated like what happened in some areas in Mudug region. As per the current situation the only way to mitigate is to families to send the young to government controlled districts and for long term solutions is to liberate those areas from the iron grip of the militants.
