Project Summary

The NGO will support 1,500 households in NES experiencing financial shocks and who are dependent on negative coping strategies to meet their urgent needs. The program will be in line with the Cash Working Group’s strategy to cover IDPs in host communities in identified through gaps. A multipurpose cash modality will be used that will better the position of affected household to meet their own urgent needs at the end of a one-month transfer period as their situation stabilizes. To address cases where households are unable to meet their urgent needs, the number of transfers of assistance will range from one, three or six rounds. Households facing extreme levels of vulnerability are identified based on follow-on household level assessments and post distribution monitoring. Cash assistance will employ the vulnerability criteria for beneficiary selection used for assistance (e.g., age and gender of head of household, reduced food (rCSI) and productive asset ownership, displacement status, shelter status, income-expenditure, and dependency ratios).
In addition, the NGO will support the affected population to build saving and start-up business skills through a training curriculum. The NGO will provide financial literacy training to MPC clients with three sessions of financial management curriculum. The primary topics will include the differences between needs and wants, living costs, a test of a business idea, finding money for the business, investing in the business, knowledge of the business cycle, adding value to the product or service. Following the three rounds of MPC, NGO will aim to integrate project participants into its livelihood-based programs based on fund. NGO implements a number of projects designed to put participants on the path to early recovery, including vocational training, apprenticeship, small-to-medium enterprise support, and financial management training. In addition, NGO provides training to beneficiaries on how to set up village savings and loans to enable communities to set up projects that serve their communities, build social cohesion and promote joint resource management.
The graduation of livelihood activities (vocational training, apprenticeship) will be supported with startup kit to launch out micro business in market and generating income, 20 % of MPC beneficiaries will referral to livelihood service based on beneficiary needs and preference.

4 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2020 - with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. As The Syrian pound is in virtual freefall having lost 78% of its value since October 2019, while price increases for staple goods are at an all-time high. More than 90% of the population is now estimated to live below the poverty line. The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated this economic downturn by further reducing already sparse income-generating opportunities in context where 50% of the work age population is now. Economic insecurity led households to adopt severe coping strategies, including sending children to work and reducing food and water consumption. In communities where livelihoods coping strategies were reported, a quarter of communities reported that households skip or reduce meals to cope with a lack of food. Water trucking services were widely perceived to be too expensive, forcing households to limit water usage, including reducing drinking water consumption (cited in nearly half of communities reporting challenges). COVID-19 continues to negatively impact the health and economic situation in NES. As of 6 December, 7,256 cases of COVID-19 have been confirmed in NES. In 68% of communities , as the livelihoods sectors were reportedly affected by the pandemic, with manufacturing the most commonly impacted. The pandemic has prompted markets to reduce opening hours and days in nearly 70% of communities, which has greatly impacted the population. The program will be integrated with other sectors such as Protection delivered by the NGO.

5 - Implementation of activities

NGO will provide a transfer value of 100% of the survival minimum exchange basket (SMEB) coordinate with CWG technical guidance. The average transfer value is USD $100 but will vary based on local prices and REACH’s SMEB calculations. When markets are functioning and accessible and cash is an appropriate modality, evidence points toward a strong positive impact of cash programming on people’s ability to meet their urgent needs as evidenced through increased total household expenditure, household consumption, maintaining productive assets while simultaneously decreasing reliance on negative coping strategies (such as child labor and sale of productive assets). There is a strong body of evidence, including in humanitarian contexts, that people overwhelmingly spend cash transfers wisely in a way that addresses their urgent needs. The cash transfer will be in Syrian Pounds (SYP) or US dollar depending on the CWG recommendation, and other factors, such as liquidity, and SYP value against the dollar. NGO will target 1,000 households in Raqqa with 6,000 individuals and 500 households in Deir Ez Zor with 3,000 individuals. NGO will vet and contract Hawalas to deliver the MPCA in the target locations. Hawalas remain the most appropriate mechanism to transfer cash assistance into Syria in the absence of a functioning banking system or other formal payment mechanism. Hawalas are part of the socio-cultural fabric of the region and are the most established and cost-efficient mechanism for transferring funds inside Syria. Program will include market monitoring observation, key Informant Interview with Financial service provider, cash WG monitoring & recommendation, feedback mechanisms, cash risk analysis, post-distribution monitoring.
