Project Summary

The sociopolitical crisis that started in the Anglophone regions in 2016 has resulted to the humanitarian crisis, which has led to the separation of families, loss of lives, destruction of properties (crops and material) and disabilities of house hold heads. As the crisis deepens more population are been displaced thus, increasing the number of persons in need of humanitarian assistance. Due to this crisis, many families are now forced to live below the poverty line. They cannot provide for their basic necessities (clothing, shelter, WASH, and especially food) for survival, many young girls are now forced into early marriages, have sex with men for food and money, while the young boys have resorted to joining armed groups. Most families have reduced their food consumption rate, adults skipping meals for children to feed and others living at the mercy of others. The UN office for coordination estimated in January 2019that about 437500 people have been displaced. These displaced persons have created camps in far and near bushes. Some are also concentrated in the urban and semi urban towns like kumba while others have fled to neighboring Nigeria.
The nutritional situation in the SW as per the nutrition cluster is deteriorating. Households are experiencing food shortages due to over-stretched markets, limited access to farm lands, limited access to essential health services especially those in the hard to reach areas. Deteriorating hygiene situation and limited access to clean drinking water for both IDP (mostly in the bushes) and host communities.
Since February 2018 AMEF in partnership with IRC has been involved in the voucher program. The voucher program is an economic recovery program that is designed to ensure food security to the affected population. It comprises of food items, NFIs and WASH. It entails vendor selection taking in to consideration the stock’s capacity. Through this process, the needs of the affected population (children, women, men, boys, girls, elderly, persons with disabilities) in the area will be met.
AMEF has proven experience providing food assistance, protection and WASH services in the South West region, particularly in Meme and Manyu since 2017. For the purpose of this proposal, AMEF would be involved in Mobile Voucher Markets (MVM) and Non-Mobile Voucher Markets (NMVM). The MVMs would be used to meet the needs of IDPs and the host communities in distant locations (12km) from Kumba for beneficiaries in Meme Division and Mamfe for those in Manyu Division. MVMs consists of voucher distribution and the supply of selected voucher items to the affected areas directly. This means selected items would be transported and distributed to beneficiaries at their door steps. The NMVMs would be used for IDPs and host communities that are less than 12km from the main towns. In Meme and Manyu, the Kumba and Mamfe central Markets will be used for voucher redemption. There will be a market assessment, followed by selection of vendors that meet prescribed requirements for the voucher program. To make the project more effective, AMEF is proposing a one-year project of both hygiene and food security service voucher to be implemented as an integrated package.Therefore, AMEF is proposing a complementary set of interventions that target the same communities to ensure that the most vulnerable receive the full package of intervention to address the immediate causes of food insecurity and malnutrition.The reason for MVMs is to ensure that beneficiaries receive all of their items at doorpost, and the fact that there are no ready markets , thereby ensuring their safety.
The purpose is to reduce food insecurity and hygiene related diseases through an intergraded package of food and hygiene items for beneficiaries in Meme and Manyu and targets women, men, girls, boys, children, elderly persons and persons with disabilities, a total of 10,900 households would be reached.
