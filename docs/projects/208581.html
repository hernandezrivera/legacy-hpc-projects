Project Summary

This project will undertake food assistance and provision of access to fuel efficient technologies, income generating activities and agricultural resilience support to affected persons in BAY states. SHO is proposing to achieve these through the food distribution (Wet feeding, in kind), delivery of cash-based emergency food assistance, support of income generating activities through cash grant and business skills training and development for livelihood, and improve agriculture production through seeds and other inputs, and transformation of skills for food and livelihoods to households. This project will also include a Wet Feeding component as part of the food assistance which is going to provide emergency response and act as first responder to new arrivals and returnees in the selected LGAs with high level of new arrivals.
Additionally, Agricultural Resilience Support - livelihood inputs/assets or community based productive asset creation/enhancement activities through the most appropriate modalities (ex: in kind, voucher, cash or multi-modality and create new innovative approaches to food production and resilience which will contribute signi¦cantly to the healthy, physical and mental well-being of IDPs. Salient Humanitarian Organization aims to support addressing the underlying causes of continued and seasonal food insecurity through Agricultural resilient building and agro income-generating activities. Similarly, aims to move towards an increasingly integrated Food Security and agriculture Livelihoods response tailoring in innovative means of food production at will be locally accepted, building on existing projects of our wet feeding program, experience and capacity within SHO in Food security sector, supporting assistance increasingly to areas where this is targeted. This is to provide resilience-building activities for affected
populations. Knowing the complex nature of Northeastern Nigeria, SHO and the food sector will coordinate for participatory planning sessions in the communities at the start of the program implementation. This is to get to the community involvement in the planning of the project activities and getting the affected community share ideas and best approaches in implementing.

1 - Needs

As a result of the conflict, inflation, and climate-related challenges such as flooding, 4.4 million people living in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States are projected to be food insecure in the lean season. Of these, 31 per cent are girls, 27 per cent are boys, 22 per cent are women and 20 per cent are men. In terms of population groups, the majority (69 per cent) are host community members, followed by IDPs (18 per cent) and returnees (13 per cent).According to the October 2023 Cadre Harmonisé (CH) analysis on food insecurity projecting that about 3.3 million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states will face high levels of food and nutrition insecurity from October to December 2023. This is a 10 per cent increase from the 3 million people projected within the same period in 2022. The number of food-insecure people in the BAY states is expected to increase to 4.4 million during the 2024 lean season (June to August). This is slightly (2 per cent) higher than it was during the 2023 lean season when about 4.3 million people faced a severe food crisis. Over 4 million people have needed urgent food assistance annually in the BAY states since June 2020. Trend analysis indicates consistently high or rising food insecurity levels since 2018. Food insecurity and malnutrition are among the main drivers of humanitarian needs in the BAY states. Access to farmland is still limited in many areas because of insecurity and many of the 2.2 million displaced people do not have alternative livelihoods. They are often forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as survival sex and child labour to stay alive. Over the past year, dozens of farmers have lost their lives, and others have been abducted or injured trying to eke out a living by venturing outside the security perimeters of Borno’s garrison towns.The reliance on food assistance to meet daily needs is high among vulnerable households because of limited access to livelihood opportunities or income generating activities – largely due to the limited availability of land (as a result of widespread insecurity) for the production of both livestock and crops. Limited access to agricultural inputs such as fertilizers (which are heavily restricted), further reduces productivity and production. However, the October 2021 crop change analysis indicates increases in access to land for production in Borno State, including in some hard-to-reach and inaccessible areas such as Abadam, Marte, Monguno, Guzamala, Kukawa, Ngala and Kala Balge. This provides an opportunity for increasing resilience activities in those locations. Limited access to water – especially for dry season farming, fisheries and livestock production – further reduces livelihood opportunities and productivity for farming households.

2 - Activities

Despite largely favorable crop development and improvements in livestock conditions, localized flooding and dry spells coupled with persistent high levels of conflict in the north-eastern. Furthermore, this is leading to an increase in humanitarian assistance needs in the ongoing rainy season, as the harvest of early maturing maize, groundnuts, potatoes, and millet is not leading to large scale improvements in food insecurity.Conflict-affected areas in the Northeast are expected to face the most severe outcomes with Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes anticipated throughout the entire projection period. In these areas, displaced households outside of IDP settlements and urban centers have no food stocks with limited incomes and are likely to have difficulty in meeting their food and non-food needs until the harvest in October. With the harvest, households that engaged in crop production are expected to consume own foods; however, access to food is still expected to be generally limited and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) outcomes are expected persist in much of Borno State. Areas, where populations are affected by significant losses of livelihood activities and remain inaccessible to humanitarian actors, are likely facing similar or worse outcomes as adjacent, accessible areas. A risk of famine (IPC Phase 5) persists throughout this period in a worst-case scenario in which there is a significant shift in conflict that would reduce household access to food and income. SHO shall conduct assessment BAY state prior to implementation of the program to screen vulnerable Persons of Concern households and deliver in-kind food assistance and provision of access to Fuel efficient Technologies, income generating activities and Agricultural resilience support. Activity 1: Prior to implementation, SHO shall conduct a need assessment to screen vulnerabilities within IDPs’ households, returnees and host communities of the selected LGAs in BAY state.This is going to be conducted with the host community, IDPs, women and youth forming the committee and will be meant to achieve needs assessment, project design, proper delivering of assistance based on the needs assessed and review of projects. Activity 2: Provision of adequate/improved Wet feeding for New Arrivals for Five days. - IncorporateProtection Mainstreaming. Activity 3: provision of fuel-efficient technologies (stoves) to vulnerable households in targeted LGAs in order to reduce reported cases of Gender Based Violence during collection of firewood in the nearest bushes. Activity 4: Provision of Agro-based income generating activities in crop cultivation, livestock andaquaculture,Activity 5: Provision of accessible, effective and confidential complaint mechanisms that are well understood and respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can immediately be put in place. Staff the mechanism with both men and women and ensure it is accessible for children. - SHO will set up a rudimentary Help Desk and rely on PMC. Draft a report of complaints and feedback collected. - In other locations Help-desks will be made available and PMC consulted before and after a registration or distribution. - SHO will sensitise the PMC members, the community leaders and affected populations about the use and purpose of the Help Desk. - SHO will locate Helpdesks in safe locations at the sites under shades. SHO may also have to walk aroundcollecting complaints and feedback or conduct a community outreach initiative with the PMC. Activity 6: Documentation of differences in dietary needs, preferences and restrictions and Monitoring whether women and men (boys/girls) receive fair and comparable benefits from interventions in food assistance, agriculture, livestock, fisheries, and/or livelihoods. Other Activities: Capacity building training, Provision of Cash Vouchers, Procurement of food for distribution and Monitoring and Evaluation.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SHO will ensure prevention and addressing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) in the project through a comprehensive approach, involving policies, training, communication strategies, and complaints mechanisms. Below is SHO’s plan on how to implement mechanisms to prevent and address sexual exploitation and abuse, along with a PSEA communication strategy and complaints mechanism;- Ensure the policy is communicated to all staff, partners, and beneficiaries.- Provide mandatory training on PSEA to all project staff, including contractors and partners.- Include information on identifying, preventing, and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse.- Establish and enforce a code of conduct that explicitly prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse.- Regularly communicate the project's commitment to preventing and addressing PSEA through various channels, including community meetings, posters, and social media.- Establish a confidential and accessible complaints mechanism for reporting instances of sexual exploitation and abuse.- Ensure that the mechanism is clearly communicated to all stakeholders.- Train staff on how to handle complaints sensitively and confidentially.- Reporting and investigation- Supporting the victim and monitoring and evaluation
