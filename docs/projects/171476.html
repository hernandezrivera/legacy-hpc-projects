Project Summary

The proposed intervention will contribute to supporting vulnerable farmers in Area C of the
West Bank in pursuing and protecting their livelihoods as well as increasing their capacity
to access vital resources and respective markets, addressing needs identified within the
Food Security Cluster Response Plan for the West Bank, while combating the negative
effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. The proposed action is drawn from and in line with the
Strategic Objective SO3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with, and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
The project interventions were determined based on the FSS Cluster eligible actions for HRP 2021: “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”, were endorsed by the FSS cluster, and coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture. Therefore, WeWorld-GVC and ESDC are proposing to implement the following:
1. Rehabilitation of 604 dunums of land (8 communities)
2. Provision of 194 livestock dairy production hygienic kits (10 communities)
3. Provision of legal advice to increase farmers’ awareness and overall protection
The action will mitigate the risks faced by target communities and support resilience-
building towards protracted threats and vulnerabilities. This support will be through
responding to the communities’ needs and improving overall quality of life for farmers
and herders by securing safe and efficient access to resources with a focus on an overall
increase in income generation for vulnerable households. Specifically, the rehabilitation of
604 dunums of land in 8 communities will also increase access to agriculture production
and secured food for 666 individuals (121 men, 100 women, 210 boys, 235 girls, 9 PWD)
from 93 vulnerable farming households, thus maximizing production quantity and quality;
the provision of inputs to livestock production for 195 breeders in 10 communities will
enhance quality dairy production and access to local markets; finally, the provision of
legal advice will improve resilience in face of the continue threat posed by the occupation.

The action will target 18 communities in five governorates of Area C: Hebron, Bethlehem,
Ramallah, Middle Jordan valley of Jericho, and North Jordan Valley of Tubas. The
intervention is complemented through WW-GVC’s Community-based Protection Approach
(CPA), which entails protection mainstreaming elements throughout all phases of the
project, especially community empowerment and active participation of the community in
the implementation of the activities, thus reducing protection risks, improving coping
mechanisms, accountability, and enhancing resilience and capacities to respond to
protracted crisis and preparedness. Therefore, the provision of adequate inputs coupled
with the CPA methodology will contribute to mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities, and risks
associated with pursuing sustainable livelihoods given the coercive nature of Area C.
The partnership will capitalize on the partners’ presence and experience in such sectorial
interventions as well as outreach capacity to the communities, as WW-GVC is the focal
point within the WBPC for 73 communities in Area C and ESDC has formidable experience
in land development. The action aims to serve beyond mere material needs, rather
address broader issues of safety and dignity with the intent to promote gender and age
responsive protection, ensure equitable access to services, prevent and mitigate potential
negative effects of the action on different gender and age groups, and ensure adequate
participation.

1 - Needs

According to the latest national Socio-Economic and Food Security survey, conducted before the COVID-19 outbreak, a third of Palestinian households are suffering from food insecurity. The high prevalence of food insecurity in the West Bank lies mainly in Area C at more than 30% in comparison to the overall West Bank at 28% according to the HNO, and is mainly due to the socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities, which are connected to the physical and political characteristics of Area C. With the increasing number of COVID-19 cases in the West Bank, poverty and unemployment increased significantly, with Palestinian families being at an even greater risk of food insecurity than before. The Bedouin and herding communities residing in Area C are among the most vulnerable in the West Bank. These communities are facing threats of forcible transfer and increasing challenges to their vulnerable livelihoods by the presence of Israeli forces issuing administrative orders, settlement outposts increasing land confiscation, and volatile settler violence. Furthermore, movement restrictions linked to COVID-19 have substantially impacted these communities’ livelihoods. The intervention is designed on the basis of several updated and evidence-based assessments conducted by WW-GVC during 2020, collecting quantitative and qualitative data on the vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, and coping mechanisms facing the targeted communities. The assessments highlighted the persisting coercive environment, increasing protection threats, movement restrictions, and restricted access to basic livelihood inputs and services imposed on the targeted communities. An in-depth field assessment update, conducted in September 2020, further identified at a household and community level the needs of food security protection and livelihood sustainability. This assessment, along with instrumental coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture, confirmed that in the 8 communities of Al Jiftlik Abu Al ‘Ajaj (Jericho Governorate), Ad Deir (Tubas Governorate), Bardala (Tubas Governorate), Al Farisiya Al Jubiye (Tubas Govenorate), Khallet Sakariya (Bethlehem Governorate), Wadi Fukin (Bethlehem Governorate), Beit Za’ta (Hebron Governorate), and Al Walaja (Bethlehem Governorate) there is a substantial gap in access to land cultivation, in turn obstructing the livelihood of 93 vulnerable farmer households (121 men, 100 women, 210 boys, 235 girls, 9 PWD). Therefore, there is urgent demand for immediate response via land rehabilitation in order to enable farmers to access quality produce and essential resources to sustain their livelihoods. The assessment also found that there is a significant lack in adequate access to livestock quality products among herders in the following targeted communities: Ein Ar Rashrash (Ramallah Govenorate), Ras Al Tin (Ramallah Govenorate), Ein Samiya (Ramallah Governorate), Al Mu’arrajat Centre (Ramallah Govemorate), Wadi as Seeq (Ramallah Governorate), Marj Na’ja Herders (Jericho Governorate), Ein al Hilwa-Wadi Al Faw (Tubas Govenorate), Al Farisiya Naba’ al Ghazal (Tubas Govenorate),Khashem Ad Daraj (Hebron Govenorate), and Al Ganoub (Hebron Govenorate). All of this impacts access to the market as a consequence of higher costs on dairy production inputs, from which 195 households consisting of 1183 individuals (210 men, 213 women, 364 boys, 396 girls, 23 PWD) are negatively impacted economically and are facing a growing threat of food insecurity.

2 - Activities

WW-GVC and ESDC proposed action will entail organized meetings with stakeholders and duty-bearers to coordinate activities and locations in order to avoid gaps and oversight. Community Project Committee (CPCs), consisting of village council members and community representatives, 30% of whom will be women, will be established to be responsible for activity implementation. They will also support data collection for the baseline and the endline. Beneficiaries will also be selected through the development of comprehensive gender-sensitive selection criteria in coordination with the formed project committee, and will give equal opportunity for female farmers to participate in project activities- especially where cash for work is feasible.1- Rehabilitation of 604 dunums of land in 8 communities. • Al Jiftlik Abu Al ‘Ajaj (Jericho Governorate): Rehabilitation of land will include provision of seedlings, functioning irrigation network, and fencing. The process will also consist of land preparation through tillage and plowing. • Ad Deir (Tubas Governorate): Rehabilitation of land in As Sakoot area will include provision of seeds, functioning irrigation network, and fencing. The process will also consist of land preparation through tillage and plowing. • Bardala (Tubas Governorate): Rehabilitation of land in Al Qao’on area area will include provision of seeds, functioning irrigation network, and fencing. The process will also consist of land preparation through tillage and plowing. • Al Farisiya Al Jubiye (Tubas Govenorate): Rehabilitation of land in Al Qao’on area area will include provision of seeds, functioning irrigation network, and fencing. The process will also consist of land preparation through tillage and plowing. • Khallet Sakariya (Bethlehem Governorate): Rehabilitation of land will include provision of seedlings, rehabilitation of stone wall, fencing, and land preparation through tillage and JCB usage to reach depth of at least 30 cm of soil. • Wadi Fukin (Bethlehem Governorate): Rehabilitation of land will include provision of seedlings, rehabilitation of stone wall, fencing, and land preparation through tillage and JCB usage to reach depth of at least 30 cm of soil. • Beit Ummar (Hebron Governorate): Rehabilitation of land will include provision of seedlings, rehabilitation of stone wall, fencing, and land preparation through tillage and JCB usage to reach depth of at least 30 cm of soil. • Al Walaja (Bethlehem Governorate): Rehabilitation of land will include provision of seedlings, rehabilitation of stone wall, fencing, and land preparation through tillage and JCB usage to reach depth of at least 30 cm of soil. WW-GVC and ESDC will coordinate with the MoA to finalize the selection of locations of lands to be rehabilitated. Local councils will provide relevant plans and drawings and will ensure proper handover of the nominated lands to the project team. Project agronomists will start developing the specific design of activities including creating bills of quantities. Project staff will conduct regular field visits to monitor progress and to ensure quality of the conducted work. Main sub-activities and milestones: 1. Select agricultural lands to be rehabilitated with project committee and MoA; 2. Procure material and services; 3. Carry out land rehabilitation; 4. Monitor delivery and implementation; 5. Handover to project committee/village council; 2- Provision of 195 Livestock Dairy Production Kits in 10 communities/Providing small scale breeders with dairy production equipment, not including veterinary medication. The activity will incorporate the following components: • Coordination with the project committee and MoA. • Project staff will develop the specific design-specific criteria for selecting beneficiaries and activities including preparation of bills of quantities and technical specifications. • Project partners will procure a local provider to deliver the production kits through a full tendering process to ensure quality and cost-efficiency. • Project staff will conduct regular field visits to monitor progress and to ensure the delivery of supplies. Main sub-activities and milestones: 1. Coordinate with the project committee, MoA, FSS, and relevant actors to finalize selected locations;2. Select and verify the selection of vulnerable farmers in line with humanitarian criteria; 3. Procure material and services 4. Deliver the dairy production kit;5. Provide technical support; 6. Monitor delivery and implementation; 7. Handover to farmers; 3- Provision of legal advice to increase farmers’ awareness and overall protection. Before the technical implementation, a legal analysis will be performed in partnership with NRC and/or PA community lawyers. The project will primarily encompass, when feasible, the adoption of WW-GVC Community Implementation methodology (CI), where CPCs are responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of the infrastructures and will receive direct costs through conditional cash transfer modality. Direct costs (which include material and cost of labor) is therefore paid to them upon receipt of a payment request with a progress report (prepared by the sector responsible and verified by Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that certifies the work done and the payments eligibility. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by WW-GVC along with the intervention. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the community lawyer and the CPCs will be conducted. Legal information/Updates and lawyers contact information are shared with the targeted communities through legal awareness sessions carried out prior to implementing any visible activities. As part of providing vulnerable farmers with the means to protect their livelihoods and empower them to be autonomous and resilient, WW-GVC will provide legal support and legal awareness activities to all beneficiaries (231 HHs).

3 - Indicators

WW-GVC and ESDC will monitor the work progress and quality during implementation. This will be ensured through regular field visits to the sites. Staff will monitor progress on agreed indicators and verify the validity of reported data through review of means of verification. These measures will take into account beneficiary participation and reporting throughout implementation and monitoring. Also, the monitoring implies a participatory approach that includes focus group discussions and individual interviews (with men, women, girls, and boys, with and without disability) to assess whether the identified activities take into account the needs and dynamics of the beneficiaries, analyzing the different effect of the initiative on different age and gender groups. After the completion of the intervention, the qualitative impact of the initiative will be addressed through focus groups, interviews, and surveys, collecting the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, boys, girls, elderlies, PWD and their caretakers). Moreover, through the Community Implementation approach, the feedback of the beneficiaries will be collected along with the implementation of WASH infrastructures through WW-GVC Complaint Response Mechanism. The proposed intervention is expected to improve the overall protection of the targeted communities through improving their access to livelihood resources and inputs that will contribute to food security sustainability as well as resilience in the face of displacement. This intervention also emphasizes the importance of strengthening coordination platforms. The expected results are as follow: Indicator 1.1.1: # of people (farmers HH) have access to productive land rehabilitated. Indicator 1.1.2: # of dunums made productive or with improved production capacity (full and partial land rehabilitation, piping and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, etc.). Indicator 1.1.3: # of farmers benefitting from improved access to rehabilitated land Indicator 2.1: # of kits distributed in targeted community (10 communities). Indicator 2.2: # of HH supported through dairy production equipment tools. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project’s main focus will be to provide the needed services in a safe and dignified manner. The project will instruct suppliers and service providers to implement all needed measure to meet “Do No Harm” requirements. All staff involved in the projects will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in We-World GVC’s Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The planned project considers protection as key cross-cutting approach along the design, putting special emphasis in the community participation and empowerment considering the needs of some groups of the communities that are in a vulnerable situation due to gender, age, disabilities or any different element. The “do no harm principle” will be respected and promoted in each action and the design of the project will implement the Community Protection Approach (CPA) that ensures community participation (promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community such as people with disabilities, women or children) in the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in an increased empowerment at the individual and community level. Aligning with non- discrimination policies, protection and vulnerability concerns and community participation, the identified activities are implemented through an approach that foresees the involvement of all community members. Implementation methodology is led by community members through a participatory approach. In the case of using a cash transfer modality, protection precautions are put into place to ensure that the activity will be aligned with WW-GVC’s Child-Protection policy which prohibits child labor and that the activity does not trigger harm or hostility within the community. In addition, the action engages the targeted communities in assessing their vulnerability and in mapping appropriate protection responses, aiming to increase the capacity of the community to make informed decisions about their safety. This approach is built from a multi-sectorial point of view, which allows to understand deeply the needs and the possible responses of the community, considering the specific vulnerabilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposed intervention will improve the access of the most vulnerable communities to livelihood inputs and resources. Direct beneficiaries include PWD and women, ensuring a non-discrimination approach through ensuring equality in the access to the outcomes and benefits of the project. Activities in this proposed action will directly promote the gender equality and the rights of children as a key element in the development, safety and dignity of the community itself. This approach will have a crucial role in the direct contribution to the beneficiaries’ coping strategies development. In this sense, some of the activities will promote these coping strategies in which is expected that young people have a crucial role. Coping strategies will not only be promoted through beneficiaries involvement with local councils and stakeholders, but also in a family and individual level acting through the involvement in legal awareness sessions.
