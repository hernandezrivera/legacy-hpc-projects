Project Summary

The cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict, the COVID-19 Pandemic, chronic vulnerabilities and weak essential services have left over 6 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. The country remains in a critical period of severe food insecurity with 7.24 million people projected by the IPC to be facing severe acute food insecurity in the first half of 2021 and will be in need of urgent assistance. While, around 2.5 million will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with very high acute malnutrition and excess mortality. Around 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years are likely to be acutely malnourished in 2021 and will need life-saving treatment. This includes 313,000 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition and more than a million children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition. At the same time, 483,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are expected to be acutely malnourished and in need of treatment.
Under this project, the proposed activities include prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition in children U5 and pregnant and lactating women using the national CMAM and inpatient guidelines; provision of basic health services at our Stabilization Centers (SC) and community based healthcare using iCCM approach through the outreach clinics; and provision of minimum WASH services to beneficiaries at nutrition sites and at community level. The program will directly target 78,473 children under 5 and 15,623 pregnant and lactating women in treatment of acute malnutrition at 3 stabilization centres and 40 nutrition sites (OTP/TSFP).
To ensure early detection and timely referral of acute malnutrition, community volunteers will be trained to facilitate screening and referral at the household level through Family MUAC approach. During these screenings, volunteers will also provide sensitization on nutrition and hygiene/sanitation practices. Prevention activities will focus on community mobilization and sensitization as well as addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition. In total, 44,881 caregivers will be sensitized on MIYCN topics. These activities include training community leaders, promoting mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSG), and facilitating men group discussions (MGDs) to encourage discussion among male caregivers on critical nutrition and health subjects. To address underlying causes of undernutrition, Action Against Hunger will train lead mothers of MtMSGs in kitchen gardening and cooking demonstrations in collaboration with our FSL program. In addition to these static interventions, Action Against Hunger’s emergency response teams will also continue to provide malnutrition surveillance, treatment and prevention services, and capacity building throughout the country in areas of highest need as identified by the Nutrition Cluster’s Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism.
Disaster Risk Reduction is a key element of the proposed project given the locations and both natural and man-made disasters experienced frequently in these areas. South Sudan continues to face extremely high prevalence of Gender-Based Violence (GBV). Hence, it is imperative that humanitarian interventions are gender sensitive. Action Against Hunger proposes to:
a) continue its efforts to mainstream GBV and gender considerations in its Nutrition, WaSH, FSL and health programs, and b) support the humanitarian community in South Sudan to remain accountable for GBV and gender-sensitivity.
All the proposed project activities will be carried out following the COVID-19 prevention protocols and guidelines provided by the ministry of health and nutrition cluster.

1 - Needs

The cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict, chronic vulnerabilities and weak essential services have left 7.5 million people (26% girls, 26% boys, 24% women and 23% men)—more than 2/3 of the population in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. According to the 2020 HRP, nearly 4 million people remain displaced, 1.5 million internally and 2.2 million as refugees in neighbouring countries. Limited availability and access to health services have contributed to one of the highest under-five mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) and maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) worldwide.Nutrition needs remain high throughout the country despite partners’ efforts. 2019 SMART surveys conducted in all major counties of Action Against Hunger’s interventions show GAM rates above the emergency threshold of 15%: GAM prevalence in Fangak, Gogrial West, and Aweil East was 16.2%, 20.1%, and 15.5%, respectively, while SAM prevalence was 3.3%, 4.3%, and 1.6%, respectively.According to the IPC acute malnutrition scale for May to August 2020, 20 counties including Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Fangak and Aweil South were classified as Critical (IPC Phase 4); while 28 counties including Tonj East, Tong South, Aweil East and Aweil Centre were classified as Serious (IPC Phase 3). IPC Acute Malnutrition analysis released in February 2020 showed deterioration in malnutrition situation in most counties due to high food insecurity, increased morbidity/disease outbreaks, poor childcare practices, limited access to basic services and poor infrastructure. The multi-sectoral component of acute malnutrition and the enabling factors within the country were clear drivers of the significant deterioration of the nutrition situation in South Sudan. In 2020, about 1.3 million children were in need nutrition assistance in the country, including about 300,000 and 1.0 million children projected to be severely and moderately malnourished, respectively.Major contributing factors to acute malnutrition identified during the analysis were the unexpectedly high morbidity partly attributed due to the flooding in over 27 counties in South Sudan. It was noted that the quality of food intake is poor across the country and even in states where acute food insecurity is low (based on IPC analysis), which could be related to behaviour and/or lack of awareness of child feeding practices among caregivers.According to IPC analysis released in December 2020, 6 counties, including Tonj East, Aweil South were classified as Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). The cumulative effects of years of prolonged conflict, the COVID-19 Pandemic, chronic vulnerabilities and weak essential services have left over 6 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. During the lean season:• 7.24 million people projected by the IPC to be facing severe acute food insecurity • 2.5 million will be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) with very high acute malnutrition and excess mortality. • 26 counties will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 47 counties will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4)• 1.4 million children aged between 6 months and 5 years will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021 and need life-saving treatment• 483,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women are projected to be acutely malnourished and in need of treatmentKey drivers of acute malnutrition include - high prevalence of diseases (affecting upto 36% of CU5); poor quality and diversity of food (MAD – 7%, MDD – 15%); elevated level of food insecurity (IPC AFI phase 3 and above) in most counties also contribute to acute malnutrition; poor access to health and nutrition services due to heightened sub-national conflict and flooding mainly in the Greater Upper Nile; COVID-19 related disruptions exacerbated access to services and inter-communal conflict that resulted in destruction of crops, displaced farming households, led to loss of assets, and limited humanitarian access.

2 - Project activities

Action Against Hunger will continue its high-quality nutrition services in the catchment areas of its three stabilization centers (SCs) and 40 static nutrition sites and 25 outreach sites in Aweil East and Aweil Center, Gogrial West and Gogrial East, and Old Fangak,. These services focus on both treatment and prevention of malnutrition in compliance with national CMAM and inpatient guidelines. Children U5 with SAM and MAM and PLW with MAM will be enrolled for treatment. CNV and mothers will conduct regular MUAC screening of children U5 and PLW at community level through family MUAC to ensure early detection and timely referral. Children presenting with SAM and medical complications will receive treatment at our SC. Child-friendly spaces and ECD stimulation are offered at each SC. Children with SAM and no medical complications will be treated through an Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) at nutrition sites. Children and PLW with MAM will receive treatment through Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP). Action Against Hunger will lead sensitization sessions on nutrition, MIYCN, WASH, and promotion of basic health services for mothers and caregivers of children U5 and PLW at all nutrition sites, as well as at the community level. The organization will train staff and community volunteers on CMAM, MIYCN, GBV, Health and Covid19 SOPs and key messages. Community members will be further engaged to prevent undernutrition through mother-to-mother support groups (MtMSGs) and male group discussions (MGDs). Action Against Hunger will train lead mothers of the MtMSGs on facilitation techniques and key MICYN concepts in line with South Sudan’s MIYCN Guidelines. Additionally, MtMSG members will be trained on kitchen gardening and cooking demonstrations to provide both an alternative source of income and nutritious food at the household level in collaboration with our FSL program. To encourage male participation as change agents, MGDs aim to initiate a dialogue around and sensitize men on MIYCN and women’s issues (e.g., SRHR, GBV, etc.) and promote gender equality. This increases male awareness on nutrition interventions and thereby enables women’s participation, as women often need men’s consent to participate in activities outside the home. All prevention and treatment programs will be closely aligned with the organization’s WASH and FSL programs, which ensure the equitable access of families of SAM and MAM cases to water and sanitation practices, supplies and services. This integration also aims to embed resilience into conflict- and malnutrition-affected communities. To further reach basic health services, ICCM+SAM will be introduced in the hard-to-reach area of Paguir (Fangak). This will incorporate mobile delivery of OTP/TSFP to increase coverage for communities facing seasonal stress migration. The organization also aims to introduce mobile response in Gogrial East. Action Against Hunger’s programs also encompass the emergency response teams, which provide nutrition surveillance, treatment and prevention services in areas of high need as identified by the Nutrition Information Working Group. The organization will collaborate closely with other health and nutrition actors and build the capacity of key health and nutrition staff whenever possible. Action Against Hunger will conduct regular monitoring of the nutrition situation in the project area; the information will be used for appropriate actions at the project level. Our SET teams will conduct SMART surveys in project areas regularly to assess the nutrition situation and take appropriate measures as directed by NIWG. SQUEAC assessments will also evaluate access to and coverage of services. In 2021, the organization will also introduce MIYCN Knowledge, Attitude and Practice (KAP) surveys to assess behaviors critical to mitigating under-nutrition.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At Action Against Hunger the PSEA policy is considered while developing organization-specific strategies, policies, procedures and action plans to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. To mitigate risk of SEA and GBV committed by and/or against staff and our beneficiaries, AAH staff are bound to our Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. All staff sign and agree to AAH Code of Conduct, which establishes the organization’s zero tolerance to harm, discrimination, and/or stigmatization of other staff, partners and beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our nutrition and WaSH programs. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger remains accountable to beneficiaries and to ensure that its actions are appropriate, it has continue to use the Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM), which is integrated into program activities to solicit feedback from beneficiaries and first-line implementers. During the period of implementation, feedbacks are received from the following group/category of people at the community level:A. Direct beneficiaries of program, including people with special needs and disabilitiesB. The local communities where our programs operateC. The community-based staff who are the first line implementersGiven that Action Against Hunger community-based staff are the first line implementers who are working directly with beneficiaries on a day-to-day basis, they receive complaints and information regarding the challenges beneficiaries face as well as how programs are functioning including difficulties and things that need to be changed. For feedback and complaints that need immediate action and within the project implementation domain for Action Against Hunger, they are quickly addressed and appropriate actions taken. To ensure that Action Against Hunger staff are constantly reminded of the FCM procedures, program implementation teams are regularly engaged and refreshed about these guidelines and to treat all beneficiaries equally and fairly irrespective of their age, social status or gender. Engaging beneficiaries at different levels and through different approaches has also been useful and greatly helped to shift or improve some components of the program implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Given the context of South Sudan, the risk of beneficiaries being exposed to violence while availing humanitarian services remains high due to different reasons including distance between the service area and their homes. Action Against Hunger teams on ground have we have built close relationships with local Community Leaders and authorities to ensure the safety and security of our operations, as well as, beneficiaries. We ensure close collaboration with security agencies, government counterparts, the NGO Forum, and other relevant stakeholders to exchange information. Staff trainings in safety and security SOPs are regularly done. At least two forms of communication are present at every project site. To mitigate risk of SEA and GBV committed by and/or against staff and our beneficiaries, our staff are bound to our Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero tolerance approach to such cases. We engage community leaders in inter- and intra-communal dialogues to facilitate access of beneficiaries to services and avoid protection risks. Whenever possible, we incorporate Town Hall meetings, including women and men Community Leaders, and formation of gender-balanced committees (e.g. Water User Committees) for management of project activities. This strengthens ownership by communities and contributes to sustainability of the action. By ensuring consultation with both men and women involved in the program (e.g. through Men Group Discussions), we aim to safely mitigate GBV exacerbated by provision of services. To ensure that the beneficiaries access our feedback and complaints mechanism without any fear, we engage with men and women community leaders and members, authorities at local levels and explain them about our feedback and complaints modalities and the purpose of the process. This has helped us to receive honest feedback from the beneficiaries/community to help us improve our program implementation on ground.SADD is used throughout the project as a basis of dialogue to create substantive changes in the objectives and activities; transforming and making the intervention participatory. Data is disaggregated by sex and age (at nutrition site level for admitted children) during admissions, screening (passive and active), sensitization and trainings. Treatment is monitored by sex to identify variations and ensure immediate action is taken in case of gaps. All the data collected is kept confidential. All the beneficiaries are given unique code number to keep the identity safe.

Action Against Hunger provides integrated services to beneficiaries at community level. Key protection services are incorporated within our programs which include GBV focal persons identified at all facilities; training of staffs on GBV risk mitigation and available referral pathways and services; provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA) to GBV survivors approaching the project teams; hiring more female staffs in the project team and committees formed at community/village level; conducting safety audits at our project sites etc.

The production of more food, promotion of resilient activities will go a long way in supporting populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5. The program will follow this policy and all sections of the affected population will be served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The overall food security program is aimed at providing sustainable sources of foods which will positively impact a reduction in the level of malnutrition in our intervention areas. Our nutrition sensitive agriculture activities are specifically intended to reduce the GAM rate among the U5 children. This is complemented by our intervention in the nutrition programs being implemented by Action Against Hunger at static nutrition and outreach sites. This will also lead to reduction in exposure to potential GBV risks for the beneficiaries and their caretakers.
