Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring the that women, girls, boys and men are able to meet their basic needs; and are safe in their communities and receive support when they experience harm in the targeted locations in the South West region. The project will provide multipurpose cash assistance, water, sanitation and hygiene, gender-based violence (GBV) and protection programming to beneficiaries to help them meet their basic needs, be safer in their communities, and receive support when they experience harm

1 - Besoins / Needs

The SW has faced significant levels of violence and insecurity since the escalation of the fighting in 2017 between the Government forces and non-state armed groups. The violence continues to be the highest driver of displacement, with more than 700,000 people displaced, including as the displacement of Cameroonian refugees in Nigeria as well as internal displacement in other regions of Cameroon . Protection incidents are continuing to increase on a large scale, with approximately 10,301 protection incidents reported between November 2018 and November 2019, with the violation of right to physical integrity (including killing, torture and inhumane treatment) as the highest reported incident. Women & girls represent the majority of reported protection incidents, while SGBV incidents remain underreported due to social norms/stigmatization of survivors as well as lack of access to services to report.The conflict in the SW contributed to growing humanitarian crisis with approx. 2.3 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2020, an increase of 80% compared to the previous year. Continued insecurity, displacement, lack of access to farmlands, and restriction of movement have severely disrupted household’s capacity to meet their basic food needs, compounding their vulnerability to shocks, leading to reliance on negative coping strategies with associated protection risks. State & local authorities’ capacity to provide protection for local populations has been greatly reduced, with 700,000 displaced persons and 450,268 IDPs within the NW/SW, as insecurity continues to be the primary driver of displacement. In December 2019, nine divisions, including Meme and Lebialem are classified at crisis phase.
