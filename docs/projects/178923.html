Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to mitigating the degradation of services that has resulted from the physical destruction of water and sanitation infrastructures. The challenge for the WASH sector in Syria will be to identify strategic entry points to restore WASH service delivery. If not dealt with in time, “vicious cycles” of lack of repair and maintenance services, damage and destruction, lack of service delivery may render the restoration of services unfeasible. The accumulation of incremental degradation leads to progressive deterioration of any service and an associated cumulative negative impact on people’s lives. The “vicious cycles” of cumulative impact on all three components (people, hardware, and consumables) during the current protracted armed conflicts in Syria can lead to a condition that is too technically difficult or simply too expensive to reverse.
UNICEF will therefore work to support the repair and rehabilitation of critical parts of water supply systems and infrastructure while being guided by the principle of “Minimum reliable services for All – rather than All services for Some”. The projects supported will be guided by the findings from needs-based vulnerability mapping. Several criteria will be used for the project identification and selection both from an overall and participant perspective, including cost per participant, level of project complexity, neutrality, accessibility, severity scale, number of returnees, quality and quantity of water and sanitation services currently available.
Of note, UNICEF will not support ‘rebuilding’ activities. UNICEF’s strategy in Syria aims at reducing WASH service degradation that resulted from the physical destruction of WASH infrastructure and the reduced Operation and Maintenance of WASH facilities. As such, instead of ‘rebuilding’ WASH infrastructure and ‘promoting capacity building measures for utilities’, UNICEF focuses on conducting light repairs on the critical parts of the water supply and sanitation systems/infrastructure and supporting their operation and maintenance (mainly through the provision of supplies, including water disinfectants). Wherever feasible, UNICEF will explore sustained operation and maintenance of WASH services through capacity development of Water Units and by facilitating establishment of water tariff collection and accounting system. These interventions do not restore the WASH systems to their pre-war levels which can only be achieved through large-scale reconstruction programs. However, UNICEF is able, through these small-scale targeted interventions, to restore a minimum level of service for a high number of beneficiaries.
UNICEF will continue providing life-saving WASH interventions such as emergency provision of water, sanitation, and solid waste facilities and services, WASH-related supply (NFI) distributions as well as improving hygienic behavior and practices of the most vulnerable people. Preparedness actions and contingency planning will also be kept up to date and at sufficient levels to enable timely response to emerging needs, especially in light of anticipated rapid changes in the level of access and possible new mass displacements, that in particular might affect north-west and/or northeast Syria. Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) will be promoted in the northwest, as an alternative to supply of hygiene kits and/or other WASH services.

4 - Needs

After years of conflict, access to safe water and sanitation has deteriorated significantly with 42 per cent of the population relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs and at least 70 % of the discharged sewage being untreated. This coverage drop is the result of physical damage and destruction but also deteriorating infrastructures such as water networks and sewage systems, as well as limitations in regular operational and maintenance services in water treatment stations. Many wastewater stations are no longer operational due to damage or to the non-availability of critical supplies and maintenance. As a result, at least 70 per cent of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are not functional, exposing communities to significant health risks. The lack of electricity constitutes one of the main bottlenecks for the WASH sector, as it limits the existing water network from being able to effectively distribute existing quantities.. The WASH situation in IDP camps continues to be critical, requiring the provision of full WASH service packages while promoting the transition towards more sustainable solutions (such as establishing connections to existing water networks or simplified water and sewer networks).Water quality at point of use remains a major challenge despite some investments in water treatment systems and provision of water disinfectants. This resulted from deteriorating secondary infrastructures such as water networks and sewage systems, as well as limitations in regular operational and maintenance services in water treatment stations.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNICEF will implement the proposed interventions through national/international partners in close collaboration with WASH sector stakeholders and the governmental counterparts. For public infrastructure, UNICEF will coordinate with Ministry of Water Resources, Ministry of Local Administration and Environment, WASH sector coordination mechanism, and local authorities. UNICEF is able to choose from these implementation and partnership modalities:- Direct contracting and procurement by UNICEFThe advantage of this modality is that UNICEF has full control of whole project and procurement cycle. UNICEF will mobilize third-party monitors who will support the utilities in the preparation of the technical design, BOQ...etc. The utilities will authorize the design according to applicable national standards. UNICEF will procure all supplies and services, and supervise the implementation of works- Program Cooperation Agreements with Non-Government PartnersUNICEF enters into Program Partnership Agreements or Small-Scale Funding Agreement. In these two modalities, UNICEF advances program funds to the partner in tranches and closely monitors the implementation on the ground. Since 2011 UNICEF in Syria has continuously strengthened its field presence through establishing 5 hubs in Qamishli, Aleppo, Homs, Tartous, and Damascus and in an effort to ensure close program implementation monitoring. While UNICEF Syria field offices at the governorate level will be directly responsible for implementing the proposed interventions with daily field supervision, the Head Offices in Damascus and Gaziantep will be responsible for quality assurance, technical support to field offices, standard-setting, coordination with relevant stakeholders

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In relation to the activities that will be implemented in the project following mitigation measures will be considered: - Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and "do no harm principle" all the time; - Organize joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard Include community and authorities in targeting - Ensure sensitive information is handled with care- MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities - Ensure the accessibility of collective WASH facilities to people with disabilities and promote gender-sensitive hygiene messages/actions (WASH in schools) - Train partners and contractors on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Furthermore, the key to ensuring the dignity and voice of the intended beneficiaries is the consistent consultation throughout project implementation. In doing so, UNICEF, through its partners will look to integrate suggestions from the local community that improve the implementation of the project and encourage ownership, to ensure that the presence of the organization is not conducting harm within the community. These consultations include focus group discussions with different target beneficiaries and key informant interviews (KIIs) with community leaders, local authorities, and other key stakeholders. Through these FGDs and KIIs, UNICEF and its partners will identify needs as highlighted by the various groups and address these needs through adjustments to the project.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNICEF is co-chairing the PSEA inter-country network with UNHCR since April 2018. In cooperation (and cost sharing) with UNHCR and the UN RC Office, recruitment of an International PSEA coordinator who will lead the coordination and support of UN common work on PSEA is ongoing. The PSEA coordinator will be based in the UN RC’s Office. Further, all UNICEF Syria staff and personnel have received the face-to-face training and completed the mandatory online training on PSEA. With regard to implementing partners, UNICEF is undertaking PSEA risk assessment for all implementing partners and assists in developing related Action Plans based on the findings from risk assessments Among the main points for action plans is the request from partners to roll-out the PSEA training to all their staff. UNICEF further provided and additional training activities on PSEA for most of partners, requested them to complete the online training, and is providing them with financial and technical support. On the other hand, UNICEF is supporting partners to enhance the complaints mechanisms and raise awareness to the community on PSEA and provided implementing partners with complaint boxes and related communication materials. Moreover, UNICEF has whistleblowing and reporting mechanisms and is supporting implementing partners in the due diligence process for reporting SEA cases and investigating any alleged SEA cases.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:- Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards- Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors- Water distribution not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk), children, the elderly, and people with disabilities- Risk of theft of chemicals such as Sodium Hypochlorite and WASH materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities- Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to raw sewage and gases- Lack of access affects implementation and monitoring- Project locations are subject to direct bombing and/or looting leads to loss of capital- Delays in obtaining government approvals for implementation Assumptions: - Inflation rate is stable - Equipment and materials are available in a timely manner - National and local authorities are supportive- UNICEF is the only provider of liquid Sodium hypochlorite in Syria Mitigation Measures: - Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring (water source, water trucks and end-user samples)- Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time- MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities- Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases- ensure that the distance of water collection points remains at a minimum distance.
