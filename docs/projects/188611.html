Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen protection and resilience of Syrian IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities through advocacy, coordination, provision of community-based protection services and legal services UNHCR Syria will continue to lead the Protection Sector/Cluster to ensure coordinated protection interventions programming for safe refugees return. UNHCR’s protection interventions include legal counselling and assistance, protection monitoring, referrals and information sharing, social protection through livelihoods support to most vulnerable people, GBV prevention and response, child protection, psychosocial support and in-kind assistance for persons with specific needs including persons with disabilities, non-formal education, and awareness activities on various subjects targeting women, men, boys and girls. In addition to over 125 community centres providing integrated protection services, outreach to communities will be convened through over 2,800 outreach volunteers. Accountability to affected people will be realized through meaningful participation of communities based on age, gender and diversity approach, structured focus group discussions and enhancement of the community-based feedback and complaint mechanisms. UNHCR will continue its capacity building efforts to ensure services are delivered according to the standards and that accountability to affected population and do no harm principles are respected. UNHCR Syria will continue to lead Protection Sector in coordination of response plans of IDPs and affected people as well it will execute leadership roles in refugee return programmes in collaboration with the Government. Monitoring and evaluation of the planned interventions is based on the reports and observations by the partners and on regular direct observation and ongoing assessment by UNHCR and the comparison of achievements and related financial expenditures with objectives. Evaluation is conducted through different channels including the post distribution monitoring to determine the effectiveness of/measure the satisfaction of POCs with the service and the distribution process in addition to multiple satisfaction surveys and tracking tools related to different protection interventions. Monitoring and evaluation activities are carried out at various levels by partners and agencies implementing subprojects, UNHCR Branch Office, Sub Office and Field Offices. Situation reports are submitted by all UNHCR Field Offices to their respective Supervising Office and Branch Offices submit a corresponding report to Headquarters.

4 - Needs

According to the protection MSNA and UNHCR assessments, lack of civil documentation and HLP concerns, GBV, child marriages, child labour and child protection risks were identified as key protection concerns in Syria. Protracted displacement, lack of services and deterioration of living conditions exacerbated by the negative impact of COVID-19 and currency depreciation have affected all population groups and particularly IDPs. Needs have increased, community resources are overstretched while populations’ resilience capacity has been severely eroded, undermining communities’ ability to address complex protection issues. Returnees experienced challenges caused by COVID-19, deteriorating economic situation, and delayed re-integration, resulting in an increased reliance on humanitarian support. The protracted crisis combined with patriarchal structures in the Syrian society have normalized GBV. Combined with economic hardship, the situation has forced many families to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Changing roles within family structures have resulted in increased domestic violence, causing psychosocial and physical injury for survivors and for children witnessing such violence. Children face heightened risks due to continuous displacement, including family separation, school drop-out, child marriage, child labour, exploitation, abuse, exposure to violence, deepening poverty and lack of access to services, which have a profoundly negative impact on well-being.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented through integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches including 1) targeted support to persons with specific protection needs, persons with disabilities and the elderly; 2) legal counselling and legal representation before courts and administrative bodies to the most vulnerable Syrian citizens on civil and HLP documentation, documenting civil events and enhanced the protection of women and children; 3) legal awareness raising throughout the country 4) advocacy and support to government on civil documentation, HLP and other legal matters, 5) provision of response services to GBV and child protection; 6) psychosocial support; 7) child protection and women’s committees; 8) PSEA awareness raising and response and 9) community led initiatives and community engagement to build resilience and solidary. UNHCR will strengthen its outreach to all people in need. Prevention campaigns and awareness sessions will be provided to reduce harmful behaviors and promote gender equality. Services to GBV and child protection cases will include individual case management and referrals to other services. UNHCR will roll out the global GBV policy and mainstreaming programme in 2022 ensuring GBV is mainstreamed in all sectors and GBV survivors are considered when planning for legal or livelihood activities. Engagement of men and boys on GBV response will be strengthened and women committees established to enhance GBV prevention and response.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when delivering assistance and service. Mitigating measures: Raise awareness among UNHCR, partner staff, and communities about SEA and about all humanitarian aid and services being free. Assess, monitor and enhance the capacity of partners to prevent and respond to SEA allegations. Apply mandatory SEA screening when recruiting staff members. Enhance the complaint mechanisms and ensure follow up while adhering to the standards of data protection and confidentiality. Risk 2: Increased community tension due to the assistance, and/or unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to perceived discrimination. Mitigating measures: Provision of standardized assistance packages with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Harmonized and transparent selection criteria used by all partners and conduct awareness raising and information sharing within the communities on those criteria. Regular monitoring, consultation, client satisfaction surveys, PDM and adjustments to the program to prevent and manage any tensions which may arise. Risk 3. Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement e.g. access to civil documentation and HLP documents, specific child protection interventions. Mitigating measures: Proactive coordination with relevant authorities. Technical and capacity support to strengthen national institutions in service delivery.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Inter-Country Network (ICN) in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network, to ensure adequate and multifaceted prevention and response in place regarding the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to delivering PSEA awareness-raising activities in all UNHCR community centres, PSEA mandatory training and annual refresher sessions are compulsory to UNHCR own and partners’ staff. Complaint and feedback mechanisms, including hotline numbers, email addresses, and complaint boxes, are functional -at the agency and partner’s levels- to ensure and support a confidential and effective reporting system. Since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partner capacity improvement plans have been developed for the partners scored medium and high risk, and UNHCR will be supporting and monitoring the improvement of partners in addressing SEA. Partners who fail to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Fluid context, shifting frontlines and unstable political situation in North Syria. Mitigating measures: Proactively coordinate with UNDSS and sister agencies in terms of security and access issues. Advocate at senior levels of all stakeholders to identify political solutions to ensure safe and continued access. Risk 2: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 3: Delayed/lack of approvals. Mitigation: Regular follow up with relevant authorities, advocacy for uninterrupted access. Risk 4: Resistance and non-acceptance of UNHCR activities from the community. Mitigation: Community engagement and awareness through culturally and context adapted tools and communication strategies, especially for new areas of intervention. Risk 5: Deterioration of the security situation. Mitigation: monitoring and info sharing with relevant stakeholders and temporary closure of facilities if risks raise; security/surveillance at facilities, premises.
