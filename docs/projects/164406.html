Project Summary

The project locations were selected in Idleb, Aleppo (Afrin, Azaz) Districts, Idleb (Idleb, Harim, Jisr Ash Shugur) Districts, and Tell Abiad community as displacement destination, relatively safe and secure, and has big gaps in protection issues and risks. Accordingly, as HRO has its own previous experience in providing CP services in emergency situations and is the focal point of emergency response and referral pathway in Jisr Al Shughour, in addition to different ongoing protection projects funded by OCHA THF, and IOM. Thus, HRO is planning to continue providing CP services in 5 target locations as follows: 1. Jisr Ash Shugur District: already existed CC funded by IOM till the end of June 2020, 2. Afrin district: already existed CC funded by IOM till the end of March, in addition to 3 CFSs within new CCs will be established in 3 suggested locations: Tell Abiad, Harim and Idleb districts. 1 CP supervisor, 1 team leader, 3 case managers, 2 SWs, 6 facilitators, baby sitter are hired in each location. CP staff will be trained on CP SOPs, CM, FTR, UASC, Structured and non-structured PSS, inclusion of PWD, identification and referral, life skills, CP minimum standard. Those trained staff will provide: 1. community based services including: awareness raising on (early marriage, child labour, sexual harassment, importance of education), parenting skills, FTR, PFA, PSS, capacity building for humanitarian workers and community leaders on CP basics, alternative care
and CC-based services: CM, structured, non-structured, and semi-structured PSS, awareness raising, parenting skills.
3 community committees will be established in each location, one for children, and 2 for adults (male, female), Each committee consists of 10 members who will be identified among most influential actors who will be trained by CP team on CP related issues.

4 - Needs

based on child protection situation monitoring -dashboard-February 2020, % key informants who reported the following concerns:- Child labor reported in: Afrin-D: 96.49% , Azaz-D:100%, Harim-D: 90.37%, Idleb-D: 84.25%, and Jisr Ash Shugur: 91.67. - Separation from caregivers reported in: Afrin-D: 17.54% , Azaz-D:43.75%, Harim-D: 53.33%, Idleb-D: 39.37%, and Jisr Ash Shugur: 41.67. According to the results of the 2020 HNO and CP Monitoring survey: The impact of the war continues in the Syrian interior, under the harsh conditions of displacement, displacement, and lack of safety. The needs will double and the vulnerabilities will increase for children and their caregivers. Therefore, it is necessary to continue specialized protection services to reduce the risks to children and enhance their resilience. Accordingly, it will be presented with the aim of enhancing access to child protection services and improving their quality by providing a case management service for children who have child protection issues (psychological distress - violence - separation from the family - child labour - abuse - exploitation - neglect) as it was found that there are 83.70 % Of children have child labour in Afrin, 88.89% are proudly, 84.48% are in Jisr Ash-Shughour, there are 40% of children have a risk of separation in Afrin, 52.38% of them in A'azaz and 37.93% of them in Jisr Ash-Shughour, and psychosocial support activities will be provided Structured and semi-structured for children, after it was found through the CP Monotoring questionnaire that there is 60.37% Children need PSS activities in Afrin, 71.43% of them are proud and 95.69% of them are on the bridge, and awareness raising sessions will be offered to parents about child protection issues, based on the results of the CP Monitoring survey, where it was found that there are 22.59% of carers in Afrin who need For awareness raising sessions, 38.10% of them proudly, 83.62% of them with Jisr Ash-Shughour.

5 - Implementation of activities

First month will include staffing, renting CCs and vans, the following month will be for training staff on CP Minimum standards, CP Case Management, FTR, Structured PSS , inclusion of PwDs, Afterwards, teams will proceed with conducting following activities: CP prevention and mitigation activities incl. 1.Community-based CP for girls and boys: Structured, Simi structured and PSS activities including PwDs, using recreation kits. M&E team will check pre and post-tests to measure benefit achieved. 2.Awareness-raising sessions for parents about parenting and dissemination of key messages about CP (CP principles-Alternative care-parenting skills-self_care skills). sessions will be monitored by M&E and needs of parents for new topics will be considered through conducted FGDs. 3. Adults and Children groups/committees to ensure community’s effective and continuous participation to prevent and respond to CP issues. will be trained by project team. M&E will evaluate beneficiaries’ satisfaction on sessions and performance. 4.community will be trained on CP to scale up its capabilities. As for response activities: (1) Specialized CP services including (case management and referral incl. case found.) for girls and boys, using CP SC SOPs incl. high risk cases: UASC. The project team will ensure the inclusion of PWD (especially children) in project activities through the following: 1- rehabilitation in the CCs to meet the needs of PWD including (rump, handrails, tools and devices as per needs and availability, etc). 2- Providing transportation for PWD to reach our CC and benefit from the introduced services. 3- Activities and materials are designed and updated to consider the needs and sensitivity of dealing with PWD. 4- Staff are trained on inclusion of PWD. 5- the awareness and skills of parents and teachers are raised to better deal with PWD. 6- Complaint boxes are located in safe and easy to access sites through which PWD (especially for children) can submit their complaints

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals and exclude others. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; 2. Ensure consultation with communities related to location to understand relevant community perspectives; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to site locations.4- Care is taken to ensure that specific requirements of men, women and children are taken into account. Especially for people and children with disabilities. rehabilitation works will be conducted in fixed facilities.6- We will actively look to reach children who are at-risk and disadvantaged through identification of IDP population located in areas such as collective centres, as well as children that are not attending school. This will also include children with disability. They’ll be targeted specifically through collaboration with community leaders and CP networks. 2-Risk: lack of experience to deal with PwDs and vulnerable groups incl. teenagers. Mitigation: team Training on inclusion of PwDs. 3. Risk: team and beneficiaries are not aware enough of importance of accountability. Mitigation: Establishing a complaint mechanism, familiarizing beneficiaries with the method of reporting complaints, familiarizing team with importance of this mechanism in order to develop a sense of accountability. 4-Risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups/individuals at exclusion of other groups/individuals. Mitigation: Provide regular training to staff and service providers for a code of conduct and PSEA. 5- Risk of doing harm to girls, and boys and their caregivers by SPs. Mitigation: 1. Staff training on CP minimum standards. 2. technical supervision 6. staff well-being (secondary or trauma). Mitigation: 1. Self-care strategies 2. peer to peer support sessions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Local authorities decide ban the implementing the project or part of it.Mitigation: ongoing IP coordination and sensitization with the local councils. Security situation will not deteriorate.Risk: shift in power balance, increased airstrikes and proximity to ground battles severely compromising access and implementation.Mitigation: Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. Set response plan and designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation so as to achieve the objective, temporarily suspending activities. Targeted population is willing to actively engage in activities.Risk: unwillingness or inability to participate in decision making mechanisms due to cultural norms.Mitigation: building the trust of the population through awareness raising initiatives, leadership trainings and develop sense of ownership to the project.Beneficiaries are interested in receiving psychosocial support and attending awareness sessionsRisk: Individuals, especially children with severe mental disorders may be hindered from accessing mental health serviceMitigation:Sensitivity: Project staff should and will be sensitive to their suffering, treat them with respect and dignity, and avoid creating more harm by requiring them to relive painful experiences through repeated interviewing. Project staff will avoid creating false hopes and unrealistic expectations about what protection and assistance the projects offer.Information provision: the project will support awareness raising and information activities that provide evidence-based, yet culturally sensitive, information about GBV and CP, including information about prevention, and response, care and support, for both women and men of all ages.
