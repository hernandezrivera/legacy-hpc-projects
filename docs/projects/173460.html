Project Summary

Starting early November 2020 (7th Nov), influx of Tigray refugees started crossing to Sudan via Hamdayit (Kassala state), Lugdi & Abdulrafi (Gedaref state) and the numbers started to increase in daily basis, where the totals as of 5th December stands at 48,582 (https://data2.unhcr.org)
The project planned by UNOPS to support the refugees, host communities as well as humanitarian actors in Easter Sudan through provision of technical support for a case load of 30,000 Refugees and host community persons in the following areas:
1) Energy & Environment:
Activity: Provision of collective emergency shelter: Procurement and establishment of solar energy light system for key camp and host community areas (i.e. hospitals/health centers, water collection points, communal latrines area, local market, police/security units, warehouses, offices, main camp roads, etc) (Target = 1 mini-grid solar energy)
2) Energy & Environment:
Activity: “Provision of collective emergency shelter: Procurement and dispatch of energy saver stoves, to protect camp and host community trees. (Target = 3000 units/HH)
3) Health:
Activity: Do physical rehabilitation sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services: Support in planning and construction/upgrade of infrastructure units as well as Construct clay brick incinerators for waste management (Target = 1 Health facility upgrade with waste management, etc.)
4) Health:
Activity: Provided community based health services: Mainly the activity is focusing on procurement and provision of Covid19 items. As UNOPS already the lead agency in the EU-IGAD project under implementation in 8 countries, of which Sudan is one, it will be smoother for UNOPS to place a procurement orders to support SMoH and other actors in the COVID19 response (i.e. PPE testing kits, etc.) (Target = 1 procurement lot)

1 - Needs

The Tigray conflict, which started in November 2020 in northern part of Ethiopia, has led a massive influx of refugees to the neighboring regions of Eastern Sudan. In response to the refugee management, the Government of Sudan (GoS) designated land areas where refugee settlement or camp sites can be constructed to host the new arrivals. This may tentatively involve up to ten separate locations. Land ownership determination and host community consultations should be carried out to ensure local acceptance of the sites. Presently there are couple of sites identified, both of which are in Gedaref State: Um Rakuba; Tenedba (Mafaza locality) and village 8, while in Kassala State (Wad El Heliewo is the identified site) etc. While humanitarian actors and GoS are gearing up to provide support to the influx of refugees, the following are the major needs as identified by UNOPS for both reception centers as well as newly identified refugee camps: Health needs: Since the refugee influx is high and are supposed to live in crowded area with less water supply and hygiene and sanitation facilities, the refugees are highly vulnerable to communicable disease including the recent pandemic COVID 19. Sudan COVID 19 status report revealed that the country is in the second wave of the pandemic with increasing number of positive cases and fatalities. This situation will fuel up the disease transmission to both the refugees, host communities and humanitarian workers. The host community and the refugees are without hygiene and sanitation equipment that help them prevent the disease transmission. The camps lack facilities that will help to screen and diagnose COVID 19 early enough for immediate management, care and support. As the refugee camps as well as host community villages are located away from the national grid there are no power sources readily available, this limits full-time access to certain services i.e. water collection points, heath facilities, communal latrines, police units, and this limitation of access, post a concern for women, children and persons with disabilities. As the refugee camps as well as host community villages are located away from the national grid they are dark after sunset, The lack of light in the camps (darkness during night) predisposes to security threats to the refugees, limiting their access to health, water, communal latrines, etc. Such situations will affect the psychosocial status of refugees, the situation also predisposes to GBV. With COVID19, all refugees and host community should have access at all times to health services units and water source as well as communal latrines 3. Waste management: in relation to COVID screening and other health intervention, the health care needs of refugees is higher. There are frequent medical visits made and a lot of medical waste is generated in and around the camp. The medical wastes are handled in a way it affects the health of the refugee and host community. Having a proper medical waste facilities in the different camps is an important element of protection and health of refugees.

2 - Activities/Outputs

While humanitarian actors and GoS are gearing up to provide support to the influx of refugees, the following are the major needs as identified by UNOPS for both reception centers as well as newly identified settlement camps: 1) Energy & Environment: Activity: Provision of collective emergency shelter: Procurement and establishment of solar energy light system for key camp and host community areas (i.e. hospitals/health centers, water collection points, communal latrines area, local market, police/security units, warehouses, offices, main camp roads, etc) While refugees are settling-in the new sites, there is a need to provide solar energy units to be installed in the health facilities, communal latrine areas, water collection points, local market and main offices & police units as well as main roads in the camp. This is very essential from a protection point of view for girls and women access to those facilities at all times. This power source will also enable health facilities to maintain the cold chain of vaccines and other supplies. The electric supply assures safety and security to the refugees. The KwH need for solar power will be estimated during the site assessment by identifying facilities that need lighting and equipment and machines that need electric power. The procurement and installation of solar power as a mini grid solution will be considered as a solution where there will be solar panel farm, electric utility installation and central management (control) space. The camp management unit responsible for security and power will be give training on how to operate the solar energy and minor troubleshooting. A guideline on how to use the solar power will be developed and circulated for those who use the power. It is estimated that the solar mini grid will help to light up around 500 households. (Target = 1 mini-grid solar energy) 2) Energy & Environment:Activity: “Provision of collective emergency shelter: Procurement and dispatch of energy saver stoves, to protect camp and host community trees. (Target = 3000 units/HH) 3) Health: Activity: Do physical rehabilitation sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services: Support in planning and construction/upgrade of infrastructure units as well as The medical waste incinerator is a simple two-chamber incinerator made of locally available clay bricks, it will generate at least at a temperatures of 800 degree Celsius that make it efficient to burn most materials. We will use local contractors and daily laborers for the construction. UNOPS will develop the design and assign an engineer who will supervise the construction activity. The performance of the incinerator will be to burn up to 15kg/hour. The two-chamber design helps to ensure that the combustion time is sufficient to destroy the products of combustion and minimize any harmful emissions. The incinerator is capable of burning most types of medical waste including textiles, plastics and packaging, most types of drugs, medicines, vaccines and sharps as long as they are mixed with other wastes. (Target = 1 Health facility upgrade with waste management, etc.) 4) Health: Activity: Provided community based health services: Mainly the activity is focusing on procurement and provision of Covid19 items. As UNOPS already the lead agency in the EU-IGAD project under implementation in 8 countries, of which Sudan is one, it will be smoother for UNOPS to place a procurement orders to support SMoH and other actors in the COVID19 response (i.e. PPE testing kits, etc.) COVID19 Response with the second wave in Sudan is specially important to avail PPE kits like face mask and hand gloves both for health professionals and other humanitarian workers. We will establish a centralized hand sanitization system in and around common basic services units (Target = 1 procurement lot)

5.1 - Select the impact of this project on the environment

B+: Medium environmental impact with mitigation

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will have minor infrastructure component, which involves land excavation, and deep hole drilling that may have environment impact on the landscape, fauna and flora. A mitigation measure is in place not to have severe environment damage. An Environment and Social Impact study will be conducted based on the local ESIA guidelines and UNOPS construction manuals. A detailed action plan will be developed based on the assessment result to mitigate risks identified by the ESIA. The host communities and local government representatives will be consulted in every stage of the project implementation starting from the design stage. The project will conduct geotechnical investigations and topographic studies to ensure sustainability of the project, reduce harms, and ensure safety of end users, project implementation personnel and the environment. The project will introduce solar energy for use for the refugee camp and the refugees. The solar power help reduce the use of diesel and biomass fuel that have severe consequences on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The safety and dignity of beneficiaries are considered while developing designs for each construction (incinerators and solar panels…) which includes privacy, risk to GBV, accessibility to the disabled, adequate lighting… The project implementation will have gender screening procedure that is followed by gender action plan that will ensure the involvement of men and women during project implementation. The professional workers and daily laborers will potentially be deployed from the local communities, which ensures knowledge and respect to local cultural and religious practices. This knowledge combined with the understanding of the local socio-political context/power dynamics as well as the environmental barriers.
