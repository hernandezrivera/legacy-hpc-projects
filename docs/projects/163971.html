Project Summary

Despite this precarious and fragile food security situation, household-level food production remains minimal and has not yet been used to compensate for the lack of food availability.
There are opportunities to increase home production for consumption at the household level, which would in turn increase families' resilience by diversifying food sources.
AL Ameen for humanitarian support Organization is seeking support from for Contributing to Improving the Resilience of Affected Syrian Population through Agricultural Livelihoods Initiative (Revitalize) project, which
aims to enhance the resilience of vulnerable conflict-affected population at the household and community level in northwest Syria. This will be carried out through agricultural livelihood assistance. The project aims to achieve this outcome through small horticulture system support (Agricultural Inputs), and women group support (Home-based production), and Vocational and Business skills training (vocational and business training focused on market analysis and gaps) such as Olive Oil analysis techniques, Capacity building for marketing.
This intervention will benefit 525 beneficiaries with different inputs and vocational training.
The project will incorporate support through diverse phased agricultural extension support, primary productive assets for rural enterprise, agriculture inputs, and short-term support that increases access to more effective irrigation techniques, and food processing inputs. These will be providing a foundation for market access and creating value chains where poor farmers can be better involved, both directly and indirectly and strengthen links to markets leading to increasing farm productivity. This will yield higher household-level income and ultimately improve food security. The integration of these activities, in a phased and comprehensive approach, is essential for ensuring a sustainable impact on the productive capacity and long-term resilience to shocks for communities in north Syria.

4 - Needs

Close to eight years of conflict have severely stretched the capacity and resilience of people living in Syria to cope with shocks and stresses brought about by the crisis. The loss of jobs and livelihoods, the depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt, the destruction of civilian infrastructure, large-scale displacement and the disruption of social services have all contributed to increased economic hardship that has eroded people’s ability to cope. The deterioration of basic services continues to undermine the viability of productive sectors, feeding a vicious cycle of unemployment, diminishing resources and resulting in increased levels of poverty. Deprived of the productive tools required to meet their basic needs and in the absence of state-supported basic services, many households are resorting to improvised solutions to address their needs.Regarding the food security status in northwestern Syria, according to REACH January bulletin, in 44 out of 143 assessed communities, people faced problems in securing their basic food needs, either due to the lack of enough funds to purchase available foods or due to the inability to buy food for the prohibitive prices of some items. Most households reported an income of less than 50,000 SYP per month with around 26,000 SYP needed expenditure for the monthly food basket. 78% of the investigated IDPs resort to coping strategies in order to secure their basic needs of food:• Foregoing some food items (40%)• Selling non-productive assets (32%)• Borrowing/buying food on credit (28%)

5 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1.1: Provision of agriculture consultancy and feedback services: 525 beneficiaries (125 female and 400 male) will receive relevant agricultural in the field and in-venue sessions on relevant agricultural topics and best practices. These will be relevant seasonal and specific sessions to farmers on different crop topics, where dedicated agricultural personnel will provide a group of 20-25 farmers meets once a week in a local field setting and under the guidance of agricultural personnel. Activity 1.1.2: Provision of technical agriculture and business skills workshops: 525 beneficiaries (125 female and 400 male) who will receive training workshops on a horticulture production system, modern irrigation system, and Home-based production training to women (Table olive processing - Pickles - Jams - Tomato and Pepper molasse - Dairy production). Activity 1.1.3: Olive Oil analysis techniques: Al Ameen for Humanitarian support" is planning in cooperation with Local Councils to enhance the capacity of specialized staff regarding olive oil analysis by using a spectrophotometer device (chemical analysis of olive oil, through a chemical test to determine the Acidity based on oleic acid, and The Peroxide Value). Activity 1.1.4: Capacity Building for Marketing:"Al Ameen for Humanitarian support" is planning to provide capacity building to beneficiaries.The trainer will prepare special sessions which will tackle the following:• Capacity building for conducting periodic surveys related to the "Home-based production" market. • Capacity building for conducting periodic market studies (supply and demand).• Capacity building in statistical analysis studies.• Capacity building in reports writing. Activity 1.1.5: Monitoring and information dissemination with relevant stakeholders:During the course of the proposed intervention, we will conduct and disseminate relevant agricultural assessments and information about post-harvest, seed system, value chain.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation and child marriage as the top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, the stress associated with war, gender role change, lack of documents. All these poses enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria.Displacement and living in camps are noted as being particularly unsafe environments for women/girls due to the lack of privacy (in tents or in latrines), overcrowding and mixing of people, poverty, financial desperation, and lawlessness.The WoS Protection analysis reveals that 17 sub-districts in Idleb are facing severe, critical and catastrophic situations regarding protection. While some services are available, GBV cases are less addressed and the assistance is focusing mainly on survival pathway support and less on community-based interventions, mandatory for mind-set change. Women and girls occupy a central role in Al-Ameen’s strategy for 2018-2023. Part of the development approach, Al-Ameen seeks to empower women to become more resilient in the conflict setting while securing their safety at all levels. When designing the project, Al-Ameen has applied all its security protocols to ensure that interviewed GBV survivors and leaders of communities are not exposed to any risk for their involvement.Al-Ameen and its partners have a set of policies and procedures to be respected when working with GBV cases.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:• Changes in the security situation or authorities/groups in control of project areas: To ensure that the changing security situation does not affect the implementation of the project activities "Al Ameen for Humanitarian support” will ensure flexible context-informed approaches and mechanisms to deliver the project outputs/outcomes. "Al Ameen for Humanitarian support” will also ensure projects in insecure locations have security plans for staff and the project beneficiaries and sufficient resources to enact those plans with the provision of safety training to the project staff. "Al Ameen for Humanitarian support” will assess where is the safest place to store supplies. If besiegement or road cut-offs seem likely, preposition stock, as appropriate/possible. All critical documents will be saved as soft copies. • Delay or inability to deliver supplies in a timely manner due to border crossing closure or border regulations, insecurity on transit routes or near warehouses. "Al Ameen for Humanitarian support” will carry out procurement, pre-positioning and delivery plans taking into account possible delays in borders or transit. "Al Ameen for Humanitarian support” will go to local purchase project supplies/materials as long as supplies are available in the market.
