Project Summary

The underperforming Gu rains in 2019 have heavily impacted the lives and livelihoods of households in Baidoa and Diinsoor which has led to an increase in the number of households in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and IPC Phase 4 (emergency). Rural populations in the hard-to-reach and undeserved areas around Diinsoor have been severely affected as result of poor agricultural production and limited access to services due to limited coverage by aid agencies. While in Baidoa IDPs require increased cash support to enable them to access food. Due to this dire situation, ACTED proposes an emergency short-term response to address the acute food insecurity and alleviate the humanitarian needs. The project will provide life-saving support to 3,333 vulnerable and drought affected households (20,000 individuals) through unconditional cash transfers in Diinsoor and Baidoa. ACTED plans to distribute four-months of consecutive unconditional cash transfer. Unconditional cash transfers act as a measure to prevent further deterioration of the food security of households and act as a buffer against the continued impact of the failed rains. These unconditional transfers in Baidoa and Diinsoor provide a quick response to increasing needs and enable households to access the functioning markets in Baidoa and Diinsoor.

Project Activities

Unconditional cash transfer: Upon project launch in Baidoa and Diinsoor, ACTED will undertake a kick off meeting with all relevant staff and departments, with tasks assigned accordingly. ACTED will target 3333 households (20000 individuals) in Baidoa and Diinsoor. ACTED plans to scale-up the response and requested budget in 2020, as compared to 2019, due to the deteriorating food security situation in Baidoa and Diinsoor and the increase of individuals in IPC phases 3 and 4. ACTED has the requisite capacity in Baidoa and Diinsoor to facilitate a scale-up. ACTED will establish or reinvigorate Village Relief Committees (VRC) and sign of MoUs with them. The VRCs will support beneficiary selection to ensure the most vulnerable HHs are selected. Before starting beneficiary selection, settlement leaders, elders and representatives from local authorities will be briefed on most vulnerable individuals’ definition and the way forward on selection and registration of beneficiaries. Women will be engaged in the selection and registration of beneficiaries to ensure equal representation from both genders and to ensure transparency in the whole process. Selection of the beneficiaries will follow a selection criteria that will target: chronically ill people, child headed households, female headed households, lactating women, elderly people etc. Beneficiaries will then be registered by the ACTED field team and key information will be collected about the beneficiaries, including demographics, income, savings, food consumption scores, rCSI. ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation (AME) unit will then conduct a verification exercise prior to the first cash distribution. The AME unit and program teams regularly conduct markets assessments to ensure the functionality of markets and appropriateness of cash based responses. Through ACTED’s current UCT programming in Baidoa and Diinsoor, ACTED has found the markets to be functioning and for UCT to be an appropriate mechanism to respond to food insecurity. Distribution of Unconditional Cash: ACTED will distribute four consecutive unconditional cash transfers to vulnerable households. The transfer value is based on the current rate set by the Cash Working Group. ACTED will transfer the unconditional cash to beneficiaries through Mobile Money Transfers (MMT). ACTED will perform unconditional cash transfers to reach the most vulnerable households in the IPC Phase 3 area who do not have the capacity to work, including female headed households, households without access to income, households with high levels of debt, and households who utilize negative coping strategies. In this context the UCT enables these most vulnerable households to meet their food needs whilst also acting as a buffer from further deterioration due to the ongoing drought conditions. ACTED expects to see an increase in the food consumption scores, reduced negative coping strategies, reduced levels of debt and overall increased food security. Monitoring and Evaluation: Throughout the project ACTED will collect key information about the beneficiaries in order to track the impact of the intervention on the lives of the beneficiaries. The AME team will ensure the relevance, effectiveness and efficiency of the activities over the course of the project. Under this project, ACTED outputs and process monitoring and evaluation will be implemented through: registration, one PDM after the first transfer and one endline. During these exercises, ACTED collects information including beneficiary demographics, income, savings, food consumption scores, rCSI. The AME unit will use GPS-linked mobile data collection to triangulate and increase the reliability and accuracy of data. All data collected through the above-mentioned exercises will be gender-disaggregated. While the registration process will collect information on all beneficiaries, the PDM and endline survey will be designed with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error.

Mitigation Measures

ACTED will target both IDP and host communities to ensure that displaced households have access to necessary assistance whilst mitigating risks for other social groups within host communities. ACTED will utilize an inclusive vulnerability criteria which will include displaced households and minority groups. A CFM mechanism will provide every beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to present complaints, including reporting discriminatory delivery of humanitarian assistance, from the selection stage through to the close out of the project. Throughout ACTED’s activities for this action gender and protection will be mainstreamed, with a special focus on targeting and supporting vulnerable groups including women, girls, people with disabilities and the elderly. In order to ensure that the needs of these vulnerable groups are addressed, ACTED will include these specified characteristics as part of the selection criteria and thus ACTED aims to have these vulnerable households as beneficiaries. Vulnerable households, both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, will be made aware of ACTED’s Complaint and Response Mechanism including the hotline during project sensitization, beneficiary selection, registration and verification so that women can raise any protection or GBV related concerns. Users of the CRM are provided with the option to speak to a female member of ACTED’s accountability team. ACTED will liaise with GBV and protection partners and clusters to ensure appropriate linkages are established. As ACTED will be aiming to target female headed households and vulnerable households with women, ACTED recognizes the risks associated with women being the recipients of cash transfers due to protection issues. However, to mitigate the risk of increased protection risks ACTED will strengthen the CRM in the targeted location so that women have the ability to speak to ACTED staff directly through the hotline or face to face. Moreover, beneficiaries will be able to voice their concerns to the VRCs. Moreover, ACTED will liaise with the Protection Cluster to ensure where possible, and where survivors are willing, to refer these cases to protection actors. Moreover, MMT provides beneficiaries with increased protection as they have a unique code to access the sim card and therefore the funds. Therefore, ACTED will sensitize the beneficiaries to the importance of keeping this code secure. When targeting villages ACTED will ensure that not only the most vulnerable are targeted but that there is a balancing of clans targeted. Clan balancing is a critical step to ensure that resources are shared appropriately and to mitigate the risk of violence. ACTED ensures access and safety in rural areas of operation through developing relationships with key community leaders and building community acceptance. ACTED’s field teams are composed of representatives from multiple clans to ensure acceptance and accessibility by ACTED staff in the targeted villages.

Please provide further explanation

ACTED’s emergency short-term cash response project aims to alleviate immediate humanitarian need of IDP and host communities. To mitigate the risk of the humanitarian needs reverting at the completion of the project, ACTED will identify longer-term project’s either managed by ACTED or partner agencies in the targeted area. By developing a linkage and layering this life-saving response with longer term resilience projects, ACTED is able to ensure that the gains to the beneficiaries is extended as they are enrolled in longer term projects. ACTED will coordinate with partner agencies and consortia to identify their current and planned activities, to understand the gaps they identified as well as to see how this necessary life-saving response can be linked with their projects.
