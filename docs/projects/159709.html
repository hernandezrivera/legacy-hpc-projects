Project Summary

This project is designed to provide emergency and lifesaving nutrition services to vulnerable drought-affected populations living in Balcad district of Middle Shabelle. The intervention will target pregnant and lactating women (PLW) together with children under the age of 5 years among host communities, IDPs and returnees in Balcad district where wocca is the primary rationalized partner. We will operate one fixed outpatient therapeutic center and 3 mobile out- patient therapeutic programme teams targeting six drought-affected villages/settlements in Balcad district. The static facility will be established in Muki-dheere settlement with the highest population among the targeted locations while the three mobile teams will plan their outreach in a way that serves the remaining 5 locations namely; Kurshaale, Mareerey, Bulo sheikh, Bananey and Bulo Doonka villages. WOCCA will engage UNICEF, for backstopping during training of the project staff ,community health workers and hygiene promoters and caregivers (for the mothers MUAC approach) on nutrition screening, case identification and Integrated management of acute malnutrition(IMAM).The trained staff shall be deployed to the targeted villages, mobile and static OTPs where they will screen and refer to OTP children under 5 and PLWs. Self referall by caregivers trained on mothers MUAC approach will be highly encouraged to intensify project coverage. Children under 5 with SAM conditions and no medical complications will be enrolled in the static and mobile OTPs for treatment while the cases with medical complications will be referred by the mobile clinics and CHWs to the SC in Waberi star village/Balcad town managed by WOCCA for treatment and nutrition rehabilitation. Children under 5 and PLWs with MAM conditions will be referred to TSFP center managed by WFPs cooperating partners in the region. The project will also provide micronutrient support by distributing multiple micronutrients (MNPs) and Vitamin A supplements to pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of 5years through the static and mobile facilities.UNICEF has been supporting WOCCA with nutrition supplies and technical guidance on previous nutrition projects within the targeted locations and currently supporting Wocca's SHF funded project running in selected locations in Mahaday and Balcad disricts. Therefore, for this project WOCCA will also engage with UNICEF to provide nutrition supplies and technical support for management of SAM cases .

Project Activities

(1). Training on effective acute Malnutrition management for project staff and community health workers (CHWS).(2). Community mobilization and sensitization. (Awareness events within the community with key persons - government leaders, village elders, religious leaders, women leaders and the general community, on malnutrition causes, the misconceptions about nutrition, prevention mechanisms and the services offered by the project).(3). Community screening for malnutrition and referral to the appropriate component (i.e. Outpatient Therapeutic feeding Programme (OTP) or Stabilization Center (SC) targeting children under five boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). (4). Treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children 0-59 months old through Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (Outpatient care) and/or Stabilization Center (Inpatient care) done through distributions of Ready to Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTFs) and routine medicines; medical and nutritional monitoring of the patient. (5). Treatment of moderately malnourished pregnant and lactating women through referral to targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP).(6). Treatment of moderately malnourished boys and girls under five years of age through referral to targeted supplementary feeding programme (TSFP).(7). Provision of De-worming tablets and vitamin A supplements to boys& girls. (8). Provision of Multiple micronutrients supplementation for pregnant and lactating women and Iron supplementation for pregnant and lactating women.

Mitigation Measures

1). Mitigating risks of exclusion and marginalization (especially where Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance and where Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes):- - IDPs displaced by the floods, drought and the ongoing conflict in the targeted regions face risk of exclusion from accessing basic humanitarian services at the camps. Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs and minorities from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries, discrimination from host communities, lack of access to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement, IDPs and minorities lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. Women and child care organization will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Using participatory beneficiary targeting and selection approaches cognizant of age, diversity, disability, vulnerability and gender-specific needs, to ensure the most vulnerable and minority groups are targeted 2. Recruiting individuals from both the host, minorities and IDP populations in the implementation of the project in order to benefit the local economy as well as to ensure a non-discriminatory recruitment process (Do no harm) 3. Conducting sensitization meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles, more over the project has proposed mobile OTPs or outreach teams which will take services directly to the people 2). Mitigating risk of external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection (especially where Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services and Delivery of humanitarian assistance affects peaceful coexistence and spark intercommunity conflict over access to resources)- Wocca will promote equitable and impartial access to all; we will Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities and make decisions. We will apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries to ensure the displaced as well as the mobile populations are not excluded, lack of documentation will not necessarily exclude individuals from accessing services Wocca will employ staff who are representative of all clans and groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and ensure all staff and members receive protection mainstreaming training, they will be sensitized sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. This will ensure that our staff play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voices of marginalized groups are represented. To further avoid community tensions between displaced and host communities we will provide the IDP/local/host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages. Gender balance of distribution teams and implementation committees will be considered to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. we will ensure that the mode and frequency of distribution minimizes safety risk putting in mind that beneficiaries may face, intimidation, sexual assault while going home.

Please provide further explanation

IDP's in Middle Shabelle have been displaced the past 2 to 5 years due to drought, clan conflict and floods in the area. Recent reconciliation meetings have opened the doors for most people to return to their villages of origin thus making the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable to enable durable solutions/resilience. In addition, Wocca working with DDG also supports processes to make traditional conflict resolution mechanisms more representative and capable of solving civil disputes and aligning with the formal justice sector in South-Central Somalia. Wocca with the approval of the middle shabelle region plans to uplift the Balcad stabilization center to an MCH which in addition to offering the normal nutrition services will also provide primary health care. systems strengthening will lead to resilient nutrition and health interventions able to withstand shocks in the future which this project aims to achieve through capacity development of the MoH and local structures like the community health committees and CHWs resulting to longer term interventions anchored at community systems. Sustainability/handover process is built into the project that will ensure smooth phase out process leading to the MoH and the local community taking over completely by end of project implementation. The entitlement of beneficiaries to services/utilities will be legally recognized.
