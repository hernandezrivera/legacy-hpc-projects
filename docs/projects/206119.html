Project Summary

The project intends to provide a holistic, comprehensive and intersectoral response to the problems of the Education System in EES and Lakes State, starting from the assumption that schools represent platforms through which also Protection, Nutrition, Hygiene, Health and Psychosocial needs of vulnerable children can be addressed. Moreover, AVSI response wants to support the entire education system, starting from nursery and primary school, up to secondary and university program, and including the alternative programs offered by the Accelerating Learning Program (ALP), such as the establishment of Adult Literacy centres.
As the main sponsor of St. Mary College, the only institution in the country offering a 3-year diploma program devoted exclusively to the training of primary school teachers, AVSI has contributed to mitigate the downfall in the public and private investment for the training of new teachers and dealt with the challenges of providing in-service teacher training to primary schools.
Finally, after 14 years of experience in running a comprehensive and personalized scholarship program for primary and secondary students in South Sudan, AVSI has refined an approach that has proved to reduced dropout, improve cognitive results, and promote female education. Therefore, learners and teachers from all the different steps of education system will be supported in an integrated way, by providing scholastic materials, training and sensitization sessions on subjects such as education, child protection, SGBV, health, psychological well-being including early-entrance-into school awareness campaigns.
In order to avoid the absenteeism of teachers in schools, the project intends to provide an integrated and comprehensive support to the teachers through the provision of food incentives, teacher mentoring in the schools, support to county education departments and payams, re-service trainings for female teachers in order to increase the enrolment of female teachers (only 20%). Furthermore, considered that many of the teachers have not completed S4 it will be important to complete their certificate, while they continue the teaching.
Structural interventions will be done in schools to improve the quality and safety of the learning environments, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines and school kitchens, erection or rehabilitation of fences, construction of boarding schools or dormitories, drilling or maintenance of boreholes to ensure that all the schools are having water point, for learners to get access to safe and clean drinking. Schools will be used as an entry point to promote good hygiene and health practices through hygiene sessions, HIV/AIDS prevention and reproductive health sessions including life skill trainings.
The students and teachers will also be supported in improving their food security status, by receiving training and agricultural inputs for school gardens for their daily consumption. On the basis of holistic concept of education, the project aims to involve also the other relevant actors in this process, first of all the parents and the communities at large. For these reason, parents, community members, PTAs & SMCs will be involved in campaigns and meetings to promote the importance of education and discuss about sensitive topics such as education for girls, violence and SGBV, child protection, among other.
The objectives of the project are: To improve the quality of education and to ensure access to safe learning spaces for vulnerable children, adolescents, and youth; (including young mothers and CwDs) To provide access for children, adolescents and youth to integrated social protection services; To promote resilience and capacity building of teachers, parents, and community at large.
The target will be 13,782 (60% girls and women,with a special attention to CwDs; focusing on host communities as well as IDPs and returnees in specific locations near to the border).

1. Needs

There are 3.6 million people estimated to need education services in 2022 in the Republic of South Sudan based on Feb 2022 HNO. Of the estimated population, 97% are children, 50% are female and 15% are with disabilities. The interplay of underlying forces such as COVID 19, broader socio-economic challenges, destruction of some schools in prone-flooded parts of the country and insecurity in volatile areas overwhelmed the education system and impacted on school functionality. Indeed, the prolonged closure of schools (March 2021 – April 2021) for all grades except the candidate classes (Grade 8 &S4) as a result of COVID 19 exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. Recurrent communal violence through 2021 in Lakes, Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria, Warrap, Unity and Western Equatoria led to school closures. The displacement of teachers and children and revenged killings limited children's access to schools. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. There was a significant learning loss due to these factors. The assessment findings cited the delays in the payment of teachers’ salaries and incentives across South Sudan as the main reason for the high teachers’ attrition and absenteeism rates and poor quality of teaching in schools. AVSI’s 2020 multi-sector survey findings reveal the same trend in Eastern Equatoria and Lakes for high attrition and absenteeism rates due to delays in payments of teachers’ salaries. The survey revealed that 43% of its population in Magwi County pulled out their children from respective schools for lack of teachers or truancy on the part of the teachers. Direct and indirect costs (SDF, volunteer teacher payments, uniform, examination fees) were cited as barriers to regular attendance and drop out in Eastern Equatoria and Lakes states. In Lakes state, the most stressing factors for not attending school by both children below 10 years and up to 15 years is lack of money to pay for scholastic materials & parent contribution fees. Other barriers to children/adolescents accessing education in Eastern Equatoria and Lakes state are inadequate learning spaces, untrained teachers, hunger, distance to school and lack of school supplies. 60% of ENA assessed schools do not provide food for learners; thus, the reason why hunger is a key barrier to education and one of the determining factors for regular attendance, performance and retention. Early and forced marriages and teenage pregnancies are widely reported in the ENA as reasons for drop-out. In both AVSI’s multi-sector surveys in Lakes and Eastern Equatoria, lower classes P.1- P4 girls’ enrolment is promising but it keeps on reducing in the upper classes (P.5-P.8). Over-age girls who enter into the education system late are vulnerable to early and forced marriages and pregnancies before completing their primary level education simply because of some harmful cultural perceptions inherent in the communities. During the prolonged closure of schools, a sizeable number of girls fell victims of forced marriages or teenage pregnancies in Eastern Equatoria and Lakes. Children with Disabilities (CwDs) also face numerous challenges in accessing quality education because of lack of mobility or assistive devices. Over half of schools in Eastern Equatoria do not have latrines accessible for CwDs. In addition, teachers are not trained to foster or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for CwDs. Half of the schools in AVSI’s multi sector survey arereported to have no access to safe clean water and lack menstrual health management to improve female retention and progression. Over 70% of schools in South Sudan reported having a washroom for girls and half said they had never received dignity kits for female learn.

2. Project activities

1. Non-formal education - ALP Centres/Pastoralist education • Support to the ALP Centres so that the over-age children who have missed opportunities for education can enrol in the centres (specific activities include social mobilization campaigns, provision of teaching and learning materials, provision Psycho Social Support for Learners, payment of teachers’ incentives); • Support pastoralist education in Kapoeta and Lakes • Establishment of Adult Literacy centres in pastoralist communities. 2. Teacher Education and Professional Development • Improving professional and cultural capacity of teachers, educators and operators (specific activities include training on teachers’ education professional development, Training Gender Responsive Pedagogy, Inclusive Education for CwDs): • Training for teachers, PTA, SMC and some pupils on life skills, Mental health, PSS and counselling - Establishment of referral pathway - parenting education SGBV awareness • Payment of incentives for teachers 3. Community Engagement • Strengthen community and parental engagement to increase enrolment and retention of learners, especially girls and CWD •Community mobilization of community leaders and key people to carry out outreach to sensitize families in sending their children to school at the right age • Capacity building workshop on school maintenance, resources mobilization and school data management to school administrators, Payam Education supervisors and County Education directors – Training for teachers, PTA, SMC 4. Life Skills • Provision of recreational materials and organization of recreational activities. (include: peace education training to club members who will act as peace club peer educators in their schools) • Formation of hygiene clubs in primary schools and training on how to keep compound clean • Hygiene promotion and sessions on diseases prevention (i.e. Cholera, HIV/AIDS, Ebola…), Reproductive Health, MHM (distribution of sanitary kits to grown girls) awareness campaigns - Sessions to sensitize female adolescents on girls’ education, early marriage, SGBV 5. Infrastructure • Increase the provision of inclusuve education Infrastructure (Temporary Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines, chain-link fences, kitchens/stores, desks distribution etc.), which allows CwDs to regularly attend classes as well as girls (i.e. segregated latrines) 6. Teaching and Learning Materials • Provide learning materials to formal and non-formal education (ALP centres, PS and SS - scholastic and teaching materials, textbooks (new curriculum) and hygiene supplies) • Provision of ECD kits to nursery schools 7. Education opportunities for girls • Enhance access to educational opportunities for girls. • Reproductive Health, MHM (distribution of sanitary kits to grown girls) awareness campaigns - Sessions to sensitize female adolescents on girls’ education, early marriage, SGBV 8. Child Protection • Periodic school-based meetings with PTAs, SMCs andCommunity leaders on Education and Child Protection status in their area – Nutritional screening and anthropometric measurements in schools - Provision of food and WASH facilities to vulnerable schools, • Conducting school safety assessments 9. School gardens • Training and delivery of inputs for school gardens to ensure children are benefitting from school meals • Strengthen the capacity of teachers on school garden 10. Safe Water • Drilling/Maintenance of boreholes in order to ensure 75% of schools in Lakes and Eastern Equatoria have water points • GBV/Protection risk mitigation audits in the schools
