Project Summary

As part of its shelter response, in line with SO1 Provide lifesaving and life sustaining shelter & NFI support & SO2 Contribute towards the resilience and cohesion of communities and household by improving housing conditions for the affected population, PUI aims through this project to provide vulnerable, conflict-affected populations with access to improved, safe and healthy living accommodations.
This will be achieved by providing:
- Rehabilitation of 750 partially damaged apartments benefiting in total 3750 individual in Rural Damascus, Damascus, Dar’a, Dier Ezo Zor, Aleppo, Hama
- And as response to any new displacement waves in effects to frontline shifts PUI will provide rehabilitation of 3 collective shelters (approximately 450 individuals )based on identified needs in Hama, Idleb and Aleppo.
PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2020 representing the sixth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI makes sures to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.

4 - Needs

With the protracted crisis humanitarian needs in Syria are exacerbated in 2021. According to the draft of Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, the shelter situation has continued to deteriorate with overall, 5.88 million people in need of shelter assistance. PUI has gone further in the identification of problems and needs aiming for a deeper understanding of the needs in selected areas to inform programming. The Collected information showed that: • More collective shelters are expected to be built in Hama, and Idleb, in case a shift of control and frontline occurred. • In Hama and Aleppo governorates, as per the need assessment conducted in Jan 2021; showed that there are currently 12 collective shelters established, hosting IDPs fleeing intense hostilities in North West Syria; these shelters being located in former schools, unfinished buildings and former hotels, that do not meet the standards of decent housing. No adequate privacy, nor protection and poor sanitation facilities; some rooms used by the families to cook, sleep, bath and live are in dire conditions, having openings in some of their walls and for some shelters doors and windows are also in poor conditions and do not play their role of protecting families living in the buildings. • In Damascus, Rural Damascus, Deir Ez Zor, Aleppo, and Dar’a, due to intense hostilities, populations lack of assess to adequate shelters., as their houses are partially damaged, and due to ongoing economic hardships, they are currently no longer able to rent, or fix their own houses and forced to seek support from their close relatives, staying with them in crowded and undignified spaces or to stay in collective shelters.

5 - Implementation of activities

In line with shelter sector guidance and with full cooperation with MOLEA and local stakeholders under proposed project firstly; PUI will rehabilitate collective centers to provide safe, healthy living accommodations for residing IDPs based on Sphere Minimum Standards & shelter sector Guidelines. The scope of work includes Civil works; Sanitary works , Electrical works , sensitization of bathrooms for persons with disabilities or elderly populations; and Safety/protection works for the common spaces in the building. After completion of works PUI will hand over the shelters to local stakeholders. On the other hand, PUI will rehabilitate partially damaged houses to improve the living conditions for conflict affected population , The selection of locations of the houses will be in line with the Shelter Sector acknowledgment that location is cleared of UXOs and mines; the level of damages where only light damaged apartments can be supported with no structural damages , ownership documents are available , also prioritization will be giving to most vulnerable groups (Female-headed households; Families with elderly/chronically ill members; people with physical/mental disability; PLW; Families with children under 2 and under 5; Family hosting IDPs and Large families more than 7 ). Scope of work will include removal of debris; Closing of holes in roofs; staircase; Replace/ installation of doors and windows; Repair kitchen and bathrooms; Repair floors; and Fixing / installing electric system. After completion of works apartments will be handed over to owners. PUI teams will follow up on the implementation to ensure satisfaction of affected population, and that delivered support is as per agreed standards and guidelines.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Risk: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. (Likelihood: High, impact: High)Mitigation measures:1.PUI to advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites.2.PUI to provide gender segregated sanitation facilities sufficiently close to dwellings.3.PUI to provide lighting devices around shelter and WASH facilities.4.PUI to work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them.5. PUI to conduct extending consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population including women, girls and PWD.-Risk: Safety of beneficiaries/communities are at risk due to structural integrity of buildings and/or the close presence of ERWs to potential work sites. (Likelihood: High, impact: High)Mitigation measures:1.PUI to conduct structural assessment of buildings by qualified personnel.2.PUI to Repair / rehabilitate the buildings only if the building is structurally safe.3.PUI to work closely with local authorities, communities and agencies involved in ERW clearance to ensure that the building/area is free from ERW.4. PUI to orient contractors/workers on risks associated with the presence of ERW and ask them to report immediately if a suspicious object is found and suspend the work until the suspected object is removed.-Risk: Lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights including civil documentation. (Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium)Mitigation measures:1.PUI to coordinate with MoLAE to ensure that alternative documents to ownership are recognized by all governorates and beneficiaries as well.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions 1. No curfew or lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic 2. Stable security situation 3. Consistent humanitarian access 4. No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound 5. No mass movements of populations 6. Availability of material in the local market 7. Acceptance of community Risks: (Mitigation measures) 1. COVID-19 new waves and lockdown > Mitigation measures: 1- Good coordination with the concerned stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 2- full adherence to WHO recommendations 2. Local population does not accept PUI´s intervention: ensure full engagement of the community leaders & local stakeholders in project design and well introduction of project. 3.lack of approvals; as a mitigation PUI will continue to work with other humanitarian actors to advocate for access 4. Delay and change in the approval process: PUI will ensure close and constant coordination and follow-up with the concerned stakeholders and anticipate the request for approvals as much as possible 5. Volatile security situation: PUI will ensure close follow-up with concerned stakeholders to anticipate any deterioration of the security situation, conduct security training for newly recruited staff 6. Fluctuation in the exchange rate: PUI will keep the donor updated about the evolution of the economic situation and report the impact on the project in order to discuss adaptive measures 7. Lack of contractor: PUI will conduct constant survey to expand the list of contractors and adopt Frame Work Contracts. 8. risk of eviction and closure of collective shelters, as a mitigation PUI will follow up with local stakeholders to anticipate such measures. 9. Risk of fire in collective shelters as mitigation measure PUI will provide necessary extinguishers
