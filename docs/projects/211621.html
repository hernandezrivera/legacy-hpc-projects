Project Summary

This project will prioritize the strengthening of the referral pathway as well for GBV survivors and SRH to high care and make sure follow-up is established and concluded.
In breaking the barriers of accessibility to humanitarian services/aid and inclusion of people living with disabilities, it is paramount to implement a multi-sectoral project so that all facets of needs of the women, girls, and PLWDs around protection (GBV), SRH/ASRH (e.g. family planning, childbirth, etc.). Considering that women, girls, and PLWDs make up 83% of the humanitarian need for the year 2024, LESGO has proposed a multi-sectoral project to approach the stated needs within 9 months of implementation by integrating the multi-sectoral emergency response and assistance to women, girls, and people with disabilities across Michika, Madagali and Mubi North LGAs of Adamawa state via Protection (GBV), and Health services with specially consideration for the disables.
In furtherance to support the prioritization of the protection of women, girls, and PLWDs, LESGO will be training GBV frontliners, CHV, PSS experts and health workers of varying categories to conduct active case search/finding of GBV/SGBV survivors, malnourished children (especially disables), Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs), stigmatized individuals, etc.; provision of case management of rape services, sensitization and awareness to promote behavioral change among the community members and leaders; upholding the policies and guideline that support PSEA, SGBV, ASRH, etc.; provision and distribution of needed dignity kits, hygiene kits and supporting safe delivery of due pregnant women with clean mama kits; conducting a door to door step sensitization using IEC materials certified by WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA, NCDC and SMoH. The project is targeting 800 girls and women including PLWDs for dignity kits as well as 600 hygiene kits, a total of 700 delivery kits coupled with ANC and PNC services, and lastly, 4,500 (1,500 per LGA) households with children under 5 and pregnant women especially PLWDs will benefit from the distribution of insecticidal mosquito net.

1 - Needs

In northeastern Nigeria, acute malnutrition is a significant issue, with low food consumption and poor health-seeking behaviors contributing to the problem. Poor health services, feeding practices, food insecurity, and access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services play a significant role in this issue. Children suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM) are up to 11 times more likely to die than well-nourished children. About 10% of SAM cases result in medical complications, and even when hospital care is provided, the case fatality rate remains high due to healthcare providers using practices suitable for normal children but dangerous for SAM patients. The humanitarian context in northeast Nigeria remains challenging, with 7.8 million people expected in 2024. Children, particularly girls, make up 83% of those in need. Vulnerabilities vary based on location, age, displacement status, and the interplay of factors such as conflict, insecurity, climate change, disease outbreaks, food insecurity, malnutrition, population movement, camp closures, economic factors, and historical underdevelopment. Protection concerns are pressing, particularly for women and girls who face threats from violence, abduction, rape, gender-based violence, forced and child marriage, and other violations of their rights, this is also extended to PLWDs. With decreased humanitarian funding expected in 2024, these vulnerabilities are expected to compound as they bear the brunt of limited interventions unless adequate steps are taken. Approximately 1.3 million individuals need GBV prevention and response services across the BAY states in 2022, with the majority in Borno State, Adamawa, and Yobe. For women and girls, especially pregnant women, who may face life-threatening childbirth complications, as well as lactating women, caring for newborns throughout the chaos, whether they live or die in a crisis often depends on their access to basic sexual and reproductive health services. In Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa, the three most affected states by the insurgency crisis, it is estimated that 53 percent of internally displaced persons are women and girls, of whom about 1.73 million are of childbearing age, with 276, 000 pregnancies expected in 2017. In Nigeria, about one Nigerian woman dies every 10 minutes from pregnancy-related conditions, deaths that could be prevented if women and girls have access to education and reproductive health services, including family planning. The risk of death is heightened in humanitarian crises that displace populations and undermine community social support mechanisms, limiting access to health facilities and workers to take care of the special needs of women and girls. In humanitarian settings, ~35 million girls and young women of reproductive age (15–24) are in urgent need of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) information and services. Young women and girls in humanitarian contexts are particularly vulnerable to unwanted pregnancies, unsafe abortion, gender-based violence, and early and forced marriage. We sought to understand girls' and young women's experiences with unwanted pregnancy, abortion, contraception, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), gender-based violence (GBV), and forced marriage in an IDP camp in Northeastern Nigeria.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific health needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored in to the program design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programs include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the community health volunteers to ensure it is gender sensitive. In addition, timing of health promotion sessions and other committee meetings will be considered to ensure suitability. A close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by LESGO to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding on specific issues, if needed, topics will be re discussed. After the training cycle is completed, LESGO staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members for the establishment of CPFP and the subsequent design of an action work plan and afterwards to follow up on the action plan and its results. The CPFPs will be closely collaborating with LESGO case workers at each step of the activity’s implementation. LESGO facilitator will ensure continued assistance and support in the development of community designed and led actions as to avoid imposing external model but to build on sustainable solutions.Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities. The project team will continue to assess the situation and respond to immediate needs among vulnerable groups, especially women, in the areas of intervention. All activities will also include male representation, when possible. In light of increased risk of SGBV as a result of the on-going conflict, insecurity, and subsequent displacement, LESGO will also look to increase awareness amongst staff and communities regarding SGBV and other protection risks. To ensure that a gender-based violence (GBV) prevention is integrated into all the project activities, all staff will be provided orientation on GBV core concepts and appropriate referral instruction. Furthermore, all LESGO staff is bounded to LESGO Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all LESGO operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

LESGO will ensure accountability to affected populations through various activities. Firstly, in the present project, an accountability and quality improvement committee (AQIC) has been set up which is composed of community leaders, religious leaders, representative of women, youth and other social groups and a representative of all ethnic group present in the community. The AQIC plan community actions, implement and act as a feedback mechanism between LESGO and the community. This committee has had monthly meetings since its inception and had discussed several issues affecting the community. During the meetings, problems affecting the community, even those not related to health were also aired and positive solutions sort from within the committee and in collaboration with other partners. The AQIC committee is currently acting as community sensitization agents for behavioral change on the need for visibly pregnant women to keep their clean delivery kit in a safe and hygienic environment. However, Information about the project will be shared using pictorial materials. The LESGO team is gender-balanced and speaks the local languages to ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant and timely information regarding the project.The MEAL department will also ensure a feedback mechanism that can be received using various means, such as FGD, interviews and feedback boxes. Information on relevant services including referral pathway if available will be shared in coordination with the Protection team and WASH teams. Furthermore, accountability towards beneficiaries will be guaranteed through the application of internal tools already available, such as the PSEA Policy, Child Protection Policy, Code of Conduct and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs); as for the latter, LESGO SOPs on Data Protection and SOPs on Complaint Mechanism will be particularly relevant for the current.
