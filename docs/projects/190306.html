Project Summary

As of February 2022, a total of 4.51 million IDPs have been identified thus far across the country. Of the total displaced population, the main causes of displacement were conflict which displaced 3.63 million IDPs (81%). Tigray has remained to be the priority region and continues to have significant IDPs and affected host population that remains vulnerable under the current circumstances- conflict. According to the Emergency Site Assessment (ESA) round 8, 386,741 households comprising 1,814,284 IDPs were identified in 581 accessible sites in Tigray region due to the Northern Ethiopia Crisis.
Humanitarian needs remain significantly high as the current response is not meeting the increasing needs of those in need as the resources available are not matching the requirements to ensure that minimum lifesaving humanitarian standards are maintained for the targeted population. According to the northern Ethiopia humanitarian update shared (OCHA, 14 April 2022), between 1 January and 4 April 2022, only 42.11% of the targeted people were reached with ESNFI response in Tigray region. Critical needs prioritized include, provision of adequate shelters and NFIs for IDPs living in highly congested and substandard living conditions, provision of shelter and NFI assistance to IDPs willing to return to their areas of origin, and to IDPs currently living within host communities, and rental assistance schemes or other arrangements for IDPs living in host communities.
Inadequate provision of WASH services poses a risk of disease outbreaks at IDP sites. About 9,300 water points or more than 54 per cent of the 17,080 water points in Tigray are not functional affecting the access of more than 3.5 million people to safe drinking water. About 418,000 IDPs in 131 sites require 295 trips of water trucking every day which requires 1,990 liters of fuel daily. Construction of more than 17,000 latrines at IDP sites are required to mitigate the risks of disease transmission. Low stock of key WASH NFIs, including water treatment chemical, water containers and hygiene supplies have also been reported in Tigray. Priority WASH needs include, emergency water trucking, rehabilitation of water schemes, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, distribution of WASH NFIs, and hygiene promotion activities.
The locations and activities considered for the WASH and ES/NFI interventions under this project plan are identified in the HRP 2022 prioritization list to address the immediate and life-saving needs of crisis-affected communities in line with the respective clusters' priority activities. The actions will be WASH and ESNFI services and carried out in line with cluster objectives CLWSH/CO1 (Deliver conflict-sensitive essential life-saving WASH services to affected people), CLWSH/CO3 (To enhance vulnerable households’ ability to withstand recurrent and future shocks in Ethiopia through early recovery and resilience interventions), and CLSHL/CO1, (Ensure contextualized access to lifesaving shelter and NFIs for displacement affected population with a focus on the most vulnerable and at-risk population group to safeguard their health security, privacy, and dignity )and CLSHL/CO2 (Contribute to improving protection outcomes by ensuring the conflict/displacement affected people have adequate safety, dignity, well-being, and equitable access to shelter solutions).
The projects aim to enhance the protection, health, and well-being of the conflict affected IDPs and host communities, and will directly reach 43,765 people through WASH and 40,055 people through ES/NFI prioritizing the most vulnerable, including women, girls, children, elderly, and people with disabilities in particular.
The project will ensure the active participation of the targeted beneficiaries and stakeholders throughout the project cycle. Protection and safety concerns will be given priority and DRC will ensure providing safe, easy, and dignified access to the facilities by the most vulnerable.

1. - Projects activities

Emergency water trucking: DRC will provide emergency water supply at a rate of 7.5 lit/person/day for three months. This activity will address the minimum daily water demand needs of displaced and non-displaced communities in the Tigray region. Water supply scheme rehabilitation work (motorized schemes, shallow wells): DRC proposes maintenance of motorized water supply schemes, to continue providing safe water to the targeted population. This includes; pipeline maintenance, pipeline extension, rehabilitation of hand-dug wells/shallow wells, and installation of plastic tanks. For effective management of water supply schemes in this project, DRC will establish or re-establish and train WASH committees. Sanitation: Targeted people will be provided with better access to improved sanitation facilities. Access to sanitation facilities is expanded through maintenance and construction of emergency communal latrines with handwashing facilities, bathing facilities, desludging and decommissioning of filled or abandoned latrines, solid waste management, distribution of cleaning, and environmental cleaning kits.Hygiene promotion: Hygiene and sanitation awareness-raising activities will be designed to reinforce and encourage positive health and hygiene practices that reduce water-borne and sanitation-related diseases will be promoted and conducted throughout the targeted sites. Community Hygiene promoters will be trained on community mobilization, key hygiene domains, and basic principles of protection. Distribution of WASH NFIs: DRC, adhering to the national WASH cluster guidance, will provide a full package of WASH NFIs mainly including containers (jerry-can, bucket, and washing basins), and water treatment chemicals, and hygiene items (multi-purpose soap). Distribution of core-relief items - NFIs: The project will provide core relief items to conflict-affected households. DRC will distribute a package of bedding sets, kitchen sets, and hygiene items to the most vulnerable IDPs in line with the ESNFI cluster guidelines. Proper targeting, verification, and compliant handling mechanisms will be established in coordination with MEAL.Construction of duplex shelter: DRC will construct shelters for conflict-affected IDPs to provide immediate, life-saving shelter assistance. DRC will harmonize cluster standard designs and will construct duplex-type shelters in the sites identified and endorsed by local government, CCCM, and ES/NFI clusters.ES kit: DRC will distribute up to 3,241 emergency shelter kits - comprised of plastic sheeting/tarpaulin (3pcs) Rope: Min 20 meters, 6-14 mm), and 30USD cash for local materials to rapidly respond to the immediate shelter needs of conflict- and displacement-affected households in Northwestern Tigray.ES+NFI kits for disabled persons: DRC will follow humanitarian protection principles, specifically targeting and serving the most vulnerable. The most vulnerable persons with disability will be targeted and provided with ES and NFI kits in accordance with Shelter/NFI Cluster standards. Cash for rent: Cash for rent to cover the housing needs of vulnerable families living within the host communities. Through this approach, DRC aims to ensure that 210 extremely vulnerable households have a short-term shelter solution, adequate protection against the cold weather, and privacy for family life as their most immediate basic needs will be met, through their received cash assistance according to the ESNFI cluster set standards.Shelter repair kits: DRC will provide shelter repair kits for returnees as defined by the ESNFI cluster and based on the vulnerability status of affected people. By using the Shelter repair kit provided, households with partially damaged houses will be able to repair homes to a level that will provide adequate protection. As per the national Shelter and NFI cluster, the minimum standard kit includes CIS, gate latch, Nails, T-hing 6'', and cash for local material and labor.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

As a result of the conflict in the north, banks and other financial services are not yet functional. There is still shortage of cash and the only means of cash flow is throw humanitarian actors carrying cash which is limited to two million. The federal government has recently declared an immediate "humanitarian truce" to allow aid into the Tigray region and reiterated its commitment to working with relevant organizations to expedite humanitarian aid delivery to those in need. According to reports, some needs will not be met by in-kind assistance. For instance, people are able to get rental houses in the region but unable to pay due to lack of access to cash. The opportunity of carrying cash to the region following a formal process with NDRMC and the humanitarian truce will create an opportunity for more cash flow to support affected people in the region with cash-based interventions.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

DRC will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations on DRC procedures, structures, and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices. DRC will establish feedback and complaint that actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate, and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in the policy. DRC will ensure that communities play an active role in the decision-making process through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized (women and children) and affected are represented and have influence. Prompt response to complaints will be made. DRC will organize awareness-raising sessions so that people know how it works and will consider a joint complaints mechanism with other sectors (e.g. Protection)as well to minimize confusion.
