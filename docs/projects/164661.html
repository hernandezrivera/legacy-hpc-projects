Project Summary

The projects aims to provide support to water systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria through support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems, and to deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people through provision of water, distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion, and improve access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services:
• BE will restore 15 wells in the year 2020 in the north of Syria, where each well service in average 5,000 persons.
• BE will provide water (25L/P/D) monthly to vulnerable beneﬁciaries in the target areas of operation, where we will distribute 5,000 tanks to 5,000 HHs, and service them with water trucking.
• BE will provide hygiene kits to vulnerable beneﬁciaries in the target areas of operation, where we will distribute 6,000 hygiene kits.

4 - Needs

WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity.42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs,at least 80 per cent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. Lack of regular maintenance,repair and power supply,drain of qualified national technical staff,and unilateral coercive measures that limits import of critical water supply equipment including water treatment and disinfectant products significantly impact water systems’ efficiency and reliability in several areas. As a result,many people are forced to complement water received from the network or solely rely on alternative sources such as unregulated water operators and shallow wells. These often provide poor quality water increasing the risk of contracting water borne diseases. Many communities that are not served through water networks face serious issues regarding water affordability which contributes to poverty and impacts needs in other sectors. Finally,contamination of water stations with explosive hazards have resulted in challenges related to provision of safe water and safety of WASH staff. Delivering WASH assistance in IDP site settings is costlier and more complex when compared to response in host communities. In Idleb and surrounding areas high dependency on alternative and often unsafe water sources prevails. This significantly increases the risk of waterborne diseases in these IDP sites. Likewise,continuous drilling of wells in search of water has negatively affected groundwater resources,with a significant depletion of water aquifers observed especially around IDP sites near the Turkish border. Based on WASH assessments,53 per cent of the Syria population requires WASH assistance,with 28 per cent of these people considered in acute need (severity four and five).

5 - Implementation of activities

BE will start a rapid assessment in target sites,this assessment will help BE toinspect the following key issues: 1. Number of IDPs in targeted sites in sex and age disaggregated ﬁgures. 2. Prevalence of diarrhea cases and skin infections. 3. Availability and affordability of WASH related NFIs and hygiene detergents. 4. Availability and number of large water containers to compare storage capacity with the required water quantity. 5. Main water sources in sites and identify the quantity and quality of existing water supply sources. 6. Water treatment methods and average water consumption per household. 7. Coping strategies when water is not suﬃcient. BE will restore 15 wells in the year 2020 in the north of Syria. • BE will provide water (25L/P/D) monthly to vulnerable beneﬁciaries in the target areas of operation. • BE will provide hygiene kits to vulnerable beneﬁciaries in the target areas of operation. • (BE) will distribute water from pre-agreed distribution points within communities. Distribution points will be chosen in coordination with the site managers or local authority and will be walking distance from the homes of beneﬁciaries,in case of vehicles cannot access houses. For beneﬁciaries who are not able to access the distribution points,BE will provide home delivery. •BE has developed a more detailed distribution checklist that will be used when planning and holding distributions. Program staff will ﬁll out the checklist prior to the distribution,and monitoring staff will check it has been complied with during the distribution. BE takes seriously feedback from beneﬁciaries with regards to access for people with disabilities/ elderly/ pregnant,gender considerations in crowd control,access to drinking water and waiting times. It is expected that the introduction of a more detailed distribution checklist, with responsibilities to implement good practices outlined,will result in more digniﬁed distributions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

populations living in camps,informal settlements and collective centres,women and girls,people with disabilities and elderly,together with female-headed households face more constrains and various protection risks in accessing WASH services.All population groups in the community could be affected by limitations in functionality and efficiency of WASH infrastructure and services. Inequality in accessing to WASH services results from direct or indirect damages,lack or limited maintenance,brain drain of technical staff,and years of functioning way above designed capacity. People living in damaged shelters and unfinished buildings (11 per cent) are more likely to face issues with sanitation. However,dysfunctionality or lack of WASH services increases risk of violence,abuse and harassment for women and girls,particularly in overcrowded IDP sites. •There is a risk of Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. • Gender insensitive contents verities of kits with inconsistent items,therefore it is important to harmonies and standardize gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use. • Not enough water distribution points available so people have to wait for extended periods to get water,fights,and harassment could occur; inequality in distribution,in this case,ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; • Available means of transporting water not supporting equality and accessibility of water distribution for vulnerable groups,to avoid that BE must ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Distribute smaller water containers more convenient for women and elderly people; Consider specific support to people with disabilities to ensure they access a sufficient quantity of water;

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards or improper level of chlorine dose; ,BE will hiring experts to supervise the process and make sure that water is free of contamination and is potable,and ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible.Shelling,airstrikes,etc,and also potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations,towers,distribution networks),BE may Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Add explosive hazard awareness material to the programming.-In case there is a military operation and a resultant escalation of conflict,there may be large displacement of people needing WASH assistance,BE will ensure consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points,delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets,congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes,avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; -Water distribution not safe or accessible,especially for women,girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution,Ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Make sure that distance of water collection points remains at minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with extra workload in particular;-There are possibility of beneficiaries selling of some items from hygiene kits to secure cash,so Beyaz Eller will closely monitor utilization of supplies and keep a track of what is being sold out in local market. An analysis will be carried out that will advise future procurement and/or selection of beneficiaries.
