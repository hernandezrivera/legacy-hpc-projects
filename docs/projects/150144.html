Project Summary

This project will provide legal and para-legal assistance to Palestinians in the West Bank, occupied East Jerusalem and Gaza who are confronted with denial by Israeli authorities of their right to move freely throughout the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) and to travel and return from travel abroad. Three Specific Objectives follow from this overall project objective:
SO1: Individual Palestinians in the oPt, especially women, children and those without the means to engage private counsel, receive critical, cost-free legal aid needed to overcome restrictions imposed by Israel on their freedom of movement and access.
SO2: Strategic litigation cases target Israel's discriminatory and coercive "permit regime" and other laws and policies that effectively deny Palestinians access to their lands and businesses and restrict their freedom to travel within and outside the oPt to sustain livelihoods, access medical care, take advantage of educational opportunities and maintain family ties.
SO3: Monitoring and documentation of human rights violations help hold Israel to account, deny it impunity, and strengthen the rule of law protections for Palestinians in the oPt.
The project is intended to address the 2018-2020 HRP Strategic Objective 1 by ensuring that the rights of Palestinians living under occupation, including those living under the blockade of Gaza, are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL. Project activities will focus particularly on the "Seam Zone" of Area C, on East Jerusalem and on facilitating movement to and from the Gaza Strip. They are also intended to assist those most harmed by these restrictions and their impact on livelihoods and family unity, including especially women and children. Favorable decisions arising from individual and public-interest litigation will create lasting precedents in case law, strengthening Palestinians’ ability to claim their rights and protect themselves long after the project concludes.

Needs

In this, the 51st year of Israel’s occupation of the West Bank and East Jerusalem and the 13th year of its illegal blockade of the Gaza Strip, Palestinians are suffering the devastating effects of discriminatory and coercive rule in almost every aspect of their daily lives. UNCTAD estimates that a free Palestinian people would produce at least twice the GDP it does today, “while employment and poverty would recede significantly.” Instead, Israel’s crushing siege, together with the effects of war and poor governance, have brought Gaza’s estimated 1.8 million people to the brink of despair and economic collapse. In the West Bank and occupied East Jerusalem, restrictions on freedom of movement, denial of access to agricultural lands and businesses, and frequent “closures” of whole neighborhoods, villages and towns undermine self-sustaining economic activity and contribute to food insecurity and a sense of subjection to an alien and hostile rule among the nearly 3 million Palestinian residents. These stifling restrictions have an especially injurious effect on the most vulnerable among the Palestinian population, including women and children dependent for their livelihoods and hopes for the future on the free access to agricultural lands, businesses, schools and health care so frequently denied them. Fruit and vegetable cultivation that formerly provided families with employment and income is gradually disappearing in the Seam Zone because Israeli restrictions on the number of family members and the frequency of visits to the land permitted make this type of profitable but labor-intensive agriculture impossible to maintain. As these traditional forms of agriculture are taken from them, adult women family members are deprived of a way to contribute their farm labor to family income and are more than ever restricted to the home, where it is often they who absorb the frustrations of male family members without work. Added to these concerns is the worry that with less frequent visits to the land, Israeli authorities will confiscate their Seam Zone property under the pernicious “Abandoned Areas Ordinance.” In all, Seam Zone lands represent about 9% of the total West Bank area. For many young Palestinian women, education provides a path towards self-fulfillment and independence, yet Israel's restrictions on movement within the West Bank, between the West Bank and Jerusalem and on travel abroad obstruct access to private and public schools, and universities in Jerusalem, Israel and overseas. HaMoked's case files document many instances of young Palestinian women having secured admission and financial support for university studies, only to have their hopes dashed and their futures restricted by Israel's arbitrary decision to deny them permission to travel. Israel's restrictions on movement to and from Gaza also cause anguish for separated families in which one spouse, generally the husband, is Gazan and the other is from the West Bank, East Jerusalem or Israel. Since 2003, Israel has barred Gaza residents who marry West Bankers or Israelis from receiving legal residency status anywhere but Gaza. This presents the couple with the agonizing choice of living separately or living in Gaza where one partner, most frequently the wife in HaMoked's experience (in 94% of the 283 cases handled in 2016), is completely cut off from contact with and emotional support from her extended family in raising her children and in the interactions of everyday life. Her position is especially precarious should the marriage encounter difficulties, leaving her at the mercy of her husband's relatives, even in situations of domestic violence. This project will provide critical legal aid and facilitate access to the Israeli judicial system for Palestinians denied their fundamental freedom of movement and access; fully document these violations; and attack the unlawful and oppressive regime that sustains them through strategic litigation.

Activities

Drawing on its experience of handling more than 100,000 individual cases and its achievements in strategic litigation attacking Israel’s restrictions on Palestinians’ freedom of movement and access, HaMoked will provide critical legal aid to Palestinian rights holders seeking to access their lands, move freely within the West Bank and between the West Bank, Gaza and East Jerusalem, live united as families, and travel abroad for business, medical care, educational opportunity and family matters. Israel restricts Palestinians’ freedom of movement within the West Bank and between the West Bank and Jerusalem by imposing frequent “closures” on whole villages or regions. Roadblocks closing the entrances to affected areas deny thousands of residents the right to leave their village for education, medical care, business and other needs and subject them to humiliating treatment and harassment when attempting to enter or exit their own homes. HaMoked first visits these areas to consult with village residents concerning the best way to protect their rights. We then deal directly with the military, Israeli civil authorities, and security ministries to document and challenge the threats to Palestinians’ safety and dignity inherent in these collective punishments. These interventions very frequently result in a partial or complete lifting of the closure. Strategic litigation will target the oppressive and spuriously rule-of-law-based regime of movement and access permits doled out by Israel, but which in fact constitutes a breach of its commitments under the 4th Geneva Convention, the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, the Convention on the Rights of the Child, and other international humanitarian law instruments and human rights agreements. These public interest cases will seek to change Israeli laws and practices by reminding the courts of Israel’s responsibilities and obligations under international law and its own Basic Law on Human Dignity and Liberty. An immediate objective will be to challenge the legality and seek repeal or amendment to the Israeli military’s “Seam Zone Standing Orders,” which it uses to severely restrict Palestinians’ access to their land trapped between the separation barrier and the Green Line. In consultation with residents of some of the approximately 150 Palestinian villages divided by the wall, HaMoked will devise legal arguments with care, in order to avoid worsening the situation for residents. The project’s extensive documentation of human rights violations will promote access to justice for Palestinians. Monitoring and documentation will form the basis for the development and dissemination of critical analyses of Israel’s restrictions to freedom of movement and access, their injurious impact on the Palestinian population, including its most vulnerable members, and on legal recourse available to contest these restrictions. The following activities are planned and are keyed to the project's three specific objectives: A1.1: Palestinians assisted to overcome restrictions on access to agricultural land and businesses in the Area C “Seam Zone.” A1.2: Palestinians assisted to travel to and from Gaza and between the West Bank and Gaza. A1.3: Palestinians assisted to travel abroad. A2.1: Strategic litigation or public interest cases attacking laws and policies restricting Palestinians’ freedom of movement filed and/or managed. A2.2: Collective punishment “closures" of neighborhoods, villages and towns imposed by Israel in the West Bank and East Jerusalem are challenged. A3.1: Freedom of movement cases initiated by the project are recorded and documented. A3.2: Website updates and reports posted. A3.3: Diplomats, policy-makers and opinion-leaders briefed on Israel’s unlawful restrictions on Palestinians’ freedom of movement and access in the oPt.
