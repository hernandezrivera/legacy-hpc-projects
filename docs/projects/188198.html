Project Summary

World Vision Syria Response through this project aims to provide child protection services, mine action and GBV prevention and response services in northwest Aleppo, north-west Syria in addition of Tal Abyad and Ras Alin to ensure improving the quality of community- based services through focusing on behavior change and holistic approach including most of the community stakeholders, expand the reach and facilitate the access of widows and their children to CP and GBV services, in addition of strengthening the capacities of local actors in the child protection and gender domains. The project will include community centers, CFSs and WGSSs and will cover urban areas and camps specially widow camps. To ensure the access for the hard-to-reach areas the project also will include mobile teams. The main objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and response of child protection and GBV concerns and ensure the mitigation of related risks with adopting a holistic approach that include the delivery modalities to be utilized during the COVID pandemic: through the provision of quality child protection services which will include general and specialized CP services (case management) including children with disabilities, the provision of GBV prevention and response services which includes (case management) and both will include establishing effective and community-based service mapping, PFA, PSS for children and adults and PM+ for adults, in addition of supporting the most vulnerable children and women with CVAs and Cash for protection. Risk education and awareness will be provided in supporting the implementing partners to provide quality, gender and age appropriate messages including child friendly material that’s why the project will ensure the provision of quality mine risk education services in targeted locations which will include advocacy to address CP and GBV issues and enhance strategies that empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with
a particular focus on adolescent girls and to reduce the impact of explosive hazards. The project aims to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and stakeholders at the national and community levels to assess, analyze and address protection risks and needs and to respond to child protection concerns in Syria and build the capacity of GBV specialist and non-specialist to respond, prevent and mitigate GBV.
World Vision will provide protection interventions include legal counselling and assistance, protection monitoring, referrals and information sharing in additional to protection case management to PwD and the elderly
As part of accountability to affected populations and taking into consideration that WMGB are the primary stakeholders WV will ensure that is addressed and considered throughout the project through regular MSNAs and other needs assessment processes as CPHA ADAPT which is a regular analysis process that WV is doing on the local and national levels.
All above activities will be taken into consideration the Safeguarding and PSEA minimum standards and best practices specially after the COVID pandemic and the increased risks after the online modalities and the stressors that raised due to the social isolation and shortage of services.
Likewise, monitoring and evaluation activities will be carried out for all the above-mentioned activities taking into account the sensitivity of some activities (such as case management),
All monitoring and evaluation activities will be in accordance with the guidelines shared by the coordination teams (CP,GBV,GP and MA AoRs), in addition to the periodic reporting mechanisms (4Ws, CM reporting tool, and other reporting mechanisms.

4 - Needs

As of 2022 HNO, the total people in need are estimated to be as 14Mwhere 43.9% of them are children, 1.69 M children between them with disabilities and 27.6% of them are adult females. Its estimated that 96.24% of them are above the mid of the severity rank and more than 51% of them are in need for GBV support as GBV is a dominant feature of the Syrian humanitarian crisis. Women and girls are subjected to increased denial of economic resources, movement restrictions, exploitation, forced and child marriage71% of the communities identify it as an issue, intimate partner and family violence, technology facilitated violence, and physical, psychological, emotional, sexual, and social violence. As of children and adolescents face violence, abuse and exploitation in many life situations including exposure to new forms of violence due to harmful coping mechanisms within the family, according to Voices 2022 report Sexual, emotional, psychological, and physical violence. Explosive ordnance contamination endangers the lives of civilians, limits safe movements and impacts economic opportunities, hampers rehabilitation of public infrastructure, Lack/loss of civil documentation was reported in 76% of assessed Communities, Restrictions of movement were reported in 51% of assessed communities and 61% of assessed communities reported HLP concerns, WV done an assessment in collaboration with local partners for 27 widow camps in northwest of Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

WV will ensure enhancing equitable access to quality child protection, mine action and GBV services, including children with disabilities and hard to access areas as widow camps through safe spaces. which will build on raising the awareness of the community using the CPMS and GBV toolkit that endorsed by the CP and GBV AoR to safely identify and refer children and women as PM+, GBV and CP case management, CVA and Cash support in addition of other services as PFA, PSS for children/adults, lifeskills for children/adults using the interagency standards and WV models, in addition of MRE. All activities will include capacity building of staff about CPMS, Safeguarding, PSEA, CP CM and GBV SOPs WV will ensure the implementation of all activities in the centers and outside the centers for reaching all members in addition of the proper coordination with the camp management and the support for and through the committees will be arranged on the most appropriate and context specific way. 4Ws will be ensured to provide coordinated response, avoiding overlapping also WV has its field staff which will be responsible on monitoring, field visits and support for the partners’ staff, Likewise, monitoring and evaluation activities will be carried out for all the above-mentioned activities taking into account the sensitivity of some activities (ex:CM), All monitoring and evaluation activities will be in accordance with the guidelines shared by the coordination teams and AoRs

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WV will consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed site or mobile service provision, ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access, engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community, related to certain activities and the effect on the community expectations on not existing services, for MHPSS services and the risks related to the limitation of specialized mental health professionals inside Syria, furthermore; the physical security risks due to conflict activities, the protection risks related to CVA and Cash distribution, barriers for certain groups in access centers, that’s why WVSR will ensure developing clear communication to manage community expectations, include updated service mapping part in all sessions with the proper training for related staff, in addition of collaboration with stakeholders via community consultation related to accessibility. Furthermore, with a focus on GBV stigmatization, family resistance to girls’ mobility, few disclosures and lack of trust in services. WVSR will ensure the distribution of GBV service which will be regularly updated and location based and will ensure that all staff is well trained based on their expertise and role Also, there’s a high risk of creating a stigma for girls, boys, and their caregivers by the staff members during the CP case management imp, VI will ensure to traing the staff and service providers on the identification and CPCM SoPs.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WVSR adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards SEA and it is committed responsibility at all times to ensure that all people – women, girls, boys and men – are treated with dignity and respect, furthermore, uphold humanitarian principles and ensure the integrity of the humanitarian operation. Support will be provided for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and their caregivers which include the technical support related to the safeguarding and PSEA available policy review and development with partners, ToT and training to be provided for first line workers, assigning field focal points with partners in addition WVSR field and national focal points, raising the awareness of the community in proper way including child friendly material, regular reporting on all related activities and incidents in coordination with other clusters in addition of PSEA network which is led by IOM. Child safety concerns are identified and monitored through regular child/beneficiary checklists which are used by WVSR field staff which all is constantly monitored through the project lifecycleFurthermore, WVSR has its own incident preparedness plan which is understood by all staff and partners and is summarized by the procedures and actions related to proper responding to safeguarding and PSEA incidents in multiple levels and categories, in addition WVSR uses a standard online platform which is called Ethics Point that ensures the proper and timely response


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

WVSR developed an extensive knowledge of security and access issues related to NWS and is able to manage them and design programming accordingly in close coordination with Partner LNGOs along with WVSR being actively engaged with OCHA Access Working Group through Humanitarian Access Briefing (HAB) platform and bilateral engagement and other multilateral arrangements. Existing and new agreed on arrangements allow WVSR and Partners to avoid and/or address security and access-related challenges taking into consideration the areas severity ranks, while maintaining principled and safe access across operational areas. The risk of not preserving impartiality of intervention is present in NWS due to interference by stakeholders (Local Councils, military groups, etc.) in the beneficiary selection process, in activities in the “Widow Camps” and GBV services as well as recruitment, procurement, etc. WVSR will adopt clear beneficiary selection criteria and will be communicated with members of the communities targeted and authorities to increase the transparency and limit possible interference by third parties. In order to limit possible interference in activities, MoUs with Camp management and other relevant entities will be signed along with a coordination at cluster levels with CP and GBV working groups. Finally, WVSR comply with the minimum procurement standards while being aware of context-sensitivity, and staff recruitment must adopt impartial and transparent procedures.
