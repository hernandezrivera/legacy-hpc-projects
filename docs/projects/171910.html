Project Summary

The project will target vulnerable and affected population in conflict-affected eastern Ukraine, Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, ECA (GCA). With a focus on humanitarian and development issues in GCA it aims to design and implement durable solutions as part of humanitarian-development nexus. UNFPA has already built core elements of a response mechanism as part of its humanitarian interventions and is keen to continue strengthening this mechanism. It will place a greater emphasis on assistance, strengthening community resilience, and improving access of humanitarian actors to the affected population. Special attention will be given to supporting the most vulnerable population: people with disabilities, people living in rural areas, people living in isolated settlements (in particular 0-5 km) with limited access to government service providers, social services, and single women-headed households along the contact line. Differentiated approaches to programming will be applied in 2021 considering the prevailing humanitarian situation, exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic. The primary focus for humanitarian response will remain to be on the communities 0-5 km and 5-20 km the contact line (Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts). Critical security/safety needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and comers from ECA communities will be prioritized in 20+ area, where durable solutions for GBV response are implemented in a safe and survivors-oriented manner. Due to restricted humanitarian access to NGCA for protection activities, the project will seek alternative partnership opportunities with local actors; will reach out to those in need through remote and silent solutions; will use existing access points such as healthcare facilities for delivering aid. Project implementation areas will include: Assistance – developing a network of accessible, affordable and accountable safe spaces for DV / GBV survivors living in areas close to the contact line (also along the contact line) These protection solutions according to needs assessment are the most relevant for 5-20 km and 20+ km zones due to security issues and the presence of capable service providers. Protection – capacity building and sensitization of service providers, community activists along the contact line and isolated settlements; awareness-raising within the general population, training and developing distant learning solutions for actors responsible for GBV response; strengthening accountability to affected population (AAP) mechanism. Resilience – supporting the development of community-based early warning system (for 0-5 km, 5-20 km zones as a priority), involving humanitarian actors, community activists; SGBV coordination to strengthen the response and prevention systems, and to gradually transfer to national ownership over provision of humanitarian assistance Access – supporting targeted PSS MTs interventions, awareness-raising/sensitizing sessions and events in selected settlements (with emphasis on isolated settlements, rural areas, and those in 0-5 km zone). UNFPA supported models of service delivery will eventually build trust in those services among survivors and trigger their willingness to report their cases (as a part of AAP) and seek help. Engagement of non-state actors (local NGO’s, FBO’s, CBO’s) to act as service providers at the local community level will strengthen the prevention pillar of the overall GBV response strategy. A monitoring and evaluation plan, defined through previous interventions to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets, is used to ensure the quality of project results. A feedback mechanism from project beneficiaries will be operating, including through regular GBV WGs and GBV SC meetings, National Toll-Free Hotline. In order to achieve maximum complementarity, the project will seek to coordinate, and to the extent possible work jointly, with other complementary projects and initiatives in the area of gender equality and GBV.

1 - Needs

Protracted and complex humanitarian crises in eastern Ukraine, sharpened by the COVID-2019 pandemic continues to increase the need for mitigation, response, and prevention of the risks of gender-based violence (GBV), in particular, intimate partner violence (IPV) and domestic violence (DV), disproportionately affecting women and girls.The critical lack of sustainable safe space solutions in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts continues to challenge the effectiveness of the GBV referral mechanism. Limited information and access to psycho-social and life-saving services for GBV survivors remain an acute problem, particularly in remote and rural areas of ECA. Processes of decentralization and new approaches to coordination with the humanitarian community may develop new challenges related to strengthening the system of GBV response, in particular in remote rural areas. Apart from it, there are risks that conflict-affected people with specific needs, in particular persons with disabilities, older people, female-headed households, are at risk of further marginalization, due to lack of individual security, protection, healthcare, and social assistance.In Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts the number of DV cases reported to the social services has increased by more than 86 % increase in the number of applications in the second quarter of 2020 compared to the first quarter 2020; 54 % increase in the second quarter of 2020 compared to the previous year for the same reporting period accordingly (information was given by the GBV SC co-chairing bodies, July 2020). Service providers in ECA (GCA) consider psychological abuse as the most commonly encountered form of GBV, followed by physical assault (70%), with economic violence (34%) and sexual assault cases less commonly encountered. Nine percent (9%) of service providers encountered rape cases (Gender-based violence service provision in selected hromadas of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, ACTED/AGORA, July 2020). National Police of Ukraine (NPU) gives the data, that the number of appeals related to DV/IPV in the first half of 2020 increased up to 40% in comparison to the same period of 2019, and NPU has already received 101,000 calls, in 2018 the total number of registered incidents was 115,000 and in 2019-142,000 (Deputy Minister of Interior, Kateryna Pavlichenko; briefing August 7, 2020). At the same time, there is a permanent lack of comparative GBV/DV baseline data collected in ECA, and data collected by duty bearers is not disaggregated enough. Another issue is the lack of confidentiality of personal data in rural settlements, where perpetrators could play a significant role in the community. The Ukrainian legislative framework recognizes the GBV/DV response as one of the priorities (Ukraine has gained official status as a member of the Biarritz Partnership; the Presidential Order on Urgent measures to protect the rights and interests of DV/GBV survivors came into force, September 2020). However, people at risk of GBV and GBV survivors still do not have appropriate access to life-saving information and essential services, including psychosocial support, health, and legal assistance. Due to an insufficient number of shelters in Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts, restrictions related to the COVID-2019 pandemic GBV survivors have no option but to remain in unsafe living environments. Thus, GBV response remains a significant challenge in eastern Ukraine, in particular in locations close to the “contact line”. Another challenging issue is still access to NGCA to provide survivors and those at risk with services and support.Coordinated psychosocial support, healthcare services, provision of shelters and safe spaces should be scaled-up to bridge unmet needs. Community-based support mechanisms should also be fostered. There is finally a need to strengthen the capacity of local/community-based service providers, including specialized ones, to respond to and prevent GBV in an inter-sectoral way.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

1. Support to sustain essential GBV services (PSS Mobile Teams and referral to health, “safe space”, protection, livelihood) are available and accessible for the most vulnerable women and girls living in the areas close to the contact line (also along the contact line).2. Support GBV Sub-Cluster coordination mechanism with a focus on gradual transfer to national ownership over the provision of humanitarian assistance and mainstreaming of response to GBV into other clusters' workю3. General and specialized PSS service providers have the capacity to effectively and safely deliver integrated services for the protection of GBV survivors adapted to the requirements of the COVID-19 epidemic in the areas along the contact line.4. Police and justice services, including legal support to GBV survivors, are integrated as part of multi-sectoral GBV response and prevention systems at the regional level.5. Remote modalities of assistance are available to adopt response to COVID related restrictions (hotline, assistance through messengers, etc).6. Provision of targeted information on safety plan in the caseof GBV, access to services, and GBV prevention.7. Capacity building of community activists from communities along the contact line and isolated settlements to develop an early warning system and provide essential information on available GBV services.8. Local service providers (including providers operating along the contact line) equipped with information materials on GBV and incapacitated to implement community-level GBV prevention, advocacy, and communication actions.
