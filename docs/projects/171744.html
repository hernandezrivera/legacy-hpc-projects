Project Summary

Project aims to provide live-saving WASH assistance to disaster affected displaced and
non- displaced population in order to reduce the excess morbidity and mortality rate from
preventable crisis-driven diseases and public health outbreaks among most vulnerable
people. The project will continue to restore access to safe and equitable basic WASH
services in targeted locations in a dignified manner. Lack of adequate WASH services to
the population is a main cause of spread of Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD), leading to
malnutrition in the country and contributing to increasing the existing stunting rate of
25.3% in children below 5 years of age.
Access to safe water will be increased through construction and or rehabilitation of water
sources including 8 boreholes (2 new), 20 shallow wells, 2 river water intake system with
water treatment. New borehole construction will be preceded by hydrogeological survey
and safe pumping regime will be designed. River water intakes will be preceded
hydrological study. In all cases, an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) will be
undertaken for new water sources and mitigation measures put in place for negative
impacts. All water systems will be equipped with distribution infrastructure and hybrid
energization systems. Pipeline extensions will also be made. Taps stand will be located
within a radius of at least 500m to reduce distance and enhance security of women and
girls. Project will also construct 2 major improved water dams in locations that lack other
options. All well heads will be raised at a minimum 0.5m to reduce risk flooding and
contamination. Water committees comprising men and women from all community
groups including the minority and the marginalized will be trained on management,
operation and maintenance. Local technicians will be trained and provided with O&M
tools to undertake repairs whereas consumers will pay for the technician’s services. For
major water supply in urban areas, Private Public Parternship (PPP) management system
will be adopted. Where possible NRC will partner will local private investors to establish
sustainable water sources and equip them with SmartTAPs to enhance revenue collection
and accountability. Project will construct 400 latrines for families reintegrated through the
durable solutions. NRC undertakes sanitary surveys periodically to identify contamination
risks for timely mitigation. All latrine will be installed with handwashing equipment and
community sensitized to use soap for handwashing. Design of infrastructure will take into
consideration the needs of special groups in the community including the disabled, old,
children to ensure they have equitable access to the services. Life-saving WASH
interventions will be implemented in case of sudden disaster to reduce the related
mortality and morbidity especially among children. This will be through temporary
provision of safe water through direct delivery or restricted cash depending on context and
market functionality, provision of hygiene kits through cash (3000 HH), construction of
550 simple design emergency latrines, latrine emptying (450 latrines) and safe fecal
sludge disposal and hygiene promotion. Schools and health facilities will be provided with
latrines offering private space for menstrual hygiene (200 institutional latrines). All
latrines will be raised above ground level to reduce the risk of flooding during rains.
Women and girls will be involved in identification of sites for facilities to ensure their
safety and security is taken into account. Latrines will be gender separated, provided with
lockable doors and handwashing equipment. Solar street lights (35 posts) will be provided
near water points and latrines to improve the security and safety of women and girls
especially in crowded IDP settlements. Throughout the implementation period the
beneficiaries will be provided with adequate information on NRC’s complaints, response
and feedback (CRFM) mechanisms.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

There is an existing water supply market in all locations within Somalia whereby emergency water is provided to willing buyers through existing water suppliers who deliver water using trucks. The project will utilise the opportunity whereby community or household water vouchers will be provided to most vulnerable communities and or households to enable them access water from the existing local suppliers. Hygiene kits will be provided through cash or vouchers only where functional markets for such items exists and can sustain the demand created through provision of the cash. Past and present experience have shown the modality as a viable and sustainable solution for supporting households and small WASH businesses. Using cash promote dignity of beneficiaries in comparison to in-kind humanitarian assistance.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

NRC has gained experience in provision of WASH services through Cash and vouchers. In particular, cash and vouchers have been used for delivery of emergency safe water supply and for provision of hygiene kits. In locations where NRC is currently operating, information on market functionality has been collected and it informs the project design. Where there is inadequate information, CTP will be preceded by market assessment to inform the appropriate modality of response.

Mitigation Measures

Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Likelihood and Impact are both High. Mitigation Measures: • Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. • Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all • Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among household clusters. • Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be, outside the settlements. • Condition implementation to an equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepers. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance Likelihood: High Impact: Very high Mitigation: • Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal WASH facilities considering their security and safety and ensure the location and access routes to WASH facilities and distribution points are safe. • Locate water sources in visible, central locations and not more than 500 metres from settlement and install lights near communal WASH facilities • Where possible avoid central distribution on hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneficiary to purchase items at own convenient time • Construct gender separated latrines, lockable from inside, and add lighting • Increase number of water points to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness • Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of WASH facilities

Please provide further explanation

Through various donors including DFID, UNHCR and NMFA, NRC has been at the forefront of the Durable solutions agenda and has supported thousands of displaced people acquire land and issued them with land title documents. The support by the government offers a protection environment that if well utilized will see many IDPs reintegrated. In the target locations, the IDP are protracted and have therefore intermingled with the host community over time and established positive cohesion. Project contributes significantly to the Resilience Recover Framework and the National Development plan which envisions "Adequate and safe water, hygiene and sanitation for all". Project contributes to achievement of the NDP 4 of the 6 NDP WASH targets Water systems serving large population will preferably be managed through private public partnerships to enhance functional sustainability thus long term solution. Mini water supply system at village level will be managed by water management committees to be selected by the community and trained by NRC’s competent WASH staff. All water supply systems will be designed, implemented and periodically monitored jointly with the beneficiary community and the government authorities. Once completed, the facilities will be handed over formally to the community and the government and a certificate of hand-over signed by all parties. It is envisaged that promotion for use of hygiene items through provision of restricted cash will instil a culture of purchase of this vital items among the beneficiaries and also support the market chain that can run even after the project ends.
