Project Summary

This proposal specifically aims at ensuring that attacked schools are able to fulfil their crucial role as protective spaces for students and communities, to mitigate the impact of crisis and ensure that provision of education is inclusive and of quality in this time of crisis.
The activities proposed in this project will be under Education Objective 2: Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school based PSS services. Activity: Support the most vulnerable Schools in West Bank and Gaza in responding to emergencies through structured preparedness packages and post emergency responses
Two priority areas of focus are identified;
1) Support most vulnerable secondary schools in the Gaza Strip to plan for and respond to emergencies affecting adolescent girls and boys' quality education.
2) Strengthen the ability of principals, teachers and students in the targeted schools to cope with threats and shocks.
The activities proposed in this project align with UNESCOs technical and mandated capacity, and its overall strategy for education in post-conflict and post-disaster (PCPD) situations. UNESCO’s added value in PCPD responses stems from its interdisciplinary mandate, which stretches from relief to recovery and development. UNESCO Ramallah Office has the capacity and comparative advantage in crisis Disaster Risk Reduction (c-DRR), which was successfully implemented in Gaza (see more below, under Palestine: Lessons from UNESCO’s crisis-disaster risk reduction programme in Gaza).

Needs

In the current context of Palestine where barriers to the right to education are increasing, enhancing meaningful access to education for adolescent boys and girls in shools located in high risk area’s in the Gaza Strip is more critical than ever before. All children have the right to quality education and they have the right to teachers who are trained to meet in these standards, in how to anticipate/ act for conflict and disaster risk reduction (C/DRR), to be able to plan for contingency and mitigation, and apply conflict-sensitive education. According to the last updated list of the most vulnerable schools in Gaza strip (prepared by MoEHE in coordination with Protecting Education from Attack WG), there are 110 schools located in high risk areas and ARA and there is an urgent need to train staff at these schools on strengthening emergency preparedness and response to develop DRR plans and protect the schools from any future attacks or crises. From these most vulnerable schools, this proposal, in coordination with MoEHE and UNICEF, will be targeting all secondary schools (33 schools in total) from the list (of which 23 boys schools and 10 girls schools), therefore targeting most vulnerable secondary schools situated in high risk areas in the Gaza Strip. There is a high need to ensure that children and caregivers in highly risk areas have access to education and protection services, esp. given the recent UNRWA cuts (UNICEF 2018)The 2014 war has affected women and girls (including adolescent girls) disproportionately in many aspects of life (UNESCO/GPI, 2018*). Both girls and boys have different experiences coping with the crisis and may also have different coping strategies (GAGE, 2017*; UNESCO/GPI, 2018). These differences should be acknowledged and built in the training of teachers on emergency preparedness. Protection violations such as safety, access to services such as education and psycho-social wellbeing have a higher impact on women and children (HNO, 2018). Therefore, one component of this project aims to deliver meaningful access to education and build the capacity of female teachers in secondary girls school on emergency preparedness, addressing the differentiated needs of adolescent girls in crisis situations in the Gaza Strip and further mitigating direct humanitarian risk faced by the target groups.In the Gaza Strip, recurring conflict and the resulting damage and destruction of educational facilities have disrupted services and impacted the learning outcomes of children and teachers (OCHA 2018). Adolescent boys and girls in Gaza are especially vulnerable, particularly to school drop out. The drop out rate for boys is higher than girls, due to low academic achievement or to pressure to contribute to their families’ economic support (GAGE, 2017; UNWOMEN 2018). At the school level, boys and girls drop out due to a variety of reasons, such as unqualified teachers. Many teachers lack the pedagogical skills and the school environment is not always inclusive/conducive for learning (HNO,2018). Consequently the capacity of the MoEHE in districts and schools level needs to be supported in order to plan for and respond to emergencies affecting children`s access to education. It is therefore essential for school staff and students to learn essential life-saving skills and how to deal with crisis situations to ensure Building the Resilience of Communities to Disasters, with special focus on the different impacts of crisis on adolescent boys and girls. When incidents occur, it is imperative for teachers to be able to conduct first emergency care and for schools to be adequately supplied with first aid materials.*UNESCO/Gender Policy Institute (GPI),Qualitative analysis of gender equality in policies endorsed by the PA, 2018. * Gender&Adolescence:(GAGE), Adolescent Girls in Gaza, 2017* UNWOMEN,Needs of Women and Girls in Humanitarian Action in the oPt, 2018* OCHA Hum. Activities

Based on successful activities developed by UNESCO in Gaza: in 29 schools in ARA through c-DRR, responding to and mitigating threats against schools. Coordination with MoEHE and school safety committees: UNESCO in coordination with MoEHE and school safety committees, incl. parents (that will be established at each school from the local community) will conduct a series of planning meetings with other same services providers and the implementing partners and relevant MoEHE departments to agree on programme implementation and monitoring. MoEHE officials have already been part of an earlier project on Crisis Planning and Management, and will continue to be involved in the design of activities, through the drafting of school based contingency plans. Protection is being mainstreamed throughout the project by the incorporation of protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in all activities, through meaningful access to education of the target groups according to their gender differentiated needs and through mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints (see below for M&E). From the list of identified vulnerable secondary schools located in high risk area's which we mentioned in the “Needs” section, and in coordination with the MoEHE and UNICEF, this proposal will be targeting all secondary schools (33 in total) from the list, of which 23 are boys schools and 10 are girls schools, therefore targeting most vulnerable secondary schools situated in high risk areas in the Gaza Strip. Within the 33 target schools, UNICEF has implemented emergency preparedness practices in 13 schools. In these 13 schools, as discussed with UNICEF, we will complement their intervention by targeting a different set of teachers within the established emergency committee and develop peer support groups with teachers and students. Proposed interventions: To ensure that most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures and remedial programs (within Education (Cluster) objective 2- Activity 1) : • Provision of basic safety equipment and materials to 33 vulnerable secondary schools (23 boys schools and 10 Girls schools) • Capacity building of 354 female teachers in 10 girls schools on emergency preparedness and response capacities adapted to the specific needs of vulnerable adolescent girls in the Gaza Strip. (Training materials will be adapted to gender and age differentiated needs in the Gaza Strip)• Awareness raising sessions for 1087 teachers (354 female, 733 male) and 20,354, students (6429 girls, 13925 boys- age 15-18), safety committees and parents (especially mothers) in 33 schools (23 boys schools, 10 girls schools)on better preparedness and response capacities improvement, including first aid training (adapted to the specific age and gender differentiated needs of adolescent girls and boys)• Guidance sessions on emergency preparedness for 1087 teachers (354 female, 733 male), MoEHE, safety committees and parents, especially mothers in 33 schools.• Human Rights/protection orientation sessions for 1087 teachers (354 female, 733 male) Expected outputs: • Students (age 15-18) and education staff feel safer in schools and schools are better equipped.• improved access to education for boys and girls improved through gender and age differentiated approach of the teacher training on emergency preparedness and response• Students and education staff are able to respond to crisis and disasters appropriately and have the tools and skills needed in these situations.• Increased awareness among teachers on emergency preparedness and response capacities to the specific age and gender needs of adolescent girls.• The quality of education is enhanced through a greater sense of well-being allowing teachers and students to effectively teach and learn, thus improving learning outcomes

Indicators

As mentioned above, the 33 targeted schools are identified as most vulnerable secondary schools situated in high risk areas in the Gaza Strip. Of these target schools, 23 are boys schools and 10 are girls schools. Which means that the majority of vulnerable secondary schools in high risk area's in the Gaza Strip are boys schools. The needs and coping mechanisms of adolescent girls (age 15-18) are different from boys in the same age cohort. Adolescent girls within the 15-18 age group report particular dissatisfaction with the instructional and managerial methods used in Gaza’s overcrowded classrooms (GAGE, 2017), also children from different age groups have different coping mechanisms to crisis (UNICEF 2018). Hence the suggested teacher training will be adapted to the specific age and gender differentiated needs of adolescent girls and boys. Specific indicators of the activities listed above:• 1087 teachers (354 female teachers, 733 male teachers) ,safety committees and parents (especially mothers) in 33 schools (10 girls schools and 23 boys schools) trained on safety practices and first aid;• 20,354 students (6429 adolescent girls and 13,925 adolescent boys (age 15-18) trained on safety practices and emergency preparedness • 33 vulnerable schools (10 girls schools and 23 boys schools) in Gaza equipped with basic safety equipment and materials• 354 female teachers in 10 secondary girls schools trained on emergency preparedness and response capacities adapted to the specific needs of vulnerable adolescent girls in the Gaza Strip • 6429 adolescent girls/students (from age 15-18) in 10 girls schools benefit from emergency preparedness and response capacities adapted to their needs. Overall, it is expected that education staff will be exposed to essential knowledge on how to protect schools male and female students alike. Schools will become protective environments for children with improved safety and learning. In addition, female teachers in the identified girls schools will be better able to respond to the specific gender and age needs of adolescent girls from age 15 to 18 in times of crisis. Targets/Priority focus: • 33 Schools (23 boys schools, 10 girls schools) in vulnerable areas depending on assessment on the ground and update of the matrix of vulnerable schools by the Education Cluster/Protecting Schools from Attacks Working Group.• Ensure gender equality in planning, implementation and evaluation phases: Student, teachers and counsellors seem to be equally affected in terms of trauma and of their need for resources to cope with the consequences of the conflict in terms of the security of learning environments. However, there are specific needs linked to gender. For instance, trainings need to be adapted to be gender-sensitive to the different kinds of manifestations of distress, and male and female teachers and counsellors need to be adequately equipped to respond. As all activities will be promoting active participation as a pivotal element, attention needs to be given to the different positions in terms of access to resources and decision making for women and men. • Ensure that age differentiated needs are incorporated in the project, especially the needs of adolescent girls (age 15-18) who have different coping mechanisms and capacities in times of crisis, and will receive adapted assistance according to these needs. Monitoring and Evaluation:Appropriate protection mechanisms will be put in place through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. UNESCO will develop a simplified model with number of indicators for each component. Also, the focus of UNESCO’s M&E efforts will include analysis of information provided by implementing partners combined with physical monitoring directed at the school level. Furthermore, UNESCO will conduct teacher/community focus groups related to perceived satisfaction and the effectiveness of the project.
