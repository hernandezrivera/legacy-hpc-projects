1 - Needs

COVID-19 has been declared a global pandemic. As we continue to navigate this rapidly evolving situation, it is essential to stay informed on the latest news, updates and resources about the virus and its effect on children.The Corona virus otherwise known as COVID-19. Since its emergence, the COVID-19 pandemic continued to change the environments in which people live. The disease outbreak is having both short term and far-reaching implication for families are particularly vulnerable Children. Disruptions to families and the wider community can have negative consequences for child well-being and development. As the virus continues to spread across the world, individuals are facing multiple new stresses, including physical and psychological health risks, school and business closures, limited access to basic services, family confinement, isolation and economic vulnerability. Through all of that, children, persons with disabilities (PWDs) and Aged population are particularly vulnerable. Children especially those with disabilities are among those most dependent on face-to-face services—including health, education and protection—which have been suspended as part of social distancing and lockdown measures. Children living in institutions and detention – including child migrants – face a different kind of vulnerability. Their continued care is easily put in jeopardy at a time of crisis. Contested authority over these settings poses self-evident challenges for instituting measures to control and mitigate the spread of the virus. Lockdown measures risk ensnaring children in unsafe situations. Longer-term effects The ultimate impact of the crisis on children hinges on how much time it will take for the pandemic to end. Children during this period may likely face not only a higher risk of child marriage, child labor, and teenage pregnancies, but will see their lifetime earnings potential precipitously fall. The preventive measures being put in place only mildly consider gender-specific needs for girls and women and vulnerabilities of PWDs. Furthermore, access to information is often a barrier for especially persons with Children and care givers especially those with disability who have specific impairment and communication needs. These may increase their protection risks and lead to negative coping mechanisms.To reduce this protection risk on children WINN will use the following approach: This project intends to ensure 60 Children (Children with disabilities, CAAG, UASC, and other Vulnerable Children) receive Child protection case management including referral to specialized services, 4,000 (boys, Girls, Women and Men) benefit from protection messages targeting children and caregivers and child friendly messaging (Developed and disseminated appropriate pre-recorded (audio) and other IEC key messages on the prevention and detection of COVID-19 in local languages) on COVID 19 and Provision of mental health and psychosocial support services to at least 2,000 children and caregivers. WINN through its ongoing program intends to capitalize on the capacities which already exist (including human resources) that can be strengthen and leveraged to deliver project target.

2 - Activities

Through this project, WINN with the support of Donors and other stakeholders proposes to implement a broad-based programme using seven main strategies. These include: a. Building the capacity of project staff, Case Workers, and Community Volunteers to prevent and response to COVID-19.b. Awareness activities targeting households and vulnerable sites are well aligned with government strategies specific to the disease preventionc. Conduct Communities aware on COVID-19, it's mode of transmission, signs of infection and mitigation as well as services available. d. Case Management of UASC, CwDs, and other vulnerable groups. Children (PWDs, UASC, and other Vulnerable groups) affected by COVID-19 received protective and specialized, including access to basic services and referral to other basic needs and where relevant WINN will conduct family trace for UASC.e. Provide psychosocial support services to children and caregivers: WINN will comply with the government directives in terms of avoiding grouping people and adjust their group-based activities plans accordingly. Nevertheless, awareness-raising will be done individually (door to door) or through different modalities by using/playing prerecorded audio using public address system speakers (woofers). Public address system (woofer speakers) will be procured to support the dissemination of recorded information. This activity will be carried out by WINN community Volunteers. Weekly Sensitization of community through radio messaging and jingles. The team will not expose the safety of their beneficiaries and staff and ensure duty of care by ensuring that the recommended precautionary measures to prevent and mitigate the spread of COVID-19 will be considered during all activities.In order to actualize these strategies, WINN will ensure an effective coordination and partnerships arrangement are in place with other agencies to ensure service deliveries are not duplicate and others are not lift behind.
