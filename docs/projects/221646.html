Project Summary

The IRC Child Protection (CP) program will deliver life-saving support to 18,500 (3700 IDPs, 1850 Returnees and 12,950 Residents) and their caregivers across returnee, internally displaced persons (IDP), and host communities in Priority 1, 2, and 3 locations in Unity State (Rubkona, Panyijiar, Koch, and Pariang Counties), Northern Bahr el Ghazal State (Aweil West County), and Central Equatoria State (Juba County) in South Sudan. The proposed CP interventions align with the Humanitarian Country Team’s strategic objectives (SO1 and SO3) and the Protection Cluster objectives (PCO1 and PCO3).
Key services will include comprehensive case management for children at risk, family tracing and reunification (FTR) services, alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and cash assistance to address basic needs. Community-Based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) will target children and caregivers experiencing trauma and psychological distress, leveraging proven IRC approaches such as Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) through Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS), Families Make the Difference (FMD) for caregiver parenting skills, Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE) for adolescent life skills and psychosocial support, Now I Am Stronger (NIS), and IRC’s Brighter Futures model for malnourished children with protection concerns.
Additionally, community-based child protection (CBCP) structures will be established and strengthened, alongside community awareness sessions on CP issues, risks, available services, and positive parenting practices. Capacity-building initiatives will support frontline CP staff, community volunteers, leaders, and government social workers. Child-friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms will also be reinforced to ensure accountability and responsiveness.
The IRC CP program is backed by funding from donors such as BHA, SIDA, PRM, UNICEF, SSHF, and GFFO during the proposed Humanitarian Needs Response Plan (HNRP) period. This CP response will be integrated with IRC’s ongoing programs in GBV, general protection, education, nutrition, health, and livelihoods to ensure holistic and comprehensive support for vulnerable children and their caregivers.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to intensify due to ongoing conflict, climate shocks, food insecurity, mass displacement, disease outbreaks, and economic decline, leading to an increased demand for humanitarian assistance. The Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) projects that, in 2025, approximately 11.29 million people, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and residents—will require assistance. Among these, about 9.18 million (81% of the affected population) face at least one of the top five protection risks: gender-based violence (GBV), forced eviction, child marriage and family separation, discrimination in accessing resources, attacks on civilians, and restrictions on freedom of movement (South Sudan PROMO, 2024). Within this population, 3.07 million children are in need of child protection services, with the IRC targeting 7.2% of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR) population for 2025.Children in South Sudan face grave protection risks, including abduction, recruitment into armed forces or groups, family separation, abuse, neglect, exploitation, and violence. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has exacerbated the crisis. By November 2024, over 877,240 individuals had entered South Sudan from Sudan, with 52% being children and 76% identified as South Sudanese returnees (UNHCR-IOM Dashboard, 2024). These children, especially in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Unity States, endure harrowing conditions during their displacement, including exposure to violence, attacks by armed groups, and prolonged deprivation of basic necessities.The 2023 VAS revealed severe protection challenges, including armed violence, domestic abuse, and inadequate complaint mechanisms. The Participatory Assessment Report for Unity State (September 2023) highlighted the extreme vulnerabilities of women, unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and child-headed households. Many children, forced out of school, face increased risks while engaging in activities such as cattle herding or collecting firewood. Additionally, IRC’s routine case management assessments have documented widespread psychological distress among children who have witnessed violence, been separated from family members, or endured abuse. These challenges compel families to adopt negative coping mechanisms, such as engaging children in child labor (e.g., fishing or canoe rowing), early marriages, and other harmful practices, particularly in Rubkona, Koch, and Panyijiar counties (IRNA, 2024).The IRC documented 473 cases (236 boys, 237 girls) of children with various protection concerns, including unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) in alternative care, child neglect, child labor, domestic violence, and sexual exploitation across Rubkona, Koch, Panyijiar, Aweil East, and Pariang counties (SS CPIMS+, November 25, 2024). UASC face significant challenges in meeting their basic needs due to the absence of parental or extended family support, leaving them without food, water, clothing, shelter, or care. These children, often out of school and lacking future opportunities, are left in unsafe environments that severely impact their mental and psychosocial well-being.The IRC has been addressing these child protection challenges through case management, psychosocial support, Social and Emotional Learning (SEL), and capacity-building initiatives. However, the scale of needs far exceeds current response capacities. Community-based child protection structures have been established, but these require further strengthening to effectively address the vast protection needs.In 2025, the IRC aims to provide life-saving child protection services to 18,500 (3700 IDPs, 1850 Returnees and 12,950 Residents) across six priority counties.

2. Project activities

The IRC proposes life-saving CP interventions for the HNRP 2025, targeting 18,500 (3700 IDPs, 1850 Returnees and 12,950 Residents). These interventions will integrate both protective and preventive modalities delivered through static and mobile services. The IRC’s primary objective is to establish a safe and protective environment for children by providing quality protection services while empowering vulnerable boys and girls including children with disability. Concurrently, the IRC will strengthen local government and community-level prevention mechanisms in alignment with internationally recognized standards, including the Minimum Standards for CP in Humanitarian Action, IASC Guidelines for MHPSS in Emergencies, and the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). Additionally, the IRC will adopt and implement standards and tools developed by the South Sudan Child Protection Area of Responsibility (CP AoR).The proposed CP activities include:1. Comprehensive Case Management: The IRC will deliver holistic case management services for children exposed to violence, neglect, exploitation, and abuse. Specific attention will be given to: Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC); Children at risk of or affected by recruitment by armed groups or forces and Children subjected to harmful traditional practices including child marriage and children with disability. Caseworkers will perform comprehensive assessments, develop and execute case plans with children’s participation, facilitate referrals to specialized services, and conduct regular monitoring to ensure safety and well-being. The CP Case Management Emergency Fund (CMEF) will be utilized on a case-by-case basis to address specific needs. The service mapping will be conducted every three months, and the referral pathways will be updated in all targeted counties. To maintain quality, case management officers will be trained in supervision and coaching, ensuring effective support for case workers.2. Community-Based Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS): The IRC will implement proven approaches such as: Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) through Safe Healing and Learning Spaces (SHLS); Families Make the Difference (FMD) to enhance caregiver parenting skills; SAFE to provide life skills and psychosocial support; and Now I Am Stronger (NIS) for community resilience building. Activities will include rehabilitating existing centers and constructing new SHLS to create safe environments for children. PTAs, and SHLS facilitators will be trained in PSS using UNICEF’s PSS manual and IRC’s SEL standards. Schools will be equipped with CP help desks, teacher PSS training, and play materials for extracurricular activities.3. Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPM): The IRC will establish and strengthen Community-Based Child Protection Committees (CBCPCs) to enhance prevention and response to child abuse, neglect, exploitation, and violence. CBCPC members will be trained in: role responsibilities of CBCPC, CPiE; DRR and Case identification and safe referral practices.Community awareness campaigns will be delivered focusing on CP risks reduction and prevention, available services, and positive parenting practices through various platforms.4. Capacity Building: The IRC will deliver targeted training programs to IRC staff, partners, government social workers, and community leaders. These trainings will cover: Case management, including the use of CPIMS+; Key MHPSS curricula (SEL, FMD, SAFE); PFA and CPiE standards and best practices.5. Child-Friendly Feedback and Complaint Mechanisms: To enhance accountability, the IRC will strengthen the existing and establish new accessible and child-friendly feedback and reporting mechanisms, ensuring that children and caregivers can safely voice concerns and provide input on services.
