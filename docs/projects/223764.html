Project Summary

Unprecedented rainfall events, a consequence of the climate crisis, have escalated water-related diseases, notably cholera and acute diarrhea. Furthermore, extraordinary floods, coupled with the dynamic population shifts within internally displaced persons (IDP) camps, characterized by high population density and an influx of returnees to their places of origin, have posed formidable challenges to the operation and maintenance of water and sanitation infrastructure. Properly managing fecal sludge and solid waste is compromised, leading to a substantial environmental and public health impact.
These challenges unfold within a context marked by a surge in people requiring water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) assistance, predominantly concentrated in the country's northeastern part. According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) conducted by IOM between 2021 and 2022, Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states experienced a significant increase in the number of people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance, rising from three million to five million individuals. This surge is evident across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+113%), and host communities (+95%).
In response to these identified needs and alignment with the objectives of the Nigerian WASH Sector, IOM is poised to enhance its provision of WASH services. The focus extends toIDPs, returnees in camp and camp-like settings, and hosting communities in BAY states. Additionally, IOM will distribute hygiene kits following national/cluster standards and reinforce the WASH sector's common pipeline strategy to facilitate access to hygiene kits for regional humanitarian actors.
The primary activity will ensure access to standard hygiene kit supplies through IOM's common supply unit. This includes various hygiene kits such as Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), replenishment, Cholera, and sanitation kits. This comprehensive approach supports rainy season preparedness and response, including critical cholera preparedness and response activities.
The project's implementation targets 1,193,206 individuals in the BAY states, specifically focusing on hotspot areas identified through previous flash reports and occurrences. Various local government areas (LGAs) have been earmarked, including those in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe states, carefully considering specific geographic and community contexts.
IOM is committed to ensuring that the design and implementation of all WASH activities are inclusive of age, gender, and disability-specific needs, explicitly addressing the unique requirements of women and girls, including MHM. Engaging with community structures on the ground during missions and establishing communication channels where possible is integral to assessing, monitoring, and evaluating gender, age, and disability-specific needs and the impact of activities.
Using the IOM WASH Monitoring and Evaluation Framework and the Gender Inclusion and Gender-based Violence (GBV) Risk Mitigation Toolkit for WASH Programming, IOM will uphold "Do No Harm" principles throughout WASH interventions. This commitment is particularly emphasized in activities involving latrine and water point locations and construction, with mechanisms such as consultations with vulnerable groups playing a pivotal role.
In analyzing potential risks associated with context-specific environmental degradation, IOM will prioritize actions that mitigate ecological risks. This includes a preference for locally made items and local procurements within the BAY states for cost-effectiveness, time efficiency, and support to the local economy. Furthermore, in procurement, IOM will prioritize products with greater durability and high recycled content, contributing to sustainability and environmental responsibility.

1 - Needs

WASH needs, particularly the essential provision of critical supplies like Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, replenishment kits, soap, sanitation kits, and cholera kits, continue to escalate in magnitude and severity throughout Northeast Nigeria. The evolving dynamics of population displacement and return and the persistent impacts of the climate crisis, marked by cycles of floods and droughts, have intensified the demand for WASH services. In the Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for 2025, the WASH Sector underscores the significance of in-kind supplies as a primary response modality for life-saving interventions in areas grappling with emergencies. This is particularly pertinent in regions, notably in northeast Nigeria, that have been profoundly affected by mass displacement and recurring cholera outbreaks since 2019. Many of these areas are geographically remote, lacking fundamental infrastructures like roads and the capacity to deliver alternative aid forms, such as cash transfers. The absence of basic services and the recurring shocks experienced by households pose significant barriers to recovery from years of conflict and violence.IOM Nigeria, through its oversight of the WASH core pipeline program, is dedicated to effectively supporting the in-kind response modality. This commitment involves ensuring a dependable, cost-effective, and consistent flow of core relief supplies to partners for distribution among crisis-affected populations across BAY states.The collective pipeline response extends beyond planned camps to encompass new arrival sites, transit and reception centers, spontaneous camps, camp-like settings, and host communities facing a deficit or absence of WASH supplies in the BAY states. This comprehensive approach reflects a commitment to reaching diverse and underserved populations with the critical relief items they urgently require.

2 - Activities

IOM will strengthen its humanitarian activities in line with the objectives and guidelines of the Nigerian WASH sector and the global IOM's WASH principles of implementing actions that are immediate; appropriate, innovative, and compatible with needs; scalable to respond to the changing dynamics of the Nigerian context; and sustainable to respond to environmental issues and the challenges of WASH resource management and environmental risk management.Management and provision of hygiene kits for the affected population through WASH Sector partners. • IOM ensures this through its common pipeline unit through robust management of procurement, transportation, prepositioning, replenishing, proper storing, and dispatching of WASH essential items. As a result, agencies that deliver frontline services can access the items in as little as 48 – 72 hours and provide the crisis-affected population with lifesaving assistance. Prepositioning • Common pipeline intervention is also an effective solution to tackle the complex logistical context common in many humanitarian emergency responses. Access to remote locations in Northeast Nigeria has been the biggest challenge due to a lack of infrastructure, seasonal changes, and security constraints. In addition, the roads in many parts of Northeast Nigeria are only accessible during the dry season. Prepositioning basic hygiene kits in critical locations will help respond to cholera outbreaks during the rainy season.Information Sharing • IOM Common Pipeline will provide online real-time updates of pipeline stock status to inform WASH Sector partners of available critical WASH supplies in the key IOM warehouses across the BAY states. The IOM Pipeline also assists in collecting utilization/distribution reports from partners to support the Sector in ensuring that in-kind assistance has been used/distributed accordingly. Localization and Capacity Building • The pipeline will support localization efforts and strengthen the capacity of national WASH partners to respond to needs – insofar as the significant cost implications of procuring, transporting, and accessing warehousing for kits have impaired national partners’ ability to respond. In emergency response scenarios, the pipeline will prioritize the initial response to the most urgent needs within 72 hours. Additionally, IOM will develop capacity-building activities for key stakeholders, authorities, and partners, including capacity-building on humanitarian project cycle management focusing on WASH NFI Distributions, Good Distribution Training developed by IOM and focusing on PSEA and GBV risk mitigation and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) activities. Further, affected communities benefit from community-tailored gender and age-sensitive critical WASH Supplies aiming to promote hygienic practices through sensitization sessions with community members and stakeholders to minimize the spread of WASH-related and other communicable diseases such as COVID-19 through the distribution of hygiene, MHM, cholera, replenishment, and sanitation kits.Finally, activities to strengthen the resilience of conflict-affected people and promote the humanitarian-development-peace nexus (HDPN), early recovery, durable solutions, WASH and peacebuilding, and social cohesion.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protecting the rights of persons displaced and affected by the humanitarian crisis has always been paramount during programming by IOM. The programs are designed not only to enhance the protection of vulnerable populations but also ensure that protection and humanitarian principles, such as do-no-harm, are mainstreamed into its programming. This continues to allow IOM to deliver assistance in a manner that promotes the protection, safety, and dignity of the affected population and provides equity to women, girls, men, and boys. As part of this project, the IOM WASH program will closely coordinate with the IOM Protection staff and community-based WASH committees during the planning, design and adaptation of the proposed activities. Protection staff will also support the training of WASH staff, most notably those undertaking community-based assessments, on how to respond to protection issues, including instances of GBV. This will ensure that protection concerns are identified early on and integrated into the implementation of the project. In addition, any protection concerns raised through the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism will also be addressed.IOM is an active member of the Protection Sector Working Group, Child Protection Sub-Working Group, and GBV Sub-Working Group and will consider any in-country standards and principles agreed upon by the Sector. In addition, IOM will integrate the global Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and IOM standards and practices, including those outlined in the IOM Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In line with the WASH Sector and its strategies, IOM will maintain its commitment to engaging with affected communities at all phases of the program cycle. The pipeline project works directly with organizations that will implement their own projects but will also work within the direction of accountability to affected populations within their respective sectors. IOM's Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework will ensure that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with the community needs and humanitarian frameworks. IOM will utilize feedback from previous assessments and PDMs conducted to reflect the needs and specific vulnerabilities of the affected populations. This will ensure that the materials and kits supplied adequately support the targeted population. IOM's existing complaints and feedback mechanism will continue to encourage and provide a platform for affected communities to submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. After completing the project, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and reinforce knowledge management practices, particularly on best practices and lessons learned.
