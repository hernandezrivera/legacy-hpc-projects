Project Summary

The year of 2020 has been characterized by several emerging and anticipated shocks that have impacted hugely on the nutrition situation of children as well as nutrition service delivery in South Sudan. The COVID-19 pandemic, floods in Jonglei and heightened inter-communal conflict were shocks that tremendously impacted access to nutrition services in the country. Based on the IPC report (December, 2020), Bor South, Budi, Kapoeta North and Pibor Counties are among the counties in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). The nutrition situation in all the four counties is expected to deteriorate significantly. This project seeks to address nutritional needs of children <5yrs and PLWs affected by acute malnutrition in Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta North counties through both OTP and TSFP. GREDO will be seeking to enhance efforts to reduce malnutrition including promoting the broader goals of improving knowledge related to childhood nutrition and MIYCN practices and health seeking, establishment of kitchen gardening for food diversification as part of the nutrition-sensitive agriculture agenda as well as strengthening active case finding and integrated community outreach program. GREDO will be using both static and mobile/outreach response approaches in the four counties to reach all malnourished children and PLWs. In order to have high impact response, GREDO will provide IYCF nutrition counselling to 50,300 primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months, conduct 20 cooking demonstrations sessions, provide Vitamin A supplementation to 49,500 children 6-69 months and conduct a campaign to deworm 44,500 children 12-59 months. In addition, 6,200 (1,200 in Budi, 2,400 in Pibor, 1,600 in Bor South and 1,000 in Kapoeta North), and 9,800 (1,800 in Budi, 4,000 in Pibor, 2,000 in Bor South and 2,000 in Kapoeta North) children 6-59 months will be admitted for treatment of SAM and MAM respectively. GREDO will also admit 9,200 PLWs (1,600 in Budi, 4,000 in Pibor, 1,800 in Bor South and 1,800 in Kapoeta North) PLWs for treatment of acute malnutrition. SAM and MAM management need to be complemented with preventive responses targeting vulnerable and at risk children and PLWs. For this reason, GREDO will also include integration of cluster priorities such as Integrating malaria testing, NFI distribution, WASH supplies into CMAM activities and MIYCN activities.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview, an estimated 1.9 million people are anticipated to suffer from acute malnutrition in 2021, including 313,391 children (163,913 male and 149,476 female) with severe acute malnutrition (SAM), 1,078,867 children (562,697 male and 516,170 female) with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), and 483,383 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). Acute malnutrition, one of the top nutrition-related causes of death in children under-five, continues to affect children under-five and pregnant and lactating women. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis October 2020 – July 2021, December 2020, At least 53 (68%) counties of South Sudan are classified as IPC Acute Malnutrition (AMN) Phase 3 and above. Out of this, 29 counties are in IPC AMN Phase 4, and 24 counties in IPC AMN Phase 3. Further deterioration of the nutrition situation is projected during the lean season of April to August 2021. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases (up to 36%), poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%). Elevated levels of food insecurity (IPC AFI Phase 3 and above) in most counties also contribute to acute malnutrition. Furthermore, poor access to health and nutrition services due to heightened inter-communal conflict and flooding mainly in the Jonglei and Greater Upper Nile States are also contributing to acute malnutrition. COVID-19 related disruptions, including those rightfully implemented to curb coronavirus infection rates, as well as changes in SAM and MAM referral protocols for children have exacerbated lack of access to services. According to this report, all the four counties Budi, Kapoeta North, Bor South and Pibor in IPC Phase 4 and the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate significantly as a result of unprecedented levels of food insecurity, flooding experienced in Pibor and Bor South limit access to services due to the heavy rains. During this time these counties are expected to reach Critical levels of acute malnutrition. These counties continue to experience high levels of malnutrition due to the deteriorating situation necessitating continued provision of nutrition services through existing OTP and TSFP sites and scaling up services through outreaches. In Budi County, low livestock ownership at 31% of the population coupled with delayed rainfall, poor market access and high food prices are contributing to Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Thousands of people in are likely to face severe hunger due to lack of rainfall and unexpected crop failure according to situation on the ground in the greater Kapoeta consisting of Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Budi counties as the first phase of harvest has gone without food crop yields. The areas of Ngauro, Loudo and Nagishot Payams in Budi County can be considered hard to reach as a result of the inaccessibility of the area considering its mountainous topography which has led to a decline in the nutrition situation affecting consumption, access to services and severely disrupting life-saving activities pushing communities deeper into a nutrition emergency. The ultimate result of this will be reduced access to food and other services pushing families deeper into malnutrition. According to data provided by the nutrition cluster, the case load for SAM is 26,826 (2412 for Budi, 3616 for Kapoeta North, 12,447 for Bor South and 8351 for Pibor) and for MAM is 86821 (6999 for Budi, 10,496 for Kapoeta Nort, 41490 for Bor South and 27,836 for Pibor). Additionally, the data revel that the case load for PLWs is 41261 (4174 for Budi, 6260 for Kapoeta North, 18449 for Bor South and 12378 for Pibor). Treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) continue to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk children < 5 years and PLWs in these counties.

2 - Project activities

To address the aforementioned needs, GREDO will carry out several activities including but not limited to the following: Active case finding and referral of malnourished children (both SAM and MAM) and PLWs into appropriate nutrition programs for appropriate care. Active social mobilization to encourage communities to send malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites. Treatment of both severe and moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among PLWs. Training health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition. Provision of MIYCN services in nutrition sites and the community. Test and treatment of malaria among children with SAM. Increase access to IYCF interventions to mothers and caregivers. Rehabilitate 10 nutrition sites (Nahochod, Kadumakuch, Ngauro, Tulugi, Kathac, Hibongorok, Haliba, Ngatuba, Kapeta and Ngathilani) and ensure quality service delivery is provided through strengthening referral system and linkage through other nutrition services and sectors. Rehabilitate 5 nutrition sites in Pibor with 3 outreaches in Lekuangule in the greater Pibor County. Rehabilitate 4 nutrition sites in Kapoeta North with 3 outreaches. Rehabilitate 4 nutrition sites (Mareng PHCU, Jarweng PHCU and Pan Apet PHCUs in Anyidi Payam and Arek PHCU in Makuach Payam) in Bor South County. Timely, accurate and credible information sharing of program activities with relevant stakeholders. Ensuring safe and dignified participation by beneficiaries in the program activities. Implement complaint and feedback mechanisms. Conduct safety audits and ensure GREDO’s nutrition sites are safe for her beneficiaries. Conduct one a SMART survey in Budi County and one in Bor South County. GREDO will be implementing minimum package of Integrating Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) within existing health facilities by providing routine treatment using simple medical protocol to SAM and MAM children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Mother’s (PLW’s). In addition, GREDO will work towards integrating Health, WASH and FSL into Nutrition targeting 23 OTP/TSFP sites and 8 Outreaches. GREDO will provide these lifesaving nutrition services in collaboration with MoH, partners and local authorities through supporting initiatives for the decentralization of TSFP sites, increasing awareness through community mobilization. GREDO will in essence: - Carry out door-to-door screening (with the adoption of family MUAC in the context of COVID 19) of children and PLWs in the community for malnutrition and routine screening at the health facilities; Advocate for IYCF/MIYCN Practices for prevention of Malnutrition. HRP funding will enable GREDO to provide targeted emergency nutrition supplies and services to a MAM caseload of up to 35,666 for under 5 children and 27,525 PLW’s, and to a SAM case load of 15,546 for the year 2021. GREDO will train CNVs and MIYCN groups from host community to acquire skills and knowledge on Integrated Management of SAM/MAM and MIYCN so as to provide quality nutrition services. Existing health facility staff will be capacitated for integration of Acute Malnutrition management into health programs especially on malaria screening and referral of malnourished children under 5 and PLWs to the OTP/TSFP interventions. GREDO Project focal persons will undergo the mandatory IASC Gender Marker online assessment to ensure clear understanding of best practices of gender in the humanitarian context. GREDO's Health and nutrition staff including clinicians, nutrition assistants, nurses and midwives will be shared in this programme to increase cost effectiveness and strengthen output results.

GREDO does not anticipate major risks to the project except for the insecurity that canresult in looting of food from her stores. This risk will be mitigated by having securityguards to guard the main warehouse 24/7 and only take adequate nutrition supplies toOTP/TSFP to meet the nutritional needs of those sites on the days of follow-up so thatnot too much supplies are kept at the nutrition site. Also, accessibility issues due tohard terrains considering some locations like Bor and Pibor are prone to flooding while the uplands of Budi County is mountainous with only 20% of the facilities being accessible by a vehicle . For this GREDO will seek toutilize local staffs who are familiar with the terrains and will pre-position supplies inthe targeted locations to ensure proper flow of services to the community. GREDO willalso engage the community to carry the supplies to the targeted location in caseswhere the location cannot be accessed by a vehicle.
