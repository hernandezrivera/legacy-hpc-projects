Project Summary

Malnutrition is one of the top causes of death in children under five years and has a long-term negative impact on the children’s abilities to live to their full potential. South Sudan (SS) has experienced continued crisis including floods, conflict, and food insecurity which have impacted on the key drivers of acute malnutrition and aggravated the nutrition situation within the country. The Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 28 and 2022 SMART surveys report GAM prevalence at 16.0% (15.4 -16.7) in lean season. This indicates no improvement since 2019, when the GAM prevalence was 16.2% (13.8-18.5) in lean season the rate of acute malnutrition remains above the emergency threshold of 15 percent (very high acute malnutrition level- WHO classification). The situation calls for strengthened multisector investment to address the impact of aggravating factors, and to maintain gains in tackling the nutrition situation in South Sudan.The objectives of the action will be; To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents, and women .To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition .To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in Aweil South, Panyijar, Rumbek East, Rumbek North and Yirol West counties by the end of 2023. The proposed intervention aims to increase coverage and access to the most vulnerable community by providing equitable and accessible nutrition prevention treatment services. The nutrition services will deliver by scaling up the emergency nutrition responses through 62 static OTP/TSFP (10 in Aweil south,10 in Panyijar, 23 in Rumbek east, 7 in Rumbek North, and 12 in Yirol west), 5 Stabilization center (1 in Panyijar, 1 Aweil south, 1 in Rumbek east, 1 in Rumbek north, and 1 in Yirol west) and through 4 RRM mobile tam (2 in Aweil south, 2 in Panyjar,). In addition, the IRC will implement integrated CMAM and MIYCN in all sites. Promotion of optimal nutrition and prevention of malnutrition among young children (0-23 months through the implementation of MIYCN to ensure vulnerable children have optimal growth. IRC will strengthen community participation using existing community structures and active engagements of the affected community. IRC will conduct meetings with MtMSG, regular community sensitization sessions and scaling up of the existing Nutrition response to ensure timely service availability. Scale up family MUAC to ensure early detection of acute malnutrition that contributes to early detection. An estimated 20,085 SAM children,37388 MAM, and 30131 PLW MAM will be reached through this action across all implementation areas in OTP and TSFP, respectively. Continue with the capacity building of CHD, primary health care service providers and nutrition staff. Train health and nutrition staff on CMAM and MIYCN to ensure timely nutrition services. A rapid response team will be deployed to the hard-to-reach area to provide nutrition services that are accessible and vulnerable groups have got timely treatment. Subsequently, the interventions will reduce mortality and morbidity associated with malnutrition. Integration of nutrition activities with other sectors will also be implemented, including integration of health and nutrition education messages, Malaria testing and treatment, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, training on GBV and CP for nutrition staff. Similarly, with food security, establish kitchen garden, cash programs for children discharged cured from SAM treatment, and cooking demonstrations to improve dietary diversification, conduct kitchen garden and cash programs.

1 - Needs

The prevalence of wasting in South Sudan has been consistently high, among the highest in sub-Saharan Africa. According to the 2023 projections, an estimated 1.4 million children under the age of five in South Sudan will likely suffer from acute malnutrition over the course of 2023, including 345,000 severely malnourished, and will need treatment. Acute malnutrition in the country remains a public health problem, mainly affecting the most vulnerable groups of the population (particularly children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women). In addition, South Sudan (SS) continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict, and food insecurity – all contributing to high malnutrition levels. Of the five counties IRC is implementing, Aweil south and Panyijar counties, the recent SMART survey 2021 reveals that the nutritional status in both counties was at a Critical level with GAM of 23.1 % and 15.7%, respectively. On the other hand, according to the FSNMS round 28, Yirol west counties have severe nutrition status with proxy GAM rates of 13.4%. The remaining two counties have poor nutritional status, with Proxy GAM rates of 8.9% and 8.9% in Rumbek north and Rumbek east, respectively. Additionally, IPC food security classification in the 5 counties is reported as IPC3 crisis and IPC4 Emergency and is projected to deteriorate in 2023. Despite the low GAM rate in the four counties, multidimensional causes still exist that aggravate the nutrition situation. Periodic flooding, severe food insecurity, the sub-optimal practice of Maternal infant and young child feeding, and a high rate of under-five mortality and morbidity are attributed to worsening nutrition in the counties in the coming seasons. Despite the low GAM rate in two of the counties multidimensional cases that aggravate the situation are still significant these includes, flooding, severe food insecurity, sub optimal practice of Maternal infant and young child feeding, and high rate of under-five mortality and morbidity. These drivers contribute to worsening of the nutrition situation in the counties in the coming seasons. In the counties suboptimal practice of MIYCN remain prevalent in South Sudan especially complementary feeding practices. Though EBF practices seems to be seen improving however, MDD, MAD and MMF remain extremely low. These indicators have significant contribution in high prevalence of malnutrition (both acute and chronic). According to the FSNMS round 27 report the Minimum dietary diversification remains low, in unity MMD is at 31% Lakes 23 % and NBEG is at 21 %. Similarly, Minimum acceptable diet in all state remains extremely low, reported at 10.6 % in unity, 7.9% in NBEG and 7% in Lakes. Under this proposed action IRC intend to reach though this proposed actions. IRC targets 20,085 SAM children,37388 MAM, and 30131 PLW MAM will be reached through this action across all implementation areas in the 62 OTP and TSFP centers respectively. IRC will reach 54803 primary caretakers of children 0-24 months women through Key IYCF messages, and IRC will engage in national immunization days; an estimated 116,456 and 95905 children will be reached through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming, respectively, across the targeted counties.

2 - Project activities

The proposed response will focus on addressing the immediate nutritional needs and addressing the underlines cases through integrated response. First, to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition. IRC will establish robust community mobilization screening and referral mechanisms for early detection and treatment through community outreach activities. Family MUAC will be prioritized. The MtMSG, CHW, and CNV will be trained in MUAC screening and provided with MUAC tapes. Support and strengthen static 62 OTP and sites: IRC will provide the timely provision of quality nutrition therapeutic through 62 OTP/TSFP sites in all counties (AS:10, Panyi=10, RN;7, YW;12 and RE;23). Additionally, five stabilization centers (SC) will be supported and ensure 24/7 treatment for SAM children with medical complications needing inpatient services. All nutrition sites will have at least two Comminute Nutrition works. IRC will establish 4 Rapid response mechanism teams in Panyijar and Aweil south to serve hard-to-reach areas within the counties and improve program coverage. Secondly, Staff capacity building is a critical strategic part of the emergency response mechanism contributing to sustainability. IRC will capacity build and share capacity with the MOH, State MOH, county health department, community members, and IRC nutrition team. IRC will train staff on CMAM, MIYCN, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, GBV and Child protection. IRC will provide monitoring tools and national guidelines for the nutrition sites; conduct quarterly on-job mentorship and supervision of nutrition workers by qualified nutrition officers; facilitate joint (SMoH, CHD, UNICEF, WFP, and IRC) support supervision in all the counties. Establish feedback/complaints mechanisms at all nutrition sites in form community meetings and surveys; timely and regular nutrition information reports submission from all the nutrition sites into cluster nutrition dashboard; actively support and facilitate national surveys (FSNMS). Thirdly, promote and support safe and appropriate feeding for Maternal infant and young children Nutrition focused on preventing malnutrition through the provision of Behavior change and communication. IRC will establish and support 310 MTMSG linked to nutrition centers and conduct community nutrition education sessions. IRC will reach 54803 primary caretakers of children 0-24 months women through Key IYCF messages, and IRC will engage in national immunization days; an estimated 116,456 and 95905 children will be reached through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming, respectively, across the targeted counties. Fourth, to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups. IRC will establish kitchen gardens and conduct cooking demonstration at the nutrition centers. IRC will refer beneficiaries from TSFP to GFD. Similarly, all service points at the PHHCC/U will screen under five and PLW for malnutrition at MCH, EPI, OPD, and strong referral linkage. Conduct prevention and treatment of Malaria. IRC will refer mothers and children for ANC follow-up and immunization. Providing WASH kits for SAM clients attending nutrition sites. IRC will continue to integrate cash programing for children discharged cured for SAM. IRC will also ensure all gender and age groups entitled to equal access to nutrition services receive quality services.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The IRC will food insecure households with unconditional MPCA. The food insecure households will receive cash assistance equivalent to at least 50% of the Multi-sector Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (MSSMEB), based on the South Sudan Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) values. The IRC will coordinate with the WFP through its partners supporting GFD to ensure the cash assistance complements the 50% food ration provided by WFP in the communities. To transfer cash, the IRC will use a cash transfer agent, since there are no other locally available financial service providers in the communities, most households have no mobile phones, and there is very limited mobile service network in the communities. The IRC has an existing contract with experienced financial service providers including Alpha Commercial Bank, Kush Bank of South Sudan and Cooperative Bank. These cash transfer agents have been vetted and trained on beneficiary protection during cash distributions and have proven previously to be able to deliver cash transfers safely, efficiently and effectively in these remote locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

With the increasing displacement caseload caused by flooding and conflict in the counties that IRC works, which is having a negative impact on people’s wellbeing, coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate, with food insecurity, and lack of livelihood opportunities forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies. In response to the growing food insecurity characterized by rising food gaps and risk of starvation. The IRC will provide assistance through the distribution of unconditional MPCA to severely vulnerable and food insecure households (in IPC 4 or worse acute food insecurity) to enable them to meet immediate basic needs, as well as to avoid negative coping strategies which makes them more vulnerable in face of shocks and stresses. Targeted households will gain sufficient purchasing power to buy food and other basic needs from the local markets and be able to regain a normal life whilst working around alternative livelihoods opportunities.The cash assistance will enhance the purchasing power of the targeted households to meet food and non-food items (NFIs), to address underlying economic barriers to adequate food and nutrition, as well as to mitigate negative copying strategies.
