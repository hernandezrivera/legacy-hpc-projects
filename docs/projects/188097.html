Project Summary

This program aims at supporting the resilience of the conflict affected people in northwest Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorate. Shafak's proposed action is designed to contribute to supporting the transition to the early recovery phase, as it supports the targeted communities with long-term assistance activities, programs that focus on building/restoring the communities' infrastructure, local economic empowerment, and initiatives that ensure engagement and participation of the community in recovering from the protracted crisis. Over 2022, this program will achieve its objectives through the following:
a)Temporary Employment: 5000 vulnerable HHs within the targeted communities are supported through temporary income generation opportunities through Cash for Work activities.
Cash-for-Work activities will enable the provision of 5000 temporary labor opportunities focusing on the rehabilitation of community-identified projects related directly or indirectly to agriculture and community infrastructure, also Cash-for-Work activities will help the beneficiaries to meet their essential needs, be involved in the labor market and break the cycle on unemployment and retrieve self-confidence of the affected populations.
b) Establishing or expanding Small-Micro Enterprises through provision of business skills training and financial or in-kind support with value 750 USD to 1000 selected beneficiaries and access to financial services. in addition improving job skills and linkages to the job market. The identified participants will receive Business Development Skills training (BDS) including operations and financial management. The trained participants will be requested to submit their business plans which will be reviewed by a committee from Shafak. The grant will be delivered in two tranches. The identified beneficiaries will receive facilitation for the creation of savings and loan groups using the Village Savings and Loan Association (VSLA) methodology.
c) Financial Services: Facilitating the Establishment of 10 Village Savings Loaning Associations (VSLAs) to improve access to financial services. An estimated 10 Savings and Loan Groups, each consisting of approximately 10-15 members, will be facilitated to establish. The groups will constitute a common fund for members to save and borrow to finance a business or pay for life expenses. Groups will follow a standard savings and loan groups methodology; however, this will be adapted to the Syria context. Participants will elect their own group leaders, set their own rules. Individuals opt to join a group and identify their own members.
d) Providing vocational and skills training to 400 youth, men and women applicants and then support successful trainees (Expecting 300 trainees) with small-scale cash grants to enable them restart/initiate their small enterprises, each grant total will be 750 USD on average (between 600-900 USD) per beneficiary.
e) 400 HHs, Graduation Approach: Support self reliance of vulnerable households from those supported with Cash for food. The The Graduation approach will include also ,market facing skills , transfer of productive asset, financial services through VSLA, mentoring and Coaching, and Referral mechanism.
f) Rehabilitating the local economic, public and social infrastructure (20 Projects) through linking with the Cash-for-Work activity or through service providers. Each project cost will be about 15000 USD.
g) Supporting local community and social cohesion initiatives: Through forming community engagement team representing the different segments in the community. develop community action plans and identify the prioritized initiatives to be supported through the appropriate modality.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement the same activities with the same size and budget.

4 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021 (HNO), the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance in northwest Syria (NWS) has increased from 2.8 to 3.4 million people. 2.7 million are IDPs, many of whom are living in overcrowded locations with limited access to essential services. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone). Women and children represent 80 percent of this caseload while more than 22,000 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific. Most of the households (HHs) depended on humanitarian assistance, and the most prevalent dependency in the community are among widows, elderly headed HHs, PWDBased on Shafak Organization (SO) Needs Assessment, 38 per cent mentioned that the high price of food creates a challenge facing people in accessing food, 40 per cent the lack of money to buy food, 7 per cent the available food is not enough, 8 per cent the distance to the market creates a challenge in accessing food. Despite some signs of recovery, the financial capital development is severely lacking, with about two-thirds of government technical and vocational education and training (TVET) centers and complexes damaged or out of service and suffering from a serious deficiency of modern equipment. As a result, people can't do training, so they can’t get jobs that severely restrict the early recovery of the livelihood sector.
