Project Summary

The project aims at providing safe access to education and child safeguarding for 5,000 children in Konduga, Jere and Maiduguri LGAs of Borno State. The project has two results area:
Result 1: 5000 children from age 5 to 18 will benefit from the provision of basic education through enhancement of school enrolment and retention of out of school children;
Result 2: 100 women and children will benefit from the establishment of a protection centre and strengthening of child referral pathways in Borno State through enhanced partnership.

1 - Needs

The ongoing conflict, which is now in its eleventh year, and its associated displacement of people has continued to put pressure on communities in the north east. According to a secondary data review by the Education in Emergencies Working group, attendance and enrolment rates continue to be low across northeast Nigeria, with 2.2 million boys, girls and adolescents having no access to basic education. Attendance rates vary significantly by population group (e.g. displaced vs. non-displaced) as well as between and within states. Across most locations, girls are less likely to be attending schools than boys.Barriers to accessing education have been identified by previous assessments. Financial constraints and poverty are the most frequently cited barriers, followed by early marriage and cultural beliefs for girls and to a lesser extent, distance from schools. The inability of most households to carry out livelihood activities due to a combination of factors such as security constraints, lack of access to land and displacement has made it difficult for caregivers to support their children and keep them enrolled in school. Maiduguri, Jere and Konduga LGAs with a large number of IDPs and out of school children continue to need support to ensure that children are enrolled into schools and retained. These needs require a multisectoral approach to ensure that children acquire education in a safe and conducive environment where their protection is taken into full consideration. In addition tot eh needs for education and food, and livelihood assistance for families, girls, boys and women who are victims of abuse often do not have anywhere to turn to as there are limited safe homes for such victims and survivors of violence to stay and receive support.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 Basic Education1.1 Provision of Education Aids to 9 Basic SchoolsThe project aims at providing education supplies to 3000 children in 9 basic schools. Each education supply pack will consist of a bag, stationery and age-appropriate study materials based on Borno State Universal Basic Education curriculum.1.2 Training and Engagement of 60 Basic Education in Emergencies Teachers60 basic education in emergencies teachers will be supported to provide educational services in the target schools. They will receive training in child protection and safeguarding in accordance with SOS Child Safeguarding protocols.1.3 Set Up of 6 Child Friendly Spaces across 3 LGAsThe project will set up 6 child friendly spaces to serve as avenues to attract and educate 2000 out of school children in 3 LGAs. Each child attending the CFS will be given education pack containing a bag, stationery and study materials using the Teaching at the right level (TaRL) strategy. Activity 2 Establishment of Protection Centre and Strengthening of Child Referral Pathways2.1 Establish a protection House in MaiduguriThe project will equip a safe house for the use of the humanitarian community to refer cases of children and women who have suffered one form of violence or the other. The location and set-up will be carried out in consultation with relevant stakeholders in the protection sector. The centre will also serve as a counseling service for such cases, by providing psychosocial support for all persons in need.2.2 Set-up A Referral System for Children and Women in Need of Temporary ShelterTo ensure unhindered access of deserving survivors, SOS CV will provide pick-up services for survivors and utilize UNHAS helicopter service for pick-up of cases outside Maiduguri. Activity 3 Family Strengthening3.1 Provide Quarterly Food Assistance to Vulnerable Families of Children at the CFS and Basic SchoolsTo ensure that children are well taken care of and reduce school dropout rates, the project will provide quarterly food assistance to vulnerable caregivers of children at the CFS and Basic Education. 3.2 Provide Income-Generating Activities for Caregivers of Children in the CFSAs a sustainability strategy, caregivers will be trained on income-generating activities and supported to establish businesses to enable them care for their children.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SOS Children's Villages consults widely with all community members during project design and provides a platform for all beneficiaries to give feedback freely. The project will target school children and utilize child representative platforms to seek their views on all decisions that concern them.SOS Children's Villages also implements projects in consultation with caregivers of children children, who are part of the Key Implementing Partners (KIPs) and are fully involved in decision-making. To ensure that everyone's voice is heard, SOS CVN utilizes community volunteers and zonal leaders who are selected from the beneficiaries and serve as the first point of call for beneficiaries to be able to give feedback. In implementing this project, SOS Children's Village will publicize a toll-free line for all beneficiaries to be able to give feedback of the intervention and raise any concerns they may have. this toll-free line will be printed on all vouchers and pasted across all project locations, including schools and Child Friendly Spaces

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will collect feedback in three ways:1. Toll-Free Line- to be publicized in all project locations, including schools, Child Friendly Spaces and the Referral Centre. There will be arrangement for Complaint Response Mechanism officers to be able to speak the available local dialects in the area of intervention. All beneficiary feedback and complaints will be logged in and responded to by SOS CVN senior management using our safeguarding guidelines.2. Zonal Leaders are selected from caregivers in the community and are accessible to the community members for easy access and assistance.3. Periodic feedback sessions with different categories of the communities and camps will be carried out during quarterly surveys. Attention will be given to feedback from all categories, including boys, girls, men, women, elderly and persons with special needs. The monitoring and evaluation team of SOS CVN will carry out quarterly post distribution monitoring of the food and ensure that beneficiaries receive their entitlement and give feedback on the support received.
