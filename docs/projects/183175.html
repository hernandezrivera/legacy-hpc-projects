Project Summary

Responding to the devastating impacts of economic depression and political instability on the education system, in July 2021 AVSI conducted a rapid need assessment (26 public schools, 372HHs having children enrolled in 1st shift schools, 48% female) showing that: Majority of schools (80%) working at full capacity, expecting an influx of new students (17%), with increased challenges for students to afford stationary and learning supplies. Schools (almost 50%) inability to guarantee a sustained electricity and fuel supply for generators. Increased dropout rate confirmed by UNICEF at 15% and reasons as per AVSI’ records: socio-economic issues (55%), lack of accessibility to online learning (25%) and working children (19% with highest rate in South region). Increment in children stress and anxiety (1 in 3 children), whereas only 35% of schools have a referral mechanism in place for students in need of specialized services.
The action will take place in three Governorates: South, Nabatiyeh and Bekaa, where AVSI is well rooted since 2006 within the Lebanese community (schools, NGOs, municipalities). The selection of beneficiaries will be based on: Sphere Protection Principles and Core Humanitarian Standards, the socio-economic vulnerability criteria coupled with the interagency technical criteria defined by the Education ERP. Target group will be 87%Lebanese, 9% PRL and 4% migrants, with 50% F and 50% M, while 5% of the children target group will be CWD. The intervention includes identification of 2000 Out of Education Children (OOEC) through referrals from MEHE/Education Sector, community mobilization and hotlines, to reach the school-aged children who have missed their classes last year. The project foresees the provision of monthly cash assistance for 1500 HHs having in school children, by supporting equally all students within the same public school for the academic year 2021/2022, along with the provision of monthly cash support for 600 Households having OOEC. The HHs having children aged below 13 years old will be prioritized to avoid overlapping with other cash assistance interventions. The cash support aims to address basic needs, prevent negative coping strategy and promote access/retention in formal education. To ensure equal access to inclusive distance learning, the project will support 840 families (40% of families targeted with cash) with connectivity tools and low-tech materials, along with assistive technology and specialized services for 105 CWD. Moreover, and building on AVSI’ experience for caregivers’ engagement into distance learning, foreign language and digital literacy courses (16 sessions) will be conducted targeting 500 caregivers.
Within the socio ecological model approach, PSS activities for 2000 children and caregivers will be implemented addressing CP risks; by trained PSS monitors identified within the schools’ network in coordination with MEHE regional, National Education Scouts and schools. PSS sessions for adults will be facilitated by using the Caregivers Awareness tools implemented with public schools since 2017.
In parallel, for enabling schools to reopen with adequate standards, the project foresees institutional support to vulnerable schools receiving influx of students by contributing to the allowances of 450 existing or newly recruited teachers over 8 months to ensure a ratio of 25 students per teacher, with an average of 30 teachers per school for an estimated number of 15 public schools. Moreover, to compensate the missed learning objectives/competencies of the two previous academic years, the program will support public schools to implement inclusive catch-up courses targeting 1500 students in ECE, primary and secondary education. The targeted students and teachers will be equally provided with the necessary stationary and learning supplies. The list of targeted schools for the provision of teachers' allowances will be in compliance with MEHE and the Education Sector’s guidance.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

SO1: The project foresees the identification of vulnerable Lebanese and migrant OOEC through outreach, contacting schools, referrals from MEHE and provide families with the necessary information (using leaflets, list of inclusive public schools...) to facilitate their referral to basic services, including Education. The selected families will receive monthly cash support over 8 months. The project equally provides cash support to HHs having in school children (according to the number of children per HH) aiming to promote their retention in formal education over the upcoming scholastic year. To ensure inclusive distance learning, connectivity/low tech materials (data bundles, solar lanterns, solar mobile chargers...) will be provided for families according to the needs and assistive technology tools will be provided for CWD. Seeking a holistic response, foreign language and digital courses will be implemented to promote caregivers’ engagement in their children education; moreover, the project will provide PSS activities for children and caregivers to promote their well-being and resilience. SO2: The project foresees institutional support to the selected schools (receiving large influx of new comers) with allowances for teachers to improve the ratio teacher/student. Finally, the program will support as well the implementation of catch-up programs by schools along with the provision of learning materials for students/teachers.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The project assessment was conducted with a gender sensitive approach (77% female school principals, 51% female students, local NGOs led by women), AVSI integrated IM web-based platform includes SADD to track protection risks and trends. The action will ensure equal access and opportunities for males and females along with equitable processes for the control of resources and decision-making roles. Activities will be implemented remotely and/or physically in mobile or static modality, to reach working children and girls with social norms forcing them to stay home. Moreover, PSS activities and safety evaluation will be carried out to ensure safe learning environment. The project covers: allowances for schools’ teachers (90% are female in average), learning supplies neutral of any gender stereotype and assistive technology tools to CWD. AVSI will deploy qualified staff trained on PSEA and gender mainstreaming, making sure all People of Concern are aware of their rights and entitlements.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The project respect IASC principles on AAP. The selection criteria and process will be explained to beneficiaries mainstreaming Conflict Sensitivity and Do No Harm approaches, along with consultative and functional participatory approach involving the target groups into the project phases (assessment, planning, implementation, co-development of recommendation and monitoring). Moreover, a Feedback and Complaint Mechanism is in place with a dedicated Accountability Officer. For the cash assistance, AVSI will sign an engagement agreement with each head of HH stating the scope of cash support, in line with AVSI policies and with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS). A post monitoring distribution will be conducted to assess beneficiaries’ feedback on the program effectiveness. The program envisages partnerships with schools and stakeholders build upon their long-term relationships and trust; moreover, existing multi sector referral mechanisms will be endorsed.
