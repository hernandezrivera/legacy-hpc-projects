Project Summary

GOAL’s work in the WASH sector is designed to address key needs and integrate with other sectors, particularly to ensure effective WASH and IPC in health sector and work with community health and nutrition to prevent disease vectors, malnutrition, and outbreaks. Water supply will be provided in communities and in health facilities where there is a major lack of water identified during needs assessments. Environmental health will work in a comprehensive way to promote a sustainable solid waste management system along with tools needed for cleaning and latrines for reducing open defecation. Hygiene promotion is largely implemented by the community health and nutrition teams, with key handwashing and other hygiene behaviors being provided through SBCC groups in those sectors conjointly with other health-seeking behaviors. The proposed WASH interventions are designed to address the specific needs of men, women, boys and girls and at the same time ensuring the most vulnerable are prioritized for assistance. At the initial project stage, GOAL will ensure both men and women are represented to enhance joint decision making of the rehabilitations being done. Water management committees will have at least 40% women. The design of sanitation infrastructure will take into consideration needs of both men and women and those with disabilities. For instance, all latrines being rehabilitated will have doors lockable from inside to ensure privacy of the users, especially women and girls. Shared latrines (twin pits) will be constructed such that they face opposite directions to enable women and men enjoy their privacies. The project will work with Community Health Promoters who will be gender balanced so as to reach both the males and females community members. Hygiene, water, and environmental health affect everyone, but women and adolescent girls are facing particular needs and risks related to these. Often responsible for fetching water and cleaning their households, they also face menstrual hygiene and related reproductive health challenges which are often unseen by men. For these reasons, GOAL ensure a comprehensive response factoring gender, age, and disability considerations in the WASH sector. Infrastructure design explicitly factor gender, age, and disability access – ensuring that latrines and health facilities are accessible and easy to use for elderly and persons with disabilities, and of course gender-segregating latrines and considering women’s safety in the locations. Also, GOAL’s community health and hygiene promotion work directly reaches women with information and positive behavior models for menstrual hygiene and reproductive health services. Furthermore the environmental health activities engage all stakeholders to ensure that men and women both take responsibility for the cleanliness of their communities.

1 - Needs

Environmental health is critical for prevention of WASH-related diseases such as acute watery diarrhea (AWD), cholera, dysentery, hepatitis E, typhoid, acute respiratory infections, polio, and malnutrition. COVID-19 is also linked to poor handwashing facilities with soap and water. A needs assessment conducted in December 2021 which covered Kutum, Al Waha, Rashad, Talodi, Abukarshola, Habila localities, found critical WASH needs especially in Talodi, Habila and Rashad localities, with both the general population as well as health facilities in particular unable to access clean water, without adequate sanitation infrastructure and services, and using poor hygiene practices that can contribute to disease. A high number (63%) of households lacked basic sanitation, while 23% did not have access to a hand-washing facility with soap and water. The 2021 needs assessment established that waste management and infection control practices were almost absent with 78% of the communities not having designated garbage disposal areas. In the 4 new proposed PHC facilities, there is a critical need for functional incinerators, which is essential for infection prevention and control in the facility. Latrine access in PHC, schools, and communities is also a challenge for many, with open defecation continuing in all localities - 60% for Kutum, 69% for Habila, 68% for Rashad, 70% for Abukarshola and 70% for Talodi. Water supply needs are observed all across the intervention areas. In GOAL’s 2021 needs assessment, 57% of respondents lacked access to basic safe drinking water. In Kassab IDP camp in North Darfur, 14 of 21 hand pumps, and 1 of 2 mini-water yards, are not functional. Most people, 63%, still had to walk for more than an hour to a water source which poses protection challenges for women. In South Kordofan, most of the population of Habila and Rashad rely on seasonal ponds (hafirs) for water, with only 58% of hand pumps functional in Habila, and 25 hand pumps and 10 water yards out of operation in Rashad. In the new proposed health facilities under Purpose 1, GOAL’s assessments found that Bridik in ND and Abu Alhassan in SK both lacked connection to nearby water points, relying on water brought by carts. From the above data it is clear there is a high usage of untreated water among the populations, resulting in a possibility of contamination during water trucking and storage. The lack of clean water or inability to access clean water is creating a high risk of disease outbreak, including Hepatitis A, typhoid, bloody diarrhea and acute diarrhea. With concerns over the potential for cholera and other water related illnesses to spread in targeted areas, it is now particularly critical to ensure that households have access to clean water, and the knowledge to protect themselves from waterborne illness.Handwashing practice remains very poor in all targeted areas from GOAL’s 2021 needs assessment in rural areas, with only 20% of people in Rashad, 13% in Talodi, 21% in Habila, and 32% in Kutum knowing all of the right times to wash hands, and less than 50% of people in Abukarshola and Habila locality using soap regularly. Handwashing has been promoted in many urban areas for a long time but in rural parts of these localities, there is a clear gap.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

GOAL Sudan incorporates protection principles in every aspect of programming and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian action while making sure that response is participatory and that it is accountable to the people GOAL seeks to assist. For GOAL Sudan, protection mainstreaming is crucial both internally and externally to ensure that extremely vulnerable groups including children, women, people with disabilities and the elderly can access services and are protected from both intentional and unintentional harm. GOAL has ensured that all staff and partnersare aware of protection issues through training, and protection mainstreaming tools have been used in programming and internally to ensure the safety of the extremely vulnerable groups. Throughout its intervention GOAL is promoting gender equality and promoting beliefs and norms that foster respectful, non-violent gender norms to help prevent and mitigate GBV. Safety, respect, confidentiality and non-discrimination in relation to survivors and those at risk are vital considerations for GOAL.
