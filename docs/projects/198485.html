Project Summary

This proposed HOPE360 project aimed at scaling up equitable access to quality livelihood and food assistance through the most appropriate modalities (in kind, voucher, cash or multi-modalities, ready to eat food kits, wet rations, dry rations) and also move forward to build livelihoods that will enable the targeted households to build resilience. The proposed project is a bridge from relief to future development interventions. This aims to move Households beyond relying mostly on aid and build capacity and resilience. through agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods to support household food security. The resilience will further be achieved through the start-up and development of Village Savings and Loans (VSLA) model adapted to the local context and capacities,
Overall Objective: The HOPE360 proposed project aimed on the FSS objective 1 To improve the most vulnerable crisis-affected people’s access to timely and appropriate food assistance, including fuel and energy related support, to meet their immediate food needs with the CO01-INDO1 and CO01-IND02 and FSS objective 2 To strengthen resilience of crisis-affected people by re-establishing, improving and diversifying key agriculture livelihoods (including crop production livestock, fisheries, forestry, and natural resources management) with key indicator CO01-IND04

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in North-East Nigeria due to conflict resulted in widespread displacement of more than 2 million people. Due to increased insecurity, people do not expect to return to their homes and have to remain within their temporary settlements. On the average, IDPs in the target area are displaced for almost 3 years. In Jere, the Multisectoral needs assessment conducted in September 2019, showed that expensive food couple with no livelihood skill to build their resilience is a barrier for 79% of the respondents. Jere continues to be classified as “Severe in living standards and wellbeing” measures and in over-all severity in the Humanitarian Needs Overview.The target of this intervention are the crises affected Internally displaced populations (IDPs) in MMC and Jere. Based on need assessment, the youths/adults (with both genders inclusive) are faced with much anxiety and depression because of uncertainty of their future with no skills and coupled with the fact that some of them are over aged for conventional education. The youths presently, find themselves in idleness and joblessness that lead them to aggression, frustrations, depression and anger, due to the insurgency that displaced them out of their communities, and place them under restriction in camps as they can’t earn a means of livelihood as before, such as farming, fishing, animal rearing and small-scale businesses. And as a result of that, they find themselves involve in an illegal activity such as theft, gangsters, exploitation, substance abuse etc. However, due to lack of vocational training facilities and awareness, the youths have never been offered these techniques to create their micro enterprises in a profitable manner. They are mainly undertaking some local workload to generate daily meal, and as such they never get the opportunity to explore other skills and markets beyond the existing one due to lack of skill and training support. This project would include directly vocational and skills development and will be reaching the targeted people with appropriate capacity building to alleviate their suffering, emotional distress and mischief aimed at raising the standard of living, distribution of food card also aimed at building their food security and nutritional status of the vulnerable women. Thus, there is a high and urgent need to address the situation that will contribute towards alleviating the emotional distress and mischief.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community entry meetings and monthly feedback meetings. These meetings explain to the community the project and develops champions from within the community. HOPE360 will continue employing community friendly approaches to reach to the targeted states and LGAs areas through Vocational Training Skills and development. Also the approach will involve various levels of stakeholders. Each of the components of the project will be tackled technically by designing interventions that effectively and appropriately address vocational training skills and associated activities that can contribute to community development. It is anticipated that by the end of the project period, there will be realization of positive living and reduced levels of dependency among young people in project districts. Activity 2: Identification/Selection and registration/enrollment of 2100 which shall include women between the age 18 years and above, GBV Survivors and female headed household and child headed household who will be reached and the service will be managed jointly with community leaders, organizations and HOPE360 staff. It will still focus on assessing the nature of the community and the status of beneficiaries. Identification/Mobilization will also be in preparation for identifying and forming Livelihood Vocational Training skills training sessions: 2100 participants will be trained in sustainable activities which include: Odour control making, Room freshener and Soap Making, catering, cap knitting etc. Activity 3: Start-up grant After receiving business training and drawing up a viable business plan, each beneficiary will receive a cash grant of 30,000 NGN to set up a small business or strengthen an existing small business. The above mention activities will be implemented as the beneficiaries will be engaged and trained extensively and promoting their livelihood resilience. The various skills training sessions will last within the period of 5-months with livelihoods officers visiting each business on a bi-weekly basis, supporting the beneficiaries to grow their businesses, and addressing any challenges early on. The beneficiaries will also be encouraged to take place in a savings scheme over this period, saving a pre-agreed amount every week (approximately 200 NGN), which helps to entrench a culture of saving. To increase sustainability, VSLAs may be set up or strengthened in the target communities, with beneficiaries transitioning from individualized savings support to peer-to-peer savings support through the VSLA system by the end of the project. Start-up kits/grant at the end of the training and will be carried out across the implementing MMC, Konduga and Jere LGAs. Activity 4: Food card will be given to 2100 women (18years and above) within the project locations which will last for 5- months which aim at building their food security and nutritional status within the training period, distribution of food card will be conducted with beneficiaries separated in smaller groups. HOPE360 distribution system through the use of color-coded cards and different distribution dates by batch of beneficiaries to avoid drawing crowds and reduce security risks as part of HOPE360 s commitment in protection mainstreaming by ensuring the safety of beneficiaries at distribution sites. The integrity lay-out of distribution sites also ensures appropriate sections and priorities for vulnerable groups such as elderly and pregnant and lactating women. Activity 5: Sensitization on Protection issues and gender based GBV. These are sessions where women and girls join focused groups discussions that identify risks, threats, vulnerabilities and align these with community capacities to mitigate or external sources for reporting and possible referrals of GBV case for proper management.
