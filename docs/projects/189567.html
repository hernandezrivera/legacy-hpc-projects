Project Summary

CIDO has been working with vulnerable conflict affected communities across South Sudan to provide life-saving interventions and also build resilience to create environments in which local population can thrive and overcome situations that adversely affect their livelihood by providing humanitarian aid and support. This project will be implemented in Akobo in Jonglei State, Tambura and Ezo in Western Equatoria State, Lainya and Yei in Central Equatoria State targeting 134,517 beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees and host community), due to continued conflict, population has been disrupted and trapped in villages of Pibor and Akobo not able to access basic facilities including market where they can buy food or replenish farm tools and equipment, while community fully depends on fishing, there is currently no available fishing gear to carry out this activity, community struggling to make their needs fit within little available resources has resulted in to destitution and malnutrition among young children and elderly persons among these communities, every efforts being made to stabilize the situation has never been successful since the community lack basic tools and seeds leading to a very severe food insecurity. This project contributes to cluster objective 2 and 3 which is set to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support through agricultural seeds and fishing inputs, and to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. The project aims at improving food security by increasing capacities of the affected people through training on best modern farming practices besides, increase kitchen gardens distribution of improved quick maturing vegetable crops alongside hand tools in both in the five target counties. During the project 134,517 beneficiaries will be provided with assorted vegetable kits out of which 30,000 will benefit from capacity building and training, asset building and market support. Another 240,000 livestock will be vaccinated/treated while 30 nutrition facilities will benefit from kitchen gardens. During distribution CIDO will ensure that complain and feedback desk is made available for the beneficiaries, and a post distribution monitoring will be done after. The project implementation strategies will include: capacity development for sustainability and resilience; protection programming; Do No Harm, conflict sensitivity planning, and gender and youth mainstreaming and empowerment. This project will also be integrated with other CIDO cluster projects such as Nutrition and Shelter/NFIs, child protection, and health for joint missions and other projects that CIDO are undertaking in the same locations. The project will be guided by the following principles and in relation to AAP: leadership and empowerment of women and girls; women and girls engaged in all aspects of the space; safety and accessibility; integrated in community and contextually relevant; inclusivity - all women and girls consulted – especially marginalized; coordination for multi-sectorial support and services – and referral; outreach to communities to encourage women and girls to participate – and activities organized in communities; feedback mechanisms in place for women and girls – and for community; safety and ethically data collection and management; sustainability of FFS – including transition and exit strategy. During distribution CIDO will ensure that complain and feedback desk is made available for the beneficiaries, and a post distribution monitoring will be done after. 1 - Needs

FSNMS+ Round 27 Food Security Analysis February 2022 estimates 8.3 million (includes refugees) people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022 in South Sudan. This is due to covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks such as floods and dry spells, high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence. Another driver is household level idiosyncratic stressors such as death of bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services.According to Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2022, a continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country, including Jonglei (Akobo), and Western Equatoria (notably Tambura), will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022. In South Sudan, where 95 per cent of livelihoods depend on traditional rain-fed agriculture, crop farming, pastoralism and animal husbandry, climate shocks are likely to lead to an increase in livestock mortality and a decline in the amount and viability of land farmers can cultivate. This will also lead to traditional seasonal migration routes of pastoralists to be disrupted and less land will be available for cattle to graze, which may further increase the risk of conflict in affected areas. The primary challenge driving humanitarian needs in South Sudan is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The other main challenge is lack of access to WASH services which increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. The counties of most concern are in the states of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity and Warrap (specifically Greater Tonj), with the most extreme case being Fangak County in Jonglei State, where the situation is comparable to Pibor County in 2021. Food consumption gaps are extreme, and people have largely exhausted their emergency coping strategies. According to OCHA South Sudan, Flash Update No. 1 as of 14 September 2021, close to 80,000 people have been displaced by fighting in Tambura County since June, with reports of civilians killed and wounded. Many of the people displaced have fled to neighboring Western Bahr el Ghazal State. An estimated 45,000 people have fled to Ezo County, with thousands of others moving to Yambio Town, Nagero, Namutina, Wau, Nzara and Mosso. Some of the key findings included their urgent need for food and shelter. Their homes were burnt down by the armed groups and livelihoods destroyed. Some people were noted to have adopted negative coping strategies, including restricting food consumption, and eating treated seeds that were initially distributed for planting. The nutrition status of children, pregnant and lactating mothers is likely to deteriorate unless emergency food assistance is provided to the displaced communities. Some 3,000 people from Tambura County were displaced to Ezo County in Western Equatoria according to OCHA South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot of January 2022. OCHA South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot of January 2022 indicates that armed conflict displaced civilians in different parts of the country. Fighting between armed factions in Lainya County, Central Equatoria, displaced approximately 13,300 people. Currently ,CIDO has physical presence in the Akobo, Yei, Lainya Tambura and Ezo implementing integrated GBV and child protection projects with fully equipped offices and 87 staff.

2 - Project activities

CIDO will intervene in cluster objectives 2 and 3 with the following activities: CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines); Activities: • Emergency food production: main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits; • Nutrition vouchers (plus poultry & shoat distribution; Seed fairs;• Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits/ vegetable kits; rapid response kits (fish kit/ cow pea & three variety of fast maturing vegetable seed)• Livestock treatment and vaccination; livestock disease surveillance, Community Animal Health Workers Training and inputs support, cold chain establishment and maintenance, prevention & treatment especially zoonosis e.g. RVF; • Livelihood support to returnee households• Provision of timely food security information CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines); Activities: • Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement for FAO ITFs & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance; • Support value chain addition e.g. through agriculture, fishery and livestock products/ by-products preservation & marketing• Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation (business skills), vocational training etc. • Multi sectoral models: livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation• Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW & DL surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication• Reduction in postharvest losses and introduction of appropriate preservation technologies (e.g. hermetic storage bags)• Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes & micro finance etc.• Use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves • Training HH on proper safe management of food supply chain• Trainings and interventions to promote market access• Training natural resource management• Enable conflict related meetings to strengthen local peace structures• Setting up and training community and county-level disaster risk reduction committees to lead the development of hazard information tools • Implementing local climate-sensitive agricultural and natural resource and disaster risk reduction strategies

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed toensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper CommunityCommunication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by theAccountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through theproject implementation. 8.11. Mitigation Measures.

CIDO will ensure all staff are trained on data management and security. Themanagement will provide lockable safe boxes in all field locations for keepingconfidential files and encourage the use of passwords to protect soft copies of data.CIDO will conduct rapid HLP assessments with focus on the immediate shelter andprotection needs of affected people and the potential risks they face, including theiraccess to different shelter types, security of tenure, including risk of forced eviction.Assessments at this stage may also be able to get an overview of the level ofdamaged/destroyed HLP and any (secondary) occupation trends. This will defineways of engaging the local authority and raising awareness on HLP rights amongcrisis-affected populations, including the services available to support claiming suchrights (e.g., legal assistance, dispute resolution and shelter assistance) and local rulesand regulations on HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements). Community engagement and communication including with the local leadership in allaspects of the project will mitigate external interference likely to spark conflict. CIDOwill adhere to principals of protection and the Do no Harm policy while engaging withall stakeholders .CIDO will work closely with county security organs and local authorityto prioritize safety of staff, lives and property of community members through localdialogues and peaceful negotiations. To promote beneficiary access to CRFMmechanisms, CIDO Project team will use Community Communication andEngagement (CCE) strategies while being guided by the Accountability to the AffectedPopulation (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will put inplace AAP committees in all project locations to monitor and promote rights ofbeneficiaries as far as access to quality services is concerned. To mitigate the risk ofexplosive hazards in the project locations, CIDO will coordinate with Mine Actionpartners through sharing data in subnational and national cluster meetings and usetheir expert recommendations to advise on safety of both beneficiaries and staff.
