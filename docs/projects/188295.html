Project Summary

The decade-long conflict with protracted hostilities has left many 4.9m PLW &children in need of life-saving nutrition intervention, of whom %80 is in acute need. The prevalence of malnutrition is a significant public health challenge in Syria. The main groups affected by malnutrition are infants, young children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The different forms of malnutrition include wasting, that is, severe acute malnutrition (SAM) moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) as well as chronic malnutrition or stunting (low height for age) and micronutrient deficiency especially iron-deficiency anaemia which occurs in young children, PLW but in girls of reproductive age. Chronic malnutrition continues to be one of the humanitarian concerns, which is partially resulting from poor nutrition practices among HHs. Every 1 woman out of 4 is not able to optimally feed their infants in Syria. The IDP HHs in NWS show nutrition conditions where almost 60% of infants do not receive diets of acceptable quantity &diversity(HNO,2022). WHH along with its implementing partner aims at preventing malnutrition by raising awareness levels on nutritional matters among women & caregivers by providing awareness-raising sessions and IYCF counselling, and at providing supplementary micronutrient supplementation to PLW and children U5 to accelerate anaemia reduction. Per awareness-raising component, ToT sessions will be delivered to 20 women in A’zaz, 20 in Afrin by WHH where they will work as nutrition mobilizers and conduct trainings in their camps with aim of multiplying the awareness levels in their communities. An allowance worth 90$ will be provided to women for conducting their own sessions for 2 months. Further, the Partner will conduct awareness-raising & counselling in/off camps through its mobile teams by visiting door by door where they will also share information with women, men &caregivers in Idleb. Promotion of optimal maternal, infant, and young child nutrition topics will be basically covered. As part of 2nd component, in the cases of referrals with malnutrition in certain locations, supplementary food assistance will be provided to the targeted HHs through self & partner implementation. Cash Voucher Assistance for vulnerable PLWs and children under 5 will be provided through this activity. - Nutrition sector monitoring will involve standard nutrition outcome indicators including GAM, SAM, MAM and IYCF. Routine data will continue to be monitored using the revised 4Ws tool to measure progress towards planned sector targets, severity scales and PiN. Through 4Ws reporting, the coordination among nutrition sector partners coordination shall be assured for timely, comprehensive and accurate reporting for routine evidence based nutrition programing.

4 - Needs

In 2021, 85 subdistricts across Syria had 25% of children suffering from stunting (severity 3 classification). In 44 subdistricts there were 46% children 6-59 months with iron-deficiency anaemia (severity classification 4) this was coupled with poor infant and young child (IYCF) practices including low minimum acceptable diet (MAD) in children 6-23 months for example (NWS Smart Survey, 2021, Damascus SMART Survey, 2019 and whole of Syria (WOS) Surveillance data, 2021) Furthermore, according to the SMART Survey (NWS, July 2021) the trend in malnutrition has been rising in recent years. In Aleppo, for instance in sub-districts such as, Bulbul, Raju, Sharan, Jandairis, Ma’btali we can see examples of communities with high severity ranking in stunting (children 6-59 months) where the rate was 25% (that is, in severity rank 3). This is coupled with high iron-deficiency anaemia rates reaching 46% in children 6-59 months, 54% in PLW (that is in severity rank 5) (SMART Survey NWS, 2021). In the case of the WOS there were increased rates across malnutrition parameters since 2020; stunting (increased by 4%), SAM cases (increased by 29%), MAM cases (increased by 44.7%) and PLW with MAM (increased by 144%) (Damascus SMART Survey, 2019 and Surveillance data, 2021).

5 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will be partly direct implementation by WHH and partly with WHH’s implementing partner. Per WHH’s activities, 20 women in A’zaz and 20 in Afrin will be selected to receive ToT to become a mobilizer. Mobilizers will be selected based on the criteria and in coordination with camp management. Mobilizers will train women in their camps and receive allowances worth of 90$ for conducting sessions per month. MEAL staff and field staff will monitor the activity and report to project team on daily basis. CRM systems are in place and well-know in the community targeted. There will be a lessons learned session facilitated by the trainer. WHH will be reporting 4Ws to the cluster.As per partner activities, the partner organization he project component is a pilot of nutrition specific activities like Promotion of optimal maternal, infant, and young child nutrition, Cash Voucher Assistance for vulnerable. As part of the supplementary food assistance for those in need, the Partner will identify the people who are in need of the assistance either through their activities funded by other sources or referrals they received. Following the verification of beneficiaries, the assistance will be provided to them. The partner will do the 4Ws reporting and WHH will check, as well. WHH has monitors in the field who will be monitoring and reporting back to project staff in Gaziantep. The partner has its own monitors, as well.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches offundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuseof those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values.It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassmentof our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, andtaking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UNsystem, donors, and civil society organizations.Strategy covers both SEA and SH,bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. WHH is a member of PSEA Network, and train its staff on a regular basis. Additionally, both PSEA and WHH posters are in visible activity sites and explained to beneficiaries and informed about how to report the cases. WHH also produced video to inform people about the PSEA and how to report them. There is team trained on investigation, as well.
