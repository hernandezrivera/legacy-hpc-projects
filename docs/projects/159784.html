Project Summary

This project implemented by Action Against Hunger (AAH) will support vulnerable families in the Gaza Strip (GS) (including women, men, children (boys and girls), elderly) who suffer from a shortage of domestic water and are prone to stormwater/wastewater floods during emergencies to ensure their safe access to the basic WASH services. The aim of this project is to reduce the negative impacts of public health risks associated with poor water quality, poor wastewater collection and treatment, lack of stormwater infrastructure and lack of proper hygiene practices. Interventions (meeting the Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3 and WASH Cluster Strategic Objective 1) will focus on sustainable domestic water supply, basic sanitation and proper winterization preparedness to mitigate the harsh winter weather and flooding. The recurrent flooding poses a threat of contamination of sources of water and of overflowing of the sewage systems, increasing the risk of waterborne disease.
Under the proposed intervention, Action Against Hunger (AAH) will support the targeted communities with emergency preparedness service contract (operating) for conducting critical emergency repairs for WASH facilities and will carry out electrotechnical repairs for critical WASH facilities prone to stormwater floods.
Public awareness campaigns will be organised to address hygiene practice issues at the household level to improve the capacity the families in the targeted locations to effectively respond to emergencies.
AAH will advocate to support the service provider to overcome operation and maintenance obstacles (lack of financial resources, lack of spare parts and shortage of fuel of emergency uses).
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and is taking into account safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability as well as participation (through regular meetings, focus group discussions and workshops throughout the project) and empowerment.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO-2020, Gaza people suffer from regular interruptions of access to water and sanitation (due to the electricity power crisis); recent damage to water infrastructure; import restrictions on materials; and decreased funding from donors. The Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU) as the main public service provider for WASH service in Gaza strip is facing a financial deficit of $13M and is unable to provide services or conduct the operation and maintenance of the 484 water and wastewater facilities in the GS due to decrease in international donors’ financial support, lack of material and spare parts available to conduct preventive and/or emergency repairs, and decreased quantity of fuel to operate the standby generators. This is likely to cause severe lack of domestic water at household and public institution levels, floods from both stormwater and wastewater leading to serious public health hazards affecting the Gazan population, as well as have negative impacts on the environment. CMWU operations also suffer from the failure of water wells (13 wells according to CMWU feedback in Oct-2019), reduction of operating hours of wells and wastewater pumping stations due to frequent interruptions of power supply and lack of fuel to operate the standby generators. As a result, domestic water consumption in some places has been reduced below 61 l/c/d compared to WHO guidelines of 100 l/c/d, and the untreated sewage flowing directly to the sea causes pollution to the environment and health risks to the population. In September 2019, AAH conducted a community rapid assessment through FGD in the targeted areas and found the following:•89% of HH members suffer from water borne diseases including women and children under 5 years old in addition to PWDs. •23% of HHs were forced to spend two days in the nearby schools due to stormwater floods inside their houses during the 2018 winter. •95% of HHs have sanitation facilities with poor hygiene and unhealthy conditions.In July 2019, CMWU circulated the list of critical facilities needing urgent maintenance and repair works, especially in preparation of the winter season. In September 2019, AAH conducted a field technical assessment and validation of the status of 77 WASH facilities (35 wastewater pump stations (WWPS), 40 water wells and 2 stormwater facilities), serving around 1.18 million of Gaza inhabitants. The main findings included:•30 WWPS are located in flood prone zones without emergency overflow. This is matching with the AAH’s flood risks assessment (December 2018);•35 WWPS and their generators are in need of major electro-mechanical repairs works;•40 wells are connected to community electric grids and operate for an average of 14 hrs/day. 13 wells are out of operation and require intensive maintenance works to resume operation.•85% of spare parts used for maintenance and repairs works are missing from CMWU’s store. •48,378 liters/month of fuel are needed to run generators during emergencies and power cut off; currently no quantities are available in the storage tanks.As a result, AAH intends to support the affected communities to maintain their access to basic WASH services and protect them from public health hazards and from stormwater/wastewater floods by carrying out emergency repair works in high priority WASH facilities.AAH will target 15 WWPS prone to stormwater floods and 12 unoperated wells, in coordination with the service providers (CMWU and municipalities). Finally, AAH will organise HH hygiene awareness activities in addition to distributing hygiene kits to ensure that specific needs from all family members; women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities and elderly are considered.AAH is an active member of the WASH cluster as well as other working groups (Water Quality, HPG, GWG, WASH in Health WG…etc.) and has extensive experience in supporting vulnerable communities and engaging with them effectively on WASH initiatives.

2 - Activities

This intervention is an immediate response to ensure that women, men, boys and girls have safe and adequate access to community water and sanitation facilities and are protected from natural or man-made hazard crises. The following activities are proposed to ensure coverage of all targeted families, prioritising safety and dignity and taking into account gender, age and diversity: Activity 1: Support the targeted communities to secure uninterrupted WASH services and protect families from potential flood risks during winter season, by conducting critical electromechanical repairs and maintenance works required for the 15 wastewater pumping stations and 12 wells, that are prone to stormwater floods (based on the recent assessments for critical facilities and AAH’s floods zones map). This action will diminish the risk of floods and will protect the communities from suffering damage in their houses, having to be displaced if their houses get flooded, lose their livelihoods or miss days out of school. Works will be carried out in coordination and cooperation with the service providers (CMWU and municipalities) to ensure full compliance with the operation and maintenance strategies and activities. Activity 2: Strengthen the capacity of the service providers (CMWU and municipalities) by preparing a service contract to carry out an emergency electromechanical repairs (on pumps, bar screen, fittings and electric distribution boards rehabilitation, and generators to overcome power cut off) to ensure the operation of WASH facilities and avoid shortage of domestic water or wastewater floods from wastewater pump stations. Activity 3: Local partners with support from AAH, will conduct hygiene awareness sessions for 700 households (live beside or nearby the targeted facilities and will be selected in coordination with the community leaders in each area) through home visits, trainings, IEC material and distribution of hygiene kits in which all family members: women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities and elderly’s specific needs are considered. These hygiene promotion activities are to enhance safe hygiene and water management practices (personal hygiene, hand washing especially, and safe water handling).Activity 4: Design and production of material to support the service provider to raise crucial funds needed for operation and maintenance, replacing of spare parts and provision of fuel for emergency use.-2 factsheets presenting service provider gaps and needs (to raise crucial funds needed for operation and maintenance, replacing of spare parts and provision of fuel for emergency use) to be distributed at presentations and briefings/meetings-Presentation of findings and needs at WASH Cluster-Needs addressed in at least 4 meetings with diplomats/ international donors.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm. Overall the intervention aims to mitigate the impacts of the conflict on water and infrastructure. The proposed intervention addresses safety and dignity concerns. Increasing access to WASH access as well as preventing and mitigating floods is core in the intervention thereby focusing on increasing safety and dignity of vulnerable populations. In terms of dignity, the activities contribute to the access of safe and good quality domestic water as well as preventing the flooding of houses several times a year whereby the women in the household normally spends a lot of time on removing the mud and on cleaning. People’s overall health status is foreseen to increase due to the increases access and household equipment as well as furniture remains in a better and healthy state. In term of dignity and health safety, the interventions also aim to prevent all flood related risks such as skin and waterborne diseases which sometimes cause stigmatization. Also, safer access for instance for children to go to school and for people with mobility impairments is facilitated. For now, people are not leaving the house or need to be evacuated through undignified manners. Children are often kept from going to school at the time of flooding as it’s impossible or too dangerous to leave from the house. The HHs in the targeted locations will be provided with hygiene awareness sessions on safe hygiene and water handling to ensure safe practices among their family members.The strong community involvement and feedback mechanism in the project will prevent harm in the first place and will allow project management to quickly react in case of concerns coming from beneficiaries and the wider community throughout the project duration. As part of its do no harm approach, AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuseDuring the verification and final selection of the pre-identified target facilities, all project information will be shared with the target communities to inform about the provided assistance and services. The communications between AAH and communities will empower affected populations to request information, raise concerns and provide feedback related to provided services. During the implementation phase, the targeted communities will be provided with clear and effective information on complaint and feedback mechanisms available throughout the project (Distribution of little cards with different complaint mechanisms (phone, email, address) written in Arabic and English and explained orally).In addition to safety and dignity, AAH considers meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. As a specific protection mainstreaming component AAH is in the process of developing its own protection mainstreaming toolkit and to start working with its own sector specific gender and protection mainstreaming checklists. Up to date AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection Mainstreaming Tip Sheets.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. This project will ensure sustainable operations of the targeted WASH facilities to guarantee safe access to good quality water, safe and adapted sanitation facilities and will contribute to reducing public health related risks related to poor hygiene conditions and stormwater floods within the targeted areas. The target communities in this project live among the most vulnerable and marginalized areas who suffer from the lack of employment opportunities, lack of access to basic services, (lack of access to domestic/drinking water, electricity cuts) and are prone to stormwater floods. The intervention concerns public facilities (managed by CWMU and municipalities) thus making sure we reach equally and impartially to the population needsWith regard to meaningful access, the intervention is focused on supporting service deliveries of public services. The target of the intervention is in urban areas where most people are connected to the water system. The interventions aim to increase the domestic water availability from below the WHO guidelines to the standard of 100 liters per day in accordance with WHO standards. As the intervention supports public service delivery, the intervention contributes to equal and impartial access to the affected population. The flood prevention impact increases both direct and indirect mobility access for all people including for people with specific vulnerabilities. The intervention will ensure access for people to go to their work, to go to school and it will facilitate mobility for people with impairments who are normally the first and have to suffer most severely from the flooding. In a more indirect way access through the area where the intervention will take place benefits the whole of Gaza as movement will not get even more restricted because of flooding. In light of participation and empowerment, the needs on which the activity are based are discussed with community representatives and relevant stakeholders following which the intervention is designed together with Action Against Hunger’s WASH specialists. Both women and men were involved in the assessment as well as in the design of the intervention through mixed focus group discussions.
