1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In 2021, FHI 360 commenced implementation of the BHA-funded IRAMP project in Ancuabe and Metuge Districts, providing integrated emergency health, protection, and WASH assistance. Through this modification, FHI 360 will continue its lifesaving assistance, while also expanding comprehensive support in the target areas by integrating emergency nutrition interventions—an area that has been identified through the Health and Nutrition Cluster, as well as consultation with other partners, as an area of deep need and significant gaps. FHI 360 will extend its assistance for an additional 11 months (making the total award period 21 months). The modification will build upon FHI 360’s well-established operational presence and platform, its strong existing team on the ground, and positive relationships with local authorities, health staff, and community members in its target areas. Additionally, this proposed modification will incorporate civil/birth registration activities into its Protection sector interventions, building upon the strong experience of FHI 360’s COVida project (also in Mozambique) and tailoring these activities specifically to support IDPs in seeking required documentation to access essential services/rights. • Number of People Targeted: 31,500 (8,500 male, 10,395 female)• Number of IDPs Targeted: 28,350 Beneficiaries are host community members and IDPs living with host families or resettlement sites in the target geographies.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

CP and GBV interventions will continue to center around five key strategies and services: case management, community-based protection mechanisms, SHLS, protecting excluded children and at-risk women/girls, and mainstreaming GBV and CP into WASH, nutrition, and health. These will be delivered through static safe space models and IMMUs to provide confidential CP and GBV case management and PSS. Case management will include emergency support to cover basic needs after physical assault/abuse, serious injury, or rape (e.g., food, water, clothes, ambulance, or other referral transport) and will be complemented by referrals to health clinics for CMR. FHI 360 safe spaces will offer PSS activities, provide information to women/girls, assist them to access lifesaving services, rebuild social networks, and scale up livelihood opportunities through vocational training and informal education. Target areas- Ancuabe and Metuge Beneficiaries are host community members and IDPs living with host families or resettlement sites in the target geographies.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Protection activities will seek to prevent unintended negative effects of case management such as exposure of the client to further forms of violence by maintaining confidentiality. Clients may also experience psychological risks such as flashbacks or post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) due to their experiences. As part of the referral pathway, FHI 360 will be prepared to refer and support access to MHPSS. FHI 360 will coordinate with IOM, and MHPSS TWG to establish the MHPSS referral pathway.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FHI 360 will continue to ensure that the Protection team conducts an internal Gender Sensitivity Analysis with participation from all IRAMP staff from each sector, and staff familiar with the different cultural contexts, nuances, and gender dynamics of each proposed operating location. As the protection activities will target re-settlement, transit sites/camps, and host communities with a potentially high influx of IDPs, the protection team will specifically address GBV Case Management vulnerabilities and IDP women's and girls’ needs. The team will discuss how Case Management services may be perceived by family members, especially male family members, and what the repercussions could be for clients as well as mitigation strategies. FHI 360 recognizes that confidentiality is necessary to deliver these services and will ensure that services are conducted discreetly and confidentially

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

As part of the case management process, FHI 360 will identify clients with accessibility issues and address issues in their case management plan. FHI 360 will arrange for their access to assistance and services. Training will be conducted for staff from all sectors to ensure the internal referral is strengthened, which will allow the non-protection sectors to safely refer all clients including those with accessibility issues to access GBV services and conduct counter referral after providing services while maintaining their confidentiality. As member of DWG, FHI360 will refer program participants with special needs to partner organizations that provide specialized services.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

FHI 360 highly considered the environmental impact of the project to ensure all activities possessed either no or very minimal negative environmental impact by prioritizing the sustainability and ability of communities to adapt and build upon the introduced services. As such, FHI 360 incorporated the following environmentally conscious activities: designed activities to mitigate spillover of infectious diseases and conducted environmental assessments before handing out dignity kits.
