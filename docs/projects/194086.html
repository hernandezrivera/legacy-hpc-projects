Project Summary

Previously, and until 2020, there had been no nationwide MSNA conducted in Sudan. Primary data collection has historically been challenging, limited in scope, and often focused in areas where there is a high amount of humanitarian presence. Coinciding with the changes in Sudan’s political landscape and needs and gaps identified by the humanitarian community, the first nationwide MSNA in Sudan was led by OCHA and IOM and supported by REACH Initiative. It provided updated information and analysis on the country’s humanitarian situation to feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and to inform response planning under the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). In 2022, the nationwide assessment was completed for the third consecutive year under IOM implementation. Its results were one of the main sources of inter-cluster primary data and were used to complement data from inter-cluster and cluster-specific assessments.
The MSNA is the central component of providing a sound evidence base for the HRP and is coordinated to support cross-sectoral strategic decision making in Sudan. The specific needs and human rights concerns of IDPs and other vulnerable groups do not automatically disappear when a conflict or natural disaster ends. Nor do they fade away when people initially find safety from ongoing conflict or disaster. Rather, the displaced, whether they return to their homes, settle elsewhere in the country, or try to integrate locally, usually face continuing problems, requiring support until they achieve a durable solution to their displacement. Given the volatile security situation in Sudan, humanitarian actors face serious challenges in collecting data in a systematic and comprehensive way, often resulting in significant gaps in the information required to design, plan, and evaluate their programming.
Considering that the MSNA is implemented across all 18 states and covers remote and hard to reach locations, it is critical for addressing urgent information gaps on priority humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities outside of areas where humanitarian partners are currently operating, to provide more robust and evidence-based analysis of the needs of affected populations. With the current MSNA’s geographical scope and the methodology, this assessment serves as a perfect tool for assessing cross-sectoral needs of populations across Sudan.
Since 15 April 2023, military conflict has erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) across Sudan. The fighting has been centralised in Khartoum, and has also broken out across the Darfur region (including cities such as Ag Geneina, El Fasher, and Nyala), as well as in the North (Merowe) and Kordofan (El Obeid). The conflict has led to rapidly increasing civilian vulnerability, significant losses and casualties across Sudan, the destruction of key infrastructure in affected areas (such as hospitals, warehouses, and markets), the immediate suspension of humanitarian activities, the widespread looting of UN facilities, and the evacuation of international staff and foreign nationals. Despite the limitations to DTM's logistical operations (given the deteriorating security and economic environments) and telecommunication challenges, DTM has been able to maintain key data collection operations, and has provided the UN community with preliminary estimates of displacement and return. These estimates provide baseline information for inter-agency coordination on future humanitarian intervention and support DTM's partners to identify hotspots of vulnerability where later data collection activities related to the MSNA can be targeted. Given the context presented by this military conflict, DTM is best placed to provide evidence-based information about the humanitarian context which other organisations rely on for their programming. DTM forms a core component of IOM’s response to the unfolding humanitarian crisis, among IOM’s other PC1 programming.

1 - Needs

Two years after Sudan embarked on the path of political transition and one year into the Juba Peace Agreement (JPA), humanitarian needs continue to grow across the country, despite progress and gains on the road to a new Sudan. These needs are driven by an economic crisis, exacerbated by disease outbreaks, protracted internal displacement that is yet to find durable solutions, increased insecurity, and localized violence in parts of the country, coupled with floods, and more than 1.16 million refugees and asylum seekers hosted by Sudan. With multiple crises already occurring across the country, Sudan has witnessed a deterioration in humanitarian access which may be further exacerbated by the current political situation and ongoing War. Conflicts between armed groups had already increased at the end of 2021 and continued in 2022 increasing protection concerns for the population, this notably includes the increase of violence in North, South, and West Darfur, as well as armed opposition groups controlling areas in the Blue Nile and South Kordofan states, creating barriers to the delivery of humanitarian aid. In the last quarter of 2021, IOM registered 258,308 newly displaced persons with the observation of continuous increasing trends of displacement as a result of the challenging political and security context in Sudan, as well as the deteriorating situation in the Darfur states at the end of 2021. By December 2021, the total internally displaced persons (IDP) caseload was already twice as high as the overall 2020 IDP caseload captured due to conflict-related displacement. By October 2022, IOM had registered 364,225 newly displaced persons due to conflicts and natural hazards. The military conflict between the SAF and RSF commencing on 15 April 2023 has also had wide-reaching implications for DTM’s operational context. Initially about 15.8 million people needed humanitarian assistance in 2023, but due to this conflict, there has been a 57 per cent increase in the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance, as a result an estimated 24.7 million people will be significantly affected in 2023). This has in turn raised considerations which are reflected in our revisions to the budget for this project. Firstly, the new context will create a need for a tighter and more robust security monitoring of data collection operations. Secondly, DTM anticipates that the negative externalities of the conflict on the domestic economy will be profound. Damage to infrastructure and the looting of local businesses will reduce the pool of services available relating to the procurement of transportation. Field teams report dramatic increases in the price of fuel and consumable goods, as well as severe disruptions to internet connectivity and telecommunications services. These factors will also compound on the cost-of-living, which will in return place inflationary pressures on enumerator salaries. Thirdly, DTM anticipates that the displacement caseload is set to continue rising (increasing to approximately 1.8 million IDPs estimated displaced within 6 months of 15 April 2023 - notwithstanding changes in context - as per DTM’s internal projections). These projected new needs and assessments will also require additional operational contingencies (such as expanding key informant networks).

2 - Activities/Outputs

Overall Project Objective: To conduct multi-sectoral needs assessment at the state level across all 18 states of Sudan, providing a robust evidence-based analysis of the severity of needs country-wide to feed into the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and to inform planning in the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Main Output: Updated analysis and information on Sudan’s humanitarian situation to feed into the HNO 2024 and evidence-based response planning to address urgent information gaps on humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities, thereby generating a solid information for a multi-sectoral humanitarian response. Sectors to be covered, but are not limited to Protection, Gender-based violence (GBV), Mine action, Health, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Emergency Shelters and Non-Food Items (ES/NFI), Nutrition, and Accountability to Affected Population (AAP).

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM's DTM activities are conducted in strict adherence to data protection principles and international best practices. The exercises draw on IOM's global expertise in conducting similar activities to ensure that information is non-identifiable, stored securely and only accessible, by request, by approved partners. Furthermore, DTM is committed to collecting information on main age and sex breakdown per location of assessment, which is the foundation to protection programming, providing insight to the demographic and specific gender nuance for protection focused programming.

7.1.1 - Please specify other means to collect/share feedback or information if there is any
7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

IOM provides regular PSEA training for all IOM staff, including those who will be carrying out the activities under this project. The DTM teams support communities and their representatives by holding discussion sessions so that beneficiaries could ask questions or raise issues of concern with the team directly or through the complaint desks, as well as an email complaint mechanism. DTM teams utilize community feedback to enhance data collection tools by adding relevant indicators to refine specific activities. During each exercise priority will be given to the most vulnerable people (such as the disabled, elderly, pregnant and / or lactating women, female headed households) to ensure that they are registered and verified without delay. Additionally, throughout all activities, DTM team secures gender-responsive data collection, by establishing gender mixed field teams, collecting sex and age disaggregated data, and capturing other forms of social difference (wealth status, ethnicity, etc.). IOM will also provide water, shade, and sanitation facilities to ensure that the beneficiaries are comfortable while the exercises are ongoing. DTM teams will implement safety and crowd control measures as needed during data collection activities. Data so far has only been used for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. However, the data can also be used for longer term planning and as a baseline for in-depth studies that monitor the progressive resolution of displacement situations.
