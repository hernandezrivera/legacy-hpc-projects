Project Summary

Narrative Hub, a women-led organization, will provide life-saving WASH activities aimed at meeting life-threatening, severe needs of the population of host communities, IDPs and returnees. This project will be implemented in Fashoda and Twic counties which was affected by conflict, floods, high malnutrition rates, disease outbreaks and GBV threats. NH will have a static presence in both locations with flexibility to address needs across the whole counties, as part of crisis modifier strategy. This project aims to support the provision of access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations, increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases and aims to reduce gender-based violence-related issues. Since women and girls are the primary, direct users of WASH services, the project will contribute to GBV mitigation among the targeted population. This project will implement life-saving integrated WASH and GBV activities, targeting 11,111 individual beneficiaries suffering from inadequate access to safe drinking water, inadequate sanitation and poor hygiene conditions.
The interventions will include but not limited to:
• Provision or rehabilitation of sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design;
• Safe water provision through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children;
• Hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns);
• Capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs);
• Distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community;
The activities will include a detailed multi-sector needs assessment to identify the needs, affected gender, age and diversity groups, the vulnerabilities of the targeted community and coping strategies of the affected population. The main priority is to provide emergency WASH necessary to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by WASH-related diseases, contributing to malnutrition and limited access to water. The project design aims to integrate with other projects in other sectors for a holistic response. This will include skills training and resilience building. The project aims at centralizing protection to promote principled access to basic services to vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected population by ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive and responsive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, including people living with disabilities, etc. With the involvement of our M&E department, the indicators will track cooperation with communities through an existing beneficiary feedback mechanism, to address GBV and protection threats in WASH.

1. Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022), the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. The report notes that covariate climatic, conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, such as poor water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. People’s access to safe and improved water and sanitation is low in South Sudan. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Lack of latrines and/or overfull latrines lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and lack of accessibility to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions, with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrhea diseases, cholera and Hepatitis E. According to REACH Monitoring Report of September 2023, high reported levels of food insecurity combined with high disease prevalence, limited access to clean water and functional hygiene services. This is evidenced by SMART surveys conducted in April 2023 by Action Against Hunger in Fashoda Counties respectively. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) rate of children in Fashoda was estimated at 31%, above the ‘Extremely Critical’ threshold. Reported latrine usage remained low but stable throughout the reporting period, in 49% of assessed settlements across Upper Nile State no people were reportedly using latrines in September (53%). In Warrap States, reporting was particularly high in assessed settlements in Twic county (71%). The primary reported reasons for people not using latrines were that there were none available (35% of assessed settlements), that latrines were overcrowded (24%), and for cultural reasons (12%). Boreholes were reported as the main source of drinking water in 70% of assessed settlements in September, consistent with findings from March. However, as in March, there were reportedly no functional boreholes in any assessed settlements in Twic County in September, which likely led to a continued reliance on rivers (71% of assessed settlements), and swamps (21%) as the main sources of drinking water. A reported presence of boreholes did not guarantee widespread access to clean water. Furthermore, despite it being reported in 92% of assessed settlements across Warrap State that most people were aware of how to protect themselves from the spread of communicable diseases, most people reportedly washed their hands with soap in just 25% of assessed settlements in September 2023, unchanged between reporting periods, which is likely a consequence of access barriers to clean water.

2. Project activities

Narrative Hub will provide the following activities: • Provide or rehabilitate sanitation infrastructure (latrines, hand washing facilities and solid waste management) will be supported at the institutional level and IDP sites. At the HH level the latrine construction with local materials will be encouraged with GBV prevention aspect taken into consideration for latrine design; • Provide safe water through borehole construction and rehabilitation, ensuring access to water for the affected communities, where safe access to water points mitigates risks for girls, women and children; • Conduct hygiene promotion (handwashing campaigns in communities and institutions, safe water chain, safe solid waste disposal and jerry-can cleaning campaigns); • Provide capacity-building of community institutions (pump mechanics, water user committees, community hygiene promoters, nutrition workers, school's hygiene clubs); • Conduct distribution of WASH NFIs (soap, hygiene kits, menstrual hygiene kit, water containers, PUR or others) for the most vulnerable people in the community; • Provide training to hygiene promoters; • Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations; • Create awareness on appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases; • Enabling of vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate; • Provide gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers; • Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis; • Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention interventions; • Provide safe drinking water and nutritional centres (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM; • Provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC down to the household level; • Ensuring small stock water requirement and promote appropriate hygiene practices to prevent small stock open defecation at household level WASH in Health for outbreak preparedness, response and risk reduction; • Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community; • Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level. • Provide community engagement (AAP) sessions, complains and feedback mechanisms aimed at enhancing accountability; and •Participate in coordination and assessments at county, state and national level.
