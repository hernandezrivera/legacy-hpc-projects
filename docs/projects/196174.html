Project Summary

HNO 2023 estimates that 15.3 million people need help (47% of them children), with the highest proportion in Aleppo at 2.8 million and in rural Damascus at 2.6 million. Human and economic indicators continue to deteriorate, and basic services are collapsing. The deteriorating situation is exacerbated by the ongoing outbreak of cholera and other epidemics and basic services such as electricity, combustion, fuel and other infrastructure are near collapse, including schools, which have suffered destruction and lack of basic equipment, such as electricity, mattresses, and educational means, this affected negatively the educational process and increased children's dropout rate. The HNO 2023 report indicates that more than 2 million children in Syria are out of school during 2023.
In this project, which will be implemented within a full year, it will contribute to the humanitarian response plan within the education and protection sector. SSSD will target the most dangerous areas on the scale of the 4-severity scale. As the education and protection sector reports indicate, 4 selected schools will be rehabilitated in coordination with the MoE and planning directorates in the governorates of Aleppo and Damascus. (1 school in Meskanah, 1 school in Al Bab, and 2 schools in Jaramana) Schools will be lightly rehabilitated, water and sewage systems will be maintained, schools will be provided with a solar power system and 90 rows will be provided with tables, seats, as well as boards, and teachers' supplies, where more than 5,000 students will benefit.
Back-to-school campaigns will also be carried out in Meskanah, Al Bab and Jaramana areas targeting 4,000 parents, children and key persons in the community. Compensatory education courses for 1,800 of the most vulnerable children will be implemented also in the four rehabilitated schools in addition to help 200 dropout children in order to return back to school and compensating their lost education. Classes will be opened for 200 children within the ECE early childhood education programme and school bags, notebooks and stationery will be distributed to 5,000 students in the four schools. Training courses will also be conducted for 80 teachers (males and females' teachers) in these schools, providing them with the needed tools to carry out their lessons. Psychosocial support sessions will also be held for them. Health awareness and awareness sessions on the dangers of explosive remnants and life skills sessions will be conducted for 5,000 students and caregivers.
Building capacity of humanitarian and local key actors will also be made to improve the quality of presented protection services delivery within the protection sector by training 60 people on protection principles and 40 people on CP and GBV principles. Psychosocial support sessions will be offered to 4,000 beneficiaries and awareness sessions on child protection and gender-based violence cases to 6,000 beneficiaries.
Within the project, 8 groups of protection committees for children and young people will be formed and will work to implement 16 community-based initiatives commensurate with their community's needs.
This project will focus on attracting children with disabilities (2% of the target) by facilitating their access to schools and rehabilitating schools to ensure the integration of children with disabilities. The project team will work to reach out to children of employment and girls at risk of gender-based violence.
The project is expected to contribute to alleviate the suffering of the population in the targeted areas, and will ensure that more than 5,000 children will have access to their right of education. It will also alleviate parents' financial burdens to secure education for their children and contribute to children's access to adequate protection and psychosocial support. It will also contribute to the restoration of those schools' infrastructure and human resources, which can last for so many years.

3 - Needs

The areas in this project were chosen based on HNO 2023 ( DEC 2022 ) and WOS PiN Severity 2023 ( NOV 2022 ) of the protection and education sectors, which indicate that 22.03% in Aleppo are exposed to protection risks (1.747.337 GBV Risks- 1.278.503 CP Risks) and individuals 15.02% in rural Damascus are exposed to protection risks (1.439.257 GBV Risks – 863.690 CP Risks).And based on the evaluation visits by the SSSD team in December 2022 in Rural Damascus (Jaramana) and Rural Aleppo (Maskaneh and Al Bab), in cooperation with the key people, the mayor, school principals, and based on the home visits by the mobile teams, the needs in the regions were similar to some extent (health, schools rehabilitation, psychosocial support...) Most of the population in these areas are IDPs, more than 39,000 IDPs in Maskana and 250,000 in Al Bab, and 300,000 in Jaramana, with various needs. Based on the mobile team's visit SSSD monitored 2,940 people with disabilities, 1,252 children at risk of child labor, 2,119 children who need educational support as a result of dropping out of school and returning to classes that are not commensurate with their educational level, 3,036 children who have dropped out of school, most of them due to employment, 1,323 girls in risk of gender-based violence. DoE was given the names of 4 schools that need internal rehabilitation works, and its students have been studying in a very bad condition, All of those schools are classified on a Severity 4 in 2023.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Physical risk due to the structural integrity of buildings or the presence of ERWs.Mitigating: Structural assessment by qualified personnel, work closely with authorities involved in ERW clearance to ensure schools are free from ERW, Orient contractors/workers on risks of ERW and ask them to report immediately if the suspicious object is found and suspend the work until the suspected object is removed safely. - A problem in the area in which rehabilitation is taking place due to the selection of specific schools for rehabilitationMitigating: Clarifying the reason for selecting specific schools, in addition to agreeing with the Directorate of Schools in the region and taking nominations from them and raising the school's other needs to obtain new funding for them, establishing complaint mechanisms, and training humanitarian staff. - Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community is not served. Lack of a sustainable protection intervention due to limited involvement of national stakeholders and targeted community members in child protection service delivery.Mitigating: Community participation in needs and assessment, and activation of their role to be influential and effective in society, supporting youth with training to be able to implement initiatives and monitor the needs of their regions.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SSSD seeks to avoid any cases of sexual exploitation and abuse within its scope of work, by conducting awareness sessions for beneficiaries as part of prevention, to raise awareness of those receiving aid of their rights and reporting mechanisms when exposed to abuse by service providers, to provide a safe environment and to provide all services free of charge.Also SSSD provides periodic training for employees and volunteers to raise awareness about PSEA within all centers communities and offices It is part of prevention.SSSD publishes posters containing reporting mechanisms and how to receive complaints in all its places of work, periodically develops reporting mechanisms to be comprehensive, available, and appropriate for all segments of society, does not cause harm, and guarantees confidentiality and Respect for submitters.Anyone within the scope of work of SSSD, including employees and volunteers of the Society, or its clients and beneficiaries of its services, can access safe and effective reporting mechanisms and submit reports on these cases, through the following mechanisms: ( hotline - WhatsApp - Facebook - email - Visit the association's headquarters or one of its offices or centers - SSSD website - Complaints received by the association's employees - Reports received from the M&E team).In the event of any complaint about a case of PSEA action is taken according to registration, classification and archiving, response and follow-up, and closure.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Delay in obtaining all approvalsMitigating: Prior coordination with the concerned authorities, and the existence of a memorandum of understanding with the MoE. - The difference in prices from the date of writing the project to the date of implementation.Mitigating: Flexibility in discussion throughout the project period and studying the economic situation of the market. - High transportation costs may be a burden for teachers coming from far areas.Mitigating: Provide teachers and education personnel with monetary incentives. - The caregivers did not agree to the children’s participation in the sessions.Mitigating: Carrying out sessions with caregivers to clarify the services that will be provided to children and implementing focused discussion sessions with children and caregivers to choose times for activities that suit all beneficiaries. - Not targeting all children in the area.Mitigating: Inviting children in the region to various activities and implementing some initiatives to target the largest number of children in the region. - Difficulty accessing people with disabilities.Mitigating: Provide the necessary facilities for persons with disabilities according to their type - Risk to the well-being of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma)Mitigating: Self-care strategies as part of programs and organizational HR policies, Establish peer support mechanisms.
