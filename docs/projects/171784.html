Project Summary

PAH will provide multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable conflict-affected older people (60+ years old), residing in Donetska Non-Government Controlled Area (NGCA) and Donetska and Luhanska Government Controlled Areas (GCAs).
In Donetska NGCA, PAH proposed program that is focused on covering the most acute needs of older people residing in Donetska NGCA through provision of food and hygiene assistance. In total, 5000 older people planned to be covered under the project. With support of Partner, the clients of the local Territorial Centers of Social Services, who are the most vulnerable elderly people living in project locations, will be assisted. Each person will receive three food kits that will cover their needs during three months’ period, ensuring their full nutrition requirements (2100 Kcal per day per person). Also, each older person will receive a three-month hygiene kit including hygiene promotion component. Additionally, 1000 older people with special needs will be provided with hygiene kits for people with special needs. The materials on mitigation of risks of COVID-19 transmission will be provided and presented to the beneficiaries during distributions. Taking into consideration the COVID-19 pandemic in Ukraine, the distribution will be based on COVID-19 Ukraine Assistance Delivery protocol.
In the areas along the “contact line” in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts in GCAs, PAH will run 12 Community Centers to provide psychosocial support services to older people, suffering from social isolation and psychological distress. PAH’s psychologists will provide group structured PSS, as well as individual structured PSS at home or remotely to those older people who have a complex psycho-emotional state, such as increased anxiety, depression and others. Psychologists will be also providing individual consultations. At the same time, PAH community workers will provide individual PSS sessions based on a simplified programme at home or remotely to older people, who have slight and moderate mobility, or slight and moderate problems with hearing, vision, speech, and attention, as well as home-based care services, including psychosocial support, to those beneficiaries who have severe mental and / or neurological problems and / or serious mobility problems, such as dementia, Alzheimer’s disease, effects of a stroke, etc. Primary legal assistance will be also provided to those older people who have no access to legal services, for example, to obtain benefits for caring for a person with disability, to restore passport, etc. Moreover, 300 assistive devices and 200 WASH NFIs will be provided to the most vulnerable older people. At the same time, 500 most vulnerable older people with disabilities will be provided with individual protection assistance in cash. Multipurpose cash assistance will be provided to 1250 the most vulnerable people to decrease the use of negative coping mechanisms by covering basic needs which in its turn will contribute to the improvement of their psychosocial well-being. Beneficiary group has access to local market, as essential goods and services are mostly accessible on the local markets along the “contact line” in Donetska and Luhanska GCA, although not always affordable for elder people due to high prices (for 60,6% of respondents) and problems with transportation (for 13,0% of respondents). All above mentioned activities will be implemented in line with COVID-19 restrictions and relevant recommendations from the Clusters on reducing the risk of transmission.
Combination of protection, multipurpose cash assistance, WASH and food security assistance shall reduce vulnerabilities of the conflict-affected older population living in Donetska and Luhanska oblasts through improved access to and provision of humanitarian assistance and improving their overall well-being.

1 - Needs

PAH conducted multisector needs assessment (NA) of older people residing in within 0-5 km along the “contact line” in 7 rayons of Donetska and Luhanska GCAs in January-February 2019. In total, 1047 older people were interviewed, where 77,5%-women, and 22,5%-men. 98% of all the respondents were 60+ years old. According to the NA, 73,6% of total women and 62,3% of total men have mobility restrictions, where 29,2% of both older men and women reported they are home-bound persons, with around 22% in need of assistive devices to overcome their mobility restrictions. Moreover, almost 72% of elder people are more likely to be insecure in case of escalation of the armed conflict in eastern Ukraine due to inability to go anywhere independently. 93,6% of surveyed have chronic diseases, 18,4%- disability. At the same time, 61% of older people are in need of PSS. More than 70% of the respondents are in need of external support. 58% of the respondents live alone, 40% have nobody else to live with. For 98% of the respondent’s pensions are the main source of income, where reported income per household during the last 30 days was equal to 3,264 UAH (116 USD). The rest primarily rely on aid from different agencies and income of family members. The main expenditures of interviewed are as follows: food –47,2%; medicine – 47,7%; winterization – 22,5%, medical treatment – 15,3%; utilities–14,7%. 54,6% of the interviewed believe their income loss should be linked to the armed conflict. The level of vulnerabilities of elder people was assessed through Livelihood CSI. The NA showed that 98,2% of respondents used (or would use) at least 1 stress coping strategy, 92,9% used (or would use) at least 1 crisis coping strategy, and 85,2% used (or would use) at least 1 emergency coping strategy. Moreover, according to the NA, elder men are more likely to rely on their savings and selling household goods than women, while women are more likely to borrow money. One more conclusion could be made based on Livelihood CSI: women would rather save on food to purchase medicines, while men would rather save on medicines to purchase food. While designing this project for Donetska NGCA, PAH used the registration data of 3109 older people, who are to be targeted by the given project as well. This data was collected by PAH Partner in February-March 2020. The most vulnerable of registered older people were provided with the assistance under the previous PAH/BHA project in April-May 2020 and are also planned to be supported under this project. The results of the analysis of registration data showed that before the intervention 55% were in the Acceptable Food Consumption Group (FCG), 37% were in the Borderline FCG, and 7,5% belonged to the Poor FCG. These findings can be further confirmed by REACH NGCA Multisector Needs Analysis (June 2020), where only 34% of assessed households in Donetska NGCA are food secure according to Food Secure Index, while men are likely to be more food secure than women (42% in comparison to 33% respectively). 74% of older people interviewed by PAH Partner in Donetska NGCA claimed that during the last 30 days they mainly did not have access to hygiene items. Moreover, around 40% of older people stated that they are in need of urological pads/diapers. For the absolute majority of older people (99,6%), pension is their main source of income. Furthermore, according to the registration data, approx. 84% of older people have chronic diseases, and approx. 46% can move independently only inside their house/flat and approx. 28% are able to move independently around their neighborhood. The registration data indicates the high needs of older people in support in food and hygiene items, as the emergency intervention from the previous project did not cover the needs of these people for a long time, taking into account the situation with the COVID-19 pandemic that led to restriction of access to their pensions in Ukraine and overall increase of the prices.

TARGET - 60% While implementing the given programme, PAH will ensure system of data collection on people with disabilities using Washington Group Short Set of Questions. PAH ensures accountability to affected population (AAP) through 3 stages: Needs Assessment: Findings of NAs, including collected beneficiaries’ feedback, are incorporated into the project design in order to assure the relevance of PAH’s humanitarian response and the effective participation of affected population on the stage of project planning. Design: The needs of the older people and people with disabilities highly influenced the overall design of the project. The project design is based on PAH’s previous experience in terms ofplanned activities and feedback from beneficiaries, authorities and coordination system (relevant clusters and WGs). Implementation: To ensure a Do No Harm approach during implementation and adequate information sharing and transparency, PAH is committed to share program information with beneficiaries on every stage of project cycle: all beneficiaries will be informed about selection criteria, project duration, project objectives, feedback and complaints channels (F&C), timeframes and aims of each data collection activity, which will be done by PAH project staff, through project leaflets and during data collection activities. Feedback and Complaints: Acknowledging the crucial importance of beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints (F&C) for improving the quality of programming and for better accountability, PAH is deploying a 3-level F&C mechanism: LEVEL I: Beneficiaries have possibility to leave the feedback and/or complaints (Channels: telephone number and email managed by PAH MEAL team, boxes for F&C, KoBo form available for project team to submit beneficiaries’ feedback to MEAL team, trough enumerators and MEAL team members during periodic and regular data collection); LEVEL II: Feedback and complaints are documented and registered in a database (The database of all relevant F&C received under all PAH projects, classified by the level of importance, the subject of F&C and sensitivity, managed by MEAL team.); LEVEL III: Follow-up on feedback and complaints is ensured and documented (Program follow-up is ensured on a regular basis (ad hoc in case of urgent F&C), responsible persons are assigned, follow-up is documented in the F&C database.) Being independent from Program team, PAH MEAL team is responsible for managing F&C channels and registering F&C. Overall, AAP is tailored to be fully compliant with the IASC’s Five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations and other key inter-agency standards on AAP.
