Project Summary

Mercy Corps’ Food Security and Livelihoods in Crisis For Affected Communities in Unity and Warrap State intends to impact the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host individuals by alleviating stressed populations of hunger and enabling resilient opportunities for communities facing extreme climate and economic crises. The project aligns with Objectives 1, 2 and 3 of enhancing and sustaining emergency food production while reducing dependency and increasing households’ resilience to shocks, according to the HRP and Cluster’s focuses.
The project aims to complement traditional food assistance and livelihoods through resilience using Mercy Corps’ Market Systems Development (MSD) in Crisis approach, a framework used to enable proper food production, inclusive market access, and equitable livelihood opportunities. Food Security and Livelihoods in Crisis for Affected Communities will focus and impact communities in Unity and Warrap State which are classified as Priority 1, 2, and 3, aligning with the Humanitarian Response Plan’s (HRP) intersectional coordination of response and needs. Among the communities most affected include Rubkona, Koch, Leer, Guit, Mayendit, Panyijiar and Tonj South which are the targeted areas of Mercy Corps’ HRP plan that will enhance the food security and livelihoods of extremely vulnerable persons.
Mercy Corps’ approach to Food Security and Livelihoods using an MSD in Crisis framework is tried and tested in South Sudan with programs like Mercy Corps’ Strengthening Resilience in Agriculture, Livelihoods and Markets Through Local Institutions (STREAM) which enhanced the food security of over 6,350 households through increased market access, agricultural production, and off- and non-farm livelihood opportunities. By STREAM’s first phase endline report, households feeling the impact of hunger reduced while the percentages of increased access to income-generating opportunities increased across all sites in Greater Mundri, Western Equatoria, and Koch, Unity State; the outcomes greatly improved when compared to the baseline assessments which saw high-stress levels of hunger, extreme losses of crops, and unprecedented rates of unemployment in vulnerable communities.
By using MSD in Crisis and lessons learned through STREAM, among other programs, Mercy Corps expects to reach IPC- 3 and 4 rated communities in Unity and Warrap State, using modalities involving Cash, Economic Recovery in Markets through linkages, livelihood growth, agriculture production, seed system security, and value-chain development. Ultimately, the purpose of Food Security and Livelihoods in Crisis for Displaced Communities is to decrease hunger, diversify and develop income-generating opportunities, and enable resilience to occur in environments of crisis in Unity and Warrap State.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face extremely high levels of food insecurity, livelihoods, and political instability. The context is negatively evolving due to endemic violence, conflict, climatic shocks resulting in extraordinary, localized flooding and droughts, inflation, and the economic impact of COVID-19 (OCHA, 2023). With a severe conflict and climate crisis, exacerbated with a weakening economy and faltering markets, it is suspected some 9.4 million people out of a population of 12 million need humanitarian assistance according to the most recent OCHA Humanitarian Snapshot report. Despite South Sudan’s rich agricultural resources, four in five households skipped meals or ran out of food in both urban and rural parts of South Sudan, reflecting the severe impacts of the pandemic and the compounded crises on the ability of households to meet their daily food needs according to the World Bank. Currently, 7.76 million, or over half of all South Sudanese are food insecure with an IPC rating of 3 or above, with common food staples like White Sorghum costs rising over 153% for 3.5KG in the last year. Among the states in South Sudan facing increased emergency fronts include Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei, Lakes, Warrap, and Northern Bahr el Ghazel, according to the Humanitarian Response Plan priority areas. Of these states, Mercy Corps’ active programming and presence in Unity and Warrap State continues to observe deteriorating conditions; Unity’s communities are challenged with recurring floods, severe food insecurity, conflict, and resource shortages. The situation evolved on April 15th when the Sudan conflict caused large displacements, leading to over 350,000 people into South Sudan as of November 1st. From the total displaced, Unity has observed the second largest arrival rate of refugees and returnees after Upper Nile, according to UNHCR and IOM, stretching resources to the limit where the existing needs of communities are unmet. As a neighboring state to Unity, Warrap ranks 9th out of the 10 states on the Human Development Index, making it one of the least developed states in South Sudan with exceptionally high food insecurity. From the increasing implications on resources and vulnerable communities in Warrap State posed by inter-communal violence, cross-border agro-pastoralist fighting, lack of livelihood alternatives, and stressed hunger levels, food and livelihood insecurity will persist. Mercy Corps conducted a Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) in Unity and Warrap State in June and August 2023, however, due to ongoing shortages of food security and livelihoods investments, much of the same chronic needs persist or have worsened. The RNA surveyed 549 households across Rubkona, Koch, and Panyijiar of Unity State and another 485 households in Tonj North, East, and South of Warrap State. The staggering results found 76% of households in Unity categorized as ‘poor’ or ‘borderline’ under the Food Consumption Score while 84% of households in Warrap were either ‘poor’ or ‘borderline’. Overall, food was asserted as the highest need across both stats of which was indicated from 89% of all Unity and Warrap households; however, four out of five households reported having access to a market where basic food/non-food items are available. The highest primary barrier found was insufficient money, which two-thirds of respondents indicated from Unity while four-fifths from Warrap; this is a clear example where the modality of cash can support existing market structures with support from market recovery approaches while responding to the food security and livelihoods crisis. Likewise, without alternatives to humanitarian assistance, affected communities will continue to depend on NGO’s for years to come, necessitating coordination from the FSL Cluster and implementing partners to enable agency and resilience for vulnerable communities. Therefore, Mercy Corps will respond to approximately 9,740 households across Unity and Warrap states.

2. Project activities

1.1.3 Cash for Asset (CFA) | Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) effectively enhances access to basic needs, food security, and resilience among South Sudanese families in crisis by diversifying food consumption, stabilizing household consumption, and reducing reliance on extreme coping methods, assuming functional markets support increased demand. 1.1.6 Conditional Cash Distribution | Mercy Corps’ Cash for Work (CfW) component will be centered on two key focus areas: 1) construction of roads and 2) construction of dykes. Mercy Corps opted for the CfW approach due to the absence of income-generating prospects in the targeted communities, coupled with elevated levels of food insecurity in these regions. The CfW component will target individuals in productive, safe employment, who would otherwise be forced to migrate to find work. 1.1.7 Sensitization sessions on proper utilization of cash | To ensure cash activities stemming from MPCA are maximized for household adaptation and resilience to shocks and stresses, Mercy Corps will facilitate sensitization sessions regarding the proper use of cash and its benefits. 2.1.1 Distribution of dry season agricultural Kits (VK, FK) | Mercy Corps seeks to boost food security in Warrap and Unity states by providing high-quality dry season agricultural kits to participants, focusing on the most vulnerable communities while ensuring enhanced agricultural outcomes. 2.1.2 Distribution of main season agricultural Kits (CK, VK, FK) | Mercy Corps seeks to boost food security in Warrap and Unity states by providing high-quality dry season agricultural kits to participants, focusing on the most vulnerable communities while ensuring enhanced agricultural outcomes. 2.1.3 Support to Fisheries | The program will also support fish capture, cleaning, preservation, value addition and storage to ensure healthy fish reach the markets. This will involve working with fisherfolk groups to improve fish capture by provision of fishing gear. Fishing gear will be composed of a canoe, fishing rods, fishing hooks, fishing nets, and fishing twine. The program will also train the fisherfolk on improved ways of cleaning and preservation of fish for longer shelf-life and create linkages with markets. 3.1.2 Good Agronomic Practices | Mercy Corps will use the Farmer Field School (FFS) or Agro-Pastoral Field School (APFS) approach to engage producer groups, cooperatives, and post-harvest processing groups to learn technical skills focusing on resilient, smart agriculture techniques to improve production and preserve nutritional value through better soil and water management. 3.2.6 Vocational & other enterprise/business-related support | Mercy Corps will train individuals between Warrap and Unity State through economic recovery and market system interventions, focusing on new livelihood development such as vocational training linked to the farmers and temporary employment activities focused on rehabilitation of feeder roads, rehabilitation and construction of dykes and drainage systems to address seasonal flooding issues. The project will prioritize vulnerable groups, including GBV survivors, single women heads of households, families with malnourished children under five, and youth. 3.2.3 Village Savings/Loans Associations | Mercy Corps will establish, train, and enhance Self-Help Groups, which are savings groups that store and borrow funds from within the association while undergoing business and financial training, including life-skills on how to raise issues in their communities. 3.3.3 Pre-position grain and seed protection kits (Seed system Security) | Mercy Corps will support farmers to access a wide variety of seed options with emphasis on locally preferred varieties through both formal and informal seed systems with the aim of increasing food production.
