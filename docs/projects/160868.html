Project Summary

Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan in September 2018, has led to better access. However, the slow process of peace implementation is leading to uncertainty while the effect of conflict, climatic change and poor economic situation is still being felt in the country; no state has been spared with more than 7 million people in need of assistance, a number increasing due to high numbers of IDPs and returnees (IOM, July 2019). The health system pillars are fragile and seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases (i.e. malaria and measles) continue to pose challenges (SSHF 2nd Allocation, 2019). The high disease burden and < 5 Mortality at 93/1000 live births and maternal mortality ratio is at 789/100,000 births, a young girl in South Sudan is three times more likely to die in childbirth than to finish primary school (according to South Sudan national human development index 2016). Gender roles have changed due to absence of men; women and children have taken up the role of breadwinner in the family somehow has exposed them to exploitation and sexual violence. Moreover, FSNMS round 24 result shows that the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) for children has increased to 16.2% in 2019 and 13.3% in 2018 which is above the level of WHO threshold; therefore, deterioration of nutrition situation is likely to cause upsurge of diseases especially among the vulnerable groups (women and children). In WBeG the population is displaced and settled where social services have never existed. In some cases, individuals walk 35 km to access health services. The Health Pool Fund (HPF3) does not currently support health facilities within Baggari and Bazia (IMC March 2019 assessment). Only 3 (23%) out of 13 health facilities are functional and according to Health Service Functionality monthly bulletin Sept. 2019 the facilities offers either limited or moderate Basic Primary Health Services leaving those in the proposed area extremely undeserved. On that backdrop, Islamic Relief intends to continue to provide lifesaving humanitarian health assistance to approximately 20,833 individuals with focus on women, children and elderly as the most vulnerable population in Kapoeta East, Wau (Baggari and Bazia). The interventions will respond to the immediate needs and contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality in the conflict affected host communities and IDPs in greater Baggari and Bazia of Wau County, Western Bhar El Gazhal and Kapoeta East Eastern Equatoria. Through Guidance of relevant health guidelines in the country. IRW will focus in preventive as described in the activity section; in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases. The organization will participate in country coordination, Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM); mass immunization campaign and routine vaccine to protect the beneficiaries from vaccine preventable diseases (VPD) and be part of out-break response team in the locality where possible. The project will contribute to Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable population through established facilities; linking the activities with other sectors like WASH and nutrition and promotes, reproductive health services. In Kapoeta East (Lopua) and Wau (Bazia and Baggari). IRW will continue to support Ngisa, Mboro and Gede PHCU in Wau and Lopua PHCU in Kapoeta East and establish more 5 Out-Reach sites; there will be consultation ad treatment in this section. In the third Objective Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders. Staffs will be trained on case identification and referral for continuum of care. IRW have experience incidence of vandalization of health care infrastructure in its previous response in 2018 and therefore, IRW will be oblige to contribute to the Surveillance of attacks on Health care and provide such information in a timely manner.

1 - Needs

The effect of conflict is still being felt in the country as no state has been spared. More than 7 million people in need of assistance, a number increasing due to high numbers of IDPs and returnees (IOM, July 2019). However, the coverage of humanitarian actors does not meet the need. The high risk of Ebola in 12 counties and the seasonal outbreaks of communicable diseases including cholera and measles, with malaria continues to be endemic; while the health system pillars are fragile and continue to pose challenges (SSHF 2nd Allocation, 2019). In WBeG the displaced population have settled where social services have never existed. In some cases, individuals walk 35 km to access services. The Health Pool Fund (HPF3) does not currently support health facilities within Baggari and Bazia (IMC March 2019 assessment); while the functional facility only 23% (3 out of 13) offers either limited or moderate basic primary health services (Health service functionality monthly bulletin September 2019). For the last 5 years active conflict in Wau resulted into health facilities destruction and out of the 29 health facilities in Wau, most of the non-functional facilities are in the Baggari region, leaving those in the proposed area extremely underserved. There is no upgraded PHCC in Bazia and Baggari, and referrals are a challenge due to lack of eligible ambulance services that can access the area. Basic infrastructure have been destroyed; and access to qualified staff remain a big challenge in the 3 Payams (Baggari, Bazia, and Bessilia) of Wau County. Vaccine storage remains a challenge in the three war affected Payams of Baggari. Bazia, and Bessilia. Most of the cold chain fridges were stolen and others destroyed during the time of war. The poor road network contributes to breakage of weekly vaccine supply, mainly when it is raining and the road is inaccessible; while vaccine dropout rate between DPT/Penta1 and DPT/Penta3 coverage is ≥ 18% (Routine Facility data). Apart from RDT stripes techniques, whole of Baggari, Bazia, and Bessilia health facilities do not have a well-established laboratory services. Diseases like TB can only be diagnosed clinically to be transferred to Wau town. Referrals of such diseases and other complicated illness including HIV has been a big challenge since all the facilities in the 3 Payam operate at the level of a PHCU and this has to lack of continuum of care especially to the population that was cut of services during conflict. Moreover most of the deep lying villages that cannot access health service within a day and go back due to poor road network needed to be reached by Carrying out Health Mobile outreaches has shown that integration of activities remain a challenge as most of the villages in the catchment areas do not have WASH facilities and yet they present Huge number of children and women. Places like Kangbwa, Ngonjeiko, and Ngombwere of Fanjar, Winji and Ufew of Gaitan and Maju. Kapoeta east as a marginalized community; surrounded by history of inter-communal cattle raiding, disease outbreaks increasing their vulnerability to food insecurity. Concerns include high cases of malaria 38% of the total Morbidity in the facility cases; occurrence of measles and cholera outbreaks. The maternal health indicator are poor due to social related factors; For example individuals in Lopua primarily women used to walk 16km to the closest health facility in Narus. In the absence of sustained humanitarian assistance Lopua will be severely exposed and become more vulnerable due to lack of health care services and nutrition intervention. With the above mentioned challenges; there is need to re-establish proper health system including upgrading primary health care center with proper Obstetric and emergency services with clear referral system; laboratory investigation; mental health and psycho social support, HIV and TB services within the locality.

2 - Activities

IRW will undertake provision of emergency health services in Kapoeta East (Lopua) and Wau (Bazia and Baggari). Continue to support Ngisa, Mboro and Gede PHCU in Wau and Lopua PHCU in Kapoeta east and establish more 5 Out-Reach sites (3 in Wau and 2 in Kapoeta East). IRW, will contribute to reduction of excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases with the selected project sites. IRW will recruit staff according to the health cluster essential clinical package and MoH basic health and Nutrition guideline. Recruited staff will be trained on infectious disease surveillance reporting; In order to actively and passively identify cases from the community and facility respectively. There will be weekly reporting and incase of any incidence the EWARS will provide an alert for action. IRW, will focus to maintain incidence Case fatality Rate (CFR) low and within the WHO threshold of ≤ 2%; however IRW will work with other stakeholders including health cluster to achieve this. Annual internal contingency plan will be developed; prevention of epidemic prone diseases through health education, provision of Immunization will Target (9000 children) through this project, IRW will be providing immunization against vaccine preventable diseases at Lopua PHCU in Kapoeta East and Wau county at (Ngisa, Mboro, Gede, Gytan and Taban) PHCU, the response will involve recruiting of Vaccination 2 per each facility and mobile unit, recruitment of vaccine Social Mobilizers 4 per each facility and 2 per each mobile clinic, to provide routine immunization to <5 girls and boys, pregnant mothers and women of child bearing age as per standards. In order to improve the quality of EPI (Expanded Programme on Immunization) service, IRW will conduct trainings on EPI, including vaccine antigen, routine immunization and cold chain management for 15 female and 15 male staff (30 staff) also provide logistical support to the facility for the access of vaccines from the county cold chain deport. Treated Mosquito nets and hygiene promotions within health facilities and community. IRW will participate in campaigns including SIA within the locality. In regards to the second objective that is to improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations – Through the recruited staff Maternal Health Target (2000 Pregnant, lactating women, women of Reproductive age and girls in adolescent. IRW will work to improve and scale up maternal health care services to vulnerable population in Wau and Kapoeta East, and in order to achieve that IRW will conduct recruitment of skilled health personnel that to include (1 midwife and 2 community maternal health workers per facility in 4 static PHCU and mobile unit). Provide basic reproductive health services at static PHCU facility as ANC service, identification of high risk pregnancies, prenatal complications, normal deliveries, postnatal care visits and appropriately refer complicated cases for further evaluation and treatment. The outreaches service will ensure provision of ANC, supplements, and delivery kits and provide skilled assistance to eventual deliveries. IRW will also provide family planning care and services to eligible cases based on demand, conduct follow-up on cases to control defaulters IRW will purpose to build capacity of human resources providing reproductive health service through conducting training of (10) skilled birth attendants on essential obstetric and neonatal care and Integrated Management of Neonatal and Child illness (IMNCI).Similarly IRW will access essential drugs and supplies through core Pipe line and UNFPA . In the third objective, Increase access to services for survivors of GBV, disabled and mental health disorders. IRW intends to conduct refresher training for at least 30 staff on GBV risks, case identification and referred for management. Like-wise in MHPSS Support will be referred accordingly.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

IRW has established a feedback and Complaints mechanisms and reporting procedures at the field offices where all the beneficiary s are given the leeway to communicate their grievances directly to the office. A complaints and feedback mechanism focal point (CFM focal point) is available in each of the field office with a dedicated phone number where network is available. During the project implementation, accountability committees (community health committee) will be formed on community need vulnerability criteria. The committee will be trained on the purpose of the complaints and feedback mechanisms as well as how to collect the information from the community and report through the established system. Visibility boards will also be installed at the project sites with the hotline telephone number put on it and the name of the CFM focal point. The CFM focal person therefore records grievances for each complainant in the complaints/feedback form while greatly considering the confidentially of the complainant and share with the management team at the country office for appropriate decision to be taken. IR responses to this complaints/feedbacks are always clear, accurate, balanced, simple, fair and easy to understand. All the issues raised in the complaint will be addressed. In summary, below are the mechanisms IRW in South Sudan is employing in tracking complaints/feedbacks. In person, i.e. verbal feedback or complaint to any staff member of IRW especially the CFM focal person. This is really beneficial as people with low literacy levels are also given a platform to express themselves and make recommendations on how IRSS can further improve on the project. By phone/Mobile (call or text SMS). Focal person is assigned to collect and record feedback and complaints and through Via a ‘suggestion/ complain box/visibility and boards erected at the project sites.
