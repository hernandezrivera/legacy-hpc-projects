Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups in Bab Al Salameh and Bab Al Nour camps clusters in Aleppo Governorate, and in Al Karameh and Atmeh camps clusters in Idleb Governorate, focusing on appropriate Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices in emergency situation. Also, this project will improve the equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases for boys and girls under five years and PLW through assessment, physical measurements, screening and community-based management of acute malnutrition. In addition, it is aimed to build the capacity of IYCF facilitators through routine training on IYCF-E and Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) topics, and build the capacity of Community Members through awareness raising sessions.

Needs

Across Syria, 152 out of 270 sub-districts are categorized with severe nutrition problems, which are considered acute and require urgent humanitarian assistance. 96 sub-districts were ranked with major problems and five sub-districts were categorized as moderate, and likewise require humanitarian assistance. Chronic malnutrition was a problem in Syria even before the crisis hit in 2011, and since then the rates are on the rise. In addition to data that indicates ‘High’ stunting levels in Lajat, Dar’a in 2017 (27.5%) and in the sub-district of Jarablus in Aleppo in 2018 (25%), high rates of Chronic Malnutrition were confirmed to be ‘Very High’ in Tell Abiad (32%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) in 2018, as indicated by SMART surveys and using the 2018 WHO-UNICEF stunting thresholds. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first six months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency. IYCF practices remain poor in Syria, with exclusive breastfeeding rates below pre-crisis levels at 24% in 11 governorates. Barriers to appropriate feeding of young children include breastfeeding difficulties, illness of the mother, affordability, and lack of support from family. The provision of IYCF services is done through raising awareness messages rather than the needed specialized services such as one-to-one counselling to better support pregnant women and caregivers on maternal nutrition and to safely feed their young children during the critical window of 0-24 months of age. Capacity of health and nutrition practitioners needs to be strengthened on a national scale. Early marriage is extremely prevalent in Syria. Many female adolescents who have been married at early age and had children very soon, need more attention, care and focus on raising awareness on IYCF practices and maternal nutrition. High acute and chronic malnutrition rates might be related to gender-inequitable access to nutritious foods, quality health care and WASH services. Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services including nutrition. Furthermore, Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), young girls, and adolescent mothers who access nutrition support services frequently need additional support in GBV and PSS, to which there is limited capacity and skills of nutrition staff to address or recognize signs of distress. There is need to build capacity of nutrition service providers in these areas and to identify existing referral pathways to caseworkers who can deliver appropriate services to those in need.

Implementation of activities

Through this project, the NGO will support four IYCF facilities and four CMAM Clinics in Idleb and Aleppo. The activities will be implemented by 20 trained IYCF facilitators, four IYCF cite supervisors, three CMAM doctors and two CMAM nurses. In addition, the NGO’s Nutrition program will support four Mobile Medical Unites (MMUs) and one Maternity Hospital in Idleb city. Over the course of the project timeline, the NGO’s Nutrition team will support facility and community based Infant and Young Child Feeding activities through providing consultations on WHO recommended infant and young child feeding practices in the four supported IYCF facilities on the following topics:- Timely initiation of breastfeeding within the first hour after birth- Place babies skin to skin contact with their mothers immediately following birth- Exclusive breastfeeding among children 0-5 months and the rules of breastfeeding- Continued breastfeeding among children 6-23 months- Timely introduction of complementary feeds among children 6-9months- The lactation process among the separated mother or caregivers. - Common Misconceptions about Breastfeeding- Breast milk substitutes codesThese consultations will be provided by trained IYCF Facilitators specialized in working with Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and other caregivers. Moreover, Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) doctors and nurses will provide Women and Children through four supported CMAM clinics with Micronutrient supplementation for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies. The NGO will also support facility and community-based screening for acute malnutrition through Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for both PLW and, MUAC z-score for children aged under 5 years with intensity of oedema. CMAM clinics will provide treatment for acutely malnourished children under 5 years and PLWs through Outpatient Therapeutic Program (OTP) and Supplementary Feeding Program (SFP), through community mobilization via referrals pathway by IYCF facilitators and other humanitarian workers. Other activities will be implemented through this project include, conducting camps clusters lever IYCF practices survey in the areas covered by the NGO programs, strengthening the capacity of public health staff on program reporting and rapid nutrition assessment/SMART surveys, as well as strengthening the Nutrition surveillance system through producing comprehensive 4Ws reports and other reports requested by the Nutrition Clusters.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks• High levels of violence and criminality is a risk to safety of staff, particularly in Idleb. Beneficiaries and humanitarians alike face a real risk to their physical safety and security due to ongoing violence, targeting by different groups and criminal elements and being caught up in conflict related clashes.• Working with PLW and children under 5 years encompasses risk of potential sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), physical abuse and harassment, gender based violence and trafficking from a range of actors including their own family members, persons in positions of power including local officials, service providers and NGO's own staff and staff of other NGOs.• The lack of quality services and referral increases risk of further stigmatization and harm, lack of trust in available services and fewer disclosures. In addition to the risk of missed opportunities for screening PLW and children• Risk of developing post-traumatic stress symptoms on women and adolescent girls during pregnancy and following childbirth. Mitigation Measures• The organization has developed an extensive security management structure, encompassing a security manager, as well as security officers, assistants, analysts and guards who establish valuable community based contacts enabling acceptance and a steady flow of information. The organization undertakes full Security Risk Assessments (SRAs) of all activity locations identify threats, estimate impact, and assess risks. Strategies developed from the SRAs, take the form of recommendations, security directives, and are incorporated into location specific Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). • Staff have emergency contacts and safe houses in Syria in case of emergency; receive regular security updates; are able to receive treatment at NGO supported clinics and medical evacuation in organizations supported ambulances; and only enter an area if they feel comfortable doing so.• The organization maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and follows internationally established protocols and standards for child safeguarding. The organizations staff and partner staff are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children. The organization has an established community based feedback and response mechanism to including a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases. All community centres and facilities have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback including reporting child protection concerns anonymously. The organization considers maintaining a gender balance in its outreach teams and at static centre, in order to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable with the services provided.• The organization provides staff wellbeing activities to its field staff, in order to help them cope with stress at the workplace and in their daily lives.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The NGO has developed an extensive security management structure, encompassing a Security Manager, as well as security Officers, Assistants, Analysts and Guards who establish valuable community based contacts to ensure acceptance by beneficiaries and a steady flow of information. The NGO undertakes full Security Risk Assessments (SRAs) of all activity locations to identify threats, estimate impact, and assess risks. Strategies developed from the SRAs, take the form of recommendations, security directives, and are incorporated into location specific Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Regular communication between security officers and field based staff promotes safety, and “safe houses” identified inside Syria are available for use when routes are not safe for travel or borders suddenly close. Staff deployed in all the camps, are hired from the local community. All staff in country receive daily security briefings and weekly security and contextual updates. Community sensitization of the project goals, admission criteria, and process of CMAM programs are carried out to minimize risk of creating community tension.The NGO also maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards SEA following internationally established protocols and standards for SEA protection. NGO staff, interns and volunteers are trained on SEA protection, child safeguarding with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA and child protection concerns. The NGO’s supported centers and facilities have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback including reporting SEA and child protection concerns anonymously. Moreover, the integration of the NGO's programs in NWS facilitates cross program referrals where suspected cases of GBV or child abuse are referred to the respecting programs for case management and support.
