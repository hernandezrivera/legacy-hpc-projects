Project Summary

This project proposal targets particularly 400 vulnerable teenage/young mothers who are either single or prematurely married as victims of the crisis for Psychosocial support, early recovery and empowerment in some livelihoods activities. They will be trained and supported in some quick income generating and sustainable livelihoods activities, social sciences, health and hygiene, safety/security and protection tips. The project shall equally lays emphasis on 1000 Vulnerable adolescents and young boys and girls of 14-24 years training in vocational skills in quick income generating activities and promotion of local handicrafts. The project also intend to reach out to 100 parents men and women in groups of the 10 each from the various 9 villages and 1 from the Muslim Mbororo community as community leaders who will nominate a sub divisional supervisory team for the protection of children and the fight against gender based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse in the communities. They will constitute the Community Committees for the Protection of Children and Fight Against Gender Based Violence and Sexual Exploitations and Abuse. These committees will be sensitized on the need to protect the children in the their communities and also design cultural mechanisms for case management. A referral pathway for further case management will be established to handle cases of violations. They shall be trained on local community emergency response, child protection, safety and security tips.
The project intend to revamp and rehabilitate in collaboration with the traditional ruler the Community Education Action Center (CEAC) in Ngwo Village as a Pilot Centre long abandoned with some structures. This shall be the Pilot training centre for both the demonstrative income generating activities - Pig Farming, Poultry and Market Gardening and for the Vocational Skills. Given that CEAC secured, strategically located and has good space with large lecture halls, the community committees will be assembled and trained there. Piglets, table birds and seedlings will be distributed to the beneficiary participants as either small groups holdings or individuals depending on their registration.
Giving vulnerability rate of young girls and boys in Njikwa, this proposal will be concerned with GBV and SEA education, WASH lessons, civic education and morality. A community Office as a reference centre will be set there to manage cases of abuses and other data collections.
Giving that we live in the Covid - 19 Pandemic era, the project will do serious sensitization talks on covid -19 to 12 communities and provide 6000 locally but standard face masks to the people, 200 handwash buckets, 200 cartons of soap, placed in the various public spaces and disinfection against Covid - 19 as measures to curb it if not eradicate the spread of the virus.
In our preliminary need assessment with participants psychosocial and livelihood activities were prioritized by communities, parents and young people to build their capacity for resilience, even in challenging humanitarian situations. Pig, Poultry and market Gardening, live skills especially local Handicraft were top in their list. In line with the principle of accountability, best interest of the child and do no harm, humanitarian responders should listen to the self identified needs of the affected by crisis and respond accordingly.
Humanitarian responders are uniquely placed to meet recovery and livelihood needs in crisis situations, with the agility and capacity to reach young people that development actors cannot provide. Consideration to this project as top need will be appreciated by the people and traditional leaders of Njikwa Communities. Much ground work is done in the communities for the acceptance of the project by IVSPD.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Fierce fighting started in Njikwa on March 4, 2018 following the killing of a Police officer in Oshie and as a result left a trail of destruction and pains in the community and in the hearts of the people and children have been most affected especially the young girls that need urgent attention even as the crisis protracts. Njikwa has been highly hit especially Ngwo and Oshie Villages by this crisis that have kept serious consequential effects in the communities. Sixty eight (68) Homes and fifty one (51) shops burnt, over 1000 other homes damaged and looted affecting over 1500 households directly. Over 7000 children have lost four school cycles as school dropout while some other 3000 have been deprived of the opportunity to begin schooling. All the schools in Njikwa - 7 secondary/technical/SAR-SM and 28 primary are closed and teachers fled. Many children lost school and civil status documents, many lost their livelihood, others became orphans, while others just became radicalized. Now in Njikwa there are no schools, no healthcare services, no administrative services, markets and churches occasionally shut down Njikwa has entered a level of economic recession that is so terrible and unbearable . Great suffering came and many children who could not fern for themselves resorted to promiscuous activities that have only landed most of them either in the armed camps or as teenage mothers. Worst still, some have been infected by STDs and HIV which is very prominent in Njikwa. The main and biggest market in the sub division which serves not just the sub division but also parts of Ngie, Widikum-Menka, and Akwaya sub divisions was burnt down. The biggest district hospital in Momo Division which is in Ngwo - Njikwa, the Health Centre in Oshie and other health posts in the various villages were shut down and all the doctors and nurses fled. From our preliminary inquiries, about 400 school dropped out girls have been impregnated as teenage mothers excluding the over 200 cases of abortions. Some are now unwillingly married while a good number remain single teenage mothers. Unofficial verbal report from the nurses in Njikwa says HIV/AIDS is most prevalent in Njikwa than any other sub division in Momo. Now, promiscuity is at its peak especially situation of GBV and SEA. Njikwa has neither electricity, nor any communication media or network, and broken water pipelines with no repairs since all technicians fled and other water points left in thick bushes. The common playing grounds for children had been the many large school fields which are all deserted and no go zones. There are no community recreational or friendly spaces and no social services for the children. The Mbororo Muslim community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence trust was lost and the Mborolos are being chased away and have suffered a great losses of their livelihood - cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Their children are unable to attend the few learning centres we established. The need to create theirs is an urgent necessity. From our findings, apart from Indigenous Volunteers for Sustainable Peace and Development that is doing some humanitarian work in Njikwa, no other organisation has visited the communities for any assistance but for data collections. Given these varying problems in the sub division, we hope that this project proposal if funded will bring some relief to the traumatizing population especially the children who are out of school for four years.
