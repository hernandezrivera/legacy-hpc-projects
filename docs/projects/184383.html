Project Summary

Armed conflict and erratic climatic conditions remain the primary drivers of displacement in Somalia. This not only hampers access to basic services, but also leads to increased marginalization of minority groups, displaced persons and persons with special needs, heightened protection risks, loss of livelihoods and income, and limited freedom of movement. In addition, communities have reduced coping capacities and resilience, with women and girls, minority clan members, and people living with disabilities being disproportionately affected. As the country prepares to holds its parliamentary elections, indiscriminate attacks on civilians remains rife, access to basic services and goods remains constrained, protection risks continue to rise, and future projections on those at risk of loss of life remains bleak.
In 2022, an estimated 7.7 million people will be in dire need of humanitarian assistance, comprising of women (1.2 million) and children (4.9 million). The child protection AOR estimates that 2.3 million children will be in dire need of child protection interventions while reports from Protection Return Monitoring Networks (PRMN), Somalia Protection Monitoring System (SPMS) and Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) indicate that there has been an increase in child rights violations including FGM, rape and other forms of sexual violence, early/child marriage and intimate partner violence in addition to recruitment and use of children, and an increase in the number of unaccompanied and separated children. According to data collected through MRM, Somalia is now the most dangerous conflict on earth for children, with over 23,000 recorded grave violations in the last 5 years, with the number of child recruitment and abductions far exceeding any other country in the world.
The proposed interventions thus aligns with the CP AOR objectives 1, 2 and 3 of the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan which focuses on boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress have access to well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services by 2022, through strengthening the protection of most vulnerable girls and boys, affected by conflict and climatic shocks by reducing risks and vulnerabilities and increasing community capacities. It will employ participatory and right based approaches, age, gender and diversity (AGD) approach and community-based child protection approaches which will involve, inclusion of children and caregivers from refugee’s/asylum seeker communities, migrants, IDPs, persons with disability and most vulnerable host communities in addition to local authorities in the project management cycle.
The project’s objectives will be to strengthening capacity and the commitment of families and communities to protect children and women from violence, exploitation and harmful practices, through elimination of harmful practices and all forms of gender-based violence, provide survivors with holistic and quality age and gender appropriate services and strengthening child protection systems.
DRC targets to reach 96,785 individuals ( 17,450 girls, 26,800 boys, 26275 women, 26260 men) in selected districts in Somalia.

1. - Projects activities

The activities will be implemented in Somalia and Somaliland and are in line with the CP AOR 2022 HRP objectives and indicators and the 2018-2021 Somalia Centrality of the Protection Strategy.(1). Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) of grave violations and support to children affected by armed conflicts in South Central Somalia and parts of Puntland regions (Monitoring 4 million children in conflict-prone zone, reaching 5000 boys and 1000 girls). (2). Strengthening child protection referral pathways in Banadir, Hirshabelle, Puntland, Southwest and Jubaland states. (3). Conducting safety audits and child protection assessments in the target areas to inform programming and interventions (five IDP camps in selected districts). (4). Provision of timely and adequate response for child violations survivors and those at risk through quality case management, Family tracing and reunification of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and provision of holistic support for persons with special needs (PSNs) through trained case workers. (5). Provision of quality age-appropriate mental health and psychosocial support for girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities, and their caregivers (4000 children and caregivers). (6). Conduct awareness-raising and outreach sessions on child protection among communities through prevention messages on recruitment of children, COVID 19, positive parenting, GBV, harmful practices, family separation and Children's rights among others (80, 000 persons). (7). Capacity building and training of non-institutional service providers, this will include training of community-based child protection structures, duty bearers and humanitarian workers and on child protection approaches in emergencies. (8). Establish and support community-based child protection structures to engage in child protection activities. The Child Protection Community Based mechanisms (CBCPMs) will regularly carry out, among other functions, awareness sessions on children’s rights with communities. (9). Establishment of Child helplines in two selected districts to provide a wide range of critical services, including active listening, counseling and referral services or direct intervention. (10) Provision of individual protection assistance through cash to caregivers of vulnerable children

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1. 1. Mitigating the risk of minority clans/ethnic groups not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. Minority groups in Somalia are rarely targeted in humanitarian project cycle and are mainly marginalized. Women and children and persons with special needs among minority groups are even more sidelined and excluded and do not benefit from interventions that accords them some degree of protection. The environment in Somalia as a result of ongoing armed conflict and cyclic often deem minority persons and groups more vulnerable. In addition, at least 15% (10% children) of the population are people with disabilities who face heightened risks and exclusion from services. To mitigate on this risks, DRC will intentionally target and empower, protect and promote the rights of minority communities, with a focus on women and children who experience multiple-discrimination on account of their gender and minority status. DRC will follow a selection criterion for beneficiaries that include members of minorities groups and PSNs as vulnerable groups that require special attention. The selection of beneficiaries will be through participatory methods in collaboration with trained community and local authority representatives. Through protection monitoring, DRC will identify trends of exclusion and marginalization. Data collected will be used for targeting and prioritization, ensuring that no one is left out. DRC will consult people with disabilities and/or their representatives during the design and Identify and address barriers to participation for people with disabilities. DRC will include specific needs of women, girls and boys when designing and implementing the project with the aim to increase the women and girl safe spaces to provide opportunities for recognizing their role within communities, as an enabler for protection outcomes.2. DRC will continuously monitor risks that could have a significant impact on the achievement of project objectives or have a negative impact on the intended beneficiaries and/or cause deviation from the project’s expected results, these risks include the risk exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. All DRC staff and contractors ascribes to DRC’s code of conduct, child safeguarding policy and the PSEA policy to mitigate any harm against beneficiaries by DRC personnel and programmes. Measures have been put in place to monitor, report and investigate sexual exploitation and abuse in addition to mandatory annual staff training on PSEA.
