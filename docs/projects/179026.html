Project Summary

NGO will deliver comprehensive lifesaving primary health care services and support to secondary level health facilities through direct and partner implementation. NGO’s health services will reach roughly 310,000 vulnerable conflict-affected individuals in Raqqa, Hasakeh and Deir ez Zor through 30 health care interventions including 16 static primary health care clinics (four will deliver BEmONC services), 11 mobile medical units, community health outreach, two COVID treatment centres and one referral desk.
NGO’s primary health care intervention integrates reproductive and community health within its service delivery model. NGO’s health care services are provided in line with the essential health service package for northern Syria and WHO guidelines for Syria. This includes prevention and management of communicable and non-communicable diseases (NCD)s, integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), reproductive, maternal and child health, clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS), provision of pharmacological and nonpharmacological mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), health education and risk communication and community engagement. NGO’s health services in 2021 also include COVID-19 risk mitigation measures with strict adherence to standard infection prevention and control measures (IPC). NGO will also continue support with COVID-isolation units and its work with implementing partners. The NGO will adopt Two referral mechanisms: Internal NGO intersectoral pathway (Health- Protection-ERD), at the health facilities level between the health components ( Health-MHPSS- Reproductive health-community health) and external referral pathway from the MMU and static clinics to secondary health care centers. The referral between the NGO component is tracked and documented through referral tracking tools and focal points from each component who are following the case through regular meeting. For the external referrals, the NGO uses the ambulances to transport the beneficiaries to the secondary health care and receive documented feedback about the case through accompanying medical personnel.
The NGO will also contribute to disease surveillance through systematic collection, analysis and reporting of health data and weekly reporting through the Early Warning and Alert Reporting System (EWARS) to inform and support outbreak preparedness and response. In light of the COVID-19 pandemic, the NGO will aim to increase support to COVID-19 isolation units and integrate its screening, triage, and referral protocols throughout its health interventions. The NGO will also expand Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) across its health programming.
The health program sits within a multi-sector response provided with NGO’s Integrated Protection and Economic Recovery and Development programs, with all three sectors, whenever possible programming in the same locations to provide a comprehensive service delivery package for NGO clients. The NGO responds to the humanitarian crisis in Syria, providing a range of health, protection, basic needs (cash and non-food item distribution) and livelihoods services across Al-Hasakeh Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. The NGO implements camp-based and community-based programs serving internally displaced persons, refugees, and vulnerable host communities. Accessible and available healthcare will strengthen resilience by cascading effects on beneficiary communities beyond individual health status. Free services allow patients to use limited resources to meet other basic household needs. Accessible services reduce chances for unmanaged or worsening chronic diseases to negatively impact individuals' livelihoods. Community-based interventions will increase skills and knowledge that positively impact a community's ability to engage with healthy behaviors as well as cope and respond to preventable health risks.

4 - Needs

Lack of essential medicine and health care workers are key barriers to health access, particularly for non-communicable diseases which account for 45% of all deaths. COVID-19 has put additional stress to coordinate, prepare and respond to outbreaks while continuing routine primary health care. Women of reproductive age and children under five make up more than 35% of the population, with ongoing disruptions or access issues for basic SRH services. With 498,480 women expected to be pregnant in 2021 the gaps in BEmONC & CEmONC services will have devastating impacts on maternal and neonatal outcomes. There is a high incidence of mental disorders among NES communities. Psychiatric disorders range from mild to severe, as are PTSD, depression, anxiety, psychosis, high number / high incidence of convulsions and epilepsy in all age groups and genders. Out of 16 public hospitals, only 1 is fully functioning. No district in NES meets the emergency threshold of at least ten beds per 10,000 people nor the IASC standard of 1 hospital per 250,000 people. There is great need for medicine, PPE and supplies within health facilities, with particular need for NCD medication and psychotropics. NGO will target IDP catchment areas where residents are unable to meet the demand for services and increased IDP movement puts immense pressure on stretched infrastructure. Based on the WHO mortality update for Al Hol camp in August 2020, the mortality of children under five represents 69%% of all mortalities. Many public reproductive health (RH) services are no longer or partially functioning, due to understaffing, insecurity and access constraints and insufficient supplies and equipment.

5 - Implementation of activities

Primary health care delivered across Ar-Raqqa, Al Hasakeh, and Deir-ez-Zor by static PHCCs, MMUs and local partners with essential health service package and WHO guidelines for Syria including malnutrition screening and treatment referrals.NGO integrates primary, reproductive, MHPSS and community health across service delivery points to ensure comprehensives curative and preventive services. NGO will build on its mhGAP MHPSS services. Each facility has an MHPSS counselor with counselling, psychoeducation, psychotropics by psychiatrists, and referrals. Remote services offered during COVID-19. MHPSS is in line with IASC standards.NGO will utilize emergency experience for IPC and quality assessments conducted at facilities with refresher training for health staff. NGO will support 24/7 BEmONC services and work with partners to create a network for BEmONCs centers linked to CEmONC facilities. Integrated will be CCSAS services for GBV survivors linked with the existing protection programs. Provision of life saving pharmaceuticals, PPE, and medical supplies will be provided to all NGO supported facilities. NGO has internal referral system between health, integrated protection and early recovery and development, creating a case management team for referral follow up. NGO ambulances support external referrals and cases are followed by the medical focal points. Primary and secondary health inter-sectoral and between service referrals will be provided. NGO will provide secondary inpatient pediatric care services delivered 24/7 Al Hol camp and support two COVID-19 isolation units. NGO MEAL implements spot checks, data audits, data review and satisfaction surveys, exit interviews with beneficiaries to maintain conditions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Rupture of service delivery during security situations: NGO mobile services will be established in temporary locations while facilities are evacuated and relocated, to maintain health delivery. Risk: Community stigmatization and discrimination of children, survivors of sexual violence receiving specialized services such as case management or health services Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation: Use community acceptance strategy, including non-specialized services as entry-points and trust-building. Ensure specialized services are properly messaged to community. Embed specialized services in broader service-provision to de-stigmatize, using confidential and discrete spaces. MHPSS:There will be awareness sessions and psychoeducation on MH conditions and services, to reduce stigmatization and support awareness of specialized services. Risk: Access barriers due to physical impediments as well as normative access restrictions Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation: Conduct inclusion analysis, deploy MMUs to reach vulnerable populations; deploy community outreach teams to support referrals of vulnerable individuals and integrate health service delivery across other sectors. Risk: COVID-19 and impact on health and wellbeing. Likelihood: High, High mitigation: the NGO will work with its health facilities to create a safe and clean environment at all the health facilities including IPC control measures and PPE adherence for both staff and clients access the health facilities. The NGO will conduct screening and triage to ensure that clients do not have nosocomial infections of COVID-19.The MHPSS services will be available to reduce the stress and anxiety feelings due to COVID-19, as well as support loss.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls’ clients by staff and volunteers. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very High Mitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. NGO adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment for reporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all NGO staff attend a full day NGO Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on NGO’s commitments to beneficiaries and NGO’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. NGO will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity building activities with partners to reach these standards, as necessary. The NGO is a member of PSEA networks inside Syria and provides regular updates on the number of staff receiving PSEA training and number of complaints received through feedback and complaint channels.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: rupture of pharma and medical supplies due to mass influx: NGO has master purchase agreements with local suppliers to ensure continuous supply to supported facilities, uses the HAWALA transfer for local payments taking into consideration SYP fluctuation.Risk: KRI border closures resulting interruption of lifesaving health services mitigated through pharmaceutical contingency stock. NGO actively participates in NES Forum contingency preparedness and response planning to effectively address health needs across NES.Risk: Access and security, NGO regularly reviews contingency plans for Syria operations. This has recently been updated for NES including multiple potential scenarios regarding changes in territorial control, continuation or interruption of cross-border access and access to locations of implementation. Humanitarian access staff conduct negotiations with local actors, work closely with program leads to identify and manage risks and engage with local councils to avoid any interference or aid diversion, which NGO has zero tolerance. NGO has a Security Management Plan and SOPs to guide operations and staff to provide lifesaving assistance using the safest possible methods.Risk: Community acceptance, NGO works with the communities to build trust and community engagement through its community health program. NGO also works with local actors to ensure services are provided to the most vulnerable and in need. NGO ensures program management and implementation aligned with humanitarian principles. Close coordination with local actors for implementation, management and monitoring of project activities to avoid any overlap with other services in same geographical areas and to apply humanitarian principles.
