Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the living conditions of vulnerable groups thorough providing a comprehensive Shelter and NFI services for vulnerable IDP households and host communities through the following:
- rehabilitation of emergency shelter spaces in collective centers, unﬁnished buildings, and damaged houses reaching 1730 households, where 380 medium-damaged houses will be rehabilitated in Maret Tmasrin sub district, 150 unﬁnished houses will be upgraded in Daret Azzeh and Maaret-Tmasrin sub districts, 200 light-damaged houses will be rehabilitated.
- Distribution of shelter kits to 1000 HHs (IDPs and host communities).
- Distribution of 2,000 new arrival kits/NFIs for 2,000 IDPs households (12,000 individuals disaggregated by gender: 1850 men, 2150 women, 3650 boys and 4350 girls) in Atareb Harem, Salqin, Armanaz, Kafar-Tkharim, Idleb and Maret Tmasrin sub-districts.
- Distribution of 3000 winterization kits/vouchers to 3000 HHs/ 18000 individuals disaggregated by gender (2850 men, 3150 women, 6250 girls and 5750 boys).

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support in Syria due to inadequate conditions in existing accommodation and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters. The people in need include different population groups, such as IDPs, returnees and HC. During the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed which goes beyond the capacity and remit of the Shelter & NFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. In view of increased self-organized returns in 2018, there are increased needs related to the repair and rehabilitation of damaged shelters and infrastructure as people seek more durable solutions. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2019, along with those of the 6.2 million long-term IDPs in Syria. A significant proportion of the population is living with at least one shelter adequacy issue Similarly, a significant proportion of the population living in rented accommodation has been unable to afford rent in the last 3 months. This issue disproportionately affects IDPs, of whom 64% are renting. In addition, HCs face significant pressures in terms of shelter capacity and adequacy, as well as services. Most people displaced since Dec 2019 have been moving towards northern Idleb as well as Afrin, A’zaz and Al-Bab in northern Aleppo. It is estaimated by recent CCCM reports that 961,286 (187,121 men, 197984 women, 268,493, and 297688 boys) people have displaced since Dec 2019. , Increasingly large numbers of people are being squeezed into an increasingly small pocket of the northwest.
