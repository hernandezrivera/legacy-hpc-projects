Project Summary

The overarching objective of this project is to enable the farming communities in the target areas to sustainably protect and improve their livelihoods, as well as promote social stability thanks to increased water availability and improved management at community and farm level through quick impact interventions addressing key threats facing agricultural production.
This project falls under Strategic Objective 2 of the HRP 2018-2020 “Ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in the Gaza strip and the West Bank have access to essential services”, as this intervention will be addressing both immediate needs of water among farmers as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. The project is designed as a multi-year project, due to the importance of this intervention for various locations in both the West Bank and the Gaza Strip.
The project also falls under Objective 1 of the Food Security Sector: “The resilience, food security and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected.”

Needs

According to the latest FAO’s livelihood profiling, there are roughly 16 000 farming households that are classified as low resilience farmers, of which 11 600 in the West Bank and 4 100 in the Gaza Strip. Low resilience farmers are characterized by very low daily income levels (17 NIS in West Bank, and 9 NIS in Gaza Strip), which limits their access to food and compounds an already limited access to public services, weak adaptive capacity and high reliance on social safety nets. Without immediate support through livelihood protection and resilience building activities, low resilience farmers are at risk of adopting negative coping strategies, which will further reduce their assets base and income generating potential and increase their aid dependency. In the Gaza Strip, the consequences of the 2014 conflict that sustained Gaza’s agriculture sector at USD 266 million in damages and USD 284 million in economic losses. In three years on, no significant recovery was witnessed in the agriculture sector. Despite the fact that 70% of destroyed greenhouses, 55% of water wells and 56% of vegetable fields have been reconstructed or rehabilitated primarily through humanitarian and development interventions., the needs are still huge to complete rehabilitation of 30% of ground water wells including the repair/renovation of the main irrigation conveyance systems and rehabilitate water the damaged storage facilities. In addition, no less than 44% of damaged greenhouses and open fields were not rehabilitated. In the West Bank, the current management structures used in the various agricultural water facilities are not effective and have resulted in numerous problems and created social conflicts between the farmers from one side and the wells’ owners’ and the operators from other side in addition to poor performance. In addition, the continued deterioration of the water network is causing significant water losses. This needs to be immediately addressed while a storage facility needs to be rehabilitated in order to restore water distribution schemes at farm level and eliminate conflicts on water. In rural communities of both the West Bank and Gaza Strip, women and youth are known to suffer more from irrigation water shortage as they tend to take the main responsibility for ensuring the availability and transport of irrigation water especially for small scale farming activities. In certain areas, the distance between the nearest water source and the farm can be few kilometers, causing women and youth extra work burden and time to perform these tasks. The availability of reliable reservoirs, networks and storage cisterns will have significant positive impact in reducing this extra burden on women and youth. A previous study commissioned by FAO and conducted by Almarkaz Center, showed that women consider these agricultural activities as part of their household role, which has limited financial return. According to the Gender Alert Report (2015) by UN Women and OCHA, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered“helpers” regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Overcoming this gap requires the development of a new mechanism of women affirmative actions while raising their awareness of and skills in the roles they are playing in agriculture. Despite the large number and wide diversity of project targeting agriculture, women do not tend to effectively participate due to the socio0cultural obstacles limiting that participation with men. The needs and responses presented in this project were identified and designed following intensive consultations and frequent field visits to the targeted communities in the different governorates engaging MoA and PWA, CBOs, and the villages councils.

Activities

All project activities will be coordinated with MoA and PWA. Operational coordination aimed at maximizing synergies will take place through the Agriculture Working Group of the FSS. Output 1: Restore farming’ households’ (HHs) production capacities through immediate rehabilitation/repair of damaged water facilities and damaged assets in the targeted areas for at least 1250 vulnerable farmers from Gaza Strip. Activity 1.1 Reduce per cubic metre pumping cost through restoring pumping efficiency of 10 groundwater wells in Gaza Strip and the provision of 10 appropriate solar energy units for the benefit of at least 300 farmers’ HHs. Activity 1.2 Reduce leakage and water loss of main irrigation water supply systems (pipeline) and increase water distribution efficiency through replacing damaged parts and provision of 30 KM HDPE pipes of 3, 4 & 6 inches including their fittings in Gaza Strip for the benefit of at least 450 famers’ HHs. Activity 1.3 Provision of cash and/or in-kind assistance for the restoration of damaged/destroyed productive assets, greenhouses (GH) and damaged agricultural land, and distribution of essential inputs (e.g. plastic sheets & lateral irrigation network equipment) for at least 500 famers’ HHs in Gaza Strip. According to MoA, support to maintaining GH productivity is critical, given their significant role in agricultural production, particularly during winter. Each dunum of GH produces 3-4 times more than one dunum of open field and requires agricultural labour for around 300 days per year. However, windstorms and high temperatures experienced in 2018 have drastically reduced the shelf life of these materials, with farmers unable to afford to replace them due to falling market prices and reduced revenues. Hence, the agricultural productivity of GH is at risk if support is not provided Activity 1.4 Construction of 3 water towers, which will be owned and managed by the farmers, and which will relieve farmers and their HHs from shortages of irrigation water and electricity cuts, as irrigation will depend on gravity to irrigate the farms; and will reduce cost per cubic meter (an agreement will be signed between tower owners & MoA). Activity 1.5 In coordination with MoA extension department, conduct an awareness raising campaign for women, students and youth in areas surrounding targeted communities through extension visits, field days & workshops, focusing on natural resource management (NRM), specifically water use optimization in irrigation; water scheme development principles; and water system maintenance & network repairing. Output 2: Preparedness to shocks for low resilience farmers is enhanced through more efficient management of water resources for at least 1200 vulnerable farmers’ HHs from West Bank and Gaza Strip. Activity 2.1 Conditional cash assistance to low resilience farmers through the rehabilitation/construction of 300 rainwater harvesting cisterns in West Bank (each having storing volume not less than 60 m³) for the benefit of at least 300 farmers’ HHs. Activity 2.2 Rehabilitation of 20 irrigation water ponds in Jordan Valley of West Bank with total storage capacity up to 60 000 m³ for the benefit of at least 600 famers’ HHs. The ponds will be selected based on needs criticality criteria. Activity 2.3 Raising farmers’ and their HHs’ awareness through extension visits, field days and workshops on disaster preparedness and coping mechanisms, specifically optimized irrigation practices and crop strategizing in West Bank and Gaza Strip. Activity 2.4 In coordination with MoA extension department, conduct an awareness raising campaign for women, students and youth in areas surrounding targeted communities through extension visits, field days and workshops, focusing on NRM, specifically water use optimization in irrigation; water scheme development principles, and water system maintenance & network repairing. Activity 2.5 Pilot water committees/cooperatives for the management of water resources.

Number of women and youth participating in awareness campaigns. Monitoring and EvaluationProject progress will be monitored based on the below indicators. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted, interviewing a sample of male and female beneficiaries to monitor use of the inputs distributed and prospects for sustainability of the intervention and to assess accountability to affected populations. Collected information will be gender disaggregated. Project activities and achievements will be monitored consistently to ensure continued alignment with the FSS response plan and the FAO Country Programme Framework 2018-2022.All project activities and results will be coordinated with the Food Security Sector (FSS) to avoid duplication of emergency support efforts. Relevant results, analysis and lessons learned shall be shared with the other Food Security Sector members. Additionally, FAO, MoA and local implementing partners will work closely with representatives from the community, community-based organizations, village councils, women’s associations and relevant stakeholders. This also applies to representatives of water source owners/operators and water user committees (WUCs). This is to discuss project planning and execution and allow for a feedback mechanism on progress and challenges from the various stakeholders. Regular meetings, focus group discussions and field visits, in full coordination and engagement of the beneficiary communities, will be facilitated. A Grievance Redressal Procedure on the basis of FAO guidelines and taking into account local best practices has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the officer in charge of receiving the feedback/complaint, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until final decision and the subsequent follow up. The procedure includes several well-defined steps tied to specific time frames including: preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and will be publicized among targeted communities in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post distribution mounting as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant.
