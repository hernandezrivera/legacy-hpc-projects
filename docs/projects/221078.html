Project Summary

Action Against Hunger in South Sudan employs a multi-sectoral and intensively integrated program approach especially in life-saving sectors such as, WASH, nutrition, Health, food security and livelihood as well as, protection focusing on Gender-based Violence. The main objective of the approach is directly addressing underlying causes of hunger and or malnutrition by implemented multifaceted WASH activities at both institutional level and catchment or community WASH, the priority target for the WASH activities will be Counties and or Payams reporting high cases of malnutrition, prone to water related diseases outbreaks, poor access to WASH services and infrastructures, food insecure or Counties experiencing emergency levels of food insecurity as reflected in the November 2024 IPC report. The WASH intervention at both nutrition, health and friendly spaces and centers are directly aligned and contributes to the WASH cluster objectives of 1) Reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition, 2) Provision of access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. 3) WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation. 4) Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure. Through provision of access to safe sufficient water through, drilling of new water points targeting locations with limited or no protected water points including those Counties Payams hosting displaced populations and or returnees, Provision of household water treatment items and alternative ways including awareness on boiling, filtration etc, Rehabilitation of non-functional water points of all types in a bit to restore access to safe sufficient water, safe and dignified disposal of excreta for both human and livestock especially within the home stead, this will be through, construction or rehabilitation of depleted sanitation facilities, improving environmental waste management focusing on provision of garbage bins/pits and awareness on safe collection, management and disposal of domestic waste, for improved hygiene practices, ACF will engage locally identified among the affected communities and location youth, female and male trained to conduct a participatory hygiene and sanitation transformation awareness including triggering behavior change, ACF will adapt its' social Behavior Change and communication strategy in identification of the key barriers to best hygiene practices, enablers and bridges for designing the messages for effective hygiene awareness, WASH items will also be either accessed from the WASH cluster core pipeline or procured depending on the availability to support in uptake and practice of hygiene promotion. The project will aimed to provide meaningful access to safe sanitation facilities by designing and constructing, disability friendly gender-sensitive latrines, conduct thorough safety audits of WASH facilities through tailored focused group discussion targeting women and girls, installation or construction of flood resilient WASH infrastructure to ensure, uninterrupted access to safe water including during floods.

1. Needs

Approximately 5.3 million people in South Sudan lack access to WASH infrastructure, driving WASH insecurity, increasing vulnerability, exposing communities to increased watery related illnesses, with morbidity impacting children and PLW rates of malnutrition, market access and economic fluctuations reduce community resilience. Shocks, climatic events, IDP camps and returnee movement increase WASH access burden, due to high density population locations overwhelming provision of services. A joint monitoring programme highlights significant disparity between rural and urban water, and sanitation access, with rural water basic access falling by 3 per cent. This burden is projected beyond 2025. The ISNA suggests that rural sanitation is significantly under-served, with over 60% practicing open defecation. Clinical watery diarrhea cases are chronic in several counties with gradual increases during 2024, particularly those counties identified with high open defecation, highlighted by lack of access to rural WASH services. Hygiene needs in 2024 responses highlighted issues with safe water storage, overall hygiene habits and access to soap. The 2024 FSNMS 25% of female users reported feeling unsafe when accessing WASH services. According to the IPC report published on the 18 November 2024, An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months are suffering or expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and will be in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2024 to June 2025. This shows a 20.4 percent deterioration of acute malnutrition compared to 1.66 million registered in 2023. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden concentrated in the five states of among Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Warrap. Key drivers of these trends are, poor WASH practices coupled with low access to safe water sources, limited or inadequate access to health services, especially with the impact of floods affecting access to scarcely functional health facilities, this led to high disease prevalence of diseases with 49.4 percent of children having reported being ill within two weeks prior to the assessment. Action Against Hunger intentionally targets Counties classified or projected to be in emerge level of Acute malnutrition or IPC 4 such as Fangak, Duk, Pibor, Aweil Center, Aweil East, Gogrial West and Twic as well as, phase 3, Gogrial East. So far, 629,000 South Sudanese nationals have crossed into South Sudan from Sudan due to the ongoing conflict which started from 15th April 2023, these returnees and displaced are settled in Aweil East, in Northern Bahr El Ghazal, Warrap and Western Bahr El Ghazal. According to the April – July 2025 projection of returnee’s food insecurity levels up to 5% (31,000) would be in IPC phase 5 or catastrophe while 40% or (252,000) in emergency level 4. While cases of cholera outbreak are on the rise and widely spreading across the Country, the Ministry of health in its' 28.10.2024 confirmed 6 of 33 samples received from Renk County testing positive using culture as of 11th October 2024.

2. Project activities

Action Against Hunger propose activities as per the WASH cluster guideline to directly contribute to restoration of safe access to sufficient water, provision of gender-segregated sanitation facilities provision of WASH kits for most vulnerable communities including the SAM/MAM caregivers, ACF will also identify women and girls at the reproductive age for support with dignity kits, these proposed project will be conducted in an integrated manner complementing ACF nutrition, Health, GBV and food Security and livelihood across the targeted counties of, Fangak, Duk, Kassingor, Aweil Center and Aweil East. The key specific activities will include;i) Rehabilitation or construction of gender segregated 10 blocks of flood resilient latrines, ii) rehabilitation and or construction of 10 flood resilient water points across the targeted Counties, iii) Construction of disability friendly designed 3 blocks of latrines for safe access and disposal of excreta these activities will directly contribute to the reduction of environmental hazards. iv) Mobilize 25 (13 Males and 12 Females) qualified member of the targeted communities who will be provided with training on basic hygiene approaches and awareness techniques and engaging them to carry out participatory hygiene and transformation sanitation aimed at reaching to 80,477 individuals (46,221 children; 24,655 Male and 21,566 Female, 29,754 Adults of whom, 13,283 Males and 16,471 Females with elderly consisting a total of 4,502, 1,540 Males and 2,962 Females) for improve hygiene practices focusing on hand hygiene, environmental hygiene, safe water chain and menstrual hygiene management. v) ACF will either procure or when available access WASH kits from the WASH cluster core pipeline to target 405 SAM caregivers enrolled and receiving nutrition/health services at the facilities within the targeted Counties, vi) Conduct safety audits within the WASH facilities to identify potential safe concerns particularly for Women and girls while accessing WASH facilities for effective design and implementation of set of activities as per finding recommendations, vii) provision of WASH kits to at least 386 vulnerable households particularly during emergencies. viii) Identify 8047 individuals to be supported through cash and voucher with priority being the SAM caregivers, vulnerable returnees and displaced. The above key activities will contribute to cluster objectives; "Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition", "Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties", "WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation" and "The Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure", . Action Against Hunger activities will target the most vulnerable communities consisting of returnees, displaced and residents as defined by the WASH cluster.
