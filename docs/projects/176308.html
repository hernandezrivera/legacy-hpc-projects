Start Date

01/01/2021

End Date

31/12/2021

Project Summary

This project will aim to provide integrated emergency lifesaving health services will target a total of 11,529 vulnerable individuals; Children 3,642 (1,929 Girls, 1,713 Boys, and 4,295 Women, Elderly 845, Men 2,747 Men through fixed sites and mobile approaches in Pariang (Unity), and Duk (Jonglei) of South Sudan during 2021.
Disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and adequate surveillance systems will be in place through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS).
Hold the Child will provide health care services adapting to the current COVID-19 context and ensure COVID-19 prevention and appropriate response activities are integrated while continue providing essential lifesaving health services. EVD prevention, surveillance and response readiness to mitigate the impact of an Ebola outbreak will be continued as part of disease outbreak, prevention and response. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and services for persons with disabilities will be integrated and strengthened.
During 2021 of the project cycle, Hold the Child is anticipated to utilize 390,000 $; target to reach 10% of the health cluster People in need in the two counties of Duk and Pariang through 3 cluster strategic objectives; (1) Reduce morbidity and mortality, protection threats and incidents for the most vulnerable populations in severity levels 4 and 5.
1-Reducing excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases
Under this objective, key activities including but not limited to;
- Conduct disease surveillance in 2 Counties to support the Weekly Reporting through IDSR for all the supported health facilities and EWARS for IDP Sites.
- Capacity building for health care workers at health facilities in integrated emergency preparedness and response.
- Integration of IPC/WASH at all health care facilities will be central to the implementation of this project. IPC/WASH will be integrated at 4 PHCUs, 2 Mobile clinics.
-Conduct surveillance of attacks on health care in the selected counties and report any vandalization of health care infrastructure, theft of equipment, essential medicines and supplies.
2. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations.
Hold the Child will aim to increase access to integrated quality essential health care services through a wide range of activities including;
- Support 6 health care facilities to provided integrated and quality static health care services, 4 PHCUs and 2 Mobile Clinics across the selected Counties.
- Conduct curative consultations at 6 health facilities to reach estimated 26, 394 beneficiaries
- HCO will set up 2 Mobile Clinic that will be manage by 5 Mobile Teams– Both will be in Duk. Each team will conduct integrated mobile outreach at least once every week.
- Training of health care workers in diagnosis and case management of malaria and safe motherhood. This trainings will target to train 42 health care workers in total, 6 clinicians and 12 nurses, 12 midwives in safe motherhood to ensure skilled deliveries and 6 data clerks across the 6 Health facilities in the selected counties.
3. Increase access to services to survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.
Under this objective, Hold the Child will carry specific activities tailored to;
- Training of HCWs on MHPSS. HCO intends to train 24 HCWs in MHPSS,
- Provided MHPSS services at 6 health facilities to reach 662 beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

According to (SSHHS 2008 NHSSP 2017-2022), Estimated 56% of the population live at least 5km away from the nearest health facility and have much difficulties accessing essential health care services (SSHHS 2008, NHSSP 2017-2022). This is associated with poor health indicators, for example high mortality rate of under 5 children at 90.7/1000 and maternal mortality rate of 789/100,000 (South Sudan Mortality Statistics, 2016) among others. Displaced populations suffer more constraints in accessing lifesaving and essential health care, with children under 5 most vulnerable to vaccine preventable diseases as well as women of reproductive age facing high health risks of unskilled care during pregnancy and child births. Immunization coverage over 2 years (2018 & 2019) for all vaccine preventable diseases have persistently remained below 50%. This has created a big gap of 42% of children under 1 year at risk of measles (WHO EPI Report Sept 2018). Recurrent outbreaks, and or upsurge of epidemic prone diseases including acute watery diarrhea/cholera, measles, malaria, meningitis and hepatitis E compounded by food insecurity presents increased susceptibility to morbidity and mortality especially to pregnant mothers and children under 5 years of age. Over stretching the health care system is the current looming threats of importation of viral hemorrhagic fever (Ebola Viral Disease) in the Country. With the ongoing revitalized peace process the anticipated returnees would even pose greater risk to the importation of EVD. This necessitates for early and adequate preparedness and response to potential outbreaks. Cross cutting issues like people with disabilities, mental disorders, HIV/AIDS, TB and non-communicable diseases including lifesaving trauma care are being cut-off from life-saving services. This has been related to contextual disruption of services and unpredictable health care funding in conflict affected locations with increasing IDP burden. Under this response plan, Hold the Child aims to fully integrate health care activities with nutrition, IPC/WASH, Protection and Food Security and Livelihood. Protection areas of focus will mainly on SGBV and MHPSS services.Heavy floods, communal conflicts as a result of youth fighting, Cattle raiding, Disease outbreak including measles, floods, Population Displacement, acute watery diarrhea and with a wide spike of Covid-19 globally affecting humanitarian response worldwide.According to IPC analysis released between Jun – July 2019, Duk and Pariang have GAM of 26.61% and Pariang 19.59% above the 15% threshold respectively. IPC severity levels of Duk and Pariang is level 4 and 3 respectively.The report further more show that estimated 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Approximately 1.4 million South Sudanese children under age 5 and another 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2021. Pariang County has reportedly increasing number of Covid-19 cases with high infection rate; IDSR report Week 5. February 2021. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Low coverage of preventable diseases, Low skilled human resource especially skilled birth attendants as opposed to delivering mothers attending maternal services from traditional birth attendant and dilapidated health structures due to fighting thus affecting access of essential health care services. In Jonglei State, food insecurity remains high with an estimated 1.4 million (73% of the population) facing Catastrophic and Crisis level (IPC Phase 5 and 4) or worse acute food insecurity. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Reporting of PSEA related incidents will be mandatory through the project cycle. All staff, associates and volunteers should receive regular and refresher training on child safeguarding and PSEA, do no harm, psychological first aid, and risk assessment. Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan is facing; through this protect specific measures and activities will be tailored. Through our child safeguarding policy and other related policies like PSEA will be maintained and always will be referred to for guidance. All staff must sign a code of conduct before providing frontline services and engagement with beneficiaries. Through awareness of other services and referrals will all contribute to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This project is designed observing inclusion of different beneficiaries with every person has equal rights. Production of IEC materials locally translated with pictorial communication to ensure the fraction of illiterates is maximumly involved during project implementation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Hold the Child has a developed culture of engaging the community from the initiation of the project in order for the community to own the project. The community will be consulted and provided with information regarding the project implementation. This will include engaging existing CHD, Community elders, Women, Girls, Youth groups, religious leaders, people with disability and representatives of beneficiaries. HCO will engage communities using appropriate language that the community members understand best to express their concerns, views and feedback. These feedback will form part of the project performance reporting to the health cluster and will help guide the fine tuning of the project to enhance positive beneficiary experience. Hold the Child provides feedback to beneficiaries through weekly and monthly meetings, share weekly and monthly reports with the authorities at community level whereas dissatisfaction by the beneficiaries are channeled through the County Health Department to the SMoH for appropriate corrective measures.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

This project will clearly understands the risk involved in delivery of humanitarian assistance and services to IDPs, hot communities and returnees vulnerable communities. In order to avert the eminent risk identified, Hold the Child will undertake the follow approaches; - Established and operationalize treatment 4 at PHCUs, and 2 mobile clinics - Coordinate activities with other partners and UNDSS to gazette areas suspected to have explosives from access by humanitarian actors and beneficiaries. - Work with the local authorities to ensure service delivery points are well placed out of disputed areas. - Carry out violent prevention through effective communication and community/beneficiary mobilization for services. - Ensure compliance with established tax policy and report to Health Cluster/UNOCHA any intention to Levi tax by authorities in the field outside of the existing policy. - Establish standardized criteria for registration of beneficiaries in IDP or Returnees settings and set up restrictions to regulate registration. - Conduct continuous awareness creation to beneficiaries to indicate availability and service delivery points to survivors of SGBV and support legal assistance services whenever required. - Ensure appropriate data management and reporting systems are in place. Confidential matters will only be reported to specific individuals or institutions having responsibility to handle such matters. - Establish a feedback desk/information desk with free access to beneficiaries to be able to provide feedback or any concerns. - Ensure a mobile clinic services made available to reach out to locations which hard to reach.
