Project Summary

South Darfur is suffering from a protracted humanitarian crisis stemming from conflict exasperated by multiple shocks especially communal conflict, flooding and disease outbreaks. Mercy Corps (MC) plans to implement a multi sectoral response covering WASH, SNFI, Protections, Health and Nutrition.
MC will address WASH needs through rehabilitation of water sources at both community and institutions, formation and training of WASH committees to ensure improved access to clean and safe water. Mercy Corps will then complement the water supply activities through hygiene promotion and train community hygiene promoters who will carry out hygiene promotion awareness raising activities to enhance health and wellbeing of the targeted individuals. During the implementation, MC will ensure COVID-19 prevention measures are intertied to reduce the risk of the spread.
Despite a number of recent peace agreements between the Government of Sudan and armed opposition groups, violent conflict, especially at an intercommunal level, continues to affect the State. In late 2020 and early 2021, intercommunal violence in South Darfur resulted in the displacement of tens of thousands of people. This has compounded an already acute situation for IDPs in the region many of whom have sought refuge in the large IDPs camps which were established at the start of the Darfur crisis in 2004. Through this project, MC will provide ES/NFIs to affected populations inline with SPHERE standards. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) support will be provided during ES/NFI distributions especially in relation to fire and flooding.
Multiple surveys point to extremely poor health indicators within the state especially in relation to morbidity and mortality among children under five and pregnant and lactating women. South Darfur is also vulnerable to disease outbreaks such as Cholera, Malaria, COVID-19 and Chikungunya. The program will aim to support the State MoH to increase the availability of, and access to, the life-saving primary health care services the health centers provide. We will also support the SMoH to improve the availability of quality preventative and curative nutrition services at the targeted PHCCs. These interventions will be supported by awareness-raising activities and promotion of healthy behaviors within the target communities. MC will also support the targeted PHCCs to improve Infection Prevention Control ((IPC) measures at the facilities through a combination of training and the provision of supplies / equipment.
The protracted humanitarian situation within the state has resulted in multiple protection concern. GBV has been identified as a priority concern and MC will ensure the safety, health and dignity of women and girls by provision of quality multi-sectoral GBV response services with a survivor-centred approach and GBV risk mitigation measures. Activities proposed include support to Women and Girls Safe Spaces, under take Protection monitoring activities through missions and remotely through regular communication with the community based protection networks to identify the most vulnerable individuals, protection concerns in the areas of physical safety. Mercy Corps will also establish early warning system within community based protection networks.
MC will deliver a range of nutrition activities targeting children under 5 and pregnant / lactating women (PLW). We will support health facilities with outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) services whilst also training mothers support groups (MSG) to addresses the multi-sectoral causes of malnutrition, such as inadequate diet diversity, poor WASH behaviors, and unsafe food preparation and conservation methods. MC will integrate project activities with respect to nutrition interventions (For example, provision of WASH kits to SAM/MAM children, or referral of SAM/MAM cases to FSL livelihood/GFD response to prevent them from relapsing.)

1 - Needs

The latest Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that about 13.4 million people – 29 per cent of the population – will need humanitarian assistance throughout 2021. This is 4.1 million people more compared to 2020 and an increase of 44 per cent in the year. Overall, the number of people in need in Sudan in 2021 is the highest reported in the past decade (OCHA, 2021 ‘Humanitarian Needs Overview’). South Darfur has been affected by violent conflict since 2004 which has led to mass displacement of the population. Inter-communal violence continues to create new displacement within the state and a number of serious incidents have occurred in the past 6 months within the region (OCHA, 2021 ‘Humanitarian Needs Overview’). According to available data, the total population of the 5 target localities is 726,827 (including refugees). Within the target area there are an estimated 617,014 People in Need (PIN) which represents approximately 85% of the population. Beliel locality has the highest concentration, with 377,636 PIN and over half the total PIN caseload for the 5 localities. The 5 localities have high numbers of IDPs approximately 318,178 in total, with 23,491 returnees . Most of the IDPs are from within the Darfur region especially in and around Jebel Marra. The refugees are predominately from South Sudan and are especially concentrated in Kateila locality. The number of vulnerable residents is also increasing. In 2018, the number of vulnerable residents was recorded as 115,695 while the current estimate for 2021 is now estimated to be 276,809 (38% of the target population). Needs assessments estimate that the five localities have 107,802 people with disabilities and 14,374 elderly people (65+). The nutrition situation in Sudan has been characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and under nutrition since the first S3M survey conducted in 2013. Malnutrition in all its forms is a serious public health challenge in Sudan. When nutritional needs are not met, it is impossible for an individual to achieve optimal physical and mental health. This creates limitations on educational and employment opportunities. The rate of malnutrition in Sudan has changed little in the last three decades, with an estimated 52 per cent of cases living in the nine non-conflict states. Drivers of malnutrition include high income poverty, poor WASH conditions leading to epidemics, limited access to health services, illiteracy and high food prices contributing to inadequate food intake and dietary diversity at household level. Inappropriate feeding practices and cultural norms affect children’s growth and development from an early age. The nutrition statistics for Sudan are estimated as follows, GAM 13.6% at national level; SAM is over 600,000 and MAM is 2.2million based on the updated 2022 HNO analysis. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), over 413,000 people in South Darfur are in need of nutrition support.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will provide vital humanitarian assistance to IDPs, Returnees, Refugees, Host Communities in South Darfur state. The proposed interventions will meet the needs of these groups in terms of WASH, FSL, Protection, ESNFI, Health and Nutrition. Protection monitoring activities through missions and remotely through regular communication with the community based protection networks to identify the most vulnerable individuals, protection concerns in the areas of physical safety, material safety, legal safety, freedom of movement and residence. Protection monitoring findings will be analysed, reflected in regular reports and shared with PWG South Darfur, identified cases will be assisted through this project as well referrals to other services providers and partners. Mercy Corps will adopt a multi-sectoral approach to maximize the impact and added value of the proposed activities. The health, nutrition, protection, WASH, and food security sectors are key interventions that are likely to directly and significantly impact morbidity and mortality in emergency contexts. The protection cluster ensures linkages between the psychosocial services provided through the protection cluster partners and mental health services provided by the health cluster partners. Protection interventions promoting safety have clear links to reducing incidence of violence. Increasing access to health services for survivors of gender-based violence and relevant referrals between the protection and health clusters will ensure that the health consequences (both mental and physical) of gender-based violence are addressed. The health consequences of violence are mitigated through multisectoral service provision coordinated across the two sectors. The shelter and NFI clusters will impact health outcomes. Overcrowded settlements and camps can contribute to an increase in disease, while poor lighting and lack of safety considerations can contribute to risks of sexual violence, and poor site planning for hygiene and sanitation can lead to disease. Infrastructure investments in water and sanitation services play a critical role in multi-sectoral food security programming by addressing root causes of many humanitarian crises that stem from droughts, floods, and natural resource-driven conflict. Integrating WASH activities has many benefits in term of reducing the spread of water borne diseases which in turn has health and nutrition benefits. Mercy Corps will lever this multisectoral approach to enhance the outcomes and impacts of the proposed interventions. Target information GBV - the 5 target localities are suffering from a range of GBV issues including child marriage, child labour, IPV, SGBV, rape, FGM and other harmful practices. An estimated 162,573 PiN are in need of GBV support and assistance within the target localities. Bielel has the highest caseload with 130,948 PiN needing GBV assistance. WASH - the targeted communities suffer from a range of WASH problems including the availability of water, water quality and poor hygiene / sanitation conditions. An estimated 454,975 PiN are in need of WASH services within the target localities. Bielel has the highest caseload with 356,969 PiN needing WASH support. Health - the state has poor indicators in terms of health. Within the 5 target localities, the last nutrition survey in 2018 identified an estimated cases of GAM 85,169. Beliel, As Salam and Kateila localities in particular had high incidents of malnutrition. An estimated 428,659 PiN are in need of Health services within the target localities. ESNFI - protracted and sporadic conflict in the area has created a need for both emergency shelter / NFIs as well as more durable shelter solutions. An estimated 321,119 PiN are in need of Shelter and NFIs within the target localities. Bielel has the highest caseload with 293,901 PiN needing WASH support.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program, Mercy Corpswill abide by Do No Harm principles. MC will also strive for gender balance in therecruitment of staff at all levels and will train officers, responsible for deliveringservices and inputs to participants, on gender issues and gender-sensitive approaches.Mercy Corps’ in-house gender expertise will provide a solid foundation for the program.Recognizing the key role of women in agriculture and household nutrition, a minimumof 40 percent of participants will be women. Specifically under WASH interventions, inthe target locations, water collection is typically the responsibility of women and girls.Limited access to water sources forces women and girls to walk long distances insearch of water, often exposing them to additional protection risks. This interventionwill help to reduce these risks through rehabilitating infrastructure which will increasethe availability of water and reduce the distance women and girls are forced to walk tocollect water. In addition, Mercy Corps will ensure that women and girls are representedin the community-level WASH committees – minimum 50 percent of WASH committeemembers. Mercy Corps plans to encourage the involvement of women and girls in allelements of the community-based operation and maintenance of the water sources.Ongoing discussions with some of the targeted communities have clearly shown thatwomen are more interested than men in taking key roles in the implementation andmanagement of the proposed user fee. As part of the user fee implementation strategy,Mercy Corps will form an advisory board with women taking key decision-makingpositions regarding rates, exemptions and managing collected money.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Mercy Corps places the issue of accountability at the core of this project design,implementation, monitoring and evaluation processes. Ensuring relevant, quality andtimely programming and interventions reflects this commitment. MC considers thetarget communities as leading the solutions for the humanitarian issues they face, andthus are regarded as key stakeholders in the design, implementation and monitoringand evaluation of the project. MC will work through existing community structurescomprising of a representative of women, youth and community leaders to representcommunities during project implementation. Due to the limitation of gatherings asresult of COVID-19, the information will mainly be shared through public media,posters, call centers, community microphone information broadcasts, and the use of apublic address system in the target areas. Information dissemination will be adaptedto the context of COVID-19 to ensure actions contribute to containment. Rather thangathering large groups, some activities will be done through a variety of approaches,including voice messaging, to cater to the vast majority who are illiterate. The projectwill use participatory, gender- and age-sensitive monitoring approaches to ensure girls,boys, women, and men, as well as CBOs and services providers, are involved centrallyin the process of project follow-up, provide their feedback and concerns on the projectperformance and are informed about decisions made in community meetings anddiscussions. MC will provide timely, relevant, and precise information about theorganization including feedback and complaints mechanisms. Communities andbeneficiaries will be kept up to date in changes in feedback mechanisms and will beprovided with information on how they can feedback remotely, such as via hotline,WhatsApp etc. Information sharing will be via different formats to ensure informationis available promptly while taking into account language barriers and literacy status –such as flyers and posters left in communities, health facilities, hospitals etc. withsimple and clear messages/standards and accountability assigned a number to allproject locations. Mercy Corps’ functional Complaints and Response Mechanism willcollect feedback where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, and a dedicated call-innumber – any meetings held (where still possible) will be in open air locations, inspaces large enough for distancing; and otherwise can be conducted remotely.Complaints/feedback will be captured, categorized, and shared with relevant staff forresolution and logged into the action plan trackers. Depending on the type of feedbackand complaint, some will immediately be closed through sharing the requiredinformation (different methods dependent on scale of response – face to face usingdistancing, telephone etc.). At the same time, for some, an investigation will beundertaken to verify the facts before a concrete decision is made.
