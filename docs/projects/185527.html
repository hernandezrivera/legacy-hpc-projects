Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate the impact of the armed conflict and COVID-19 on the populations of the North West and South West regions of Cameroon. Specifically, the project seeks to improve access to housing, land and property for conflict affected populations in Fako, Meme, Ndian and Manyu division in the SW region, and Mezam, Donga Mantung, Bui and Boyo divisions in the NW region. Returnees and host populations will have their capacities enhanced to prevent and address HLP disputes and tensions amongst the displacement affected populations.
This will be achieved through an integrated, multi-sector approach comprising Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA), Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) interventions. Through the ICLA Program, NRC will promote the respect of Housing, Land and Property (HLP) rights to enhance social cohesion among displaced and host communities, train local and regional authorities, traditional leaders and humanitarian actors to address HLP-related disputes through Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR).
NRC will reinforce its response and provide support to displaced populations and vulnerable host communities to access their HLP rights. NRC will deliver information and awareness sessions to IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host communities on HLP issues.
NRC mobile teams will also provide counselling to the above target population in displacement and return areas, including IDP sites and refugee camps. These populations will be informed and assisted in accessing and securing customary and formal rights over HLP assets.
Additionally, NRC will provide legal assistance to conflict-affected population (IDPs, refugees) in need of securing their HLP rights. NRC will assist vulnerable individuals to obtain signed agreements from local authorities and traditional leaders (chief of villages) for beneficiaries at risk of forced eviction. Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. This process will contribute to the prevention of forced evictions.
Furthermore, NRC will build the capacity of local and traditional authorities on HLP issues, including through the provision of training and coaching on HLP rights and CDR (Collaborative Dispute Resolution), to strengthen their HLP response. NRC will also train humanitarian actors operating in sectors such as shelter, WASH, education, and food security. This will help ensure that these sectors prioritize land tenure agreements instead of the direct and customary land transfer in their interventions and prevent or solve land-related conflicts. For due diligence approach, NRC will work with beneficiaries of Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), Shelter, Wash and Education programmes to secure access to land. In order to avoid or reduce forced evictions, NRC will assist Shelter, Wash, LFS beneficiaries to access land with increased security of tenure.
In order to strengthen the coordination and give further visibility to the needs of affected populations with regard to access to land, housing and property, NRC will lead the HLP Area of Responsibility. This will also contribute to an integrated and multi-sectoral response to HLP from national and local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors.
NRC will advocate for the HLP rights of conflict-affected populations through events and round tables with relevant government officials and humanitarian actors at national and regional levels. Similarly, actions plans will be developed to follow-up on the challenges and recommendations identified by NRC’s studies.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The North West and South West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon continue to face a dual emergency; the humanitarian consequences of the socio-political crisis which turned violent in November 2017 and the COVID-19 pandemic which continue to have devastating effects on the lives of people in the two restive regions. The volatile security context and the continues violence are aggravating humanitarian needs as affected people continue to flee their homes, seeking safety in the bushes and neighbouring communities. Continues violence has triggered the internal displacement of 712,800 persons in the North West and South West regions mostly to the West and Littoral regions, while 67,500 more fled to neighbouring Nigeria as refugees, and 333,900 returnees. This has created untold humanitarian needs as 2.2 million people in the two restive regions are affected . Apart from the destruction of houses and looting of property, multi-sectoral needs assessments by INGOs and LNGOs also revealed that incessant attacks on farms continue to scare many farmers and prevent them from cultivating their lands. According to a Rapid Needs Assessment conducted by NRC in September 2020 in eight villages of the South West Region, findings indicated that only 25.9% of people affected by displacement (IDPs) have access to land with a certain level of security of tenure, and 55% of IDPs reported having experienced HLP related conflicts with host communities. The non-access to arable land for displaced persons further increases their need for livelihood as IDPs in 94% of villages assessed following a Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment in August 2021 in the two restive regions reported that farming is a primary occupation for IDPs. Furthermore, 45% of the affected population, mostly displaced persons have difficulties in finding decent and affordable housing, and 70% of the displaced persons likely to suffer forced eviction . An HLP research carried out by NRC in the NWSW in December 2020 finds that the inability of conflict-affected populations to access their HLP rights shapes decision-making about returning to IDPs’ place of origin, and, in some cases, prevents successful return given the inability of displaced populations to return to their house, land or property after displacement. HLP incidents represented 28% of the total number of protection incidents in the NW and SW regions reported by protection monitors in the month of March 2021. Similarly, 70% of cases of denial of resources and opportunities reported in August 2021 were linked to HLP rights violation (i.e. women not allowed to inherit land due to discriminatory customary laws and practices)NRC will undertake the following activities to address the HLP needs of displacement-affected populations in the NW and SW regions identified by this project:Capacity building in HLP rights and CDRInformation and counselling sessions on HLP rightsLegal assistance on HLPAdvocacy for the promotion of the rights of displaced people to HLP rights
