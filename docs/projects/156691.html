Project Summary

The project aims to provide primary health care services in two facilities in the north of Syria (Sharan and Maydan Afrin). with supporting referral of the cases to a higher level of care, the facilities are located in under-served areas, crowded with camps, whose people are suffering from the lack of medical services and are having difficult living conditions; particularly the most vulnerable people (IDPs, women, children and the elderly).
• The two proposed facilities (Sharan and Maydan Afrin) are serving the host and IDPs in the area of northern Afrin, with supporting the referral to higher level of care in the area, and they serve the population with primary health care services, reproductive health care and maternity care at the level of BEmoNc, in addition to introducing free laboratory tests and medication, with the services of EPI program and vaccination under the direct coordination with SIG “Syrian immunization group”led by WHO.

Needs

The health situation in Syria continued to deteriorate. People most at risk in the health sector include people living with chronic diseases, children who are not vaccinated, pregnant women and neonates who have no access to life-saving obstetric care, girls and women with poor access to essential reproductive health care, those in need for mental health and psycho-social support, and survivorsof trauma, including the war wounded. Attacks continued to result in damage and destruction of health infrastructure, as Less than half of Syria’s health facilities are fully operational, and death and injury of health workers causing major disruption and closure of services, limiting the ability of vulnerable people to access essential lifesaving medical assistance and critically affecting service sufficiency ofremaining facilities and availability of staff to provide services (HRP 2018).SEMA's overall Syria strategy is based on the humanitarian response strategy, recognizing the need and the effectiveness of a holistic approach. In response to the needs of conflict-affected Syrians and corresponding to the humanitarian mandate to save lives, alleviate suffering and reduce the economic and social impact of the crisis.

Implementation of activities

1. Provide PHC services to both hosts and IDPs in the targeted areas; Internal, Pediatric, Emergency, Lab, Pharmacy and raising awareness services to strengthen the referral to advanced facilities2. Providing IDPs and the hosts with reproductive health care and maternity care at the level of BEmoNc.

Protection Risk Analysis

SEMA has its own written feedback and explanation mechanism "SAFE" (System for Accountability feedback and explanation) which provides multiple channels for submitted complaints and suggestions from the clients, staff as well, the mechanism has the standard procedures of complaint priority and importance categorizing, as well, a written SOP of investigation committee in place for the critical and high priority complaints to follow up and reporting minutes; critical complaints considered the highest risk which also could be related to PSEA, the SAFE system has tracking mechanism for received complaints and feedback whether through complaint Box, hotline (Whatsapp), Email, and electronic form as well. Additionally, the system has a referral strategy based on the complaints priory and importance to follow up until closing the complaint.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The Points below analyze the main risks, likelihood, impact and mitigation measurements taken by SEMA 1. Resistance or backlash from the community for a woman only space. likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating Measure. Community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leader 2. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility and. Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as a safe entry point and. Engage with local authorities and clear communication on project objectives. 3. Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of quality services also lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: 1. Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor-centered services 2. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral 4- The occurrence of conflict between the host community and displaced due to ethnic differences in the location likelihood: high Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Staff will be recruited from both host community and IDP’s, host community will be strongly encouraged to participate in all activities, SEMA will coordinate with the local council and all other local initiates to promote for the project activities.
