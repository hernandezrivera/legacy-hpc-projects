Project Summary

Continuous breakdown of water facilities and interruption of water supply in host communities of NE Nigeria is a concern as the conflict persist. This development has added pressure on the existing water facilities in host communities that the IDPs are living in, thereby increasing the rate at which these facilities breaks down. The problem of continuous water facility breakdown has remained a concern, considering the growing damages to these assets, which require holistic approach. According to reports from 2019 WASHNORM, water facilities are becoming non-functional at alarming rate, which will affect the achievement of the SDG.
The report further revealed that only 74% of publicly owned water facilities are functional, translating into 26% of publicly owned water facilities being non-functional. About 11% break down in their first year of operation. Of the facilities that are not functional, 56% have been down for more than 6 months.

The data indicate that of the number of people who gained access to safe drinking water in a particular year, about 26% are likely to lose their access within that same year, with a high possibility of not regaining it due to absence of support systems to bring the facilities back into operation.
This means there is need for paradigm shift to move from programmatic approach to community base approach that will enhance ownership and behavioural change of the people.
Host communities are mostly affected; where women and children of school age struggles to look for water including unsafe water in unsafe locations, exposing them to real dangers of harm such as kidnapping, rape, banditry and other violent attacks.
This problem has affected the government’s effort to provide water to its citizenry.
The VLOM approach is the way to go that will get the desired outcome.

2 - Activities

Caritas Nigeria’s methodology include bringing in State Government to own and lead VLOM implementation. Relevant stakeholders will be mobilizedto develop statewide plan to own the vision of VLOM. This action will help imprint the vision of ownership to all Stakeholders at all levels and ensure its sustainability. We will ensure VLOM Institutions, systems and capacities are entrenched at state level and local government levels. 5 VLOM Units will be established/strengthened at State RUWASSAs within the targeted states and 26 VLOMs at the LGAs level (2 in each LGA). They will monitor progress of LAMs and functionalities of the water facilities. They will also be used as pilots, to help setup VLOMs and LAMs in other LGAs not targeted in their respective States. This action will revive water supply in the LGAs to help in reaching the nation’s SDG goals. Local area mechanics with capacities will further be trained to provide paid facilities repair services in their communities.65 Local artisans from the communities with interest and availability will be trained and kitted with tools as Local Area Mechanics for repair and maintenance of broken-down water facilities. One will be selected from each ward through a competitive process. They will be trained and assigned specified water facilities to maintain. The LAM can also charge communities for his services aside the one assigned to. This model will be reproduced across other LGAs that are not target in this project.100 non-functional water facilities will be rehabilitated. Baseline assessment will be conducted to establish depth of rehabilitation required and the facilities. The process will be used as a hands-on training for the LAMs. 60 hand pumps and 40 Motorized water facilities will be rehabilitatedThe LAMs activities in the communities will Sustain and guarantee functionalities of the water facilities. 65 LAMs will be trained to provide this service to their communities and market these skills for commercial purposes too to other communities with nonfunctional water facilities. 975 WaSHCOM members and Water facility caretakers will be identified, selected, and trained in routine maintenance, minor repairs and setting up tariff system, monitoring and reporting and hygiene promotion.This action will provide technical support to technical Staff of Ministry of Water Resources to be involved in monitoring and evaluating the performance of the initiative. 100 MWR/RUWASSA staff will be supported to review and produce VLOM communication tools/IEC to promote facility management and VLOM operations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Caritas Nigeria will mainstream protection activities through the following measures:• Avoid exposing people to further harm and maintain the principle of do no harm.• Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance – in proportion to need and without discrimination.• Protect people from physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion.• Assist people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuseTo achieve the objectives above, Caritas Nigeria will ensure that in all our activities, women, children and persons with special health conditions are given priority as beneficiaries. To this end, children, pregnant/lactating women and persons with special health conditions will be the primary target for acute malnutrition screening that will be done. On the wet feeding , food will be given to all the returnees, IDPs and refugees at the quarantine centers, but we will prioritize distribution of food ration to children, persons living with disabilities/special needs, pregnant/lactating women , the sick and elderly. This will be applicable to beneficiaries of WASH and Nutrition too who will be receiving NFIs. To mitigate sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), all staff will receive Safeguarding/PSEA training/refresher course at the beginning of the project. They will then step-down this training to the returnees, IDPs and Refugees, community leaders and community mobilisers who will then ensure that all beneficiaries are sensitized and educated on their protection rights, possible occasions of violation and how to report in a secure and effective way, using the community based feedback mechanism. They frontline mobilisers, returnees, IDPs and refugee leaders will be trained on how to detect and report occasions of SGBV within the quarantine centers or help refer GVB survivors for immediate assistance. All partners, vendors, contractors and casual workers that would be involved in this project will sign commitment to adherence to standard safeguarding principles and standard code of staff conduct. Informal Safe spaces will be created for mentoring of younger women/girls and children by the older returnees, IDPs and refugees women. They will be encouraged to freely discuss challenges they have faced or are facing, especially with regards to SEA and GBV and child abuse, while in transit or at the quarantine centers.
