Project Summary

Within Salamieh District of Hama Governorate, local markets and economies continue to function despite significant disruptions in supply chains, limited financing, and irregular access to productive assets. Furthermore, the misalignment between labour supply and limited demand has resulted in segments of the population experiencing chronic unemployment. Employment opportunities within vocational trades have emerged as an area of opportunity. Nevertheless, the preparedness of the labour market in terms of skill set has resulted in limited ability to meaningfully engage with these emerging employment sectors. Besides, the enabling environment for business creation has been disrupted since the onset of the crisis.
Despite the multiple layers of challenges placing pressure on local markets, several businesses have emerged filling gaps in social services (e.g. water trucking, communal power distribution) and other market needs (e.g. food services, clothing, etc.). These micro-businesses can capitalize on growing market demand and become important actors within the local labour market. E4R seeks to build the current local economic conditions within Salamieh District to improve the employability of affected people and vulnerable groups and contribute to the creation of local enterprises. The overall objective of this project is to strengthen coping mechanisms of affected people and vulnerable groups, especially women, youth and People with Disabilities (PwD), through vocational training and business development services. During the life of the project, 300 affected and vulnerable people will be reached, of which 45% and 4% will be female and PwD, respectively. The project target is reflective of the number of unique project beneficiaries and has been determined based on the maximum capacity of vocational training courses as well as the number of new enterprises which can be supported during the life of the project.
Specific Objectives:
• Increase access to market-relevant skills/vocational training for 175 affected people and vulnerable groups, especially for women, youth and PwD. All trainees will be equipped with kits and tools related to their respective vocation.
• Improve business development and management knowledge for 300 emerging and existing entrepreneurs. In addition to business to business training, all trainees are required to partake in Life Skills courses which include modules on communication, leadership, personal financial management, conflict resolution.
• Support socio-economic establishment and recovery of 80 micro and small-scale enterprises through grants, loans, and asset replacement with a particular focus on vulnerable groups. Upon completion of the entrepreneurship and business management training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. In total, 80 business plans will be selected and funded with a blend of grants, loans, and business facilitation services.

4 - Needs

13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance – a 21% increase compared to 2020 – with needs increasingly being exacerbated by economic decline. More than 90 % of the population is now estimated to live below the poverty line (2021 Needs and Response Summary Report).ERL needs remain large in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people (51% Female) in need for dignifying early recovery and livelihood support (HNO 2021).As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9% of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only 9.7% can do so. About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost 75% to 100% of their income.Salamieh District has a population of 318,163 people, (54%) females, 36% PwD, 9% IDPs and 0.40% returnees. The living cost increased greatly over the last year as the prices of commodities and services have more than doubled while the average income remained the same making it difficult for vulnerable families to afford minimum requirements.According to AKF Syria’s 2020 Rapid Need Assessment conducted in Salamieh District, a total of 37,261 households/162,995 individuals (50,35% females) are vulnerable; diversity of income sources remains tenuous with the majority of vulnerable households being engaged in seasonal employment and exposed to protection risks, particularly for women.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will provide vocational and skills training opportunities to 175 vulnerable young individuals (40% females) and equip them with kits related to their respective vocation through AKF established Continuing Education Center (CEC) which offers a variety of market-relevant courses.A 2-week course will be developed on entrepreneurship and business management. 300 individuals will be equipped with the knowledge to generate business ideas, start a business, conduct a market analysis, create and accurately price product offerings and maintain basic accounting records. The target group is 175 CEC trainees and 125 vulnerable individuals who express interest in starting a micro-enterprise or seek to grow their current business.The project will launch a micro seed start-up fund designed at increasing access to financial resources for individuals wishing to start or strengthen an enterprise. Upon completing entrepreneurship training, individuals will be invited to submit a business plan for funding consideration. 80 business plans will be funded with a blend of grants and loans in partnership with the First Microfinance Institution (FMFI). AKF will provide a grant through enterprise-related assets by bulk purchasing common equipment and/or disburse grant financing directly to the entrepreneur. These 80 enterprises will be supported with business facilitation services such as mentorship, and legal advice.Process and outcome monitoring activities will include baseline assessments of the target group, rapid labour market assessments, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-vocational training monitoring, labour market transition monitoring, and FGDs with project stakeholders.

A: Gender-related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit the ability of women to participate in some vocations. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium.Selected female trainees will be asked preference to the specific vocation in order to ensure their comfort. AKF will maintain regular monitoring of women contribution along with an active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness-raising or orientation sessions to targeted individuals to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants.B: Women may be hesitant to choose a non-traditional vocation or may not feel confident enough to manage enterprises themselves due to social pressure and cultural perceptions of women knowledge and capabilities in such knowledge which may expose them to exploitation.Likelihood: high, Impact: high.Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against them. Strengthening women decision-making through increasing their access to technical knowledge and promotion for their participation in such vocation. AKF will facilitate gender equality awareness-raising or orientation sessions to targeted individuals to facilitate and promote female participation and capabilities to be involved in non-traditional vocations.C: Increased risk of inter-community tension where CBOs members might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries.Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium.AKF will communicate the project objectives and targeting approach and criteria, oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

There is a risk that the project activity may lead to safeguarding issues and staff or beneficiaries might be exposed to sexual abuse or social stigma. The project activities time and locations might be unsafe for the vulnerable groups, especially for women.In line with AKF safeguarding policy, manual and standards, the project PSEA strategy will ensure the following mitigation measures are in place:• Train all project staff on safeguarding policies.• Ensure all involved partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct• Develop gender-responsive referral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms.• All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies.• Conduct an assessment of safety aspects in spaces used for activities implementation as well as the surrounding environment.The project will adopt measures and mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and hold superiors and subordinates to account alike. AKF follows specific process and procedures to integrate safeguarding in the Project Cycle which are:• Determine expected safeguarding risk using (Risk Register tool)• Determine safeguarding measures to reduce the risks through (mitigation plan and indicators).• Evaluating safeguarding at the project Cycle before, during and after implementing the project, using Project Cycle Checklist.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within Salamieh District is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk2: Misalignment between labour supply and demand widening.Mitigation: Conducting a rapid labour market assessment in order to validate the current and future selection of vocations will mitigate any potential labour market gaps widening. Risk 3: Low desire to start micro-enterprises.Mitigation: Given the context, the decision to start an enterprise takes into account a number of factors. It is possible that individuals who have successfully completed the entrepreneurship and business management training may wish to not start an enterprise.
