Project Summary

This project aims to provide learning opportunities to promote school enrollment, increase attendance and retention through community mobilization and engagement and building the capacities of education actors (teachers) and communities (PTA) in protecting the rights of children for education in this humanitarian crisis. The project will target 10,500 beneficiaries (male 5,550; female 5,000). Of this target, 10,210 are children (male 6,500; female 5,500), who will directly benefit from the education services, including Early Childhood Development, during the project period.
The project will also provide safe, inclusive and protective learning spaces, environmentally friendly and local building materials will be used where possible, to minimize environmental impact) with gender segregated WASH facilities. Additionally, supporting recruited EiE volunteer teachers through incentives, according to recent national and cluster guidelines and standards. The project will give special attention to girls’ education as their enrollment rate is very low and the dropout rate is very high in the country. This vulnerable segment of the society will be primary targets of the project as they are victims of harmful traditional and cultural practices. Teaching and recreational supplies will be distributed and psychosocial support will be provided. Teachers will be trained in using the Skills for Life Kit. Teachers, education officials, and MoEGI staff will be trained on education in emergency and Code of Conduct. PTAs and SMCs will be trained on school monitoring and action planning.
The targeted IDPs, returnees, and host communities will receive orientation on how to share limited resources, child protection, and GBV prevention and response in emergencies. Schools and communities will be sensitized concerning sanitation, the prevention of disease transmission, and HIV/AIDS. All schools will be supported to become safe and inclusive spaces where children, parents, and the community can receive improved psychological awareness for other possible crises.

1 - Needs

The conflict in South Sudan has had an impact on an already fragile public education system and impacted the education of school-aged children (3-18 years of age). In addition, a recent report on Out of School Children; authored jointly by the UNESCO Institute for Statistics, the Ministry of General Education and Instruction of South Sudan and UNICEF, with financial support of Global Partnership for Education (GPE), reveals that at least 2.2 million children in South Sudan are not receiving an education – representing one of the highest rates of out-of-school children in the world, Years of conflict, displacement and economic collapse continue to deprive children of education, harming the future of the country. Huge numbers remain internally displaced in United Nations Protection of Civilian (PoC) sites. Literacy rates are among the lowest in the world. The impact of the conflict has not only been on the visible school structures but also the minds of young children who suffered enough during the peak of the crises and recurring and prolonged hardships. Within the assessed schools, 40% of school-age children are learning under trees or open-air which not conducive for learners. Most of teachers’ in assessed schools are unqualified teachers with low capacity to teach the new South Sudan Curriculum. (OCHA Field monitoring visit report – September 2019 in Koch). Children and youth have been made increasingly vulnerable, as they have had to discontinue their academic activities to search for food and safety, with increased exposure to protection risks such as sexual violence, child labor, early marriage, and recruitment into armed groups. The low levels of education attainment among children has contributed to a trends of high levels of street children, child marriages, child pregnancies, gender-based violence, domestic violence against child and adolescent health indicators. WRSS is currently assisting in Bentiu PoC, Koch, Guit, Fangak, and Ibba Counties for multi-sectoral response to humanitarian needs. This response includes education, but the projects are limited, the needs are significant, and the context continues to change, offering additional opportunities for response. With years of static presence in these locations and ongoing similar programming, WRSS has a unique understanding of the local contexts and needs of the communities, particularly towards education. WRSS has been present in Koch since 2005 and has active education projects that need to continue, based on the needs and that only 14 out of 57 primary schools are open for 2019. Lack of WASH facilities and psychosocial support is a gap in some of the schools. School aged children and youth are loitering in the communities because of lack of adequate infrastructures and resources for quality education services. In Bentiu PoC, classrooms are overcrowded due to increased IDPs, most households remain in the PoC monitoring the security situation in their home areas. The classroom teacher to learner ratio is approximately 1:125, which has negatively affected classroom management. In New Fangak, many children miss school due to lack of conducive learning environment and few teachers in schools. For girl children in particular, early and/or forced marriages of girls and the burden of domestic chores at home cause them to drop out, and only 42% of pupils currently enrolled in WRSS-supported school are girls. In Mvolo County, only 10 of 45 schools are functioning and some areas continue to experience conflicts, mostly related to cattle raiding. Only 36% of the 3,371 children enrolled are girls. The ratio of teachers to learners is 1:65 and many teachers are unable to manage their classrooms due to the sizes but also inadequate knowledge and skills in classroom management. In Ibba County, only about 47% of school aged children are enrolled in school and a meager 20% of those enrolled are girls.

2 - Activities

Through the HRP 2020, this project will significantly contribute to the planned Education Cluster response modalities: 1. Construction/rehabilitation of learning spaces and classrooms in line with INEE-Minimum standards for education in emergencies. 15 TLS will be established or rehabilitated with environmentally friendly materials, along with 15 Gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities for children and youths. 15 Gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities for children and youths will be constructed. The learning spaces and classrooms will be improved in order to contribute to a safe and protecting learning environment. 2. Support ECD centres with learning and play materials and ECD being integrated into nutrition OTP sites to save mothers’ time as mothers often don’t have the time or knowledge to best stimulate brain development for their infants and young children. In partnership with UNICEF, the play and learning materials will be packaged to an ECD kit and then distributed to the ECD centres to enable schools/ECD centers be able to reopen at the earliest time after being affected by conflict. Some ECD play materials will be improvised locally within the schools e.g. swings using locally available materials. Provision o10 TLS will be established or rehabilitated with environmentally friendly materials. 3. Mobilize 150 EiE volunteer teachers and provide monthly incentives. 4. Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children. Teachers will be trained in using the Skills for Life Kit and trained on psycho-social support. Additionally, teachers will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy, and teaching methodologies. Teaching and recreational supplies will be distributed. 5. Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management governance: Training on school management will be carried out in Bentiu PoC, Koch, Guit, Fangak, Ibba and Mvolo. This will include School Management Committees, Parent Teachers Associations and Board of Governors of primary schools in order to promote and improve the quality of school management. SMCs, PTAs, and community leaders will be equipped and involved in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). Community-based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCMs) will be set up for SEA and protection of children. IDPs and host communities will receive orientation in peacebuilding and trauma healing activities, how to share limited resources, child protection and GBV prevention and response in emergencies. With poor pupil enrollment, attendance, and retention in the school system, parents will be provided with adult literacy classes to improve children's enrollment, as the high illiteracy rate in parents has contributed towards the lack of value given to children's education. 6. Provide support to National examination of PoC children and hard to reach areas through delivery of examination scripts and facilitate invigilators.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiaries’ rights as citizens of South Sudan are recognized and respected in the implementation of all project activities.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Projects are planned in locations in which local populations are affected by conflict and which host IDPs (including camp settings). Activities are targeted at reaching displaced and host populations alike. Currently, despite much speculation about returns, populations in targeted areas remain very transient between home areas and areas of displacement or camps. With static presence and programming in all three locations, WRSS is in a unique position to monitor any influx of returnees and to respond to their humanitarian needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

WRSS maintains ongoing coordination with government line ministries, local Relief and Rehabilitation Authorities, the County Commissioners, and with camp coordination in Bentiu PoC. Capacity-building activities, especially for line ministry staff are key to program implementation and long-term sustainability of project activities. WRSS reaches communities directly through needs assessments and surveys as well as through community meetings and feedback mechanisms at project sites. Community members continue to stress the need for assistance with a commitment to participate in project activities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

All project activities are designed in accordance with national guidelines where they exist, particularly for education, as well as with UN agency guidelines, where relevant. Anticipating additional IDP and returnee beneficiaries during the project period, the project will follow all relevant national strategies for these population groups.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The ongoing peace process within the country is expected to create a more stable environment for implementation of humanitarian activities. WRSS works closely with local authorities to ensure humanitarian access, negotiate safe access to project sites, and to report and address violations when they occur. WRSS also links with Protection actors in all project locations and trains staff on pathways through which to refer beneficiaries who are victims of SGBV or other violence.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

WRSS will link short-term relief activities to development as much as possible. WRSS will aim to make beneficiaries and communities more resilient through key inputs and emphasis on self-reliance, to the extent that it is possible, in each context. WRSS recognizes the need to involve and build community leadership and ownership of programs from the beginning in order to increase sustainability, as opposed to trying to quickly hand over responsibility at the end of a grant. WRSS will involve local leaders and beneficiaries through quarterly stakeholder meetings throughout the life of the project, with the intent of familiarizing stakeholders with the project management and easing the closeout and transition. • Collaboration and Capacity Building: WRSS will support partner organizations in the project through appropriate responsibilities, relevant trainings, and empowerment. WRSS has promoted national staff into management positions to promote greater national and local leadership. WRSS will also work closely with the Ministry of Education at all levels and SMCs/PTAs, with the goal of hand over after the project.• Full-user Participation: Community participation implies a proactive process in which the beneficiaries influence the development and management of projects rather than merely receiving a share of project benefits. Community participation creates an enabling environment for sustainability by allowing users, as a group, to select the level of services for which they are willing to pay (for example, the case of improved water sources), to guide key investment and management decisions, and to make choices and commit resources in support of these choices.• Coordination through Clusters: WRSS will participate with partners in all relevant humanitarian coordination mechanisms for information sharing, needs assessment, activity planning, and materials distributions to ensure complementarity and prevent overlap.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes the risk of beneficiaries, especially women and girls being targeted with sexual or physical violence by armed groups on the way to and from accessing services. Gender-based violence has unfortunately been increasing in South Sudan, having been sanctioned by certain armed groups as a tactic of war. WRSS is serious about the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), including the demands for sex in exchange for goods or services. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. Staff are also trained on referral pathways for beneficiaries who identify as survivors of gender-based violence.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

A variety of feedback mechanisms are used to give beneficiaries a choice in how to express concerns and to ensure that vulnerable community members have access to a forum for giving feedback. First, comment boxes and/or complaint desks are made available at implementation sites to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback directly to WRSS in a confidential manner. Secondly, volunteers are able to receive feedback from participants one-on-one during home visits in settings where participants may feel safer sharing their comments in the privacy of their own home. This also ensures that elderly, disabled, or other people confined to their homes or with difficulties visiting project sites have the opportunity to submit concerns. Thirdly, beneficiaries will be informed of their right to submit complaints during community-level meetings in case they feel more comfortable in a group setting or directly to a World Relief Area Coordinator at a project site.
