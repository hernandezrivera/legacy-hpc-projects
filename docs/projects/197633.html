Project Summary

Through the proposed project, SIF will directly contribute to strategic objectives of Education Sector for Syria through providing safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education (NFE) for out-of-school children (OOSC) and children at-risk of dropping out both within and outside public schools providing a path to enter formal education.
Proposed locations were identified according to the severity of needs: 4% of targeted locations are in scale 2 as SIF considered to target Hisn in Homs and Ariqa in Sweida since the needs assessments conducted by SIF MEAL team involving key informants and SIF work in the localities reported to have a large population of OoSC (Self-Learning Programme); 50% are in scale 3, 37% are in scale 4 and 9% are in scale 5; their level of accessibility, and SIF experience. The comprehensive, multisector and multiyear approach was the determining element in the selection of the locations for 2023, with the aim of promoting sustainability of interventions, community self-reliance and resilience.
In line with the results of consultations with local community and authorities, and taking into account sector priorities, proposed intervention will contribute to enhance access to quality education and teaching for 36,863 in 2023, with an estimated 5% of target Children with Disability. Response will seek to address education needs of children currently not in school and those at risk of dropping out through:
1. Supporting the learning access of OoSC: 4,345 children will be supported through the Self-Learning Programme in 2023
2. Encouraging the retention of children in education: 6,205 students will be supported through remedial education in 2023
3. Establishing Early Childhood Education programme for 320 preschool children in 2023 in PHC/community centers
4. Enhancing safe access to school through rehabilitating 9 damaged schools in 2023
5. Strengthening teachers’ capacity: 390 teachers will be trained on professional development in 2023
6. Promoting the engagement of community in the education process through supporting 30 Parents and Teacher Association
7. Encouraging children to enroll into formal education through reaching out to 6,000 children and 4,150 parents - Back-to-Learning campaigns.
The estimated cost per beneficiary in 2023 is estimated to be $105.
SIF has ongoing education programmes in Daraa and Homs and has previously implemented education activities in Hama, Rural Damascus and Quneitra. SIF has a field office and intervenes in Aleppo. Regarding Deir-Ez-Zor and Sweida, SIF is currently in communication with local authorities and partners to gain access in these locations.
This proposed project will be part of a Protection, Shelter, WASH and Early Recovery and Livelihood integrated programme which will include the rehabilitation of vocational training schools, implementation of PSS activities, case identification and referral to specialized services. Girls and boys will also be provided with awareness sessions on Child Protection concerns, including gender-Based Violence and risk education. COVID 19 Prevention measure will be strictly followed.
Safeguarding and protection principles including the Do No Harm approach will guide SIF team throughout all phases of project cycle. SIF is fully committed to the SPHERE standards and humanitarian principles.
Accountability to affected population will be realized through strengthening child-friendly community-based complaint mechanisms, improved participatory assessments and enhanced outreach through SIF MEAL team.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with various stakeholders on the ground for an effective response, including Ministry of Education, Education sector, NGOs, contractor, SARC, local communities in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services.

3 - Needs

Access to education and teaching quality in Syria have significantly deteriorated as a result of the crisis. Findings from the Education Sector for 2023 showed that nearly one out of five children is out-of-school. In addition, a large percentage of educational facilities were either destroyed or used for purposes other than education, as a result, most of the schools are congested and overcrowded. Most of the families reported high costs of school stationeries for children. Coupled with scarcity and unaffordability of transportation, this is further dissuading parents from sending their children to school. Other negative coping mechanisms, such as early marriage, child labour and violence both at school and home, are further limiting children access to education. In this context, girls and female adolescents are particularly subject to gender-based violence. Moreover, children and youth with disabilities face additional challenges to access basic services, including education, and are exposed to higher risks of violence and other protection issues. Access to education is also discriminatory as children with disabilities are facing more difficulties in being integrated in the community while most of the schools are not equipped with disable-friendly facilities or sex-disaggregated WASH facilities. Teaching quality has drastically decreased as most of the teachers and education personnel are no longer in their teaching posts which is effecting learning outcomes of students.

4 - Implementation of activities

SIF will implement NFE activities in coordination with MoE in public schools after school hours, and in community centers throughout the week. OoSC will be reached through the BTL campaign implemented in collaboration with SARC. Children aged 3-5 will benefit from Early Childhood Education in community centres and schools. TPA will be supported in the targeted learning spaces. SIF will conduct regular tests of the children to evaluate their initial level and progress. Developping a Monitoring Programme Plan will ensure a coordinated and focused approach to monitoring, and track proper use of resources. It will include specific measures by which the beneficiaries are able to provide feedback on programme implementation. Activities and child progress will be also monitored on a regular basis by SIF through field visits using tracking tools (CLM). Intervention will include the rehabilitation of schools and WASH facilities adapted to CwD and gender-based specific needs by contractors selected by SIF as part of a transparent and accountable tender process. SIF will coordinate with MoE and ensure that a period of guarantee is included in the contracts for sustainability. Participatory approach will be implemented through structured dialogues with groups of different ages, gender and backgrounds particularly those with special needs on issues to ensure that any protection concerns are addressed. Close coordination with sector will be kept and reporting ensured (4Ws and FTS).

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Education personnel and students participate in physical punishment or bullying of students. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: 1) establish a code of conduct in learning facilities; training and prevention 2) provide PSS activities in learning spaces. 2. Risk of sexual abuse and GBV. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: 1) application of Child Safeguarding measures: recruitment of staff according to the safe process 2) training of all staff and associates 3) signature of SIF code of conduct by SIF staff, volunteers and third parties 4) establishment of effective child-friendly complaint mechanism 5) staff training on PSEA, CP and GBV 3. Risk of children and adults’ exposure to physical or verbal harassment and unexploded ordnances on the way to centers and schools. Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigation: 1) safety assessment conducted by SIF team in centers and schools’ surroundings prior to beginning 2) coordinate with other actors in the area for risk assessment 3) ensure that unexploded ordnances’ clearance is conducted 4. Risk: Community members or local authorities attempt to exert undue influence or to change the beneficiaries list or the type of support provided Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: 1) Prorgramme designed to be context and conflict effective 2) Community and local authorities involved in the whole cycle of the project and close coordination is kept throughout project life.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Sexual exploitation of community members, including children, in return of promise for assistance. Mitigation measures include the following: (1) SIF ensures that specific PSEA indicators are included in the monitoring framework.(2) Vetting process is conducted during the recruitment of a candidate at application and reference stages. (3) The code of conduct, which includes the strict prohibition of SEA, is signed by everyone working for and with SIF, including staff, SIF volunteers and contractors. After the selection of the contractors, as the same time of the signature of the purchase order, the contractor signs the SIF Code of conduct which is then attached as an annex to the contract/agreement.(4) SIF employees are trained on PSEA and a PSEA focal point is assigned in all governorates targeted by SIF. Awareness sessions of the community members on PSEA are conducted every year. Sessions includes the definition of the what is SEA and channels of reporting/whistleblowing. (5) SIF community-based complaint mechanisms is accessible to affected populations in all response locations. (6) A process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of allegations of SEA has been developed by SIF.
