Project Summary

The COVID-19 outbreak pandemic affected the entire food system in the country. This included all elements of the food system, from primary supply, to processing, to trade as well as logistics systems. In response to the outbreak, FAO along with other humanitarian organizations in Cox’s Bazar are working closely with the government of Bangladesh to provide effective support to the agri-food system at different levels: enhance agricultural production for the marginal and smallholder farmers, cash transfers to the poor families to improved households’ nutrition and transportation support for the aggregation centers engaging youth as local service provider.

Objectives

Secure the continuity of the food supply chain by supporting the food production system, aligning with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and Ministry of Fisheries and Livestock (MoFL) policies and directives in order to ensure continuity of crop, fish and livestock production and marketing throughout the year.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar is a district whose Bangladeshi population has had, and continues to have, limited socio-economic opportunities that were connected to traditional agricultural production systems. About 75% of the households cultivate cereal crop i.e. rice and maize as the staple food, of which two rice growing seasons are rainfed aman rice (i.e. June to November) and irrigated boro rice (November to April). In addition, six different varieties of vegetables are commonly grown in all seasons in the fields and homestead areas. Due to the COVID-19 impact the marginal and smallholder farmers’ farming practices in growing rainfed aman rice (12,000 ha of lands) will be severely affected. Because seeds supply system will be completely disrupted in the present lockdown system. A large number of farmers will have no cash in hand for meeting the labor, seeds, fertilizer and irrigation costs. Similarly, the smallholder and marginal farmers cannot grow vegetables in the field as well as in homestead in the next winter season due to unavailability of vegetable seeds and shortage of cash in hand. FAO also intends to support irrigated winter rice season (i.e. boro rice), where 55% farmers depend on high breed rice seeds will face inadequate supply of seeds due to COVID-19 impact. The scarcity of rural labor is a trigger for cost-effective, mechanized cropping that also now provides a key entry point for agricultural intensification, post-harvest management and sustainability of the resource base through conservation agriculture. FAO intends to support the farmers especially rice growers, as well as all other arable crops and extend the geographic focus for farm mechanization in this regard. Fisheries sector in Cox’s Bazar is extremely important for its contribution to food security, livelihoods and overall poverty reduction. About 28% of total employment in Cox’s Bazar comes from fishing and related activities. In this context, FAO and the DoF intend to work collaboratively with other development partners to extend support to the vulnerable fishing communities through a holistic, multi-sectoral programming approach including increased safety at sea and alternative livelihood activities. Moreover, FAO will provide support to the poultry and cattle farmers to meet their immediate feed needs while restoring their farm production and adapt COVID-19 coping mechanism for animal health, nutrition and infrastructure. Targeting: The project primarily addresses the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on the livelihoods and natural resource base of 125,000 vulnerable beneficiaries (of which, 47% women) comprised of 25,000 farming households of the host communities in the targeted areas (Five Sub-districts i.e. Cox’s Bazar Sadar, Ramu, Ukhiya, Teknaf and Chakaria). Women and youth in agricultural production and supply chain: In addition, the project will work closely with the public and private sectors to identify market opportunities and strategies to expand crop production into new and underdeveloped areas with the potential to increase household incomes, improve nutrition and contribute to greater economic empowerment of women. DRR and Environmental Rehabilitation: FAO will continue to address the increased vulnerability to floods, drought and cyclone and play a major role in supporting the host communities in developing climate resilient food security for the poor farmers together with government and non-government partners, including other UN agencies. Accountability to Affected People: FAO will continue to utilize its existing internal systems of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure that all activities incorporate measures of information sharing and recognition of vulnerable groups, as well as grievance gathering and address.

Activities or outputs

Output 1: Enhance rice-based crop intensification systems to improve livelihoods for the COVID-19 affected vulnerable farmers in addressing government policy Activity 1.1: Provide seeds support to 20,000 rice growing farming families for growing irrigated winter boro rice in 12,000 hectares of land. This activity will contribute removal of critical market based barrier to grow staple food and ensure adequate supply to meet the local market demand. Activity 1.2: Provide winter vegetable seeds support to 20,000 farming families (including marginal and landless) for cultivating vegetables at homestead and field. This will increase households’ nutritional status, especially for women and children and income from surplus sale. Activity 1.3: Cash transfer support to 20,000 farming families for meeting the crop production cost i.e. labor, irrigation and fertilizer and direct nutrition support by purchasing of foods for marginal and landless households. This cash transfer activity will ensure households’ food security for poor families and support subsidized crop production costs for marginal and smallholder farmers. Activity 1.4: Farm Machinery support to 100 farmers’ groups to direct support 2,000 faming families and indirectly 3,000 beneficiaries for plowing, irrigation and crop threshing. This activity will promote farmers’ adoption to modern farming technology, access to productive assets, reduce crop production cost and meet agricultural labour scarcity. Output 2: Enhance pond aquaculture in fish production and reduce risks in capturing fish from open water sources Activity 2.1: Provide fish feed support to 1,000 fish farmers for producing culture fish through practicing pond aquaculture. This activity will contribute in removal of market barrier in increasing fish production and ensure local market demand for fish supply. Activity 2.2: Provide safety at sea equipment (i.e. life jacket, flash light, first aid kits) support to 1,000 fisher folk in order to reduce risks and loss of death for fishing communities in capturing marine fish. Activity 2.3: Cash transfer support to 2,000 fisherfolk and fish farmers for meeting the fish production cost i.e. fish feed and direct nutrition support by purchasing of foods. This activity will contribute in fish production systems and ensure households’ food security for poor fishing community people. Output 3: Enhance poultry rearing and cattle farming through engaging women for increasing households’ nutrition and income Activity 3.1: Provide poultry feed support to 2,000 poultry farmers (i.e. households, small and medium farms) for producing eggs and meats. This activity will contribute in removal of market barrier in increasing eggs and meats production and meet the local market demand in supplying of animal sourced protein. Activity 3.2: Provide cattle feed support to 1,000 cattle farmers (i.e. households, small and medium farms) for producing milks and meats. This activity will contribute in removal of market barrier in increasing milks and meats production and meet the local market demand in supplying of animal sourced protein. Activity 3.3: Cash transfer support to 3,000 poultry and livestock farmers for meeting the poultry and cattle rearing costs (i.e. feed, medicine, vaccines etc.) and direct nutrition support by purchasing of foods for marginal and landless households. This cash transfer activity will ensure households’ food security for poor families and support subsidized livestock production costs. Output 4: Support rural market system engaging youth in agricultural value chain Activity 4.1: Provide battery power operated 100 rickshaw vans to the Local Service Providers (LSPs) or market aggregators in carrying agricultural goods from farmers’ houses to market and transport agricultural inputs (i.e. seeds, fertilizers, fish and livestock feed) from market to farmers’ door steps. This activity will contribute in employment generation for youth.
