Project Summary

The proposed activities under this action will address access to food sources which will impact on both food insecurity and thus dietary uptake. The FSL approach adopted by GOAL recognizes that food security cannot be treated as a basic need, separate from wider livelihoods considerations. Households may go hungry in order to preserve seed, tools, animals or other assets. GOAL plans to provide emergency FSL support to conflict-affected households, including IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host households, in three localities in South Kordofan: Reif el Shargi (1230 households), Rashad (900 households), and Talodi (290 households). FSL activities will be integrated with Health, Nutrition, and WASH interventions in the same localities implemented by GOAL and/or partners (including the IMC SHF Consortium in Reif el Shargi), and closely coordinated through the FSL sector and directly with other partners to ensure complementary approaches with no overlapping locations of intervention. GOAL's FSL intervention will focus on agriculture and livestock activities, including seed and tool voucher fairs for agricultural inputs for both cereal and winter-season (vegetable) crops; goat restocking activities providing local female and improved-species male goats to increase assets, improve milk consumption, and small income; and in some cases support women to start VSLA groups to better manage imporved inocmes. In Talodi, GOAL will also pilot support for beekeeping, a common activity in the locality but damaged by conflict and economic crisis; the pilot aims to restore this as a viable economic activity for vulnerable households.
In North Darfur, GOAL will target 18,263 direct beneficiaries comprising of IDPs, rural underserved communities, nomadic pastoralists and host communities experiencing protracted crisis. Activities will complement the ongoing Health and Nutrition as well as WASH interventions, holistically addressing the ongoing critical situation in Kutum and Al Waha localities. Improving access to food sources and food availability will be provided by increasing access to locally adaptable improved crop seeds and varieties through the implementation of seed voucher and fairs as an alternative seed distribution approach (seed fairs have been acknowledged as one of the best mechanisms to assist farmers in emergency and early recovery situations, such as currently in North Darfur). Besides, vegetable seeds for micro gardens will be distributed to vulnerable households attending nutrition supplementary feedings programmes. In Darfur, it is noted that for many households livestock plays an important role in promoting food security by diversifying food sources and providing a potential source of cash income. The importance of small ruminants in improving food security among women and children is especially great. Therefore, GOAL proposes to distribute small ruminants (goats) as well as chickens to target communities. GOAL will also work with vulnerable and disempowered women to provide training, support and mobilisation in livelihoods diversification and income generation through implementation of Village Saving and Loan Association (VSLAs). GOAL will also continue to support Nursery sites located in strategic locations that were established in 2018.

Needs

The protracted Darfur crisis has resulted in the abandonment of land and traditional coping strategies. In 2012, more than half of the people were dependent on food aid as a means of survival. The 2016 MICS showing 85.1% of the population depending on food bought from the market, this may not necessarily mean that they had enough food available but could instead be linked to the reduction of food aid available through WFP and own food production in general. In 2019, Kutum locality remain a priority locality for humanitarian intervention. Findings from the SMOA and WFP comprehensive food security assessment carried out showed that the highest percentage of food insecure households is found in North and West Darfur (23 percent each). Moreover, about one-third of households in North Darfur were vulnerable to food insecurity at the time of the survey. Darfur farmers severely face lack of technological options, poor support services such as agricultural and livestock extension service. Agriculture and livestock research institutions are weak and underfunded with weak planning capacity,. In South Kordofan, GOAL plans to implement FSL programming in Reif el Shargi, Rashad, and Talodi localities. Elrif el shargi has a total population of 31,174 and over 33% of the population falls under IPC phase 3 and 4 (and for this reason was targeted under SHF's 3rd allocation in 2018), and GOAL's needs assessment and stakeholder consultations have revealed that many households in Reif el Shargi, Talodi, and Rashad have lost livelihoods due to conflict. The main crops planted in South Kordofan are staple cereal crops, namely sorghum and millet, and groundnuts, sesame, maize, and cowpeas are also grown for consumption and cash. Staple cereal crop harvests in South Kordofan for the 2018-19 season were down by 20% from 2017-18, returning to typical levels during the years of active conflict. GOAL’s needs assessments identified two main factors behind this. First is poor rains, highlighting the vulnerability of small-scale farmers to weather. Second is lack of access to inputs or land, which limited the amount of land that was planted in 2018. The high price of fuel, and lack of access to cash to pay labor, was a barrier to farmers to planting and harvesting. In Rashad, up to 150,000 acres were not cultivated due to the lack of improved seeds, fuel, finance, or land still being too insecure to access. GOAL’s assessment found farmers did not harvest enough to meet their families’ needs through next year, meaning not only will they be in critical need of support during the lean season but. Not only have many host communities lost land, but also Talodi’s many IDPs largely do not have access to agricultural land, and must rely on these other strategies, which are constrained as well by both security and economic crisis. Residents in Talodi locality have also struggled to rebuild other livelihood strategies as well such as beekeeping.

Activities

- Seed and tools fair for cereal (rainfed) crops and winter (vegetable) crops- Support for community extension workers to provide extension sessions for supported farmers- Goats restocking for IDPs and vulnerable host populations, including local female species and improved male goats, all vaccinated and treated for common diseases. First generation of live offspring for each female will be passed to another vulnerable family, doubling the impact. Beneficiaries will be selected to link with nutrition programme (MAM / SAM case) as well as other vulnerability factors determined by the community. The main purpose of goat restocking activities is to support diet diversity and nutrition of the targeted households. Income from goats is a secondary objective.- Distribution of chickens including local hens and improved-species roosters, as well as training for women on feeding, housing, to restore nutrition-sensitive livelihoods- Support and training for Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) in Kutum and Al Waha localities- Nurseries support: rehabilitate and support inputs for 6 nurseries, and distribute at least 6000 seedlings in IDP settlements near Kutum (Kassab and Fata Borno)- Create and support VSLA groups reaching IDP and other vulnerable women to support household saving and improved financial management- Provide vocational training to women in VLSA groups, and provide small grants for income-generating activities (IGAs), in order to increase incomes for vulnerable women- Beekeeping pilot for 90 beekeeping households, part of the host community. The pilot will provide improved training, equipment, and income-generating techniques to restore this livelihood option
