Project Summary

The project is designed to include the 4 protection principles:
- Avoid exposing people to further harm as a result of your actions.
- Ensure people’s access to impartial assistance.
- Protect people from physical and psychological harm due to violence or coercion.
- Assist with rights claims, access to remedies and recovery from abuse
The four Protection Principles apply as much to specialist protection activity as to general humanitarian action, though the activities may be different. The protection-related activities of all humanitarian agencies can be classified broadly according to the following three modes of activity, which are inter-dependent and may be carried out simultaneously:
Preventive: Preventing physical threats or rights abuses from occurring or reducing exposure or vulnerability to such threats and abuses.
Preventing protection threats also includes efforts to foster an environment conducive to respect for the rights of women, men, girls and boys of all ages in accor-dance with international law.Responsive: Stopping ongoing violations by responding to incidents of violence and other rights abuses.Remedial: Providing remedies to ongoing or past abuses, through repara-tion and rehabilitation, by offering healthcare, psychosocial support, legal assistance or other services and supports, and helping the affected population to access available remedies and claim their rights.
Advocacy, whether public or private, is a common element linking these three modes of activity. The threats to the affected population arise from deliberate decisions, actions or policies and many of the related protection responses are about attempting to change such behaviours and policies. Advocacy by humani-tarian agencies and others, such as human rights organisations, is central to the attempt to influence such change. There may be tensions for humanitarian agencies between ‘speaking out’ about abuses and the need to maintain an operational presence, and these tensions may dictate whether and how they can undertake advocacy on a given issue.Where advocacy is pursued, its success generally depends on access to reliable evidence, stakeholder analysis and thorough context analysis. It is thus linked to the assessment standard in the Core Standards.
Beneficiaries and locations:
Comprehensive report of the recent humanitarian actors' joint rapid assessment mission to East Jabal Marrah indicated the miserable situation of 78,300 IDPs spread in the bush of Dirbat: 47,000 person, Jawa: 29,101 and Soni: 2,214 lacking the minimum protection standard.
This project is designed to immediately respond to the NFIs and shelter needs.
Despite of the signature of the Peace Process in Juba, difficulties to access the areas and the high risk, GFO will used its communal relationship to implement this project.
Thes project will contribute to reducing the impact including morbidity and mortality of conflict and disasters on vulnerable boys, girls, women and men in East Jabal Marrah through timely and effective assessments, appropriate responses and enhanced coordination among humanitarian actors with regards to NFI/ES needs.
It is anticipated that 2021 will see continued high levels of humanitarian needs, increased protection risks, and further aid restrictions across East Jabal Marrah.

1 - Needs

The Inter Agency joint rapid assessment mission in East Jabal Marrah (Sharg El Jabal) locality discovered the huge number of displacement of population due to the conflict between the opposition fractions which resulted to many livelihood aspects, including long living in outreached around the main cities.This situation created fragility and poorness among population. Security in the area remain unpredictable but accessible to humanitarian actors. Before last year 2019, East Jabal Marrah was totally NO GO area to Humanitarian actors and due to the long cut of humanitarian services and the continuous fightings between the armed groups, IDPs witnessed high range of lack of means of living including shelter, education, health. According to the Rapid Need-based assessments November 15th, 2020 . Finding of these assessments and the Sector Indicator allow GFO to add ress the needs of identified locality target. 79,214 individuals PiN are found in need of NFI kits and environment shelters IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities are found totally depending on the grass and wood material for shelters and NFIs including jerrycans, blanket, cooking sets and mosquito net beside the emergency shelter kits. 23,000 individuals are requiring immediate NFI ditribution for emergency, 2000 HH including PWD HH, Women Headed Household, Children headed HH requiring emergency shelter and 1,000 HH sick for shelter rehabilitation of cash voucher. 200 youth, including boy and girl and training in SSB making technique. Vocational masonry training to 100 to undertake reconstruction of new shelter to give them opportunity for IGA for sustainability. Provision of preposition kits to 200 households to maintain effectively the project results, training of implementing staffs will be organized for monitoring and reporting, also reorganization of participation of women in the leaders' committees will strengthen accountability of the project. As of Covid 19 pandemic is now a threat world wide, the project will considerate the prevention of the affected community through conducting awareness session, social separation during activities to avoid crowd,

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

NFIs for humanitarian are mainly plastic, including plastic sheets, Jerrycans, plastic mats impacted badly in the environment. During the distribution, beneficiaries are given awareness on how they will use in items and where they should put them when they are useless to avoid environment pollution. Beneficiaries are aware of all these environmental challenges before receiving the services.
