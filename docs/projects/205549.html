Project Summary

The occurrence of multiple crises including insecurity, subnational violence, economic crisis (local currency depreciation) challenges compounded by the effects of climate-related shocks (flooding and dry spells), the ongoing effect of Sudan conflict (causing displacement) all adversely impact the food security of South Sudanese families. The country remains one of the most food insecure countries; with low agricultural production, household stocks depletes earlier than usual in many areas and staple food prices are atypically high, exacerbated by disrupted trade flows and increased demand related to the conflict in Sudan and influx of refugees and returnees. According to IPC report (Nov, 2024) 5.83 million people (46 percent of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Between July 2023 and June 2024, an estimated 1.65 million children between 6-59 months are expected to suffer acute malnutrition (AM) including 480,000 million children expected to suffer Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1.16 million expected to suffer Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM).
Pibor County is one of the hard-to-reach areas with frequent cattle raiding, inter- communal conflicts/displacement, age set fighting, negative cultural practice and high level of complex vulnerability. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence in the area is estimated to be 18.8% (3.9% SAM and 14.9% MAM) (Nutrition Cluster PIN data 2023). Under-fives, PLW and adolescent girls remain the worst affected by malnutrition. ForAfrika Positive Deviance Inquiry Study, Pibor County (2022 Report) shows that, among the poor households 53.7% (812 were non-positive deviant and only 40.2% of the population in Pibor utilize form of health care services in an area of high prevalence of diseases. Further, some women practice inappropriate feeding including poor breastfeeding/weaning and poor caring practices to the children whom are also at risks of many form of social and environmental norms including abductions. There is need to address the mentioned underlying causes of malnutrition.
ForAfrika will work with MOH/CHD to improve their capacity the proposed plan is to contribute to increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among 19,222 children, 5,592 adolescents, and 21,676 women as Sectoral Objective 1 and in line with Strategic Objective (SO) 1: Vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and SO3 Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand or cope with the impact of new and protracted crises is increased, and durable solutions to root causes of their vulnerability and shocks are enabled by humanitarian action.39 static nutrition sites (standalone and those embedded within health facilities) and 5 outreaches will be supported through Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) to reach out to 6,772 SAM cases in Outpatient therapeutic program(OTP) and 12,450 under 5 and 21,676 PLW in Targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP). 163 children with SAM and medical complication will be reached in one Stabilization Centre. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for 9804 girls and 9418 boys under five years of age and 12,450 PLW will be reached as a contribution to (SO1 & SO 2). ForAfrika will pilot the PD- Hearth menu and scale up the local nutrient food to combat malnutrition, integrated the response with health, Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Food Security and Livelihood (FSL), Education and Protection sectors to address key drivers of malnutrition. Stakeholders engagement AAP, protection mainstreaming and monitoring of the protection environment will be supported.

1. Needs

The changing seasonal patterns in Pibor County and the environment is likely to drive the increase in frequency and intensity of cattle raiding, age-set fighting’s and revenge killing coupled with natural disasters lead into destroyed infrastructure, but more importantly in further distancing the communities from basic services that have led to their regressive behaviors that negatively impacts people life. Displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of Gender Based Violence - Forced marriage and this is the most GBV experienced in the community due to cultural belief and social practice in the GPAA community [1] In addition, traditional notions of men as decision-makers and socio-cultural views on maternal health continue to present challenges to male involvement in maternal and child health and nutrition issues.ForAfrika, Pibor Positive Deviance Inquiry Study - 2022 Report; found that, out of 1516 children aged 6 to 35 months who were screened using weight for age 37.9% (n=575) children were underweight (<-1 z score), Some women practice poor maternal infant and young child feeding practices starting from poor breastfeeding, poor weaning and wrong feeding practices; too few children have access to minimum acceptable diet diversity (MADD) attributing to high malnutrition burden. Children are at risks of many form of social and environmental norms including abductions; there were no pit latrines in the various homes and almost everyone practiced open defecation as reported; “The majority of households, 99.2%, practice open defecation similar to children's defecation practices, led to poor hygiene and easily contaminated the food and water where a large proportion of children defecate in the open place Exposure to diarrhea-causing agents is frequently related to the use of contaminated water and unhygienic practices in food preparation and disposal of excreta, 40% of respondents cited drinking contaminated water and 37% cited eating without washing their hands. The mothers don’t wash their children’s hands frequently, especially when they eat; Some homes were not swept these behaviors lead to ill-health including AM. It is expected that insecurity and displacement affecting feeding and caring practices, increased morbidity during Oct 2023 - Jan 2024 due to increased water borne and water related diseases such as malaria and diarrhea, outbreak of Measles and Hepatitis E, worsen or contribute further to cyclic acute malnutrition. High food insecurity (wide food consumption gap) directly affects dietary intake and consequently contributing to acute malnutrition. In underlying causes or factors of malnutrition respectively, socio-cultural factors related to child feeding and maternal health (breast-feeding, food taboos) are widespread. Lack of education for mothers, Inadequate access to safe clean water, hygiene and sanitation to prevent waterborne diseases, high morbidity rate among under-five children this conjunction of factors accounts for the higher incidence of infant and Under Five (U5) malnutrition.This program seeks to support the entire Pibor County (Non-IDP, IDP and returnees) in the county and 8 Payam by providing lifesaving nutrition interventions to 19,222 U5 and 21,676 PLW, 168 TSFP IFP caretakers to children discharged from Stabilization center (SC), preventive health/nutrition measures closer to the community as possible. The program will continue to address the problem of under nutrition identified in Pibor County during the proposed period. The approach will focus on: strengthening nutrition-specific/nutrition-sensitive integrated actions to address underlying causes of wasting. Access to quality nutrition services for under 5s, adolescent girls, women and caregivers while working with other partners and involving stakeholders including community members throughout the project cycle.
