Project Summary

FADM works in the states of in Upper Nile, Warrap, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and Western Bahr El Ghazel States providing much needed humanitarian support in the thematic areas of WASH, Nutrition, Health, Food security and livelihoods and protection. This proposed project will address short- to-midterm WASH concerns of conflict, emergency and disease prone affected populations by providing integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations. This will improve water supply, sanitation and hygiene behavior for the affected populations and host families and reduce waterborne diseases. The project therefore intends to contribute to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of lifesaving integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, men and boys through a flexible, responsive and synergistic approach. Inline with the cluster strategy, the project will Integrate WASH response with Nutrition/(Food Security) and Health, enhance access to basic services in emergency, Mitigate WASH related GBV while building WASH resilience in the counties targeted.
This project will be targeting 40,000 individuals (Male: 4,800, Female: 9,600, Boys: 11,200 and Girls: 14,400) in Renk, Manyo, Tonj East, Tonj South, Tonj North, Aweil West, Aweil East, Aweil South, Aweil North, Aweil Centre, Wau, Raja, Jur River.

1 - Needs

The counties of Renk, Manyo, Tonj South, Tonj North, Aweil South, Aweil North, Aweil Centre, Wau, Raja, face a couple of humanitarian challenges as a result of multiple shocks affecting mainly internally displaced persons and returnees. Several of the community clusters comprise of persons multiply displaced, with little or no livelihoods and a myriad of WASH related challenges emanating from accessibility challenges by partners, influx of IDPs as a result of forced displacement due to cattle raids from neighboring villages. Access to safe water is low in these counties, with the WASH cluster severity mapping indicating below acceptable thresholds. Less than 30% of households in these communities have access to a safe water source, majority of these relying on unsafe surface water, which is shared with livestock. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to high level risk of vulnerability to water related illnesses. Flash floods undermine efforts by these communities to attain a level of well-being, destroying WASH infrastructure, livelihoods, leading to displacement and an upsurge in water related diseases. There were many crowded people in particular locations leading to overstretched use of existing social services. Cases of diarrhea and other infectious diseases were reported due to lack of clean drinking water. There were reported cases of high admission malaria especially in health facilities due to lack of enough mosquito nets. Because of lack of clean drinking water, many people suffered from dehydration. Stagnant water, severe overcrowding, inadequate drainage and a dire shortage of functional latrines, all create ideal conditions for the spread of diarrhea diseases including cholera and skin infections. Spikes in inter and intra-communal Protection, shelter, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), education, and health concerns, remained constant, while there have been increasing reports of poor food access in assessed settlements leading to negative coping mechanisms, some of which are causing long-term livelihood depletion for affected populations . The latest IPC release places the counties targeted under phase 3,4 and 5, with a direct link between high levels of food insecurity, malnutrition, disease and poor WASH services. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes, further more, the absence, presence or placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The project will conduct awareness creation on sexual exploitation and abuse to both women and men. PSEA policies present within the organization, FADM will also use project staffs and together with community members to identify SEA victims and report. We will establish referral pathways and feedback mechanism such that SEA cases are reported and managed while keeping conﬁdentiality. Staff training will be conducted on PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable member are key in project success and they will be involved at various levels right from the project design, planning, implementation, Monitoring and evaluation. All these will be done in a participatory way.Feedback will be obtained from these by having designated persons dedicated to them, while depending on context, culture and available means and infrastructure to design best mechanism of beneficiary feedback. These will range from setting up hotlines, suggestion boxes, interviews with key informants, focus group discussions as well as house to house visits while observing C -19 IPC protocols.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

FADM will provide public information to the beneﬁciaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and health facility based awareness and health promotion activities. FADM will conduct consultative meetings with the community to identify people with disability their needs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Project will have selected place within the community where one staff member will sit once or twice a week to receive and give feedback to the community members. There will be a box for feedback as well as receiving direct feedback from the community members. Staff will also get feedback during activity implementation and provide feedback to the community. Feedback obtained will be fed into project designs and appraisals, to ensure needs are met and necessary mitigations carried out.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

FADM will provide public information to the beneﬁciaries about the programmes provided through local government, community outreach and health facility-based awareness and health promotion activities. FADM will consult with local authorities, community leaders, Department of Water and sanitation, CHD and health staff throughout the project implementation regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete programmes. Emergency Rapid Team’s assessments include key informant interviews and focus group discussions amongst community groups. In static facilities, FADM will use annual household surveys to assess programme coverage and post exit interviews are used at the facility level for monitoring the quality of health service provision. Every staff member who joins FADM will get orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign upon it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the Staff Guidelines. In addition, FADM will conduct consultative meetings with the community to identify their needs. The project will conduct awareness creation on sexual exploitation and abuse to both women and men. Will also use project staffs and together with community members to identify SGBV victims and report. We will establish referral pathways and feedback mechanism such that SGBV cases are reported and managed while keeping conﬁdentiality. Project will have selected place within the community where one staff member will sit once or twice a week to receive and give feedback to the community members. There will be a box for feedback as well as receiving direct feedback from the community members. Staff will also get feedback during activity implementation and provide feedback to the community.
