Project Summary

Gender based violence remains a serious challenge within northeast Nigeria, causing more psychological imbalance for armed conflict-affected IDPs as well as returnees, especially women and girls. Displacement and continued movement of IDPs creates major protection concerns women and girls which has increased gender-based violence and access services remain a serious gap especially in hard to reach Local Governmental Areas. According to Humanitarian Response Plan, 2019, an estimated 1.3 million people have been identified to be in need of gender-based violence prevention and response across Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe states. Women and girls are often targeted while performing basic tasks such as travelling to water points, collecting firewood to cook food and going to communal latrines. They are often exposed to SEA, forced into prostitution, early marriages, and survival sex in exchange for food, their movement is restricted and basic needs are deprived. Women and girls are abducted by armed groups, are often raped and forcibly married and may be used in person borne improvised explosive device (PBIED) attacks. These women and girls, along with their children born as a result of rape, are often feared by communities and ostracized from society because they are suspected to be sympathizers of armed opposition groups. women and girls have been targeted with rape, abduction, to serve as sex slaves and also conscripted into a broad spectrum of roles including serving as spies and human beings forced to carry person-borne improvised explosive devices (PBIED). Upon their return, they require access to a full range of services to meet multiple immediate and reintegration needs as well as those of their children. Despite the consequences, families are still forcibly marrying daughters as young as 12 years old to men or members of armed opposition groups, citing poverty as the main reason. Between October 2018 and September 2019, the IRC provided case management services for over 400 gender based violence survivors in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe, indicating the high need for continued support. The majority of crisis affected people have experienced violence, repeated displacement, loss of or separation from family members, loss and destruction of property deterioration of living conditions, disruption of livelihoods. According to the HRP, 2019, most this vulnerable groups include, female headed households, particularly adolescent girls who are forced into a role as breadwinners. Pre- existing gender inequalities due to unequal access to opportunities have contributed to limited resources and skills among women and girls which have increased their vulnerability and exposure to abuse including sexual exploitation. The crisis has significantly affected the dignity of women and children. This further entrenches pre-existing gender disparities. In the global Gender Gap index, Nigeria ranks 118th out of 144 countries

1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs remain acute in northern Adamawa (Michika and Madagali), Borno (Konduga, Magumeri, Mongonu, Gwoza and MMC/Jere) and Yobe (Damaturu and Gujba) where the crisis shows no sign of abating and there is an increase in violence including GBV, SEA, and other protection needs, especially for women and girls who are the most at risk and have limited access to services as well as protection or redress.Additionally, these locations are also hard to reach and according to the Nigeria HRP 2019, are subjected to military and AOGs attacks, making it difficult for aid workers to provide needed services for the affected population. The IRC intends to scale up programming to improve access to quality lifesaving and well coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk through the provision of comprehensive services including appropriate health assistance, GBV case management, psychological first aid accompanied with referrals for appropriate services, other forms of psycho-social support, provision of critical material support, as well as enhancing access to multisectoral services through establishing and strengthening referral mechanisms. The proposed program is designed to ensure the IRC is capable of delivering a comprehensive and GBV timely response and prevention activities in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. The proposed intervention is intended to contribute to women and girls’ safety and healing by ensuring that women and girls can access case management services that address the physical, psychological and social consequences of GBV to prevent further harm. The intervention will also engage community members and stakeholders in order to ensure women and girls’ safety and protection from physical and sexual violence and to promote a safe and supportive environment where women and girls can freely access services without being blamed, stigmatized or socially excluded. Women and girls will participate in Economic and Social Empowerment (EA$E) activities, Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLAs) and Discussion Group Series (DGS) to increase their access to financial resources and control thereby reducing vulnerability to GBV and empowering them to contribute to their basic needs and as well strengthen the community resilience and systems that prevent and respond to GBV including harmful practices.In an effort to respond to ongoing GBV concerns and ensure women and girls have access to services in a dignified manner, The IRC will work with relevant ministries (Women Affairs, Justice and Health), local organizations and community structures under this proposed intervention. In communities where no safe space exists, the IRC in cooperation with local authorities and community members will identify a safe space for both distribution and CM service provision. In each safe space, confidentiality of survivors will be supported by well-trained GBV caseworkers who meet with GBV survivors in a private space to provide case intake and ongoing management support. The primary goal of social work case management is to optimize client functioning, wellbeing, emotional and physical safety. Women’s Action Groups (WAGs), formed and/or strengthened in each of the WGSS will act as local focal points for women and girls seeking access to GBV services. WAGs, each composed of 25 women, will participate in an 8-week programme aimed at enhancing their knowledge on preventing GBV. The IRC will utilize previously trained caseworkers who are providing case management, GBV prevention and response messaging on voluntary basis, to enhance the sustainability of the program

2 - Activities

IRC establish four additional satellite Women and Girl Safe Spaces in additional to existing spaces to increase at-risk women and girls’ access to GBV services as well as increase social support and linkages for additional services in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. 1,200 GBV survivors (Michika 150, Madagali 100, MMC/Jere 100, Konduga 150, Magumeri 150, Mongonu 150, Gwoza 150 Damaturu 100, and Gujba 150) have safe access to quality case management services and psychosocial support. Using a survivorcentered approach to GBV case management. Psychosocial support activities will foster healing and recovery which will be done through counselling and social networking activities including recreational activities, literacy, and numeracy classes. Life skills classes for women and adolescent girls are tailored to their age and cover SRH and GBV education sessions, safety planning, and communication skills. 5,720 women and adolescent girls are also engaged in skills acquisition including tailoring, knitting, cap making, etc. to build strong support network to strengthen resilience for healing and recovery as well as becoming self-reliant by using acquired skills for income generation. Pychosocial materials for skill building and recreational activities will be provided to women and girls during project period, as a way of strengthening support networks for women and girls to freely discuss their experiences which impact their healing and recovery positively. To enhance girls’ safety, the IRC will use the Girl Shine Curriculum, aimed at reducing violence against girls and increasing their safety and wellbeing. IRC will work with adolescent girls between the ages 10 – 19 categorized in separate groups based on age groups to build trust and confidence, become self-reliant decision making as well as use financial skills to improve savings and become self-sufficient in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe. 300 adolescent girls will be linked with 30 trained female mentors from same communities to increase support for girls. Sessions will include life skills, reproductive health, gender-based violence and financial education and provisions will be made for girls to share fun, learn and relax.Girls’ female and male parents will also be engaged in monthly discussion sessions to promote safety at home and the community and to improve parenting skills.The IRC will conduct the following trainings in Gujba and Magumeri LGA to enhance understanding and promote GBV prevention and response through coordination and collaboration: Six 3 day GBV core concept trainings will be done for 150 stakeholders including relevant ministries (women affairs and health) Three 5 day trainings for 75 health workers and non-health workers in clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS). Three 5 day GBV case management training will be done for 125 participants including IRC WPE case workers, Ministry of Women Affairs case workers, local partners’ staff and WAG volunteers Eight SASA! trainings with a total of 180 participants (20 each in Michika, Madagali, Konduga, Gwoza, MMC/Jere, Magumeri ,Monguno, Damaturu and Gujba) 750 women (25 women per group) including IDPs and host community members in Economic and Social Empowerment including village savings and loan association activities, to support them to access resources to contribute to basic needs and increase their financial control. In order to mitigate their risk to intimate partner violence due to cash control, VSLA members in partners will participate in 8 sessions on financial management and household decisions. Monthly safety audits and strategies developed will be coordinated and implemented. 5,800 at risk women and girls will benefit dignity kits. Through coordination key actors,39,160 community members will be reached with GBV awareness 10 Engaging Men in Accountable Practices groups community leaders, will be engaged in discussion sessions on concerns raised during females sessions will challenge males to change
