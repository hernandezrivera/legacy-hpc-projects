Project Summary

The project aims at continuing and strengthening Site Management (SM), Site Development (SD), Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and informed humanitarian decision making within the Ukhiya and Teknaf areas through direct implementation and partnerships with international and national NGOs. Through this project Rohingya refugees and the host community will benefit from improved service provision, Disaster Risk Reduction measures, site improvements and site planning interventions which contribute to ensuring equitable access to assistance, protection, and services for refugees living in the sites, improving their quality of life and dignity during displacement.
Site Management activities will continue to lead the SMSD Sector within the ISCG, and support the office of the Camp-in-Charge, ensuring services are monitored and coordinated, and community engagement activities are appropriate, target those with vulnerabilities and special needs, and are an effective avenue for two-way communication with the affected population. Providing humanitarian and development actors with data and information on needs that supports informed decision making, the project ensures targeted and appropriate humanitarian service provision within the camps as well as the host community. DRR will also be streamlined into site management & development programming, with a focus on community based community response and referral mechanisms, camp level contingency planning and mitigation measures in place for all seasonal risks present in the camps. NPM will continue to provide support to humanitarian contingency planning and operational programmes and support the monsoon response through incident tracking.
Site development works implemented through IOM, its partners, and its involvement in the Site Maintenance & Engineering Platform (SMEP), will contribute to a safer, more dignified, and more accessible living environment for the camp population, working to ensure further community involvement in the identification of priority works. It is expected that the specified programme activities will result in reinforcing the overall capacity of unions in Ukhiya and Teknaf and surrounding host communities to cope with natural disasters like floods and cyclones and respond better through strengthening structures to manage natural disasters and emergency situations. Approaches used to reach the overall goal of the project will mainly focus on community participation, age, gender, diversity inclusion, social interaction and peaceful co-existence. This will include working with a wide variety of stakeholders, including UNWOMEN and their gender programming at camp level.

1 - Identified Needs

Although living conditions have improved in the camps refugees continue to live in congested areas vulnerable to natural hazards. While there has been an increase in access to services due to SM support, Covid-19 has reduced access to services across sectors due to decreased humanitarian presence. This results in a need for continuing SMS presence to ensure equitable access to humanitarian assistance and referral mechanisms.Coordination needs to be re-established to a similar standard as before the pandemic, with regular and sector-specific meetings across all sites. However, the number of actors remains high; efforts need reinforcement due to turnover of CiC and humanitarian staff. The stability of SMS at camp level provides the bedrock of the humanitarian intervention, ensuring that institutional knowledge is not lost, and effective systems consistent.Community participation remains an area to be strengthened in the absence of a strong community representation system and to ensure all segments of the community participate in camp life. IOM will continue to work with key stakeholders to ensure structures are in place at which adequately reflect the different demography and specific needs of the community. The perpetual nature of the of seasonal risks and hazards require a tailored and harmonized approach which incorporates community-based response mechanisms, camp level contingency plans, and effective and life-saving rapid systems. The environment has improved greatly, with SD teams constructing and repairing roads, pathways and drains. To mitigate landslides, slope protection projects were prioritized together with widespread forestation. However, the terrain remains vulnerable to natural hazards. Covid-19 slowed works due to the limitations access. According to the MSNA, 68% of the population reported either no improvement or worsening conditions in the camps. The effects of the monsoon require rehabilitation of drainage, slope stabilisation and repair of access routes, with durable materials where possible.Infrastructure inspection, repair, maintenance and subtle upgrades continue to be needed, ensuring year-round access for humanitarian, refugee and host communities. The construction/maintenance of drainage as well as slope stabilization will be also be required to mitigate risks to beneficiariesSMEP will support the ADB and WB infrastructure initiatives by ensuring connectivity/sustainability of planned infrastructure. In 2018 the RRRC requested SMEP to operate and maintain the vehicle access roads—the request continues to stand.NPM aims to support informed planning, prioritization and delivery of assistance in the camps and host community. As the crisis persists, so does the need for evidence-based interventions capable of providing granular information on populations. While the fluidity of population movements within the camps has stabilized, facilities/services frequently change. Analysis requires consistent accurate updating to understand key gaps. Furthermore, challenges presented by the monsoon and cyclones requires an information management framework to ensure the impact on populations is monitored effectively. NPM will continue to support SMS with the Daily Incident Reporting Mechanism. IOM will also build upon previous DRR activities in the host community. DMCs receive continuous support, but there is scope to improve capacity and effectiveness. Along with institutions, it is crucial to build capacity of local communities at-risk, including women, to be more resilient to natural disasters. Within these at-risk populations are the marginalized/vulnerable, such as the fishing communities along the Naf River. In response to the influx of 2017, the GoB banned fishing in the Naf, inadvertently diminishing a livelihood without alternative opportunities. The loss of sustenance/income diminished the community’s ability to maintain/upgrade shelters, reducing their resilience in the face of natural hazards.

2 - Activities or outputs

-Strengthen participation & inclusion by forming additional & strengthening existing AGD groups & support them in designing/implementing projects. Ensure the Women’s Participation Project is rolled out based on the UN Women’s Empowerment, Participation & Leadership curriculum -Harmonize approach to community engagement, feedback & information within IOM’s AoR. Ensure refugees better lead programming via the new Common Feedback Platform. Strengthen committees within IOM’s AoR & create legitimate representation structures wherever possible -Contribute to the Sector’s Capacity Sharing Initiative to train government staff deployed to support the CiC in camp management & providing oversight to site improvement -Lead & staff the Sector within the ISCG, continue to strengthen camp-level coordination of different service providers & monitoring to highlight gaps, prioritize facilities & services & avoid duplication & advocate for solutions -Improve living conditions & reduce risks by improving infrastructure across the camps & building the capacity of Rohingya volunteers to maintain the camps. Includes continuing to coordinate infrastructure interventions across the camps, ensuring they are in line with the Macro Settlement Development Plan, guiding other sectors in rationalizing facilities, & re-planning for decongestion. This also includes SD for the newly approved two story shelters, as well as upgrade of bamboo bridges to modular steel bridges -Implement DRR activities such as drainage, access & slope stabilisation via Cash for Work which includes men, women & PwD -Continue the development & maintenance of vehicle access roads & major drainage. This work will be complimented by small road & drainage installations. The activities shall be operationalised via multiple work crews positioned within the camps, resourced with light machinery, operators familiar with working in wet conditions & technical personnel to provide practical guidance for implementing the works (SMEP) -Three casting yards will be maintained strategically across KBE with equipment & operatives, tools & materials to fabricate reinforced concrete cross-drain slabs, slope stabilisation panels & bamboo fencing (SMEP) -SMEP teams will maintain three casting yards, with >30% female participation & safe inclusion of between 5 & 10 people with disability (PwD) in each -Mainstream Disaster Risk Reduction via institutionalization in educational curricula, local & national level policies & adopt school-based safety disaster management initiatives (TRD) -Conduct Climate Vulnerability & Capacity Assessment (CVCA) & implement the findings (TRD) -Strengthening Cyclone Early Warning System (CEWS) capacity in the communities (TRD) -Distributions of emergency equipment & small-scale mitigation activities for the Naf river-based communities (TRD) Emergency preparedness & Response: SMSD daily incident assessment: NPM will continue to provide ongoing support to the Site Management sector via the “SMSD Sector Daily Incident assessment. The purpose of the system is to capture key information on the impact & damages caused by weather related incidents. NPM provides the technical support to this framework & produces daily &/or weekly datasets & factsheets which are shared widely to all humanitarian actors -Facility rationalization: -Facilities Mapping -Facilities monitoring in cooperation with SMS sector - NPM will continue supporting the SMS sector via the establishment of a facilities monitoring tool. This tool would allow actors to monitor the changes in infrastructure in the camp and provide an overview of service coverage across a geographic area according to sector standards; The tool can be used by a variety of stakeholders, both for site planning & development purposes, as well site management operational activities on the ground. It will grant humanitarian partner’s a better understand of service provision within the camp thus ensuring adequate coverage
