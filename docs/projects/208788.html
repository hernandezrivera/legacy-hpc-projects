Project Summary

This project aims to improve access to quality preventative and curative primary health care services, including reproductive health services to vulnerable populations especially in hard to reach communities in Gwoza and Damboa LGA. This project will maintain provision of essential primary health care services to IDPs and the host community in Gwoza and Damboa LGAs whilst improving access of quality life­saving services to pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years.
The project will also enhance health workers capacity to act as “First Responders”, support the Ministry of Health and other relevant authorities in emergency response capacity, training local male and female health
workers to respond to health emergencies and providing training in disease surveillance, outbreak response, case management, and reporting systems. It will enhance access to emergency Primary Health Care services for vulnerable populations (with focus on women, girls and boys) within the IDP sites through the
provision basic equipment, drugs, medical supplies, basic lab equipment, human resources and supplies.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crises, including the relocation of IDPs has placed women and girls at a heightened risk, poor access to health services, Gender-based violence (GBV), including sexual exploitation and abuse, and livelihood opportunities. Among those in need, women and children constitute 81%(6.7 million), with women and girls comprising the majority at 53%. According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview, displaced people in camps have limited or no access to integrated primary health care services, and about 5.76 million people are in urgent need of healthcare services, with 36% children under five years of age, 24% being girls and 7.5% being women. Gender is a key determinant of vulnerability as such women and children are disproportionately impacted by the conflict and bear the brunt of humanitarian needs especially persons living with disabilities. Likewise, the delivery of reproductive health services to women caught up in conflict-affected areas is constrained by several factors, including access and availability of services and service providers and the risk of women dying during pregnancy, childbirth, or post-partum remains high. Also, the closure of camps in Borno state and relocation of IDPs is stretching the already limited health care, education and other basic services. In addition, There is a need for enhanced preparedness and response to epidemic-prone diseases, especially diphtheria, cholera, measles, and circulating variant poliovirus (cVPV2)/polio outbreaks in the state.

2 - Activities

This project seeks enhance continuation of health services delivery and outbreak response; deployment of mobile teams as a rapid response mechanism; prepositioning of Essential medicines and supplies to 4 primary health care facilities; provision of RH services to support mother and child health care including IMCI and SAM cases; improved early detection, reporting and laboratory confirmation of disease outbreak and case management whilst strengthening community engagement and mobilization. AHI will continue to work closely with the MoH, health cluster and partners to coordinate, assess and respond to prioritized locations of greatest need to avoid duplication of services. AHI will ensure the provision of basic life-saving quality healthcare services to affected IDPs, returnees, people in extremely hard-to-reach areas, and host populations; by strengthening Primary Healthcare services to 22,000 vulnerable populations Project Activities include:Training of 10 Nurses and 20 CHEWS and deployment of 4 Mobile Health Teams to enhance access to preventive and curative healthcare services including active case search and management of Cholera, malaria, and typhoid, emergency response mechanism through RCCE; uptake of services and referrals in extremely hard-to-reach communities. Also, there will be procurement and Pre-positioning of Essential medicines and supplies including malaria and cholera kits for 4 PHCs AHI will engage a needs based team of voluntary emergency health service providers, The Midwives and Nurses will assist in providing SRH Services, Doctors will provide general consultations and specialized services, while the CHEWs will assist in community mobilization and referrals for women and girls to visit health facilities where services will be provided in target host communities/IDP camps listed for adequate service delivery. AHI health workers will provide RMNCH ( ANC care , Childbirth care (including emergency obstetric care) and Family Planning services (all methods - including long-term and permanent, as well as emergency contraception) during mobile health outreaches moving from shelter to shelter in the IDP camps on a weekly basis. There will also be adequate referrals for complicated cases

The implementation methodology also provides a platform for full participation for women, girls, boys and men in the project area by ensuring their participation in service delivery, accountability and preferred feedback mechanism.
