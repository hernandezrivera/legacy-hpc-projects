Project Summary

Over ten years of crisis in Syria has drastically eroded the living conditions of Palestine refugees and Syrian nationals alike. The rapid depreciation of the Syrian pound accompanied by staggering inflation levels, the impact of Lebanon's financial and banking crisis, and the tightened trade restrictions imposed on the country since June 2020, have all contributed to a worsening of the socio-economic conditions. The COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on basic services that has impacted the livelihoods of Palestine refugees and Syrians.
To support Palestine refugee youth access to employment opportunities, the UNRWA TVET programme will continue to provide short-term courses to 850 students in Damascus, Dera’a, Homs, Aleppo and Latakia, offering a variety of specializations that respond to the needs of the local market. Short-term courses (three months on average) are designed to strengthen technical skills, with minimal financial costs to the refugee students, to improve their prospects of accessing the labour market.
In 2022, UNRWA will also expand its provision of long-term courses, usually only offered at the Damascus VTC, to its TVET centres in Dera’a, Homs, Aleppo and Latakia. This is in response to the increasing demand of refugee youth in these areas for similar courses and to promote equal access for all Palestine refugees to livelihood opportunities to support their resilience.
Depending on the trajectory of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2022, the TVET programme is ready to adjust to either remote or blended (in-person and remote) modalities for course delivery. Internet packages will be procured for students and staff to support this approach when needed. Hygiene measures, including the provision of masks to students and staff, and the distribution of sanitizers to all TVET centres will also be put in place to mitigate the risk of exposure and spread of COVID-19.
The various components of UNRWA's interventions in 2022-2023 will also contribute to supporting refugees who have spontaneously returned to their places of residence either internally or from other countries. Where possible, UNRWA will aim at restarting services in camps that were heavily destroyed during the conflict (Dera’a, Yarmouk and Ein el Tal) through the rehabilitation of the Agency’s installations (including schools and clinics) in these camps.

4 - Needs

Out of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be inside Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. Prolonged displacement further compounds existing instabilities, and entrenches unemployment, and loss of livelihoods opportunities within established Palestine refugees’ communities and camps. . An UNRWA survey conducted in May 2021 found that 82 per cent of the refugees interviewed now live on less than US$ 1.9 a day and that 48 per cent of total household expenditure was spent on food, indicating severe distress.In 2020 and 2021, the already deteriorating economic situation has been further compounded by measures taken to limit the spread of COVID-19, which had a negative impact on the availability of employment opportunities. As such, an assessment conducted in August 2020 by UNRWA on the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 indicated that Palestine refugees’ ability to secure their income has faced a devastating setback. Close to 30 per cent of Palestine refugee respondents said that the head of the household had lost her or his job over the past months, while 30 per cent also said that the wages of the head of household had reduced due to COVID-19. Close to half of Palestine refugee households mentioned that they currently have sufficient money to cover daily needs just for one day and only 21 per cent said the household has a stable monthly income. In such context, livelihood interventions are critical.

5 - Implementation of activities

In 2022-23, UNRWA will continue to fundraise for livelihood activities, which are critical to increase the resilience of Palestine refugees in Syria and to support their access to employment. To support youth employment and livelihood capacity, the UNRWA Technical and Vocational Education and Training Programme (TVET) will continue to provide short-term courses to 850 students (467 females, 383 males including 17 persons with disabilities) at Damascus Training Center in addition to Homs, Aleppo, Daraa and Lattakia training centers, based on funds’ availability. In order to support young adults in the areas outside of Damascus and Rural Damascus, UNRWA will also launch long-term TVET courses in Latakia, Daraa, Aleppo and Homs benefiting up to 260 students. As demand for TVET services grows, the Agency will continue its efforts to better link students with the job market in an effort to increase employability rates of graduates through developing their employability and soft skills. Stationaries and supplies will be procured to support delivery of courses. Premises will be maintained to ensure quality education/training. The various components of UNRWA's interventions in 2022-23 will also contribute to early recovery efforts through supporting refugees who have spontaneously returned by restarting services in camps that were heavily destroyed during the conflict (Dera’a, Yarmouk and Ein el Tal); this will through the rehabilitation of the Agency’s installations in these camps.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 55% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. Risk: UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization material will be made available at work locations.
