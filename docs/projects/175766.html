Project Summary

This response plan aims to increase access of emergency lifesaving health care services that will be provided through mobile and static primary health care services (i.e. PHCC & PHCU) via clinical packages to vulnerable populations of Akobo, Yei, Lainya and Morobo counties of Jonglei and Central Equatorial State respectively. Approximately, 28, 286 (10,168 Akobo, 5,110 Yei, 4,318 Lainya and 8, 690 Morobo) vulnerable hosts, IDPs and Returnees are targeted to be reached in the mentioned States with a budget of 1, 350,000 US$. The response focuses on provision of basic essential health services as per the national standards and centered on the context epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, seasonality and trauma related emergency requirements. This will ensure prevention, preparedness, response and mitigation activities responding to people in need, with attention to access for vulnerable population such as women, children as well as people with special needs . It aligns with Health Cluster Strategic objectives as follows;
1 Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic-prone diseases and health insecurities
2 Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems
3 Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly
The response will support 6 mobile health clinics as follows: -2 Mobile Clinic in Akobo and (one in each of three locations – Lainya, Yei and Morobo) and 8 Static health facilities (one PHCC and one PHCU – in each county) that will enable Comprehensive integrated emergency health care services which will see intersectoral coordination of nutrition, food security and livelihoods, WASH, and protection clusters realized at all levels.
CIDO will engage the communities and existing development health programs along with the County Health Department in the planning and organization of emergency health services, this will also be entrenched in the cluster coordination mechanism both at national and sub national level to enable coordination, collaboration as well as liaising with other partners and authorities to implement, monitor and review the response activities. Referral pathways for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and GBV survivors and for maternal and child health emergencies will be streamlined and strengthened. Referral pathways will link the community and facility based health care system to provide range of care. In order to protect both patient and highly needed health staff the CIDO will support IPC/WASH measures including COVID-19 prevention and management activities.
The response considers deployment of qualified health workers to maintain and improve the quality of care to mothers of childbearing age, children & persons with disabilities. Affected populations involvement and capacity building of CHDs and health workers. Use EWARS and weekly IDSR system of reporting to monitor the morbidities and trends of outbreaks. Use the HMIS & other internally improvised tools to ensure the quality & consistency of data. Obtain beneficiaries` feedback by supporting the existing Health Facility Committees and establish committees in health facilities that do not currently have. Establish suggestion boxes in the health facilities & CIDO offices, organize FGD as part of AAP. Consideration of this response will enable CIDO to optimize its 7 years’ experience and technical expertise in providing services and exploits its capacity to ensure ultimate objective of coordinated response to save life.

1 - Needs

Health needs of 40% of 739,212 (221,022- Akobo; 294,818- Yei; 113,186 Morobo and 110,939 Lainya) vulnerable individuals continue to grow as health services are stretched to the breaking point due to inaccessibility, insufficient medicines, equipment’s, staff and inadequate infrastructure besides food insecurity, flooding compounded with localized violence and revenge killings. Owing to the above, the four counties (Akobo, Lainya, Yei and Morobo) remain disrupted from acquisition of essential health service hence contributing immensely to increased incidences of malaria, ARI and AWD among others within the response catchment area. South Sudan IDSR reports 2020 affirms that 64% morbidity and 45% mortality countrywide are caused by Malaria, diarrhea and other preventable diseases. Malnutrition, notably, places the two states at the helm of needs with Akobo recording a GAM rate of 25.24 % much far away from WHO threshold of 15% GAM, while Lainya placed at 5.88%, 5.26% for Morobo and 4.20% Yei (FSNMS round 26). The attributes of this are manifold, including severe acute food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions & hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by sub national conflict, floods and Covid-19 related disruptions. South Sudan is among countries with worse health indicators worldwide. Maternal mortality rates of 789 deaths per 100,000 live births, <5 mortality rate of 90.7 deaths per 1,000 live birth according to UN Inter-Agency Group for Child Mortality (2019 estimation) One physician for every 65,574 people according to WHO report on access to health services. Humanitarian & developmental partners support 57% of the functioning health facilities and only 44% of the population live within 5 km of a health facility. This calls for the need to provide resources and scale up health response within the regions. IDSR & DHIS reports from these counties in which CIDO operates also indicated an upsurge of water related diseases attributed to open defection and poor hygiene practices, respiratory complications contributing immensely to the mortality for < 5s, low ANC uptake attributed to vastness of areas with some health facilities nonfunctional. For instance, in Akobo East 2 health facilities are functional against 8 health facilities in the four payams (Bilkey, Nyandit, Dengjok & Gakdong) whereas Akobo West 2 out of 15 health facilities are functional. In Lainya – Wonduruba by December 2020, 0 out of 10 facilities were operational while this stands vulnerable communities are subjected to walk for an average of one to six hours in a bid to search health services. In Morobo 58% of the population lack access to healthcare and are forced to take more than 1 hour to walk to the nearest health facility. (AoK Sept 2020 South Sudan Report)Exhibited Sexual violence remains high, with a recent 45.8 per cent of all households reportedly having experienced an incident of gender-based violence (GBV) directed against females in the last year (IOM Study Report 2020). As a country neighboring the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) and Ethiopia, South Sudan remains at high risk of Ebola virus disease (EVD) and COVID 19 due to cross-border movements of people and goods, and the long-term humanitarian crisis and security situation. Convergence of COVID-19 compels preparedness & response mechanisms to enable scale up of services to include the provision of sufficient spaces in the health facilities to ensure adherence to social distancing, provision of PPEs to frontline health workers that need to be optimized by this responseTherefore, this appeal will help not only to reduce on the number of people in need of humanitarian services but also reduce morbidity and mortality by scaling up health care services to the vulnerable populations and contributing toward global course of saving life.

2 - Project activities

In this response CIDO intends to out out the following activities:-1.1.1: CIDO will carry out routine immunization services for common preventable childhood illness through static and mobile outreaches within the 4 counties 1.2.1: CIDO will conduct timely disease surveillance and outbreaks detecting, reporting and response 1.3.1: CIDO will carry out OPD curative consultations for common morbidities including management of non-communicable diseases in 4 PHCCs, 4PHCUs and 6 mobile units. 1.4.1: CIDO will support laboratory capacity to undertake testing, counseling and referral for communicable and Neglected Tropical diseases including inpatient case management in the PHCCs 1.5.1: CIDO will provide Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services, referral and necessary counselling to the SGBV survivors within the 4 counties 1.6.1: CIDO will support Infection Prevention and Control for COVID-19 through risk communication and community engagement, triaging, screening, case detection and isolation at the health facilities 1.7.1: CIDO will conduct ANC, delivery by Skilled Birth Attendants and Post-natal services at the health facilities including BeMONC and CeMONC centers 1.8.1: CIDO will provide basic psychosocial support and mental health services at the health facilities 1.9.1: CIDO will coordinate with WASH sectors to support health facilities through rehabilitation/construction and installation of latrines, incinerators and washing facilities 1.10.1: CIDO will carry out conduct MUAC screening and referral of malnourished children and Pregnant & Lactating Women to the nutrition care centers 1.11.1: CIDO will support the installation of boxes for feedback mechanism in the health facilities and the organization`s offices in the area 1.12.1: CIDO will build the capacity and knowledge of health workers and the County Department (CHD) officials through formal and in-services trainings 1.13.1: CIDO will procure drugs, medical supplies, laboratory reagents and other consumables 1.4.1: CIDO will support sub-national humanitarian coordination and reporting activities

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CIDO has a functional policy on PSEA. All staffs will be obligated to sign against the policy in respect to the 6 core principles of PSEA. The respective project staff leading this project will be guided by the standard operating Procedures (SOP) of PSEA and where applicable will use the community based complaint Mechanism to address cases of SEA. CIDO will ensure that PSEA IEC materials are used and are visible in all designated areas of service delivery. In collaboration with other partners and recognition that CIDO is an active protection partner, efforts will be consolidated to conduct capacity building of front line workers on PSEA and extend applicability of SOP to other NGOs at grassroots level

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

These project activities are designed to give priority to safety and dignity of all men, women boys and girls and prevent negative coping strategies and mitigate risks including issues of discrimination in regards to accessing services. Through regular coordination meetings, CIDO will work with other partners to train the frontline workers on disability mainstreaming and also report and share protection concerns for persons with specific needs. The four principles of South Sudan HCT Protection Strategy. (2015) i.e. • Prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm, • Equity and Meaningful access, • AAP • Participation and Empowerment will be at play

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

An integral part of this project delivery will be participatory Learning and Continuous Improvement. CIDO will organize regular program performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries and partners to assess progress towards objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback, identified risks and risk management strategies during implementation. At the end of the project CIDO will carry out a project evaluation and document the process used to achieve project outputs and outcomes. The report will also be shared with the beneficiaries

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO’s policies and Culture Prompts Senior management to organize proper orientation sessions to all employees on PSEA issues. As such, before engagement with the beneficiaries the team is well informed on the PSEA consequences and code of conduct is signed by each staff as part of the contractual obligation. The is also obligated to organize awareness sessions for the beneficiaries, women leaders, community leaders, law enforcement agents and every stakeholder in the counties on what this project considers as sexual exploitation, channels of reporting and the available resources within the organization.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CIDO will put necessary measures to ensure that project beneficiaries are protected not only from external threats that impedes them from service acquisition but also from data mismanagement. This will involve ensuring that the available data does not trickle in the wrong audience by keeping confidential information well and educating health workers on importance of confidentiality. Referral pathways will be created for survivors of SGBV and CIDO will ensure that they access health services without impediments in to the health facilities. To ensure that beneficiaries access feedback and complaint mechanisms, CIDO will use existing structures such as health committees to address accountability issues. Suggestion boxes will be placed in the health facilities to ensure that the beneficiaries can easily access feedback and complaint mechanisms. This will also include having a dedicated line for receiving feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries. CIDO will work hand in hand with protection partners to conduct safety audit at every health facility to determine protection issues that might jeopardies service delivery and provide recommendation on addressing identified risks. In the initial stages of response, CIDO will engage all stakeholders in consultation meetings which will enable joint mapping of the locations and identification of gaps, this will also help to trim expectations of various stakeholders, identify security hotspot areas alongside land issues that may have a bearing on the response, build consensus and buy in of the project by the community while addressing unpredicted risks and threats to the response. The project staffs will also be taken through PSEA policies and obligated to sign code of conduct.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

• The project will ensure Management of medical complications among children U5 with SAM and surveillance through MUAC screening for under 5 children who seek medical care at the health facilities and refer all malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites, Convergence of health and nutrition activities across all health facilities and enabling Joint health and nutrition education/promotion initiatives.
