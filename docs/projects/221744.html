Project Summary

Action Against Hunger implements nutrition interventions informed by needs of the affected communities. The core strategy of the nutrition programme is to combat the key drivers of malnutrition in the affected communities in South Sudan through an integrated approach where nutrition services will be implemented by integrating WASH, Health, FSL and protection services throughout the project life cycle. In 2025, Action Against Hunger will implement preventive interventions to improve nutrition situation and provide treatment services for boys and girls under 5 years of age and pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWG). To improve the coverage of treatment interventions, Action Against Hunger will strengthen active case finding and train mothers on family MUAC for early detection and timely referrals of malnourished children. Nutrition interventions will be jointly implemented with Ministry of Health (MoH) envisioned at transitioning to the government in a phased manner. In the field, nutrition activities will be implemented through local community structures to foster localization efforts. Innovative activities for malnutrition prevention will be implemented in the project areas in collaboration with UNICEF, WFP and MoH and coordinated through the nutrition cluster.
In order to strengthen nutrition surveillance functions in South Sudan, Action Against Hunger will implement SMART+ surveys in 21 counties classified as high priority counties in 2025. Additionally, Action Against Hunger will provide capacity building training on SMART+ infrastructure to all Nutrition Information Working Group (NIWG) members and partners implementing nutrition SMART surveys in South Sudan.
To improve the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP), Action Against Hunger will schedule regular meetings at the nutrition sites to plan and monitor the progress of nutrition interventions through provision of feedback and corrective actions. All the modalities of the feedback mechanism implemented will be introduced to the community members to enable them to choose the modality. The Focus Group Discussion (FGD) questionnaire revised during the implementation of the GBV Risk Mitigation research with the incorporation of the questions related to GBV will be used. Feedback from beneficiaries will be analyzed and used to improve the nutrition intervention outcomes. Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality at the nutrition sites and this will be conducted using the tool provided by the nutrition cluster and at community (using the Community Safety Audit tool developed through the GBV Risk Mitigation research). As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be requested to share their concerns with the program team and suggest improvement plans.
To mitigate risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV) committed by or against staff and beneficiaries, Action Against Hunger staff are bound to its Policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), which prohibits sexual exploitation and abuse by employees and its representatives and affirms its zero-tolerance approach to such cases. In addition, deviations from these policies will be thoroughly investigated and actions will be taken against the perpetrators.

1. Needs

The 2024 IPC Acute Malnutrition has indicated a worsening nutrition situation in South Sudan due to multitude of overlapping and complex humanitarian emergencies. Nearly 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring urgent treatment (IPC-AMN 2024). In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. In 2025, Action Against Hunger will provide integrated life-saving nutrition interventions in three (3) states of Jonglei, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. These three states have experienced overlapping emergencies including flooding and refugees and returnees influx in Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Action Against Hunger will specifically provide life-saving nutrition interventions in Fangak, Duk, Ayod, Gogrial West, Gogrial East, Twic, Aweil Centre and Aweil East that are all in IPC phase 4 (critical) classification in the recently released 2024 IPC AMN results. Additionally, Action Against Hunger in 2024 implemented integrated nutrition SMART surveys (integrated needs assessment surveys) in five (5) of the eight (8) proposed counties. Aweil East County: Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence was very high 19.0% (14.0-25.3 95% CI), Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) 4.1% (2.5- 6.6 95% CI). Infant and young child feeding practices were suboptimal; Minimal Meal Frequency (MMF) was 3.25% (1.06-7.41) and exclusive breastfeeding was 65.22% (42.73-83.62). Aweil Centre County: GAM was equally very high 19.0% (15.3% - 23.4%), SAM 2.2% (1.0 - 4.5%), MMF was 6.2% (2.6% - 13.7%) and exclusive breastfeeding was 72.1% (54.3% - 84.9%).Gogrial West County: GAM was very high 19.4% (15.5-24.2 95% CI), SAM 3.8% (2.3- 6.0 95% CI), MMF was suboptimal 9.8% (5.46-15.88) and exclusive breastfeeding was 89.38% (83.53-93.69).Fangak county: GAM was 16.8 % (13.2 - 21.0 95% C.I.), SAM 3.6 % (2.1 - 6.2 95% C.I.), MMF 11.9% and minimal acceptable diet (MAD) was 3.3%.Duk County: GAM was 19.3% (15.3%, 24.0%), SAM 2.9% (1.8%, 4.8%), MMF 29.0% (17.8%, 43.4%) and MAD 10.9% (5.0%, 22.3%).Ayod County: FSNMS round 30 indicated a GAM of 19.2% (9.9-33.9 95% CI) and SAM 4.0% (1.4-10.9 95% CI). Twic County: FSNMS round 30 indicated a GAM of 22.9% (16.1-31.4 95% CI) and SAM of 8.6% (4.6-15.5 95% CI). Gogrial East County: FSNMS round 30 indicated a GAM of 10.4% (6.7-15.9 95% CI) and SAM of 0.9% (0.1- 8.7 95% CI). In order to improve the nutrition situation and reduce the very high GAM prevalence in the eight (8) counties mentioned above, Action Against Hunger will scale-up and strengthen nutrition treatment and preventive interventions in coordination with Ministry of Health as a transition strategy and work with community structures on ground as a localization approach in addressing of malnutrition in South Sudan.

2. Project activities

With the deterioration in nutrition situation in 2025, Action Against Hunger will scale-up and strengthen nutrition specific interventions through timely provision of treatment for children under 5 years with acute malnutrition through Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP), Stabilization Center (SC) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) and for malnourished Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women/Girls (PBWG) through TSFP, and expand malnutrition prevention portfolio in all the proposed eight (8) counties. Action Against Hunger will specifically implement the following reinforcing interventions: 1. Protect, support, and promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. 2. Provide Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) to vulnerable children 6-23 months of age and PBW 3. Provide micronutrient supplements to young children and women of reproductive age (WRA). 4. Screen and treat girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls diagnosed with acute malnutrition. 5. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development interventions in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition. 6. Collaborate with the WASH sector to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities on WASH messages. 7. Collaborate with FSL sector to promote chicken rearing and other small ruminants, establish kitchen gardens, promote seed multiplication for vegetable gardens, orange flesh sweet potatoes and support fish preservation. 8. Collaborate with health sector to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhea at the nutrition sites. 9. Work with the Protection sector to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified at the nutrition sites. 10. Link with Cash and Voucher Assistance programs to benefit nutritionally vulnerable children and women. 11. Undertake community engagement activities during the design, implementation, and monitoring of nutrition services to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations. 12. Promote school feeding among vulnerable communities and use schools to raise awareness on optimal nutrition practices. 13. Conduct regular program and field monitoring and supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making. 14. Conduct nutrition coordination meetings with the government, implementing partners, and donors. 15. Innovative malnutrition prevention activities like training of families of children under 2 for poultry rearing, integrate Baby WASH in nutrition program, Nurturing Care Group at community level, with a focus on 1000 days for improved behaviour change for malnutrition prevention 16. Hold regular community meetings at project sites with local leaders during planning and implementation of the project activities 17. Support the scale-up and implementation of Shock Responsive Cash Transfers (SRCT) in Urban and Peri-urban counties in South Sudan 18. Together with MoH, cascade and integrate GBV risk mitigation findings from Gogrial West County across all the counties in South Sudan in order to improve access and outcomes of nutrition treatment interventions. 19. Provide SMART+ capacity building to all partners implementing nutrition SMART surveys in South Sudan. 20. Implement high quality SMART+ surveys in 21 counties considered as a priority or has phase 3 or higher IPC for AMN in order to strengthen nutrition surveillance in the country in consultation with the NIWG. 21. Implement the pilot operationalization of the new WHO nutrition guidelines and scale-up of Conditional on Demand Assistance (CODA 2.0) in Aweil East and Aweil Center counties.
