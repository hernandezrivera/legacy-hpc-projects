Project Summary

The proposed project focuses on Food security and Livelihoods sector interventions. FH is proposing distribution of livelihood kits and training of farmers to increase agricultural production. FH proposes to promote conservation agriculture as a climate smart model that seeks to maintain agricultural productivity. FH also proposes to promote low input nutrition gardens to support diversified food production which reduces the rate of malnutrition. The lead farmer approach will be used to ensure the training rendered reach to as many farmers. FH also proposes to support vulnerable and poor households with chickens. Chickens are wholly owned by women at the household level, are easy to rear and can be slaughtered providing ready food at the household level. FH will integrate and collaborate with nutrition, protection and FFE partners through the involvement of CNV in agriculture training programmes, school feeding and setting up of kitchen gardens at nutrition centres. FSL interventions will target vulnerable food insecure populations who include, women, the elderly, female headed households and the disabled.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is experiencing a high food insecurity rate projected to worsen, with 60% of the population facing severe acute food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (IPC acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition analysis Oct 2020 to Jul 2021). This has been driven by two consecutive years of flooding affecting already vulnerable populations that experiencing low productivity from the impact of protracted conflict and violence. Other underlying causes include limited access to seed and tools, inefficient traditional farming practices, the economic crisis, high morbidity and high incidences of pests and diseases of crops and livestock. Following the flooding in 2020, multi-sectoral rapid assessments were conducted between December 2020 and January 2021 to ascertain the impact of the flooding and determine priority needs. In the four FH priority counties under this proposal (Upper Nile State –Panyikang, Ulang Counties; Jonglei State – Fangak and Pigi/Canal Counties), the following was recorded; a) 135,163 people were affected with 91% of them displaced, b) over 33,000 feddan of crops were destroyed including the destruction of stored food, c) thousands of livestock drowned, d) reduced livestock productivity (low or no milk and egg production) due to reduced grazing and stress, e).27 schools suffered physical infrastructure damage, including the destruction of both teaching and learning materials, and learning spaces for 6,034 children (56% of them girls)). This shows a dire situation because in a typical season, this is the period just after the main harvest when access to food would be best. In Ulang and Panyikang county, 70.8% of the population and in Fangak and Pigi/Canal, 84.7% of the population is estimated to require humanitarian assistance at the peak of the lean season, May to July 2021 (ibid). Inadequate food intake and low dietary diversity have caused a rapid increase in malnutrition rates with all targeted FH counties exhibiting IPC Acute Malnutrition IPC 4 outcomes (GAM rates between 15% and 29%) in October 2020 They are expected to deteriorate further as the food security situation worsens. Schools on higher ground are now hosting IDPs and are no longer used for school activities (Rapid Fangak, Pigi/Canal, Ulang and Panyikang Counties IRNA assessments - January 2021). Consequently, all selected Counties selected were classified IPC 4 (Emergency) in October 2020. The rapid assessments identified the following needs: FSL • Hunger due to lack of food • Increased child and early marriage accelerated by school closure due to COVID-19 • Lack of seeds and tools to support emergency and rapid food production and fishing• Lack of improved farming knowledge and disaster risk reduction • No/limited livestock to supplement agriculture production• Protection needs especially among most vulnerable groups

2 - Project activities

Proposed FSL Interventions in Fangak, Pigi/Canal and Ulang Counties1. Provision of livelihood kits - FH proposes to source livelihood kits from the FAO Pipeline and distribute to 24,525 food insecure households for rapid agriculture production. 2. Farmer training (Conservation Agriculture-CA)- Increased production is dependent on farmers' knowledge. CA is a farming system that promotes minimum soil disturbance, maintenance of a permanent soil cover and diversification and rotation. It showed potential under FH’s Relief to Displaced and Flood Affected Populations of South Sudan (RDFAP-SSIII) project in 2020, averaging 0.5MT/feddan compared to 0.3MT/feddan from farmers practice. FH proposes to train 5,040 farmers on this model using the lead farmer approach. The training will cover;i. Field sitting (on high ground, less susceptible to flooding) ii. Land preparation and sowing (including dyking, introduction of flood tolerant varieties, modifying cropping cycles to avoid flood periods)iii. Field crop management (including weed and integrated pest and disease management)iv. Post-harvest management and storage (improved storage to reduce flood losses)Demonstration farms will be established with lead farmers to showcase this farming model. FH will hold field days to showcase the model and subsequent yields to encourage its adoption by more farmers.3. Low Input Nutrition Gardens – This model uses low cost inputs and diversified cropping in a symbiotic manner, creating an ecosystem that ensures sustainable garden production. Cultural disease and pest management techniques based on crop rotations, intercropping, trap crops, habitat manipulation, natural enemies, mulching and garden site selection. The model is 100% organic with no use of fertilizers or chemicals. FH proposes to train 180 lead farmers and 4800 cascade farmers on this model. The training will focus on;i. Garden setupii. Nursery establishment and managementiii. Transplanting and sowingiv. Garden managementv. Seed selection and storagevi. Post-harvest management, preservation and storageField days will be conducted to showcase this model. 4. Support 3,300 households with local chickens through fairs. – Under RDFAP-SSIII, women said they prefer chickens to other livestock as they have control over them. Chickens are readily available for slaughter and egg production. They do not require intense labour, so they can be reared by the elderly and PWD. Each beneficiary household will receive 2 pullets and 1 cockerel. As a precondition to receiving chickens, beneficiaries will be required to attend training on free-range chicken management.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

FH has a robust protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) policy. FH will ensure this policy protects target communities through raising awareness and reporting provisions within the policy. In all project related gatherings, FH will take time to explain its PSEA policy, emphasizing the right of the community to report any SEA cases and the available reporting mechanisms. FH will also produce banners in English and in local languages, with basic information on its PSEA policy and available reporting options. The banners will be displaced at all community meetings, at the gate of the respective FH field offices and in public spaces and schools. There are existing child protection clubs in each of the targeted schools. Through this proposed project, FH will reinforce training on reporting of suspected SEA cases through the toll free phone numbers and the suggestions boxes.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As part of the activity planning process, FH will separately engage groups of PWD or their representatives, women, girls and the elderly, to understand their special needs/barriers that can prevent them from participating in the project. FH will also conduct security and safety audits before the project starts to understand how vulnerable groups can safely participate in the project without being exposed to risk. This will include but is not limited to, agreement on places to hold activities like distributions, registrations and training, days and time for conducting project activities that do not interfere with other livelihood or social activities, and modifications necessary to improve access to project activities especially for PWD and the elderly, (for example, expanding the gate into the demonstration gardens for easy wheelchair access. Through the GESS 2 project, FH, with technical support from Leonard Cheshire (FH disability technical partner), is involved in sensitizing the families with children with disabilities (CWD) on how they can support them at home and what it takes to enroll them into school and so they can continue learning. Plans are underway for FH, in partnership with Leonard Cheshire, to administer the Washington Group of Questions (WGQ) to schools/communities. This tool will collect information and data forintervention planning and implementation that involves the participation of CWDs. Quarterly, FH will Vulnerable groups to review address and modify the approach to project activities based on their feedback

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Inception meetings will be held at the county, payam and school levels with government representatives, stakeholders and community representatives at that level. Inception meetings will also be done at the Boma level with traditional leadership, village heads and youth, elderly, women and PWD representatives. After that, a series of community mobilization and sensitization meetings will be conducted at the start of the project with the community to ensure everyone has enough information about the project including, objectives, targeted population, caseloads, and the role of different community actors in the project. During registrations and distributions, FH will display banners showing beneficiary entitlements. At the school level, FH will hold quarterly meetings with the SMCs members to plan, implement and monitor each school’s activities. FH has an established complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM), which is part of its accountability system that ensures targetpopulations have access to safe and reliable reporting mechanisms. The CFM includes establishing a properly constituted help desk at every community meeting, suggestion boxes at strategic locations in the county identified together with the community, anonymous complaints email option, an international toll-free number, and a local reverse charged mobile number with a WhatsApp option that is managed by the Human Resources Manager at Juba Level. A local accountability committee with representation of women, elderly, PWD, will be established to continuously interact with the project and capture their feedback. These reporting details are displayed during all community meetings and at public places and schools across the counties. FH also encourages the community to speak with FH staff on the ground or seniormanagement staff when they visit project sites regarding any complaints they might have towards project staff or the community. When senior project staff visit the project areas they have confidential FGDs with separate groups of school children including girls and boys, men, women, the elderly and PWD and get any complaints and feedback directly. All complaints are treated with confidence and all FH staff must act accordingly with due diligence on any complaint raised to them by the community. All Project Managers and field supervisors are responsible for ensuring e that the feedback loop is closed by giving feedback to the community on all issues raised through the CFM.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All complaints/feedback received will be logged on to a complaints/feedback log sheet. If the complaint/feedback is something that can be addressed by the supervisor at the field level, he/she has an obligation to adapt activities basedon the feedback. For instance, if a complaint of an inclusion error is reported, the field supervisor will raise the complaint with the Project Management Committee (PMC), and a verification household visit will be conducted. If the inclusion error is confirmed, the beneficiary is removed from the list and a replacement is identified and verified with active involvement of the PMC. If several inclusion and exclusion errors are reported, FH will call for a community meeting to refine the targeting criteria. If complaints cannot be dealt with at the field level, they are escalated to the FH Office in Juba for action by themanagement team or the senior leadership as appropriate. The feedback loop is only closed when it signed off by a senior member of FH staff as having been satisfactorily resolved. At the school level, the complaints/feedback raised by learners will be discussed during monthly SMC meetings and addressed.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

● Risk of interference from external stakeholder. FH will hold inception meetings with all stakeholders to ensure all are aware of project objectives, target and targeting criteria. FH will use a community participatory process to refine targeting criteria to be context specific and acceptable to all stakeholders. FH will ensure representatives of vulnerable groups are incorporated in all project structures and that they are consulted throughout the project cycle. FH will ensure continuous awareness raising of its CFM and will react promptly to feedback raised through the CFM. FH will do ongoing stakeholder and local power analysis and mitigate negative interference.● Risk that marginalized groups, will not participate in project. Before project start, FH will conduct an induction training for all project staff, SMCs, PTAs, County and Payam Education authorities on disability awareness. Facilitated by Leonard Cheshire (FH disability technical partner), the training will focus on disability awareness, improving the physical activity environment, and packaging and disseminating information in alternative forms compatible with PWD. FH will: Identify groups of PWD, organizations for PWDs, the elderly, youth, women and girls to inform them about the project, identify those interested, and collect data of PWD interested using the WGQ (for PWD); Budget more time for activities to accommodate PWD and the elderly who may be slow in accomplishing tasks; Ensure activities begin with confidence building for PWD who may lack self-assurance due to prolonged stigma and exclusion; Ensure the environment for all activities is safe for PWD and the elderly to perform tasks without risk of injury of fatigue; Arrange for family member support where needed to ensure the PWD and elderly can be supported during the activity and at home; In consultation with Leonard Cheshire, support PWD, elderly and other frail individuals with basic adaptation of the home environment to enable them to work at home; Simple modifications may include raising garden beds; creating more space between garden beds to enable wheelchair movement; Siting gardens close to water source and closer home; creating sitting/resting places around the garden. - Link PWDs with organisations that provide appropriate assistive devices; School authorities trained on disability and inclusion will act as advocates to ensure schools’ infrastructure is inclusive for all physically and mentally challenged children, teachers and family members. For example, advocating for building classrooms that are wheelchair accessible by CWD. The Community volunteers will conduct house-to-house sensitization of the IDPs/returnee community to ensure CWD and other disadvantages children are supported to enroll and attend lessons regularly.● Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using CFM.FH will; Set up the CFM in consultation with communities so that the CFM is accessible to all (women, elderly, PWD); Offer more than one feedback option so beneficiaries can choose a mechanism comfortable to them; Ensure confidentiality and closure of feedback loop to build confidence and trust among the target communities; Periodically review and modify the CFM with active participation of beneficiaries, especially vulnerable group.● Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence.FH will; Conduct a safety audit as part of the inception process to identify potential safety and security concerns of the targeted communities, particularly speaking with vulnerable groups (elderly, PWD, women, girls and boys); In consultation with key informants and vulnerable groups, identify and map safe and accessible zones that can be used for project activities; Endeavor to deliver services as close to the communities as possible to reduce the distance travelled by beneficiaries to access assistance. Community Mobilization Volunteers will track any suspected violence and FH will work closely with the protection cluster.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the two Collective Outcomes, i.e. Food insecurity and Gender-Based Violence?

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

FH will pay particular attention to impacts of insecurity on girls and women and vulnerability to GBV (e.g. provide escorts to and from school for girls, employ classroom assistants, provide girls with reporting guidelines and follow-up procedures, orient teachers on GBV and distribute codes of conduct to each volunteer teachers). The FH staff who will be recruited and the SMC members will monitor sexual harassment; early child or forced marriages and provide confidential complaint reporting mechanisms and follow-up with clear procedures. FH will work with protection partners and use existing referral systems already established.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The proposed intervention will contribute to increased access to food through increased production agriculture production. The proposed activities will also increase household food increased diversity which will contribute to reduced malnutrition rates (protein from fish, chickens/eggs plus diversified vegetables from the garden). Where a surplus is produced, it can be sold to buy other food items like oil, salt and money can also be used to improve access to health services which improves well-being and utilization of food. House to house and school visits by the community mobilization volunteers will encourage children on malnutrition treatment to continue with their treatment. The school gardening will ensure children have access to balance diet and hence improve their health statuses.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

FH will establish some of the demonstration plots on schools to support learning and the produce will be used to complement school feeding programs. Most teachers in rural schools are untrained. FH will invite teachers to participate in the lead farmer training intervention for personal development and acquire practical farming knowledge they can pass to learners. The involved of the learners in the school gardens will enhance their practical skills and knowledge on gardening and enable children to build positive attitudes towards farming.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Households with under-fives on malnutrition treatment will be enrolled in FH nutrition gardens initiative to prevent them from sliding back into malnutrition after treatment. FH will establish low input nutrition gardens at 15 selected malnutrition treatment centres across the targeted 4 counties to promote these as a sustainable strategy to light malnutrition at the household level targeting households on malnutrition treatment visiting these centres. FH will also incorporate 30 Community Nutrition Volunteers (CVN) in low input nutrition gardening training so they can manage the gardens at the nutrition centre and also pass this information to malnutrition patients. FH will team up with CVN to conduct cooking demonstrations in the community to improve utilization of farm produce. The Community Mobilization volunteers will visit the targeted schools and households to ensure children who are on malnutrition treatment do not miss their treatment.
