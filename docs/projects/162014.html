Project Summary

Targeting: The project primarily addresses the impact of the refugee crisis on the livelihoods and natural resource base of 49,711 unserved beneficiaries (of which, 48% women) comprised of 9,038 farming households of the host communities in the targeted areas (FSS ReportHub). The project aims to provide inputs and innovative technology support that will develop and promote productive and climate smart technologies, which will enhance farm profitability and household incomes. Moreover, the capacity building support to the public and private extension agents will enhance the GOB’s efforts to integrate nutrition-sensitive and gender-inclusive agricultural extension services to the targeted communities. FAO will continue to support the actors of the Food Security Sector, through its role as co-chair of the Livelihoods Working Group, to improve the targeting of all of the humanitarian response and development actors in 2020.
Approaches to Food and Nutrition Security: For food security and nutrition enhancement, FAO has identified two distinct approaches for achieving the FSS JRP 2020 outcome one: (1) The first approach targets individual, marginal and smallholder farmers and groups that are not currently in a viable position to enter into commercial farming. This approach focuses on food diversity and nutrition awareness amongst the poorest households. These farmers will receive appropriate inputs and technical support to increase farm production and household income. (2) The second approach targets agricultural groups with higher potential for commercial or collective production. These farmer groups will see investments in their technical capacity to benefit from the ongoing market expansion due to the increased demand. Increased local farmer incomes will contribute to social cohesion in areas around the camps.
Women and Markets: In addition, the project will work closely with the public and private sectors to identify market opportunities and strategies to expand crop production into new and underdeveloped areas with the potential to increase household incomes, improve nutrition and contribute to greater economic empowerment of women.
DRR and Environmental Rehabilitation: One of the main environmental issues has been widespread deforestation due to the collection of firewood as a source of fuel to cook food. More than 2 000 hectares (5 000 acres) of stripped forest area has been severely damaged. Water resources have also been put under considerable pressure and are a source of social conflict between the Refugee and host communities. FAO will continue to address the increased vulnerability to landslides and floods and play a major role in coordinating host community needs through its role as co-chair of the Energy and Environment Technical Working Group.
The project aims to work closely with government and non-government partners, including other UN agencies, to promote erosion control and watershed management strategies, and to identify areas vulnerable to landslides in achieving outcome two. Land stabilization and reforestation activities are planned for 500 hectares of degraded lands inside and outside the camps are designed to contribute to the immediate protection of communities by reducing risks of flash flooding and landslides. The restored tree cover will also help to recharge ground water and protect from cyclone wind damage. The effects of the last two-year’s plantation efforts are already proving effective in this regard.
Accountability to Affected Participants: FAO will continue to utilize its existing internal systems of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) to ensure that all activities incorporate measures of information sharing and recognition of vulnerable groups, as well as grievance gathering and address.

Objectives

Strategic Objectives: Sector Objectives: SO2- Promote portable skills development opportunities for Rohingya and enhance the livelihood and resiliency of host communities; SO3- Address and mitigate environmental impact caused by Rohingya refugees influx and support social cohesion through enhancement and restoration of natural resources. The overall objective of the project is to enhance economic and climatic resilience of affected host communities through improvement of household income and environmental rehabilitation. The specific objectives are: (i) To improve household income through strengthening of farm production and market linkage in enhancing sustainable livelihood activities for the host communities; and (ii) To enhance environmental rehabilitation and disaster risk reduction through eco-system restoration and watershed management.

Needs

The population of Cox’s Bazar, especially those living in Teknaf and Ukhia, were already vulnerable before the recent influx of Rohingya. These households also face high dependency ratios, and gender gaps in the working age population with a relative shortage of men. Secondary school enrollments among children are also lower than the national average. As per Cox’s Bazar Panel Survey by the World Bank shows that the host community in Cox’s Bazar is young (with 38 percent below the age of 15) and has high dependency ratios (with 0.89 dependents per adult of working age and 1.7 dependents per employed adults of working age). This situation further deteriorated with the ongoing Rohingya refugee’s crisis which has put enormous strain on the host communities affecting their socio-economic circumstances, particularly in four sub-districts of Cox’s Bazar namely Teknaf, Ukhiya, Ramu and Cox’s Bazar Sadar. Recently conducted Baseline Study by FAO shows that household (HH) income in these sub-districts is mostly (38.32%) agriculture (crops, livestock and fisheries) depended. About half of the HHs had the lowest dietary diversity and belong to borderline or poor food consumption status. It is evident that the affected population is in dire need of support to improve their livelihoods and dietary diversity. The livelihood potential of majority of the population who are agriculture dependent often limited by loss of agricultural land, inaccessibility of improved technology, limited technical knowledge and skills, poor market linkage, lack of organization, inadequate extension and advisory support, limited private sector investment, and vulnerability to natural disasters. In particular, women are less empowered in two domains – ‘resource’ and ‘leadership’(FAO, 2019).Despite having significant potential, fisheries and livestock sub-sectors are also constrained due to lack of technical knowledge and awareness on livestock and fishery management and development, inadequate supply of inputs, middlemen-dominated value chain and lack of organization among the farmers, particularly among marginalized smallholders. Since the Rohingya influx, approximately 8,000 acres of forestland has been razed and trees have been cut down to set up camps and cook food resulting in an enormous threat to wildlife habitats, biodiversity, and entire ecosystems in the region. In addition, water resources and groundwater reserves have degraded significantly. The deforestation rate on that particular area is devastating, as the land has stripped of all above ground biomass and entire root systems from below the ground. As a result, the numbers of water pumps in the camps are no longer functioning due to failed recharge of underground water sources, despite controls placed on new installations. This unusual situation greatly decreases the potential for natural regeneration of forest resources. Therefore, awareness raising and advocacy efforts for planning and mitigation of both are sorely required. A UNDP, UN Women conducted study on environmental Impact of Rohingya Influx identified eleven environmental impacts that have been or could potentially be exacerbated by the Rohingya influx. Six of these were physical environmental impacts on: ground water; surface water; acoustic levels; indoor air quality; solid waste management; and soils and terrain; and the remaining five were impacts on ecosystems: natural forests; protected areas and critical habitats; vegetation; wildlife; and marine and freshwater ecosystems. To address this impacts, reforestation, soil conservation and land need to be taken as priority through community/social forestry, artificial natural regeneration of shrub dominated areas, afforestation along the coastal line and agroforestry in the village common forests.

Activities or outputs

Outcome-1: Improved Households’ Income through Sustainable Livelihood Activities Activities 1.1 Agriculture 1.1.1 Strengthen Farmers Field School (FFS) approach for introducing improved technologies and collective actions; 1.1.2 Strengthen Market Farmers’ Group (MFG) in accessing potential markets; 1.1.3 Engage public (DAE, DLS, DOF, LGs) and private sectors in enhancing coordination and improved ICT based extension services to the farmers; 1.1.4 Strengthen farm mechanization and farmers’ access to quality inputs and agricultural credits; 1.1.5 Promote innovative water conservation technologies and practices to prevent resource depletion and disputes. 1.2 Fisheries 1.2.1 Support capacity building of fish farmers’ through Common Beneficiary Group (CBG) approach in improved technologies and market linkages development; 1.2.2 Promote improved technologies for integrated aquaculture and fish processing; 1.2.3 Promote good aquaculture practices through advocacy and community awareness programs; 1.2.4 Promote alternative livelihood opportunities for the fisher folk, particularly during off-season and restricted periods. Activities 1.3: Livestock 1.3.1 Develop Local Service Providers (LSP) to support the livestock farmers in livestock disease and health management practices; 1.3.2 Develop capacity of livestock farmers on feed and fodder production, preservation and management practices; 1.3.3 Strengthen public (DLS) and private sectors engagement to facilitate quality services for the livestock farmers; 1.3.4 Strengthen farmers’ groups for collective selling of livestock products and access to input market. Outcome-2: Enhanced Environmental Rehabilitation and Disaster Risk Reduction 2.1. Create alternative income generation opportunities for forest dependent people and communities; 2.2. Enhance capacity of field level officials and ensure necessary resources (logistics and physical support); 2.3. Support restoration of degraded forest areas following the FLR (Forest Landscape restoration) approach with special consideration on watershed and its catchment areas; 2.4. Conduct hydrological resources and water use survey – Ukhiya and Teknaf

Key project/performance indicators
