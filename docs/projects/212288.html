Project Summary

Human Appeal (HA) has developed a comprehensive proposal to provide Early Recovery and Livelihoods assistance to the most affected communities in Northwest Syria. The primary goal is to enhance community resilience and reduce socio-economic vulnerabilities. To delve into the key components of this project, it will be through:
- Short-Term Livelihood Opportunities:
HA will create 200 short-term work opportunities through the Cash-for-Work (CFW) approach. Unskilled laborers from the targeted community and camps will contribute to road rehabilitation, market restoration, and bakery rehabilitation activities.
- Improved Physical Access:
By rehabilitating approximately 8 kilometers of roads, HA aims to enhance access to the main market. This will facilitate the movement of goods and people, benefiting both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities.
- Vocational Training and Skills Development:
HA will provide vocational training for 200 individuals in various fields, including babysitting and parenting, electronic literacy, knitting, wool manufacturing, homemade detergents, sewing, and hairdressing.
Additionally, a startup business grant program will empower 200 participants in the targeted camps.
- Support for Local Businesses and Entrepreneurs:
The project aims to restore and support local small businesses and entrepreneurs, contributing to economic revitalization.
- Essential Community Services:
HA will design a solid waste and rubble removal plan, ensuring proper waste management.
Personal protective tools will be provided, and the rubble removal process will be supervised and monitored by CFW laborers.
- Social Engagement and Monitoring:
Community-selected members will actively engage in project activities, ensuring transparency and accountability.
Human Appeal (HA) aims to directly benefit 23,000 individuals through various project components, including market rehabilitation, bakery restoration, road improvements, and other essential services. These beneficiaries consist of 6,200 households from both internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities. The breakdown by gender and age groups is as follows: Men: 4,680 /Women: 4,720/ Boys: 6,720/ Girls: 6,880. Additionally, approximately 650 Persons with Disabilities will be included in the project.
As for Do No Harm Approach, HA is committed to minimizing negative impacts on local producers. To achieve this, HA will actively engage with community and local stakeholders during project design and implementation. This collaborative approach ensures that the project aligns with local needs and avoids unintentional harm. Furthermore, related to the coordination and harmonization: HA will work closely with relevant Clusters and NGOs operating in the targeted areas. This coordination aims to prevent duplication of efforts and ensure efficient delivery of assistance, particularly in Cash-for-Work (CFW) activities.
This project aims to promote inclusivity and equal participation across diverse groups.
Additionally, approximately 650 (PwDs) will actively participate in the project. Throughout the project lifecycle, women and individuals with disabilities will be actively consulted during the design, planning, and implementation phases.
The project will proactively identify and address heightened risks faced by individuals with disabilities. Measures will be put in place to ensure their safety, well-being, and full participation.
Barriers hindering the involvement will be systematically identified and addressed.
Robust feedback channels will be established to address concerns, complaints, and suggestions from project participants. Transparent and efficient mechanisms will ensure timely resolution. The project will adhere to PSEA standards, safeguarding the rights and dignity of all participants.
By implementing these strategic interventions, HA aims to foster sustainable recovery, strengthen community bonds, and improve overall well-being in Northwest Syria.
