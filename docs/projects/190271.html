Project Summary

The Food Security Monitoring System (FSMS) is a household survey conducted to track trends of key food security indicators and report the current food security and nutrition situation in terms of household food availability, access and utilization. The findings are used to strengthen food security and nutrition early warning systems, evidence-based programming, and for advocacy purposes. This survey complements the data collected from other food security and nutrition data collection systems in the country, especially the food security multisectoral assessments led by the government etc.
FSMS main objectives: Identifies, analyses and monitors risks and vulnerability trends in food availability, access, utilisation stability (food insecurity and malnutrition) of target population; therefore provides timely and reliable information to determine emergency interventions while also informing medium and long-term measures required to tackle acute and chronic vulnerability development/resilience related social protection and safety net programming on chronic food insecurity and livelihood vulnerabilities to government and development partners, working in Ethiopia.
In relation to the ICCG and assessment working group to the HPC process, the FSMS will contribute to the evidence base, in identifying key needs, gaps, opportunities and informed targeting. Additionally, the results of the assessment provides an addition reference towards the needs and requirements that clusters may be aiming to assisting and advocate for through their humanitarian operations.
Indicators around the three food dimensions will be collected, analysed and reported on as follows:
1.Availability Indicators: Agricultural production and household access to farming land (reasons for cultivating less this season, expected yield this season (larger, same, less, constraints to crop/livestock production)
2. Food Access Indicators: Food consumption Score; dietary diversity score, reduced; household hunger scale; Coping strategies index (rCSI), livelihood based coping strategies, households debt, and expenditure patterns, main income sources, humanitarian access coverage)
3.Food Utilization Indicators; Health and nutrition situation (Water & sanitation, incidence of illness, any child admitted due to malnutrition). MUAC measurements may also be included on case-by-case basis.
4.Context indicators: status of the HH head (Sex, age, education level, disability, etc), average HH size, Access to basic infrastructure and amenities
METHODOLOGY: The FSMS survey will employ a cross-sectional study design with a quantitative and qualitative data collection method. The survey is designed in such a way that the final analysis of the data will provide a reliable estimate of population in need at Admin zone level. The proportion of population in different level of food insecurity at the unit of analysis/livelihood level will be applied to the woredas in the livelihood to estimate the number of populations in different phases of food insecurity at a woreda level.
Frequency and timing of data collection: The proposal is to conduct the FSMS to detect the highest and lowest levels of food insecurity in all livelihood zones (Belg, Meher, pastoral lists) dominant areas to 1) allow for early release of the findings to enable decision makers make projections of the population likely to be food insecure before the peak of the lean season, to be able to plan and deliver support when most needed at the peak of the lean season for the respective livelihood zones: and 2) to assess the impact of the long rains on food and nutrition security, taking into account the cumulative effects of previous seasons and other shocks and hazards.

1. - Projects activities

The main activity is to conduct FSMS surveys twice a year in all livelihood zones (Belg, Meher, pastoral lists) dominant areas of Ethiopia as per the below calenderMeher dominant areas including Tigray; conduct FSMS in MAY lean season monitoring & OCTOBER (harvest; annual to feed into HNO analysis) Belg: conduct FSMS in MAY (monitoring)(mid year HRP) & OCTOBER (update, annual)Northern Pastoral conduct FSMS in JANUARY medium pasture monitoring & SEPTEMBER good pasture in a normal rainy season (update, annual)Southern Pastoral conduct FSMS in JANUARY lean dry monitoring) & OCTOBER rainy season (update, annual)Associated activities included the following:For each round of assessment:Updating the implementation plan, questionnaires and tools preparation and programming, enumerator recruitment, training for data collection, data collection in the field including supervision, data consolidation in the databases, management, analysis, reporting and dissemination of the findings in the various coordination forums.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

To the extent possible the FSMS data collection will consider a participatory approach. The sensitization responsibilities will be conducted by the EDRMC at the regional, zonal and woreda level office regarding the data collection. Before the start of the interview the selected households will also be briefed on the purpose of data collection and confidentiality of the information they will provide, in ensuring their maximum participation. Although the FSMS questionnaire and tools will be translated into local language to ensure that communities participate fully, for the case of some languages where translation may not be feasible, services of CSOs and advocacy groups will be sought during data collection for these areas to ensure that the participate to the maximum participation.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

During qualitative data collection through focus group discussions, all cross cutting groups in the community-e.g youth,. Men and women, civil society, beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, government officials, civil servants, are represented in the discussions to ensure holistic feedback from the communities. The FSMS questionnaire and tools will be translated into local language to ensure that the most vulnerable in the community households under which data is being collected are well represented.Sampled beneficiary lists will be provided to the enumerators by supervisors. At the beginning of each interview, voluntary verbal consent will be obtained from the respondent for conducting the interviews. The objective of the survey will be clearly explained to the respondents to avoid suspicion.Respondents will be informed that all information will be treated with strict confidentiality. Respondents will be given the opportunity to do the interview at that time or to be revisited at a time that is more convenient to them. The name of the respondents will not be recorded without the respondent's consent/ and or asked for this. No raw data will be accessible to any person who is not part of the WFP and its partner in this survey if any.Furthermore, the analysis will consider a gender and age group lens, ensuring that for most of the indicators to the extent possible the findings are disaggregated by gender of the household head and age groups.
