Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Somalia is worsening due to the ongoing impacts of drought, conflict, locust infestation and the COVID 19 pandemic. In the last few months of 2021, almost all parts of the country have been experiencing severe drought conditions. According to the Food Security Nutrition Analysis unit assessment of September 2021, nearly 3.5 million people in Somalia would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. This includes 1.1 million Internally Displaced Persons who will face moderate to large food consumption gaps. In addition, 3.7 million people are classified in IPC phase 2 (Stressed) engaging in negative coping strategies to meet their food and non-food needs, bringing the total number of people in Somalia experiencing acute food insecurity to 7.2 million by late 2021. A total of 2.9 million IDPs have been displaced by conflict, insecurity, drought and/or floods, the majority of whom are currently located in over 2,400 IDP sites across Somalia, often in informal settlements on private land in urban areas. The majority of IDPs across Somalia are poor with limited livelihood assets, few income earning opportunities, low communal support and high reliance on external humanitarian assistance. As a result, a large proportion of IDPs (in both rural and urban settlements) face significant overlapping inter-sectoral needs and moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. Furthermore, the majority of the IDPs have no official documentation for the – mostly privately owned - land on which they reside, exposing them to repeated evictions. People living with disabilities (PLWDs) are especially the most disadvantaged groups in the society especially in all areas of political, social and economic life in the region. Consideration of their needs is almost nonexistent. Currently, there is no specific national legal or policy framework regarding PLWD, although the Provisional Constitution does recognize and provide for the protection of their rights. This particular project will give specific consideration to PLWDs with 7% of the project services set a side for PLWDs. It is against this background this WASDA is proposing this is an emergency health project that will be implemented in Badhaade and Afmadow district -Lower Juba Somalia. The project will support provision of primary healthcare services at three (2) static Maternal & Child Health Clinics & five (5) outreach sites, Communicable disease, Reproductive health, Community health, Pharmaceuticals and other medical commodities. Fixed site will be located; Deg-elema and Waberi village of Dobley (WADA nutrition center); mobile sites will be in Dardaar, Abaq bib, Hagar dabataag, Abdi sugow and Adan Santur villages. The project will target a total beneficiary of 30,000 beneficiaries consisting of under-five, pregnant and lactating women as well adults. The emergency Health project will focus on scaling up curative and preventives services through fixed facilities and mobile sites to reach the most vulnerable affected populations in Lower Juba. Continuous monitoring of project activities will enhance proper implementation of project activities and reach more vulnerable. Support supervision will be done by the Health Manager and other senior management team to provide support and strengthen the staff capacity as well ensure effective project implementation. WASDA has been working in the lower Juba for the past 21 years implementing project such as WASH, livelihoods, Nutrition and Health projects. WASDA is currently implementing SHF funded integrated WASH and health projects in Kismayo and Badhadhe districts.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1: Provision of primary health care services at two (2) static Maternal & Child Health Clinics & five (5) outreach sites: WASDA will provide equitable access to integrated health and WASH services at both fixed health facilities and through mobile teams. The health team will be comprised of 2 qualified nurses, 2 midwives, 1 auxiliary nurse, 1 pharmacist and 1 CHW. The team of 7 will provide both curative and preventative health services including outpatient consultations, immunizations, reproductive health services such as ANC and PNC. The mobile teams will provide the same services but through an outreach modality. Activity 2: Capacity building for health staff; WASDA will provide capacity building to 18 nurses, pharmacists and midwives working in the health facilities and in mobile teams: They will be trained on case management, EPI, Reproductive health, Health Management Information System (HMIS) and medical supply management. Activity 3: Provision of prevention, control & treatment for epidemic-prone & communicable diseases at 2 static facilities & 5 mobile sites: WASDA will provide technical leadership to health WASDA staff and ensure training and treatment guidelines are provided to all health centers to treat and control communicable diseases. WASDA will also conduct community based health educations through community based health workers. During the outpatient consultations care givers and patients will be assessed using the IMCI guidelines and undergo simple rapid diagnostics test if need be. Activity 4: Reporting in support of the Communicable Disease Surveillance and Response Early Warning and Response Network – EWARNS: WASDA will support health management information system in Jubaland by submitting monthly district level health reports to the DHMIS tool. Reproductive health; Activity 5: Provision of equitable access to Postnatal care (PNC) health services at 2 static and 5 outreach sites: WASDA will strengthen reproductive health service provision at the supported sites. 14 (midwives and nurses) will be trained on reproductive health services provisions and will provide Antenatal care (ANC) and Postnatal care (PNC) to all pregnant and lactating mothers attending the health center/site. The health center will also provide Basic and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric WASDA (BEmOC). WASDA will install solar panels to provide electricity and to make facility-based deliveries and sterilization of delivery equipment’s possible to prevent cross contamination. Activity 6: Provision of access to facility-based safe deliveries at 2 static sites: Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is one main causes of maternal mortality in Somalia. Following baby deliveries at health facilities or at home, the midwife will observe the mother and check her vital signs to ensure that she is healthy and there is no indication of PPH. Following discharge there will be a follow up visit within the first 24 hours and if not possible within the first week. Additionally, all mother who delivers at HFs and mobile sites will be provided with appreciation kits (kit is composed of delivery basic items, shawls, bar soaps, bathing soap, nail cutter etc.). Activity 7: Training of health staff on ANC, early detection, treatment & referral of pregnancy-related complications, PNC and other RH services: Nurses and midwives will receive training on the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health to ensure they provide good quality RH services. They will be trained in the early identification, treatment and referral of pregnancy complications. Community health; Activity 8: Support of the provision of individual counseling on positive heath practices at 2 static sites and 5 outreach sites: Activity 9: Provision of hygiene kits and NFI to most vulnerable HHs. Activity 10: Installation of hand washing stations at all HFs and mobile sites.
