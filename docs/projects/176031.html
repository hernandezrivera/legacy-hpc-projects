Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide food assistance and livelihood support to 1,086,00 vulnerable people in the former states of Greater Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria and Greater Bahr el Ghazal. The project will implement activities that will prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies of target beneficiaries in IPC 5, 4 and 3. The project l also seeks to enhance emergency food production and contribute to the reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs. World Vision will adopt a mix of intervention approaches including but not limited to unconditional and General Food Distribution (GFD), cash transfers, complementary livelihood support, capacity building (e.g. on agronomic practices), and resilience building at the household level. The proposed project will also be implemented in synergy with other sectors implemented by World Vision in the target locations such as health, nutrition, WASH, gender and protection related programming.

1 - Needs

Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report (December 2020) estimated that 6.35 million people (52.6% of the population) faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 2.102 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Moreover, the report further discussed that an estimated 24,000 people were classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County (11,000) in Pibor Administration Area, and in Tonj North County (13,000) in Warrap State. The most food insecure states are Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Lakes, Warrap and Northern Bahr el Ghazal where more than 50% of their respective populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. WV operates in most of the states designated as the ‘most food insecure’. Contrary to last year’s food security situation projection where the rainy season contributed in part to the reduction in number of people that fell back to food insecurity trap, this year’s projection period December 2020 to March 2021, an estimated 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity and an estimated 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During rainy season, the projection shows that between April to July 2021 an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) with 31,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity and an estimated 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity.As part of the 2021 HRP, World Vision aims to reach the most vulnerable populations in Warrap, Upper Nile, Western Equatoria, Central Equatoria, and Greater Bhar el Ghazal states with food assistance, emergency food production through support of agricultural and fisheries inputs, and to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. This support will improve households’ resilience to floods, insecurity incidents, macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. The food security situation in the targeted counties is very critical (IPC 3, IPC4 and IPC5) in terms of food consumption, dietary diversification, reduced coping strategies, level of hunger and livelihood coping strategies. The December 2020 IPC report revealed that 55.3% of the population in Warrap, 52.7% in Greater Bahr el Ghazal, 58% in Upper Nile states need immediate humanitarian assistance with Crisis (IPC Phase 3), Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and catastrophic (IPC phase 5). IDPs, Refugee, returnees, and host communities are particularly vulnerable and many of them switched to negative coping strategies including both selling their assets and decreasing the number of meals per day per person. Due to continued conflict, flood, COVID-19 restrictions, and economic meltdown, the market situation is hampered. Some counties try to revive the market but lack of productive asset remains a challenge. World Vision in partnership with different donors (WFP, FAO, USAID, DFID, GAC) has been implementing Conditional Food and Cash distributions in the target locations but huge food gaps still exist and the communities need immediate support. Cash Based Intervention will be done in proper manner because it may trigger a risk of returnee’s influx in the target locations. More so, community cohesion and mobilization, empowerment of women and youth development is a priority in addressing tensions between refugees and host communities that influence refugee-host community relations, limiting the local protection space and opportunities for interdependence. Such tension results from use of land, natural resources, firewood collection, access to medical, educational and infrastructure facilities, family disputes, overstretched absorption capacity of the conflict affected communities.

2 - Project activities

Project activities include: Objective 1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & 3)• Food assistance-general food distribution • Cash and voucher distribution (Conditional and unconditional)• Conditional food transfer (i.e., FFA)• Focus on seasonal food assistance with other multi sector integrated actions (Protection, WASH, Nutrition & Health actors)• Post distribution monitoring (PDM)• Focus on incorporating flexible contingency plans to adapt (CCAP) Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support• Emergency food productiono Main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fish, vegetable and crop kits• Emergency food & livelihood supporto Fishing kits/ vegetable kits• Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatmento Vaccination campaign supporto CAHWs refresher training and vet kits support Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb shocks• Organize seed fairs using locally sourced seed• Support market functionality and support value chain addition• Training & capacity buildingo Adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots)o Income generation (business skills), vocational training• Multi sectoral modelso livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitationo Reducing pest and crop diseaseo Post-harvest losses management including introduction of appropriate technologieso Village saving & loan schemes & micro finance

3 - Project indicators

World Vision South Sudan uses a results-based management to deliver project results. The project results will be closely monitored through a dedicated monitoring and evaluation system, consisting of tested tools such as Indicator Tracking Tables, Detailed Implementation Plan, and project tracker. This tracking is done on regular bases (monthly, quarterly, and annually) with results measured and quantified. The following key indicators, among others, will be closely monitored:- Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers - Number of beneficiaries receiving agricultural inputs - Number of people trained in improved agronomic practices- Number of livestock vaccinated and treated During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked on a rolling basis.

5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project is designed in a way that cash interventions are based on the actual context of the local markets. Cash will be used where markets are accessible, provide the needed quantities and qualities of commodities, and the operating conditions remain stable. World Vision will conduct regular market assessment to inform the decisions around the use of cash. The assessment will capture the functionality of market in the target areas and also consider the changing needs of the target population.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

WV recommends the use of cash because using cash revives the local economy, promotes beneficiary dignity, and empowers the household to make choices that best suit them. Before embarking on the actual modality selection, WV will conduct a quick market assessment for actual modality recommendation. Moreover, WV is not recommending cash for all counties but for counties where the market is functioning well enough to support the right quantities and access to commodity/service needs of the affected populations. Currently, World Vision uses the same modality (cash and voucher) in its operation areas using funds obtained from different donors.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Mass awareness will be done to the beneficiaries on PSEA and other protection concerns this will include topics such as prevention, reporting of PSEA cases, and services related to psychological first aid.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

During feedback collection sessions, the team will take deliberate steps to make sure the voices of the vulnerable groups are recorded and their needs met. Other feedback mechanisms will also be made accessible to people with special needs, women, girls, boys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Information sharing with the beneficiaries will be done through the channels that will be preferred by the community at the time. This could be through public announcements, radio messaging, open meetings (mindful of covid-19), project management committee, or through local authorities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary feedback will be used to make specific decisions such as the choice of distribution sites, beneficiary selection, distribution protocol, meeting special needs of vulnerable groups

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The specific risks identified above will be addressed as follows:8.1: Registration and distribution points will be thoroughly assessed and located at the safest points in the village. Distributions will be done at appropriate times in the day to allow closure before the dark8.2: Women, girls and boys will be sensitized on protection concerns, prevention, and where to get psychological first aid in case of violations8.5: When doing assessments, information sharing, and awareness sessions, the team will ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project by directly involving them in the discussions.8.6: The project will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all members of the community through increasing access points, diversifying feedback channels, etc. Overall, the project will be based on a protection risk analysis considering the specific needsof different population groups. The project will do protection risk analysis by identifying various possible risks and ascertain their level of severity, likelihood and impact. Barriers to access for different groups will be identified and addressed. The team is mindful the barriers to access could be social, distance, or security concerns. These barriers will be identified and addressed as appropriate.
