Project Summary

The protracted humanitarian crisis in Somalia is multi-layered and complex. Limited development coupled with recuring climatic shocks, such as drought and riverine-/flash-flooding give rise to high levels of need among affected populations, while insecurity and conflict severely hinder access to humanitarian actors. The majority of internally displaced persons (IDPs) reside in overcrowded shelters in densely populated urban areas, further increasing their exposure to the risks and impact of COVID-19. (REACH DSA Baidoa Factsheet March 2021)
A joint IDP site verification exercise was conducted in Baidoa in April 2021, 548 IDP sites were identified hosting 61,073 households or 430,991 individuals. Compared to the last site verification conducted in August 2020, there was an increase of 31 IDP sites and 1,152 Households or 38,717 individuals.
In response to this, READO will strengthen in Baidoa, site-level coordination structures to ensure access to quality basic services, improve living conditions in sites and mitigate protection concerns strengthen community-based led activities promoting on community self-management.

1. - Projects activities

Overall Objective: To improve the living conditions and protection of IDPs in sites and settlements and ensure equitable access to services and assistance of all persons in need, with a clear focus on moving toward attaining durable solutions with full participation of the displaced and host communities Activity 1: Site-level coordination structures to ensure access to quality basic services: READO will set up of CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts and establishment of CCCM in relevant coordination forums. Field level coordination structure in Baidoadistrict for government counterparts, line ministers, partners and IDP leaders and CCCM in relevant coordination forums at 􀂦eld level. READO will form SOPs for camp leaders/gatekeepers and partners working at the site level for a harmonized approach at maintaining high standards to improve coordination at the site level. As part of the exit strategy, READO will set a procedure for camp closure of the IDP settlements in line with the cluster’s exit strategy for long term durable solutions. Activity 2: Site maintenance to improve living conditions in sites and mitigate protection concerns: Relying on data collected on DSAs, partners, local coordination meetings, clusters, as well as throughmulti-cluster assessments, READO will map existing community infrastructures in the IDP settlements to ascertain which need improvement to minimize protection risks and ensure safety within target sites, READO intends to install 50 solar lights in communal areas (especially in areas leading to latrines and water points), which will aim to increase protection and prevent SGBV at community level. Activity 3: Access to information and community participation. READO in collaboration with Shelter and CCCM cluster and other relevant partners (including IOM DTM) will conduct intention surveys with ensuring the intentions of populations are understood and supported. This will involve conducting Detailed Site Assessments on an annual basis then establishing/updating the master list of sites at an appropriate geographic level. After collection and analysis of this data READO, disseminate to the relevant stakeholders to inform humanitarian programming.READO under the leadership of/collaboration with the Ministry of Health at federal government as well as Southwest Level - The National Risk Communication and Community Engagement Task Force, will conduct massive awareness-raising campaigns on COVID-19 in Baidoa IDP camps in Southwest state. This will involve risk communication and awareness campaigns (including debunking of myths, rumors and stigma) among vulnerable populations including IDPs, persons with disabilities and marginalized communities. In addition, COVID-19 messaging will be done through mass media through radio, television, print posters and billboards. 4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion and marginalization/Protection mainstreaming: To avoid tensions with the local community, READO will include host communities in decisions on-site location and planning. Also, READO will consult host communities in identifying assistance needed and consider providing the host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic services and facilities. In regards to evictions, READO will liaise with the local authorities to know the existing land and property tenure arrangements, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land when selecting sites. Also, READO will advocate with local authorities and/or propertyowners to obtain formal agreement to use the land, as a precaution to reduce the risk of eviction, rental costs or conflict erupting due to lack of clarity on these issues. READO will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the camp services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. For instance, READO will ensure this by introducing special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing water collection or distribution points or are unable to carry provisions, assemble their shelters eg. community mobilization, ‘home’ distributions, youth groups assisting older persons. Also, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming. Also, READO will that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities in the camp and that they receive the same benefits for their work. Also, offer livelihood opportunities for persons with disabilities and older persons. Furthermore, READO will consider the gender balance of camp staff and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. READO will also establish a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for accountability. READO will use information boards to display CRM phone numbers during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaints. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaintswill be documented and reviewed regularly. Protection of civilians/conflict violence: READO will ensure, that the location and access routes to camp services are safe.This will be done by identifying central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. READO will identify areas in and around the camp that could be potentially unsafe or risk of violence, especially gender-based violence e.g. around showers, latrines, child-friendly spaces, schools and routes to schools, health facilities, water collection points, etc.This will be done READO will also advocate for installation lights outside and on the access route to the facility with the government of South West State Somalia. Besides, READO ensures effective safety and security measures are in place including establishing community patrol groups, where possible, to support the prevention of forced recruitment, looting, GBV, and other threats. Disability and marginalized groups inclusion: READO will intentionally target people living with disabiltiies making sure CCCM services and disability sensitive services. In regards to marginalized groups, READO will identify the power dynamics in the area which will include who has access to camp services, who manages facilities, who can influence decisions on camp services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups.
