Project Summary

Children, adolescents and women have access to diet, services and practices that support the nutritional status in the humanitarian context of Lake Chad Basin, Far North Region:
34,194 children (17,438 boys and 16,756 girls U5 admitted for SAM treatment)
65,892 children (33,604 boys and 32,288 girls) U2 admitted in the Homebased Food fortification programme
125,000 people (M/W) sensitized including 25,000 women trained for SAM detection
(mother MUAC)
50,000 adolescent girls receiving weekly iron and folic acid supplements
85,000 pregnant women receiving weekly iron and folic acid supplements
Activities or outputs
• Caregivers of children aged 0-23 months are supported to adopt recommended infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, including both breastfeeding and complementary feeding
• Children aged 0-59 months have improved nutritional intake and status through age-appropriate nutrient-rich diets, micronutrient supplementation, home-fortification of foods and deworming prophylaxis.
• Adolescent girls have access to community-based package of interventions that includes at a minimum: iron and folic acid supplementation, deworming prophylaxis, nutrition education, counselling and support.
• Pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers - with special attention to pregnant adolescent girls and other nutritionally at‑risk mothers – have access to a package of interventions that includes at a minimum: iron and folic acid/multiple micronutrient supplementation, deworming prophylaxis, weight monitoring, nutrition counselling, and nutrition support.
• All children aged under five years in affected areas are screened regularly for the early detection of severe wasting and other forms of life‑threatening acute malnutrition and are referred as appropriate for treatment services.
• All children aged under five years suffering from severe wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition in affected areas benefit from facility and community-based services (Fix or mobile) that provide effective treatment.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The results of the latest nutrition survey (with the SMART method) conducted IN November 2019 by UNICEF and the government of Cameroon showed a GAM rate of 5.2%with 1.4% of SAM. For chronic malnutrition, Far North had the highest prevalence with38.2%. In addition, a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening carried out in August 2020, on a sample of 1,601 children aged 6-59 months during the survey revealed that the proportion of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) is at 6%. These results indicate that the nutritional situation in the Far North region remains precarious. The proportion of SAM children was 2.4%; that of MAM was 3.6%. The departments of Logone-et-Chari and Mayo Sava were the most affected. The situation related to the main underlying causes of malnutrition remains fragile with limited access to potable water especially for refugees, IDPs and their host communities (availability slightly below the minimum emergency water standard (15 litters / person / day) in the departments of Diamaré, Mayo Tsanaga and Mayo Danay). Regarding the health situation, across the region, 43.6% of children show signs of malaria and 41.3% of diarrhoea. The department of Mayo Tsanaga records the highest number of cases of diarrhoea (57.5% of households surveyed for the indicator) and malaria (52.5%). A minimum of 125,000 people will suffer from GAM in2021, including 20,000 pregnant and lactating women and 105,000 girls and boys under the age of five. It is estimated that approximately 44,578 girls and boys under the age of 5will suffer from severe acute malnutrition.Far-North Region is characterized by a high prevalence of acute malnutrition between 5 and 10%, high stunting rate (38.2%) and micronutrient deficiencies (57% of children and 40% of women of child-bearing age with anaemia), which in turn lead to increased risk of death among the affected population and among vulnerable groups. Malnutrition and crises go hand in hand, malnutrition impacts on and is affected by crises. In protracted crises, like the Lake Chad Basin crises, the factors responsible for malnutrition are exacerbated. It also points to fundamental structural problems that trap populations in a state of constant precariousness and help explain why emergency situations resulting in alarming increases in acute malnutrition regularly arises and why chronic malnutrition rates remain unacceptable despite aid assistance.Women, girls, boys and men face different risks in relation to a deterioration in their nutritional status in emergency contexts. These different vulnerabilities are related both to their differing nutritional requirements and to socio-cultural factors related tog ender. Needs from birth until the age of two differ from the ones of other age groups, because the nutrition needs of infants and young children are different, while the texture of foods and the frequency of feeding have to be adapted. Women and girls are more likely to reduce their food intake in favor of other household members. This can contribute to under-nutrition among women and girls. Women may be disproportionately affected by under-nutrition due to their increased physiological requirements. More than 15 % of the women of reproductive age are at risk of malnutrition or malnourished, it is 20 % forpregnant women. While remaining the main caretakers of children, women take on additional activities to support household food security, especially in situations where male are absent. In the Far North, about one-fifth of households headed by women are food insecure 22% of households against 9 % of those headed by men.Children with disabilities are more likely to be malnourished as malnutrition can cause disabilities and disability can also lead to malnutrition, creating a cycle. At the same time, they may become mal-nourished due to difficulties swallowing and feeding, frequent illness, difficulties absorbing nutrients, caregiver’s lack of knowledge on feeding and neglect.
