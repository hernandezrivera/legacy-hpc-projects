Project Summary

In Syria, MH needs are at an all-time high after years of protracted crisis. PHCs record surging service demand, notably for depression and stress disorders, underscoring the urgent need for enhanced MHPSS. The health system faces challenges of limited specialized care, insufficient MHPSS in education, and resource deficits in HTR areas, with NGOs playing a key role in emergency response and MH promotion. GBV is a grave public health issue intensified by conflict & displacement, affecting women's health, and requiring an adapted approach within health system's existing capabilities. PSEA stand alone activities of awareness raising and capacity building must be combined with mainstreaming in the health sector to protect those seeking care, and ensure a dignified & safe environment, especially given the increased vulnerability due to conflict & displacement.
MHPSS interventions:
-Governance scaling
-Enhance coordination through TWG
-Refine referral processes
-Increase MHPSS accessibility in PHCs, education & communities
-Continue response and awareness initiatives
-Psychotropic procurement
-Collaborate for pre-service and ToT with MH entities
-Adherence to WHO & IASC quality standards
GBV interventions:
-Fortify health responses
-Train HCWs in survivor-centered care, CMR, ToT
-Mainstreaming initiatives
-Refine service quality
-Bolster referral systems
-Improve partnership sustenance
PSEA activities:
-Mainstream survivor support
-Raise awareness & building capacities in partnership with public health bodies & NGOs to alter attitudes, reduce stigma around reporting
-Strengthen AAP
In NWS, there are only two psychiatrists for 4.5 million people, and the mental health conditions of the population are extremely fragile, with substance use and suicide attempts reportedly increasing especially among youth aged 11-28 years old. Lack of livelihoods, a poor education system, and an increase in hostilities are only some of the struggles many Syrians face. In 2023, MH emerged as a top priority resulting from prolonged exposure to conflict and disease outbreaks, exacerbated by the devastating earthquakes, which significantly increased the prevalence of MH illnesses. WHO estimates nearly 1 million people in NWS have MH disorders and 230,000 have severe MH disorders while just 4 health facilities are providing crucial psychiatric services.
In 2024, coordination, technical support, and integrated approaches are the key focus for MHPSS.
WHO will:
1. Enhance multi-sectoral interventions in line with WHO MHPSS minimum service package, including coordination within the MHPSS TWG, suicide prevention, and substance use Task Force.
2. Capacity building for humanitarian workers, including management of substance use disorders, strategies for suicide prevention, self-help techniques, gPM+, school toolkit, and PFA.
3. Scale up the training for PSS workers and behavioral support strategies for first-line emergency responders.
4. Scale up the suicide prevention activities among different communities, including training on detecting early signs, awareness sessions and school counseling, and suicide case management.
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
