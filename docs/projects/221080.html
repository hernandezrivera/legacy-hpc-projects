Project Summary

While building on the achievements and lessons learned from the education in emergencies (EIE) interventions implemented from 2019 to 2024 under ECW FER, CERF education project, ECW Cameroon COVID FER project, MYRP and GPE COVID-19 Emergency Response Project in the Education and Training Sector in Cameroon, and in coordination with Education Cluster, the UNESCO proposes to continue targeting out-of-school children to access relevant, quality and inclusive learning and minimize drop-out for those enrolled in formal and non-formal settings, in particular, girls and Internally Displaced Populations (IDP’s). The project will continue to improve the quality and delivery of inclusive distance learning opportunities in the NWSW, West, and Littoral crisis-affected regions, and improve pupils learning outcomes to promote the transition to formal education.
The EiE projects in 2021, 2022 and 2023 enabled access to the online education platform myschoolonline.cm for over 360,000 learners (170,165 girls and 190,196 boys) while an average of 28,672 learners out-of-school children (16,478 girls, 12,194 boys) are accessing learning in non-formal community learning spaces operated by the local NGOs in 7 Sub-divisions in NWSW. Additionally, UNESCO has ensured multi-channeled diffusion of learning content national wide through 47 community radios targeting 886,418 learners at the primary level and 425,582 learners at the secondary level. Further 2,093 teachers (909 female and 1,184 male) have been trained on how to provide psychological support and facilitate resilience within learners. 307 facilitators and supervisors of temporal learning spaces have also been training on e-learning and supervision of learning through the radio.
UNESCO will therefore use these achievements and lessons to improve the quality of learning and inform further expansion in NWSW, as well as Littoral and West Regions, to provide access to learning for the IDP families that have settled there from NWSW. Through this action, UNESCO seeks to reach in 2024 at least 154,355 learners of school-age 4-17 years old (86,202 girls and 68,153 boys) by: i) strengthening the quality and delivery of distance learning (online and off-line) to improve pupils learning outcomes and promote the transition to formal education. This includes building the capacity of learning facilitators and supervisors on learning content, EiE, pedagogical skills, learner assessment, and PSS. Further UNESCO will strengthen the E-learning platform with more learning materials. Thirdly, UNESCO proposes to improve the distance-learning infrastructure through the provision of IT Equipment and learning materials (mobile learning units, Tablets, USB sticks, and radios, etc) including the production and dissemination of additional prerecorded lessons and internet-based online/offline learning modules, to effectively reach the additional learners. ii) To improve the quality of the learning environment in crisis-affected regions UNESCO will train at least 200 teachers, supervisors, and other education staff (110 females and 90 males) on EiE and related topics, supervision of learning through the radio and other media, and Information management. iii) To further improve access, UNESCO proposes the provision of capitation grants targeting disadvantaged families to reach 8,400 (4,400 girls and 4,000 boys) pupils and ensure that school-aged children do not remain out of school. The grants will be delivered to schools that are operating at a significantly reduced enrollment rate to absorb more learners. iv) To improve access to education for children with disabilities and promote inclusivity, UNESCO will purchase and distribute specialized materials to inclusive schools to facilitate learning for children with disabilities.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Max length: 4/4000The socio-political crisis in NW/SW has led to massive population displacements, with spillover effects in the West and Littoral regions and increased vulnerabilities of people who have often left their villages to live in the bush or as IDPs. Additionally, the number of IDPs from NW/SW in the Littoral, West, and Centre regions has also remained high in 2022. A majority of the displaced are women and children amongst whom are unaccompanied and separated children. The ban on education by non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and attacks on education have forced many families to send their children to the Littoral, West, and Centre regions where they can pursue their education. On 6 September, school doors for the 2021-2022 academic year opened in Cameroon, including in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions. However, two out of three schools remained closed, keeping over 700,000 children and adolescents out of school. According to statistics from SW education authorities, collected after the first week of schooling, 53 per cent of secondary schools in the region were not functional. At the basic education level, 49 per cent of primary schools and 47 per cent of Nursery schools still have their doors closed. In the NW region, the situation is worse as only 23 per cent of secondary schools were functional as of 15 September. Non-State state armed groups (NSAGs) continue to intimidate parents, children, and teachers with attacks on education. Education continues to be politicized and a surge in attacks on education was observed in the run-up to the academic year 2022/2023. In September 2022, NSAGs enforced a two-week violent lockdown from 5 to 16 September to boycott the school start. Over nine attacks on education were registered in August and September 2022. About 15 teachers from two schools in the NW were abducted and later released upon payment of a ransom. They were accused of enrolling students for the new academic year 2022/2023. In September, 24 children were abducted by NSAGs. In five other incidents, three schools in the SW were partially burnt, allegedly by NSAGs. Since January 2022, partners have reported overall the abduction of 73 teachers and students. Five teachers and students were killed, seven teachers and students were injured and about eight incidents of arson on educational facilities were reported. Access to basic services including health, education, and livelihoods was severely affected, and protection activities were severely impacted. During the lockdown, all schools and community learning spaces were closed, except for some schools in a few urban areas which operated at less than 60 per cent of their capacity, compared to the first week of the 2021-2022 academic year. With the above scenario, the needs that gave rise to this response still suffice as the education situation continues to be in distress. Out-of-school adolescents, youth, and particularly girls continue to be at risk of gender-based violence, as they face early marriage, recruitment into armed groups, or adopt other negative coping mechanisms, like child labour to survive. Girls, including primary school students, are reportedly recurring to prostitution to be able to pay for their education. Children with special needs continue to be marginalized and face significant barriers to access education, as they do not have the appropriate support, equipment, or materials to learn at home, while the few open schools are ill equipped and with low capacity to support them effectively.
