Project Summary

This project is aiming to provide the required support to the landmine and Explosive Ordnance (EO) affected people in Sudan through raising the awareness about the threat of landmines and explosive ordnance, release land previously contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with landmines/EOs; opening roads/routes contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with EO; and provide the required support to the humanitarian actors to deliver the aid to the affected populations. The project also aims to support the government of Sudan to meet its obligations under the Ottawa Treaty (landmines ban treaty). The government of Sudan is obliged to meet its commitment under article 5 of Ottawa treaty by April 2023. Also, this project is aiming to support the landmine/EO survivors to reintegrate socially, physically, and economically to their communities. UNMAS in cooperation with the mine action implementing partners will release more than 3.2 million square meters of land contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with EO and open about 375 kilometers of roads and routes for productive use. About 404,000 people will receive risk education awareness through direct sessions or indirect sessions such as radio broadcasting or community campaigns. Also 400 people from the community leaders and humanitarian service providers will receive safety training and risk education. The project also will provide the required training and courses to enhance the capacity of 200 people from the national implementing partners including personnel from NMAC and national NGOs. The project also targets 95 landmine/EO survivors or victims through different interventions to reintegrate them to their communities.

1 - Needs

A number of long-lasting conflicts has led to the contamination with anti-personnel and anti-tank mines as well as explosive remnants of war (ERW). South Kordofan maintains the highest contamination records of landmine and ERW, followed by Blue Nile and West Kordofan, while Darfur states are affected by ERW only. The presence of landmines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) not only poses a direct threat of killing and injuring the local population but it also limits their access to the basic life support facilities, to the livelihood activities and limits the access of the humanitarian aid reaching the vulnerable communities, explosive hazards contamination further hampers early recovery and development efforts. IDPs, returnees and refugees are at most risk due to the limited information/awareness about local landmine or ERW contamination within their settlement areas. Furthermore, and following the Juba Peace Agreement and current peace negotiation with Al Hilu cross line movement of people and goods will resume which is increasing the risk of interaction to landmine and ERW which hinders the humanitarian assistance delivery. Sudan has committed to comply with the Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC) known as Ottawa Treaty, under Article 5 to clear all known landmines by April 2023 however the government of Sudan is seeking extension of the deadline to meet its obligations under the APMBC or Ottawa Treaty. Explosive Ordnance (including landmine and ERW) contamination remains one of the key concerns with an estimated 2.5 million men, women and children affected in Sudan. To Date, IMSMA has registered 2,441 casualties as a result of landmines or ERW accidents, including 1,808 people who have been injured and 633 fatalities. In 2021 boys and girls accounted for 68% of the known victims of landmines and ERW. The highest number of victims in 2021 were registered in Darfur states. Explosive Ordnance contamination also limits access to food, livelihoods and markets, as people cannot fully engage in agricultural and animal grazing activities. It also puts people at risk when fetching water or collecting firewood. Furthermore, explosive ordnance effects extended to hinder early recovery and the development activities of communities. Explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) remains insufficient to the scale of needs, building resilience and adapting safe behavior is essential for communities’ safety and wellbeing.As of Q3 2021, Sudan Mine Action Programme had registered 4,767 hazardous areas with 264 newly registered in 2021, out of which 4,428 areas - covering more than 136 square kilometers of land - have been released and handed over to communities for productive use including 52 kilometers opened in 2021. Even though there are about 136 square kilometers of land in 339 locations across Sudan confirmed to be contaminated by landmines/ERW, still the actual level of contamination is not yet fully known due to inaccessibility and movement restriction.According to the HNO severity analysis, 28 localities were reported to be impacted by landmines/ERW, out of which 12 are ranked as being highly, very highly, or severely impacted. Landmines/ ERWs affect the lives and livelihoods of more than 1.7 million people, particularly IDPs, returnees, pastoralists and nomads. Additionally, more than 38,000 km of roads have been opened since 2002, there are still hundreds of kilometers of roads suspected to be contaminated with landmines/ERW blocking access to services, socioeconomic activities, or delivery of humanitarian services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1- Provisioning of Explosive Ordinance Risk Education among 404,000 people at risk to facilitate the adoption of risk-avoiding behaviors among the vulnerable population including refugees, IDPs, and returnees. This will be achieved through conducting mine risk education (MRE) and awareness raising including direct sessions and community-based risk education CBRE; printing MRE material for distribution; dissemination of MRE messages through radio broadcasting to reach communities in restricted and remote areas. Provision of mine risk education to populations living in proximity to the threat of explosives and humanitarian workers will help reduce risk of death and injury from hazardous landmines and unexploded ordnance as a result of acquiring necessary knowledge and behavioral changes. 2- Release Land for productive use through land release operation to contribute to the protection of local communities by ensuring improved access to basic services; restoring livelihood capacities as well as safe access of the humanitarian actors to enable the delivery of life-saving aid. Release lands previously contaminated or suspected to be contaminated with the explosive ordnance for productive use through the following activities which will contribute to release about 3.2 million square meters of land and open 375 kilometers of roads: Survey activities to Identify and record areas with landmine and ERW contamination through non-technical and technical survey (NTS & TS); clearance of landmines and ERW from lands that confirmed to be contaminated with landmines and ERWs; conduct verification and/or clearance of routes suspected to be contaminated by landmines and ERW; removal of landmines and ERW through EOD spot tasks. 3- Improve the life conditions of 95 people from the explosive accidents survivors and promote their socio-economic reintegration and positive perceptions within the community; equal participation of landmine/ERW accidents survivors in the communities will be achieved through provision of medical care, prosthetics and assistive devices, psychological counseling, and income generation kits as well as the collection of data about the landmine/ERW survivors and registration of these data into the mine action systems such as IMSMA or any other government systems; the promotion and advocacy with the governmental entities for rights of PWD. 4- Provision of technical and financial support to the national mine action center (NMAC) to enhance their capacity to manage a prioritized principled and efficient mine action programme. This will be achieved through provision of advisory to the NMAC through UNMAS professional personnel; conducting the required training and courses to about 400 people from the national authorities including the NMAC and the partner’s NGOs; and providing the required financial support to allow the NMAC to do its technical and managerial roles

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNMAS will ensure that mine action activities are carried out in compliance with the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG) and International Mine Action Standards (IMAS) for what concerns environmental preservation. UNMAS and NMAC will identify and assess relevant environmental aspects and determine appropriate and effective measures to mitigate adverse impacts of explosive detonations on soil, airand immediate surrounding environment. This will include the inclusion ofenvironmental requirements in the selection of implementing partners and their incorporation in SoP, proposals, and quality assurance monitoring plans.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mine action is basically a protection intervention aiming to protect people lives from the threat of landmines and explosive remnants of war, MA sub sector ensures equal participation, consultation and inclusion of affected populations in the planning and implementation of mine action programs as well as ensuring that the different exposure to and knowledge of risks related to explosive ordnance among women, girls, boys and men from diverse groups is incorporated into risk reduction efforts, including clearance and risk education. It will also facilitate and support multi-sectoral assistance to victims of explosive ordnance that is responsive to the needs of women, girls, men and boys from diverse groups, and advocate for the mainstreaming of gender and diversity considerations in national mine action policies, institutions and programs. Additionally, mine action implementation partners are encouraged to ensure they “do no harm” to the environment or livelihoods, and avoid increasing the long-term vulnerability of affected communities. Therefore, this project considers protection mainstreaming through all phases of the project starting from the project concept throughout project design, implementation and closure of the project.
