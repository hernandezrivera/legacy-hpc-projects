Project Summary

UNESCO's response to education in emergencies (EiE) in the Far North region of Cameroon is part of its overall education strategy aimed at meeting children’s educational needs in emergencies to improve access to quality, inclusive and safe educational opportunities. To achieve this, UNESCO proposes to expand its current education interventions in distance learning that have been successfully undertaken in NWSW regions and through the national platform by targeting 34,000 (18,700 girls and 15,300 boys), refugees, returnees, and host children in three districts that include; Mora (Mayo - Sava), Mokolo (Mayo Tsanaga, and Waza (Logone et Chari). UNESCO will integrate protection, Psycho-social support (PSS), GBV, gender, and sexual & reproductive health (SRH) for adolescents and particularly girls, as critical crosscutting issues in the communities. Specifically, the project will target the following components: i) Enable access to distance learning in community learning spaces using online/offline learning materials. This will include mobilizing parents and respective community leadership to ensure out-of-school children are enrolled in safe learning spaces within the communities. Additionally, UNESCO will train and monitor learning facilitators on the organization, structure, content, and delivery of alternative learning including the use and maintenance of the IT equipment to ensure smooth learning with minimal interruptions. The facilitators will be trained on the use of the UNESCO online learning platform, which will ensure that at all times learners have the option of using both online/offline learning materials effectively. ii) UNESCO will train teachers, learning facilitators, and selected PTA’s on Psycho-Social support, Disaster Risk Mitigation/Reduction, Early warning systems, and topics of Education in Emergencies so that they can provide and/or facilitate learners and teachers with the required support. iii) In addition to the distribution of learning materials (IT Equipment, mobile learning units, and materials) To improve access to education for children with disabilities and promote inclusivity, UNESCO will purchase and distribute inclusive & sexo-specific supplies to children living with disabilities. including children with disabilities to promote inclusivity. iv) UNESCO will train selected community leaders, youth, and implementing partners in Education in Emergencies, Disaster Risk Reduction/Early warning systems, Protection, and Psycho-social support for learners and teachers. v) To further improve access, UNESCO proposes the provision of capitation grants targeting disadvantaged families to reach 8,000 (4,400 girls and 3,600 boys) pupils and ensure that school-aged children do not remain out of school. The grants will be delivered to schools that are operating at a significantly reduced enrollment rate to absorb more learners.
COVID-19 messaging will be cross-cutting themes.
