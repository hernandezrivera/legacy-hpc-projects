Project Summary

This project aims to address critical child protection challenges in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions of Cameroon, exacerbated by ongoing conflicts and socio-political instability. The intervention focuses on reducing the risks faced by vulnerable children, including family separation, child recruitment by armed groups, sexual violence, and lack of access to education and healthcare.
The project will strengthen existing child protection systems through coordinated efforts between government bodies, non-governmental organizations, and international partners. Key activities include establishing and expanding inter-agency referral pathways, providing Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), promoting birth registration campaigns, and supporting Disarmament, Demobilization, and Reintegration (DDR) programs for children associated with armed groups.
Targeting approximately 40,000 children and their families, the project will prioritize internally displaced persons (IDPs), unaccompanied minors, and children at risk of violence or exploitation. By improving service delivery, building community resilience, and advocating for policy reforms, this intervention seeks to ensure a safer and more supportive environment for children to grow and thrive, even amidst ongoing crises.
