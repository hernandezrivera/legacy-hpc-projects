Project Summary

As a result of armed conflicts since 2016, thousands of people have been killed, three times as many have been seriously injured or permanently disabled and countless others have witnessed or taken part in violent acts. The destruction of food crops, water supplies, health services, and subsequent large displacement of families and communities takes a heavy toll on children and women especially. Even greater numbers have died from malnutrition and disease during this period in the SW and NW Regions of Cameroon. Highlights from Cameroon: NW and SW Crisis, Situation Report N°9 as of 31 July 2019 show that more than 700,000 children, representing almost 9 out of every 10 children, have been out of school for nearly three years with 80% of schools closed. An estimated 462,000 people in the two regions fall within the Crisis and Emergency phases of food insecurity calling for urgent action to protect and revive livelihoods, reduce food consumption gaps and reduce acute malnutrition. It is estimated that up to 7,000 children between 6-59 months with Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) can be expected in the two regions if care is not taken. Reports of hospital admission of children with SAM requiring inpatient care are increasingly being recorded in the regions. The rapid Emergency Food Security and nutrition Assessment (EFSA) and mobile Vulnerability Assessment and Mapping (MVAM) conducted by WFP in 2019 revealed that 1.5 million people in SW and NW are food insecure.
In a needs assessment exercise carried by NADEV in the five municipalities in Fako and Moungo Divisions, 5000 vulnerable displaced persons were registered. Among these, only 200 have been reached in an ongoing project with funds from the UNDP ERM-SGF Grant Project to run small businesses and short term food crops production.
This new project will therefore target another 700 IDPs in five municipalities including Buea; Limbe II; Tiko in Fako and Dibombari; Fiko in Moungo which have been fairly stable and providing access to education for children since the beginning of the crisis. In these locations approximately half of the current total populations are IDPs who have been absorbed by the towns people rather than living in separate IDP camps. This has led to high number of IDPs with close to 10 or more persons living in a room. Host families accommodate as many as 25 persons.
This project aims at supporting vulnerable persons to start up or rehabilitate their basic livelihood activities that have been disrupted by the crises. It will increase income, strengthen the capacity of local and national actors to deliver basic services in a participatory and conflict sensitive manner, improve the local level dispute resolution and community security.
Project Outcome is to increase income of IDPs and host families through skills training, financial and farm input support to enable them meet their basic needs food, clothing, health among others.
Indicator: At least 70% of IDPs reached in the project target zone are running small businesses that can yield profit within 2 months and meeting 50% of their basic needs by 31 December 2020.
Activity
1. Organize 5 project orientation meetings in target communities.
2. Put in place beneficiaries selection committee and carry out selection
3. Carry out baseline data on selected beneficiaries in 5 target municipalities
4. Organize 10 x 2 day capacity buidling workshops on Small Business Management skills.
5. Organize 5 x 3day vocational skills training workshops.
6. Disburse funds to beneficiaries for petty trading/small businesses
7. Support short term food cultivation by providing farm input to farmer beneficiaries
8. Organize 30 trauma healing sessions for beneficiaries and communities.
9. Carry out monitoring visits to benef.
10. Organize 5 mid-term review meetings per target municipality with beneficiaries and community leaders.
11. Organize an end of project evaluation exercise and share project results with partners.
