Project Summary

This project will be targeting a total number of 5000 households approximately 30,000 individuals which include IDP 9,000 individuals, Returnees and host communities 21,000 individuals. Out of these population, 24,000 beneficiaries will be supported with access to safe drinking and domestic use water, 6000 will be supported with sanitation infrastructures (latrines) in schools, health facilities and community gathering places and all of them will also be covered by hygiene promotional activities.
Cordaid has pre-positioned one drilling pad machine at Bentiu, one manual village drilling machine stationed and one SWAT system at Upper Nile. Cordaid has successfully installed the SWAT system in Panam (Canal/Pigi) and it is fully operational. These equipment, together with our 3 field offices in Wau, Bentiu and Malakal, enable us to respond in a timely manner during emergencies (e.g. new IDP movement), and to support more durable solutions in recovery process (e.g. for returnees).

Needs

Since 2013 the South Sudan entered into a cycle of conflict which impacted negatively on the population within the country in both IDP camps and host communities, including damages to the already scarce water and sanitation facilities. Field assessments show that vast localities are still suffering from lack of safe and clean drinking water. Unity state, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal have been characterized by conflicts, both military confrontations of government forces as well as inter communal clashes which have seen many people displaced from the places they call home. Many have moved and settled in other communities while others have moved to designated camps known as Internally Displaced People’s camps. This movement has caused a lot of pressure on the existing functional water facilities in the host communities as well as deterioration of the sanitation in the same localities as a result of the population increasing with lack of sanitation facilities. CORDAID is currently carrying out a need assessment in the locations of its projects intervention, result of which will be fed into the implementation of the proposed activities in 2019. Over 1.5 million people are confined to Internally Displaced Persons’ (IDP) camps. The risk of diarrhea disease outbreaks is high due to overcrowding in the IDP camps, despite progress made in improving the living conditions of displaced populations by some agencies. Pre-crisis surveys conducted estimated use of improved water sources at 69%, although the access to safe drinking water was only 9%, according to UNOCHA. Similarly, the use of improved sanitation facilities such as latrines was low and estimated at 7%. Now……. With the improvement of security in most places, the communities are returning to their home areas. In their home areas their needs are immense in terms of WASH as a result of destruction of their infrastructure. Some of the areas where community members were displaced, most of the pump parts were stolen hence boreholes are not functional. During the fighting, also the sanitation facilities were destroyed hence the need for interventions in the communities where the populations were displaced, the IDP camp sites as well as the host communities where the IDPs are settled through rehabilitation of broken down hand pumps, construction of latrines, distribution of WASH NFIs to help the returnees to improve their hygiene.

Activities

The proposed interventions will be timely response to people affected with catastrophic situations and emergency crisis through provision of safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene promotion. Result 1: Improved access to safe drinking water, sanitation facilities and hygiene promotional activities among the most vulnerable & conflict affected populations Activities or outputs 1.0 Coordination with WASH cluster at national and state level to ensure no duplication of services with other agencies in same location1.1 Geological survey and drilling of 10 new water points for the communities1.2 Assessment and rehabilitation of 25 dysfunctional water points1.3 Training of 35 community Hand pump mechanics at 20% female1.4 Training of 35 community Water management committees (at least 60% female)1.5 Construct 60 stances of Shared house hold emergency latrines for new IDP settlements targeting 1800 individuals. 1.6 Training of community hygiene facilitators 20 hygiene promoters (12 female and 8 male)1.7 Formation and training of Children’s Health Clubs targeting 1200 (50% female) children for 6 clubs per state in schools. 1.8 Conduct 3 rounds of Community and Household hygiene promotion campaigns in three counties of targeted states targeting at least 9000 individuals (60% female in attendance) 1.9 Provision of Hygiene kits to 2000 Households acquired from WASH cluster.1.10 Sensitization and distribution of Menstrual Hygiene Management kits to vulnerable women and girls of child bearing age acquired from WASH cluster- 2000 females (800 adults of reproductive age and 1200 young girls of reproductive age preferably school going girls )1.11 Promotion of community led sanitation through CLTS and PHAST methodologies in two villages one in Upper Nile and One in Western Bahrel Ghazal. Cordaid has pre-positioned one 500T PAT drilling machine at Bentiu, one manual village drilling machine stationed and one mechanical operational water treatment plant (SWAT) system for emergency preparedness at Upper Nile, which will enable Cordaid to response quickly and immediately where there are water supply needs. Cordaid has successfully installed one more water treatment plant (SWAT) system in Panam (Canal/Pigi) and it is fully operational.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

During Project launching, stakeholders (local authorities and communitycommittees) will be provided with what the beneficiaries are entitled to duringthe project period. During beneficiary verification process, awareness will alsobe made on the project entitlement. CORDAID also does Post distributionmonitoring (PDM) to get feedback on the items distributed and probableirregularities which may have occurred during distributions.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages will be contextual to specific activities. For example, this Project will integrate with other actors e.g. GBV, Protection and Nutrition who are implementing in the same project location, and protection messages will also be included in hygiene promotion sessions.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CORDAID has a strong policy on sexual exploitation which is displayed in everyoffice to remind staff on code of conduct. CORDAID normally links up withPSEA focal persons in the awareness workshops in every project location.CORDAID also provides telephone contacts of focal persons in each location forreporting sexual exploitation issues, while during project monitoring chancesare given to secret meetings in case issues that can not be raised in publicmeetings. This gives avenue for reporting such abuses not only sexualexploitation.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CORDAID had recently conducted needs assessment in 3 states (Wau, Upper Nile, and Unity), with consultations through focus group discussions (FGD) with men and women, allowing beneficiaries to prioritize their needs and prioritized solutions. The results will be used to guide the planning of detailed actions in 2019. During the project implementation period, more consultations will be done.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes the project has in built complaint mechanism: Complain and feedback mechanisms will be shared with target communities during the inception meetings. Channels like meeting our various staff in person or calling to designated mobile numbers will be disseminated. At the office level, the program manager is responsible and is the focal person for handling complaints, while at the community level, project committees will be trained on how to report suspected cases and complaints to project managers in each location of implementation. Project managers, community mobilizers at the field level will be focal persons for report issues of concern.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community will be involved in monitoring and evaluation. We shall conduct Focus Group Discussion (FGD) and Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) households surveys to collect views of the affected communities which will be factored in for fine-tuning our intervention. Also, formation of the Projectcommittees are meant to ensure community participation in the running of the Project and community feedback. The committees act as advisory body to the community and are tasked with beneficiary identification after training them on the criteria. Local contribution will also be encouraged e.g. fencing materials, labour for transport of materials, land allocation for WASH sites. We will promote key committees (e.g. Water User Committees) with representation of both male and female.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

This project will take into consideration the risks involved in the implementation of the project activities and to mainstream protection concerns in design. E.g. for emergency latrines construction, sites location will ensure safety for women and girls. Separate blocks for different gender will be arranged and locks will be installed on on latrines doors. Security of distribution locations and time will be assessed. Also other partners involved in protection in project locations will be liaised with for coordinated approach.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CORDAID acknowledges that implementing projects in South Sudan involves access barriers. CORDAID will liaise with all key actors (e.g. both government and opposition) to coordinates for humanitarian access, with support from OCHA. We will also consider transportation of project supplies can be requested through the Logistics cluster and where there are alternativesCORDAID will always work towards maximizing the benefits to the communities e.g local procurement. Also, CORDAID will ensure journey are coordinated with other partners implementing in the same locations, as well participating in cluster meetings for sharing of information.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

During the project implementation especially project beneficiaries identification, emphasis will be ensured on inclusive beneficiary selection where all vulnerable categories are included. Where some categories may be foreseen to be left out, equity measures will be enforced e.g. specific numbers orpercentages are put compulsory for instance 60% women beneficiary for hygiene kits distribution. Serving pregnant/ lactating mother, sick person, PWDs with priority. Where protection issues of high magnitudes are reported, protection actors will be invited to address the specific issues in the project locations and offices. During the formation of committees and groups, CORDAID will ensure gender representation is a priority.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

CORDAID beneficiary selection criteria ensures all categories of vulnerable persons are included based on vulnerability. e.g. in the criteria CORDAID prioritizes IDPs, Host community, Returnees, PWDs, Pregnant/lactating mother, households with SAM/MAM cases, and others. It also ensures men and women are selected to the project. Project committees are trained on key protectionconcerns which will factored in the project implementation e.g. selection of relatives, coercion by chiefs for family members and soliciting for money in favour of getting registered.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Where beneficiaries are not easily accessible, coordinated joint response will be used especially in Wau areas of Bagari, where Caritas Wau has better access than other partners, Caritas will be partnered to implement in such locations to ensure hard to reach areas are served. FGD with beneficiaries and community as a whole to identify if certain group of people are excluded not owing to selection criteria.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes, this project will ensure safety of beneficiaries is taken into consideration by conducting assessment of feasible options of implantation which do not put beneficiaries in danger. E.g. female and male latrines shall be separated with clear signage, with low risk to the beneficiaries. In PDM, their views on protection issues related to the operations will also be explored.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of the beneficiaries will be measured through low level violence report, distance to access project inputs, confidence of the beneficiaries to approach project staff, and confidence to report abuses to committees.
