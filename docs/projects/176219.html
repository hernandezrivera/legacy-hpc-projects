Project Summary

The signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement by former protagonists in South Sudan has sparked two key events in the country. One of them was the decision by the UN mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) to withdraw its peacekeepers from South Sudan’s Protection of Civilians sites (PoCs) and reclassify them as Internally Displaced Peoples (IDP) camps. Secondly was an increase of mobility both within the camps and the whole country. In a press conference on 4 September, 2020, Mr. David Shearer, the head of UNMISS announced improved security situation in the country following the peace pact the parties signed on February 22, 2020. The accelerated an inflow of returnees into the country. The signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement by former protagonists in South Sudan on 22nd February 2020 has led to slow but continuous flow of returnees to their homes. This led to the decision by the UN mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) to withdraw its peacekeepers from South Sudan’s Protection of Civilians sites (PoCs) and reclassify them as Internally Displaced Peoples (IDP) camps. The return and resettlement into the country has therefore been accelerated. Juba has been a place of first stop for returnees from East Africa, before they get their bearing to other parts of the country.
By end of 2019, MTT had continuously educated communities in Canal/Pigi and Twic East to the risks of unexploded ordinances and other remnants of war to the places they were returning to. With the increased return traffic, MTT proposes to renew the EORE to IDPS, returnees and host communities in Canal/Pigi and extend to Malakal County, while observing COVID-19 protocols. The two teams will actively involve the targeted populations in peer education across all gender and age groups. The reconstituted teams will focus on the two main approaches with key EORE messages in all areas of operation. These will namely be primary and secondary approaches. Primary approaches will be used for groups identified as the most-at-risk in the community – those living in or close to contaminated areas or other suspected hazardous areas. They will benefit through direct presentations. Secondary approaches will involve the most influential stakeholders. These are people regarded as very close to their population, who act as agencies of change in their community. Religious leaders, teachers, group leaders or representatives fall into this category. They will be educated in Explosive Ordnance Risks to pass on other members of the population who might not be reached directly with the EORE messages. Armed with EORE information to pass out to other members of the community not directly reachable, they will also be directed to channel the information to local authorities, NMAA and UNMAS. MTT will coordinate with other clusters such as Education, WASH, Nutrition, Child Protection and Health to pass on key EORE information alongside local authorities to alert hosts, returnees, IDPs and the disabled on hazards and mitigation. The project aims to educate the community on how to stay securely in their environments. The project outcome will be to enable beneficiaries obtain solutions to their displacement, choose places to live or stay for safe, dignified voluntary returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses and support coping capacities and livelihoods within Canal/Pigi and Malakal counties.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Trainings will be oriented on the risks associated with sexual exploitation of communities. MTT will facilitate beneficiaries (particularly girls) to report cases of gender-based violence and / or sexual harassment from relatives or strangers, which will be documented and referred back to the line cluster partners.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Delivery of interventions will consider individuals living with disability, gender and age appropriate as highlighted in the delivery of EORE. Through the secondary option, EORE agents will be given lessons tailored towards persons with disabilities deliberately tailored to accommodate inclusiveness of gender disadvantaged populations. MTT teams have been given basic gender trainings and the outcomes of these trainings will be reflected in programming. The operational framework takes into consideration disability, gender and age as quality markers in activities and the uniqueness that comes along with these divisions are put into practice in data collection methods, analysis and reporting. Minimum standards in emergencies will be used to maintain quality and mitigate negative effects

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community members will be included in assessment, design and implementation of the project. MTT will be committed to accountability to affected populations by integrating feedback and accountability mechanisms into EORE strategies. Community members will be recruited, trained and inducted in the project. Reports will include monitoring and evaluations of the project. Transparency:The project will ensure accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between MTT the targeted populations.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

MTT will actively seek the views of the targeted beneficiaries to improve EORE, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. Mitigation MeasuresEORE will provide substantive education to avoid making the community stay in constant fear of the environment. With the understanding that most families are headed by women – and it is them who daily experience difficulties in accessing food, water, education, livelihoods and recreation, this project will administer psychosocial support to the vulnerable population. This will include Psychological First Aid as well as other forms of non-formal psychosocial support. The project will network with other protection sub clusters such as Child Protection and GBV. Even as we increase the capacity of communities to identify, report suspected ERWs, we will strengthen and establish community networks for sustainability of the project.
