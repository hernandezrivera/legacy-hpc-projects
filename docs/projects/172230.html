Project Summary

Over a year and half since Cyclone Idai hit Zimbabwe, 931 individuals are still staying in three formal and one informal camps in Chimanimani. It remains unclear when the Government will facilitate relocation of the IDPs from camps. The outbreak of COVID 19, makes the population in camps more vulnerable making Camp Coordination and Camp Management activities very critical in IDP camps.
925 disaster displaced individuals in Binga are still staying in a camp 10 months after they were displaced by floods, while 3300 individuals in Chiredzi and Hwange were displaced due to land disputes in Chiredzi and Hwange. The affected population is still staying in camplike settings with huge protection needs.
More land dispute cases and disaster displacements are anticipated in the coming months due to the forecasted normal to above normal rainfall 2020/21 rainfall season particularly from January to March 2021. This is likely to cause displacements and establishment of camps and collective centres.
The proposed response will strengthen camp management and coordination capacity and support Camp Management authorities (national, sub-national, local) improve Living Conditions and Community Participation in IDP camps and sites, through the provision of CCCM support. The intervention will support IDPs within targeted camps and sites, through Coordination & Information Management, Site Improvement, Community Participation and Self-Governance and Capacity Building.
Communities affected by disasters are at risk of further exposure to more vulnerabilities and unless their resilience is strengthened, their situation may become even worse. The project will improve Disaster Risk Management among IDPs in camps and CCCM role players to mitigate, prevent, prepare, respond and recover from disasters.
In the context of COVID-19, CCCM will adapt standard community participation activities to focus on Risk Communication, in coordination with Health and WASH actors.
Use of feedback mechanisms such as the toll-free hotlines, suggestion boxes and so on will be escalated to ensure that the complains/feedback from IDPs is effective and timely. Staff managing the hotlines will receive training on how to ethically respond to protection concerns, especially any disclosure of a GBV incident.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

1. Site improvement: Camp/site improvement works will include drainage improvement, repair of communal kitchens, improvement of toilets and bathrooms and rubbish disposal pits and so forth. This will improve the living conditions of the affected population and provide a healthier, safer and more dignified environment. 2. Site maintenance: through existing IDP camp committees, composed of IDPs and host communities’ representatives, site maintenance activities will be conducted, such as organizing clean-up efforts, fire safety awareness campaigns, and debris removal and management. 3. Support and establishment/strengthening of governance structures and community groups ensuring equal participation and engagement of the communities. 4. Capacity building in DRM through training of DRM committees in camps and local authorities.5. Establishment of a complaints and feedback mechanism along with ensuring established referral pathways and communication of feedback to relevant stakeholders;6. Service mapping and monitoring to regularly identify critical gaps in service provision and IDP needs, and facilitate multisectoral responses in the camps/sites. Through the response IOM will develop site profiles accordingly and disseminate to relevant stakeholders for action.7. Information management at camp/site level, maintaining up-to-date information on population and how to access services (contact list of service providers, referral pathways etc.). Information boards are displayed to ensure IDPs, camp committees and government appointed camp managers have access to relevant information to access or provide services as well as to refer vulnerable individual to specialized agencies. 8. Camp/site level coordination meetings with displaced community members, local authorities and partners active in relevant collective sites and surrounding host community to advocate for adequate service provision and programmatic adjustment based on affected communities feedback.Facilitate development and operationalization of relocation/exit strategy for IDPs and decommissioning of camps/collective centres.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

National, subnational and local stakeholders as well as affected populations will be involved at in CCCM activities to ensure effective accountability. Camp structures will be put in place together with complains and feedback mechanisms will be put in place to ensure timely communication of IDP issues for timely redress. Feedback mechanisms will include suggestion boxes and use of toll free numbers.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

IDPs in camps will be sensitized on the importance of preserving the environment. The response will provide lighting and alternative sources of energy to minimize cutting down of trees.
