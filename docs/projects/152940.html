Project Summary

Tdh will preposition NFI kits and build up internal preparedness capacity to respond quickly and distribute critical life-saving household items to newly displaced families in Anbar, Salah Al Din and Ninewa governorates. Furthermore Tdh will train the local authorities in assessment and distribution operating procedures.

Needs

The S/NFI cluster has identified 513,390 people I need NFI support across Iraq. This is based on the S/NFI cluster analysis of HNO 2019, achievements of SNFI programs in 2018, and financial considerations. To be specific, the S/NFI cluster has identified the following in need of NFIs: - IDPs in-camps 206,400 - IDPs out of camps 150,000- Returnees 150,000- Host Community 7,500 Given Tdh Iraq’s experience with conducting CCCM / CRC activities to support out-of-camp IDPs and returnees from our respective bases in Anah (Anbar), Shirqat (Salah al Din), Tikrit (Salah al Din) and Tal Afar (Ninewah), the above noted geographic locations are accessible for Tdh to provide the support needed. In note of the repeated and ever increasing cycles of conflict and recovery in Iraq since 2003, institutional dysfunction, and the roots of ethno-sectarian tensions largely remaining unresolved – there is an sustained likelihood of conflict displacing populations in newly accessible governorates. Consequently, Tdh will maintain preparedness capacity to respond quickly and distribute critical life-saving household items to of families through the prepositioning of NFI stock, and building the capacity of local authorities as prioritised by the HRP 2019 S/NFI Cluster Objective 3 “Contribute to preparedness levels, contingency plans and capacities for large-scale emergencies through shelter interventions and NFI provisions”.

Activities

In note of the repeated and ever increasing cycles of conflict and recovery in Iraq since 2003, institutional dysfunction, and the roots of ethno-sectarian tensions largely remaining unresolved – there is an sustained likelihood of conflict displacing populations in newly accessible governorates. Consequently, Tdh will maintain preparedness capacity to respond quickly and distribute critical life-saving household items to of families through the prepositioning of NFI stock, and building the capacity of local authorities as prioritised by the HRP 2019 S/NFI Cluster Objective 3 “Contribute to preparedness levels, contingency plans and capacities for large-scale emergencies through shelter interventions and NFI provisions”. Tdh will preposition both Basic NFI kits and Winter-top up kits kits in its warehouses to ensure quick response capacity to the population in need. Distribution of ready NFI kits rather than cash distribution was selected due to focus on emergency preparedness for areas where purchase by beneficiaries might not be possible (e.g. flooded areas). The Basic NFI kits will address basic household needs such as sleeping comfort, water collection, maintaining hygiene, lighting and security, and contribute to a family’s ability to cook. In addition, with the onset of winter in December 2018, Tdh will also support vulnerable families with winterisation kits. That is improvements or support to shelters to withstand the extremes of temperature or climate. Tdh notes that in all of targeted locations, the mixed populations either reside in public or private housing. Both the Basic NFI kits and Winter-top kits comply with S/NFI Cluster “Non-Food Items (NFI) Technical Guidance V13 - June 2018. The cost of the Basic NFI and Winter-top up kits are 206 USD and 77 USD respectively. As the response moves from an emergency to a post-emergency phase, the general consensusamong humanitarian actors is to implement a more targeted approach, Tdh will ensure that only beneficiaries identified as most vulnerable through the use of a vulnerability assessment tool. In note that the Iraq S/NFI Cluster is currently revising the Cash Working Group tool, Tdh will consider its use once made available for implementation. As such, Tdh will target highly vulnerable out-of-camp populations who are in critical need (including IDP, returnees, host community and non-displaced). In summary, Tdh will perform:- preposition emergency NFI kits - capacity building activities for own staff as well as authorities Four to six weeks after each NFI distribution has ended, Tdh will conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). PDM data is collected through interviews with individual beneficiaries based on a sample survey format. PDM results are largely quantitative, and highlight broad trends in order to inform programming. These results should be fed back into the project cycle in order to improve the way assistance is designed and delivered, responsive to the preferences of beneficiaries and sensitive to potential protection risks that could be caused by NFI assistance. Tdh will also ensure complex approach to emergency preparedness by maintaining the capacity to respond to WASH needs of displaced population (water access, sanitation, hygiene) as well as first-line child protection (PFA, child friendly space). Tdh will ensure joint multi-cluster assessment carried out by its teams from multiple departments.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks that beneficiaries are exposed to during the implementation of project activities are multiple: 1. Marginalisation of specific groups of people due to partiality of local government authorities. 2. Government pressure to obtain beneficiary data collected as a result of needs assessment and referrals 3. Vulnerable groups which are unable to leave their homes or have movement limitations (e.g. PWD, elderly, pregnant women, female- and child-headed households) 4. Operational access to area of intervention may be impeded due to deterioration of the security situation or lack of clearance from the local authorities

Mitigation Measures

The proposed project systematically incorporates protection principles in programming and implementation, and in particular: 1)Tdh will also advocate to local authorities for service provision to be impartial, and advocate through appropriate bodies and mechanisms to address incidents of biased service provision. 2) Responsible program data procedures will be implemented to safeguard the rights of people; to ensure their dignity, respect and privacy; enable them to make informed decisions, and not be put at risk, when providing data. Tdh will ensure vulnerable groups’ representation (e.g. women) indata collection; safeguard the accuracy of data, and ensure that information is up-to-date and securely stored. Tdh will appropriately collate, analyze, and disseminate the information it collects to ensure participants’ right to be heard, especially within marginalized populations, and furthermore ensure that a participant’s personal dignity is maintained throughout data life-cycle. Tdh will also ensure that participants are informed to make a decision about their participation in any data activity, and their consent is informed and voluntary. Where the process involves minors, Tdh will get both their parents / caregivers’ consent. Tdh will not discuss or share in any form, information gathered from participants with any unauthorized (by Tdh) persons and store them securely to avoid exposing participants to any risk as a result of its data activity. In addition, Tdh will also ensure inclusion of age/gender disaggregated data in site population figures, and as possible collect indicators disaggregated by gender on the different activities and services provided. 3) Meaningful access is ensured by programming and implementing that promote a non-discriminatory access of beneficiaries, determined by needs and exposure to risk only. Particular attention will be given to proactively promote meaningful access for girls, women, persons with disabilities and particular vulnerabilities Consequently, Tdh focuses on involving equal input from women and men in the affected population (e.g. establishing women and youth representative committees), and thereby ensuring their participation and involvement in humanitarian coordination and planning. In particular, Tdh will consider house-to-house or tent-to-tent distributions with female staff present or transportation support for people who are unable to leave their homes or have movement limitations (e.g., PWD, elderly, pregnant women, female- and child-headed households). 4) Operational access is monitored by dedicated trained staff through a set of key actions carried out by a dedicated team at coordination and field level, among them: field coordinator, liaison officer, risk advisor. Daily monitoring of safe access to the area of intervention it is carried out at base level and through inter-base networking. Throughout the project cycle, Tdh will prioritiye safety and dignity through ad hoc and continuous and sound protection and risk assessments. Activities are designed and implemented in order to promote safety and minimize exposure to risk. A specific Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy will be implemented and disseminated and measures will be taken to ensure that no harm is resulting from Tdh’s activities and staff.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Tdh is accountable the affected population which is recognized to be a dignified key actor to ensure meaningful participation and voice in all phases of the project cycle. Tdh is committed to the Accountability to Affected Population IASC framework, specifically by: - Promoting leadership and governance and ensuring that staff is aware of, and programs and approaches are in line with accountability to the affected population principles. A commitment to build on the capacities of local population and strengthen the resilience of people affected, whenever possible. Collaboration and consultation with local actors in order to ensure implementation of activities that meet the needs and will encourage beneficiaries to actively participate. - Transparency of communication in order to help affected population to take informed decisions. A focus on providing clear and transparent information on available services, consequences and risks will be given in particular to case management services’ beneficiaries in order to enable them to take informed decisions. At all project cycle steps, continuous and transparent communication on Tdh’s activities and objectives will be ensured with local authorities, as well as all other actors operating in the area. Tdh will also observe full coordination and informationsharing with relevant cluster and sub-clusters both at national and governorate’s levels. - The establishment of feedback and complaint mechanisms that are child friendly and adequate to the age and the gender of beneficiaries.Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be designed to be inclusive, accessible, confidential when needed, and to not to be factor of exposure to further harm. Feedbacks and complaints will be systematically taken into account, FGDs will be regularly conducted with beneficiaries. This will allow to assess the level of satisfaction within the beneficiaries and adapt the design of activities and their implementation to the current needs and available approaches used for beneficiaries involvement. Tdh will take all necessary measures in order to ensure operationalization of Tdh’s Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), including the identification of a Child Safeguarding (CS) focal point, set-up of safe and accessible reporting mechanisms, adoption of measures to ensure data protection, confidentiality and dignity. All Tdh employees, partners or visitors are carefully briefed on the document that must be read, understood and signed before starting any assignment, collaboration or visit. - Participation of beneficiaries to the principle of accountability is incorporated at all stage of the project cycle. A particular focus will be given to children participation and specifically children girls’ participation. During all activities children will be encouraged to participate and speak out. A wide range of participatory activities will be implemented in order to make sure that all opinions, particularly opinions of those moremarginalized, are taken into account. Community-based design activities are prioritized. - A participatory Design, Monitoring and evaluation of activities is ensured through ongoing consultation with beneficiaries. Focus groups discussion, bilateral and informal group meetings and other activities will constantly aim at improving the capacity of the project to better meet beneficiaries’ needs and collect best practices and recommendations.
