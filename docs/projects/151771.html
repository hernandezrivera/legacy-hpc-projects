Project Summary

The project aims at addressing Health and Nutrition and building resilience of the drought affected communities in Buhodle district of Togdheer Region.
The project targets an estimated 29,220 households, particularly women, children the elderly and the poorest of the poor families, living in East, West and North of Buhodle. Assistance will help affected households to meet their immediate health and nutrition wellbeing by providing 3 fully equipped mobile teams.

Needs

With a prolonged drought over the past years, mainly 2016 and 2017, Somalia experienced one of the worst humanitarian challenges mainly in terms of loss of the main livelihoods and economic mainstay of the local community. With a significant loss of livestock due to consecutive seasons of poor and below normal rainfall, resulted in very low of animal production consequently creating severe acute food and malnutrition levels particularly within the poorest of the poor households. Despite relatively better outcomes of the last two seasonal rains and also with an increase of humanitarian assistance to those worst affected, the impact of the prolonged drought and the subsequent impact on the livelihoods and the food security of the people continues to be a major challenge. This is further exercebated by the large number of pastoral families displaced due to the drought where they lost their livestock which was their main livelihood assets in the predominantly rural and pastoral settlements in the north especially in Sool and Ceyn (Buhoodle) regions. In addition, Prevalence of acute malnutrition rates, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM), as measured by weight-for-height z-score (WHZ), continues to be a major concerns as the trend remains the same; mainly within “Serious” (10.0 – 14.9%) or “Critical” (15.0-29.9%) ranges. On the other hand, acute malnutrition is attributed to the fact that food availability remains below normal in most of the affected areas, coupled with chronic poverty and outbreak of disease as well as limited access to health services. With a poor health conditions where diarrheal diseases accounting for the majority of deaths among children, along with respiratory infections, Somalia has one of the highest total fertility rates in the world at 6.7. This is in addition to other poor health practices/access and facilities where 98% of women experience female genital mutilation/cutting which leads to serious obstetrical and gynaecological complicatio

Activities

The proposed project will be implemented in a hard reached and underserved district of Buhoodle in Togdheer region. While the health care services delivery is very low, with the few existing health facilities only located in Buhoodle town, these integrated health and nutrition project will extend services to the rural villages and IDP camps, where health services are either inadequate or non-existent. In total, the project will target 61,500 people in the target project locations. The structural causes of poor health and malnutrition imply that such an intervention has to be implemented on a relatively long period (12 months) compared to usual standards for emergency programs. The first stage of the intervention – presented in this proposal – will focus on an intensive strengthening of the MoH capacities to treat common diseases and acute malnutrition. This includes technical and logistical support to the 15 existing health centers with OTP activities for treatment of severely malnourished children without medical complications; and to the TFC in Buhodle hospital for case referrals and the 24 hours follow-up of severely malnourished cases with medical complications. In parallel, to the health cases treated, the intervention will include an important focus on a community approach in order to allow the early detection of acutely malnourished children in their villages and their referral to the health structures with nutritional services (OTP &/or SFC). These activities will require recruitment and training of staff in order to strengthen the MOH team in the different structures as well as training of MoH staff already running health nutritional activities. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Stable security situation in the project areas will enable the inclusion of idps in the project interventions which will prevent risk of exclusion for the displaced population in the area. Hence, the proposed project will enhance accessibility to project services including for any displaced population in the area.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In line with the HRP 2019 that considers centrality of protection, the project will consider inclusion of all beneficiaries based on gender and age in the provision of life saving as well as integrated health and nutrition interventions. Hence, will involve gender - specific data collection and analysis with other social dimensions such as age, income level and education and others to prevent risk of exclusion particularly on gender and age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Throughout the project life span, RDA will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with local authorities and community to ensure that appropriate prevention and mitigation measures are implemented timely in case of any insecurity.
