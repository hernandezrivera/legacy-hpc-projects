1 - Needs

According to an OCHA humanitarian report released on the 6th of May 2020, the number of people in need of urgent assistance in North East Nigeria rose from 7.1 million in 2019 to 7.9 million in 2020 as a result of displacement emanating from the activities of insurgents. Round 31 Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) assessment conducted by IOM with the aim to improve the understanding about the scope of internal displacements, returns and the needs of affected populations in conflict-affected states of northeastern Nigeria covering the period of 15th January to 15th February 2020 states that 31,2,046,604 or 420,072 households `were recorded as displaced, an increase of 7,512 persons against the last assessment conducted in December 2019 when 2,039,092 Internally Displaced persons (IDPs) were identified. The current crisis affecting North East Nigeria has brought about huge civilian displacement in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) states, PoCs struggle to flee from life threatening incidences caused by Non-State Actors which could include killings, kidnapping/abduction, forced recruitment, rape, sexual assault or harassment, and arbitrary detention by the military on a daily. SHO Protection team conducted regular community, household, and individual level protection assessments and protection monitoring in MMC, Jere, Konduga and Kala-Balge Local Government Area in Borno State and results of the assessments have revealed that in addition to Human Rights violation/abuse incidences suffered during this displacement period such as serious threats to life, sexual violence, forced recruitment into armed forces, and kidnappings, other signiﬁcant protection concerns continue to be faced in areas of displacement. These include exposure to abuse and exploitation, Sexual and Gender-Based violence, forced and early marriages, child labour, begging, and odd survival practices. In IDP settlements, we have discovered inter-community tensions, insecurity and restrictions to freedom of movements which would also amount to protection risks as rights are violated.Protection incidents like Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) remains one of the major concerns in the above mentioned LGAs, in the midst of the crisis. Protection needs for SGBV and female health are huge and complex. The Inter-Agency capacity to respond remain insufficient and challenging (poor coverage of specialised services, inadequate Individual case management in these locations, poor quality of collected data, drastic reduction of financial resources) in the current security situation. Inaccessibility to certain areas and socio-cultural constraints has led to under reporting of SGBV incidents by the community. Therefore, establishing the real scale of SGBV among women, girls, men and boys of Internally Displaced and Returnees population in north east Nigeria during and after the conflict if not addressed, remains a challenge.

2 - Activities

Communities and cultures are not static; they constantly change. Communities of refugees, Internally Displaced Persons, and returnees have social and cultural traditions, religious practices, economic activities, associations/networks, protection mechanisms and political structures that existed prior to displacement. Displacement disrupts many of these familiar ways of life, but it can also be an opportunity for learning new skills and incorporating new knowledge, activities and structures into daily life. Implementing a community-based approach during humanitarian crises involves mobilizing individuals and communities and supporting them in their decisions about how to cope with the upheaval; SHO has considered different stages of the community-based approach by putting in mind situation analysis, including stakeholder analysis, participatory assessment, and planning. Not leaving out the different aspects of community mobilization and empowerment, from mapping leadership and management structures to community-based action planning, monitoring and evaluation. SHO has also highlighted the need to identify individuals with heightened risks and to establish individual case management systems based on working with the community. In the drafting of this plan, community leaders ranging from youth leaders, women leaders, men groups, people living with disabilities and the aged were contacted in meetings and general discussions at various stages where they expressed their fears for protection concerns that are increasing everyday as a result of regular displacements. Complete range of protection expertise will be deployed to attain desirable protection results that will afford; fair protection processes and documentation, security from violence and exploitation, protection from effects of armed conﬂict through protection monitoring and protection by presence. SHO will assuredly strengthen its protection monitoring system like always and enhance presence in the ﬁeld to support affected populations as issues arise by involving community members with the identiﬁcation of protection risks and incidents as well as human rights violations faced by the PoCs through community-based protection monitoring systems to capacitate them (community volunteers) to be able to refer/respond to issues, and provide comprehensive protection response including through referrals to other stakeholders. Analyze information on protection risks and incident trends as well as develop and disseminate high quality protection products as a basis for evidence-based advocacy and programming in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming among other partners implementing in other sectors. Salient HO will follow up vulnerable IDPs and returnees through the vulnerability screening process to allow for perfect delivery of assistance tailored to speciﬁc needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response, especially during this COVID-19 era. SHO will focus on massive awareness creation, sensitizations and distribution of IEC Materials in English and all Borno State languages. To ensure referral to quality legal aid and services, provision of pro bono lawyers, including legal advice; this includes legal referral on Housing, Land and Property, referrals of Human Rights violations, access to justice and abuses including provision of mobility or transportation fare for those who are referred by us to seek redress or medical attention within our area of coverage. SHO will conduct advocacy interventions for IDPs and IDP returnees on access to services, we will also build the capacity of relevant community representatives to support the resolution of minor issues at the camp level, even in our absence as other organisations will be consistently encouraged to mainstream protection at all instances of their implementations.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Salient Humanitarian Organisation will at all times liaise and coordinate with partners on site to call to mind their mandate which requires the incorporation of all principles of protection so as to prioritize safety and dignity, avoid causing harm, enhancing meaningful access, for accountability to the project beneficiaries and enhancing participation with the intention to empower the project participants. The involvement of humanitarian workers in acts of sexual exploitation and abuse is a grave violation of our responsibility to do no harm and to protect people affected by crises. Sexual exploitation and abuse violates universally recognized international legal norms and standards. It brings harm to those whom humanitarian agencies are mandated to protect, and jeopardises the reputation of these agencies and their ability to provide protection, therefore, Salient Humanitarian Organisation will in all areas of operation activate kin attention to monitoring and reporting PSEA modalities that will help in identifying and handling PSEA cases accordingly. Although States have obligations to respect, protect and fulfill rights related to SGBV, a growing backlash caused by the activities of insurgents has weakened political, financial and psychological support for protection concerns of the displaced persons living in camps and host communities. These barriers relate to availability, believes, accessibility and cultural norms as well as the quality of the services provided. Entrenched social norms in Borno and gender inequality around women and girls, means women behaviours are controlled and they may be stigmatised for being violated. Due to their age, teenage girls’ ability to express herself over violation may not even be respected but rather, damned. All these make it difficult, and often prevent young people and other minority groups from accessing services having to do with SGBV and SEA.Salient Humanitarian Organisation will on a weekly basis create door to door awareness and carry out mass sensitizations to open the eyes of the Internally Displaced Persons to Sexual and Gender Based Violence among divergent topics affecting the PoCs, the Persons of Concern will also be taken through the intricacies of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and how to report cases regarding these concerns.At the management level, SHO already has policies (PSEA Policy, Gender Policy and Child Protection Policy) that are assented to by all staff and by implication, the policies are binding on all and the organization does everything possible to educate her staff concerning these policies and how to mainstream them while in their places of primary assignment.Succinctly, to make easy the reporting of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Sexual and Gender Based Violence, SHO will provide telephone lines, help desks, complaint boxes and at certain occasions conduct interviews and Focused Group Discussions to gather information concerning SGBV and PSEA among the IDP population.
