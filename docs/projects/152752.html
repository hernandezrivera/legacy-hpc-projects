Project Summary

The DRC mobile response team aims to support the most vulnerable and underserved populations in hard to reach communities affected by conflict. DRC will provide lifesaving WASH services, ensuring adequate and safe access to sanitation and hygiene services, for newly displaced IDPs, returnees and hosting communities in hard to reach locations. In addition, the emergency response aims to strengthen the local government capacity to manage WASH services through training and community participation. DRC will provide gender and disability appropriate emergency WASH service to the displaced IDPs and affected returnees. DRC will provide training of community members to disseminate hygiene message on good hygiene practice and cholera with a greater focus on critical hand washing practice. All responses are led by a protection centred multisector need assessments including detail analysis of WASH that will determine the exact nature of the response. Through this project, DRC will fund a mobile team based in Juba with a rapid response capability to cover various hard to reach and newly affected areas across South Sudan. DRC will prioritize locations prioritized by the cluster and NAWG. By having a mobile team based in Juba DRC will better be placed to support new displacements and spontaneous returns in areas with no WASH actor or areas with limited WASH capacity. All WASH supplies will be provided by the cluster pipeline and will not be budgeted under the project. The response missions will be followed by a rapid monitoring exercise during the response mission in hard to reach locations where access is limited to redeploy team for evaluation or post-distribution monitoring. The PDM and the rapid monitoring exercise will highlight any risks associated with DRC’s WASH intervention as well as draw lessons for considerations for future responses. during the rapid monitoring, exercise beneficiaries will be able to provide their opinions on how DRC's and cluster interventions can be improved and they will be given the opportunity to make a complaint. A formal feedback mechanism will be fed through DRC’s hotline whereby complaints or queries may be logged and responded to remotely.

Needs

The revitalization of the South Sudanese peace process and freshly signed agreements by the parties to the conflict promise to offer new opportunities in 2019 for the country and its conflict-affected women, men, girls and boys. Yet, after half a decade of the most recent conflict, sustained violence and destruction of lives and livelihoods, about two thirds of the people in country remain in dire need of humanitarian assistance and protection – the same proportion as in 2018. People’s protection needs remain severe, while violence and violations of international humanitarian law (IHL) persist as a reality of daily life for many South Sudanese – in particular for women and children. Close to 2.5 million people have fled the country and sought refuge in the region, while nearly 2 million people are estimated to remain internally displaced. Together, this represents close to one in three South Sudanese. The conflict has caused about 382,000 excess deaths, approximately half from violence. Conflict-related sexual violence is a defining feature of the crisis. Sexual violence represents 2 per cent of all reported cases of gender-based violence. In first 9 months of 2018, there were some 800 known survivors, including very young children. An estimated 5,674,000 South Sudanese women, men and children will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019. In addition, some 300,000 refugees lack sufficient WASH conditions. The number of people requiring emergency WASH services in 2019 increased by 2 per cent from the previous year, explained by the expansion of WASH data with a higher reliability and more detailed analysis. Per the adjacent severity map, WASH needs are the highest in Canal, Fangak and Pibor in Jonglei, Awerial in Lakes, Panyijar in Unity, and Ikotos in Eastern Equatoria, and while Awerial and Fangak remained one of the counties with the highest WASH needs, the new counties represent a shift from last year which saw Fashoda of Upper Nile, Ayod of Jonglei, Rubkona of Unity and Juba of Central Equatoria. Women and girls face increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products, and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need. IDPs in PoC sites do not have sufficient hygiene and sanitation and are at risk of disease outbreaks in the congested conditions. WASH needs are also high among IDPs in non-camp settings and among their already stretched host communities.

Activities

Activity 1: Conduct 6 WASH analysis in newly affected areas highlighting key recommendation for response interventions or any associated risks and mitigation measures. The mobile team in Juba will be deployed to prioritized areas for WASH assessment/ analysis for a period of one week. The assessments/ Analysis aim to identify the WASH needs and identify the best way of delivering WASH services considering the protection risks associated with such assistance and a key consideration of DO NO HARM. DRC will use the existing methodology of protection centred approach where WASH team are jointly deployed with the protection team in all areas prioritized for assessment and response. DRC during the project period aims at conducting 6 assessments/ analysis that guides the response. Activity 2: Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations.Response interventions include Rehabilitation of dysfunctional water points; Boreholes/Hand Pumps including repair of slabs, fencing etc.; Refresher Training of Hand Pump mechanics; Refresher Training of 15 Water user Committees; Donation of hand pump repair kits to trained hand pump mechanics; Distribution of emergency NFIs (safe water collection and storage containers, PUR Sachets and filter clothes). Activities will be implemented based on the analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and DO NO HARM considerations. The WASH response missions will be conducted over a period of short mission not exceeding three months’ deployment. The same team who conducted the analysis would be deployed to the location. DRC will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its interventions. DRC aims to reach 15,000 Individuals through this activity. Activity 3: Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. The response interventions includes Refresher/training of hygiene promoters; Sensitization of communities on the safe water chain and household water treatment options; Hygiene promotion campaigns, cholera preparedness and awareness campaigns at community level and household levels including in schools and Market Centers, targeting IDPs returnees and Hosting communities and distribution of hygiene related NFIs including soap and hygiene kit to adolescent girls and young women. DRC aims to reach 15,000 Individuals through this activity. Activity 4: Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services.The response activity includes construction of emergency gender appropriate communal latrines equipped with hand washing facilities. DRC will conduct a proper protection analysis to mitigate the WASH related GBV through prior consultation. DRC aims to reach 5,000 Individuals through this activity. Activity 5: Monitoring of quality service provision (Remote or field monitoring). For each mission DRC will deploy a quick monitoring tool to monitor the quality of service provision. Rapid response monitoring will be conducted during the response phases of the intervention and will be factored into mission planning and daily field activities. Beneficiaries will be consulted on their opinions of how to improve DRC’s and the cluster interventions and be given an opportunity to make a complaint. DRC strives for transparency in its monitoring and evaluation processes and makes all findings available to the cluster members in a timely manner including assessment reports, intervention reports and post implementation monitoring reports. DRC will use in independent team from the MEL department to conduct monitoring activities.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

Each response missions will be guided by the analysis report that comes out from the community consultation. DRC will use the Protection and sector specific analysis tool in each location of response and will produce an analysis report. Where possible DRC will follow the cluster recommended number of community consultations to ensure information is gathered from a different margin of the communities. DRC will use the list of recommended analysis and consultation questions that will serve as a guide to ensure that appropriate information is obtained to inform post crises WASH response. DRC will deploy the HHs questionnaire and Focus group discussion with the different margin of the communities to gather consultative and representative information. HHs questionnaire will be gathered from a minimum of 90 representatives HHs to bring a qualitative data to guide response. Furthermore, DRC will conduct Focus group discussion to gather qualitative information including suggestions and opinions of the affected community from a different margin of the affected populations. Though there is no standard guideline on deciding how many Focused Group Discussion DRC will Ideally gather information from 6 FGDs in areas where the WASH assessment is planned (1 woman and 1 man with the affected group and 1 woman and 1 man with the host families and 2 with other segments including vulnerable groups based on age, gender and diversity). The FGD will ideally participate 6-12 participants. The participants comprising different segments of the proposed community, including separately from men and women, Host communities and IDPs/Returnees. In cases where the time frame is limited the number of FGDs can be reduced to 3 to 4, ensuring participation from different segments or semi-structured discussion can be held with a small group of people (3-5). Ideally, DRC aims to gather consultative information from 900 (360 Women, 65 Men, 225 Boys, 225 Girls) Individuals representing 6 affected communities. The information will be summarized and then a debrief consultation will be held with the community representative to re-verify and validate the information gathered through the HHs consultation and the FGDs with the community representatives.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

DRC has put in place global organisational policies and procedures to safeguard against harmful behaviour; DRC is in full agreement with the UN zero tolerance policy relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Violations constitute acts of serious misconduct and are grounds for disciplinary measures up to and including dismissal, as described in sections 3.7 and 3.9 of DRC’s Code of Conduct (CoC). DRC implements awareness raising in project sites on PSEA, provides training to staff on PSEA and CoC, and has complaints and feedback mechanisms in place in all operational areas. Moreover, DRC also commits to organisational diversity and gender equity through adherence to the DRC Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) Vision and Standards policy.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritizing, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) and as such fulfils the HAP benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. Fundamental to all of DRC's activities is adherence to Age and Gender Diversity Mainstreaming (AGDM) by engaging the various community members and existing institutions and CBOs including women groups, youth groups, and elderly groups. Through both formal and informal interactions with representative groups and other community members, members of the community are empowered to inform the type of activities and services provided by DRC and ensure their implementation is relevant, effective, and responsive to the needs of displaced populations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

In all project sites, DRC operates complaints desks to enable residents to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline number that beneficiaries can call to register complaints or requests.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Community members will be engaged in the project implementation from the assessment phase through the response and evaluation phases, whereby they will be encouraged to identify gaps and needs, confirm response strategies, and they will be highly involved in the identification of beneficiaries, and the information dissemination strategies. To ensure two-way dialogue, DRC will also implement a two-way feedback and accountability mechanism. This will include community focus groups whilst on the ground, but also use of DRC's national complaints and feedback hotline based in juba which will enable affected populations to communicate with DRC by phone even once the mobile team has left the area.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With a mandate for protection, DRC mainstreams protection into all sectors of the DRCs programs in South Sudan and will do so for the proposed activity in accordance with the Global Protection Cluster guidelines. Furthermore, DRC’s Mobile response is designed with a protection lead approaches whereby service provisions are seen to reduce protection risks associated with access to services and enhance the safety and dignity of affected populations.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed to minimize negative effects which could increase beneficiaries’ vulnerability to risks. DRC adheres to AGDM standards and will ensure that beneficiaries have assistance linked to their needs, without any barriers. Once risk factors have been identified through the integrated protection and WASH assessments, DRC will ensure the recommended response is conducted in adherence with the protection recommendations.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project addresses inequalities and protection threats by conducting thorough protection risk assessments throughout the project cycle, which inform protection programming and allow for immediate responses to identified inequalities and protection threats. DRC will mainstream protection into its WASH response activities to ensure affected and at-risk communities and individuals are identified and assisted in a safe manner. Following the Sphere standards and the IASC Guidance on assessments, DRC will ensure ethical principles are strictly adhered to. These considerations include do no harm for survivors (for example by ensuring all staff members are trained on PFA), ethical, survivor-centred response, and best interest of the individual.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

All WASH team will be trained in protection mainstreaming including in protection principles, do no harm, meaningful access, safety, dignity, accountability, and participation in the day to day activities. All WASH interventions will also adhere to the GBV principles which include: safety, confidentiality, survivor-centred approach, respect, non-discrimination, and do no harm. DRC will manage an accountability system to ensure that affected populations receive information, provide feedback, and complaints. The DRC hotline will compliment this accountability. Sensitization to communities on the functionality of DRC hotline contributes to the accountability. In these areas of poor coverage, DRC will work with communities to ensure they are provided with avenues of reporting, through interviews, follow-ups, or the presence of MEL specialists to ensure DRC remains accountable to targeted populations.
