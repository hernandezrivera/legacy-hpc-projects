Project Summary

The projects seek to ensure that those most in need, most vulnerable or at risk, so-called Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs) in IDP, returnee and host communities are supported with tailored protection services and assistance, focused on lifesaving support, while also investing in people’s resilience to shocks. Restoration of housing, land and property rights, civil documentation, child protection and prevention and response services for GBV will be part of this general protection project. The activities will be implemented through UNHCR’s offices in Juba, Wau, Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Yei, Yambio and prioritized respective counties in line with the Cluster’s prioritization.
The project will support the implementation of the Protection Monitoring System that was launched in October 2022 under the umbrella of the Protection Cluster aimed at informing the protection and wider humanitarian response with protection analysis and based on a harmonized methodology and tools. Protection concerns identified will be dealt with both on individual protection services/case management basis and through community-based protection responses tailored to the specific needs and considering the age, gender, diversity among the people served. Community based protection components of this project will involve community owned projects with social cohesion and peace benefits in areas with social tension as well as areas where durable solutions are pursued. Particular attention is paid to segments of the community particular at risk or marginalized, including women and youth empowerment, tailored support to GBV survivors and children at risk as well as people with disabilities and older persons.
Protection risks and social tensions will be prevented and mitigated through awareness and information campaigns, community sensitization using an AGD approach and community engagement, such as community action committees, peace dialogues, including by involving leaders of faith-based institutions. Quality procedures to identify those most in need, vulnerable and at risk will be implemented across all project locations, building on the protection monitoring reports and implemented through protection desks and protection outreach, including through static and mobile teams. Protection services and assistance will be multi-faceted and tailored to the specific assistance and protection needs identified, including to survivors of gender-based violence and children at risk amongst other at risk categories. Direct protection services and assistance includes identification of people at risk, counselling, social accompaniment, enabling access to safety, case management, psychosocial support, legal aid (including assistance and representation), as well as material support tailored to the individual, including in kind (e.g. mobility device, core relief) or cash, implemented through a cash plus approach. The project will also specifically address housing, land and property rights and implement individual assistance and community-based mechanisms, such as through community-based land committees, to restore access to housing, land and property as well as to related documentation, including HLP, identity and civil documentation. This is complemented by community services, including child friendly spaces, rehabilitation of community infrastructure or basic services through community-owned approaches.
Overall, the project empowers communities affected by displacement, conflict and disasters, and invests in their own agency and resilience through the multi-faceted nature of the protection response provided under this project.

1 - Needs

IDPs, host community and returnees continue to be affected by multiple challenges leading to a complex protection environment and heightened risks and needs. The perpetual and persistent nature of floods, inter-communal violence, armed conflict, inflation, lack of rule of law and access to justice pathways, forces people to flee and exposes them to a host of protection risks. Secondary displacements, including in areas affected by multiple causes leading to displacement, are compounding humanitarian needs and compound protection concerns for those affected, notably due to the depletion of their resilience and capacity. These protection risks include gender-based violence, conflict-related sexual violence, damage and destruction of housing and properties, including livelihood assets such as farms and livestock, forced eviction, a range of child protection concerns, including child labour, child recruitment, or family separation, as well as risks related to the direct or indirect exposure to violence and conflict. Trauma and related MHPSS needs are increasing due the compounding and multiplicity of shocks communities suffer. Structural factors, such as gender inequality, also compound protection risks, notably for women and girls. Women, men, boys and girls, and people with specific needs, notably older people and persons with disabilities, are differently impacted by crisis and displacement, and require adequate and tailored responses using an AGD approach. Old age and disability have been identified as particular risk factors in situations of flight, putting people at risk of being unable to access and reach safety and being left behind, as well as factors that inhibit safe access to assistance and services. Youth on the other hand lack opportunity and perspective and require tailored engagement and support. In areas affected by or at risk of inter-communal violence and armed conflict, social and ethnic tensions have been rising. This requires community engagement, through dialogue, community-based projects and empowerment initiatives, to de-escalate and reduce tensions, and promote social cohesion and peace. This is similarly required in areas where IDPs settle or return to, in order to enable peaceful co-existence with host communities and communities in areas where a durable solution is pursued.

2 - Project activities

Based on the 2023 HNO of the Protection Cluster, and above summary of needs, in addition to analysis of available protection analysis and information, the following activities are prioritized under this project: 1. Support to the Protection Monitoring System under the umbrella of the Protection Cluster, using the harmonized method and tools to inform protection analysis for the response and advocacy. 2. Identification of protection and specific needs among populations of concern under this project, including among IDP, IDP returnee and host communities in project locations. This serves to enable a tailored protection response to the particular protection risk and specific need. Identification procedures will be implemented through protection desks and protection outreach activities, using static and mobile response components. 3. Tailored protection services and assistance to identified persons with specific needs, including direct service provision and assistance provision, which can consist of in-kind or cash plus support, depending on identified protection and specific need. This is supported by a case management approach. 4. The GBV specific response activities include preventative measures to reduce exposure to risk and occurrence, including through community awareness and community engagement (e.g. community action committees), capacity building and sensitization, engagement with law enforcement actors, while response and services components include psychosocial support, legal assistance, in-kind and cash based assistance through a cash plus approach, case management including safe and dignified referrals to specialized services if needed, in addition to the rehabilitation of safehouses. 5. Child protection specific response activities include the identification of children at risk and safe referrals for identification, documentation, tracing and reunification with family members, alongside risk reduction activities related to risks of child recruitment and child labour, as well as support to child-friendly community structures. 6. House, Land and Policy: Support will seek to empower those affected to by illegal occupation, land grabbing or loss of documentation. Activities will include: outreach on HLP issues, provision of legal aid, counselling and representation and assistance in the re-issuance of ownership documents, replacement of lost documents through working with the Nationality, Civil Registry, Passports and Immigration. 7. Community-based initiatives to reduce conflict and social tension in areas at risk as well as in areas where IDPs settle or seek a durable solution include economic empowerment, notably of women and youth, as well as rehabilitation of community infrastructure and basic services through community-driven initiatives.

3 - Project indicators

Rights-based and AGD approaches will be applied throughout the program cycle to ensure that the views and feedback of people in need will be used to ensure accountability to affected communities in the entire response. This project was informed by participatory assessments and is therefore designed to be accountable to the needs of people. Specific measures include bolstering feedback and complaint desks, hotlines, focus group discussions, community-based networks, and community coordination meetings. Indicators will also be monitored through regular reporting by partners, including on the implementation rates of targets, participatory assessments FGDs and KIIs regularly held with displaced populations to receive feedback and use of community-based approaches, assessment of activities conducted and post distribution monitoring.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Despite the challenges in South Sudan markets functions and are able to supply goods and some limited services to persons of concern at a fee. Regional infrastructure through roads which bring supplies into the country from Kenya, Uganda and Sudan mostly ensure goods are available. In addition, some items IDPs may require that are not available in NFIs can be easily purchased if IDPs have access to funds.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of Cash is based on preference noted by IDPs during the UNHCR 2022 and 2023 Participatory Assessments. Cash assistance is also favored among IDPs due to its flexibility, allowing the IDPs freedom of choice on assistance they receive. For instance, UNHCR has used cash-based assistance to support the most vulnerable IDPs access medical care that was only available at referrals hospitals, support GBV survivors with further support or to support transportation costs to access services or even buy formula milk for orphaned infants in instances where lactating mothers are too ill to feed or have died. UNHCR has developed a protection and conflict-sensitive approach to the use of cash, so-called Cash Plus approach. Cash Plus is a case management approach that looks at the needs of a person holistically, and therefore integrates multi-purpose cash assistance with needed service provision and/or assistance to either enable the use of the cash and/or to address additional services and assistance needs, in addition to proactive risk mitigation. UNHCR is adopting this approach as a cash assistance as a stand-alone intervention does not adequately address the needs of the most vulnerable/those most at risk in the community, linking cash provision with services through coordination tends to be unsystematic and not always reliable and risks unique to the individual, due to their particular situation and/or for being a cash recipient, cannot be effectively mitigated or prevented through post distribution monitoring. The Cash plus approach is protection- and conflict-sensitive, it ensures that no one is left behind by identifying those most in need, vulnerable or at risk, it reduces protection risks and risks emanating from the use of cash and it can have an economic empowerment impact and enable access to basic services.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism is the regular tool used by UNHCR and its partners in all project locations, which would be capable of handling complaints related to any of its activities and will have several entry points for lodging complaints comprising of in-person office visits, protection desks, suggestion box and through community visits. For women and girls, as well persons with disabilities (including those with sensory impairments), community workers will be engaged to identify them and Focus Group Discussion will be held to ensure their feedback is collected and incorporated. The FDGs will also consider the diverse age groups to ensure the information collected is relevant.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Using a combination of protection monitoring - participatory assessments, key informant interviews, focus group discussions and the community-based approach, UNHCR will maintain regular contact with persons of concern to collect data and analyse the needs of the population. This will inform programming and implementation of activities. Feedback will also be sought through existing feedback mechanisms such as the toll-free line, suggestion box and community structures to facilitate corrective actions. The opinion of the community on how best to implement activities will be considered where operationally feasible. Feedback will also be received through individual interviews, focus group discussions and post distribution monitoring.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNHCR and its partners conduct regular awareness-raising activities with beneficiaries on PSEA, including reporting mechanisms, as well as providing information on PSEA to beneficiaries in multiple formats. UNHCR has the information materials on PSEA in protection desks, community centres with contact information on PSEA reporting mechanism. UNHCR and its partners are also actively implementing SASA Together and there are dedicated focal points appointed for PSEA. Partners are also peer assessed on PSEA elements ahead of any partnership agreement renewal.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This project is designed based on participatory assessments carried out and UNHCR’s day to day interactions with communities in areas served under this project. It is therefore based on the views and preferences of the communities and designed to be accountable to the affected communities. Feedback is sought proactively through a variety of means throughout the project cycle to enable the quick and flexible adaptation to the project as needed. While various factors can contribute to the project adaptation, including context changes, feedback of beneficiaries will remain a main driver of prioritization, design and implementation of community-based approaches, and the components related to community cohesion will pursue a community participation process.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project has safeguards to ensure the risks do not occur. Humanitarian assistance will be well coordinated, with community leaders engaged ahead of distributions to limit possibilities of conflict linked to humanitarian resources. The most vulnerable among host communities will also benefit to ensure inclusion. SEA risks will also be minimized as community leaders and partners receive SEA related training and are aware of the zero-tolerance policy, while efforts will continue to proactively reduce SEA risks throughout the entire response of UNHCR. Beneficiaries too will be empowered through training on their rights and how to report SEA. Banners and visual material in local language will also be hang at distribution locations and at protection desks and partners premises. Feedback mechanisms including suggestions boxes and a toll-free number will be availed. Protection services and referral services will be widespread due to static and roving protection monitoring, persons with specific needs if identified will be assisted accordingly. UNCHR has an existing methodology for identifying and assisting persons with specific needs including disabled persons and older people (please see below). As such persons with PSN codes are deliberately included in assessments, consultations and decision making. UNHCR identified Persons with Specific Needs through fields mission with implementing partners, using mobile teams to conduct household surveys, to distinguish persons with specific needs from the general population. Once identified, PSNs are classified into codes to establish the nature of assistance they may require: Some PSN codes include Single Parent (SP) without support, Woman at risk (WR), SGBV (SV), Child at risk (CR), Unaccompanied or separated child (SC), Elderly at risk (ER), Person with Disabilities (DS), Serious medical condition (SM), Person with specific legal and protection issues (LP), Family Unity (FU) and Torture (TR).
