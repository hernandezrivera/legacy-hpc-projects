Project Summary

This project will cover GBV respond to protection needs of 7,845 IDPs in central Darfur (Nertiti, Golo and Rokoro) who faced threats of physical violence, exploitation, abuse & neglect with special focus on prevention CP risks, gender-based, domestic & other violence in Central Darfur. The project will respond to urgent needs by providing life-saving support to extremely vulnerable communities through establishment of community base protection networks (CBPNs), psychosocial support, awareness raising, establishment of referral mechanisms for GBV interventions, &distribution of dignity kits for 1000 women and girls. The project will strengthen resilience’s efforts through supporting capacity of community structures & local authorities in response to GBV concerns. Rapid identification of persons with specific needs/vulnerable groups including GBV survivors will be contacted prior to any activity. The project will improve the capacity of targeted communities through establishment & training of 3 CBPNs in Nertiti, Golo and Rokoro to respond to GBV concerns. The project will consider accountability to affected population through community participation on conducting needs assessments & implementation of activities.
“DO NO Harm” principle will be applied during the consultations with the community to avoid any harm that might be brought by the intervention; based on sensitivity of the protection issues particularly GBV. To contribute to sustainability of the activities, animators will be selected from communities & social workers from MoSA. Government officials & media personnel will be trained related on GBV issues. This will contribute to sustainability of the activities through improving the understanding of relevant stakeholders especially decision-makers. 100 active community members will be trained in GBV, through this activity, GBV awareness & information will be widely disseminated and become part of social awareness.

Needs

The Sudanese army has been fighting a group of armed movements in Darfur since 2003. UN agencies estimate that over 300,000 people were killed in the conflict, and over 2.5 million were displaced. WFP figures indicate 21,415 IDPs in Nertiti and 55,000 IDPs in Rokoro.Armed conflict, as well as intertribal tensions/disputes and competition over resources still continues in Darfur by various armed groups and militias across the State leading to new displacements, disruption of family life and livelihoods, deaths and recruitments of men and boys, women & girls exposed to violence (SGBV) and Poor security in IDP areas, with limited or no available special safety measures and the Weak or non-existences of effective law enforcement mechanism, absence of strong & well understood referral pathway mechanism, or access constraints to the fundamental services and the destruction of children's normal life patterns. Men are usually experienced to death, recruitment, detention, violence and exploitation, in all settings (IDP camps, rural areas, and in towns) and they are the perpetrators of women crimes such SGBV, child marriage, domestic violence, exploitation and neglect. That is mainly as a result of inherited traditional norms in the Darfur community where men are dominating the lead and boys were socialized to be men at the expense of women and girls.Women and girls are exposed to increased general vulnerability due to social exclusion and discrimination. In the traditional IDPs community in Central Darfur, women are not included in the decision-making process related to their household or at community level. Despite that they significantly contribute to the household income, domestic work and raising children, women are not consulted at any level due to cultural norms. Women vulnerability has increased during the conflict as women became more contributing to the household income hence spending more time outside the house exposed to different level of threats within a community that does not pay respect for working women. While no concrete data is available, women and girls continue to face risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence in IDP camps/settlements and host communities when they leave the camp or villages. Affected communities in the target areas have to adopt new coping strategies for economic survival exposing them to the risk of GBV (e.g. collecting fire wood, water and food).

Activities

1. The project seeks to ensure protection assistance for 7,845 IDPs in Nertiti, Golo and Rokoro, Central Darfur, with particular focus on those extremely vulnerable groups with specific needs. The project will be under the following activities: - • Strengthen/formulate 3 community-based protection structures (CBP), one in each locality. Each CBP will formulated by 15 members.• Provide training for the CBPCs' members in protection issues. The training targets 45 members (3 CBPs each have 15 members)• Conduct 3 Training in GBV and protection issues for 150 government official and community leaders. One training in each locality.• 150 women and girls will be supported through referral mechanisms and cash assistance, 50 in each targeted locality.• Share regular information with the community members about the available services and referral. • Conduct awareness raising sessions and campaigns on protection issues among targeted 6000, 2000 in each locality. • Distribution of sanitary (dignity) kits to the most 1500 vulnerable women and girls. 500 beneficiaries in each locality.• Regular assessment, monitoring and reporting on the humanitarian situation to be shared with partners.
