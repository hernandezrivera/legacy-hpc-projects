Project Summary

The persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. According to the latest findings of 2021 post Gu results of FSNAU, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) at the national level has remained at a serious level of 11.1 per cent weight-for-height z-score. Based on this analysis and the latest population figures, the cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of 5 years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services urgently. Out of this, 295,515 children under the age of 5 are likely to be severely malnourished and around 986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM).In addition to this, around 245,909 PLWs will be at risk of acute malnutrition. A total of 463,204 pregnant and lactating women are estimated to be in need of either preventive or curative emergency nutrition assistance. In this regard, it is estimated that around 629,567 children between 6 to 23 months and 283,305 PLWs will be at greater risk as well. The total number of IDPs that need nutrition services will be around 0.3 million including women and children.High levels of acute malnutrition continue to persist driven by several factors, including high morbidity, below sphere immunization and Vitamin-A supplementation, poor childcare practices, inadequate access to clean/safe drinking water and acute food insecurity. Consequently, the country is facing a Triple Threat of Desert Locust, flooding’s and the global pandemic COVID-19 contributing towards the deterioration of production outputs, social economics increasing vulnerabilities of households. These culminations result in impact on the states of acute malnutrition situation affecting children and women. A Critical (4-5.6%) prevalence of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) was observed in the Riverine livelihood of Beletweyne district and in urban Beletweyne. Morbidity among children was high (≥20%) in 15 out 33 population groups surveyed. However, Crude Death Rate and Under-Five Death Rate were relatively low in most areas. Integrated interventions should be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation. Hiraan, Bay and Bakool region were considered hotspot areas with a projected Critical GAM prevalence, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9% or by a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference below 125 mm of ≥10%, IDP areas of concern are Baidoa and Beletweyne is an urban area of concern for acute malnutrition and need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions This project therefore target to reach a total 9,000 vulnerable boys, girls and pregnant and lactating women through provision of outpatient therapeutic and supplementary feeding intervention. It is intended to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought on populations and communities directly affected in Bakool, Bay and Hiran region, in particular, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Beletweyne town. The main objective of this project is to provide integrated lifesaving emergency Nutrition services to 9,000 drought affected direct beneficiaries and intends to achieve the following, capacity building of nutrition staff on IMAM. Referral of malnourished children with complicated cases will be done to the Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) centers and directly linked with primary health care services managed by HIDIG and other actors in the program areas thus providing more holistic opportunity to the target beneficiaries HIDIG will closely coordinate with Local MOH as well as Federal level MOH and social mobilizers, elders all involved in activities on mobilizing communities. To support increase access to basic nutrition services in the area

1. - Projects activities

1.1 Through outpatient therapeutic feeding program HIDIG will scale up screening, admission and treatment of 7,000 moderately malnourished boys, girls <5 children and 2,000 pregnant and lactating women including disable people in all target outpatient therapeutic and supplementary feeding site through coordination and referral with health and livelihood programs in Bakool, Bay and Hiran regions. 1.2 Provide preventive and therapeutic treatment to severely acutely and moderately malnourished children <5 3,000, boys, 4,000 girls and 2,000 pregnant and lactating mothers including disable people linked with health in Elbarde, Rabdhure, Baidoa and Beletweyne district. 1.3 provide multiple micro nutrient and deworming to 7,000 <5 children boys and girls including disable people in the three target district 1.4 Provide active community screening sites to 3000 boys, 4,000 girls and 3000 pregnant and lactating women through outpatient therapeutic and supplementary feeding static and outreach sites.1.5 Provide vitamin A supplementation and deworming among children (6-59) 1.6 Capacity build 30 community health workers/volunteers and promoters (15 men and 15 women) to provide active case ﬁnding, and referral services. 1.7 Train 40 HRDO technical nutrition staff (30 males and 10 female) on effective management of IMAM/IYCF/NHHP and mother MUAC led training to enable enhance efficiency and improved capacity among the project team and community health workers
