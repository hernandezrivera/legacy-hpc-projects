Project Summary

A state of the Anglophone crisis has led to 3.9 million people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance in Cameroon, as reported by UN OCHA October 2022 report. According to the Cardre harmonise 2022, over 980,000 people were severely food insecure between June and August 2022 compared to 955,000 people during the same period in 2021. This food insecurity is also a result of the Ukraine- Russia war. The crisis has not only resulted in the displacement of persons but has harmed the region's economy, thereby impacting the source of livelihood of IDPs and the host families. Through this project, RADA will select and empower 500 IDPs and vulnerable host community members in Bui and Mezam divisions by training them on running sustainable mini-businesses including animal production, piggery and dealing in commodities. We will also receive pitches of small businesses from participants and support 350 with start-up capital for their businesses. This project will help reduce further insecurity, curb the growing poverty and prevent nutrition-related diseases. At least 300 families will be supported to meet their other basic needs as well.

1 - Besoins / Needs

A state of the Anglophone crisis has led to 3.9 million people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance in Cameroon, as reported by UN OCHA October 2022 report. According to the Cadre harmonise 2022, over 980,000 people were severely food insecure between June and August 2022 compared to 955,000 people during the same period in 2021. In 2020, RADA conducted a Trauma and livelihood survey, which indicated that several people are showing up to churches needing at least food to survive in addition to the traumatic experiences they already have from gunshots, kidnaps and other attacks. The conflict has increased poverty and inequality through the destruction of villages and the inability of families to work farms due to security challenges empoverishing households through economic uncertainty and instability. This project is needed to assist the 500 persons in the community and contribute to food security, prevent dependency, reduce vulnerability, enhance self-reliance and develop or build specific skills for displaced and vulnerable members of current hosting communities such as Bamenda, which will assist them in recovering from the economic challenges associated with the current crisis.
