Project Summary

In Syria, 14.9 million people need healthcare, with around 4.5 million in NWS, facing strained systems due to disrupted facilities and ongoing health concerns like NCDs and communicable diseases. Mental health conditions affect approximately 1 in 10 Syrians, with 1 in 10 children requiring focused mental health care according to the HNO 2024 report. The cholera outbreak declared by the Syrian Ministry of Health in September 2022 is expected to persist in 2024 in NWS, with seasonal fluctuations in caseloads. As of January 2, 2024, 194,169 suspected cases of acute watery diarrhea (AWD)/cholera have been reported in NWS, resulting in 24 deaths. There is an urgent need for medical supplies, trauma care, surgical equipment, Intensive Care Unit (ICU) kits, and mobile teams' capacity, including surgeons, orthopedics, paramedics, and logistics support. The 2024 HNO also underscored gaps in reproductive, maternal, and child health services, along with the limited availability of clinical management of rape. Access to secondary and specialized healthcare services remains challenging across all health needs, with issues in referral mechanisms, coordination, and availability.
The IRC’s intervention aims to enhance access to essential healthcare services and psychosocial support for approximately 475,720 individuals in Idleb and Aleppo governorates by supporting 16 healthcare facilities operated by the IRC or implementing partners, focusing on integrated primary and secondary care, NCDs, Mother and Childcare, mental health support and COVID-19 mitigation. The plan includes the delivery of over 1.6 million consultations, integrating Covid-19 and cholera mitigation, and ensuring pharmaceutical supplies and PPE. Additionally, the project will provide health promotion activities and emergency referrals. The IRC and its partners will maintain a flexible approach, adapting interventions according to priority needs defined in coordination with the Health Cluster and Technical Working Groups (TWGs). Monitoring will be conducted to ensure activities align with objectives and have a positive impact on targeted beneficiaries.
Given the high risk of GBV in the Syrian context, IRC and its partners staff will facilitate safe referrals for GBV through inter-agency referral pathways. Additionally, efforts are made to enhance coordination on protection responses and capacity building within the Protection Cluster and sub-clusters. Ensuring gender sensitivity and privacy in service delivery is paramount. Health facilities are equipped with private rooms to ensure confidentiality during examinations and counseling, and a secure data recording system is in place. Orientation sessions on GBV are conducted for staff, and specialized training on the Clinical Management of Rape is provided to selected personnel. A comprehensive strategy is implemented to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse. Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials are provided to beneficiaries, outlining reporting channels for incidents of PSEA. IRC maintains a robust internal code of conduct to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse, ensuring a safe environment for reporting. Collaboration with partners is emphasized to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and accountability. Regular updates are provided on staff training and complaints are received through feedback channels. Attacks on healthcare facilities remain a concern, necessitating contingency plans and regular training.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Directly, the IRC will continue supporting Biret Armanaz PHC (HeRAMS code: G0703133) and Biret Armanaz Mobile Clinic 1 (HeRAMS code: G0703134) in the Harim Districts of northern Idlib governorate. Indirectly, the IRC will collaborate with partners to extend services across various districts by supporting: • Syrian American Society Foundation (SAMS): Kafr Losien PHC (HeRAMS code: G0703081), Al Imam PHC (HeRAMS code: G0703107), Alebzimo PHC (HeRAMS code: B0200051), Kafr Deryan PHC (HeRAMS code: G0703056), Jandairis (AlFreiriyeh) PHC (HeRAMS code: B0203051), and Alatareb Hospital (HeRAMS code: B0200046).• Syrian Expatriates Medical Association (SEMA): Al-Shahba PHC (HeRAMS code: B0204087) and Idleb Surgical Hospital (HeRAMS code: G0700032).• Union des Organisations Syrienne de Secours Médicaux (UOSSM): Al Bab Mental Health Unit (inpatient and outpatient) (HeRAMS code: B0202021).• Independent Doctors Association (Bağımsız Doktorlar Derneği) (IDA): Azaz PHC (HeRAMS code: B0204064) and Kafr Karmin PHC (HeRAMS code: B0200178). • Relief experts’ association (UDER): 2 BEMOnC - Sarmada PHC (HeRAMS code: G0703011) and Zarzour PHC (HeRAMS code: G0704018). The targets in section 4 and the budget in section 5 include only activities directly implemented by the International Rescue Committee. With regards to capacity strengthening, the IRC aims to contribute to the sustainability of the intervention and provide direct benefit forbeneficiaries of the action in terms of quality of care. The IRC will prioritize all components of the EHSP,with a strong emphasis on addressing the existing gaps based on needs including child health,nutrition, NCDs, SRH, IPC, and Mental Health. In line with the WHO CHWs guidelines, CHWs willattend a training package before initiating service delivery in the field or refreshment training in casethey have received it. The package includes six key modules covering NCDs, CD, Family Planning,Nutrition, Healthy lifestyle, and the basic operational module. In addition, and considering thecurrent outbreaks in northwest Syria, CHWs and other medical staff are proposed to receive relevanttraining and refreshment sessions regularly.IRC will conduct capacity building for the medical staff in the two facilities, including online andface-to-face training, to ensure the quality of the provided services. The training topics include Infectionprevention and control (IPC), PM+ counseling training, and MH-Gap training for MHPSS staff, inaddition to non-communicable diseases, and reproductive health, among other health topics.
