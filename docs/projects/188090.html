Project Summary

Considering the scale and continued rise of needs, in alignment with HRP 2022-2023 objectives, the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor (MoSAL) national strategy as well as the ERL sectors priorities, the Action aims at supporting community recovery and livelihoods and to preserve social cohesion ensuring equitable access to services and mitigating protection risks linked to low income families. For 2022-2023 planning INTERSOS aims at fully integrating livelihood into protection, health and education response, preventing and mitigating negative coping mechanisms such as school drop out, child labor, early marriage and GBV as a consequence of dire economic conditions faced by Syrian families. The Action will be implemented in the Governorates of Hama, Rural Damascus and Southern Idleb targeting newly accessible- hard to reach-rural areas. Clear criteria for BNFs selection are developed based on the vulnerability level of the individuals prioritising Female Headed Households, families with People with Disabilities and IDPs but also HHs that are unable to cover the cost of minimum expenditure resorting to negative coping mechanisms. BNFs are chosen through ongoing Education, Protection and Health intervention promoting integration and population resilience. Based on in-country experience and lessons learnt the intervention builds upon Labor Market Assessments (LMA) carried out in the targeted locations in order to fully link Vocational Training provided to to the characteristics of the labor market in order to support productive sectors and provide employable skills. INTERSOS aims at conducting a specific context analysis of the communities served through the engagement of different layers of society. This will be carried out through KIIs and FGDs involving local entrepreneurs, local authorities’ representatives and vulnerable individuals from the same targeted community (identified through INTERSOS protection-education intervention). The analysis will investigate key barriers in accessing the labor market and livelihood in specific targeted areas, the main services available and referral pathways in place, the soft skills and practical capacities needed by the beneficiaries. The context analysis will investigate the main protection risks faced by individual at risk and Persons with Specific Needs. Such analysis will be linked to the evaluation of the demand of the labor market defining possible pathways for dignified and capacitated access to it. To ensure gender balance and empowerment of girls and women entering into the labor market consultations with communities and focus groups discussion will be carried out enhancing women active participation in the choice of the training topics. Hence, the action encompasses the following key components: 1. LMA directed at the analysis of demand and supply sides of the local labor market 2. provision of technical and vocational training to build and strengthen beneficiaries’ skills 3. delivery of business and marketing skills workshops to transfer critical skills in communication and marketing to better connect with customers, promote local products and boost business activities, 4. provision of start-up kits to trainees to foster self-employment and broaden income generating activities 5. support to micro and small enterprises in the agri-business sector by provision of assets which are necessary to increase production. As part of the action, INTERSOS is will conduct rehabilitation works in Protection Community Centers (MoSAL)aimed at enhancing accessibility for PwDs. INTERSOS staff and SARC volunteers are provided with briefing sessions on core concepts and policies related to humanitarian principles, Child Protection and PSEA. Awareness-raising sessions are delivered on hazards, risks, preventive and mitigation measures related to the COVID-19 pandemic. INTERSOS regularly coordinates at National and local level with INGOs ERL partner ensuring coordination and avoid overlapping.

4 - Needs

11 years of crisis in Syria have eroded the socio-economic foundations of the country, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, soaring inflation, and increased enforcement of international sanctions. In 2022, 14.6M people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2M from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8M people per month in 2021. Needs in the ERL sector are considerable with an estimated 13.8M people seeking for dignified early recovery and livelihood support. Over 90% of the population are living under the poverty line and COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated an already dire situation where PiN for 2022 increasingly grew compared to 2021(11.6M). People’s ability to meet basic needs has further decreased compared to 2020, with a disproportionate impact on FHHs, elderly without family support, PwDs and children. An increasing number of women and adolescent girls are forced to work to provide for their families. The income gap has continued to widen, with average HH expenditure now exceeding income by 50%, only 10% of HH have an income above the cost of Syria’s Minimum Expenditure Basket. (HNO 2022) Across Syria, HHs are now reverting to negative coping mechanisms more frequently than before. This includes child labor and child marriage and the sale of productive assets all of which increase protection needs and/or reduce HHs’ capacity for self-sustenance in the future.

5 - Implementation of activities

In the light of an integrated intervention with Protection, Health and Education sectors INTERSOS will mainstream protection and PSEA throughout the activities. Preliminary briefing and awareness-raising sessions will be conducted on Protection principles, CP and PSEA targeting SARC volunteers and suppliers; risks, preventive and mitigation measures related to the COVID-19. MoV training reports/pre & post test/ BNF satisfaction survey. 1.#1 LMA: appraisal of supply-demand of the local economic ecosystem to identify stakeholders, constraints and main drivers. 2.#300 Delivery of tailored vocational and technical training for unskilled and semi-skilled vulnerable beneficiaries selected against score system of vulnerabilities from caregivers and youth engaged in Education and Protection activities. Specific focus on FHH, youth who dropped out from school in underserved areas, PwDs, low income families at risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms. 3.#300 Provision of business and marketing skills workshops and life skills training comprehensively promoting women, man and youth engagement in the labor market. 4.#300 Distribution of start-up kits to foster self-employment:VT Trainees receive start-up kits(IKA)to initiate self-employment initiatives in relevant sub-sectors to the needs of the labor market. 5.#100 Support to rural enterprises and entrepreneurs: provision of productive assets to rural micro-enterprises of the agri-business sector (specific focus on Women and youth).

Mitigation:Comprehensive consultations undertaken with communities, needs- tailored and adapted intervention; Productive/employment sectors selected in a way to ensure participation of all target groups; c)Adopt a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms.2:Exclusion (physical access) of People with Disabilities (PwDs) -poor infrastructure/lack of facilitation. Likelihood: High; Impact: High.Mitigation: training halls with available access for PwDs;Consultations and engagement of PwDs throughout the implementation; awareness-raising/briefings for SARC staff and trainers on measures to be taken to properly address PwDs needs.3: Increase of tensions between Returnees and HC due to unequal assistanceprovided. Likelihood: Low-medium; Impact: High. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. One PSEA Referent and two PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

INTERSOS has developed a Contingency Planning Process (CPP) with risk analysis and related mitigation measures: Reputational Risk: unable to deliver on-time, breach of sanctions and/or do not harm principle. Procurement protocols in place, thorough market research/due diligence conducted for verification of quality standards/legal requirements, reporting mechanisms and robust monitoring strategy followed; Security and Access: unable to reach and implement in targeted areas. Internal security assessment and protocols, combined with SARC guidance, supervision and networks in the field. GBV/PSEA: risk of reinforcing gender stereotypes by excluding women from participation; risk of PSEA for BNF. Gender-sensitive and inclusive approach mainstreamed; complaint and feedback mechanisms in place, mandatory PSEA training for staff and implementing partner; Financial Risk and Accountability: exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversion or misuse of project financial resources. Internal system and SOPs to secure compliance and accountability respected. Systematic and rigorous monitoring of project activities and spending carried out; financial controlling rules and processes in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shock and re-alignment of targets. Health Risks-COVID-19 pandemic: staff and BNF exposed to health hazards and risks of contagion. INTERSOS takes into account precautionary measures related to COVID 19.
