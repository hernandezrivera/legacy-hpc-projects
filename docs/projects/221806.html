Project Summary

This response is designed to support 30,618 (19,743 male, 10,875 female), i.e., 29,416 school children and youth (19,018 boys and 10,398 girls) and 1,202 (725 male and 477 female) teachers, PTAs/SMCs, and Education officials.The target groups are emergency-affected IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Warrap (Tonj North, Tonj East, and Twic counties), Central Equatoria (Terekeka county), Upper Nile (Nasir and Maiwut counties), and Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta North and East counties). These include 29,416 learners (19,018 boys and 10,398 girls), 854 teachers (514 male and 340 female), 330 PTAs/SMCs (178 male and 152 female), and 18 Education officials. ADRA targets 62 primary schools, 8 secondary schools, 28 ALPs, and 45 ECDs, with the ALP and ECDs integrated within the 62 primary schools.
Out of the 29,416 learners targeted, 20,836 (12,712 boys, 8,124 girls) are targeted in primary schools, 720 (440 boys and 280 girls are targeted in secondary schools, 1,260 (740 boys and 520 girls) are targeted in ALP, and 6600 (4574 boys and 2026) girls are targeted in ECDs.
The proposed project will deliver the three education cluster objectives: access, quality, and coordination. It will, in particular, facilitate 29,416 (19,018 boys and 10,398 girls) emergency-affected children and youth's access to safe, protective, inclusive, and education services appropriate to their age, learning level, and needs by establishing 18 TLS and 24 WASH facilities done according to education cluster minimum standards incorporating best practices around girls and disability access.
The intervention will also provide children and youth with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. This will be achieved by training 854 teachers on EIE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills and distributing learning materials to 12,000 primary, ALP, and secondary schools and ECD kits to 6,600 (7,200 boys and 4,800 girls) learners according to education cluster minimum standards.
Finally, the intervention will strengthen coordination and crisis management capacity to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergency response. This will be achieved through the training of 54 (33 male and 21 female) school management committees (SMC) members/Parent-Teacher Associations (PTA) and 18 (10 male and 8 female) education authorities on school management, governance, climate change, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR).
As part of localization, ADRA South Sudan will work with Women Advancement Organization (WAO) in Warrap and Central Equatoria, Community Development and Support Services (CDSS) in Eastern Equatoria and Community Health and Development Organization in Upper Nile. These NNGOs will be supported by ADRA to distribute the learning materials and ECD kits and training of education officials, PTAs and SMCs in school government, DRR and climate change mitigation and adaption.
The response will target 2,942 (1,795 boys and 1,147 girls) children and youth with disabilities across the different levels of education (ECD, ALP, Secondary and primary. All data will be disaggregated by age, gender and disability.

1. Needs

The current context in South Sudan is characterized by conflict mainly due to inter-communal violence, revenge killings, and cattle raiding. Floods have displaced about 1.4 million people as of November 2024 in 43 counties. The economic situation in the country has also deteriorated, with commodity prices skyrocketing and the devaluation of the South Sudanese pound against the US dollar. Floods have affected all six counties of Warrap State, including the targeted counties of Twic, Tonj East, and Tonj North. In Twic, 15,000 individuals have been affected by floods, 30,000 in Tonj North and 24,876 in Tonj East. In Upper Nile, 118,408 individuals have been affected, including in Nasir and Maiwut counties. About 10 schools are inaccessible in Nasir, and 4 have been submerged in Maiwut county. In Central Equatoria, about 21,823 people have been affected by floods; meanwhile, 18,178 people have been affected. The floods' repercussions extend beyond displacement. The delayed payment of incentives to teachers and other civil servants has led to a lack of school support, as parents struggle to support the education of their children. About 20,000 returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia have come to Maiwut and Nasir, increasing competition for the overstretched resources and services. Clashes between armed groups have continued in Nasir County, displacing over 3000 people with IDPs settling in neighboring payams where education services were already limited and will need to be increased. The targeted areas also face food insecurity, as they are either in IPC 4 or 3, making it challenging to run education activities as parents prioritize food security. Displaced, host community, and returnee children in all targeted areas lack learning materials, ECD kits, teacher incentives/salaries, TLS, and WASH facilities. According to ADRA’s observation, the average pupil-to-latrine ratio in all the targeted locations is 1:100 for boys and 1:90 for girls, way above the recommended standard (1:60 for boys and 1:30 for girls). Meanwhile, the average classroom-to-pupil ratio is 1:80, also above the recommended standards. About 60% of the teachers in the targeted locations are volunteers and are untrained. Due to constant changes in the county and payam levels, about 50 % of the education officials are new and require training.Due to flooding and drought, PTAs/SMCs and education officials require training in DRR and climate change adaptation. Records from ADRA women groups in Nasir, Maiwut, Twic, Terekeka, Kapoeta North, and Greater Tonj indicate that many girls face GBV cases, such as early and forced marriages, when they are out of school. Through the activities of our inclusion project (Together For Inclusion/TOFI), we have established that about 2000 (1200 boys and 800 girls) children with disabilities in Eastern Equatoria, Warrap, and Upper Nile lack mobility and other assistive devices, which limit their attendance at schools.

2. Project activities

The proposed project is designed to respond to the educational needs of emergency-affected IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Upper Nile (Nasir and Maiwut counties), Warrap (Tonj East, Tonj North, and Twic counties), Eastern Equatoria (Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East counties), and Central Equatoria (Terekeka county). This response is designed to support 30,618 (19,743 male, 10,875 female), i.e., 29,416 school children and youth (19,018 boys and 10,398 girls) and 1,202 (725 male and 477 female) teachers, PTAs/SMCs, and Education officials. Out of the 29,416 learners targeted, 20,836 (12,712 boys, 8,124 girls) are targeted in primary schools, 720 (440 boys and 280 girls are targeted in secondary schools, 1,260 (740 boys and 520 girls) are targeted in ALP, and 6600 (4574 boys and 2026) girls are targeted in ECDs. ADRA targets 62 primary schools, 8 secondary schools, 28 ALPs, and 45 ECDs, with the ALP and ECDs integrated within the 62 primary schools.The response prioritizes the three education cluster objectives of Access, Quality, and Coordination. In particular, it will establish 18 TLS and 24 gender-segregated WASH facilities to enable children and youth to access safe, protective, inclusive education services appropriate to their age, learning level, and needs. These facilities will be established according to the minimum standards of the MoGEI/Education cluster.Under quality education, the response will train 854 teachers in EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills, distribute learning materials to 12,000 children and youth (6-17 years old), and ECD kits to 6600 children (3-5 years old) to enable them access quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle.In terms of coordination, the response will train 54 (33 male and 21 female) school management committees (SMC) members/Parent-Teacher Associations (PTA) and 18 (10 male and 8 female) education authorities on school management, governance, climate change, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Out of a proposed budget of 2,240,212 USD, ADRA has already secured 2,200,000 USD for the response in Upper Nile State (Maiwut and Nasir), Central Equatoria State (Terekeka), Eastern Equatoria State (Kapoeta North & Kapoeta East). The remaining 1,533,686 USD will likely be secured in the first quarter of 2025 for the response in Warrap (Tonj North, Tonj East, and Twic counties). Currently, ADRA is present in all the targeted geographical locations, implementing the Girls Education South Sudan project in Eastern Equatoria and Warrap as a state anchor and also implementing Action to Strengthen Partnership in Inclusion, Resilience, and Education.ADRA will link this response to her FSL projects in Greater Tonj, where we implement two FSL projects in partnership with WFP and promote school gardens response to climate by making dykes around schools and DRR activities. We shall also link the response to the GESS projects in Eastern Equatoria so that the supported schools will benefit from Capitation grants and cash transfers. ADRA will also link this project to FSL projects in Maiwut and Nasir to address DRR, Climate change adaptation, mitigation, and the introduction of school gardens. It will also be linked to the protection project in Nasir for referrals and Individual protection assistance. We shall also link it to the Health Sector Transformation Project to ensure referrals on health issues. ADRA’s MEAL department will monitor the project through regular visits and changes are adapted to achieve desired results. A MEAL officer based in Juba will be hired to coordinate data collection and submission of 5Ws to the Education cluster and filling of the FTS. As part of localization, ADRA South Sudan will work with Women Advancement Organization (WAO) in Warrap and Central Equatoria, Community Development and Support Services (CDSS) in Eastern Equatoria and Community Health and Development Organization in Upper Nile.
