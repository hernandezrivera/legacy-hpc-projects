Project Summary

This proposal aims to collaboratively implement vital lifesaving measures in specified areas of a Mozambique province. Its goal is to secure resilient, sufficient, fair, affordable, and inclusive access to basic services for children and families, especially the most vulnerable. This involves:
- Addressing urgent life-saving needs post-natural disasters/conflict events.
- Providing comprehensive Health, Nutrition, Protection, and WASH services, prioritizing enhanced access for vulnerable households, including those in schools and health facilities, with specific attention to the needs of children with disabilities and girls.
- Supporting the implementation of Behavior Change interventions and skills development. This empowers parents, caregivers, and children, including those with disabilities, to adopt healthy practices and demand accountability from community leaders, government, and service providers.
- Strengthening government and local authority-led systems to ensure timely, effective, resilient, affordable, inclusive, equitable, and sustainable access to basic services.
- Enhancing the capacities of government, local authorities, and communities to prepare for and respond to disasters, focusing on vulnerable households.
- Improving the collection, analysis, and visualization of disability, gender, and age disaggregated data to inform the adaptation and monitoring of an inclusive humanitarian response.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

In these contexts of humanitarian crises, MENTOR works with partners and health structures to reach communities that are difficult to reach, displaced populations or those most in need of health assistance. Our major activities in Cabo Delgado in all districts less Macomia, Mocimbua da Praia e Muindube, until we finish a security assessment MENTOR aims to: Access to high quality healthcare is critical to reduce deaths from tropical diseases and improve health in humanitarian and post-crisis contexts. MENTOR’s work in this area focuses on three key areas: • Support to community health workers; • Training and supervision; • Supply chain support. Integrated vector management uses a range of tools and strategies to tackle vector-borne diseases. Each tool plays a role within the integrated vector management framework, addressing different stages of the vector life cycle and targeting various points of disease transmission. These include: • Indoor Residual Spraying (IRS) is the application of insecticides to indoor surfaces where disease-transmitting mosquitoes’ rest; • Larval Source Management (LSM) prevents larvae from developing into adults at mosquito breeding sites; • Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) strategies promote behaviour change, encourage the use of preventive measures and adoption of vector control interventions. • Entomological Surveillance involves monitoring mosquito populations, their behaviour and insecticide resistance patterns. This helps inform decision-making and improves targeted interventions.• Long-Lasting Insecticide-treated Nets (LLINs) nets provide physical protection against mosquito bites and kill mosquitoes resting on net surface reducing the risk of vector-borne diseases. Monitoring and evaluation enable MENTOR to assess the effectiveness of interventions, track progress towards programme goals, and make informed decisions to improve health outcomes. We work closely with local health authorities and partners to develop and improve disease surveillance systems. These systems:• Enable the timely detection, reporting and tracking of cases • Help monitor disease trends • Identify transmission hotspots • Evaluate the impact of interventions Water, sanitation and hygiene is integral to our work tackling vector-borne and neglected tropical diseases. • By addressing the faecal-oral route of transmission and reducing breeding sites for multiple disease vectors, healthier environments are created to help prevent the spread of diseases. To improve access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene practices we focus on:• Infrastructure development - MENTOR works with local authorities and other agencies to improve water supply systems, build or repair sanitation facilities, and establish safe and sustainable hygiene infrastructure.• Urban WASH - In urban areas, addressing water supply and storage alongside sanitation and waste management challenges require approaches that consider population density, limited space and varying socio-economic conditions. • Tippy Taps - are simple, low-cost handwashing devices that promote proper hand hygiene, especially in settings with limited resources. They are an innovative and sustainable solution to encourage handwashing. • Community-led total sanitation empowers and engages communities to take ownership of their sanitation and hygiene solutions. Through a sense of ownership and shared responsibility, community-led total sanitation create lasting changes in sanitation behaviours, reduces open defecation and contributes to overall improvements in health.• Waste management is essential for maintaining a clean and healthy environment and to reduce mosquito breeding sites. Waste management strategies are implemented that promote safe disposal practices and removal of waste, in partnership with local stakeholders. Activities focus on raising awareness, training local HW, and establishing waste collection and disposal systems.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

An initial risk assessment will be conducted in order to identify the potential risk that the programme may encounter or cause. Follow-up assessments and meetings with representative beneficiaries will occur throughout the programme to understand if life and condition has changed in the population and if any kind of harm is being caused. MENTOR coordinators will be trained to monitor these risks and identify potential harms. The findings of the risk assessment will be used to design specific action plans to mitigate the risks identified. All MENTOR staff will be trained on PSEA and they will be committed to respect the Code of Conduct. Specifically, for the different activities the practical points that we will implement are: - Implementation of a green line available to denounce any kind of harm. - Implementation of suggestion boxes across the sites of implementation.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

MENTOR will include women and elderly people within the disaggregation of the data collection indicators. This will ensure that specific groups are reached. MENTOR will include in the trainings of staff and short-term activists PSEA points and about elderly population and people living with disabilities. When implementing the activities, the activists and MENTOR staff will need to map and target people that may encounter difficulties in accessing assistance. Supervision of the activities will also focus on this matter. In all activities proposed, MENTOR will look for gender parity across all human resources - if they are hired by MENTOR. MENTOR will also work together with representatives of different groups of the communities including women groups and community leaders. These meetings will serve to communicate and collect feedback from the activities at the planning phase as well as in the evaluation phase in line with accountability of affected population.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

People living with disabilities may have social and physical barriers to access assistance. In the different activities of these project, the community leaders, health technicians and all WASH FIT group members will be trained to map individuals with difficult access to assistance and will be encouraged to give attention to this. Durind on job visits MENTOR will monitor the activities in order to include mapping of people living with disabilities.
