Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen water, sanitation and hygiene response to affected population in Far North region of Cameroon with a focus on IDP camp, communities hosting IDP and returnees. In water domain, rehabilitation of boreholes and construction of small water supply network powered with solar energy will be prioritized. On hygiene and sanitation issues, communities approached for total sanitation will be implemented at municipality level. Construction of community latrines and showers will be limited to IDP sites. Hygiene promotion with distribution of minimum WASH kits will be implemented in target communities. The area targeted cover the division of Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Logone and Chari. With these interventions, this project targets 60,000 people for access to safe water, 50,000 people for acces to adequate basic sanitation and 150,000 people reached with critical WASH supply (including hygiene items) and services.
This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of WASH actor at regional level on key issues such as design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, cholera response, flood response, protection and gender mainstreaming in WASH sector.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In Far North region of Cameroon, internally displaced persons living in the main IDP sites of Mayo Sava (Kolofata, Mémé) and Mayo Tsanaga (Zamai, Tourou) do not have 15l / person / day and are more than 20 people per latrine. Investments made in 2018, 2019, 2020 and 2021 did not significantly improve this situation at standard level due to the continuous increase in people in the different IDP sites and insufficient investment in this sector.A multisectoral evaluation was carried out in 2021 by OIM where needs of different groups were collected. Off-site, in general, the situation is most critical in the Mayo Tsanaga and Mayo Sava divisions. 32% of the water points identified in the two departments are non-functional; It is estimated that more than 950 additional new boreholes are needed. The practice of open defecation is a common practice in the region regardless of the sex of the individual. It is more common among men, women, girls and boys of IDPs, off-site refugees and returnees than among host populations. In the 03divisions of Logone et chari, Mayo-Tsanaga and Mayo Sava, at least 34% of the population practices open defecation and more than 51% of IDPs. Which increases the risk of gender-based violence and undermines the privacy of female and male usersTo this situation is added the risk of spreading from these regions of the cholera epidemic declared since November 2021in South-West, Centre and Littoral regions of Cameroon.
