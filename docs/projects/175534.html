Project Summary

ADA -HRP aims at addressing emergency shelter and non-food items needs of newly & highly vulnerable populations, protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities through a multi-sector and single response approach to holistically restore community dignity. The project directly targets 24,000 individuals; 6,000 in Ayod, 4,400 in Fangak, 4,000 in Nyirol counties of Jonglei state, 4,000 in Nasir county of Upper Nile State, 1,600 in Kapoeta North county of Eastern Equatorial State and 4,000 in Mayendit county of Unity state. Fangak, Ayod and Nyirol counties are found in north of Jonglei State; Mayendit County South of Unity State; Nasir county in South East of upper Nile state and Kapoeta North in east of eastern Equatorial state, and these counties have been identified at emergency IPC (or IPC 4 counties).
In the multi-sector approach, ADA plans to collaborate with partners; ACTED is to implement CCCM, and ADA to implement an integrated ES/NFI and MaCDA to implement Protection program in Kapoeta North County in Eastern Equatorial State. While ADA proposes to response alone in Ayod, Nyirol, Fangak, Nasir and Mayendit counties through a single sector approach. The overall project goal is to ensure that the target communities have safe and dignified access to CCCM, shelter & NFIs and protection services. The project will directly target 24,000 conflict and disaster affected persons in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Mayendit, Nasir and Kapoeta North counties through integrated S/NFI, CCCM and protection services and support. During the project implementation, ADA will deliver assistance through a static response in Ayod, Fangak & Nasir and mobile response in Nyirol, Mayendit and Kapoeta North counties.
In Kapoeta North County, ADA, ACTED and MaCDA will implement a joint and integrated response aimed at crosscutting protection monitoring to inform all responses; that is to say protection mainstreaming, including on community infrastructures like housing, land and property issues, to all S/NFI responses; establish community social networks and safety nets and conduct protection trainings to committees to implement, and conduct mine risk awareness sessions through the committees to ensure safety to the community. All services (Protection/CCCM/SNFI assistance) will be implemented within Kapoeta North county and priority will be given to areas where high vulnerabilities exist to ensure a holistic programming. In Ayod, Fangak, Nirol, Nasir and Mayendit counties, ADA will implement a SNFI single sector approach, however will ensure integrating it with protection mainstreaming, and land, housing and propertyh and covid-19 guidelines, besides engaging all key stakeholders to achieve holistic service delivery to the affected communities in all target areas.
In all project areas, ADA, ACTED and MaCDA aims to address CCCM, protection & SNFI needs of newly & highly vulnerable conflict and disaster affected communities through in-kind S/NFI assistance, for both repairs and maintenance. ACTED will continue to strengthen the cluster state level coordination through SNFI State Focal Point in Eastern Equatorial State, which is under the technical guidance of ACTED. MacDA will implement the general protection and HLP activities, including identification of PSNs, referrals to service providers, information dissemination on key protection needs, advocacy for gaps and life skills training, hence these will complement other ongoing protection efforts done in Eastern Equatorial state by partners. ADA, through a mobile approach will coordinate responses with partners and SFPs and monitor both the single and multi-sector services through its static and roving teams to deep field locations in all its project areas.
The project will be implemented over a period of 12 months in both counties through single and multi-sector interventions, which will be boosted by Agencies' existing funding streams.

1 - Needs

Few years ago, after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the humanitarian needs of the South Sudanese people including refugees. Hence, South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020 and lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services are holding people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, Ayod, Fangak, Mayendit, Nasir, Nyirol and Kapoeta North were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified armed conflict and sub-national inter-communal violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19, all are compounding factors, hence worsening the needs of vulnerable persons. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced within and another 2.2 million as refugees in the neighboring countries like Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Sudan, Egypt, Central Africa Republic, DR Congo among others. Insecurity, lack of basic services such as shelters, non-food items -plastic sheeting, mosquitos nets, cooking utensils, blankets, community dignity, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevented people from returning home in large numbers (HNO Jan 2021).Overall food insecurity worsened, communities including Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, mayendit, Nasir and Kapoeta North are facing emergency IPC (phase 4) needs (IPC issued 18th December 2020). Women and girls continued to face extreme levels of gender-based violence and psycho-social distress. Affected people’s coping mechanisms are weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks and lack of access to resources, leading families to adopt negative practices such as forced labour, sexual exploitation and child marriage. The economy continued to spiral downwards, pushing people to the brink, especially in urban areas (HNO Jan 2021). Access to essential services, like health care, education, water and sanitation, shelters, as well as protection and legal services, are already limited and much of the service infrastructure like shelters were destroyed either by inter-communal violence or floods or abandoned due to displacement in 2020 (HNO Jan 2021). Despite humanitarian aid delivered to more than 6 million people already, the target communities kept falling into deeper need, however increased violence against aid workers and assets and operational interference prevented hundreds of thousands of vulnerable people from predictably accessing the support they needed.The above impacted into people’s physical and mental well-being, living standards and coping mechanisms, hence are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 including in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, Mayendit, Nasir and Kapoeta North. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. According to the inter-sectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol, mayendit, Nasir and Kapoeta North Counties which were classified to be at emergency need (IPC phase 4, according to the IPC issued 18th Dec 2020).The increase in needs is largely driven by the rising food insecurity across the country. When consulted, food insecurity compounded by socks like flooding, covid-19, vandalized shelters were identified as the primary challenges faced by the majority of affected people across sex and age groups (HNO Jan 2021). Similarly, poor living conditions, especially for the displaced, returnees, protracted IDPs and vulnerable host community, weaken their health and security and affect their dignity. Among the most vulnerable people are newly displaced families, communities hosting large numbers of displaced and/or recently returned people, and households that are headed by a single parent or looking after older people or people with disabilities.

2 - Project activities

Community mobilization, assessment, registration, distribution of items and PDM will be conducted in Ayod, Fangak, Nasir, Nyirol, Mayendit and Kapoeta North Counties: 1) ADA will mobilize and sensitize the affected community through appropriate channels that are inline with covid-19 preventive measures (e.g. install hand washing facility, sanitizers, social distancing). Local authorities including women, church leaders, chiefs and persons with disability will be engaged & consulted on the needs survey, and be used as focal and entry points for the project activities. 2) Needs Assessment of 24,000 beneficiaries: ADA with support of partners & authorities will conduct a rapid needs analysis to determine the vulnerability of the target populations and select project beneficiaries. Data will be collected by trained enumerators, analyzed and recommendations presented for intervention, to ensure ADA clearly understands the community needs. The project will employ various data collection techniques like household questionnaire, FGD, KII and observation. ADA will use the cluster standard tools to collect, analyze and present the data. 3) Registration of most vulnerable 24,000 beneficiaries (4,000HHs), upon approved reports will be done, through consultation and coordination with local authorities, community committees, enumerators and static partners. The project will target 50% of newly IDPs; 35% of returnees and 15% of vulnerable host community, this targeting aims at promoting strong community cohesion for peaceful coexistence. Of these, 60% (14,400) will be female and 40% (9,600) male. The project will target 5% (1,200) with awareness sessions on disaster/flood risk reduction. Also, 5% (1,200) disaster affected elderly will receive SNFI replacement to de-congest from communal shelters to reduce covid-19 spread. ADA will use the S/NFI Cluster’s selection criteria of household to household verification of needs and registration of the most vulnerable affected beneficiaries to receive the assistance. Similarly, cluster tools will be used during the registration, and priority will be accorded to the most vulnerable communities especially the elderly, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), female and child-headed households, and persons with disability. Tokens will be issued to the registered beneficiaries to facilitate quick and easy identification in the distribution sites. 4) Distribution of SNFI supplies to 24,000 beneficiaries (4,000HHs): ADA will identify distribution points at all project areas and consult its protection team and partners to conduct safety audit, to ensure affected persons dignity and rights are promoted and protected. Trained community mobilizers will be tasked to move from community to community to sensitize the beneficiaries about the distribution dates, venue and time. Prior to the distribution, subsequent consultation will be conducted with local community to ensure participation; distribution will take place at an appropriate agreed points where the entire beneficiary will come to receive the supplies. The distribution points will be selected not more than 30 minutes’ walk to mitigate protection incidences during return home. Due to covid-19 pandemic, distribution will be staggered. 5) ADA will administer a PDM tool after three weeks of distribution to evaluate the project impact and collect feedback from the beneficiaries on their level of satisfaction with the project received. Local enumerators, will be identified with support of authorities, trained and deployed to the field for data collection. Different data collection techniques will be employed including FGD, KII, household questionnaire and observation to ensure rich information is gathered for analysis. A random sampling method will be used to pick respondents for the interview to ensure each beneficiary has an equal chance of being selected to provide their opinions. Raise awareness on flooding, HLP and covid-19 at all project phases.

If the forecasts go up too much it is a sign that the spending will be out of control by the end of the project. So, a decision can be made at this point.Performance to project case -finally, ADA shall regularly re-visit its log-frame and see what it has originally agreed upon, and determine how is the project shaping up. Check that the strategies and activities are still realistic and that the HRP problem this project is designed to solve does still exist.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This project aims at promoting initiatives that encourage a speak-up culture amongst the affected communities; and support field-level mainstreaming of PSEA activities. ADA Juba headquarter will assist its field offices to mainstream inter-agency complaints mechanisms at community level, strengthen communication with communities, support survivors to access services through referral pathways, and related activities. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) through ensuring its employees are trained on and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support are enrolled to access the SNFI assistance. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through identifying reporting lines and awareness raising. Also, this project is designed to provide information on who to report to. Preparators will be reported to local authorities, police and partners like Save the children and UNICEF. Reporting will be done case by case and as they arise. Map out PSEAs partners and establish safe referral pathways for survivors to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure zero tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including taking legal steps against such preparator. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee should be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA shall adopt inclusive methods for carrying out consultations with various community members, considering sexes and age groups. This shall involve conducting separate community meetings such as focus group discussion with women and girls alone, and men also separately ensuring persons with disabilities are engaged too, which allows for easy speak up of sensitive issues of disadvantaged groups for project attention. The project is design to recruit local enumerators and community mobilizers -those who can speak the local languages will be given high priority to be selected into the project, to ensure adequate translation of project activities to those who are illiterate. The project shall hold meetings with authorities and disadvantaged groups to share assessment findings, verify needs and triangulate data to ensure comprehensive mapping of PSNs is done, and that physically impaired persons are reached, consulted and involved into the project. ADA project designs typically consider ultimate beneficiaries whether and how to consult with at least a sample of some of them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ADA will consult the beneficiary and other stakeholders like chiefs, religious leaders on where they feel distribution locations shall be established. This consultation shall be done during the beneficiary registration timing. Also, beneficiaries will be consulted during the needs assessment to provide at least their top three (3) priorities items (needs) for consideration during the recommendation of supplies for distribution. The different opinions of beneficiary will be harmonized during FGD to ensure the most top three (3) items meets the needs of all affected communities. ADA will engage community leaders including chiefs, RRCs, RoSS, church leaders and women groups to agree on HLP issues. This engagement will ensure, that the IDPs or returnees have access to land to construct their temporary shelters for settlement and cultivation of crops. ADA will ensure that authorities provide written MOUs between the host community and the IDPs/Returnees on the land that the beneficiary will settle on. This aims at minimizing communal conflicts. During these engagements. ADA will have a chance to provide information on HLP guidelines in accordance to the South Sudan land rights. ADA will employ various processes and mechanisms to solicit opinions and feedback from the key stakeholders like beneficiaries about the SNFI assistance on their lives, the feedback loop will include: participatory methods and processes for needs assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation; accountability frameworks and mechanisms; complaints and response mechanisms; listening exercises between project team and beneficiary; conduct social audits to ensure protection mainstreaming in its activities and story-telling sessions, among others. The feedback loop mechanism; ADA will receive and provide information to beneficiaries through focus groups discussions, personal interviews with selected respondents especially government authorities and static partners, open community meetings, trained community mobilizers going door-to-door, establishing listening exercises and appointing reporting lines, community monitoring field visits, existing community liaisons contact persons appointed by beneficiaries, weekly coordination meetings, establish story telling by affected communities and community project committees. Additionally, ADA will train key stakeholders including community project committees on grievances procedures, especially linked to complaints mechanisms, also the community mobilizers will translate and pass key messages with aid of megaphones to read information printed on fliers or reports. The feedback and complaint mechanism shall be based on community preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. All feedback will be recorded, and responses given to the community members or groups, besides a monthly report of the feedback received and responses given is sent to ADA head office in Juba for documentation, filling and refining into implementation matrix. The project team shall include staff with specific responsibility for supporting the complaint/feedback mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. Not only that, it will provide ADA with feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, will strengthen the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Essentially, when positive feedback is received, it motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also feedback help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct, thus places ADA in position to know who to keep in the organization and who not to keep. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project has been designed to include capacity building approaches, like enhancing knowledge and networks of primary stakeholders (e.g. individuals, households, communities) through awareness creation and trainings sessions to strengthen the ability of affected persons to withstand adverse impact from external stressors including armed conflict, sub-national violence and disaster like flood.The project designs has process of incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity of affected persons while receiving the SNFI assistance in the project locations. It is the responsibility of ADA and partners to ensure safety audits is done at all its distribution points; and that beneficiaries walks for less than 30 minutes to receive their items, distributions are conducted at morning hours (from 10am-3pm) as late or early hours are always prone for armed attacks. Distributions points should be located where there is shade and at least minimum WASH facilities to reduce exposure of beneficiaries to dangers of looting, rape and diseases.The project design prioritizes safety and dignity, and with strategy to avoid causing harm. It will prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of SNFI intervention which could increase affected people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks. Also, the project aims at promoting equity and meaningful access; the approaches take pro-active steps to ensure beneficiaries’ meaningful access to impartial assistance and facilities -in proportion to need and without any barriers (i.e. discrimination). Besides that, the project approaches pays special attention to lobby for inclusion through ensuring community infrastructures like schools, health centers, training halls, NGO offices, water and supplies distribution points have facilities that enables disable individuals and groups to participate like any other persons.. These groups may include the disable persons, lactating and pregnant mothers, female and child headed house, elderly 60 years+, unaccompanied minor and person with ill health condition. Accountability to affected populations: ADA shall set up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions, provide feedback on the SNFI assistance provided, share concerns and submit complaints. This will be done through community project committees constituted from the onset of the project. Accountability is articulated internally through the project’s own mechanism, and independently through an independent feedback and complaints mechanism. The Communication with Communities (CwC) approaches shall include sharing of lessons learned and coordination of activities implemented by ADA. Participation and empowerment: The project design support the development of self-protection capacities and assist affected people in claiming their rights, including but not exclusively the rights to shelter and NFIs, among others. Participation and empowerment approaches are integrated throughout the project phases while static protection actors like McDA in the proposed multi-sector project locations proposes to assist people in claiming their rights by promoting both the duties and responsibilities of the authorities, recognized community leaders, and the beneficiaries towards the services provided.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

ADA protection mainstreaming shall ensure a survivor-centered approach to create a supportive environment in which survivors’ rights and wishes are respected, their safety is ensured, and they are treated with dignity and respect. This approach will be based on safety and security of survivors and their children are the primary considerations; and that, survivors have the right to choose to whom they will or will not tell their story, and any information about them should only be shared with their informed consent. All project actions taken should be guided by respect for the choices, wishes, rights and dignity of the survivor. The role of ADA as a helper is to facilitate access to information/services through referral pathway creation for survivor. The project shall ensure survivors receive equal and fair treatment regardless of their age, disability, gender identity, religion, nationality, ethnicity, sexual orientation or any other characteristic. ADA will apply the rights-based approach to address the root causes of discrimination and inequality to ensure that everyone has the right to live with freedom and dignity, safe from violence, exploitation and abuse. Besides, a community-based approach shall be used to ensure that affected populations are engaged actively in developing strategies related to their protection and the provision of SNFI assistance. This approach involves direct involvement of women, girls and other at-risk groups at all stages in the SNFI response, to identify protection risks and solutions, and build on existing community-based protection mechanisms. The project will apply the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality to underpin the implementation of the Minimum Standards, and are essential to maintaining access to affected populations and ensuring an effective SNFI response.• Also, ADA will apply the “do no harm” approach, which involves taking all measures necessary to avoid exposing affected people to further harm as a result of the actions of ADA. In all the project areas, ADA will apply the principles of partnership, which comprise of equality, transparency, a results-oriented approach, responsibility and complementary. Finally, ADA will put the interest of the child in its forefront of services, especially the child and adolescent girl and boy survivors of sexual abuse have the right to have their best interests assessed and determined, and taken as a primary consideration in all decisions that affect them. They will be consulted through FGD and included to receive the SNFI assistance in all project areas.
