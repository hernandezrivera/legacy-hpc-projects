Project Summary

This project will be aimed at supporting the large student population who are out of school, or falling behind due to an extended period out of the education system due to displacement, and Covid. The project will target 29,672 children (60% girls) and 400 teachers (60% female)) from both the IDP and host communities so as to reduce the risk of tension, and promote cohesion. Children will benefit from TLSs, accelerated learning programmes, receive learning and hygiene supplies, and be supported through MHPSS and protection services. Teachers will be trained in MHPSS and DRR and accelerated learning techniques, and receive teaching supplies. Back to school campaigns will also be run in all target districts.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

This project will be aimed at supporting the large student population who are out of school, or falling behind due to an extended period out of the education system due to displacement. The project’s key activities across Ancuabe, Balama, Chiure, Cidade De Nampula, Meluco, Montepuez, Namuno will be: • Establishing TLSs • Providing children with hygiene and learning supplies • Providing teachers with education supplies • Running back to school campaigns • Training teachers in accelerated learning techniques, GBV and MHPSS • Provision of accelerated learning programmes for children • Including child participation in all assessment and monitoring steps, and in the design of the feedback mechanism

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

All staff working on this project, including implementing partners, are required to have received training on safeguarding and child protection and adhere to organisation policy prior to conducting any field visits or contact with beneficiaries. In turn beneficiaries will have access to a complaints and feedback mechanism, with the promotion of Linha Verde. Street Child has extensive experience of delivering child protection in emergency projects, working in protracted crises through operations in DRC, Afghanistan, Cameroon and North East Nigeria, and integrating these into education projects, in line with Street Child’s global strategy to ensure children are safe, in school, and learning. The project activities will be delivered through coordination with community leaders and engagement with community ensuring transparency and accountability, as well as to ensure the activities are context appropriate. Should the monitoring or feedback mechanisms demonstrate a change in risk, or need to review certain activities, the project team will work with local authorities and community leaders to ensure it is providing meaningful services to as wide a range of people as possible. All project staff will also be trained on PSEA, understand their role and position as humanitarian actors, and be familiar with Street Child’s PSEA policy. The project will have a central PSEA focal point, to which all PSEA allegations will be brought to, from each organisation’s and district’s PSEA focal points. A clear line of communication will be established so that every member of staff knows who to bring concerns to confidentially. PSEA will be mainstreamed into all partner training, who will then cascade this down to communities. The project team will continuously stress the importance of preventing sexual exploitation and abuse, and remind beneficiaries and communities of their rights, and ability to report through project reporting mechanisms, with other organisations, or through Linha Verde. Should a PSEA case arise the project team will work hand in hand with the PSEA WG, following their reporting mechanisms, as well as with Street Child’s internal PSEA policies and structures, culminating in a PSEA focal point at Headquarters. In collaboration with the PSEA WG, all relevant cases will be brought to local government structures. All cases will be dealt with the utmost confidentiality and respect, and be followed through until the end, with the person in question being kept updated regularly, and given the support they require.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Mozambique has made strides in school enrolment in recent years, however school dropout rates are still very high, with under 50% of children finishing school. This predominantly affects girls, with only 46% finishing primary school, and 22% finishing secondary school. Over 56% of women are illiterate, which rises to over 70% in rural areas. During an emergency the risk of abuse and exploitation increases and girls are more exposed to negative coping strategies, such as transactional sex and early marriage. We will look to mitigate these issues through back to school campaigns to encourage marginalised girls to reintegrate back into school. Teachers will also be trained on key CP, GBV topics, to allow girls to access to an inclusive environment and promote gender sensitive discussions, increasing access to support and referral services. Teachers will also be trained in MHPSS, so as to be able to better support conflict affected children. The back to school campaigns, coupled with training teachers in accelerated learning, MHPSS, GBV, and CP will create safer learning environments, which will improve access and retention rates for marginalised groups, positively impacting learning outcomes and the overall well-being of students during school. Initial community engagement with target areas will take place to introduce the projects, leveraging of current Street Child presence in Montepuez, Chiúre, and Ancuabe, and initiating new relationships in Meluco, Balama, Nampula, and Namuno. An initial assessment at the beginning of the project will ascertain the amount of children out of school, and perceived barriers to education, so as to develop appropriate messaging for back to school campaigns and teacher training. Community leaders and key community members will also be engaged with to raise awareness on the importance of education, and understand the barriers they identify to children accessing education. Feedback will be collected on a rolling basis, as well as encouraging each community to identify at least one preferred complaints and reporting mechanism in each project area, such face to face reporting, or a complaints box that will be checked weekly. The project team will continuously stress the importance of community feedback to improve the project, so as to encourage honest dialogue, as well as the importance of continuous child participation in feedback loops. The feedback received will be regularly reviewed and acted upon where possible, with feedback always given back to the community to ensure continued participation in feedback mechanisms. Coordination with district education department will also take place for identification of teachers and in consultation for teacher training content.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

This project will target a minumum of 3500 children with disability, with back to school campaigns, accelerated learning, and temporary learning spaces. Material used will be the latest MINEDH material, adapted to include a focus on inclusion and disability, and we will also coordinate with other organisations working solely on disability. There is provision of access to temporary learning spaces with trained teachers on inclusive education, which in turn will promote and support integration into formal schooling. Identification of both children with disabilities and the specific disability will be determined through a baseline community assessment, and in consultation with district departments and community members. This assessment will be conducted using the Washington group tool.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Disaster risk reduction will be incorporated into the teacher training for cyclone affected communities and will require the participation of the school council members. They will be supported to develop a basic school emergency plan in order to respond to disasters, identify risks and dangers and prevent and mitigate their impact, promoting a sustainable culture of prevention and resilience. In addition there will be promotion of community advovacy on DRR through school council engagement.
