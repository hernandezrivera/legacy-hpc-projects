Project Summary

This project aims at building resilience of Rohingya and Host Community women and adolescent girls through increased livelihood skills, access to livelihoods opportunities including start-up support and establishing market linkages.
UN Women has five Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers (MPWCs) in Camps 3, 4, 4 extensions, 5, and 18 and three in Host Community in. In these Centers psycho-social services, 1st aid medical services and counseling and referral services for GBV cases, are provided to Rohingya women, livelihood activities have been carried out. Three new MPWCs is running in the Host Community. in Ukhiya, Teknaf and Cox’s Bazar Sadar. 6 months livelihood trainings will continue to be provided in the MPWCs to both Rohingya and Host Community women. The trainings will focus on handicrafts, fishery, beauty parlor, tailoring, production of masks, and ecofriendly jute product; production of re-usable sanitary napkins as part of a joint initiative with IOM and UNFPA. After receiving training beneficiaries will receive in kind grant including sewing machine and other start up kits. Besides training on home stead gardening will be provided. Participants to home stead garden, will receive seed and gardening tools. . In addition, ways to engage women in bulk production and linkage women’s market will be explored. Specific attention will be given to include most marginalized women and girls in the activities, including the female headed households and women with disabilities.
UN Women has established a Women’s Market at camp 5, which started operation in January 2021. . Under this project, Women’s Market will be more functional to ensure market linkages for women producers, not only for trained women from MPWCs; but also, for other women producers supported by other partners such as WFP, FAO, UNDP, AAB, BRAC, HSI, PRANTIC, SNKSS and United Purpose. As the women vendors will be both Rohingya and Host Community, the Market is also expected to contribute social cohesion. Basic entrepreneurship skill development trainings will be delivered for the women who have shops in the Market. In addition, there are 3 café and display corner with MPWC in camp 4, 4 ext. and women’s market in camp 5.

1 - Identified Needs

Large gender gaps persist in access to economic opportunities in both Rohingya and Host communities. According to REVA4, 70% of Rohingya men participate in the labor force compared with just 10% of Rohingya women; host communities show similar trends. Marginalized women and girls are faced with the vulnerabilities identified such as being unskilled and unemployed and are often forced to stay at home and take care of children and the elderly and do household chores. The livelihood and cash-based activities became more and more limited for women and girls. In this context single women heads of household (approx. 16% of the total Rohingya refugee population), adolescent girls, young women constitute the most vulnerable groups.Host community households led by women (14%) are generally among the poorest and most vulnerable. Their economic conditions vary considerably depending on factors such as marital status, the social context of female leadership and access to productive resources, and most importantly, their ability to go out and generate income. Due to limited viable livelihood opportunities for refugees and transferable skills development, women and girls are at an increased risk of forced marriage, survival sex, begging, drug smuggling, forced labor, selling of their remaining assets and relief items to mitigate economic and food insecurity. Rohingya women and adolescent girls face challenges in accessing opportunities and services in the Camps even before Covid-19. The Rohingya community is highly conservative and there are deeply rooted gender norms in the Rohingya society, which leads to women and adolescent girls having limited mobility and therefore limited access to information, assistance and services, low level of participation to decision making, low level of participation to livelihood activities; and high level of GBV. By the time of the study, only 2 per cent of women were engaged in an income-generating activity compared to 27 per cent of men. Covid-19 Pandemic also created economic consequences the existing inequalities have been exacerbating and the Covid-19 crisis have been disproportionately affecting women and girls, as highlighted in the Rapid Gender Analysis on the Impact of COVID-19 in Cox’s Bazar (conducted by the Gender Hub, UN Women, OXFAM and Care). . However, despite the challenges and restrictions, Rohingya women and girls and those in Host Community, have been playing a key role in increasing the resilience of their families and communities. Unfortunately, due to limited viable livelihood opportunities for refugees and transferable skills development, women and girls are at increased risk of forced marriage, survival sex, begging, drug smuggling, forced labor, selling of their remaining assets and relief items to mitigate economic and food insecurity. Therefore, UN Women through its self-reliance and livelihoods project, aims at providing socioeconomic development support and opportunities to marginalized and vulnerable women and girls in both Rohingya and Host Communities to enable them to become economically active through creation of sustainable livelihoods for themselves. This project will create an enabling environment for marginalized women and girls to access livelihoods skills, opportunities, and support. The economic empowerment of participating women and girls in the project shall reduce their vulnerability through assistance to enter the job market as skilled labor or as entrepreneurs.
