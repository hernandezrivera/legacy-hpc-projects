Project Summary

The prolonged humanitarian crisis has had a devastating impact on loss of life and properties in Nigeria’s Northeast with millions of people in need of emergency protection assistance and assistance.
Since the beginning of 2015, north-east Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The crisis remains one of the most severe in the world with 7.1 million individuals in need of humanitarian assistance (International Organization for Migration, Nigeria 2009).
Vulnerable civilians have suffered a lot in search for safety of life and resilience, in most case the law that safeguard the civilians are not in most ways been respected and hour by most parties involved in the crisis. Several instrument such as the Kampala convention that assist in the protection of the right of internally displaced persons in Africa is not implemented fully into action by the Nigeria government leaving the IDPs in their thousand restless going back and front in search for safety. Hence the crisis, more than 27,000 people have been killed in the three north-eastern BAY states, according to UN humanitarian coordinating office, OCHA, and thousands of women and girls have been abducted. Worthy of note also is the challenge of insufficient humanitarian agencies/actors to adequately attend to the needs of the vulnerable people of concern in camp.
Gender Base Violence, which has devastating effect mostly women and girls with over 90% survivors according GBVMIS snapshot of August 2020. The effect of GBV on IDP camps have lately been on the increase according to recent survey by joint rapid assessment in Borno IDP camps by UN-Women, OXFAM AND CARE International on the situation of IDPs especially in the era of COVID-19. The assessment reveals the increase in Gender Base Violence in IDPs camps where series of abuses, violations, exploitations are highly observed, and it calls for more work in the field.
Children are having their share of neglect, arm soldier’s recruitment mostly by the arm opposition group, exploitation of underage child and lots more. Abuse in several forms are a daily reality for many Nigerian children and only a fraction ever receive help. Six out of every 10 children experience some form of violence – one in four girls and 10 per cent of boys have been victims of sexual violence, the children who reported violence, fewer than five out of a 100 received any form of support. The drivers of violence against children (VAC) are rooted in social norms, including around the use of violent discipline, violence against women and community beliefs about witchcraft, all of which increase children’s vulnerability. (UNICEF, November. 2019)
The proposed project intends to address these challenges by providing protection services (Protection, Gender Base Violence Case Management & Prevention and Child Protection activities) through a community engagement approach with the People of Concern.

1 - Needs

Protection: • Civilians in the field have cause to face threat in the crisis zone, this is most probably due to non-adherence to the rule of engagement by all parties involved in the warfare. This brings our attention to the need for warring parties to be refreshed with the laws that protect civilians in war, furthermore, is the need of the civilians to also be updated about their right in the emergency situation they find themselves. • The need to have capacity of the humanitarian actors strengthened as most actors have the zeal but inefficient in technical capacity. • Protection monitors are not enough at most times, most people of concern have faced diverse protection challenges and risk but lack the standard process of having their flight attended to. Especially with the pandemic COVID-19 era, only but a few monitors in the field due to movement restriction for most humanitarian workers, this brought about the need to have community volunteer’s protection networks or association from amongst the people of concern, always trained and equipped with the necessary tools to overcome challenges and risk in emergency settings. GBV • The under reporting of GBV sub sector at the heart of the crisis, despite the challenges faced by survivors of GBV (mostly women and girls) such as rape, sexual exploitation and abuses, denial of resources and leadership this portrait the need for actors to constantly have prevention activities well disseminated amongst the IDP camps in Borno state and north east Nigeria at large. Recent joint rapid need assessment by CARE International and UN-women situation of COVID-19 reveals more cases of GBV in the emergency camps in Borno state in the northeast of Nigeria in August 2020 There is also the need for the capacity of GBV actors to be built to meetup with the challenges of the GBV even as it is being under reported. There is the need for more community engagement in the field to create or improve a positive social change in the various areas of intervention. Child Protection • The need to have children in emergencies respected and dignified is quite paramount in the north east region of Nigeria. Different children are faced with diverse neglect, abuses, exploitation and child recruitment and labour. Many more vulnerable children have not been identified and given attention such as referred to the relevant organization for proper care, this situation have affected many children negatively and hence the need for psychosocial support programmes for children and a comprehensive child case management especially in the hard to reach areas.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

For the purpose of safety and dignity in the accessing of humanitarian aid from aid workers, protection mainstreaming is a vital tool COWACDI uses in the implementation of her programmes, having in mind the purpose of do no harm, equality, accountability and participation for all targeted beneficiaries always in each phase of all projects.COWACDI staff share an ethical responsibility for mainstreaming protection across the humanitarian response, contract and programmes staff, from design, implementation monitoring and evaluation staff, and their supervisors are responsible for ensuring that activities within their respective sector are carried out with a “protection lens”, and for ensuring that their activities integrate protection principles. Modalities for ensuring that proposed project will ensure protection mainstreaming is the simple design of a checklist to be utilized by all personnel in the field, this check list will ensure that:members of marginalized community’s/minority groups, including people with disability are included in consultations at all phases of the project from design to exit stage. Women, girls, men and boys are duly included in consultations and decision-making processes.Youths are adequately included in consultations and decision-making processes.Assistance affect peaceful coexistence positively and not spark any inter community conflict over access to resources. The project has mechanisms to prevent and address sexual exploitation and abuse, with a PSEA communication strategy and appropriate complaints mechanism structure that is well understood and used by beneficiaries in case of any bridge of contract by either party. Worthy of note is the mandatory signing by all staff and volunteers on PSEA & Code of Conduct before the commencement of any project.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COWACDI’s staff are always obliged to work with the concrete policy of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) & donors. COWACDI has an established community-based feedback mechanism structure, which allows all targeted beneficiaries to express any complaint at any point in time; these include hotlines, suggestion box, mail and social media handles, community engagement such as the focus group discussion with beneficiaries, monitoring and evaluation processes and a prompt response to complaints from the design stage which will allow full beneficiaries participation and ownership of the project, worthy of note is that beneficiaries will be informed on how to form network through a or take advantage of existing ones at all times. Through these, COWACDI will ensure that issues of concern to individuals, as well as, to the community are brought to its attention and other partners, for intervention. Vulnerability screening will be used to identify and target those in need of protection based materials using the community based approach mechanism. COWACDI will also ensure that staff involved in the project comply with its code of conduct and the special measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Lastly, COWACDI’s firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. For this reason, a strong Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) as well as continuous communications with stakeholders, including local leaders and government authorities as well as beneficiaries through a participatory approach, are key to guide activities and monitor the evolution of the conflict.
