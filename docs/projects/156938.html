Project Summary

This project’s main goal is to provide NFI support to the conflict affected population. The consequences of the conflict have been particularly severe and have converted many parts of the different governorate into extremely hard-to-reach areas, where people suffer from extreme needs, due to the enormous destruction and being cut off from access to markets. Displaced people, returnees and the overburdened host communities face tremendous struggles while trying to provide basic products for their families and ensure a dignified survival. With this NFI support, the living conditions of 198.000 people in areas with high rates of displacement and large scale destruction will be improved and the basics of a dignified life will provided through the distribution of 66.000 NFI kits.
The number of kits, kit components and targeted locations have been determined after assessing the overall situation in the regions, needs of the population and timing of the intervention.
The project is in line with the NFI sector strategic objectives and priorities of this allocation, with a primary goal of delivering urgent assistance to people in need, prioritizing vulnerable groups, according to the identified needs in the areas of intervention.

Needs

Almost eight years of armed conflict in Syria has caused major devastation in many regions of the country, has destroyed large parts of the basic infrastructure and has led to extreme suffering. The needs among the 11,7 million people requiring humanitarian assistance remain massive. Of these, almost 4.4 million people need NFI assistance, especially in areas with a constantly high displacement rate and volatile situations, with different parties involved and frequent changes of control. Major safety, security and accessibility issues in those regions currently hinder the humanitarian community from reaching wide parts of the affected population and from providing aid and support to those in need.The hardship and struggles in many governorates across the country are overwhelming, especially in areas in the northeast region (Deir-ez-Zor governorate in particular) and areas in the southwest region (specifically Daraa governorate), where people have suffered from the negative outcomes of frequent tensions and the dynamic and fluid situation including besiegement, large displacements, damages to services and infrastructure, inability to access local markets and engage in economic activities.Many of the displaced families have minimal belongings and state that one of the most widespread challenges during their daily struggle is the inability to afford rent and non-food items. This project will serve to address the critical need for lifesaving and life-sustaining Non-Food Items, to support vulnerable families and ensure a dignified life.The needs of affected people in conflict affected areas are crucial in every NFI support, as the primary goal is to accommodate such responses with respect to the local context and the local communities background and circumstances.

Implementation of activities

Rebuild Syria Reconstruction Program (RSRP) will plan, manage and implement the NFI interventions for 2019 from its sub-office in Deir-ez-Zor and its main office in Damascus.The intervention will be implemented and monitored in coordination with key stakeholders, including the local community, local authorities and the NFI cluster, to avoid future any duplications with other implementing partners working in the area.This project is in line with the NFI sector’s strategic objectives to target crisis-affected people and provide them with lifesaving and life sustaining essential items through a timely intervention. Rebuild Syria Reconstruction Program will mobilize its capacities and resources to support vulnerable displaced people in need of core relief NFI items, returnees with lack of access to markets and the overburdened host community struggling with its limited capacities to provide for the distressed people it is hosting.RSRP identifies the most vulnerable groups in need of NFI assistance through a clear beneficiary selection criteria based on the prioritized vulnerable groups included in the 2019 HRP, we take into consideration age, gender issues, displacement status health issues and other aspects that are considered based on the components of every kit and the contextual characteristics of every location, hence the selection criteria is adapted according to the kit components.Needs assessments will be conducted to identify the required items and address the specific and contextual needs of the people in the targeted areas.With our plan to expand assistance to another governorate and after multiple site visits to Deir-ez-Zor we aim for a comprehensive NFI distribution including seasonal items such as winter clothing kits, blankets and summer kits and core essential items such as mattresses, hygiene kits and solar lamps.Accountability to the affected population will be guaranteed throughout the entire project cycle and will clearly be included in the planning and implementation process. The coordination with the local community will serve to identify needs and determine the appropriate implementation plan with respect to the local cultural norms and traditions. The monitoring and evaluation process, conducted by experienced local and international staff, will significantly contribute to the project’s compliance with the 4 core humanitarian principles: Humanity, Neutrality, Impartiality and Independence.All the above-mentioned measures and processes will be translated in assessments, surveys, monitoring and evaluation reports and photos that will be shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters to participate in the humanitarian coordination process.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Tension from excluded beneficiaries might lead to conflict between target groups and complaints against the humanitarian partner.Likelihood: HighMitigating measures: - Clear and comprehensible communication of the selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries (RSRP always aims to target the most vulnerable groups in a community, beneficiary selection criteria is always based on site visits, 2019 HNO and 2019 HRP).- Frequent collaboration with the NFI sector and local community to remain updated on the changing needs and tailor the assistance accordingly.- Complete impartiality in informing people within an affected community.Monitoring and Evaluation: A solid Monitoring and Evaluation process to oversee the selection process and registration.Resources required: Monitoring and Evaluation team, experienced and qualified registration and distribution team.Protection Risk 2: Tension within the community itself due to variation in assistance.Likelihood: HighMitigating measures: - Coordination with sectors to align kit components of every distribution with minimum standards and prioritized content.- Constant quality control and frequent sample checks to ensure that all the distributed kits are equal.Monitoring and Evaluation: Include item variance monitoring.Conduct satisfaction surveys to ensure beneficiaries are content with the kits or suggest alternate items.Resources required: Logistics (Procurement and warehouse) team, Monitoring and Evaluation team.Protection Risk 3: Inability to access registration or distribution sites by vulnerable groups in the community (the elderly, disabled people and people with diseases and chronic illnesses).Likelihood: HighMitigating measures: - Collaboration with local community to determine suitable distribution sites for all targeted groups.- Well established distribution sites to ensure an efficient distribution process.Monitoring and Evaluation: Include gender and age disaggregation in addition to disability percentage in beneficiary targets and measure results against outcome and outputs (To ensure inclusion of vulnerable groups).Frequent monitoring visits to distribution sites.Required resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, Experienced distribution coordinator.Protection Risk 4: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers.Likelihood: LowMitigating measures: - Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection.- Obligate humanitarian workers to adhere to a code of conduct.Establish an efficient and effective complaints mechanism.Monitoring and Evaluation: Conduct satisfaction surveys to ensure beneficiaries are content with the distribution process. Conduct regular door to door visitsRequired Resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, distribution coordinator.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Safety and Security Issues leading to inability of achieving the registration of beneficiaries and Post Monitoring Distribution.Likelihood: LowMitigating Measures: - Constant coordination with locals and local authority in the areas of intervention.- Transparency in the implemented activities and the desired outcome of the emergency response.Risk 2: Major Crisis in an area leading to an evacuation of the people and resulting in the inability to track families receiving the assistanceLikelihood: MedMitigating Measures:- Experienced program and project staff with knowledge of the local context in the areas of implementation and a solid contingency plan.- Constant overview of situation reports, and latest updates related to the Syrian crisis.Risk 3: Major increase in the population in the targeted areas leading to insufficient supplies (newly opened areas where returnee influx is high)Likelihood: HighMitigating Measures: - Frequent coordination with all the humanitarian actors to encourage capacity mobilization and response in the areas where the needs are extensive.- Experienced program and project staff with experience in risk management and able to adapt the overall response, and human and financial resources.Risk 4: Safety and security issues preventing the implementation of the emergency response.Likelihood: HighMitigating Measures:- Constant overview of situation reports, and latest updates related to the Syrian crisis which allows RSRP to address the needs of alternate vulnerable beneficiaries in other areas of intervention, should access to the targeted area not be feasible.Risk 5: Delayed, denied or revoked GOS approvals which prevent delivering the assistance in a timely manner or prevent implementation of the project.Likelihood: HighMitigating Measures: - Transparency with local authorities in the areas of intervention, in addition to strong communication and collaboration prior to project implementation with local partner.Risk 6: Major Fluctuation in prices.Likelihood: MediumMitigating Measures:- An experienced financial department able to stay updated on the economic situation in the area, make the correct financial assumptions and generate quality financial reports for donor.
