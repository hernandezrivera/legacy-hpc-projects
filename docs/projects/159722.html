Project Summary

The intervention targets vulnerable IDPs and their host communities with emergency shelter and NFI support. It strongly integrates protection mainstreaming in shelter and NFI and is aligned to Shelter Cluster objectives.
The project targets 9,400 IDP households and host communities, assuming 6 people per household, this is approximately 56,400 individuals (29,627 male, ( 26,773female), including in the districts of Banadir, Baidoa, Beletweyn, Bulo Burte, Jowhar, Balcad,Belet-hawa, Luuq and Marka. Beneficiaries are further disaggregated by their marginalization status, for instance, minority clan, elderly or people with disabilities, in addition to gender and age dissagregation.
SYPD will target vulnerable IDP and non-IDP population with provision of emergency shelter kits to 3,000 HHs both IDPs and Host communities and NFI support to 5,800 vulnerable IDPs and Host communities as well as provision of Transitional and permanent shelter to 200 HHs most vulnerable host communities and IDPs.
Further, to promote recovery and resilience, SYPD will train 400 youth artisans living in IDP settlements along riverine and agro-pastoral areas with vocational training, as long as the markets are supportive and functional, ensuring linkages with the private sector through mobile transfers and/or voucher systems. SYPD will also target IDPs and Host communities with transitional and permanent shelters, training youthful beneficiaries on camp management and maintenance of shelter.
SYPD will contribute to the protection objective aiming for at least one settlement hosting displaced people regarded as safe as measured by site safety audits.
Anchored on principals of exclusivity, through a gender lens, the intervention aims to address gender and age specific shelter needs of target beneficiaries. SYPD Will work closely with established community structures to ensure equitable distribution of humanitarian assistance provided under the activity.

Project Activities

NEEDS The scale of displacement contributes to high levels of acute humanitarian needs, particularly among IDP populations, due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, forced evictions, conflict and marginalization. According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), there is an estimated 2.6 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), marginalized communities and civilians in conflict-affected areas who are living in the most vulnerable circumstances, with limited or no access to quality basic services. IDPs are disproportionately at risk of gross abuses of human rights. This is exacerbated for vulnerable groups, such as women, unaccompanied children, large families with young children, the elderly, people with disabilities. Women and children alone constitute 70 to 80% of the IDP population with many IDPs from minority clans suffering pervasive discrimination without the opportunity to enjoy clan protection and connections. Sexual and gender-based violence is also widespread throughout Somalia. Further, displaced and socially marginalized groups require livelihood support to prevent a deterioration of their situation and to help protect their livelihoods, increasing their resilience against climatic shocks. Certainly, the shelter needs of about 2.3 million IDPs remains alarming, calling for urgent action to provide adequate shelter, NFIs, access to housing, property rights and durable solutions. Mitigation Measures

PREVENTING RISKS OF EXCLUSION BASED ON DISPLACEMENTInternally displaced persons in Somalia and particularly in Bay, Lower Shabelle, Middle Shabelle, banadir, and Hiraan are at the greatest risk of exclusion due to discrimination, absence of protection mechanisms, and legal frameworks such as housing and property rights.Male-dominated cultural norms and community structures disadvantage minority clans, youth, women and girls. To mitigate the risks of exclusion based on displacement, SYPD willincorporate inclusion considerations into project design by establishing andstrengthening community project committees. These committees must be representative of social groups in terms of age, gender, disability and clan diversity. Beneficiary registration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions of exclusion. SYPD will establish inclusive community communication mechanism by forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that all vulnerable groups have a voice in the project implementation. Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through established community mechanisms. Project implementation will mainstream capacity building on all dimensions of exclusion with proper hand over mechanisms that ensure mainstreaming is maintained throughout projectimplementation. SYPD will work with government authorities at the national and local level to strengthen protection frameworks, legal redress and secure land tenure and property rights for IDPs. Each household will be granted a secure occupancy of land for at least five years or as appropriate based on the prevailing circumstances. PREVENTING RISKS OF EXCLUSION BASED ON GENDER AND AGEGender and age discrimination is a major challenge to the attainment of durablesolutions and appropriate response for IDPs. Women, girls and youth and especiallythe poor or minority groups are often excluded in projects design and subsequently, projectoutcomes due to decision making processes which are often male dominated. Land and property rights are often within the realm of dominant clans and male domain, which locks out women and girls. SYPD will mainstream age and gender into the project design,implementation and monitoring. SYPD will form project implementation committeesand protection mechanisms such as age and gender balanced community projectcommittees that are balance in terms of age, gender and clan diversity. The beneficiaryregistration will ensure that needs analysis brings to the fore all dimensions ofexclusion. SYPD will also establish inclusive community communication mechanismby forming beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanisms to ensure that allvulnerable and especially women and girls have a voice in the project implementation.Feedback will be processed through trained staff and resolved through establishedcommunity mechanisms. MITIGATING THE IMPACT OF ARMED CONFLICT AND VIOLENCEArmed conflict remains the main drivers of displacement in most parts of Somalia.The correlation between conflict and poverty is now well documented globally. Due topoorly developed skills especially among IDPs, limited access to credit and capitalassets and lack of jobs, Somali youth, especially young women, face difficulties insecuring livelihoods. In a shattered economy, many youth and especially in IDPsettlements have no options but to remain idle or search for alternatives such asmigration or illegal activities. To mitigate the effects of armed conflict and violence inthe target locations SYPD will implement an assistance package complemented withcapacity building particularly in shelter construction, settlement layout and siteplanning as a way of creating self-reliance. Where appropriate, SYPD will use cash and voucher system to promote income generation, contributing to growth of the local economy.Mainstreaming protection in shelter and NFI distribution is an important element in this intervention.

Please provide further explanation

Resilience building to future shocks remains central in addressing underlying causes of vulnerability for internally displaced persons and vulnerable host communities. SYPD aspires to promote self-reliance and creating comprehensive solutions for forcibly displaced people, both refugees and IDPs, through active engagement of development actors, government authorities and other donors, for instance, SYPD is working with the Somalia Stability Fund and the World bank on broader stabilisation projects. This includes, protracted displacement environments in Hiraan, Lower Shabelle, Bay region and middle Shabelle. SYPD will work closely with community leaders, the relevant ministry at the local and national level, and local government authorities to find durable solutions including secure land tenure where feasible towards building permanent shelters. SYPD will also increase the capacity of youth to maintain and repair shelters, to ensure that distributed shelter are long lasting, and youth hav ean opportunity to contribute to their communities. SYPD will coordinate with the Shelter cluster and aim to better collaborate with other clusters towards an efficient response package that promotes resilience building.
