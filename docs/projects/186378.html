Project Summary

The Education in Emergency (EiE) intervention is aimed at increasing access to quality and inclusive basic education for crisis-affected girls and boys within a safe formal and non-formal learning environment targeting IDPs, Returnees, Host community children and children with disabilities within the ages of 5-16 years with activities focused on 1. Teacher and educator professional development, 2. Non-critical water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructure repairs and /or construction, 3. Non-critical school infrastructure repair or construction, 4. Back to School (B2S) Campaign 5. Direct delivery of education services and 6. Distribution of teaching and learning materials and other resources in Biu, and Damboa LGAs of Borno State

1 - Needs

Boko Haram — which translates literally to “Western education is forbidden” — has, since 2009, killed tens of thousands of people in Nigeria, and has displaced more than two million, denied millions of children and youths’ access to education; increased the number of internally displaced persons with dire need of shelter and has caused people to live in constant fear and anxiety. The insurgency has also led to lack of basics amenities such as food, health, shelter, education and protection. Boko Haram insurgency has affected greatly the educational sector as the sect has targeted school and colleges with students and teachers injured, killed or abducted and facilities destroyed. According to OCHA in 2019. Nigeria currently has 20 million out-of-school children, 13.2 million children out of this are victims of Boko Haram’s activities in north east Nigeria, making it the highest in the world. In north-eastern Nigeria, 2.8 million children are in need of education-in-emergencies support in three conflict-affected States (Borno, Yobe, Adamawa). In these States, at least 802 schools remain closed and 497 classrooms are listed as destroyed, with another 1,392 damaged but repairable. The aim of this education project is to support the government in achieving SDG 4 by 2030 through improved planning and by addressing some of the systemic barriers that hinder the implementation of an effective education strategy.IDS will promote 17,983 vulnerable children affected by humanitarian emergency access to quality and inclusive education in Biu, Hawul, Damboa and Nganzai LGAs of Borno State.

2 - Activities

1. Teacher and educator professional development, training, and capacity building; (a). By providing teachers and educators with training on EiE and pedagogy. (b). Provide teachers with professional development through registration, pre-qualification, and certification with the Teachers Registration Council of Nigeria (TRCN).2. Non-critical water, sanitation and hygiene infrastructure repairs and /or construction; (a). This will be achieved through procurement and distribution of water storage containers, water stands and drinking cups. (b). Mentoring of learners particularly girls on life skills especially on the production of Reusable Sanitary Pads (RSP) for menstrual hygiene management and income generation, ( c ). Provision COVID 19 Personal protective Equipment including facemasks to leaners and CVTs 3. Non-critical school infrastructure repair or construction; (a). This will be achieved through repair/renovation of damaged Temporary Learning Spaces, (b). Replacement of damaged seats for both teachers and learners. 4. Back to School Campaign, Awareness-raising activities, and related activities; (a). IDS will develop an advocacy strategy and plan based on context-specific needs (Back to school campaign, awareness-raising activities, and related activities) targeting key stakeholders such as LGAs, LEAs, SUBEB, SAME, Community Leaders, Women Groups, Religious Leaders which is aimed at increasing learners access to quality and inclusive education (EiE), increase in enrolment of learners to formal and informal schools in Biu and Damboa LGAs of Borno State.5. Direct delivery of education services and programming for children; (a). Recruitment of Community Volunteer Teachers to man activities at TLS, (b). Training of community Volunteer Teachers on pedagogy and PSS, WASH promotion, Community advocacy/awareness-raising activities (back to school campaign), ( c). Pre-enrolment and Enrolment of learners to TLS, (d). Engagement of Mentor teachers and Community Animators for mentoring and supervision, ( e). Development of scope and sequence to guide teachers for quality and inclusive teaching and learning, (f). Procurement and distribution Psychosocial support facilities, (h). Formation of CEC for resource mobilization, Monitoring TLS activities, and taking ownership and sustainability of the project. 6. Distribution of teaching and learning materials and other resources; (a). Through procurement and distribution of school bags, textbooks, and exercise books to learners so as to supply a concrete basis for conceptual thinking and reduce meaningless work responses for learners as it makes learning more permanent, (b). Through procurement and distribution of whiteboards, dusters, and markers for effective teaching and learning.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IDS will be responsible for the collection of feedback and reports directly to the Project Director/Focal person. Reports can be made through IDS GBV Helpline: 08172702242 or info@ids.org.ng, The phone number and email address that receives and responds to inquiries, complaints, feedback, and reports of safeguarding issues. This phone number and email address are managed by the trained staff to maintain confidentiality and promote the rights of the complainant. IDS has a complaint and feedback box in all areas of intervention where individuals, community members, and beneficiaries can anonymously drop their complaints, feedback, or report any safeguarding concerns. The complaint and feedback mechanism focal persons manage the boxes at the community level while the gender officer manages them at the organizational level, IDS protection manager, gender officer, and complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) focal persons in communities of intervention are trained to receive, handle and manage reported safeguarding cases, these staffs are also trained to recognize and respond to safeguarding issues. All reports are treated as soon as possible efficiently and in confidentiality. Reports are assessed by the protection manager then case management for the survivor (if need be), the investigation is launched, and the outcome of the investigation is used to make decisions and also used to inform the disciplinary action to be taken against perpetrators. Information on the investigation is shared on a need-to-know basis and with the consent of the complainant. The Email and complaint box mechanisms can be used for anonymity, the whistleblowing policy (procedures) protects people with serious concerns. Depending on the magnitude of the case IDS makes referrals for survivors to have Justice.
