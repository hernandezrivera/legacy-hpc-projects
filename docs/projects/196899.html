Project Summary

UNHCR will provide life-saving shelter and NFI assistance to the persons affected by conflict and natural disasters such as flooding across Sudan and the population affected by the fire incidents. Newly displaced population especially those affected by recent conflict and population affected by flooding and fire incidents will be prioritized. Where feasible, cash modality will be implemented especially for emergency shelter assistance. Through this project, UNHCR plans to respond to shelter and NFI needs of 1.66 million people through implementing partners. The total financial requirement of this project is $61.3 million. In Shelter/NFI cluster lead capacity, UNHCR will maintain a stock to respond mainly to the displacements in timely manner. UNHCR and implementing partners will inform and consult affected population at each stage of the project and will establish complaint and feedback mechanism.

1 - Needs

The key drivers of shelter and NFI needs in Sudan are conflict related internal displacements, refugee influxes, flooding and fire incidents. In addition, displaced population living in protracted situation also need periodical shelter and NFI assistance. The intensity of the new clashes and their indiscriminate nature is now causing widespread devastation to housing, household assets and public infrastructure, prompting many to leave their homes. Over a million are internally displaced and over 345,000 have crossed into neighboring countries. Those who are internally displaced require Shelter and NFI assistance urgently, as many, including children, women, elderly, and others with specific needs, find themselves crammed into overcrowded public buildings, or forced to sleep outdoors exposed to severe weather and threats. As a result of clashes and related displacements, the People in need (PIN) of shelter and NFI cluster is increased from 3 million to 5.7 million, while the target will see a more modest increase from 1.5 million to 1.9 million due to challenges with access, fuel, market functionality and banking services. Continued internal displacement due to intercommunal violence mainly over resources such as land have led to displacement of over 211,000 individuals in first nine months of 2022. In the previous months, hotspots for internal displacements have been Darfur states, South and West Kordofan states and Blue Nile state, however due to recent fighting, in additional to the Darfur states, River Nile, White Nile, Sennar, Jazirah and other states in the East have witnessed considerable influx which is expected to continue to increase. Flooding in 2022 affected 16 out of 18 states affecting about 349,000 individuals, destroying 24,860 houses and damaging 48,250 houses. In first nine months of 2022, 48,406 refugees and asylum seekers arrived in Sudan mostly from South Sudan. Shelter and NFI needs arising from key drivers mentioned earlier are expected to continue in 2023.Some three quarters of households live in shelters that are not adequate and do not provide protection from weather elements and privacy. Population faces a number of HLP issues including secondary occupation, property dispute, eviction and looting. Priority NFIs needs are plastic sheeting, solar lamps, jerrycans, kitchen sets and sleeping mats. Generally, population affected by conflict and fire incidents lose all their belongings and shelter requiring complete NFI kit and emergency shelter assistance whereas those affected by flooding require partial NFI assistance and shelter repair and shelter reconstruction assistance. Many of the settlements are not planned and lack physical infrastructure including roads, drainage, lighting and lack access to basic services such as sanitation facilities. Immediate provision of shelter assistance is critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, and safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve their health amid the upcoming rainy season. Provision of essential household items will be crucial for restoring basic daily activities. Items such as bedding and cooking utensils, solar lamps for safety and security after dark, and solar chargers for maintaining connectivity, as well as mosquito nets to combat the upcoming malaria season, are all vitally important.

2 - Activities/Outputs

UNHCR activities for 2023 will include provision of non-food items (full and partial kits), emergency shelter assistance which includes provision of emergency shelter kits, cash for emergency shelter, tents and construction materials. In addition, UNHCR will provide trainings on shelter construction, repair and maintenance. UNHCR will also conduct post-distribution monitoring through partners to improve the future programming and adjust based on feedback received. In addition, UNHCR in Shelter/NFI lead capacity with Co-chair with ADRA will pre-position NFIs in different warehouses to respond to the emergencies in timely manner. First) preparedness will be ensured by timely procurement and prepositioning of key NFI kits in strategic locations by UNHCR as the manager for the NFI Core Pipeline on behalf of the Shelter/NFI Sector in Sudan. This will support front line responders in hot spots receiving IDPs, and in newly accessed locations and communities affected by conflict, floods or fires. Second) UNHCR will also support beneficiaries in those areas chronically affected by floods to organize preparedness committees and do sensitization and awareness on disaster preparedness like techniques and guidelines to improve their settlements against fire outbakes or floods. This could also include trainings on shelter construction techniques that could support the communities to improve their shelters to reduce the risk of fire or include their water collection and build back better techniques.Third) UNHCR comprehensive response is to ensure timely emergency shelter and NFI assistance is provided to people affected by crisis especially the ongoing conflict or natural disasters. Full NFI kit each consisting of 3 blankets, 3 sleeping mats, 2 plastic sheets, 2 jerry cans, 2 mosquito nets, 1 kitchen set and 1 solar lamp. ESK consisting of wood poles, bamboo stick, Hazeer, Shargani (3.5x2m) for the roofing, Locally Produced Rope (Jamica), Wood Saw, Shovel, 1 Meter Rod, and Axe. Also, UNHCR will implement communal shelters to receive the refugees who are moving from the hot unsafe areas to UNHCR refugees camps located in safe states. In addition, UNHCR will deliver and install family tents in the camps that still have capacity to receive more refuges. A new refugee camp is planned to be established in the White Nile state. Emergency shelter and NFIs materials are purchased internationally and/or locally by UNHCR supply unit and delivered to the UNHCR warehouses in Khartoum and El Obeid (Central Warehouses), Kosti (supporting White Nile State), El Obeid (supporting South, North and West Kordofan), El Geneina (supporting north and central Darfur), El Fasher (supporting north Darfur), Nyala (supporting East and South Darfur), Kassala (supporting Kassala), Gedaref (supporting Gedaref) and Damazine (supporting Blue Nile) and onward to partners at the various distribution locations as and when required. UNHCR is going to open new warehouses in Madani and will increase the capacity of the warehouse in Port Sudan to improve the response to the displaced people due to the ongoing conflict. Given the limitation of resources, UNHCR will strengthen coordination of resources with partners to ensure coverage of emergency shelter & NFI needs. As sector lead UNCHR is flexible with the localities targeted to be able to reach everyone in need in case a new displacement of crises occurs and there is lack of resources or partners in the area. Also, UNHCR is presence in the main IDP camps and settlements that receive regular influx of displaced. While looking for shelter support IDPs shelter themselves in gathering points that usually are government facilities increasing the tension with the host community and affecting the functionality of the institutions like schools. In order to be able to support to government counterparts when these situations occur.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNHCR operational strategy for climate resilience and environmental sustainability 2022-25 at the global level has established outcomes to support climate resilience and environmental sustainability which UNHCR country operations have adapted. This includes, increase the use of recycled, recyclable or sustainably sourced contents for NFIs by 20%, reduction of use of plastic for packaging by 20% by 2025 etc. Further, UNHCR will integrate environmental considerations ensuring that the proposed project will have a positive and sustainable environmental impact by encouraging partners to work with beneficiaries on disaster risk activities such as awareness sessions, gender balance, environmental impacts, and safe construction of shelter and disposal of NFIs. The training sessions will also promote alternatives to firewood, such as fuel-efficient cooking and livelihood options to preserve environment. Furthermore, UNHCR will promote shelter solutions considering the geographical context, climate, cultural practice and habits of the communities serve, local availability of skills and access to adequate and locally available construction materials that ensure minimum impact in the local environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNHCR aims to strengthen protection, gender mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations as they link with the principle of “do no harm”. The particular needs of such individuals will be reflected in the assessments, programme design, implementation and monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, UNHCR will ensure the following elements are taken into account: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and post-distribution monitoring that will feed into the sector’s program cycle; and (4) sharing information, consultation, participation and community empowerment. UNHCR will strengthen the implementation of the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse through awareness-raising for both partners and suppliers. UNHCR within its capacity as Shelter/NFI Sector lead will advocate to draw attention to shelter issues such as housing, land and property rights, particularly for returnees and/or integrated IDPs. HLP due diligence tools will be set in place with cooperation of the HLP WG to harmonize the shelter responses among partners ensuring that before building durable shelter partners will ensure in cooperation with the communities that beneficiaries’ land if free of claim and refer those conflictive cases to protection partners providing ICLA support through a referral pathway. This is to reduce the risk of further harm and mainstream protection on the shelter construction programs.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

UNHCR will continue to improve PSEA across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader accountability to affected populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partner staff, and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to IDPs and returnees. The operation will continue to maintain its active participation in the inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network through which UNHCR will mainstream. A joint WFP-UNHCR call centre is functional with a tollfree helpline where affected population can reach out to provide feedback or to complain. UNHCR acts on received complaints and feedback as required.
