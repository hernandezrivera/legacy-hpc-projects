Project Summary

According to Education Sector 5Ws mapping published in September 2019, the number of adolescents and young adults currently engaged in learning center activities across all camps is still far below target’ Only about 16% of targeted among the age group 19 to 24, and about 19% of targeted among the age group 15 to 18 is engaged in learning centers. This indicates that there is a large gap for the access of education for the FDMNs in the age group of 12-24.
This programs aims at contributing towards achieving the Education Sector strategic objectives 1, 2 & 3. FIVDB generally focuses on ensuring quality education for all in a community and implement programs in a comprehensive way and in a participatory manner. Proposed geographical location of the project is camp 14 and camp 21. Though this proposal is for providing quality education for 600 FDMN girls and boys of the age group 16-24, our MHPSS and food security programs will be working for ensuring the enrollment of all school aged children in the existing learning centers in the camps. So there is an effort for inter sectorial linkage in the project. This initiative will established 12 community-based learning centers in the camps. Each learning center will be working in 2 shifts, 1 for girls (9 a.m. to 12 p.m.) and 1 for boys (12 p.m. to 3 p.m.). The program would be flexible to incorporate learner’s and guardian’s opinion about time, space and contents as well as the situation in the camps and natural disasters.
Program works for outputs below:-
1. At Least 600 Refugee Adolescence Boys and Girls aged 12-24 years’ old who have achieved their grade level competencies
2. At Least 600 of refugee girls and boys aged 12-24 years old having access to equitable learning opportunities, including life skills and resilience programs, in a safe, inclusive and protective environment
3. At least 24 of learning facilitators from refugee community trained in advanced/thematic education principles, including Disaster Risk Reduction
Activities are as follows:
1. Site selection; 2. Learning Centre establishment and operation; 3. Adaptation of curriculum
4. Beneficiary Selection; 5. Selection of Facilitators & Training. 6. Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning Documentation; 7. Regular reporting and sharing with the sector and other agencies.

Needs

According to Education Sector 5Ws mapping published in September 2019, the number of adolescents and young adults currently engaged in learning center activities across all camps is still far below target’ Only about 16% of targeted among the age group 19 to 24, and about 19% of targeted among the age group 15 to 18 is engaged in learning centers. This indicates that there is a large gap for the access of education for the FDMNs in the age group of 12-24. According to FIVDB MHPSS Program Baseline, only child enrollment in learning centers is very low. In camp 21, a total of 2,732 households have school aged children, 377 out of them are sending all their children to the learning centers, another 1,655 households send some of the children to the learning centers and 700 households send none of the children to the learning centers. The situation is similar in camp-14. Thus there is a need for awareness raising in the household level and working with them on the reasons behind not sending the children in the schools. Our MHPSS and Food Security programs are already working for ensuring enrollment of the young children in the learning centers. According to our observation there are adequate learning centers for the children in the camps, but almost none for the adolescents and adults. There is reportedly a great unmet need for trained teachers, especially females, in the Rohingya refugee communities of Camps 14 and 21, and constraints in resources and environment make the existing context not conducive for learning. Curriculum has only recently been standardized across the camps, and consequently there is suspected disparities in student outcomes. High levels of chronic, complex trauma exposure also impacts students’ ability to process and engage with material, and may also contribute to high dropout rates and reduced access to learning centers. All of these factors indicate a need for ongoing teacher training, development, and support.

Activities or outputs

Output1. At Least 600 Refugee Adolescent Boys and Girls aged 12-24 years’ old who have achieved their grade level competencies 2. At Least 600 of refugee girls and boys aged 12-24 years old having access to equitable learning opportunities, including life skills and resilience programs, in a safe, inclusive and protective environment3. At least 24 of learning facilitators from refugee community trained in advanced/thematic education principles, including Disaster Risk ReductionActivities:1. Site selection: Learning Centre sites will be selected following a process of community consultation and authorities advise. The education sector and FIVDB database would be used for preparing and implementing the monitoring frame work.2. Learning Centre establishment and operation: There will be a 7-member learning center management committee consisting members from the participants, guardians and community members. At least 3 members of the committee will be female. The learning centers will be well decorated and equipped with black board and print reach environment and furnished as much as possible according to the sector standards. The size of the students in a learning group would be 15 to 25 depending on the size of the classroom. The classroom will be community based , because the adolescent learners are not interested to go to the learning centers outside their blocks, and availability of space is a problem too. Our The teachers will be working in 2 shifts to manage teaching learning in the centers. Our community facilitators will provided training on teaching learning too and work as back up for the teachers. There will be close monitoring and supervision of the learning outcomes and regular assessment of the progress in learning.3. Adaptation of curriculum: FIVDB will implement the Cox's Bazar Education Sector curriculum and primers for Rohingya community ( English, Burmese and Math). The courses and the contents and teaching learning methodology will be adapted in a way that, the adolescent learners find it relevant for themselves. 4. Beneficiary Selection: FIVDB has a database for camp-14 and camp 21 comprising of the detailed information at household level. The information comprises household size, literacy, consumption, disability, and age and sex data. The beneficiary list will be finalized with the consultation of learning center management committee and approval of the CiCs. 5. Facilitator Selection & Training: Facilitators/Teachers will be recruited from the communities and will be trained by FIVDB education team with support from other sector partners. The teachers will be provided training on Disaster Risk Reduction, protection and inclusion and safeguarding of the participants will be a part of the planning of the learning center management and program monitoring.6. Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning DocumentationA monitoring framework will be developed in close consultation with the FIVDB Cox’s office. The monitoring and evaluation team to achieve performance indicators and theory-based evaluation. The program will also use formal surveys, classroom and impact evaluation to measure impacts and outcomes.7. Regular sharing with the sector through sector coordination mechanism and reporting in 5W templates.
