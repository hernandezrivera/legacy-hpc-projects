Project Summary

The main aim of this project is to reach 121,428 people out of cluster target of 238,830 with essential lifesaving health services in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror Counties of Jonglei State. The targeted population includes; Host community, IDPs, refugees, and recent returnees from Sudan. The project will scale up interventions to prevent and respond to disease outbreaks and other public health emergencies, integrate with nutrition, WASH, protection FSL and also strengthen community health services and engagement to bridge the gaps in accessing health care. This is in line with the 2024 Health Sector Response plan which accentuates three objectives i.e. to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases, Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.
The design of this project is anchored on improving access to integrated essential life-saving health care services through static, mobile and community health volunteers. The project will strengthen sub-national health delivery systems, quality improvements, timely detection of disease outbreaks, investigation and response in targeted Counties. High mortality rate from prevalent epidemic diseases will be addressed. In the volatile context of Jonglei, the project will target Sudan returnees, IDPs recently displaced by conflict and flooding, and host communities in areas with limited access to health services. Whereas the 2024 HC PIN is 60,241,74,525, 60,650 and 70,326 in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror respectively, this project targets 121,428 beneficiaries out of the HC target of 238,830 consisting of Host Community, IDPs and Returnees and disaggregated by County, age, gender and disability i.e. U5 =M10,011-F12,285, 6-17 yrs=M18,902 -F21,898, 18-60yrs.=M23,117-F24,776, >60yrs=M4,520 -F5919.
The flooding, conflict, IDPs, returnees, disease outbreaks, limited food and income sources all have food insecurity and health implications. In order to achieve the expected results, the project will prioritize services through both community and clinical consultations (OPD) for curative, preventive and promotive services. It will focus on MNCH as well as improvement of HF infrastructure, HMIS and capacity building of personnel. CMA will support county M&E to collect data from health facilities and BHWs and enter into DHIS2,IDSR,EWARS and 5Ws. Data will be analyzed and used for decision making. MNCH Services will include Health education; Immunization, ANC services, skilled delivery, postnatal care, IMNCI, HIV/PMTCT, BEMONC and referral. SGBV and MHPSS services to be provided will include Identification, counselling, GBV/CMR and proper referral for all including rape survivors.
A successful implementation of these activities would enhance access and quality health care to crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system significantly resulting in the overall decrease in morbidity and mortality arising from the epidemic prone diseases e.g. Measles, HEV, EVD. It will further increase access to services for SGBV survivors, PWDs and people with mental health disorders. Besides improving resilience among vulnerable populations, the activities are further expected to benefit from an integrated response involving key sectors such as WASH, nutrition, FSL and protection. In order to ensure AAP, a mechanism will be provided for affected populations to receive the information needed to make informed decisions on accessing health services, provide feedback on the interventions, share concerns and submit complaints.

1. Needs

According to current projections, South Sudan has one of the world's highest maternal mortality ratios (MMR) of over 1,223 per 100,000 live births (MMEIG, 2022). The MMR resulted in 3,790 maternal deaths in 2020, or roughly 10 deaths per day. The major direct causes of maternal mortality were postpartum hemorrhage (16%), obstructed/prolonged labor (13.3%), and antepartum hemorrhage (12.9%) (SS MoH, 2013). The weak health system at both facility and community levels is characterized by a lack of human resources and expertise, infrastructure, referral constraints, financial constraints, issues with supply availability and last-mile distribution and disease outbreaks. This health crisis is further compounded by the Sudan crisis leading to the arrival of 28,031 returnees in the targeted four counties all with high needs of integrated health services.The health care situation is worse in states such as Jonglei which have borne the brunt of conflicts and other natural crises. According to the Inter Sector Needs Assessment (ISNA) 2023, the main barriers to access and utilization of care services were long distance to health facility and lack of transport (50%), long waiting time for services (36%) and insecurity or security concerns (6%) (floods, and high cost of healthcare. These barriers have resulted in low access and utilization of health care services, especially for women and girls who face additional challenges. Only 56 per cent of the population can access health facilities within one hour’s time, while 66 per cent of the population has difficulty in accessing health services. The functionality and capacity of health facilities is also limited and inadequate. As of September 2023, only 12% of the health facilities have been reported as fully functional, offering the full package of BPHNS and open at the required hours. The quality of health care services is also compromised by long waiting hours, water scarcity, poor maternal health services, and lack of follow-up care. Women, children, elderly and persons with disabilities are particularly vulnerable and most frequently face limited access to healthcare. Malaria is the leading cause of death and illness, affecting half of the population. Measles vaccination is low with frequent outbreaks in Nyirol and Ayod whereas Cholera and Hepatitis E in Fangak county. In spite of the effects of conflicts in the targeted counties, access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) remains inadequate. WHO prevalence estimates of mental disorders in conflict settings of South Sudan, is approx. 2.5 million cases, emphasizing the risk of mental health burden in the affected population WHO (2019). A national prevalence study conducted in 2022 by The Ministry of Gender, Child, and Social Welfare (MoGCSW) and The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) revealed that gender-based violence is widespread and severe in South Sudan. According to this study, more than one-third of South Sudanese women suffer from physical violence, about 14 per cent of women face sexual violence, 30 per cent of women endure psychological violence, 30 per cent of women experience economic violence, and 30 per cent of women undergo forced marriage. These forms of violence have a negative impact on the mental health of women, as studies from post-conflict South Sudan report rates of depression as high as 50 per cent.A critical analysis of gaps in the health sector in the four counties, therefore, points to the dire need for the provision of essential lifesaving basic health services. Besides the need to put in place adequate prevention measures, there is urgent need for disease outbreak detection and timely response and enhancement of the referral pathways for MPSS, SGBV survivors as well as maternal and child health emergencies. The rise in SGBV has heightened the need for comprehensive care of victims of SGBV. Women, adolescent girls and children are most at risk and hence require targeted interventions.

2. Project activities

This project is aligned to 2024 Health Sector Response plan and the activities proposed are aimed at facilitating the achievement of the Strategic, specific and sectoral objectives. By implementing these activities, CMA seeks to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including MCH and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. It also seeks to reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases and enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms in Ayod, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror Counties. It is envisaged that scaling up of the proposed activities will ensure that disease outbreaks will be detected early, responded to in a timely manner and supported by adequate surveillance systems through IDSR and EWARS. In alignment with the HRP and health cluster objectives, the project has been planned around PHCC, PHCU, BHI and mobile team clinical packages with the following activities:1. Provision of equitable access to Health: This activity will be accomplished through recruitment and placement of adequate staff in key cadres namely midwives, nurses and clinical officers to provide key services. Capacity building for health care workers will be enhanced to ensure quality delivery of outpatient consultations, BeMONC and CeMONC in key health facilities, provision of MHPSS and CMR services in health facilities, Provision of rehabilitation services for the disabled. Capacity building of health personnel for providing key services such as CMR, MHPSS including Referral of the survivors to safe houses will be done in strict compliance with the standard treatment protocols from the MoH, WHO and any other recognized authority., 2.Infrastructure improvement: This will involve renovation/rehabilitation and equipping the health facilities to provide quality health services including OPD Consultation, BeMONC, CeMONC, PMTCT, CMR and MHPSS as well as initiate and sustain IPC measures in health facilities.3. Morbidity and mortality reduction: This will involve emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic diseases including Case management for selected diseases (Malaria, ARI, AWD, ABD, Measles) and weekly surveillance through IDSR including case-finding medical outreaches by active household surveillance and reporting of disease trends, outbreak prevention, investigations and response.4. EPI: Immunization will target vaccine preventable diseases utilizing static, outreach and mobile modalities. Supply will be maintained through procurement of outbreak response commodities and emergency antigens for immunization as well as repair and maintenance of the cold chain systems in the targeted Counties.5. Resilience and promotion of humanitarian-development linkages: Under this activity, CMA will provide technical and operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services and AAP jointly with the CHD, support targeted number of static integrated facilities, establish mobile response units and deployment of medical teams. Capacity of the CHDs will target the provision of support to HF, SMOH and CHD teams in designing and implementing State and County annual work plans, SMOH quarterly supervision and CHD monthly HF supervision. CMA will ensure integration of the health project with key sectors such as WASH, nutrition, FSL and protection in the targeted Counties and also conduct community consultative meetings within the community, promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace and development actors together to promote synergies and optimize use of available resources and establishment and maintenance of feedback mechanism at health facilities.
