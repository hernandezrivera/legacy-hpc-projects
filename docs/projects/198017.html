Project Summary

The project aims to provide access to quality, survivor-centered services to women and girls subject to violence in the BAY states. In order to meet the project objectives, focus will be in providing GBV case management and referrals to prevent and/or reduce the effects of violence, mass sensitization, capacity building, livelihood, economic empowerment program and Provision of dignity kits to women and girls of reproductive age.
The project is targeting 8,940 vulnerable individuals with the aim of reducing suffering and vulnerability to GBV, SGBV & SEA. Rehabilitation Empowerment and Better Health Initiative (REBHI) will identify and support 20 women and girl’s survivors of GBV with economic empowerment interventions to learn tailoring skills on making reusable sanitary pads and face masks which will be used as part of locally produced and assembled dignity kits to reach an additional 3,000 vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) state (priority should be given to people living with disability). REBHI would put in place flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in the BAY state who are prompt to flooding. Existing community structures will be strengthened, and new ones will be established to provide GBV sensitization, referral, tracking and reporting. The project will also establish two Women and Girls Friendly Space within healthcare facility in Adamawa and Yobe sate and renovate two existing Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Bama and Jere LGA of Borno state to provide GBV case management and MHPSS services. There would be Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) to support vulnerable women and girls across the project communities (however CVA risk assessment should be conducted also pilot testing should be done in some areas). There will be capacity building to 20 NNGOs/CSOs on need assessment and safety audit. Twenty (20) Health Workers would be train on Clinical Management of Rape (the training is aimed at strengthening the capacity of existing health workers from the selected health facilities to provide effective medical services to survivors of rape also support referrals from WGSS). Similarly, 20 Project Volunteers would be train on GBV core concept and risk mitigation to enable them conduct door-to-door sensitization on GBV/SGBV risk mitigation and response VAPP and CPL. In addition, REBHI would develop IEC materials for GBV awareness raising and commemoration of 16 days of activism. To ensure access to justice, the project will set up a legal unit that will be responsible for providing legal services/assistance across project communities.

1 - Needs

The violent conflict in North-East Nigeria have resulted in an estimated 7.7 million people requiring humanitarian and protection. As at May 2019, there were 2 million internally displaced persons and 80% of them were women and children. In 2019 alone, about 3 million people were estimated to be in need of protection from Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGV) in North-East Nigeria. (MDM August 2020).Likewise, the incidence of Gender Based Violence (GBV/ SGBV) is growing astronomically with the insurgency in the North East. Borno state particularly has been under frequent attacks by Boko Haram in the past decade, which has left several million people insecure, homeless and without means of livelihood. This ongoing conflict has led to forced displacement and migration making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV/ SGBV. Hence, the rate of Gender Based Violence (GBV/SGBV) and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) continues to increase coupled with lack of accessibility to basic dignity materials for women and girls. Incidents of SGBV such as sexual violence and physical assault are high yet under reported. More predominantly domestic violence cases are high and mostly it increases after food distribution. Some women reported constant conflict and violence between husband and wives because of lack of livelihood and husband forcefully selling food ration (cooking oil and bean) for alcohol and drug consumption. (UNFPA 2022). In the light of these emerging issues, REBHI conducted a rapid assessment to validate secondary literature on this overwhelming need. The rapid assessment sought to determine the availability and accessibility of basic dignity materials to women and girls as well as other GBV services in the target locations. Key findings from the assessment further validated the fact that there was a huge gap in the availability of dignity materials, and other GBV services for women and girls.

2 - Activities

Recent study (GBV/ASRH Need Assessment Report in Borno state 2022 by UNFPA) has shown that "incidents of SGBV such as sexual violence and physical assault are high yet under reported. More predominantly domestic violence cases are high and mostly it increases after food distribution". To bridge this gap, this project will train 20 GBV community volunteers on GBV core concept and risk mitigation to enable them conduct door-to-door sensitization on GBV/SGBV risk mitigation and response VAPP and CPL across project communities. In addition, REBHI would develop IEC materials for GBV awareness raising and commemoration of 16 days of activism against GBV in the BAY states.In order to meet the immediate, lifesaving dignity needs of women and girls of reproductive age across the project communities, REBHI will utilize its existing dignity kits production and assembly center and support 20 women and girls' survivors of GBV with economic empowerment interventions to learn tailoring skills on making reusable sanitary pads, himar and face masks which will be used as part of locally produced and assembled dignity kits, meanwhile other items will be procured and assemble (by the 20 survivors) at the production center. The project will reach 4000 vulnerable women and girls with dignity kits across the BAY states (The kits will be distributed using GBV SS established referral pathways). Since some areas in BAY state are prompt to flood every year as such, affected women and girls lack access to dignity kits (which happens to be their basic need) also increase the incidence of GBV in the society; therefore, REBHI would conduct need assessment which will inform the development of flood contingency plan for prevention and response of GBV in the BAY states. There should be prepositioning of supplies (dignity kits) in high-risk areas of flood. Since there are high incidence of SGBV/GBV in the target communities, REBHI would therefore establish two Women and Girls Friendly Space in healthcare facility in Adama and Yode state and renovate the two existing safe spaces in Bama and Jere LGAs of Borno state. Each Women and Girl Friendly Space will be managed by four (4) experience GBV workers (2 Caseworkers,1 Safe Space Facilitator and 1 Social worker) to provide effective case management and MHPSS services. In addition, project will provide Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA) to support vulnerable women and girls across project locations. In order to mitigate any risk which CVA may cause, CVA risk assessment will be first conducted also pilot testing should be done in some areas.Many local humanitarian actors in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) state, do not have capacity on safety audit and need assessment making their GBV programming less effective; to bridge this gap, this project would identify 20 NNGOs /CSOs and conduct three days capacity building on safety audit and need assessment.Five days capacity building training will be conducted to twenty (20) health workers on clinical management of rape (5 health workers from each project LGA). This training will strengthen their capacity to provide effective services to survivors of rape across project locations. The training will be conducted in collaboration with Health sector. To ensure access to justice, the project will set up a legal unit that will be responsible for providing legal services/assistance to expand access of marginalized people to equal and impartial justice across project communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

REBHI still remains well known for its rootedness and connectedness in the communities where it works. In the implementation of this project, REBHI will ensure that the core humanitarian principles as well as the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) which guides the conduct and approach of organizations and individuals involved in humanitarian response can use to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance they provide. For this response, REBHI has ensured to factor in key accountability actions and systems to ensure that communities and especially affected populations are continually consulted encourage active participation of the target beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and affected populations have been involved from the project design stage through community engagements and needs assessments. Information feeding into this proposal was derived from interviews and one on one interactions with sampled community members where feedback was documented and analyzed on project implementation gaps as well as needs assessment. Secondary data of needs and gaps from beneficiaries was derived from desk reviews of publications of GBV-Sector as well as other partners working on GBV programming on the field. During project implementation, advocacy visits will be paid to representatives and leaders of beneficiaries where they will make their inputs and recommendations on implementation strategies as well as spear head community level implementation to support impact and sustainability. Periodically, REBHI will analyze the programme accountability channels and structures developed together with the communities and seek ways to improve on the quality of work. The proposed project will ensure Complaint Response Mechanisms (Toll free line) which will be established early in the life cycle of the project to ensure that all complaints on the project are captured and addressed.
