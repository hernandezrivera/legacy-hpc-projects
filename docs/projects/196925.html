Project Summary

The project continues mainstreaming IPC and Health Waste Management (HWM) activities in several HCFs, with particular attention to the monitoring and follow-up of the systems, especially in those locations where HWZs were recently constructed and in continuation with previous Actions.
The WASH component aims to consolidate a safe system of healthcare waste management in 25 healthcare facilities in the Governorates of Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh. This will strengthen the Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities carried out in all targeted health facilities and as part of the Covid-19 response in NES. This action will also contribute to promoting safe and sustainable human settlement for Syrian IDPs and the local population in North-East Syria (SDG no. 11). Regarding the disposal of expired medicines, the project will support dedicated infrastructures providing clear SOPs, and monitor their application in all target facilities.
Specific Aim: To guarantee that WASH standards in line with international practices are respected and monitored inside supported HCFs to promote a do not harm approach and the prevention and control of communicable diseases, minimizing the same time environmental impact of health activities. Expected result. UPP/KRC system for the monitoring and implementation of comprehensive WASH standards inside the supported HCFs, including water quality and quantities, hygiene and IPC practices and the management of Medical Waste – including expired pharmaceuticals - is strengthened.

3 - Needs

The needs for Health Waste Management (HWM) remain high in all NES, especially in light of current rise in COVID-19 cases in NES that made IPC activities, even more crucial. In the framework of health interventions, a correct and safe HWM is needed to prevent the environmental and health risks related to improper medical waste management, and so to promote a do no harm approach. During the last three years, attention to HWM in NES has increased, with both local and international actors actively engaged in setting up health waste zones for the disposal of medical waste. However, important gaps remain in terms of geographical coverage and the quality of the segregation systems. In order to ensure correct segregation and disposal, the construction of health waste infrastructures is in fact not sufficient and continuous monitoring and follow-up of the system is crucial to ensure its functionality and to promote the long-term behavioural change needed. Regular provision of HWM equipment and segregation refresher training inside HCFs, as well as the prompt implementation of rehabilitation, works at the HWZs level, are also crucial to avoid system disruptions. Disposal of expired pharmaceuticals still remains a critical gap since i) no formal system is in place from local authorities; ii) no ad hoc disposal areas/infrastructures exist; iii) the specific technical skills needed to set up the system are not available locally.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activity 1 - Capacity building at central and HCFs levels to ensure that WASH standards, including water quality and supplies, HWM, hygiene and sanitation are regularly monitored and corrective actions are promptly taken when gaps are assessed. A tool for the monitoring of these standards was recently developed and partners' WASH teams’ members will be trained on their correct use, while WASH FPs for each facility will be appointed and trained.Activity 2. Supporting and monitoring the system of transportation and disposal of medical waste-In order to ensure a comprehensive and functioning system of HWM, it is fundamental to provide not only correct segregation inside the HCFs but also proper transportation and disposal of the segregated waste. The monitoring system will also be improved, and a kobo tool will be developed to make the system more efficient, the needed electronic tools will be distributed to each HWZ and the workers trained on the correct use of the tool. Activity 3: Disposal of expired pharmaceuticals according to the needs in the west area.The new project will continue this important work, setting up the same procedures in the western area (Raqqa) and supporting the disposal of expired pharmaceuticals there.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. health risks associated with unsafe disposal of health waste: health risk for the workers manipulating with waste – Mitigation: the NGO with the partner ensures the provision of PPE for the workers transporting the HW and operating the HWZ facility, and provide training and SOPs to the workers for safe waste handling and operation of HWZ. 2. health risk for the population living in proximity of open dumping sites, where the medical waste is disposed of if an HWZ is not available or the waste collection–disposal system is not well established. This poses a risk of injuries and infection, especially for extremely vulnerable groups of waste scavengers (often women and children). The mitigation is the core of the action - increasing the availability of safe HW disposal in various locations by establishing the system of safe HWM from the point of waste generation at HCFs to treatment/disposal of the HW, coordinating with health care providers, local authorities and other actors and by regular monitoring ensuring the sustainability of the mechanism. 3. health risk of environmental pollution by the disposal of hazardous waste, partners mitigate the risk by conducting feasibility assessment with consultations of local stakeholders, considering the distance from populated areas, water sources and agricultural land as key criteria, together with adaptation of the designs to eliminate the risks that could affect the public health and livelihoods.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Presence of Communicable Diseases (CD) including Covid19 in NES, inducing loss of humanitarian access, loss of part of the health workforce, evacuation or quarantine of UPP and impl. partner's non-medical staff; shortages of medical supplies; shortages of pharmaceuticals > priority mitigation measures/needs identified are: Strengthening all CD measures already in place with a particular focus on IPC. Improve coordination at the camps level to protect the vulnerable. - Financial instability in the region, especially in Lebanon, Iraq and Syria, impacting timely payment, allocation of resources on direct costs, shortages of medical commodities > monitor any change in financial regulations, maintaining regular contacts with financial authorities and banks personnel in order to minimize loss due to additional taxation and/or restriction to the use and transfer of foreign currencies - Turkish forces expand “Peace Spring” operational theatre eastward and westward to counter Kurdish presence in NES > maintaining access also unstable areas and negotiate access to accessible western territories with relevant authorities and military councils, revise project action plan and establish alternative ops procedures to restart service delivery as soon as possible in line with the Humanitarian principles. Move to remote management modalities where possible.
