Project Summary

According to Somalia HNO for 2022, it is estimated that 3 million school aged children – 45 per cent of which are girls – need sustained equitable access to quality education in a protective learning environment. The Education Sector Analysis 2021 highlighted the impact of emergencies on education as the enrolment gains made in recent years saw a setback. To retain children in school, there is need for mitigative activities such as provision of safe water, food and teacher incentives to respond to children affected by emerging shocks such as drought. Girls are mostly affected as they face socio-cultural norms which severely restrict their mobility; combined with safety concerns and supply-related challenges (such as a lack of trained female teachers or gender-segregated latrines), these barriers limit their access to education.
The proposed 12-month intervention will improve access to quality education, increase access to critical WASH inputs and enhance child protection mechanisms for 23,180 (12,430 boys & 10,750 girls) learners in 30 schools for IDP’s and host community learners in Galmudug State. The project will target 9,474 non IDP boys, 8,283 non IDP girls, 2,956 IDP boys and 2,467 IDP girls. Children with Special Needs will form 15% of the targeted leaners (3,477) with equal gender consideration. This will be through enrolment campaigns, provision of education supplies, construction and rehabilitation of emergency Temporary Learning Spaces, emergency take-away food rations for students, emergency teacher incentives, sanitary kits for adolescent girls. WASH activities will include provision of PVC storage tanks, water trucking, construction and rehabilitation of gender and disability friendly latrines and improving hygiene practices among children through engagement with community hygiene promoters (CHPs). The project is aligned with the Education-Child Protection Response Framework and the targeted child protection activities are child protection assessment in schools and community awareness on protection concerns, mapping and sharing of existing child protection service providers and their respective services to school administrators and communities, recruitment and training of child protection focal persons at each school on prevention, responding and reporting of child safeguarding concerns in schools, ensuring that all teachers in targeted school sign MoECHE code of conduct. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of use of the complaints/feedback and accountability mechanism and encouraged to report any form of harm and PSEA that could arise from implementing the project.. COVID-19 specific activities will include printing and distribution of MoECHE and UNICEF approved existing COVID-19 IEC materials to schools and airing of radio PSA on preventive messages approved by federal MoECHE through main radio stations in Galmudug. Also, the project will ensure that all WASH facilities constructed/rehabilitated are fixed with handwashing facilities and provided with clean and safe water for drinking and handwashing in all targeted schools. PSEA will be mainstreamed in training packages for teachers and CECs.. The project will enhance the school environments to be inclusive for children with disabilities by ensuring that all classrooms and WASH facilities constructed and rehabilitated have wide entrances, accessibility ramps and assistive handrails. 3,477 learners (1,738 girls) with special needs will be targeted.
CISP will provide training to CECs on their roles and responsibilities, food and water management, disaster risk reduction (DRR), preparation of school development plans, and child protection and safeguarding in the respective schools. CISP will coordinate enrolment drives through MoE and with CECs and religious leaders to encourage school enrolment with an emphasis on children affected by displacement with emphasis on enrollment and retention of girls and children with special needs

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will take deliberate actions to mitigate the risk of minorities not included in consultation or humanitarian assistance by creating community awareness through dialogue with local authorities, IDP camp managers, religious leaders, school administration and teachers, parents and students on inclusivity and equal access to the humanitarian assistance. The proposed project will ensure that individual and different needs of girls, boys and women are taken into consideration and addressed in the various components using a gender sensitive and child centred approaches. CISP has designed the project to ensure that services and aid delivered will assist all segments of the target population and will not put some at risk. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of t use of the complaints/feedback and accountability mechanisms the project will put in place and encouraged to report any form of harm and PSEA that could arise from implementing the project through safe reporting mechanisms at various levels. However, proactive efforts will be made to minimize the risks of exposure of beneficiaries to exclusion and exploitation during project implementation, through adequate training of project staff and other beneficiaries such as teachers, CECs on safe implementation. To ensure that the project has a positive impact, CISP plans to involve women, girls, boys, and men equally in the entire process of implementation, conduct regular monitoring and evaluation and all data on vulnerabilities and service delivery will be desegregated by gender, age, disability status among others, CISP will also leverage on the ongoing Protection Project with focus on GBV, PSEA and will tap on existing CISP staff expertise in strengthening local communities structures to have the knowledge and skills in preventing, responding and reporting and Child Protection and safeguarding Concerns, prevention of GBV and PSEA in their communities.
