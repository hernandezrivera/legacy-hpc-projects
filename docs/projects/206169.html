Project Summary

This response aims to support 96,420 (16070 h/h) people in vulnerable communities with humanitarian food assistance, Emergency livelihood support and resilience sustenance in Akobo, Nyirol and Pigi Counties of Jonglei with an objective of preventing famine, improve food consumption, dietary diversity and improve coping strategies for the target beneficiaries in IPC 5, IPC 4 and IPC 3 locations. On the other hand, the project intends to enhance emergency food production while contributing to the reduction and dependency on food and agricultural inputs. CIDO strategies will be married with a combination of interventions that alienates to the following cluster objectives; CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption through unconditional food distribution to returnees and refugees’ households CO2: Increased food production through in-kind support of farming inputs, fishing kits, livestock restock treatment and vaccination, CO3: Capacity Building on Good Agronomic Practices for both trainers and farmers, providing context-specific value chain inputs and training to increase food production, market interventions, fishery and farming value chains through an investment of post-harvest handling equipment, climate resistance agriculture, disaster risk reduction while considering resilience building at the household level.
In realization of limited resources vis-à-vis the need for impactful response, CIDO will ensure complementarity of interventions with its existing projects such as WASH, health, nutrition, shelter & NFI and protection from different partners within the targeted locations, while maintaining coordination and supplementing with the efforts of other humanitarian actors in the respective line ministries. In order to reach the target beneficiaries with the prioritized cluster objectives, CIDO seeks to raise a total of $511,990 for the 2024 project budget.

1. Needs

South Sudan continue to face challenging humanitarian context in which peace and security remain fragile. The Sudan crisis, Influx of returnees as a result of suspension of humanitarian aid in Gambella Refugee camps in Ethiopia, Intercommunal violence, food insecurity, and the severe impact of climate change present enormous challenges. Akobo, Pigi and Nyirol are among the counities in South Sudan with dire food insecurity situation being driven by the cumulative effects of climate shocks, particularly floods and dry spells, poor agriculture infrastructure, conflict and economic downturns. Their effect in agriculture production and productivity, disruption of livelihoods and hampered humanitarian access already puts the communities in limbo. This is exacerbated by a continued currency depreciation and the price inflation of food and other essential commodities, mainly due to the exacerbation of food prices as a result of COVID-19 and the war in Ukraine, Gaza and the recent crisis in Sudan which has affected markets and caused influx of refugees and returnees.According to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase analysis, severe food insecurity is affecting over half of the population, with 7.76 million people (almost 63 percent of the country's population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, including an estimated 43 000 people likely to face Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity is highest in Jonglei where 68% of the population are likely to face severe acute food insecurity with the highest levels of severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) at the peak of the lean season 2024. In the current analysis period of September to November 2023, an estimated 5.8 million people (46% of the population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.6 million people are facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In the lean season projection period of April to July 2024, an estimated 7.1 million people (56% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, during this period, an estimated 2.34 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Additionally, an estimated 79,000 people (0.6%) people are likely to face catastrophic (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity including Jonglei where CIDO has strong presence in the state. These main drivers have increased vulnerabilities and reduced access to essential goods and services in the proposed response locations, diminishing its most vulnerable people’s resilience over the years. Women and girls are increasingly at risk of gender-based violence, including sexual violence, while searching for food or an income to support themselves and their families. Intra-domestic tensions are also increasing, heightening the risk of intimate partner violence. Adolescent girls are facing increased risks of child marriage. Their exposure to these risks increases when communities face growing To address the above situation, CIDO proposes to intensify emergency food production interventions by distributing emergency livelihood kits, build the resilience of at-risk populations through support to animal health, a key component of food security, and establish VSLA groups, establish climate-sensitive agriculture technology to increase food production and incomes at the household level in the proposed project locations, in line with humanitarian needs driven by conflict, violence, climate shocks or food insecurity.

2. Project activities

CIDO will align implementation to FSL cluster objectives as follows: - Cluster Objective (CO1): Emergency Food Assistance: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. The response will also consider General food distribution; Cash and voucher distribution (Conditional and unconditional); Conditional food transfer (i.e., FFA) and GFD+; Humanitarian livelihood support; Focus on seasonal food assistance with other multi sector integrated actions (Protection, WASH, Nutrition & Health actors); Post distribution monitoring (PDM) Cluster Objective (CO2): Emergency livelihood support: This includes emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support. CIDO will implement the following activities: Emergency food production; Main season agriculture seeds and tool distribution; fish, vegetable and crop kits; Emergency food & livelihood support; Fishing kits/ vegetable kits; Livestock vaccination; disease prevention &treatment; Vaccination campaign support; Small ruminants restocking; CAHWs refresher training and vet kits support. Cluster Objective (CO3): Resilience building initiative: This includes activities that attempts to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb stressors: CIDO will implement the following activities: Organize seed fairs using locally sourced seed; Support market functionality and support value chain addition; Training &capacity building; Adaptive and absorptive capacities; On-farm production (farmer field schools, demonstration plots); Saving groups, Income generation (business skills), vocational training; Multisectoral models; Livelihoods, nutrition sensitive food security, hygiene, sanitation reducing pest and crop disease; Post-harvest losses management including introduction of appropriate technologies; Village saving & loan schemes & micro finance; Peace building Activities; Establish, train & support peace committees; Enable conflict related meetings to strengthen local peace structures. CIDO will use a result-based management approach to deliver project objectives. The project results will be monitored through a monitoring and evaluation plan which consist of Indicator Tracking Tables (ITT), Detailed Implementation Plan (DIP), and project tracker. This tracking will be done on regular bases (monthly, quarterly, and annually) with results measured and quantified. The following key indicators, among others, will be closely monitored: - # of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers - # of people receiving seeds and tools - # of people receiving fishing kits - # of people trained in improved agronomic practices - # of livestock vaccinated and treated - Proportion of people with improved dietary diversity - % increase in food consumption/income - Proportion of people with low Food Insecurity Experience Scale (FIES) During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes [PDM and Annual Outcome Monitoring (AOM)] will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked on a rolling basis
