Project Summary

The year 2020 was marked by a clear increase in the number of protection incidents affecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. In addition, significant flooding, as well as measures to mitigate the risks of the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic, have exacerbated pre-existing child protection problems such as GBV. Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existing difficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of opportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64% of the current displaced population are children.
This project will support formal and community-based CP systems, placing particular emphasis on (i) capacity building of adolescent girls and boys as agents of change for conflict and violence prevention, peacebuilding, and the promotion of intergenerational and interreligious dialogue, (ii) capacity building of deconcentrated CP structures in a humanitarian/development Nexus approach focusing on case management, referral, psychosocial support, and family/community reintegration of vulnerable children girls and boys and (iii) increased coordination at all levels, under the leadership of the Regional Delegation of Social Affairs (RDSA).
The key result of the project which is in line with the objectives of the protection sector, is that 132,000 conflict-affected children and caregivers are protected from violence, abuse and exploitation in armed conflict settings and live in a protective environment in the targeted localities of Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga, Mayo Kanyi and Mayo Danay Divisions in the Far-North Region of Cameroon.
The project builds on previously established dynamics and community structures and will provide PSS to 132,000 children and caregivers. PSS will be provided both through a one-on-one approach (through social workers or other trained child protection actors) and through community-based approaches, such as CFSs, adolescent clubs, and intergenerational dialogue.
Implementing partners will continue to identify unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). Based on previous trends and on partners capacities, the project plans to assist around 1,250 UASC including 60% of new cases, ensuring and providing those children with psychosocial support, alternative care arrangements, reunification with their families of origin and reinsertion in their communities. All will benefit from close individual follow-up.
To address both social norms but also to ensure smooth community reinsertion, the project will work at strengthening community mobilization and will organize community activities around prevention of GBViE including activities to abandon child marriage, to promote intergenerational dialogue and social cohesion. Based on the projections, a total of 70,000 persons (women, men, girls, boys) are planned to be reached through community mobilization activities. Partnership with religious and traditional leaders, and chiefery including female stakeholders will be an important channel to display messages and address complex issues. In complement, the work on youth empowerment and participation will enable to merge with the other members of the communities and promote prevention and protection of children from violence and exploitation.
The issue of conflict-affected children without a birth certificates was confirmed to still be a major issue.
The project aims at supporting 10,000 conflict-affected children in obtaining a birth certificate.
150 former CAAFAG are expected to be identified, will receive PSS, and will be supported UNICEF will continue to closely work with civil society implementing partners, providing them with capacity building, supporting the setting up of SOPs for case management and referral. UNICEF will also strengthen the coordination mechanism led by RDSA.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The year 2020 was marked by a clear increase in the number of protection incidents affecting children in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo Sava and Mayo Tsanaga in the Far North. In addition, significant flooding, as well as measures to mitigate the risks of the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic, have exacerbated pre-existing CP problems such as GBV. Significant population movements continue to be recorded adding to the pre-existing difficulties of access to basic social services for children as well as the lack of opportunities for adolescents and youth to develop their potential in a region where 64% of the current displaced population are children. Due to insecurity and population movements, many children have been forced to flee their villages, sometimes being witnesses or victims of violence; involving high level of stress and trauma and significant negative consequences for their cognitive and emotional development. Family separations due to sudden population displacements continue to occur and between January and September 2020, a total of 1,594 separated or unaccompanied children (UASC) were identified. Appropriate alternative care is not always available or only to a limited extent.Abductions as well as the use and recruitment of children by NSAGs are a major problem faced by children and young people in the Far North, especially girls aged 9 to 13 and boys aged 10 to 17 years. Between January and September 2020, 232 CAAFAG (including 120 girls and 112 boys) were identified. Former CAAFAG face real difficulties in reintegrating into their communities of origin despite intergenerational dialogue initiatives. Most young girls who managed to escape from NSAGs often come out pregnant, or even accompanied by children, as a result of SV they suffered. In addition, the absence of a formal handover protocol makes it complicated to ensure that some of the key international child rights standards be respected, most importantly the rapid transfer of CAAFAG to civilian authorities. Adolescent girls aged 10-17 are victims of sexual harassment and violence, as well as kidnapping and child marriage. They are a particularly vulnerable group because of the combined effect of their age and of gender-based discrimination. These various issues have been aggravated by COVID-19 prevention measures such as lock down and school closure, leading young girls to be confronted with less protective living environment. Main GBV incidents faced by girls and adolescent girls are 40% of rape cases, 21% of child marriage et 12% of physical assaults. Incidents affecting boys and adolescent boys include mainly psychological violence (43%), followed by rape, sexual assaults, physical abuse and denial of resources. Although there is a critical lack of data on children with disabilities, this category is at particular risk of violence, abuse and exploitation in the absence of adequate social services and community support. Children who do not have a birth certificate, including those who have lost them during their flights, are confronted with major difficulties in accessing education and sometimes even social services. Even though this problem affects girls and boys alike, girls suffer even more negatively because of social norms that are unfavorable to them. The absence of a birth certificate for a girl is critical as knowing the girl's age allows better protection and access to appropriate and adequate psychosocial and medical services. It exposes them to the practice of child marriage, which is an endemic practice in the region.Child protection actors are also concerned about the impact of improvised explosive devices on children. Between January and September 2020, 11 children were injured or killed as a result of handling improvised explosive devices and remnants of war, and 13 children were injured by grenades.
