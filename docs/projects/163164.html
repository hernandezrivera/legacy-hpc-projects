Project Summary

This project aims to improve on the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected in- school children (5000 learners, 2500 girls and 2500 boys, aged 3 to 17) and school dropped out (200 dropped out, 100 boys and 100 girls, aged 12 to 17) through life skills and vocational Education to build resilience for children and youth and provides them with stability, structure, and hope for the future. The Pan African Institute for Development West Africa (PAID-WA), Buea, seeks to achieve this through:
a) The Establishment of Support clubs for life skills in functional schools (30 schools, both Primary and Secondary) in Buea Subdivision for improvement on the Psychosocial wellbeing and welfare of school children managed by 60 school counsellors (1 male and 1 female per school) and 90 students volunteers (2 girls and 1 male per school) who will be trained by 2020;
b) Capacity building of 200 school dropped outs (100 boys and 100 girls, aged 12 to 17 years) in Vocational skills from crisis affected communities in Buea Subdivision, especially Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) for increased employability by 2020.

Besoins / Needs

The crisis in the South West and North West Region of Cameroon has left many children, aged 3 to 17 years out of school. In areas where schools are going on learners have limited access to quality education as a result of inadequate educational infrastructure and human resources due to the crisis. It is worth noting that the conflict that started in 2016 has forced about 4,400 schools (80%) in NW/SW to close (PAID-WA 2018). Majority of school-aged children have already been out of school for three full years, with very limited access and in most communities in the rural areas no access to non-formal education programmes, including remedial or accelerated education (PAID-WA 2019). In these crisis regions, learners are traumatized as a result of violence such as killing, maiming and threat to stay away from school and abuses (PAID-WA, May, 2019). Furthermore, support systems particularly, Families and social structures have also been disrupted in these regions. Psychosocial support systems in schools and communities are inadequate. With such many children and youth have limited opportunities to develop social and emotional life skills such as self-control, empathy, sympathy, negotiation, and joint problem solving, which are normally cultivated at the family level and further developed at schools. Consequently, school age children are Vulnerable to risk-taking behaviours and becoming members of armed groups which is a challenge in these region (PAID-WA,2019). Based on an assessment conduction by PAID-WA on May 2019, School aged children in the North west and South west crisis affected regions in Cameroon especially in the rural communities aged 12 to 17, lack life-skills in areas such as self-control, stress coping skills, problem solving challenges and reproductive health. They suffer from unwanted pregnancy, unsafe abortion and sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV, as well as smoking, drug and substance abuse (PAID-WA 2019.) Girls are, furthermore, at risk of sexual and gender-based violence, including early marriage that prevents them from accessing their right to education and livelihood opportunities. Furthermore, dropped out and internally displaced youth (aged 12 to 17 years) (as a result of the crises) in host communities especially in Buea Subdivision, South west region of Cameroon, have limited access to any form of vocational Education talk less of employability skills. (PAID-WA, May, 2019.) Children aged 3 to 17 years in the Rural areas of Buea Subdivision, are 1.8 times more likely to be out of school compared to children in urban areas. Of this, 55 percent are girls due to the fact that, female have particularly been vulnerable with reported abductions and rape incidents in the area. (PAID-WA, September 2019) Such poses the high possibility of these children indulging in risky behavior and picking up arms if the situation is not quickly addressed. Therefore there is the need to improve on the psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected in- school children and school dropped outs through life skills and enhance the employability skills of school dropped outs through vocational Education to build their resilience and provides them with stability, structure, and hope for the future in this region.
