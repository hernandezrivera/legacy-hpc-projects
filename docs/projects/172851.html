Project Summary

In the frame of the drafting of the proposal, MIGRACE conducted a assessments , by our team that already working there , the Municipality of Sebha and the project manager of Ministry of Education in addition to the WASH Sector in order to defined the priority WASH needs in the health facilities, schools and communities related to the prevention of the spread of the COVID-19 in the Mantika. The priority needs identified are the following: Improving the hygiene practices and sensitizing the population on COVID-19, as well as preventing and equipping them with required WASH infrastructures (according to the SPHERE standards) to reduce the spread of the infection,
The project is targeting the most vulnerable groups including children ,women and IDPs through the Output 1 and is dedicated to the 5 schools, and the rehabilitation of sanitation facilities and water quality and supply will respect the Sphere standards related to Sex segregated toilets; adapted to disabled persons when relevant, children-friendly and universal designed wash facility; Lighting and locks to assure safe access; etc. And the provision of cleaning materials and supplies for all targeted structures, which include (bleach, liquid soap, sterilizers etc .
in each targeted school Teachers, (Output 2). They will be made aware on the disinfection process, the maintenance of the WASH facilities and the hygiene promotion in order to pursue the activities after the intervention. In a same way, related to the prevention of the spread of COVID-19. The principle of Do no harm has been integrated to the logic of intervention and the indicators defined according to the SPHERE standards.
Output 3 People in Sebha, with focus on the most vulnerable population (IDPs, migrants, refugees, women, children and PWDs) are sensitized on Covid-19 prevention and equipped with family WASH and Gender kits (“dignity kits”) for the prevention and reduction of the infection .
By adopting a participatory approach in order not to create tensions within the identified beneficiary communities, the M&E staff will supervise the selection of the beneficiaries in coordination with the community leaders of the displaced, migrants and host communities, in addition to local organization working with PWD. This activity will target the most vulnerable groups who did not receive any assistance. Selected families will receive a Family Hygiene kit including a dignity kit for women / teenagers (12-49 years old) members of the family.
4 Hand washing stations with a 5 cubic meter tanks and 4 pedal operated taps to minimize hand contact will be installed in IDPs camps in the Mantika of Sebha.

1 - Needs

Libya is at great risk of the spread of COVID-19 due to the increasing levels of insecurity, political fragmentation and weak health system, especially in the south, where the number of cases has increased and there is difficulty in accessing aid, and also suffers from sanitation problems, lack of water and precautionary measures for the Corona virus, since 14 March 2020, the Government of National Accord declared a state of emergency in Libya. This included closing schools and universities, and limited public gatherings - indeed, in Sebha, school reopening has been delayed until mid-December. A special protocol must be established to ensure schools are opened in a safe manner.with major consequences in terms of girls’ and children with disabilities school attendance. Girls are particularly affected by inadequate WASH facilities, as the lack of hygiene and especially menstrual hygiene supplies can discourage girls from attending and staying in school. In general, lack of lighting, locks, privacy and sex-segregation increases the risk of GBV against women and girls when using public WASH facilities. The project is targeting Sebha where the higher number of COVID-19 cases haves been reported in the country. Considering the weak health system and the limited service provision capacities in South Libya, it could become a disaster, especially with the reopening of the schools. There is an urgent need to set an up an emergency response now to limit the spread of the COVID-19 in the South of the country.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

The project proposal was prepared taking into account the needs of the girls, women and boys in the target area in a distinct way. The activities, in fact, have been designed to meet specific needs related to gender, age and diversity. The rehabilitation of sanitation facilities will respect the Sphere standards related to Sex segregated toilets; children-friendly and universal designed wash facility; Lighting and locks to assure safe access; etc. in addition dignity kits for women will be distributed and sessions conducted. In defining the composition of the project staff, facilitators and community networks, and we intends to guarantee a significant and fair participation of both genders. Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection guidelines will be provided through awareness activities to MIGRACE staff by the Gender Manager. In addition, systems of GBV prevention and response (Codes of Conduct/PSEAH and CP (Child protection) protocols; referral pathways) will be established though the IPA process to ensure people at risks referred to agencies who provide services.
