Project Summary

Community Action Organization (CAO) is a national NGO working in the location of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap, offering humanitarian and recovery aid in the sectors of nutrition, WASH, protection, and livelihoods in partnership with WFP, UNICEF, FAO and UNDP.
Food insecurity stands out as one of the main issues affecting communities comprising mainly of IDPs, Host Communities, and Returnees. Integrated Phase Classifications for the first half of 2020 indicate a likelihood of a worsening food insecurity situation mainly affecting Jonglei and Upper Nile. In line with the Humanitarian Response Plan standards objectives of Physical & mental wellbeing, living standards, recovery & resilience, CAO targets a total of 72,000 persons through complementary livelihood support (cropping/ veg/ fish/ livestock) aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & some 3), enhancing emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 4 and 3) while reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (in IPC 4 and 3)
Out of the 72,000 targeted, 18,000 will be in Fangak, 21,600 in Pigi, 25,200 in Panyikang and 7,200 in Twic county, 45% of these IDPs, 15% returnees, 40% host communities 65% female, 5% elderly.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity persists in the locations of Fangak, Pigi (Jonglei), Panyikang (Upper Nile), and Twic County (Warrap State) despite humanitarian efforts to reduce it; with direct correlations to malnutrition, poor WASH standards, disease burdens, and protection issues. Former Jonglei State has the highest number of people estimated to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 1.25 million people, followed by former Upper Nile State with 845,000 people. With joint populations exceeding 427,399, (Fangak – 193,053, Pigi – 99,781, Panyikang – 64,879, and Twic - 262,546), more than 50% of these communities fall upper IPC 3 and 4, 70% in Pigi, 60% Fangak, 50% in Panyikang and 20% in Twic County. Between January and April 2020, 14 counties are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Former Jonglei State is expected to have the highest number of people estimated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity at 1.18 million, followed by former Upper Nile State, with 765,000 (55% of these female). Challenges in physical access due to insecurity, flooding and long distances continually hamper community efforts to gain stability. Majority of the populations in these locations are agro-pastoralists, and have been diversely affected by issues ranging from flooding, loss of livelihoods, livestock diseases such as (ECF, Anthrax, Foot, and Mouth) poor harvests due to crop diseases such as Fall Army Worm (FAW), post-harvest losses amongst other concerns. Most of the HHS according to the most recent FSNMS rely on markets and food assistance as their main source of food, with HH production minimal in Fangak, Pigi, Panyikang, and Twic County, despite agriculture, casual labor and livestock standing out as the main livelihood types. Economic challenges, lack of tools and inputs affect HH abilities to cope with shocks. Ownership of assets amongst female-headed HHs is low, with protection issues raised as one of the hindrances to access to livelihoods. An analysis of the Food Consumption Score (FCS) indicates gaps in dietary intakes amongst HHs, with low dietary diversification, coupled with grim figures on the household hunger scale. Poor WASH standards continue to contribute to high levels of malnutrition in Jonglei – 21.5% GAM; Upper Nile – 18.2% GAM and Warrap – 16% GAM leading to increasing incidences in child mortality. Food insecurity impacts education in these locations, with the majority of children staying out of school due to hunger and malnutrition coupled with the disease. CAO implements Nutrition, WASH, protection and livelihood projects in the locations of Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang, and Twic and has been instrumental in the response and enhancing coordination mechanisms in these locations. A Multi sectorial response is envisaged to provide a comprehensive humanitarian package while enabling communities to be resilient to future shocks.

2 - Activities

• Recruit and enroll 72,000 beneficiaries in Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang and Twic amongst vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities (male and female)• Open up farming spaces for 72,000 beneficiaries in Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang and Twic amongst vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities. • Distribute livelihood kits to 72,000 beneficiaries in Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang and Twic amongst vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities in partnership with FAO and UNDP. • Carry out emergency livestock responses in partnership with FAO in the locations of Pigi and Fangak, Panyikang and Twic County involving vaccinations, case identification, surveillance, treatment and training of CAHWs• Conduct seed fairs in locations with functional markets and apparent surplus produce to support the trading farmers and local economy while supplying locally adapted seeds to the beneficiaries.• Establish, rehabilitate and revive community-owned post-harvest handling facilities in the targeted states of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Warrap including setting up value addition mechanisms for products such as milk. • Rehabilitate and revive markets through Emergency Market Initiatives aimed at strengthening livelihoods, directly targeting IDPs and vulnerable host communities.• Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seeds; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets. • Training of farmers in basic agronomy skills, aimed at equipping them to adopt climate-sensitive agriculture, value chain addition, post-harvest handling, cross-cutting issues amongst others.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, CAO upholds stringent measures to ensure beneficiary rights are upheld, including the right to information, dignity, safety, and accountability. Standing agreements are in place with stakeholders to ensure the rights of the beneficiaries and upheld.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, IDPs form part of the direct and indirect beneficiaries targeted.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, CAO works with local community systems in the locations of Pigi, Fangak, Panyikang, and Twic. Inline ministries as well as local community structures have been instrumental in the success of our projects. These include village farmer field schools, community animal health workers and livestock committees, voluntary groups amongst others. CAO will build on existing structures rather than creating new ones and forging synergies with other sectors.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with national objectives and frameworks on emergency and resilience responses and is in line with the Republic of South Sudan Resilience Strategy, South Sudan operational strategy on food and nutrition, land tenure reforms for food security, and the humanitarian response plan.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe and stable. CAO implements protection interventions in the locations targeted and has taken into consideration the protection needs and environment of the locations. Standard IASC guidelines on protection will be enforced, including issues related to the safety of stakeholders, do no harm, accountability to affected populations, child protection and mitigations against GBV and PSEA. Security guidelines provided by leads and coordination forums will be adhered to while ensuring project activities go on unhindered.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project includes sustainability/handover through the existing community structures. CAO is a community-based organization and maintains presence in the locations targeted even at project closure. Handover is planned from the onset of the project, working with community structures and inline ministries to ensure the capacity of these is built to continue follow up of activities at project closure. Self-replenishing structures for inputs such as seeds, vaccines, inputs, and pesticides will be strengthened to ensure continuity and sustainability.
