Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect and enhance the resilience and
livelihoods of Gazan fishing communities through the provision of emergency inputs and
technical support. This project will target the fishery sector as a whole, as the fishery
sector is as a source of income for 60 000 people in Gaza and a supply of high-protein
food for the population. Due to multiple factors, including COVID-19-related closures, the
May 2021 Gaza escalations, and ongoing access restrictions, fishing households remain
one of the most food-insecure in Gaza. Over half of this group are food-insecure and
have become increasingly so, compared to other groups. In 2020 and 2021, and due to
the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, the fishery sector saw huge losses as small
harvesting business, retailers, exporters, owners of seafood restaurants, processors, input
suppliers, and transportation workers lost their jobs. The sector’s key institutions,
including the syndicate and cooperative, are lacking access to basic inputs, to the market
and to knowledge. This project is aligned with the HRP strategic objective SO3: The capacity
of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats,
is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are
pursued. Also, the project will contribute to achieve the FSS SO1 “the resilience of vulnerable
households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity
restored/enhanced” and FSS SO2 “Households suffering from limited access to food and
income opportunities can meet their basic food needs”, as it aims to rehabilitate and provide
productive assets and inputs for fishers, including promotion of technical capacities for
increased resilience.
The fishing communities will be supported through the provision of specialized fishing
materials that would allow fishers to reach deeper and wider fishing areas without being
endangered by Israeli restrictions. In doing so, this project will contribute to socio-
economic stabilization and will increase the resilience of fishing households and
communities through increased income and subsequent reduction in vulnerability and aid
dependency. The project will provide support to 400 vulnerable fishing households.
Targeted fishing households will be selected in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the fisheries syndicate based on proof of essential technical requirements such as having a fisher’s license, practicing fishing, owning a boat, and being an active member of the Fishers’ Syndicate. More than that, due to the lack of women participation in fishing sector, field days and other extension and technical trainings will be offered to female participants’ and will help ensure that women have access to expert knowledge and training sessions for the fisher’s wives. The training will pay particular attention to women with disabilities and any other vulnerabilities among women of the fishing communities. The training will focus on how to maintain fishing nets, how to preserve fish after the catch and other nutrition and food processing information.

1 - Needs

The fishery sector in the Gaza Strip is an underperforming sector that is operating at low capacity, as evidenced by stresses from both production and consumer sides. For example, fish consumption in the Gaza Strip is very low (3.5kg/person/year) compared to the average consumption of the five neighboring countries (Egypt, Israel, Cyprus, Turkey, Lebanon: approx. 15kg/person/year); as is economic access to fish for Gazan households. While on the production side, there is low catch/production output per year (approximately 3,650 MT of caught fish in 2019, though a slight increase from the 3,200 MT reported in 2017). Currently there are 3 951 registered fishers in Gaza. This translates into 18 000 people (who directly rely on fishing for their livelihood, including onshore support staff, moreover a total 110 000 people (including family members) who depend directly and indirectly on the sector of whom nearly 50 000 are women and 57 000 are children below 18 years. . In Gaza, there are 1 261 vessels registered at the Ministry of Transportation, of which 70 percent have valid fishing licenses. These vessels include the Trawler, Shanshula, and Hasaka (motorized), and the Felucca and Hasaka (mechanical). About 30 percent of the vessels are unable to operate due to one or more of the following reasons: the owners can’t afford the cost of fishing, they lack the capacity to repair and maintain their vessels, they can’t afford the licensing fees and/or the vessels are not compliant with specifications and standards. These vessels use simple technology that enable them to detect the presence of fish, their positioning and the port from which they depart. At night, ships find it challenging to return to the port because of the loss of signal due to interference in communications equipment caused by Israeli aircrafts. A limited number of vessels are able to operate within more than 12 nautical miles off the coast, but this comes with great effort. Annual fish consumption in Gaza is approximately 20 000 tons, which translates into 11 kg/person, a lower level when compared to the average consumption of the five neighboring countries (Egypt, Israel, Cyprus, Turkey, and Lebanon: approx. 15kg/person/year). Traditional fishing produces 26 percent of locally consumed fish, while in-land aquaculture produces three percent. The deficit is supported by fish imports. Both local consumption and external trade of fish— with West Bank (WB) and Israel—has experienced a steady increase since its start in 2013. The fishing industry in Gaza is one of the sectors most affected by Israeli military operations and the blockade which has been in place since 2007. Small-scale fishermen and their families are also some of the hardest-hit by food insecurity in Gaza. Over half of these fishermen suffer from food insecurity which has worsened since 2010 relative to other population groups. Since 2007, Israel has maintained a fluctuating fishing zone as part of its maritime “buffer zone” policy—i.e., the no-go military areas unilaterally imposed by Israel on Palestinian water. Over the years, Israel’s attacks, confiscation and destruction of boats and other fishing materials, Also, Israel's ban on the entry of some items which are needed for rehabilitation, and the dual-use restrictions is on the top of these reasons hampering fisheries support, and punitive restrictions against Palestinian fishers, have made fishing off Gaza’s coast a risk to life and safety and pushed the fishing community into extreme poverty. In late April 2021, the Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories announced the complete closure of Gaza’s fishing zone for 17 consecutive days as a result of the ongoing escalations. This collective punishment has directly impacted the livelihoods of an estimated 18 000 people and their families who belong to the fishing community in Gaza. The underperforming fishery sector coupled with worsening food insecurity in Gaza is posing additional stresses to an already fragile context. In total, Gaza accommodates most of the food-insecure households in Palestine, according to Department of Fisheries, 90 percent of fishers live below the poverty line. Vulnerability and food insecurity of fishers community particularly affects women, as they are the main caretakers of the house, not only they are affected by the financial hardships and the lack of income alternatives but also they face a phycological stress due to the great risk their husbands and children undertake while fishing due to the unclear demarcation of the fishing zones, the lack of advanced equipment’s to measure distances and locations and the consequent recurrent Israeli attacks on fishers due to claims of crossing fishing limits. Additionally, during their husband’s absence for fishing, women have to attend for children with disabilities and older people. This adds to female’s burden and work load. This project aims to respond to this existing problem through the provision of time-critical in-kind support to 400 vulnerable fishers, including young fishers, through the provision of tools such as echo sounders, GPS devices, drift nets and long fishnets. Notwithstanding these restrictions, the fisheries sector is mostly comprised of a poor workforce using traditional, artisanal methods with low labour productivity, providing minimal income. This was further exacerbated by the COVID-19-related closures. Economic forecasts by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) predict that losses in agriculture, forestry and fishing at USD 200 million, compared with a baseline scenario of no pandemic for 2020 (a 14.7 value addition percentage loss in the pandemic scenario for 2020 compared to the value of 2019 and a 17.6 value addition percentage loss compared to the pre-COVID-19 expectations for 2020. (PCBS, 2020)).

2 - Activities

The project will provide support to 400 vulnerable fishing households and will systematically consider Accountability to Affected Population in general and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) standards in particular as part of the project activities. Fishing communities will be informed about the project objectives, intended support and targeted group and selection criteria through information sheets that will be made available to all community members, including elderly, women and children. The information sheets will include information regarding FAO grievance mechanisms and channels of communications, including for submitting PSEA related complaints. Targeted fishing households will be selected in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and the fisheries syndicate based on proof of four main technical components: having a fisher’s license, practicing fishing, owning a boat and being an active member of the Fishers’ Syndicate. Prioritization of vulnerability factors including HHs’ characteristics, dependency ratio, income, assets, and other vulnerabilities will be further detailed in thepreparation phase. The activities will be opened for both female and male HHs, and when possible, female headed HHs will be prioritized in the selection. Output 1: Fishing households are supported with time-critical in-kind support to improve their fishing outcomes and reduce their risk exposure. Activity 1.1 Provision of time-critical in-kind equipment to 400 vulnerable fishers including echo sounders, GPS devices, drift nets, long fishnets, lighting systems, cork, strings, ropes, hooks, plastic boxes, and light signs. This will allow fishermen to better manage their fishing, identify fish shoals and avoid approaching areas where their lives or boats can be endangered by Israeli restrictions. Activity 1.2 Distribution of essential repair and maintenance materials of boats to serve 400 fishers, fishers’ cooperatives, and the Social Business of the Fisher’s Syndicate. The targeted boats are ones that have not been used for a while or need maintenance to better serve the purpose. FAO will engage in coordination efforts with the relevant authorities to allow access to materials. FAO is aware of the dual-use limitations list and will ensure the facilitation of delivering the support with no conflict. Output 2: Fishing household skills are enhanced to ensure best use of the in-kind support provided towards improved resilience and incomes, this is further explained throughout the activities description. Activity 2.1 Provision of tailored technical support to fisheries who belong to cooperatives and to the Social Business to ensure best use of the in-kind support provided towards greater resilience and improved income. The main focus of this activity will be on strengthening capacity and knowledge of fisheries who belong to cooperatives and to the Social Business.Provision of tailored technical support activity will support subsistence households with the development of practical experience in the subjects; first aid, marine environment protection, fish handling and packaging, mechanics, carpentry, ship-building, maintenance of marine engines other technical support will be coordinated with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). Activity 2.2 Provision of tailored technical and in-kind support to the vulnerable fishers’ Social Business to ensure efficient and effective operational management of their marine cage farm. At the inception phase of the project’s implementation, community mobilization workshops will be held and available communications networks will be used to raise the awareness of the targeted communities, especially those affected by PSEA-related issues. At the conclusion of the activities, a post-distribution monitoring survey will be implemented to seek feedback on the awareness and the responsiveness of the FAO grievance mechanism, including for PSEA-related complaints.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Since the majority of fishers live below the poverty line, the in-kind modality is the most preferable, as the fisheries pay high prices for nets, motors and maintenance of boat. Vessels with low efficiency may increase the operating cost of fishing by more than 30 percent. Additionally, the fisheries of Gaza have long-inherited experience in fishing, particularly in maintenance of nets and engines. However, their experience can be seen as outdated in boat building, carpentry and maintenance. Hence, service delivery and technical training contributes to 20 percent of this project’s implementation. Quality assurance is very essential when it comes to the provision of inputs and items, so the in-kind modality is the most appropriate modality to achieve and ensure the quality of provision of items and inputs, and to avoid the effects of items price fluctuations on the vulnerable beneficiaries.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The results of the grievance process will be used to facilitate the resolution of potential concerns of beneficiaries/stakeholders. Additionally, relevant lessons learned shall be derived for consideration in planning and designing of future projects and shared with all Food Security Sector stakeholders. A Grievance Redress Procedure based on FAO guidelines and taking into account best practices and affected communities preference has been established and is currently functional. It includes clearly defined responsibilities and follow up channels, starting with the Grievance Redress Focal Point, in charge of receiving feedback/complaints and handling them with utmost confidentiality, and the proceeding processing procedures, up until the final decision and resolution of the grievance or the referral of the grievance to FAO Office of Evaluation in case the subject of the grievance falls outside the responsibility of the local FAO office. FAO adopts Zero Tolerance policy on PSEA and is committed to the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referrals. FAO grievance redressal procedure includes several well- defined steps tied to specific timeframes, including registration, preliminary review for eligibility, verification process and resolution and closure. The mechanism will be publicized among targeted communities at the inception phase through the information sheets and in the context of beneficiary selection processes, post-distribution monitoring as well as in the context of partnerships agreements if and when relevant. Thus ensuring accessibility to all stakeholders including beneficiaries and partners. In general, feedback obtained through the mechanism are analyzed to produce lessons learning that feeds into program formulation and modalities/approaches of work to avoid reoccurrence.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable fishers whose livelihoods are under the threat. The selection of fishers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure, and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries at risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered livelihoods of vulnerable female and male fishing families in the Gaza Strip through securing the availability of time-critical indispensable inputs and services, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries rather than doing any harm. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the West Baka and Gaza Strip will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the West Bank and Gaza strip to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised. It is crucial to consider the protection mainstreaming as FAO is dedicated to ensuring that the provided services are accessible in a safe and dignified manner to all people regardless of vulnerability or personal characteristics. There will be special attention to consider the impact is maximized also for the potential beneficiaries of females and people disabilities. The selection SOPs that FAO use will consider scoring and verification for potential beneficiaries to ensure the support reaches the most needed population. The M&E team will have close coordination with the program team to ensure there is a well information dissemination about the Grievance response mechanisms to ensure accountability to affected people during the project implementation. FAO team will focus on ensuring protection and safety measures are taken to protect the targeted beneficiaries and implementing partners. FAO coordinates directly with MOA and its technical field staff for the verification of information and reaching the most affected population across the Strip.
