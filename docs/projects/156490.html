Project Summary

This project is aligned with Protection Objective 1: The protection of population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions, and through advocacy with duty bearers- relates to SO1, SO2 and SO3. Protection Objective 4: Reduce the impact of explosive hazards – relates to SO2. And Protection Objective 5: Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action - relates to SO 1 and 2
Project contributes to prevent and response Child Protection (CP) concerns through implementing Community based child protection activities as the following:
-Structured psycho-social support in established CFSs.
-Awareness raising on CP, FTR, CAAFAG.
-Prevent and response to separated and unaccompanied children.
-Case management
The first specific objective of the program is to strengthen community-based child protection services. The main activities that will be conducted include establishing child friendly spaces to deliver recreational, structured PSS assistance and support mobile outreach teams that will deliver recreational PSS services. Increase awareness on child protection issues such as (CP, FTR, CAAFAG) and offering Case management response to target, especially the vulnerable cases such as early marriage, UASCs, child labour and CAFFAG cases. (COOW) will include a restricted and conditional cash support component, specifically for CAAFAG, CL, and Children of female-headed household. A TOR will be developed and referral mechanism agreed based on clear criteria to provide cash intervention and economic alternatives in support of high-risk cases. A core team for Combined Cash Support will work closely with the Case Management team to deliver the service.
COOW will determining “vulnerability” and “vulnerable groups” typically according to analysis of multiple dimensions based on age, gender, diversity, specific needs, displacement status, and other characteristics.
The second specific objective of this project to strengthen the capacity for humanitarian worker, duty bearers and community member to respond to child protection concerns. As the staff and CPN member will be trained on CPMS, CPIE, Communication Skills, PFA, PSS, first Aid and case management (Children of One World) will focus on building the capacity of all staff to be first responder to CP concern in targeted areas.

Needs

As per the Draft Humanitarian Need Overview HNO chapter (2019) V.3, Coping mechanisms of individuals and communities continue to be increasingly affected by the protracted crisis and its far-reaching impact on the social fabric. This has a particular impact on children, with a high occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance and early marriage, both identified as coping mechanisms (in 80% and in 45% of assessed communities respectively) . Qualitative sources indicate that these negative coping mechanisms are major sources of concern for children. This in turn signals how more efforts are needed by humanitarian actors and responsible authorities to deliver humanitarian assistance in an inclusive way. In relation to Child Protection, Draft HNO 2019 affirms that, throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern. During the first 9 months of 2018, the UN verified 2,354 such violations against children. The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being – weakening their sense of security, purpose, control and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. Many are without civil documentation to prove their identity, access basic services and safeguard their rights. It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect. Events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations. Respondents in those assessed communities where discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance emerged identified unaccompanied girls as the most likely group to face such discrimination.The conflict and its impact on children and families have fueled negative coping mechanisms such as child labour, early marriage and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk, while straining the capacities of families and communities to protect children. Child labour is reported to occur widely in all 14 governorates of Syria. Child labour interferes with children’s schooling by depriving them of the opportunity to attend school, obliging them to leave school prematurely, or requiring them to attempt to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work. Child marriage is reported in 12 out of 14 governorates mainly affecting adolescent girls, though adolescent boys are also at risk. Girls and boys are married early as a household negative coping strategy to “mitigate” protection risks and to respond to the worsening economic situation of families. Recruitment of children, particularly adolescent boys, by all parties to the conflict continues to be reported in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria, and the use of children in the conflict has increasingly normalized. In the first nine months of 2018, the Syria MRM verified 631 incidents of recruitment and use of children (80% boys), with 92% used in combat roles, and around a quarter under the age of 15.

Implementation of activities

Activity 1.1. (Provision of quality and integrated protection services ...)Activity 1.3. (Advocacy with duty bearers and key stakeholders to inform and enhance the response to protection risks).COOW will provide structured PSS activities in CFSs using the Palestinian model for psychosocial support programs and we will adopt (little fellow) curriculum for ages 6-10. In the centres (g/b), including adolescents (ages 6-18) will participate in different kinds of activities to develop their personal and social skills such as cultural, life skills, recreation, and writing. Two shifts will be operated daily to make sure more children can access to 15 CFS - 5 days/week, every cycle lasts for two weeks (25 child x 2 shifts per month x 15 CFSs x 12 months = 9,000). Caregivers and parents (m/f) will be given parenting sessions to help them understand, prevent and respond to protection risks for children. Case management services will be included in each CFS. COOW will deploy 15 mobile teams (3 staff m/f) in 15 locations to deliver semi-structured psychosocial support interventions to children and caregivers who cannot access services in the CFS. One caseworker will be part of each team to identify and refer children that need specialized support. All children and caregivers will also get general CP awareness on prevention of family separation, child rights. Activity 5.1. (Community-based child protection ...)COOW will establish 15 Child Protection Networks in proposed locations where the CFSs will be established. Each CP network consists of 5 (m/f) members in 15 different locations. COOW will identify potential child protection enthusiasts who will be approached and invited to become members in a child-protection community committee that COOW will help establish. Existing CP actors will also be invited to join the committee that will include also members from the LCs, NGOs, religious leaders, and youth activists. COOW will train CPN members on CPMS, CPIE, PFA and community mobilizing.CPNs will also play a major role in Child Protection Monitoring programs, which would be coordinated at organization, cluster and/or whole of Syria level. Activity 5.2. (Specialized child protection services -case management-...)COOW will work with CPNs to identify entry points in accessing survivors or at risk children of CL, CAAFAG and UASC within the communities. Follow-up support will be provided to all reunified cases on a monthly basis. The CPNs will support with follow up visits, awareness raising on child rights and available services, referral of cases and advocacy at community level with local authorities. COOW will engage with the process of helping individual children and families through direct social-work type support and managing information well. Case management will facilitate case monitoring and referral to services, and therefore comprise a core component of integrated support, Combined Cash Support, in response to key child protection risks, including CAAFG, UASC and child survivors of violence, abuse and exploitation. COOW will consider the appropriate involvement of children throughout the case management process, as well as full consideration of the best interests of the child. This requires safe reporting systems, guaranteed confidentiality, clear and respected information sharing protocols, safe storage of records, etc. 5.3. (Strengthen human resource capacity to respond to CP concerns...)COOW will conduct a thematic training courses for 188 employed staff (m/f) on the following topics: PSS, CPiE, CPMS, Child Rights mainstreaming, and PFA. In addition to 90 members (m/f) of CPN. COOW will use a variety of tools to monitor the effectiveness of our proposed interventions ,In accordance with COOW's MEAL policy and procedures and IASC AAP standards, including surveys, observation, document review, Structured interviews and FGDs. COOW Complaint Mechanism will be applied in 15 proposed locations.
