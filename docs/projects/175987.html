Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (ACF) has been operational in what is now South Sudan since 1985, responding to both chronic and acute humanitarian needs. Our strategy addresses the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in children U5 by linking nutrition with WASH and FSL interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon communities’ resilience as well as prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Currently ACF is implementing program activities in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap and Jonglei states and COVID-19 response in Juba with country office in Juba, and established field offices in Malualkon, Alek, Old Fangak and Paguir Payams. In 2021, WASH projects will be implemented to ensure that host communities, IDPs and returnees have safe access to sufficient water and sanitation facilities with improved hygiene behavior. Proposed activities will improve the nutritional status of children U5, pregnant and lactating women through an integrated WASH and Nutrition response aimed at breaking the diarrhea – malnutrition vicious cycle. This project will ensure the effectiveness of addressing malnutrition in the proposed target area by reducing disease burden in communities as well as availing safe, potable water for use in appetite tests at nutrition sites. To achieve the overall project objective and WASH cluster Sectoral objectives of the HRP, ACF will provide critical services to 87,599 individuals, including 70,079 at the village level and 17,520 (non-double counted) beneficiaries in targeted Nutrition Sites and SCs. All work will be done per WaSH Cluster strategy in close coordination with line ministries’ guidelines for implementing WaSH during the COVID-19 Pandemic. The project has 6 Main objectives;

1 - Needs

Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. Only 36% of households reported having access to an improved water source in less than 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns. The remaining 64% of people across the country either rely on unimproved or surface water sources (41%), take more than 30 minutes to reach an improved water source (19%), or are able to reach an improved source in less than 30 minutes but face protection concerns while accessing the water (4%). The highest proportion of households relying on surface water were found in Upper Nile (66%), followed by Jonglei (43%) and Central Equatoria (41%). The quantity of water available per day is also below standards in many locations, with 17% of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% of people reporting insufficient access to water for handwashing. Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17% of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2% have access to a communal/institutional latrine. This means that 77% of people do not have access to a latrine. In 34 counties, zero to 10 per cent of households reported using latrines. At the country level, open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population. Regional access to sanitation varied greatly, from 75% in Western Bahr el Ghazal to 8% in Warrap, 9% in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and 10% in Jonglei. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing locations, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases.According to December 2020 IPC GAM results released, ACF targeted counties present above WHO threshold of 15% as below; Tonj East 15.60%, Pigi/Canal 24.55%, Aweil south 15.20%, Fangak 20.27%, Aweil East 21.37%, Gogrial west 20.10% and Gogrial East 16.55%. In the WASH cluster severity mapping, all the 7 counties are among the top 10 in different categories from lowest county accessing sufficient safe water, highest per cent of people reporting GBV as result of access to safe water, lowest per cent in county in access to sanitation facilities, lowest per cent availability of handwashing facilities, high per cent of people reporting water borne diseases. Canal/Pigi and Tonj East (100% and 94.4%) counties reporting insufficient access to safe water within 30 minutes in an addition to 43.5% protection concerns recorded in Tonj East, access to sanitation facilities especially in Aweil South, Gogrial East and Fangak remains a big concern at 0.93% and 3.67% respectively. While across South Sudan, 14% of the Households (HHs) reported access to sufficient water for handwashing and soap, in Canal/Pigi and Fangak (0% to 1.8%) less than 2% of the HHs is able to access sufficient water for hand washing with soap.One of the key drivers of malnutrition is WaSH-related diseases due to poor hygiene and sanitation practices, and limited access to clean water. 7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Action Against Hunger trains all of its staff and volunteers on the organization’s PSEA Policy. During our mobilization meetings, Action Against Hunger shares information about PSEA from the organization’s PSEA Policy. During project implementation, the organization regularly shares PSEA information with beneficiaries as reminder/refresher to emphasize the premium the organizations places Zero Tolerance for PSEA.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Safety Audits will serve as an additional Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM) modality in our WaSH programs and other sectors. All focus group discussions occurring as part of Safety Audits will ensure provision of an enumerator who is the same sex as the participants. As part of the MEAL system surrounding Men Group Discussions, participants will be specifically asked to share their concerns with the program and suggest improvements. In each of our project sites, we avail five FCM modalities as “minimum standards”: 1. Monthly staff meetings with first-line implementers, 2. Focus Group Discussions with Direct beneficiaries, 3. Individual interview form, 4. Comment Box at Field Office Gate and nutrition facilities, 5. Anonymous Hotline Number. The identities of all feedback and complaints mechanism (FCM) complainants will be kept strictly confidential by program management staff when provided. We have standard, context appropriate FCM IEC materials (posters, stickers, flyers), which are displayed at all project sites and on all project structures (e.g. equipment used by beneficiaries). IEC materials include images and text in both English and the local dialect to ensure comprehension by a maximum number of individuals. During all community meetings, trainings, and sensitization sessions, a standard script will be used by staff to inform beneficiaries on available FCM modalities. Depending on the urgency, feedback will be delivered to the first line program implementers during the monthly meetings. Specific responses will also be discussed bi-monthly at the beginning of focus group discussions at the site level to inform beneficiaries of what changes are being made given previous feedback, or if any concerns are being addressed at the national level. Urgent issues will be addressed immediately by the Head of Base and Program Managers through appropriate channels.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Action Against Hunger implements a comprehensive, gender-sensitive feedback mechanism. This feedback mechanism uses a range of modalities to solicit feedback from frontline program implementers (including volunteers and field staff), local communities in which programs are operating, and direct program beneficiaries. While the feedback mechanism is firmly institutionalized, the organization continues to examine how to best track and respond to the feedback received, and how to integrate this process into its M&E strategy

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Action Against Hunger considers the feedback from beneficiaries as an instrument to improve the organization’s acceptance, and improvement of our interventions in the community. Hence, all feedback from beneficiaries are analyzed and used to improve the relevant intervention/project as we place emphasis on the organization being a learning entity.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To prevent instigation of SEA and other forms of GBV by leading gender-targeted interventions, Action Against Hunger will aim to sensitize all gender and age groups on project activities before their implementation. All our project activities are designed to safely challenge harmful gender dynamics in a context-appropriate manner. Results of gender analyses will directly inform project design and implementation strategy. All staff implementing GBV referral pathway awareness, Men Group Discussion facilitation, Psychological First Aid, and any other sensitive discussion with target community members will be instructed on our mandatory policy for maintaining the identity of project participants strictly confidential. Data related to GBV and other sensitive topics will be anonymized in organizational databases to preserve the privacy of survivors. Moreover, survivors or reporters will not be solicited for further participation/discussion. Any GBV case concerning staff or beneficiaries which proceed to investigation will only involve the Country Program Ethics Committee, consisting of the Country Director, Head of Logistics and Head of Operations. All information will be confidential with access restricted to those personnel. We will train key stakeholders on the use of the feedback mechanism at each project location. Additionally, the organization will print complaint procedures in the local language and in simple English. The project team will regularly review the use of the feedback mechanism and information gathered from the review process will be used to make improvements. A diverse set of FCM modalities ensures that differing gender, age and ability groups have access.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The household safe, hygienic environment and safely prepared food with clean water, sanitation and healthy daily practices in terms of hand-washing may significantly prevent malnutrition in the family. In the case of domestic food production, storage and sales on the local market, access to water, soap and the proper hygienic practices are crucial for safety of the food.Moreover, if the safe water access is ensured, women can limit the time used for collectiong water and take care of livelihood activities instead.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Action Against Hunger has a policy on fair provision of cross-sectoral/integrated services to all sections of the affected population including women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities and special needs. The program will follow this policy and all sections of the affected population will be served according to their needs. The provision of services will be based on the organization’s needs/vulnerability analysis.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The overall WASH project is aimed at supporting nutrition facilities with WASH NFI kits and hygiene promotion to have a direct impact in the number of SAM children and provide access to safe water to nutrition facilities especially in IPC 4/5 locations, this project aims to provide sustained WASH services through range of activities as described in the WASH cluster sectoral objective 1.1.2. This will directly decrease the MAM case in the targeted locations of IPC 3 and 4 especially in U5 children.
