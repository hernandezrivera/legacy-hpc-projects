Project Summary

From 15 April 2023, armed clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan, IOM report estimates that 6.1 Million individuals (242,666 households) taking refuge inside and outside the country, as a result of the conflict. In Blue Nile, the security situation remains stable but unpredictable, in terms of displacement in BN, IOM/DTM estimates 118,093 displaced people (23,643 households) arrived in six localities since the start of the conflict, about 66% of displaced people are staying with the host community, 31% are in rented accommodations, and 3% are in open areas/informal settlements, added to that Blue Nile is one of the regions with protracted IDPs and returnees who didn’t adequately re-establish after the displacement because of the long-lasting conflict in the area, the proposed project is an integrated emergency project that will cover thematic areas of FSL and Health In order to provide timely multi cluster life-saving assistance to crises affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and to mitigate protection risk and respond to protection and PSEA needs through the humanitarian action for the newly vulnerable displaced, new returnees, vulnerable residents and vulnerable protracted displaced in Blue Nile state for 85,700 person (26,682 women, 24,581 men, 17,826 girl and 16,454 boy), from Damazine, Kurmuk, Rosaries, Wad Almahi, Bau and Geissan localities in Blue Nile state. The project is aimed at providing the affected vulnerable communities with life-saving agriculture and livelihoods support to ensure well-being and also to prevent further erosion of their coping abilities , improved food security at the HH level for 67,989 person (21,213 women, 19,581 men, 14,142 girl and 13,054 boy), Support essential public health functions and strengthen emergency preparedness, response and emergency risk management plus Support the implementation of resilience solutions to reduce the drivers of the needs for 44, 000 ((24,200 female, 19,800 men) 16,600 out of them are children . The project has been designed to respond to the different needs of groups at the activities, outputs, and outcomes levels. A significant proportion (52%) of the proposed beneficiaries are women. Girls and boys will be mobilized to benefit from hygiene sessions to reduce water borne diseases. Livelihood interventions will also ensure women participation and representation in community structures and capacity building opportunities. Some activities will directly target women and Female Headed Households in particular to enhance their self-reliance and empower them for sustainable livelihood. Nutrition education and awareness together with introduction of vegetables cultivation and fruits planting in the targeted villages will significantly improve the HHs diet with nutritious food items those are essential for children, pregnant women and lactating mothers’ health and wellbeing. The proposed project will complement and link to existing IRW projects in the proposed project sites. It will provide smallholder farmers with seeds and tools. Thus, the project aims to improve vulnerable men, women, boys’ and girls' access to basic services, while also building resilience and sustainability. IRW will coordinate project activities with other partners and respective government departments in the project sites, including UN agencies like UNICEF, WFP, UNOCHA, WHO and UNHCR. The implementation strategy will be direct implementation, while IRW will also engage implementation partners in community mobilization and engagement.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project has been designed to respond to the different needs of groups at the activities, outputs, and outcomes levels. The project implementation team have vast experience on gender sensitive implementation and will practically implement project activities in a manner calculated to advance gender equality. The implementation teams will include both men and women. A significant proportion (52%) of the proposed beneficiaries are women. Girls and boys will be mobilized to benefit from hygiene sessions to reduce water borne diseases. Education, FSL and WASH Facilities will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks and threats to beneficiaries and in particular women and girls for easy access. Fetching of water in most rural areas in Sudan is the responsibility of women, who spend a lot of time on this activity. However, this project will spare women an ample time and give them the opportunity to carry out other activities and participate in the development process of their communities, state and country through creating easy access and reducing the waiting time and distance when fetching water. School latrines will be gender and culturally sensitive and will be constructed separate for men and women, boys and girls thus promoting safety and privacy thus reducing dropout rates more so for the girl child. Hygiene promotion will be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boys being delivered separately. Female hygiene will be sensitively addressed as per the norm of the community. Facilitators will ensure that issues addressed are gender balanced and engage both girls and boys> IRS has been working with schools in Blue Nile State for more than 20 years, frequently targeting IDPs, Returnees, Refugees and Host Community and has noted that girls tend to suffer disproportionally when there is a shortage of educational facilities. By providing safe learning spaces this project will encourage girls to enroll and attend school (and families to send their girls to school). Livelihood interventions will also ensure women participation and representation in community structures and capacity building opportunities. Some activities will directly target women and Female Headed Households in particular to enhance their selfreliance and empower them for sustainable livelihood. Nutrition education and awareness together with introduction of vegetables cultivation and fruits planting in the targeted villages will significantly improve the HHs diet with nutritious food items those are essential for children, pregnant women and lactating mothers’ health and wellbeing. Agro-processing training will equip women with the required skills and knowledge to access the local market and generate income for their families, jobs will be created as youths will benefited from the vocational training in electrical works and welding and will support them with tool kits to start their own Income Generating Activities (IGAs)

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

There is clear connection between our security and program that are appropriate, timely, participative and transparent in the identification of beneficiaries and entitlements. Our program always gives priority to saving lives, doing no harm and alleviating suffering and IRS allocate assistance on the impartiality basis of need, remain neutral within conflict and independent of governments foreign or domestic agenda. The relationship between programming and humanitarian identity is of utmost importance and plays a vital role in gaining acceptance and consent from the beneficiary’s community and other social power-breakers for our presence and programme delivery. IRS has well established beneficiary selection committees that are representative in terms of gender that ensures PWD and the marginalized rights and entitlements are equally given to them and that they are discriminated against at all times. IRS also prescribes and adheres to the 9 CHS standards that over the years has ensured beneficiaries are not discriminated against and provides beneficiaries with a platform for Complaints and Feedback and in all the cases IRS takes decisive actions and decisions that ensure non-discriminatory actions by her staff at all times. The project and activity design have considered the DNH principles thus will ensure that they are not a source of conflict, disagreements and discrimination but rather source of peaceful co-existence and harmony while fostering sustainable utilization and management of community resources. In addition, the project has been designed to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Therefore, IRS will Cooperate and work with relevant partners to help promote social, environmental and economic well-being in a cohesive and inclusive manner and provide structures for handling feedback and complaints, Communities will be sensitized so that they know how to complain if project commitments are not met and finally, Appropriate awareness-raising tools and approaches are used with communities, which are relevant to age, language, social status and capacity. IRS and all partners will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, IRS will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that IRS and project partners understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. IRS will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the projects are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social. Implemented activities have minimal effect on the environment in the project target areas. IRW-Sudan is committed to implementing environmentally friendly activities,
