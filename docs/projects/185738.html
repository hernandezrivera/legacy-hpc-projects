Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to a reduction in WASH-related public health risk through improved access for refugees and affected host communities in the area of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Promotion.
In 2022, UNHCR will ensure the operation and maintainance of 54 water networks in UNHCR-managed camps covering 345,000 refugees. UNHCR and partners will continue maintaining, operating, and upgrading the existing stock of water infrastructure and the development of a large-scale, unified urban-based water system for UNHCR camps along with Teknaf and its host community and will further provide technical support to the sector and DPHE, particularly for the water supply monitoring programme.
UNHCR and partners will continue to support urban scale sustainable sanitation infrastructure, looking to cover all 14 UNHCR camps by end of 2022. Develop and get approval for integrated household latrines and bathing areas to be adopted as the standard in all new transitional shelter designs covering 3,500 households. Increase shared household latrine coverage and bathing area, especially for women and children, and bring coverage rates to UNHCR standards with iron frame bathing areas. Faecal sludge treatment will be increased with a mix of large off-site units and increased use of small-bore sewerage and appropriate cost-effective on-site disposal. UNHCR will ensure a more effective solid waste management systems and infrastructure and further enhance hygiene promotion activities with community engagement and reduce partner support in favour of refugee ownership.
Infrastructure construction and service delivery would mainstream community engagement within the Age, Gender, Diversity (AGD)/ Protection lens for better project outcomes and sustainability ensuring equal access for Rohingya refugee women, men, boys and girls living in camps, and affected host communities to adequate safe drinking water and for domestic purposes as well sanitation and hygiene facilities.

1 - Identified Needs

In 2021, UNHCR and partners have routinely updated the comprehensive and detailed infrastructure sweep and review the WASH Knowledge, Attitude, and Practices (KAP) survey by the end of 2021. These updates and reviews have contributed to better understand the WASH-related needs of refugees in order to inform programme planning. UNHCR is the area focal agency for 14 camps (1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, Kutupalong RC, 3, 4, 4 Extension, 5, 17, Chakmarkul, Nayapara RC, 26 and 27) and Kutupalong Transit Center covering 40.4% of refugees in Ukhia and Teknaf Districts. The main source of domestic water for Rohingya refugees varies between the two main camp areas: Kutupalong and Teknaf. In Kutupalong, the main water source is groundwater accessed by hand pumps and by a system of solar-powered chlorinated water networks developed throughout 2019, 2020 2021. A total of approximately 3,522 functional tube wells in the 10 UNHCR managed camps in Kutupalong supply a population of around 253,500 people, resulting in 98 persons per pump—far below the 250 person/pump UNHCR standard. UNHCR’s recent Knowledge and Practice (KAP) survey indicates that all camps recieve a minimum of 15 l/c/d, with only two receiving above the post-emergency levels of 20 l/c/d. For camps in Teknaf, the main water source is surface water collected in small reservoirs, treated, and distributed through a network with tap-stands. The three UNHCR-managed camps receive between 20 l/c/d during the rainy season reducing to only 14 l/c/d at the end of the dry season. Sanitation coverage remains an issue within the camps. Communal or shared family latrine usage averages 20 individuals/latrine in the 14 UNHCR managed camps (ranging from 13 in Camp 4X to 39 in Camp 2W), just at the post-emergency target of 20. Functional bathing area usage averages 38 individuals/facility, well above the post-emergency target of 20. Lack of full coverage places particular strain on women and girls, who report being unable to use bathing spaces and latrines, and leading to an increase in construction of ad hoc bathing areas and latrines inside shelters, causing contamination of surface drainage with faecal coliform. The large and increasing stock of latrines requires frequent desludging, posing health risks and increased costs, as sludge must be pumped, transported, and treated. Despite major developments in 2020/2021, treatment capacity remains below necessary rates. Surveys indicate High rates of open defecation for children<5, misuse of toilets, and contamination of water after collection. Contamination of stored water at household levels also remains high. Testing of greywater shows heavy contamination with faecal coliform, often running into the open environment. Few solid waste management systems are fully functional, with plastic build-up becoming a serious environmental problem. The high refugee population density and lack of sufficient land are contributing factors to the above, resulting in a lack of space for the construction of facilities/access for desludging, as well as challenging environmental conditions in hilly terrain and flood/ landslide-prone areas.
