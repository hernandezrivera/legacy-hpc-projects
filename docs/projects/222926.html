Project Summary

In 2025, WHO's Emergency Preparedness and Response team will focus on delivering life-saving health interventions to crisis-affected and displaced populations in South Sudan. The planned work prioritizes addressing health emergencies driven by acute food insecurity, escalating conflict, flooding, and population displacement due to the ongoing Sudan crisis and will cover the entire country on a needs-based prioritization strategy. This comprehensive response targets a reduction in excess morbidity and mortality through enhanced disease surveillance, timely outbreak detection, and coordinated emergency responses, particularly in underserved areas.
WHO will bridge critical gaps in the core pipeline for emergency medical supplies to Health Cluster partners, and serve as a "provider of last resort," where other agencies cannot operate, ensuring equitable access to essential health services. Key activities include the distribution of core pipeline supplies like Inter-agency Emergency Health Kits (IHEKs) and prepositioning emergency resources in flood-prone, drought-prone, outbreak-affected and conflict-affected areas. The organization will also support the strengthening of the surveillance system in South Sudan, deploy Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) for outbreak investigations and provide technical and logistical support to health cluster partners, including the Ministry of Health. This will include installing and/or supporting the Public Health Emergency Operations Centers in all states across the country, tasked with coordinating emergency response in the health sector.
To improve system resilience, the project integrates emergency health services with nutrition and water quality monitoring, and supports capacity building for local health workers and facilities. Additionally, preventive and reactive vaccination campaigns will be conducted to address threats from measles, cholera, and other epidemic-prone diseases. Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), gender-based violence (GBV) interventions, and maternal and child health services will be mainstreamed to meet the comprehensive needs of affected populations.
This approach will contribute to WHO’s strategic objectives of improving access to life-saving care, enhancing surveillance and disease response coordination, and fostering long-term health system resilience in South Sudan.

1. Needs

Based on the available documentation, the assessed needs for South Sudan include:1. Disease Outbreaks and Epidemic Preparedness: * Climate change and population migration exacerbating risk of disease outbreaks * Frequent and often concurrent outbreaks of measles, yellow fever, poliovirus, meningitis, hepatitis E, and cholera. * Low immunization coverage (72% for measles and 73% for Penta 3) leading to frequent measles outbreaks. * Malaria as a leading cause of morbidity and mortality, exacerbated by flooding and increased temperatures. * Increased risk of disease spread due to highly mobile and displaced populations and cross-border risks from the Sudan crisis.2. Access to Healthcare Services, especially in emergency contexts: * Limited physical access to health care services across the country, with 57% of the population requiring more than an hour to reach health facilities. * Insufficient functional healthcare infrastructure at national level (18% non-functional health facilities, 50% not financed through the Health Sector Transformation Programme). * Supply chain disruptions affecting availability of essential medicines, family planning commodities, and clinical delivery support.3. Maternal and Child Health Needs: * High rates of unvaccinated children due to inaccessible facilities. * Inadequate reproductive health services, including skilled personnel and essential supplies.4. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Care: * Only 28% of health facilities provide GBV care; staffing and infrastructure challenges limit service availability.5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS): * Limited integration of MHPSS into routine health services. * Need for targeted, community-specific MHPSS interventions.6. Vulnerable Populations: * An estimated 80% of the population lives below the absolute poverty line. Additionally, around 2.0 million IDPs, 2.3 million returnees, and over 500,000 refugees have limited access to determinants of health: adequate food, shelter, water, sanitation and hygiene, education and health services, contributing to an increased burden of disease. * High proportions of women, children, the elderly, and persons with disabilities in conflict and flood-affected areas. * Barriers to healthcare access exacerbated for marginalized groups.7. Public Health Surveillance and Emergency Preparedness: * Nascent systems for early detection and response to health crises and surveillance systems hampered by funding, inadquate coverage, and health systems limitations such as inadequate/lack of staff salaries, poor infrastructure. * A need for enhanced coordination at national and subnational levels for outbreak management, with only limited EOCs in place at state level.8. Capacity Building: * A need for (refresher) training for health workers, especially on surveillance and clinical management, and improving retention strategies. * A need for capacity building of local organizations to support sustainable health responses.9. Health-Nutrition-WASH Integration: * Unable to adequately acute malnutrition through integrated health and nutrition services. *Limited quality of nutrition service delivery, especially in IPC/WASH in the health-facility based Supplementary Centers caring for children with Severe Acute Malnutrition with Medical Complications * Limited routine and emergency WASH assessments and interventions to prevent waterborne diseases.10. Infrastructure and Resources: * Resource gaps in and need for prepositioning of emergency health kits in high-risk areas. * Limited laboratory and diagnostic capabilities at state and local levels.11. Sustainable Development Integration: * Need to transition from emergency response to routine, sustainable health services. * Need to align emergency interventions with South Sudan’s Health Sector Strategic Plan (2023–2027).

2. Project activities

The following response priorities and activities are central to WHO’s strategic approach to addressing HNRP priorities in 2025: 1. Support Emergency Health Response and Essential Health Services:* Provide life-saving essential healthcare services, focusing on vulnerable populations in outbreak-, conflict- and flood-affected regions* Expand access to integrated primary healthcare through static facilities, mobile medical teams, and community health outreach programs, either through sub-grants to partners or in WHOs role as "provider of last resort"* Preposition Inter-agency Emergency Health Kits (IEHKs), nutrition kits, essential medical supplies in high-risk areas to ensure rapid response 2. Ensuring Effective Disease Outbreak Prevention and Control:* Strengthen public health surveillance (IDSR/EWARN), DHIS2 reporting, risk profiling using STAR, and development of multi-hazard plans to enable the timely detection, investigation, and response to emergencies* Conduct preventive and reactive vaccination campaigns for diseases such as measles and cholera* Deploy Rapid Response Teams (RRTs) for outbreak verification and initial public health response 3. Support delivery of Maternal and Child Health and Nutrition services:* Scale up services for maternal and newborn health, especially clinical management of rape* Provide treatment for children with Severe Acute Malnutrition with Medical Complications (SAM-MC) in high-priority areas (jointly with the Nutrition Cluster)* Strengthen routine immunization services to improve coverage rates and reduce vaccine-preventable diseases 4. Ensure/expand access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS):* Integrate MHPSS into emergency health services to address the mental health needs of affected populations* Strengthen community-based psychosocial care and build capacity for mental health response as part of the MHPSS Strategic Plan (2024–2028). 5. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Care:* Improve access to GBV care services across health facilities.* Implement the Health Cluster GBV Action Plan to address gaps in service delivery and coordination. 6. Capacity Building and Local Empowerment:* Train and mentor healthcare workers, including county-level and community health workers, to enhance preparedness and response capacity.* Strengthen local organizations’ capacity to deliver sustainable health services through collaborative planning and resource sharing 7. Public Health Infrastructure and Coordination:* Establish and support Public Health Emergency Operations Centers (PHEOCs) in strategic locations.* Enhance bacteriological diagnostic capacity at state hospitals and laboratories.* Support and strengthen health cluster coordination mechanisms at national and subnational levels. 8. Integration of Health-Nutrition-WASH-Protection Interventions:* (Jointly with WASH cluster), conduct water quality testing and implement WASH measures to mitigate healthcare-associated infections and waterborne diseases.* (Jointly with Nutrition Cluster), integrate health and nutrition services to address malnutrition-related complications in areas of high food insecurity.* (Jointly with Protection Cluster), support the integration of GBV and MHPSS services into health service delivery. 9. Community Engagement and Inclusive Programming:* Support effective Risk Communication and Community Engagement strategies* Use community consultations to shape health interventions tailored to local needs.* Establish feedback mechanisms (hotlines, suggestion boxes, health committees) to monitor service effectiveness and ensure accountability. 10. Resilience and Sustainability:* Align emergency response efforts with South Sudan’s Health Sector Strategic Plan (2023–2027) and broader humanitarian-development-peace nexus frameworks.* Promote localization by building the capacities of local health authorities/organizations to transition towards routine health services.
