Project Summary

The North West and South West regions of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that is hindering access to essential protection, education and health services for an important part of the population. In most of affected areas, health services are not available or even of poor quality. The present project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internal displaced population (IDP) in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon affected by the humanitarian crisis by providing an integrated quality health assistance. The project will target 345,105 IDPs, 249,222 members of host communities (51% of this target are women, 16% are children under 5, and 11% are people over 60 years old). The areas of intervention are Fako, Meme, Manyu, Ndian and Kupe - Muanenguba divisions in the South-West, Mezam, Momo, Bui, Ngo-ketunjia and Boyo in the North-West region, Mungo and Wouri in the Littoral region and in the West region, Menoua, Mifi, Ndé and Noun divisions. The project will emphasize on timely access to equitable health care for men and women (taking into consideration the distance to the closest health facilities for women, children under 5 and people above 60) and quality of interventions (assistance by skilled attendant, referral of cases).
Given that a huge portion of the affected population has been displaced, most the assistance will be provided through mobile clinics to attain populations in their new settlements. An emphasis will be laid on community-based health interventions to strengthen the EWARS in the box project already implemented to get information on potential disease outbreaks as well as cases requiring assistance such as pregnant women. A special attention will be given to person with disabilities to ameliorate their access to health services and care.

Besoins / Needs

The crisis that began in Bamenda since October 2016 is still ongoing. It has affected epidemiological surveillance in the North West and South West regions. The cholera outbreak that started in November 2019 was picked up by the surveillance system more than a week later and this led to the wide spread of the disease in the community and the death of 14 persons. In addition, some areas are still facing measles and monkey pox outbreaks but there is no information on the cases. Given that movement between Cameroon and Nigeria have increased, it is important to strengthen community-based surveillance for early warning on public health events. Persons living with disabilities, pregnant women and teenagers are more vulnerable. They need assistance to access quality health care. Given that most of the affected populations are living in the forest and other poor living conditions, they are exposed to many pathogens that increases the likelihood illnesses. The package will also include mental healthcare to ensure an adequate follow-up for those who are traumatized.
