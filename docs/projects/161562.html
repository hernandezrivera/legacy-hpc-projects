Project Summary

This proposed LoC-Din project aims at scaling up full Camp Coordination and Camp Management supports targeting at least 10,000 populations in informal camps and host communities in MMC, Jere and Konduga LGAs in Borno State in 2020. While LoC-Din will continue to maintain its existing CCCM presence in Mashidimami, Musune and Ajajiri camps in MMC and Jere LGAs, full CCCM coverage will also be extended to other informal camps and camp-like settings where CCCM partners have no active presence in these LGAs including Konduga in the State. Additionally, LoC-Din will continuously respond to new displacements, returns and support the overall humanitarian response through comprehensive sites assessments and monitoring of displacement conditions in locations where no CCCM coordination structures are in place.
The sector’s primary monitoring tool, the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) and other reporting templates will be utilized to gather information on multi-sectoral issues and monitor displacement trends in existing and new informal camps and camp-like settings.
Throughout the project, the needs of women, girls, men and boys of all ages will be addressed right from the planning, through implementation to monitoring and evaluation of all activities. Through the DTM and regular site monitoring and tracking tools, needs and gaps will be captured, reported and shared with partners and stakeholders including disaggregated data. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to contend with GBV and protection concerns within camp and camp-like settings.
Specific activities includes: (a) Site planning and preparation (b) Site coordination/management – coordinate improved provision of humanitarian assistance in informal IDP settlements in selected locations; (c) Communication with affected communities, including complaint and feedback mechanisms, community sensitization/mobilization, ensuring participation of vulnerable groups (women/youth) and that all relevant committees are maintained and functional; (d) Information Management in areas where partners are not working, as well as additional surveys/assessments as necessary and agreed upon with the Sector. Variations in gender, ethnic origin, physical ability and age that affect vulnerability and coping strategies are identified and taken into account for planning to prevent the situation of affected populations from deteriorating even further. Additionally, trainings and capacity building on CCCM mainstreaming GBV, PSEA, trafficking in persons and CFM will be held for both IDPs and staff working in camps. Contingency plans are also developed and put in place to ensure LoC-Din can quickly adapt to and respond to changing humanitarian needs on the ground.

1 - Needs

The DTM round 28 of July 1st – 16th 2019 as released by the Sector shows that, out of a total of 140 informal settlements in MMC, Jere and Konduga LGAs alone, only 33 have CCCM coverage. The rest 107 settlements with 10,006 households, and 49,354 populations, are without CCCM coverage. With the collapse of traditional support systems following massive displacements, many groups of people with specific needs struggle to access basic services in self-settled (informal) camps and are most heavily impacted when distributions become aggressive and/or violent. With the IDPs living in a more fragile environment, tensions with the host community are on the rise. Consequently, assistance provision across the various sectors in the 3 LGAs becomes chaotic. Women and adolescent girls have increasing Gender Based Violence issues to contend with by the day. The situation is being compounded by recent cases of many more new arrivals and informal settlements due to new displacements in the State as captured in the September 2019 factsheet released by the sector . The factsheet also shows that Jere and MMC recorded high numbers of new arrivals in September with Jere having 855 and MMC 409 arrivals. While the Borno State Government, the Sector, UN agencies and other partners are making concerted efforts in responding to the situation, LoC-Din sees the need to complement the efforts of all stakeholders in bridging this huge gap through dedicated CCCM supports in Jere, MMC and Konduga LGAs. By extending CCCM supports to these locations, well coordinated food, shelter, health, WASH, education and livelihoods assistances will flow from partners across sectors into project locations. In addition, this project will mainstream protection in all forms ranging from protection of women and girls from increasing risks of GBV, protection of children from gross violations of their rights such as forced labour, lack of education facilities and programs for children, protection of youths from alcohol and drugs and protection of the aged as well as those living with disabilities from exclusion in the overall humanitarian assistance across various sectors.

2 - Activities

In 2020, LoC-Din proposed project will address the identified needs through the following key activities: • Governance structures. LoC-Din will establish camp governance committees in locations where non exists as well as strengthen existing weak structures. This will create a representative body for liaison with humanitarian actors. LoC-Din will ensure that the representation reflects age, gender and diversity. These structures will include representatives from host communities and local authorities as appropriate.• Coordination meetings. LoC-Din will organize weekly camp coordination meetings with IDPs, government agencies and relevant partners within each locations to discuss issues, challenges and gaps to inform effective service delivery in project locations. • Information management. LoC-Din will provide support to the information management (IM) role of the sector to enable the sector develop and disseminate IM products regularly to ensure more coherent information is shared across camps and partners.• Coordinate and monitor service provision. The project will support in identifying gaps and duplications in service provision and encourage partners and sectors to make appropriate changes, to ensure that minimum standards are attained.• The project will establish efficient and predictable two-way communication systems with persons of concern and the broader humanitarian community. It will create complaints mechanisms and improve accountability to persons of concern within project locations.• Camp closure. Camp closure needs to be considered during camp design and should continue to be analysed during the care and maintenance phase, to ensure that, at the end of a camp's life cycle, its transition or closure is efficient, dignified and adequately financed.• Solutions. Identify and press for short term or durable solutions that will improve the quality of life of persons of concern in areas we will be working.• LoC-Din will be very sensitive to cross cutting issues like protection, GBV, sanitation, hygiene promotion and public health, and will work to ensure that these issues are adequately attended to in locations we will be working.• CCCM capacity-building. Humanitarian partners, government officials and persons of concern may have a limited understanding of CCCM roles, responsibilities, tools and methodologies. LoC-Din will provide CCCM capacity building support to camp and camp-like settlements leaders, host communities representatives and staff particularly to meet up with high staff turnover and emerging settlements due to new displacements. CCCM training also creates opportunities for dialogue between actors, and can help to establish and codify roles and responsibilities.• Participation. The project will ensure that the age, gender and diversity (AGD) lens is used throughout the project cycle in all locations where we will work. All inclusive participation will be mainstreamed through effective feedback and complaint mechanisms.• LoC-Din will help to protect persons of concern by establishing physical presence in selected locations and engaging in regularly interactions with persons of concern, holding informal dialogues, and noting issues, challenges and concerns that may not be revealed by more formal mechanisms.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will organize platforms for camp coordination meetings, Committees meetings and Focused Group Discussions (FGD) where complaints and feedbacks from all groups will be brought to the fore. The project will develop a context based complaint and feedback mechanism that allows target beneficiaries to freely and easily express their perceptions of project implementation. To achieve this, LoC-Din will establish physical presence in all selected camps with help desks where LoC-Din Staff report every day to attend to complaints and feedbacks from project beneficiaries. in addition, dedicated phone lines will be made available to most vulnerable IDPs who will be empowered to freely and easily express their perceptions about our operations and how they are affected. LoC-din will also place suggestion boxes strategically in project locations where IDPs can easily access and drop their comments in the boxes for pickups by our site facilitators. Finally, all committees leaders will be issued a simply designed form to be used during committees meetings for recording complaints and feedbacks in a manner that allows all inclusive participation and ensures no one is left out.
