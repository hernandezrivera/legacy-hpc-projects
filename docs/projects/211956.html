Project Summary

The myriad of protection concerns coupled with scarce resources across Syria has led to harmful coping mechanisms reported to be on the rise, including child labor and child/forced marriage. The crisis continues to have a gendered impact evident in adolescent girls exposed to harmful and discriminatory gender norms including gender-based violence (GBV) within and outside their homes, impacting their safety and capacity to move freely. The economic crisis compounds protection risks leading to multiple issues including an increase in corporal punishment against children due to caregivers’ stress throughout Syria and if unaddressed, the impact(s) can last a lifetime.
According to 2022-2023 IRC MSNA data, CP findings included:
76% of KIIs reported children are in need of focused non-specialized services.
51% of KIIs reported children are in need of specialized services.
67% of KIIs reported parents (or other caregivers) are in need of specialized services.
82% of KIIs reported that children are engaged in a type of work that prevents them from going to school.
44% of KIIs reported that children living in child-headed households (CHH) engaged in child labor.
66% of KIIs reported children living in households with persons with specific needs (PWSN) engaged in child labor.
45% of KIIs reported children being separated from their usual caregivers.
54% of KIIs reported newly established institutions for children without their usual caregivers and 52% reported violence against children.
The goal of this project is to reduce protection risks and respond to the needs of children and families in Northwest Syria through the provision of holistic child protection services for children, caregivers, and community members through 1) Specialized case management teams providing: Comprehensive child protection case management to identify the most vulnerable children in NWS and supports them to full recovery from violence, abuse, neglect, and coercion.
In addition to awareness-raising for community members. 2) Emergency Child Protection Response team responding to new displacement and Adhoc situations by providing:
- Emergency Parenting Sessions for caregivers (Male and female) to equip them in dealing with stress and implementing positive discipline techniques that ensure safe and healthy child development.
- Structured/non-structured psychosocial support to children and adolescents aimed at helping them overcome trauma and distress as well as learning how to healthily express themselves and their emotions.
- Awareness-raising for community members on important topics such as early marriage, child labor, child development, and psychological needs, and COVID-19 precautions.
- Training/establishment of child protection committees from community leaders and influential individuals who support the child protection team in raising awareness, supporting existing positive community structures, and channeling assistance to the most vulnerable community members.
- Provision of PFA and referrals as appropriate.
IRC accommodates the needs of children with disabilities whenever possible through the rehabilitation of spaces (static and mobile centers) to ensure access for CWDs and by adjusting curriculums and materials, such as printing materials with Brail, using interpretation for children with hearing disabilities, prioritizing case management for CWD's and using cash for protection to purchase mobility/disability devices and conducting small scale home rehabilitation for CWD's access. IRC plans to include CwDs in all activities by supporting access and making the physical environment suitable for them, as well as providing quality, technical and inclusion activities. IRC will consider at least 15% of CwDs in PSS activities and will consider guidance notes for CwDs inclusion in both the "SEL" and "I am stronger" curricula. Providing case management services for CwDs and Cash for Protection, as well as, providing adapted parenting skills for CwDs.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The Child Protection monitoring report in NWS also found high reports of children in need of focused non-specialized services, of child labor, and of children being separated from their caregivers. The northwest is divided between Armed Opposition Groups and by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham. Child protection risks are likely to worsen in 2023, with rights violations such as child recruitment, detention, neglect, exploitation, sexual violence, abduction, attacks on schools and hospitals, and denial of humanitarian access. After the earthquake in northwest Syria, children need protection support. The CP Area of Responsibility is targeting 500,000 children and adults with awareness sessions on Child Protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) including specific earthquake and family tracing related messages. At least 800 unaccompanied and separated children need family tracing and reunification services, or family based alternative care. There is a need to scale up the availability of alternative care services to ensure unaccompanied children and those in inappropriate care environment can be placed in family-based care. Gaps have been identified by partners on non-specialized MHPSS services (level 3 of the Inter-Agency Standing Committee pyramid). There is a need to implement more inter-cluster and gender-disaggregated interventions focused on adolescents to address issues related to child labor and child marriage. Throughout the project, IRC will regularly monitor the violation of rights, barriers to accessing essential services and the protection situation in NWS to understand the changes over time and the consequences of increased displacements, prolonged stays, and relocation. IRC’s protection monitoring provides an initial entry point through which high-risk individuals, including persons with disabilities, are identified, and linked with services, including legal assistance and access to case management and psychosocial support. Protection teams will work closely with service providers (including International Non-Governmental Organizations (INGOs), local NGOs, local associations, and local authorities) included on the mapping to ensure that that service information is up to date. IRC works to target the most vulnerable populations first, this is done systematically considering the intersectionality of risk factors including age, disability, gender and diversity. Further, IRC will provide reliable and responsive legal and protection information services and raise awareness at household and community level on available services and how to safely access them through legal awareness sessions and legal consultations.
