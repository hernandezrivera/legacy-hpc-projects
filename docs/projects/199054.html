Enhancing IDPs site in North West Syria through strengthening of Fire mitigation, care and maintenance, management and coordination

Project Summary

This project aims at providing the following services to IDPs sites:
a) Care and maintenance of the most vulnerable six IDP sites in A'rima, Atareb, Albab, Idleb, Dana, and Ma'aret Tamsrin sub-districts including the following:
1. Train 222 IDPs ( youth, women, and men) on tents and roads maintenance in the six existing IDP sites in A'rima, Atareb, Albab, and Dana sub-districts and connect them with the recruitment committees for CFW activities of care and maintenance in the camps.
2. Conduct care and maintenance for the six existing IDP sites in A'rima, Atareb, Albab, Idleb, Dana and Ma'aret Tamsrin sub-districts during 2023. The major work will be through technicians and labor to conduct light to medium maintenance services such as for electricity, water, and sewage network in the camps. Shafak will use the voucher modality or CFW as a redemption method for the recruited staff from the camps. Camp selection would be through identifying the camps that are at risk of floods or have had the effects of floods previously, in addition to applying the vulnerability criteria where camps with higher numbers of women, elderly, children, and disabled persons would be prioritized.
b) Strengthening emergency preparedness and (fire) response in IDP sites through establishment of fire and safety warden committees through training of 120 IDPs on camps management in 6 existing IDP sites in the sub-districts of A'rima, Atareb, Salqin, and Ma'aret Tamsrin sub-districts. Fire and safety warden activities will include fire extinguishers and fire blankets provision in addition to providing TOT for the camps fire committees and training for camps residents.
c) Implement tailored livelihood activities designed to enable HHs to restore their assets and leave IDP sites for better solutions and develop spontaneous exit strategies through the provision of income generation activities and TVET training for 150 HHs (750 individuals) in three camps in Harim and Azaz districts to enhance HHs resilience and their capacity to restore their assets and leave the camps during 2023.
d)Develop and strengthen the capacity of camp management actors on camp governance and protection mainstreaming through the training of 60 representatives of camps on CCCM principles in Sarmin and Salqin and A'rima sub-districts.
The scope of work has been limited to training of IDPs, Care and Maintenance and camps committees formation activities, and fire safety activities as they are the top priorities in camps in order to mitigate the effects of crisis floods and fire incidents. Yet, Care and Maintenance activities are still top priorities out of these activities. During the project, Shafak intends to communicate with the stakeholders like camps managements, beneficiaries, local authorities, and donors through reporting on the displacement, floods, and fire incidents and creating advocacy messages to support IDPs in the camps in addition to the meetings. Shafak normally has with the camps representatives during signing MoUs and communication channels for the beneficiaries like hotline and complaints boxes, and WhatsApp.
Shafak keeps tracking of incident reports and displacement reports issued by CCCM and closely monitoring the displacement and incidents on the ground ( bi-weekly basis), which are reported to CCCM. In case of underfunding for these activities, camps at risk with more vulnerable groups like women, the elderly, and the disabled will be prioritized.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, over seven million people are highly dependent on humanitarian WASH assistance, including 1.9 million people living in 1,116 IDP last resort sites that require comprehensive and continued WASH assistance to survive in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate. The number of displaced people increased significantly in Aleppo, with many IDPs forced to seek shelter in planned camps, informal camps or settlements, transit/reception centres, and collective centres. The vast majority of these IDPs resides in informal settlements where shelter and WASH conditions and access to basic services, including health and protection, are inadequate or absent. According to the assessment conducted by Shafak in October 2021 covering 24 sub-districts, 42% of the sub-districts received IDPs and all of the assessed sub-districts have random tented camps. In March 2021, CCCM reported' many people in northwest Syria also rely on burning unsafe materials for heat, which can cause fires or be unsafe for breathing. From 24 February until 23 March 2021, the CCCM reported fire incidents affecting 18 IDP sites, leading to one death and one injury as well as the destruction of 23 shelters, mainly due to malfunctioning or misused heating or cooking stoves.' Last report of CCCM in December 2021 mentioned during that month only around 300 IDPs sites were flooded. Along Shafak project ' Cash for Emergency' 2021, 55% cases have been newly displaced, 30% fire incident cases & 15% affected by floods.

4 - Implementation of activities

Shafak will implement the proposed activities depending on its usual practices and SOPs derived from working with different INGO and UN Agencies. Shafak will follow the steps: -Needs assessment to determine the IDP sites affected by any humanitarian disasters which are in need for care and maintenance services. -Technical assessment and preparing BoQs -Recruitment of skilled IDPs committees for leading maintenance work and train unskilled workers from the IDPs on maintenance activities in the IDPs sites linking with CFWs if applicable with a particular focus on enrolling youth and mid-age adults -Rehabilitation activities will include 1) train maintenance and emergency responders and IDPs committees 2)maintenance of alleys between tents, electricity, sewage and water network – Prepare fire warden committees and delver ToT for them on fire safety and warden- Equip the camps with the needed fire extinguishers, train committees on refilling the extinguishers- Provide camps residents with fire blankets and provide them with a training for using the. Train IDPs groups on camps management and support them to form camps committees inclusive of youths, men and women – Conduct TVET training and income generation activities for 6 camps residents in A’zaz and Dana sub-districts and support them to restore their assets and leave the camp. Monitoring and evaluation for the activity will be done in activities monitoring, PDMs, satisfaction surveys, base and end line.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Lack of fire prevention and response would be critical for the safety of critical the IDPs leaving in already precarious conditions and environment. Mitigation: Conduct fire awareness campaigns and trainings, well-plan for fire mitigation and prevention activities, maintain the CCCM and SNFI Cluster technical guidance and reporting and tracking through Incident reporting and referral tools. Risk2: Individual residents may over-rely on any civil response/DRR committee for responding to a natural disaster and may shirk individual responsibility, Mitigation: Incorporate in any dedicated DRR response (such as a training of first responders) general awareness raising focused on the individuals capacities of men, women, and children, ensure that training indicatives include sessions on DNH and protection mainstreaming, ensure the participation of all age groups of different genders in such committees, including the most vulnerable groups, the elderly and PWDs in training and awareness raising Risk3: Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities (PWD) . Mitigating: -Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of services- During post-distribution follow-up, and feedback on the process and materials. Risk4: Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. Mitigating: planned activities to encourage women and b endorsed by community and religious leader


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by CFW male beneficiaries while registering for the services or attending the training. Mitigating: -recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA(PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children, train staff on code of conduct and PSEA and CSG policies and incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity, ensure safe recruitment of staff andsafe response standards.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: recruitment of care and maintenance committee could create some tensions because of their fees payments. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: -Include the unskilled IDPs residents in the training and maintenance activities - Ensure that skilled workers in the targeted IDPs are involved in the leading committees recruited for construction - Maintenance roles are clarified early to prevent labour-seeking vandalism -Ensure that CRM mechanism is clear and active. Risk 2: Trainings are not welcomed by the local authorities. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: -Tailor each training module to the context, coordinate with the key representatives in the camps for reducing unexpected risks -Advocacy with local authorities on all level. Risk 3: Create pull-factor and expose IDPs to physical safety risks when camps and settlements are located in unsafe areas). Likelihood: High. Mitigation: - Ensure that response is done to only captured camps by ISIMM tool-Ensure that Cash and tents distributions taking place are aligned with SNFI, CCCM and Cash Working Groups SoPs. Set clear camps selection criteria and mobilize them among the camps. Risk5 : Eviction of HHs not willing to leave due to camps closure. Likelihood: medium. Mitigation: ensure voluntary leave of the HHs, assess the HHs situation, prioritize safety of the households and report and follow CCCM guidance for camps closure and ensure referal is taking place.
