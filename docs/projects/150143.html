Project Summary

General objective: Contribute to a comprehensive Protection system of vulnerable
communities in the North-West Bank
Outcomes:
1. Mitigate the impacts of conflict related violations (Settlers violence, demolitions,
excessive use of force by ISF, displacement) on the mental health and
psychosocial (here after called MHPSS) wellbeing of Palestinian communities living
in North West Bank including the North Jordan Valley
2. Strengthen the resilience and positive coping mechanisms of Palestinian
communities living in North West Bank including the North Jordan Valley
3. Third States Parties, notably France & EU Members are deeply mobilized and
take systematic and concrete measures toward the occupation practices which
negatively impact the protection of Palestinians and their effective access to
services in the WB
Within this action, MDM will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 2,
both relating to the Strategic objective 1 of the HRP: The rights of Palestinians living
under occupation, including those living under the blockade and other restrictions,
are protected, respected and promoted in accordance with IHL and IHRL .
In relation with the Objective 2, MDM intervention focuses in the provision of MH and
Psychosocial services. MDM implement Emergency MHPSS support as well as
resilience-based interventions (to improve positive coping mechanisms) and case
management in the communities of the North-West Bank most exposed to Settlers
violence, demolitions, ISF violations and possible forcible transfer.
MDM advocacy efforts will also contribute to the Objective 1 of the Protection
cluster “Increase Respect of IHL & HRL”. The Advocacy contribution of MDM will be
through field briefings to diplomats and international media, production of periodic
reports and factsheets to reflect the impact of the violations in the MHPSS of the
Palestinian communities, in addition to a lobby tours in Paris and Brussels
addressing the same topics.

Needs

The Communities targeted in NWB are continuously affected by critical incidents related to the Occupation including ISF use of Force (Live Ammunition, physical assaults, violence against schools & child detention), Settlers’ violence and demolitions which have a significant impact on their MHPSS wellbeing. Up to August 2018, MDM has observed an increase in the number of Settlers Violence incidents, that has been reflected in the number of people in need of MHPSS support. Thus, in the first nine months of 2018 MDM has provided MHPSS to 1.129 victims of critical events, while in the last 9 months of 2017 the number was 667 persons. MDM data analysis in 2017 showed that men were equally affected as women (71% of men VS 78 % of women reporting severe stress reactions). Moreover, it was interesting to see that 56% of boys and girls report severe signs of acute stress while 44% of them report normal signs of stress, we can definitively say that children and teenagers are reporting less stress reactions than adults. If minority of people presents severe and moderate disorders, which would need the intervention of professional MH specialists, a large percentage of people experience legitimate distress and other psychological reactions, which continuously affect them and increase the risk of developing MH difficulties. This is the case of our beneficiaries. The last analysis conducted by MDM in 2017 showed that 58 % of the adult population including men and women who received Psychological first aid report severe signs of acute stress and are at risk of developing PTSD; 42% report moderate signs of acute stress and 0 % only report little or no signs of acute stress; the feeling of insecurity being the main difficulty among the psychological signs among adults , sleeping disorder being the main problems faced by children, teenagers and adults (56,8%) followed by headaches. The most reported traumatic signs were flashbacks, intrusive feelings and thoughts (about the critical event) and concentration problems; Regression in academic progress is identified among 27% children and teenagers;Psychological First Aid (PFA) is important to mitigate legitimate distress but it is not sufficient to support the affected communities who continuously face complex traumatic events, as there is people in need of more specialized services including advanced counseling, short term psychotherapy and psychiatric services. From April 2017 and until August 2018 MDM referred 50 cases to specialized services (36% females,22% girls, 22% boys and 20% men), 50% of whom did not reach the services. Insufficient specialized MH staff at community level, together with the reluctance of the individuals to go and see specialists mainly for stigma related, economic reasons, availability of services and geographical access make necessary to reinforce MHPSS services at community level through innovative approaches such as low intensity psychological interventions (WHO 2016) and capacity building of MH staff such as the Schools counselors. In complement to the above interventions which aim to treat moderate psychological problems should be implemented to strengthen the coping skills of individuals facing continuous adversity. Based on MDM experience in the NWB, Awareness raising and Psycho-education sessions are also needed, as communities still lack information and knowledge about the potential psycho social impacts of the critical events (and about the positive ways to deal with it). .Credible and evidence-based data from the field will be continuously provided to demonstrate the humanitarian impact of the occupation policies on communities’ MHPSS wellbeing. MDM advocacy initiatives will highlight the relevant legal issues, provide analysis, trends and technical inputs to depict the situation and encourage targets to reassess their positions

Activities

Output 1: Increased access to immediate MHPSS and to a holistic protection response through case management for the victims of critical events. The objective is to mitigate the psychological (distress) and psychosocial impacts of the critical incident on individuals, families and communities. This cases are identified by MDM from different sources: Directly referral from communities and local authorities, referrals from MHPSS working group and other protection cluster partners. The main activity is the provision of MHPSS Emergency Response which includes a comprehensive series of activities:(1) 1st Support visit including PFA within 72 hours , (2) 2nd Support visit including a Psychosocial Assessment (within 10 15 days), (3) Referrals to ensure the response to other basic and protection needs. This referral is done directly to members of the protection cluster working in the same area of intervention including livelihood, shelter, legal and health actors(4) low intensity psychological interventions for individuals affected by moderate psychological problems such as moderate anxiety and/or depression and (5) referrals to MH specialized services for the management and treatment of detected mental health disorders. There is 2 systems of referra, one internal withing MDM and another external to MHPSS working group actors and MoH according to needs, user preference and geographical area. For this referrals the National MH referral system is used. MDM in monthly bases reports to the MHPSS working group database about its interventions. MDM PSS ER team is a gender balanced team ensuring equal access of the critical incidents victims to services provided taking into consideration gender and cultural aspects. MDM adapts its intervention timing to match with the community members availability intervening with children after school hours, with mothers in the mornings and with adult males following working hours or during the weekends. Output 2: Increased Resilience capacity of the communities through Community Psychoeducation and MHPSS preparedness at community level. Psychoeducation: the main aim of our Community Psychoeducation is to make the Communities aware about the impact of traumatic events on their psychosocial wellbeing, physical health and behaviour and aware them about the resources and services available to alleviate it. Psychoeducation sessions usually address critical incidents impacts on women , men , girls and boys and the proper way on dealing with each impact based on the gender of participants. A specific brochure (30.000 copies) will be distributed within the sessions.MHPSS Preparedness at communities’ level including schools: The objective is to reinforce the community emergency response capacity, through school emergency PSS teams (MOEHE established 17 PSS emergency teams within schools in the WB to enable them to respond to critical incidents within the schools). MDM in Partnership with PCC will reinforce 5 Schools MoE movile PSS teams to respond to critical incidents in the NWB. MDM together with PCC and the MoE started to work with 5 MoE PSS teams in 2017, training and supervising its members. This project will continue the technical supervision, operational follow up and accompany in the field. Moreover the project will develop an operational guideline for the PSS teams of the MoE.Output 3: Advocacy initiatives that aim to enhance the accountability for violations of IHRL & IHL and the engagement ofThird States Parties :MdM collects quantitative and qualitative information to raise awareness of public opinion and mobilize French and EU decision-makers to effectively act according IHRL & IHL standards. The activities will include: production of MdM own documentation and testimonies about the impact of critical events on MHPSS wellbeing, contribution to joint reports (Settlers’ Violence Core Group, consortium, UN ,...), organization and participation in decision-makers briefing, lobby tours in Paris

Indicators

MDMF team will ensure the accountability and client satisfaction through qualitative and quantitative approaches including: (A) 3 focus groups per activity(1 with children, 1 with females and 1 with males) & (B) satisfaction surveys that will be disseminated among a selected sample of the beneficiaries including children ,females and males (70% of victims are satisfied with the interventions provided) Specific Objective Indicator# direct and indirect victims benefiting from structured MHPSS support. This is in line with the indicators of the of CPMHPSS: (1)# of children benefiting from structured psychosocial services and child protection interventions including life skills programsand (2) # of men and women benefiting from structured psychosocial services.The indicator will include the beneficiaries from the MHPSS Emergency response and psycho education activities (Output 1 and Output 2).Specific data bases are already created and functional to monitor the intervention: 1) MHPSS ER data base in which staff insert their interventions desegregated by gender and age. To complement this, the results of Impact of Event scales (revised version) and Children revised impact of event scales are added to this data base. 2) the psychoeducation data sheet which includes the number of attendees desegregated by gender and age. MDM advocacy indicators will feed the SO1 indicators of the PC: # factsheets and other advocacy material produced and # local briefings raising protection concerns to the members of international community .
