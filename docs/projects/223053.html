Project Summary

Goal: The project aims to establish a resilient, self-reliant, and protected community within refugee and host populations, with youth playing a central role in building positive social bonds, enhancing safety, and advocating for their rights and well-being.
Core Objective: To empower youth and community members to lead initiatives, strengthen humanitarian support systems, and enhance protection and resilience mechanisms through sustainable partnerships, advocacy, and localized solutions.
Total # of Participants: 158263 individual
Refugee Community: 146356 (Girls: 39857; Boys: 32858; Adult Women: 36898; Adult Men: 30380; Elderly Women: 2799; Elderly Men: 3564)
Host Community: 11907 (Girls: 1715; Boys: 1514; Adult Women:3532 ; Adult Men: 3,330; Elderly Women: 1009; Elderly Men: 807)
Location: Camps 3, 4, 12, 19 in Ukhiya, Camp 22 in Teknaf, and the host community in Hnila, Teknaf.
Key Activities:
1.Empowering Youth for Resilience: Youth(Boys and girls) will engage in motivational sessions, cultural events, sports, and debates to foster self-reliance, positive coping skills, and a sense of agency.
2.Youth-Driven Dialogue and Advocacy: Youth-led advocacy with community groups and local authorities strengthens humanitarian support, increasing responsiveness to community needs.
3.Youth-Led Conflict Resolution: Youth leaders(Boys and girls) facilitate conflict resolution and tension mitigation, enhancing social cohesion and peaceful coexistence.
4.Community-Led Mini-Projects: Community members partner with local service providers to lead impactful, small-scale projects that address local issues and foster community-driven decisions.
5.Protection Monitoring for Advocacy: Incident monitoring provides data-informed advocacy, identifying community risks, threats, and vulnerabilities.
6.Quick Evacuation Kits: Emergency kits for vulnerable individuals(Persons with specific needs, Individuals with Unaccompanied/separated child, Female headed HH etc.) minimize disaster impacts, ensuring safe, rapid evacuation for those with specific needs.
7.Emergency Cash Assistance and Protection Support: At-risk individuals( Adolescent girls female and male)receive cash assistance to reduce vulnerability, enhance their response capacity, and address threats.
8.Private Sector Engagement: Local businesses contribute resources, expertise, and funding, fostering sustainable partnerships to enhance humanitarian impact.
9.Quarterly Perception Surveys: The Community Perception Tracker gathers feedback on project effectiveness, guiding necessary improvements.
10.Strengthening CFM and AAP: Enhanced Feedback Mechanisms provide accessible channels for community input, promoting transparency, trust, and accountability.
11.Access to Protection Services: Referral pathways facilitate timely identification and response to protection needs, guiding community members to essential services.
Expected Outcomes:
-Enhanced Community Resilience: Youth empowerment and engagement in productive activities will strengthen cohesion, build self-reliance, and enhance the community’s ability to handle challenges and advocate for their needs.
-Conflict Mitigation and Increased Security: Protection-focused activities, including monitoring and rapid response, will improve safety and reduce community conflicts
-Strengthened Partnerships and Sustainability: Engagement with the private sector and service providers will improve sustainability and resource availability
-Improved Feedback and Accountability: Effective feedback and monitoring systems will create a transparent, inclusive environment, ensuring all voices are considered in project improvements.
-Informed, Evidence-Based Advocacy: Data from protection monitoring and perception surveys will guide advocacy efforts, refining humanitarian strategies to address evolving community needs

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya refugee camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf face severe and evolving security challenges, with rising incidents of abductions, extortion, and gun violence by organized groups disproportionately affecting women, girls, and young men. According to the Joint Protection Monitoring Report, protection incidents from January to September 2024 affected over 2,200 individuals, with a majority of these incidents exacerbating vulnerabilities among women and adolescent girls, particularly during nighttime movements. Gender-based violence (GBV), including domestic violence and sexual harassment, remains pervasive, further aggravated by inadequate lighting and restricted access to safe communal spaces. Organized group violence has had a devastating impact on young men, who are increasingly targeted for recruitment into armed groups, with conflicts between ARSA and RSO leading to a 70% increase in affected individuals by Quarter 3. Meanwhile, women and girls face the dual burden of caring for families in insecure environments and being at heightened risk of trafficking and exploitation. Human trafficking, which surged in 2024, disproportionately affects women and girls for sexual exploitation and young men for forced labor. From January to June 2024, 134 Rohingya refugees were identified as trafficking victims, with the majority being women and children. Urgent anti-trafficking awareness programs, survivor care, and reintegration services for both male and female victims are critical. Limited lighting in public spaces has had significant gendered implications, with theft of solar lights further restricting women and girls from accessing WASH facilities, latrines, and pathways at night. Women and girls have reported avoiding nighttime movements entirely, even when seeking essential services, due to the risks of harassment and violence. In contrast, male adolescents are increasingly drawn to organized criminal groups due to a lack of livelihood opportunities, putting them at risk of exploitation and recruitment. These challenges call for targeted interventions, including enhanced lighting, youth engagement programs, and community-based security initiatives, with a specific focus on the distinct needs of women, girls, and young men. The economic vulnerability of women remains stark, with 99% of women unemployed and unable to pursue income-generating activities due to caregiving responsibilities, restrictive social norms, and a lack of tailored livelihood opportunities. This lack of economic independence not only increases their dependency on humanitarian aid but also heightens their exposure to exploitation and abuse. For men, particularly young males aged 18-24, the lack of economic prospects has pushed many to seek dangerous informal work outside the camps, exposing them to trafficking and hazardous labor conditions. Accountability mechanisms also display clear gender disparities. While 43% of refugees reported not receiving responses to complaints, women, particularly those with caregiving roles or disabilities, face additional barriers in accessing formal help desks and complaint mechanisms. Cultural norms and safety concerns often force them to rely on male family members for representation, leading to underreporting of critical protection issues. Improved, gender-sensitive feedback systems that ensure the inclusion of women, girls, and persons with disabilities are essential. The ongoing funding gap in the humanitarian response, with only 53% of the 2024 JRP funded as of October, threatens essential protection services for women and girls, who bear the brunt of insecurity and limited access to safe spaces. Engaging the private sector through Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) initiatives can help bridge these funding gaps, ensuring that critical services like GBV survivor support, livelihood programs for women, and enhanced safety measures are adequately resourced.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

In Oxfam’s JRP25 intervention, protection mainstreaming is central to all activities, ensuring safety, dignity, and inclusivity. Oxfam employs a community-based, safe programming approach that prioritizes the well-being of all participants, particularly vulnerable groups. The program actively prevents actions that could cause harm, conducting thorough risk assessments with partners and stakeholders to identify and reduce protection risks linked to activities.Oxfam ensures meaningful access by making services available to all, including people with disabilities, without discrimination. Dedicated staff focus on safeguarding, disability inclusion, and protection monitoring throughout the project. Accountability is enhanced through tools such as the Community Perception Tracker and Community Feedback Mechanisms, ensuring two-way communication and transparency. An inclusive communication strategy provides accessible ways for reporting and feedback.Protection Principles:• Prioritizing Safety and Dignity: All activities are designed to avoid harm and respect the dignity of participants, with a special focus on vulnerable groups.• Meaningful Access: Services and information are accessible to all, emphasizing disability inclusion and ease of access.• Protection and Accountability: Tools like the Community Perception Tracker enable participants to provide feedback, ensuring transparency.• Identification and Mitigation of Protection Risks: Protection monitoring and inclusive communication help identify and address risks, guided by community insights and data collection.Oxfam’s protection mainstreaming strategy ensures that interventions are safe, inclusive, and responsive to participants’ needs, empowering the most vulnerable and creating a protected environment.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Adherence to Standards:The project aligns with international standards such as the Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD) approach and the 16 Gender Inclusion and Equity (GIE) minimum standards, guiding inclusive program design and ensuring non-discriminatory access to services.  Inclusive Programming:The project will deliver accessible activities for people of all ages and abilities, including adaptations in materials, accessible communication, and physical accessibility to sites for persons with disabilities. Efforts will ensure that people with disabilities, older adults, and youth are actively involved in decision-making processes.  Disability and Age Sensitivity in Services:Oxfam’s sectors, including Protection, Food Security, Gender, and WASH, will tailor services to meet the needs of persons with disabilities and vulnerable age groups. This includes providing assistive devices, accessible facilities, and child-friendly spaces to support children and the elderly.  Collaboration and Capacity Building:Oxfam will collaborate with disability inclusion lead agencies and local partners to enhance staff and community capacity in addressing the needs of persons with disabilities and older individuals. This includes age- and disability-sensitive training across all sectors.  Data Collection Methods for Identifying Age and Disability Needs:To identify age and disability-related needs, the project will utilize the UNHCR Population Fact Sheet, which provides demographic data on vulnerabilities. The Washington Group Short Set Questions will be used to identify disabilities, and Key Informant Interviews (KIIs) and focus group discussions (FGDs) with marginalized groups will collect qualitative insights. These methods will ensure that the design and implementation of activities are informed by accurate, age, and disability-sensitive data.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Oxfam strengthens Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) by embedding inclusive Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) throughout the project cycle. Accessible channels, such as help desks, complaint boxes, hotlines, mobile outreach, and household visits, ensure all community members, including marginalized groups like women, persons with disabilities, and older persons, can voice concerns or suggestions. Regular community sensitization campaigns raise awareness of these mechanisms to ensure broader participation and inclusivity. Feedback is systematically collected through consultations, including focus group discussions and participatory sessions, with data disaggregated by age, gender, and disability. Monthly analysis of feedback ensures adaptive programming, enabling Oxfam to address gaps and improve service delivery. Transparency is maintained by sharing updates with communities on actions taken in response to their concerns, fostering trust and engagement. Oxfam adopts a localization approach, building the capacity of local groups and partners to strengthen downward accountability. These groups are trained to lead consultations and address key issues like WASH, livelihoods, and protection, ensuring communities remain at the center of program decisions. Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) evaluates service delivery and identifies areas for improvement, while dedicated staff and trained volunteers ensure timely responses to feedback. By integrating inclusive CFMs, building local capacities, and maintaining transparent communication, Oxfam’s approach ensures community voices are heard, gaps are addressed, and protection outcomes are enhanced effectively.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

For this project, Oxfam, along with its partners, will ensure accountability towards PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) and will take timely actions when necessary. Exclusive training on safeguarding and child rights policies will be provided to staff, non-staff contacts, and community volunteers involved in the project. At the community level, all community group members will be oriented on Oxfam’s safeguarding policy, including key concepts related to sexual harassment, abuse, exploitation, and the appropriate reporting mechanisms. A confidentiality policy will be embedded within this orientation to ensure safe and secure reporting. Additionally, Oxfam proposes to carry out a PSEA risk analysis for the project activities and establish mitigation measures to address any identified risks. As an active member of the PSEA Network, Oxfam will also contribute to joint planning with other programs to address SEA-related risks and promote a safe environment for all stakeholders. By embedding PSEA principles within the project’s design and implementation, Oxfam ensures that protection from sexual exploitation and abuse remains a priority, fostering a safe, accountable environment for participants and staff.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

While this project does not initially focus on direct climate change adaptation and mitigation, Oxfam ensures that all interventions integrate environmentally sustainable practices to indirectly contribute to climate resilience. The project incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) principles and introduces measures to strengthen community preparedness for climate-related challenges. These measures include: Environmentally Friendly Equipment/Materials: The use of sustainable materials, such as renewable solar lighting and eco-friendly construction materials, reduces resource consumption and environmental degradation, aligning with climate-conscious practices. Community Education on Climate Resilience: Oxfam will integrate community awareness during intervention on climate-smart practices, such as energy efficiency solar system in shed, reuseable water pot, water conservation, and disaster preparedness, to enhance the adaptive capacity of vulnerable groups to climate-induced risks. Waste Management & Resource Efficiency: Efforts are made to minimize waste generation and promote recycling, contributing to the conservation of natural resources and reducing the carbon footprint of project activities.While the project’s core focus is on protection and safety, these measures support broader climate action by fostering sustainable practices and enhancing community resilience to environmental and climate-related risks.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, Oxfam and Partners are jointly taking actions at a regular frequency to addressing emergency preparedness and responses. We have capacitated Emergency Response Team (ERT) and they will be in close connection with the PERU, DMU at camp and DMC (Disaster Management committee) at host community from time to time. During the project tenure, project participants will be capacitated on DRR initiatives. By integrating these measures, the project aims to enhance community resilience, minimize the impact of emergencies, and ensure a rapid and coordinated response to protect lives.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

This project actively engages both young boys and girls, recognizing them as essential agents of change within their communities. By empowering youth through targeted initiatives, the project aims to strengthen humanitarian support systems, promote resilience, and reduce reliance on negative coping mechanisms. The following strategies outline how youth are engaged and supported throughout the project: 1. Youth-Led Advocacy and Support System Strengthening: Youth are encouraged to lead advocacy efforts within their communities and with local authorities. Through training and support, they become active participants in humanitarian support systems, helping to ensure that interventions are responsive to the unique needs of their peers and community members. 2. Youth-Led Conflict and Tension Mitigation: Recognizing the importance of peaceful coexistence, the project equips youth with tools and training to lead community-based conflict resolution and tension mitigation efforts. These activities foster a safer, more cohesive environment and promote youth as peacebuilders within their communities. 3. Capacity Development for Self-Reliance and Resilience: To reduce negative coping mechanisms, the project focuses on building the individual capacities of youth. Motivational sessions and skill-building initiatives help develop their resilience and self-reliance, enabling them to contribute positively to their community’s stability. By fostering youth leadership and providing them with the skills needed to support their communities, this project not only empowers young individuals but also strengthens overall community resilience and cohesion.
