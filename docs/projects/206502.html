Project Summary

Conflict, sub-national violence and heavy seasonal flooding continues to cause internal displacement in South Sudan leaving 2.4 million of its population impacted through destruction of housing and loss of essential household goods. The complex operating environment combined with accessibility challenges indicates that in-kind provision of lifesaving supplies continues to be the most significant modality for emergency responses in the country.
Since 2011, IOM has been successfully managing the shelter and non-food items NFI (S-NFI) Core Pipeline., which is an essential support to the S-NFI Cluster in providing critical services to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) partners.
Under the HRP 2024, IOM will maintain its commitment to support the S-NFI Cluster Pipeline through the provision of key shelter and NFI supplies to benefit a total of 1,390,286 people in need. Procured supplies will be transported for prepositioning in strategic logistic hubs throughout the country, namely Bentiu, Bor, Malakal and Wau from which responding SNFI Cluster partners can access the supplies for distribution hence ensuring efficiency by contributing to the reduction of response time through moving the supplies closer to final distribution sites. The Pipeline system optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies. The distribution will be prioritized to directly benefit vulnerable people affected by displacement, conflict, natural disasters (such as the heavy seasonal rains and flooding), disease outbreak, famine/food insecurity, and GBV risks.

1. Needs

In 2024, approximately 2,138,902 million people in South Sudan are projected to struggle to meet their basic needs in SNFI due to the continued destruction of houses and loss of essential goods from the ongoing floods and conflict. The increment is attributed to the current economic crisis in the country, new displacements and ongoing floods and conflict, mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Upper Nile, Unity, part of Western Bahr el Ghazel and Western Equatoria States as well as the Sudan conflict. This presents an increase of 0.5 per cent from 2.4 million people who needed SNFI support in 2022, excluding the increasing number of returnees from the neighboring countries and the displaced people in the PoC and IDP sites. Of the total population in need of SNFI assistance, 58 per cent are IDPs, 33 per cent are returnees and 12 per cent are vulnerable host community members. The continued displacement and increased movement of people, combined with sustained flooding and economic crisis, have further depleted natural resources such as timber and grass, leading to competition for the limited available resources. The competition usually generates conflict and localized violence, leading to multiple displacements. The markets lack essential items, and sometimes where available the quality of goods is compromised, or the markets have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required items for a safe and dignified life.A lack of shelter presents health and protection risks and poses a negative impact on the economic situation of the affected households. Exposure to extreme weather conditions is linked to the increased morbidity rates, mainly due to malaria, easy transmission of airborne diseases as a result of congestion, and other risks associated with the lack of proper shelter. The lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who usually go to the bushes to collect the increasingly scarce construction materials, exposes them to GBV. The lack of privacy also prevents individuals from living a dignified life and engaging in income-generating activities. Continued displacement and relocation of thecommunities from high-risk areas exacerbate conflict related to housing, land and property ownership among the displaced, returnees and host communities. As a result of these compounding factors, the affected people have limited coping strategies. Some adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as engaging in exploitative work, commercial sex, stealing and living under trees or in open shelters, exposing them to health and protection risks. Timely provision of SNFI to at least 2,390,286 people in priority locations remains critical to enable them to live a dignified life.

2. Project activities

IOM core pipeline will provide critical S-NFI supplies to implementing partners so that the most vulnerable have improved access to emergency shelters and lifesaving non-food items. The following activities are proposed: - Procure emergency S-NFI supplies for the humanitarian response following assessed needs, targeting 834,172 individuals- Procurement of essential Core Pipeline SNFI supplies - Transport procured SNFI pipeline supplies to IOM logistic hubs outside Juba (Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek) and any other potential partner warehouses across the country for prepositioning of stocks- Prepositioning of Core Pipeline SNFI supplies at logistic warehouses.- Appropriate storage and management of the shelter and NFI emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu- Regular monitoring of SNFI core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management, and supplies utilization.- Process SNFI Cluster partners' requests for Core Pipeline SNFI supplies ensuring timely release of supplies to partners.- Conduct post- Post Distribution Monitoring of select SNFI partner response to gather beneficiary feedback on the quality of the items distributed, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of the response, and assess mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues- Undertake Core pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness, and coverage of the pipeline service.- Actively participate in operations working group, technical working groups, cluster meetings, and any other forum or gathering within the S-NFI and inter-cluster operations to ensure a coordinated approach in all S-NFI activities.
