Project Summary

This project consists of 3 components: 1- GBV Prevention and Risk Mitigation, 2-GBV Response and 3-GBV Coordination and Advocacy which will be implemented in 3 Women and Girl Safe Spaces (WGSS) in camps 5, 18, 25, four PHCC in camps 25, eight nutrition centers in camps 13, 14, 15, 16, 22 and two GBV service points in Ratnapalong and Holiapalong and two WGSS in Nhilla & Jalia Palong. Response activities will be implemented by IRC and prevention activities by 6 local partners 4 of which are women-led. Component 1 GBV Prevention. Result: Social Norms support women and girls’ rights: outcomes are that social norms support women and girls’ rights and condemn GBV. Girl Shine will focus on empowering adolescent girls to acquire skills to identify GBV, access services and assert their rights. Structured PSS for male and female caregivers will help to ensure a protective environment for adolescents. EA$E includes women’s economic empowerment interventions. It will create a pathway for income generation, enhance women’s access to financial resources, foster healthy dynamics within households and build women’s business skills to develop business plans. EMAP will address the root causes of GBV by engaging men and transforming men’s beliefs and attitudes towards gender and violence. SASA! Together will use a community mobilization approach to support communities to create positive and sustainable changes around norms underlying GBV. Community engagement and outreach will be conducted using integrated community outreach module to facilitate diverse women’s and girls’ access, including those with disabilities, to services in the WGSS. IRC will provide dignity kits to women and girls at risk including GBV survivors. Safety audits will be conducted including risk mitigation measures and GBV risk mapping, allowing the community to take the lead in creating a safe environment for women and girls. Component 2 GBV Responses. Result: GBV Services are safe and accessible Outcomes: 1. quality GBV services are accessible, well equipped and provide safe spaces for confidential care. 2. Female service providers have the attitudes, skills, training, and supervision support to provide competent care to GBV survivors; 3. GBV services are effectively planned, managed, resourced and operational. (i) GBV Case Management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and activities Through WGSS IRC will ensure availability and access to quality GBV case management, PSS services, and referrals delivered by frontline service providers trained by IRC according to GBV Case Management Guidelines. Sexual Reproductive Health (SRH) services will be available in 3 WGSS, including SRH clinics and information sessions delivered by midwives. IRC will develop capacity to provide case management services to male GBV survivors by ensuring Health-GBV integration in primary health centers. IRC will collect, store, and analyze case management data in-line with the information sharing protocol (ISP) using GBVIMS and GBVIMS+. Community volunteers (Community outreach members) will be used to link case management services to the community through information dissemination, training, and referrals. IRC will roll-out Women Rise, a structured PSS group curriculum run by GBV actors. A referral system will be maintained and disseminated within the community. IRC will support beneficiaries to use a complaint and feedback mechanism incorporating safeguarding. Service providers will receive training on GBV case management, basic PSS, and GBV core concepts to improve quality of care. IRC will adapt WGSS in settings responding to natural disasters from the onset of the emergency to relief and recovery phases. Component 3: Advocacy and Coordination IRC will raise funds and influence government policy and legislation. It will provide technical support to capacitate state actors, religious and community leaders to address GBV

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

GBV has increased at alarming rates since COVID owing to a combination of factors exacerbating pre-existing gender inequalities (1) These relate to loss of livelihoods (2), increased militarisation, and a failure to integrate GBV into emergency responses. This has led to a reduction in GBV response and prevention activities, resulting in (i) inadequate understanding of GBV risks - faced particularly by gender diverse people and those with disabilities - required to design and adapt programming to prevent, mitigate and appropriately respond to GBV cases, (ii) limited male engagement in GBV prevention activities (iii) absence of community-based activities to transform oppressive attitudes, behaviors and norms underlying GBV (iv) limited engagement with women community leaders, LGBTI rights organizations, working groups and networks to reach GBV survivors made vulnerable on the basis of sex, sexual orientation or gender identity as well as disability status. There has also been (v) severe disruptions to the availability of GBV case management services (vi) suspension of GBV awareness raising and training with stakeholders that affected the provision of quality essential services to women, girls and GBV survivors (3). Following COVID, an average of one in three women and girls screened by IRC staff reported being survivors of GBV (1). IPV emerged as the most common form reflecting the need for community-based GBV prevention initiatives. KIIs conducted with IRC staff in ‘22 suggest the vast majority of GBV against adolescent girls goes unreported. Child marriage rates in Bangladesh continue to be the highest in South Asia. 51.4% of girl children are married before 18 years (4). KIIs suggest high rates of GBV among women and girls with disabilities, despite the lack of reporting to service providers (1). This is explained by low levels of GBV awareness and lack of disability sensitive GBV services. According to IRC staff gang rapes of adolescent girls have taken place in the aftermath of COVID in camp and host settings. Camp settings lack safe public spaces and basic facilities such as toilets and water and food collection points which is contributing to the risk of GBV, particularly sexual violence (5) alongside vulnerability to PSEA (7). Adolescent girls and women heading households are habitually abused and denied resources by their in-laws who often evict from the family home, making them vulnerable to homelessness, sexual exploitation and rape. Women and girls’ risk of GBV is heightened by disasters particularly the aftermath of flooding and fire which the camps are prone to (6) Compared to prevalence and survivors’ complex needs, GBV services remain scarce and there are almost no disability-sensitive facilities in any setting. Funding to address GBV is hugely inadequate to meet existing needs and expand programming. Delays in the delivery of funds is hampering consistent and sustainable programming. There is now an urgent need to (i) strengthen primary prevention of GBV through community-based mobilization to address social norms underlying GBV (ii) strengthen the quality and coordination of services (iii) ensure women and girls have access to life-saving case management, psychosocial services, social networks, and livelihood opportunities. Finally, (iv) there is a need to strengthen collaboration with the government and local partners to mitigate the GBV risks and provide solutions to gender-specific vulnerabilities faced by women girls. (1) IRC GBV Shadow Pandemic Report 2022(2) GBVIMS Quarter 4 Fact Sheet 2022(3) Health Cluster report 2021(4) Age- and gender-based violence risks facing Rohingya and Bangladeshi adolescents in Cox’s Bazar 2020(5) Needs & Priorities of Rohingya refugees & Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017: What has Changed? 2022(6) IRC Safety Audit Report 2021 (7) PSEA Bangladesh Cox’s Bazar Data Collection 2022

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

It will support the ISCG, camp level and coordination platforms for GBV. It will strengthen GBV-SS to ensure intersectoral and interagency coordination is cascaded down from the programmatic to the implementation stage by leading the GBV-SS GBV Case Management Task Team and GBV-CP Case Management Integration Working Group. The project will build the capacity of women led actors to address GBV. It will provide technical support to CiCs, relevant ministries, the RRRC, APBn, legal and health actors, Mahjis, religious and community leaders to address GBV. To ensure predictability, continuity, service integration, quality assurance and to reduce service gaps and costs, the project will provide GBV prevention and response services in WGSS in camps 5, 18, 25, in PHCC in camps 2E, 6,11 25, in nutrition centers in camps 13, 14, 15, 16, 22 and in GBV service points in Ratnapalong and Holiapalong. It will phase out of camps 2E, 6 & 11 and include Nhilia and Jalia Palong where there are gaps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will focus on protection of diverse women, girls and GBV survivors by designing, implementing and monitoring all activities by considering protection risks and potential violations and ensuring that the protective impact of assistance is maximized. This project will prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment throughout the project cycle. The project will ensure integration with PRoL and Child Protection and with local partners. Particular attention will be dedicated to protect the affected population from Sexual Abuse and Exploitation. The project will ensure increased capacity to stay safe, exercise basic rights, and access essential services. The activities will enhance rights-based, reliable, and quality humanitarian programming that considers gender, age, sex, disability, specific needs, and cultural appropriateness throughout the project. 3.2 - Gender

The project will pay attention to the goal of promoting gender equality and will focus on mainstreamed, integrated and targeted actions that protect, empower and treat diverse women, girls and GBV survivors for the consequences of GBV. It will design, implement, monitor and evaluate GBV programming by collecting and analysing gender-disaggregated data, conducting gender analysis, (GA) and gender impact assessments, gender budgeting, gender awareness raising and gender equality training. Gender analysis will provide information on the different needs, capacities and coping strategies of diverse women and girls. It will do this by examining and contextualising gender relations including power, roles and responsibilities between women, girls, boys and men in the different settings. This will inform the project’s commitment to Minimum Standards for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response as well as its approaches to gender equality in recruitment, safeguarding, partnerships, localisa

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

To ensure age appropriate responses, IRC will conduct service mapping and develop an age, disability and gender-sensitive plan to address availability and capacity gaps. It will ensure PSS and referral mechanisms are age, disability and gender-sensitive. It will develop targeted messaging and engage older women in GBV prevention activities and will ensure PSS and empowerment activities are accessible and adapted and that older women are represented on forums and support groups. IRC will provide orientation within communities on what is PSEA and how to report it. IRC will integrate PSEA and its Safeguarding Policy throughout every component and phase of the program cycle. It will conduct PSEA Assessments of IRC Implementing Partners to assess their PSEA policy and institutional and individual capacities to report and investigate and PSEA. IRC will deliver training to all staff and local partners associated with this project on PSEA/SH.

3.4 - Diversity

IRC will ensure diversity-inclusive policy and practice and actively promote accessibility participation and empowerment of women and girls with disabilities, LGBTI, older women and from religious and ethnic minorities. In program design IRC will integrate findings from diversity sensitive GBV analysis and consultations with PWD and GDP groups to understand and address GBV experiences, needs and risks, draw attention to gaps, identify strategies for programs to be more responsive to diverse women and girls’ needs. For example it will ensure safe and dignified access to GBV services for women and girls with disabilities by conducting disability sensitive service mapping and developing a plan to address critical gaps; support GBV service providers with disability inclusive case management training that is tailored and specific; monitor service access. Quotas will ensure representation of diverse women and girls in support groups and forums such as disability inclusive DRR.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will link with the Common Feedback Platform (CFP) mechanism, follow the technical standards for feedback referral and strengthen sharing referrals to link with the CFP. It will consult with women and girls on GBV assessments and feedback and complaints mechanisms and ensure AAP-related questions in templates; gather community voices for advocacy; budget for AAP activities; assess SEA risks; ensure opportunities to register complaints and feedback; share info on where and how to access services; GBV referral pathways are updated and disseminated; orient IRC and IP staff on AAP-related actions and principles, including on PSEA; ensure the implementation of safeguarding policies; establish accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms and support clients to use them; include AAP indicators in results framework. AAP reactive channels include suggestion/complain box, SMS and hotline number, proactive: client feedback survey, open feedback channels: FDGs
