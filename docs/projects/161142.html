Project Summary

UNH is a dual mandated organization that provides development & emergency response. Based on our experience in emergency response, developed this project following consultative meetings with the respective state MOH, CHDs, local authority, and affected population including elderly and persons with disability.
UNH’s Humanitarian Respond Plan for 2020 targets Pibor, Tonj North, Magwi and Terekeka targeting Host communities, IDPs, IDP returnees, Refugee returnees in place of origin and Refugee returnees in IDP. These counties except Magwi are inhabited mainly by pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, inter-clan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility and humanitarian crisis.
Only 15 (41%) health facilities out 36 in Pibor are supported by UNICEF leaving most of the health facilities nonfunctional, vulnerable population in remote areas have no access to lifesaving health services. From PHS conducted in Pibor in March 2019, only 2 health facilities provide inpatient care, no CEmONC, limited BEmONC, 12% EPI coverage (WHO2018 report). Measles transmission has persisted in the county despite reactive campaign conducted in February and mopped up in September/October 2019. The county is now flooded, health facilities affected, supplies damaged, 146,057 individuals and 29,603 HHs displaced and most have lost their livelihoods.
In Tonj North, World Vision under HPF3 supports 13 health facilities out of 18. Measles outbreak has been controlled however active measles transmission ongoing in the neighboring Tonj South, proximity and mobile nature of the community pose a high risk of taking the disease to Tonj North. The county is flooded, some of the health facilities affected, communities cut off from accessing essential lifesaving healthcare.
In Terekeka, more than 20% of health facilities are non-functional, no hospital/mobile services, functional health facilities provide moderate services (September2019 HSF Bulletin).
Only 24 health facilities out of total 41 in Magwi County are supported by HFP3, 8 out of 17 not supported are vandalized from the 2019 armed conflict. This leaves most of the returnees and IDPs and vulnerable host communities without lifesaving health care services. The County is at high risk for EVD, porous borders where more than 1,000 people cross per day from Uganda/DRC.
IPC report released on September 11, 2019 puts Pibor and Terekeka at IPC 4, Tonj North and Magwi in IPC 3 for August. Couple with poor health indicators the food insecurity in these counties will drive malnutrition rate, morbidity and mortality if emergency health care services not supported.
UNH has strong networks in these proposed counties and will work with respective CHDs, health/nutrition/FSL/Protection/WASH partners to ensure synergies. The main objective of this project is to reduce excess morbidity/mortality of epidemic prone diseases, Improve access/scale up response to integrated essential health care services and Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV/disabled/mental health disorders.
To achieve these objectives, UNH will require 499,980 USD in 2020, this budget is aligned with health cluster’s strategy on the cost per beneficiaries ($60). This resources will support 8 facilities based on the 3 clinical packages:
• 4 mobile clinics in Terekeka, Pibor and Tonj North
• 3 PHCUs in Terekeka, Magwi and Tonj North
• 1 PHCC in Magwi
These facilities will serve a total of 8,333 persons (Female 4,250, Male 4,083); Terekeka 1,835, Magwi 3,054, Pibor 1,375 and Tonj North 2,069. The whole community in the catchment areas will benefit from the response indirectly.

1 - Needs

October 2019 Health Cluster report (PIN), estimated 3,296,823 South Sudanese will need humanitarian healthcare in 2020, population targeted for response 2,049,990 (62.18%). UNH will response in Pibor, Tonj North, Magwi and Terekeka targeting Host communities, IDPs, IDP returnees, Refugee returnees in place of origin and Refugee returnees in IDP. The estimated people in need of emergency healthcare in Terekeka, Magwi, Pibor and Tonj North are 36,719, 104,602, 21,236 and 50,894 respectively, number targeted for 2020 response are 22,933, 80,379, 13,340 and 32,332 respectively.Inhabitants of Pibor, Tonj North and Terekeka are mainly pastoralists, prone to conflicts from cattle raiding, inter-clan/revenge attack resulting to insecurity, displacement, inaccessibility and humanitarian crisis. People in Magwi are farmers, conflict arises due to pastoralists from other states grazing cattle in their farmland.Health needs of Terekeka, Magwi, Pibor and Tonj North enormous, complicated with contracted economy, conflicts, inaccessibility, lack of water quality control, infection prevention/control measures, expansive malnutrition and threat of disease outbreaks. Pibor County has 36 health facilities, 15(41%) supported through UNICEF (Pibor CHD July2019 Report) leaving most of the HFs nonfunctional, vulnerable population in remote areas have no access to lifesaving healthcare. From Public Health Situation Analysis conducted in Pibor in March 2019, only 2 health facilities provide inpatient care, no CEmONC, limited BEmONC, 12% EPI coverage (WHO2018 report). Measles transmission has persisted in the county, outbreak declared in February 2019, reactive campaign conducted in March; 13,882 (30%) children vaccinated (Pibor Reactive Measles Campaign Report), mopped up done in September- October 2019. IDSR report (week40, 2019) 2,054 measles cases line listed with 9 deaths. The county is now flooded, water level continues to rise, most of the health facilities affected, supplies damaged, 146,057 individuals and 29,603 HHs affected (Boma State RRC/IRNA October report). Pibor has a significant number of refugee returnees in place of origin-1,261, refugee returnees in IDP like- 2,633 mainly from Ethiopia. Tonj North, World Vision under HPF3 supports 13 health facilities out of 18 (Tonj North CHD July2019 Report). Measles outbreak declared in February 2019, campaign conducted, scale up routine EPI services recommended. Active measles transmission ongoing in the neighboring Tonj South (41 cases line listed- IDSR report week 39), proximity and mobile nature of the community pose a risk of taking the disease to Tonj North. The county is flooded, some of the communities cut off from accessing healthcare. Terekeka, 20% of health facilities are non-functional, no hospital/mobile services, functional health facilities provide moderate services (September2019 HSF Bulletin). Most of the nonfunctional HFs are in hard to reach areas increasing the vulnerability. Magwi County, only 24 health facilities supported by HFP3 out of 41; 8 out of 17 not supported were vandalized during 2019 armed conflict (Magwi CHD October2019 report). County at high risk for EVD, porous borders where more than 1,000 people cross per day from Uganda/DRC. Magwi hosts IDP (18,789), refugee returnees in place of origin (17,908), refugee returnees like IDP (18,789). Spontaneously refugees are returning from Uganda (County RRC report). Most of these returnees lack access to lifesaving health care services and with looming EVD in DRC putting the area at high risk. IPC report released on September 11, 2019 puts Pibor & Terekeka at IPC 4, Tonj North & Magwi in IPC 3 for August. Pibor, Terekeka and Tonj North expected to be in IPC 3 while Magwi IPC 2 from September- December and January- April 2020 Pibor in IPC 4 while Magwi, Tonj North and Terekeka IPC 3. Couple with poor health indicators the food insecurity in these counties will drive malnutrition rate, morbidity/mortality.

2 - Activities

UNH proposes to implement the following activities in 2020;• Implement emergency preparedness and contingency planning for epidemic prone diseases, Outbreak investigations and case management for selected diseases.• Provide Immunization at mobile and static facilities against vaccine preventable diseases.• Strengthen IPC in our health facilities (3 PHCUs and 1 PHCC).• Conduct community sensitization and disseminate IEC materials. • Implement disease surveillance and outbreak response/EWARS and IDSR activities. • Conduct training for health care workers on EPI (cold chain management and handling of vaccines), BeMONC (focus on skilled birth attendants), and CMR and MHPSS services to build the capacity of UNH and CHD health care workers. • Participate in Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) that will be conducted in UNH operational areas in 2020. • Participate in the training on Rationale use of essential health care commodities.• Strengthen and support static integrated health facilities (1 PHCC and 3 PHCUs).• Establish and support mobile response (4 mobile units). • Provide management for SAM children with medical complication in our primary healthcare center. • Integrate WASH response at all our run health facilities. • Provide BeMONC services in our primary health center. • Provide outpatient consultations at the mobile response and static health facilities. • Coordinate with core pipeline managers to provide emergency kits (WHO Interagency emergency kits and UNFPA Reproductive Health Commodities including Rape kits) to support our mobile and static facilities. • Provide MHPSS services in all our responses. • Provide CMR services in our health facilities and enhance referral to safe houses where necessary. • Support the Surveillance System for Attacks (SSA) on Health Care and provide regular report on the attack.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Most of the Internal Displaced Persons are integrated into the host communities, they are the most vulnerable groups, are 100% targeted for response. UNH had consulted the IDPs before the development of this projects, their needs are factored into this project, and they will be involved in the implementation phase- all stages of project circle. Data of the beneficiaries served will be disaggregated by beneficiaries types (host communities, IDPs, IDP returnees, refugee returnees in place of origin and refugee returnees in IDP like situation), by sex and age groups. Counties like Pibor, Terekeka and Tonj North are prone to conflict due to cattle raiding and flooding resulting to displacement. In Such situation the displaced affected communities will be followed and served through mobile clinics without discrimination. Magwi County experiences a lot of returnees (IDP returnees, Refugee returnees in place of origin and Refugee returnees in IDP-like situation) mainly from Uganda. Such people will be tracked and served with mobile services.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The proposed intervention is in line with South Sudan Ministry of Health. Before developing this proposal, UNH had a number of consultative meetings with state/county authorities and the communities (host, IDPs and returnees of different gender and age groups and minority group- people with disability and elderly). So the outline activities are in line with Ministry of Health Policy (Basic Package of Health and Nutrition Services and Boma Health Initiative) and community needs, they will continue to be involved in all the stages of the project circle so that they own the project which will enhance sustainability.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

UNH is a national NGO familiar with the country context, works closely with Ministry of Health and has aligned this project with the government objectives and National Health Cluster objectives as well as HRP strategic objectives . We will continue to work with Ministry of Health and the UN agencies (WHO, UNPFA, UNICEF and UNHCR) to ensure vulnerable population is reached and protected.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The security situation in the proposed geographical locations are relatively calm following the signing of Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan. All the counties have IDPs, IDP returnees; then refugee returnees in place of origin and refugee returnees in IDP like situation are in Magwi and Pibor Counties. Our strategy is to provide essential services to all these categories without discrimination. The target beneficiaries will be in position to access the emergency health care services without hindrance since the strategy is to bring services closer to the target population. However there is still uncertainty if the transition government of national unity not formed on November 12, 2019, in case of any further confrontation then displacement will continue creating humanitarian crisis. UNH approach is to use the basic philosophy of extension which states; start where the people are, with what they have to help themselves which is easier and friendly to both the people (host communities, IDPs and Returnees) and the environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

UNH works closely with the Ministry of Health (MoH) at National and state levels. We will work and coordinate the response with the respective state and county authorities. 80% of our staffs implementing UNH project in the fields are from the locality, we will retain and capacitate them and recruit more from the locality depending on the availability of the qualified personnel. We will continue to capacitate them through supportive supervisions, mentorship and refresher trainings. These staffs will be in position to continue to provide services if UNH exits from the areas at the end of the project. UNH will encourage and invite participants from the County Health Departments (CHDs) where applicable for training so as to build their capacity.Integration, UNH will use the MOH harmonized incentive and engage the CHD authority to integrate the staffs into government Payroll so that at the end of the project all the staffs will be in government payroll and be in position to continue providing healthcare services. UNH conducts quarterly participatory learning meetings involving community leaders/members, CHDs and political wing to discuss progress and challenges and jointly decide on the way forward. We will continue doing this and capacitate the CHDs to take over the project and manage with capacitated local staff. With the political will from the State MoH provided that the political situation normalizes it will be possible for the CHDs to fully run health facilities in these Counties by 2021.UNH will work together with other health implementing partners in the counties. Will collaborate and handover some of the components to the developmental health partners; - LiveWell and Care International in Pibor, World Vision in Tonj North, HealthNet TPO in Terekeka and Cordaid/Health Link in Magwi which will ensure continuity of services when CHDs not be able to fully manage the services.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

UNH is health partner with a number of health care professionals. We will integrate basic messages on GBV and child protection in our awareness messages. We will collaborate and work closely with protection partners on the ground to ensure synergies and enhance referral. We will also work with other health partners and refer survivors of SGBV/GBV to their facilities where we don’t provide comprehensive care (at mobile and PHCU levels).We have a baseline data and will conduct rapid needs assessment before launching of the project. Vulnerable targeted beneficiaries will be our priority, they will be supported under this project, will train and engage the community leaders to support our strategy of taking services the project targeted beneficiaries. During the launching the communities will be sensitized to know the category of people targeted by this project. This will mitigate the risk of supporting individuals who have just left their place and moved in to get the assistance.There are instances where county authority would push an emergency responder to response in the areas of their interest (villages where they come from) not based on the needs. To mitigate such external interference, we will brief and share the project documents with the state and county authorities and let them know the target beneficiaries. Our team being South Sudanese understand the context well and based on UNH code of contact will work with the local authorities transparently to reach to the target beneficiaries. Any external forces interfering with the beneficiaries selection will be reported and action taken appropriately.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

UNH approach of operation is to provide holistic care- focuses on a person as a whole by providing medical/physical, emotional, economic and social support. We had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and the affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups and minority groups- elderly and people with disability) of Pibor, Magwi, Tonj North and Terekeka, needs of each groups of people incorporated into this project so that no activity will be carried out in isolation or in a fragmented manner. UNH will launch the project so that respective State MoH, County Health Departments and local authorities at Payam/Boma levels, community leaders, affected populations and all stakeholders are informed and actively involved, documents will be shared and meeting will be conducted with community leaders and affected population, they will be informed about the project. They will actively participate in the implementation phase, monitoring/evaluation, monthly and quarterly review/feedback meetings where affected persons give feedback on the quality of the service delivery. UNH will strengthen the existing and form additional local management and community committees, capacitate them to monitor the project and provide feedback. We have accountability and feedback mechanism in place, will ensure the system is robust, confidential and safe. We will raise awareness and inform the beneficiaries and the affected persons to know their rights and entitlements. Will install suggestion boxes in all the operation areas and provide email address where literate can provide feedback whereas the illiterate and people with disability can provide feedback through their community leaders, chiefs and county relief and rehabilitation commissions and also during monthly/quarterly feedback meetings at community level. The feedbacks received will be analyzed and use to improve our strategies on service delivery and other cross-cutting issues.
