Project Summary

According to HNO 2024, over 14.9 million of the Syrian population are estimated to be in need of life-saving primary care health assistance, including 5.33 million IDPs, 3.3 million children <5 years including 532,595 live births expected, 6.6 million women of reproductive age, 1.2 elderly people and 2.6 million persons with disabilities. A key concern is the limited access to PHC services, mainly antenatal & child health services, as well as care for NCDs. Less than 65% of PHCs are fully functional. In 2023, DPT3 immunization coverage increased from 65% to 71%, but 47 districts remain below the 80% threshold. In 2024, the EPI will focus on catch-up campaigns for zero-dose children, outreach in high-risk districts, and broader development initiatives supported by GAVI and BHA funding, including the BCU campaign.
In 2024, WHO SYRIA will:
-Procure of lifesaving supplies & equipment
-Standardize guidelines developed
-Building HCW capacity
-Conduct assessments & surveys
-Support integrated outreach and referral services
-Enhance & expand IMCI & newborn care at home coverage
-Strengthen HIS
-Monitor quality and promote improvement of service delivery
-Support printing of IEC materials
-Conduct basic restoration of priority PHCs
In NWS, out of the 5.1M of population 3.4M are IDPs, and close to 2M people, are living in camps while an estimated 3.9M people living is subdistricts of severity 4 or 5 are in need of health care services and dependent on XB operations. The current crisis is devastating: disease outbreaks were compounded by the earthquake (Feb 2023), leading to >15K deaths & injuries, increasing the demand on an already fragmented and under-resourced health system. WHO’s Health Needs Assessment for NWS points to a critical need for medical equipment, essential medications, and infrastructure support, as well as improved HIS and coordination.
Through a coordinated plan involving NGOs/donors, WHO will gradually reduce its direct service delivery contracts moving towards a last-resort approach, while aiming to mitigate potential consequences of reduced direct operational presence in NWS. In 2024 WHO will:
-Lead TWGs, including mapping services & conducting prioritization
-Monitor the implementation of the EHSP, including integration of vertical programs (e.g EPI)
-Coordinate with UNICEF within the SIG mechanism to achieve & maintain at least 77% vax coverage for all antigens at all national/SD level, with a drop-out rate of <10%
-Develop a capacity-building plan that includes a needs assessment, supportive supervision & mentoring
-Generate public health evidence through assessments
-Provide essential medicines & medical supplies
-Support all hazards preparedness & response
-Advocate for/provide support to facilitate handover of last-resort support to iNGOs while addressing critical gaps
WHO utilizes the health sector PRA to ensure protection of the most vulnerable within the delivery of its services, including health care workers themselves. Additionally, both hubs have a dedicated PSEA focal point and have instituted a pillar-based approach to PSEA to ensure awareness AND response capacity within WHO and its implementing partners. These activities are further overseen by regional focal points. WHO also pursues and integrated approach by aligning GBV, PSEA and MHPSS to enable comprehensive response capacity. Finally, both hubs have developed and regularly update a risk matrix with specified mitigation measures. Business continuity plans are updated quarterly and WHO global has also rolled out readiness checklists to ensure offices are ready to respond to emergencies.
