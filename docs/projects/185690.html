Project Summary

AMAN (Association for Mass Advancement Network) has developed the project following objectives to Improve equitable access to and utilization of essential primary and secondary health services for Rohingya refugees and targeted host community members; prevent and respond to outbreaks of communicable disease and other hazards, including risks during the monsoon and cyclone seasons and promote health and wellbeing at individual and community level.
The project has been developed with the guidelines from health sector template to operate Health Post with specialized services such as Pathology at refugee Camp 16 & 10 to cover 90,742 targeted beneficiaries in the year 2022. The estimated total budget for the project is US$ 319,973. The major activities are Curative care; Maternal and newborn health; Child Health; Adolescent friendly health; Family Planning counselling; Gender-based violence; Nutrition; Communicable disease /non-Communicable disease; Mental Health and Psychosocial Services; Referral; Laboratory; Pharmacy; Health waste management and IPC; Community Health; Information management system. All the services will be gender friendly and disability inclusive manner.
Gender responsive and people with disability and elderly people inclusion will be considered when and where applicable. Selection of beneficiaries will be followed “Do no Harm “procedures. Systematic process of program Monitoring using participatory methods is what AMAN will do to deliver the project and to ensure beneficiaries inclusion in the project design and implementation. Before intervention any projects, AMAN will conduct baseline survey, FGD, KII, etc. Collect and analyze and use disaggregated data and analysis on gender, age and diversity and equally consult with women, girls and marginalized groups, including during assessments and in the overall response monitoring.
AMAN has been running a Health Post (Health and Diagnostic Centre approves as a Specialized Health Post) and experienced to run more Health Post at Camps. AMAN in collaboration with health sector will perform the assigned job successfully.

1 - Identified Needs

In Bangladesh overall, quarter two was characterized by a surge in COVID-19 infections. Similarly, a significant surge in infections was recorded in the Rohingya refugee/Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMN) camps. Among the Rohingya refugees, 1,368 (47 percent-female, 53 percent-male) new infections were recorded during the reporting period, compared to 74 in the first quarter. Testing rate increased by 29 percent due to an increase in testing sites and use of Antigen Rapid Diagnostic Tests (Ag-RDT) in some facilities. Health sector partners, at the request of government, scaled up operational capacity at the 11 Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Centres (SARI ITCs) in the refugee camps. They activated 87 additional COVID-19 case management beds, bringing the total to 572 beds by the end of June. In addition, approximately 400 additional standby beds were available to be activated based on epidemiological indications. A decline in Out Patient Department (OPD) consultation among Rohingya refugees and host communities was recorded towards the end of the quarter. Facility-based delivery rate sustained at 67 percent in June 2021. Month-by-month, utilization slightly declined, most likely due to movement restrictions related to COVID-19 control measures ref. to Health Sector Bulletin # 15. The Health Sector has identified critical gaps in comprehensive emergency obstetric care; new-born care, family planning provision, particularly long-acting methods; availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/ intimate partner violence services; and first line support for gender-based violence survivors. Risks for communicable disease persist due to living conditions in the camps. However, Capacities for non-Communicable disease management, laboratory diagnostics are not adequate to the needs. The proposed project needs Health consultations for Rohingya refugees and host community members (Sex disaggregated), family planning for Rohingya women of reproductive age, consultations for clinical mental health and focused psychosocial support, increase of delivery base facility, doorstep and community base counselling, perinatal care and psychosocial counselling and service for disability inclusion as a whole.Ref. to JMSNA October 2021 that, Roughly 01 in 10 individuals reported having required treatment and almost 09 in 10 individuals having needed treatment reported to seek it at a clinic. The proportion of seeking treatment at an NGO clinic in 2021 has increased slightly compared to 2020, indicating a slight reversal of the negative impacts of the COVID-19 on health-seeking behavior. A positive trend with increasing facility-based deliveries over time was noted. However, the MSNA results indicated that over half of individuals below 02 years old were born at home, in particular in households that have arrived more recently and are less educated. SAG members posed questions as to the sampling strategy and noted that the largely male response rate may have played a role in the results differing from the census-based results that are obtained through the community health workers, which indicate approximately 70% facility based deliveries. Households with persons with disabilities were more likely to report requiring treatment and expecting challenges in accessing that treatment than households without persons with disabilities. At the same time, among households without unmet needs, a significant proportion of households with persons with disabilities reported having adopted negative coping strategies to meet their needs than households without persons with disabilities. The vast majority of individuals noted health facilities were accessible within 30 minutes through their conventional means of transport (>95%).
