Project Summary

10 years of conﬂict in Syria has not only led to destruction and reduced functionality of infrastructure, but also affected socio-economic patterns, and diminished Syria’s population’s ability to sustain living standards, to beneﬁt from decent and gainful livelihood opportunities and to access essential services, in addition to COVID19, which add a further burden on Syrian communities. Consequently, the protracted nature of the crisis has put an additional strain on social cohesion and community security.
Needs assessments through various sources indicate that currently PIN for ERL Support increased from 9.9 M to 11.6 M, due to the growing need for job opportunities after the massive loss of livelihoods that the crisis and COVID-19 pandemic resulted in, in addition to the increased pressure on essential services and infrastructure.
Based on that, the ERL sector’s objective framework needs, have been categorized under the four interrelated components: Scaling up interventions to contain the impact of COVID-19 crisis and economic deterioration, infrastructure, improving access to basic and social services, promoting social cohesion and support PWD through working for and with communities.
Through the area-based approach, UN-Habitat aims to boost sustainable access to basic and social services to displaced, host and returnee populations in selected neighbourhoods in Rural Damascus, Daraa, Deir-Ezzor, Hama and Aleppo. By doing so, the project will achieve an immediate response to COVID-19 affected communities by creating a source of livelihood, in addition to promoting social cohesion among different affected communities
In practice, the approach consists of the rehabilitation of public spaces, roads, sidewalks, and debris removal whenever needed in damage-affected neighbourhoods, which are prioritized and developed through ‘Neighbourhood Action Plans”, a municipality-led program supported by UN-HABITAT. In addition to that, the proposal is concerned as a COVID-19 response plan about creating a healthy market and craft areas, where it will contribute to a continuity of business and livelihood activities, while accommodating required COVID measures. The holistic notion is to serve conflict-affected, vulnerable communities within the urban areas through improvement of essential infrastructure, facilities and related services, under the objective of economically reviving neighbourhoods.

4 - Needs

Based on ERL estimates, 11.6 M PIN need humanitarian assistance to make their houses habitable, restore accessibility and safety especially in newly accessible areas, e.g. through debris removal, and restore functionality of basic WASH, electricity and related services.Furthermore, priority is given to support the social cohesion and community security, in addition to support to PWD and female headed households.Essential services provision is needed to facilitate socio-economic recovery not only for 6.5 M IDPs, but also for host communities and spontaneous returnees.According to sector estimates, more than half of 5.9 M potential members of the workforce (18 - 65 years) lack access to sustained employment, in addition to severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. One of the consequences is high likelihood of youth to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as joining extremist organizations, crime, traﬃcking etc. Prolonged conﬂict and COVID-19 have weakened social fabric in ways which equally affect host communities and IDPs. This situation can be further aggravated by increasing pressures posed by returnee dynamics, adding strain to already overburdened systems. Besides the criteria in HRP, the selection of targeted locations is based on: *Being in line with the ERL Sector and HRP 2021 strategic priorities. *Being priority locations identiﬁed through detailed ﬁeld assessments and consultants with local experts and municipalities.*Complementarity with ongoing projects of UNH*High numbers of PIN including vulnerable and expected returnees*High complementarity with area-based interventions in of UNH in WASH, Protection, and Shelter

5 - Implementation of activities

In the governorates and sub-districts mentioned above, the project will implement the following activity lines: • Restoration of and access to basic infrastructure: 1. Cleaning the neighbourhood from debris and waste and sterilizing the roads and streets. 2. Rehabilitation of roads and footpaths.3. Rehabilitation of public and open spaces.4. Provide streetlighting. 5. Creating a gender friendly learning open spaces for women, youth and children to conduct awareness campaigns and sessions. • Restoration of markets streets: 1. Market rehabilitation.2. Providing with Handwashing stations at market entrances, where it is possible 3. Cleaning market areas and streets.4. Providing street lighting. • Improve effective local public services delivery: 1. Support the communities at the neighbourhoods’ level to identify the specific needs in terms of rapid COVID risks’ assessment and response plans with focus on ERL 2. Provision of technical equipment to monitor the humanitarian support being provided. Following the respective ministries’ procedural requirements, projects will be implemented through local resources – communities, municipal technical departments, private sector entities, and workforce engaged from affected populations, in particular unemployed youth will be an important to provide short-term employment opportunities through waste management project in a specific neighbourhoods, in which can be led by contractors or IPs. Also, engaging community in identifying their real needs and contributing in the design of opens spaces and supervise the implementation will contribute in fostering social cohesion.
