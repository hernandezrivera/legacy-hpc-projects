Project Summary

Medair’s proposed Intervention will provide life-saving WASH interventions at community and household-levels to 145,350 target beneficiaries (excluding overlap) through rehabilitation of water and sewage networks and by providing water storage solutions, building on the experience, expertise and relationships from its previous project cycles to reach the most vulnerable and in-need, and with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme.

Needs

Needs in Syria remain significant, with 15.5 million PIN of WASH assistance (draft HNO 2019). Water and sewage infrastructure have been severely damaged in especially Hard to Reach areas or newly accessible locations in Medair’s target governorates due to sustained hostilities. Of the communities assessed by Medair, >50% do not meet Sphere standards for safe drinking water (15L/p/d) and of those that do, most do not meet minimum Syrian national standard of 35-40L/p/d, while the average water consumption of people across Syria is 71 L/p/d. Population movement to locations with intact infrastructure and better humanitarian conditions has led to the overburdening of host communities with competition for safe water supply, necessitating the reliance of the already vulnerable population on financially burdensome and potentially unreliable alternatives. Dar’a is newly accessible and the inter sector severity is high (average of 5). Medair’s assessment in Ash Shajara district showed many HHWTs were damaged and water infrastructure damaged as a result of the crisis.Homs+Hama: Targeted areas are considered HTR and only came under GoS control early 2018. The HNO inter-sector severity is 6 with high needs reported by the WASH sub-sector. Medair assessments showed people in Grandad and Zmeimer (N. Homs) have no access to water from the network and people receive only 10L/p/d in certain neighbourhoods of Ar-Rastan.Rural Damascus: Areas are accessible; however the number of PIN is high. A Medair assessment showed 24,000 beneficiaries in need of water supply in Khan AlShien, currently not receiving any water. In Aleppo water specific needs are existing in different areas like Shaikh Saeed (Jebel Saman), Rasm Haram and Zaarayah (Al Bab) with a HNO of 4. Medair conducted assessments in these areas and found over 32,000 beneficiaries with no or limited access to water from the network (<15L/p/d). Safe water storage solutions for households is an important basic need, without which particularly vulnerable households will continue to resort to high risk water storage options which increase the likelihood of contamination, predisposing them to an additional burden of morbidity. In areas like Dar’a people are returning to damaged dwellings that are already connected to the water system. With most of the existing HHWTs damaged as a result of the crisis and households receiving water only every 4 or more days, water storage solutions are needed. In other areas (i.e. parts of E. Ghouta), water networks have been severely damaged and repair is beyond the scope of Medair projects. Temporary solutions are needed to provide safe water and decrease the financial burden on families depending on water trucking from unknown, potentially unsafe water sources.Medair has identified that the expertise for Operation and Monitoring of boreholes by the local Water Establishment Unit is in need of improvement for impact and sustainability of its interventions. Additionally, communities at large require improved awareness of water conservation, wastage of water being a dire combination with the gradual depletion of water resources in-country. Sewage networks have been severely damaged in Deir-ez-Zor. MoWR and the local authorities in Deir-ez-Zor city (where IDPs and returnees make up >60% of the population of more than 227,000 as of December 2018 according to government authorities, and where 65% of the population are considered PIN), confirm rehabilitation of the damaged sewage infrastructure as one of their main priorities in addressing public health concerns. Damage to the sewage network has resulted in flooding of houses or streets with wastewater, a breeding ground for disease-transmitting vectors. Health surveillance sites in Deir-ez-Zor report a disproportionately high communicable disease burden. High communicable disease burden. High rates of acute diarrhoea, dysentery and acute jaundice cases are likely to be linked to poor sanitation (EWARN, Nov 18)

Implementation of activities

9 rehabilitation projects will be undertaken for existing water system infrastructure. This will include upgrade/installation of pumping lines and pumps, equipping wells, replacing generators, distributions lines and installing new components for safe drinking water systems. To ensure the impact of the water system rehabilitation extends to those who do not have the means to store water, field staff will assess the need for additional support with water storage solutions at household level for up to a total of 350 of the most vulnerable households across all project sites. Medair will distribute and install Household Water Tanks (HHWT), particularly where vulnerabilities such as Female Headed Households, the elderly and People Living With Mobility Impairment (PLWMI) exist. In areas of water network rehabilitation, Medair will also train local partners of the Water Economic Units on Standard Operation Procedures and train key community leaders on the promotion of beneficial water resource conservation and preservation of water quality to increase the impact and sustainability of the programme. 2 sewage-network-rehabilitation is planned and one is for Deir-ez-Zor city to prevent further discharge of raw sewage into the open, particularly in and surrounding residential dwellings, and to restore the previous sewage network and connectivity to the main sewage line.For emergency response, Medair responds to areas that are newly accessible or with recent or sudden population movement with one of two main first-line interventions. In communities either without functioning water infrastructure or where the water system does not provide safe water, Medair will introduce community water tanks, distribute household jerry cans and (working with the Water Establishment Unit to control and monitor) ensure safe water trucking as a temporary solution, aiming to provide 40L/d/HH (for drinking and cooking only, per Sphere, based on average household size), targeting 2,400 beneficiaries. In communities that have existing, functional water systems with direct connections to houses, but household water tanks have been destroyed as result of the conflict. Medair will assist the most vulnerable households of these communities with a targeted distribution of 1,010 household water tanks.In summary: 9 water network rehabilitations targeting 112,500 beneficiaries;2 sewage network rehabilitations targeting 24,000 beneficiaries;1,650 HHWTs distributed (to 6,800 beneficiaries) of which 350 HHWTs in areas of water network rehab and 1,010 HHWTs as emergency distributions;Provision of Community Water Tanks for a total of 2,400 beneficiaries.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS:- There is no significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria and the GoS continue to hold control over recently retaken governorates- The security situation remains stable enough to allow project and support functions to be set up and to continue- Market systems remain relatively stable- There is no excessive fluctuations affecting the value of the Syrian Pound and any inflation/deflation will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD)- Medair is able to recruit adequate numbers of appropriately qualified staff and/or contractors- Acceptance and participation of authorities, local leaders and communities during interventions- Continued cooperation and coordination among relevant authorities and partners- Equipment in specifications listed by Ministries will be available at the time of procurement- No major deterioration in logistical conditions and the supply chain for essential supplies remains reliable and necessary goods are approved for transportRISKS: - Medair cannot secure approvals from ministries within the timeline outlined in the work plan, delaying project delivery- Delays to the visa approval process which can cause delays to project implementation.- Desperation of beneficiaries with level of support or feeling of being missed out resulting in disagreement or physical protest- Significant injury to team members within Syria either due to targeting or "wrong place, wrong time"- Central Bank imposes regulations or restrictions which stop or markedly constrain Medair’s ability to maintain adequate cash flow to pay programme expenses- Medair staff or contractors submit fraudulent expenses or do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines leading to a loss of financial resource.MITIGATING MEASURES:- Conduct clear, robust, regular context analysis as part of security management- Continue with keeping security planning up to date and training a crisis management team for each location - Daily, careful monitoring of security situation and trends (INSO, UNDSS, National Staff etc.) including close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues and a clear briefing to all staff and volunteers that safety comes first- Remain flexible in project locations if access to a particular community or programme site is temporarily unavailable access is refused to certain locations - Continue to use the Medair Regional Office as a base for staff to work remotely from whilst awaiting visa approval.- Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders- Balance activities for IDPs, returnees and host population to avoid unnecessary tensions, noting that lead factor for beneficiary selection is vulnerability not status- Complete training for all new staff on procurement guidelines, conduct systematic internal/external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines and check for any fraudulent activities.
