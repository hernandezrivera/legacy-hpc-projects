Project Summary

The action aims to provide emergency assistance in 2019 to 54,000 vulnerable pastoral and agro-pastoral men, women, boys and girls in Gedo Region (Beled Xaawo, Garbaharey, Elwak, Luuq, Dollow and Baardhere Districts) to protect their livestock assets and help them recover from the impacts of the 2016-2017 drought. Emergency livestock treatments will target mainly 400,000 core breeding, lactating and replacement stock for vulnerable households. This will prevent death in the herd and facilitate the rebuilding of herds through optimal utilisation of the improved pasture and water resources and increase availability of livestock and livestock products for domestic used and sale. 600 Agro-pastoral and reiverine men and women practising fodder production will be supported with agricultural tools, seeds and training to engage in fodder production, conservation and seed bulking. The fodder will be used to feed own livestock and sold to generate income. Both the emergency livestock treatment and fodder interventions will contribute to increased household food and nutrition security, re-building of pastoral herds, recovery and resilience to disasters

Needs

Over 28 % of the men, women, children and girls in Gedo Region are in acute food insecurity. 109,000 are in stressed and 35,000 are in crisis and emergency food security, and malnutrition rates are high in Riverine, South Gedo-Agropastoral and South Gedo Riverine (FSNAU, September 2018). 2018 Gu flooding caused displacements, livelihoods disruptions, crops inundation, loss of human lives and livestock. Most HHs sold or lost many livelihoods assets in 2017 and are yet to fully recover typical livelihoods. Fodder farmers requested for seeds, tools, training on fodder production, conservation and seed bulking (VSF-Suisse, April 2018). Despite enhanced milk availability and increasing livestock numbers in pastoral areas due to improved pasture and water resources, livestock population is still at baseline, below baseline and near baseline for camels, cattle and sheep/goats respectively (FSNAU, September 2018). Several consecutive favourable seasons are needed for herd sizes to increase to typical levels (FSNAU, April 2018). The heavy 2018 Gu rains increased endemic disease incidences and high mortalities among the few remaining livestock (VSF-Suisse, assessment, April 2018). lack of adequate animal healthcare services in Gedo results in diseases continuing to cause sub-optimal utilization of the better pasture and water resources, hence delay re-building of pastoral herds, recovery and resilience to disasters. Gedo region will continue facing and will not recover from food and nutrition insecurity considering that pastoralists have not fully recovered from the impacts of the 2016-2017 drought. The low herd sizes for most of pastoral HHs makes them unable to sell sufficient livestock to purchase adequate food to meet the basic needs of men, women, boys and girls in thier HHs. Since livestock is the economic mainstay of the community, livestock-based interventions can go a long way in saving lives and reducing the vulnerability of communities to hazards.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Gedo region is borders Kenya at Ethiopia. A lot of cross-border trade takes place between the three countries. Gedo Region faces extreme poverty, conflict, demographic pressure, environmental stress, weak institutions and infrastructure, and insufficient resilience to food crises’. Formerly under Al-Shaabab control, Beled Xaawo, Luuq, Garbaharey and El-wak have been liberated with the help of AMISOM. However, security is still volatile with sporadic attacks from the militia. Restricted movement of pastoralists by complex clan conflicts, military offensive and the fear of the militia interferes with coping mechanism of the pastoralists. However, most parts of intervention districts (Beled Xaawo, Luuq and Dollow Districts) can be accessed by road, making it possible for agencies to deliver humanitarian support in collaboration with local authorities and communities. Despite the insecurity still being experienced in the target area contributes to durable solutions as follows: • The project aligns with NDP and will utilise existing guidelines and standard from the FSC, SPHERE and LEGS during implementation.• The project ensures centrality of protection. Community-based and participatory approaches allow populations of concern to participate in the planning an management of the projects, and ensuring the inclusion and protection of the socially and economically marginalized. • Participatory approaches are applied throughout project implementation. Local authorities, Ministry of Livestock, CAHWs, and communities are all involved in the project, from site to beneficiary idetifcation and selection, project implementation, monitoring and evaluation together with the feedback mechanisms. The Jubaland Minsitry of livestock will supervise and monitor the technical aspects of project implementation.• Improved animal health and livestock feed security will contribute to increased livestock production and productivity, food and nutrition security, incomes, recovery and resilience This can mitigate against displacements in search of food and other needs by rural pastoral and agro-pastoral communities. It can also act as a pull factor for IDPs wanting to return and settle in rural areas.• A robust M&E and documentation will ensure that lessons learned in this project are shared with beneficiaries and authorities for out-and up-scaling of good practises.• The project addresses the needs of both pastoralists and agro-pastoralists. Both groups benfit fron animal health services and fodder activities. Veterinary services target livestock belonging to both pastoralists and agro-pastoralists. While produce fodder for their lievtock, they sell some of it to pastorlists, who feed mainly their milking herd that stay at home when the rest of the herd migrates in search of water and pasture• Participation in coordination fora such as the NGO consortium and the FSC, both regional and national for information exchange and adoption of common standards.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The security in the region is still volatile with attacks from Al Shabaab experienced in the area. However, Al-Shabaab activity is presently low in the intervention areas but they are in control of some parts of Bardheere and some villages in the region. Most of the districts can be accessed by road though at times dangerous. The project has been designed with conflict sensitivity as a core principle, and the project will prioritise participatory community beneficiary identification and selection, acceptance, and support staff to sensitively handle clan and gender dynamics. The project will develop a communication and accountability strategy. VSF-Suisse is operational Gedo Region since 17 years has excellent working relations with –the communities, the public and private sectors, civil society, local professional and user associations. VSF-Suiss eensures broad stakeholder participation in all its interventions. For successful project implementation, VSF-Suisse collaborates Minsitry of Livestock, local authorities, CAHWS, communities and the local professional associations. Local authorities contribute to beneficiary and project sites selection criteria, Community mobilization and sensitization; advice on access and provide security to project sites, administrative and societal issues; resolving any conflicts with communities on the project. This approach allows VSF-Suisse to reach even areas that might be inaccessible due to insecurity. VSF-Suisse is registered with the Jubbaland State and Federal Government of Somalia to operate as NGO.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Persons with disabilities (PWD), older people women and children often face constraints in accessing humanitarian assistance. Protection mainstreaming will ensure that protection principles guide interventions to minimize protection risks (e.g. exploitation, abuse, attacks by armed groups, property and assets theft, sexual violence, exclusion from or denial of assistance or services). The project will apply key Protection principles to ensure inclusivity, prioritize safety and dignity of all; minimize unintended negative effects (that could increase their vulnerability to physical and psychosocial risks) and providing services without baarriers. All gender groups will be included in baseline surveys, rapid needs assessments, analyzing protection risks, ensuring participatory community approaches in beneficiaries identification and selection, ensure their concerns are heard, addressed, avoid community tension, conform to local cultures, feedback mechanism and Monitoring and Evaluation. Project indicators will be disaggregated by age, gender, and location or specific group. Assessments will describe gender-specific roles, responsibilities, asset ownership and access to information and resources related to agriculture and livestock livelihoods. Veterinary services provision will prioritise special groups e.g PWDs, older people, women-and children-headed HH. Fodder production activities will ensure that both men and women farmers are represented so that access to milk and food and nutritional security is improved in their HHs. Reporting and sharing protection concerns with the Protection cluster and Agencies working with “persons with special needs”.
