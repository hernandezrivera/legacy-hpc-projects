Needs

Thanks to its team with more than 200 employees and 600 volunteers, trained in Syria and in Lebanon, MSJM has focused its emergency response to the ongoing Syrian crisis, mainly in hard to reach areas where the difficulty of access or other circumstances deprived numerous persons in vulnerable situations of the required protection and emergency services. MSJM has demonstrated capacity of immediate and efficient response in hard to reach areas. Meetings of MSJM field officers with key interlocutors in proposed regions and focus groups discussion conducted with the population gave us accurate information of the extent to which these areas need protection services. The following protection issues and needs were encountered Drop-out of schools and child-labor due to numerous families without income; Presence of unaccompanied children who lost their parents in the war; Relevant cases of GBV. - Many girls were forced to leave their study due to of the destruction of schools and the economic situation of their families induced them to resort to early marriage. - Prolonged hostilities in the targeted areas and loss of civilian life resulted in many female headed-households. They tend to be exposed to the risk of sexual harassment and exploitation, and various forms of violence (physical – verbal) either by relatives or employer who exploits the living conditions of women - MSJM teams observed many cases of persons with disabilities due to the war, with persons in acute need of medical assistance, psychosocial support and support for integration in the community.MSJM also identified the presence of elderly persons without any care givers. (Sources on Needs assessments MSJM teams through mobile clinics working in these areas; Key interlocutors In affected areas - Focus groups discussion with beneficiaries).To meet these needs, MSJM is willing to expand its activities to create FOUR new Community Centers, one in Duma, one in Qunaytra, and two in Deraa, and to focus on situations of protracted displacements as well as an increasing flux of returnees in need for reintegration support. This will include material support for the most vulnerable and multi-sectoral protection interventions aiming at reducing vulnerabilities and risks and enhancing the protection of persons of concern (PoC). The evolving situation in Syria is urging humanitarian actors to focus on areas of return in order to support voluntary and safe returns and ensuring that they are sustainable. The project will have a focus on returnees to enhance the capacity of reintegration and early recovery, while serving also the overburdened host community that receives IDPs and returnees to empower and encourage the people to accept arrivals to their community.

Implementation of activities

MSJM's Project seeks to provide main protection services and inventive mixed solutions to returnees and other persons of concern through activities that support the early reintegration of the POCs and combine static facilities and outreach activities (two outreaching teams for each one of the centres) Our Project will implement Four Community Centres with full package of protection services: one in Duma, in an area that is deprived of any humanitarian service; one in Deraa in al-Lujat region, which is still a hard to reach area and in Qunaitera, also anhard to reach area and poor in services. Each Community Center will have a safe corner for women and girls and 2 mobile teams that will outreach to communities three times a week, to conduct awareness sessionsForeseen activities:-Coordination with relative authorities to get approvals to open and run Community Centers with protected safe space. - Choice of location not served by any other organizations and with relevant population density to make the Center irradiate to the highest number of beneficiaries- Rental and equipment of the Four Community Centers- Hiring of skilled staff and training: Project Director, Center Coordinator, Accountant, Lawyer Legal ORV,GBV Coordinator, GBV volunteer ,2 Case Management per Center, Reception and registration, Activities Facilitator, Education specialist, Social workers volunteering, PSS specialist,2 Drivers, a documentation issue facilitator –Janitors. - Our legal services will provide through lawyers that are licensed with the Ministry of Interior, - A volunteering local committee formed in the targeted area to participate in the development of targeting criteria/ selection of beneficiaries.- Reception, registration of vulnerable beneficiaries according to a scale of humanitarian severity status (victims at risk and survivors– women household, helpless persons with disability, numerous families with promiscuity, programs will provide access to services to all persons of concern (IDPs and host communities)- Safe space activities (only for women, girls and children of both sex): - Psychosocial support services- Psychosocial support for survivors of sexual violence/domestic violence – Transient Medical treatments for survivors of sexual violence/domestic violence.- Protection services for children - Recreational activities - Care mechanisms/services for elderly persons - Services for persons with disabilities - Case Management - Quality specialized GBV services and tailored measures to prevent and reduce GBV risks - Psychosocial counselling and consultation provided that do not need a mental health specialist.- Provision of hygiene and health accompaniment to GBV survivors with the utmost delicacy and dignity.- Outreach to surrounding communities at least twice a week to offer special home care to the elders and to the persons with disabilities.- Family consultations and GBV.- GBV awareness for males and ad hoc PSS and GBV consultations.- Through a community-based approach, MSJM, following UNFPA minimum standard for prevention and response to protection issue will focus on building the capacity of community-based structures to raise awareness on GBV issues in order to strengthen prevention.- Multi-sectoral GBV response services as part of the case management process will be provided in MSJM new Community Centers and through referrals to MSJM specialized services.- Targeted support to persons with specific protection needs through empowerment vocational training with grants to the most vulnerable that have succeeded in the sessions and support a viable small project that will enhance the protection situation of the individual and family, increase the level of women's self-reliance dignity and independence (including literacy/ language courses- M&E visits and reporting with special interest in monitoring the PRA and its successful mitigation measures

Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection Risk : There is a risk about the Content of message in not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment during awareness sessions campaigns and this maybe creates misunderstanding or even tensions with negative effects on attendance during sessionsLikelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating Measures: Consultation with population from the communities about the most efficient way to convey the messages. 2- Protection Risk: Establishment of community centers can generate tension between the community in which the facility is located and neighbouring communities where facilities may not be present and less services are offered. Likelihood: medium Impact: High Mitigating Measures: communicating and consultation with local Authorities and population on the situation and the needs/ ensure the location of center is adequate to be reached by most of villages surrounding it/ focusing on underserved areas and the most vulnerable groups.MSJM will consult with other partners of the protection sector in the area and consultation of mapping of services produced by the protection Sector to avoid overlapping and look at the gaps in coverage.3- Protection Risk: The population targeted is put at risk as a result of the location of centre, the possibility of explosive hazard on the way to the CC, lack or hardship of accessibility to services( freedom of movement-security – safety….) Likelihood: medium Impact: High Mitigating Measures: ensure safe access to the facility by all gender and age categories especially women –girls – elderly and persons with disabilities, in addition to put signals pointing the correct directions/ coordinating with local and security authorities to secure the pathway / following specific Guidelines developed by protection Agencies.. MSJM will make sure that the center is located in a safe place (including from explosive hazard and structure of the building); ensure that the center is accessible for persons with disabilities and old persons; ensure that the location is easily accessible.4- Protection Risk: Exploitation of the beneficiaries (especially the most vulnerable groups ) by staff or services providers when accessing assistance. Likelihood: low Impact: Very High Mitigating Measures: Reinforce the message that humanitarian services are( free of charge)\ Train staff on PSEA complaint mechanism and assign dedicated staff to follow up the complaints and treatment of them\ advertisement of Hot line for urgent complaints.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Potential Risks: - Delayed approval from the authorities to establish the community center. - Difficulty of finding the low damaged or appropriate building to be prepared and to be swiftly rehabilitated in targeted areas and at a bearable cost- Lack of essential services / utilities (electricity –water). Mitigation measures in order: - Coordination with local authorities and key persons in the communities and explain how extent the project will serve the population in targeted areas and the importance of speeding up the procedures. - Delegating of local persons who have deep knowledge in the area to search appropriate facility. - Procure a generator with fuel as well as a water tank to ensure the continuity of work in all conditions.
