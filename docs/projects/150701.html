Project Summary

The project “Combatting De-Palestinization of Education in Occupied East Jerusalem” addresses: i) the need for effective protection of the right of Palestinians in occupied East Jerusalem to preserve and develop their educational system in accordance with their own culture, values and national identity, and, ii) the right of East Jerusalem children to freely learn, express and be educated in respect to their Palestinian identity, culture, language and values. Palestinians, including children, hold these rights as protected persons under IHL, and as individuals and a people entitled to self-determination under IHRL.
This issue is addressed because of: the broad concern about the systematic manner in which Israel has undermined these rights; the failure to obtain redress in the past through repeated but uncoordinated efforts; the international community including governments and UN and international organizations need to acknowledge the serious violation of IHL and human rights; and, the serious consequences for Palestinian society in East Jerusalem, in particular its youth.

Needs

With almost 50 years of occupation of the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem, Israel continues its process of ‘Israelization’ or ‘de-Palestinization’ of education in occupied East Jerusalem. On the 13th of May, the Israeli cabinets approve a five year plan to offer financial incentive, were allocated for east Jerusalem schools 705,000,000 Israeli Shekel (242,884,200 US $) that would fully adopt the Israeli curriculum rather than using the Palestinian one. The plan heavily packed by the Israeli minister of education Nafali Bennet as well as well and Jerusalem affairs and Heritage Minister Zeev Elkin. The plan proposed is a five year strategy that aims to gradually increase the number of students using the Israeli curriculum beginning from the first grade with specific emphasis on English and math. The first year would add 15 classes, followed by 18 in the second, 21 in the third, 24 in the fourth, and 27 classes in the fifth. Another goal of the cabinet is to continue to increase the number of East Jerusalem Palestinian students who would earn the Israeli matriculation exam (bagrut).Bennet, the Israeli education minister: was quoted saying, “the time has come for East Jerusalem to also study the Israeli curriculum from first grade. Jerusalem needs to be united in deeds, not in words. The political context of the problem raises additional concerns such as: the failure to obtain redress; the reluctance of foreign aid agencies and governments to engage in what is perceived to be a “political matter”, rather than one IHL and human rights; and, the serious consequences for Palestinian society in East Jerusalem, in particular its children and youth. Part of Israel’s policy of ‘de-Palestinization’ has been aimed at schools in East Jerusalem and the push by the occupying Authorities to introduce the Israeli curriculum which can be seen clearly through the Israeli five years plan foe education in East Jerusalem. This was highlighted in the Israeli newspaper Haaretz: ‘Dr. George Mansour, who examined the history textbooks, found that the books ignore the presence of the Arab-Palestinian people in Israel and the stress is on the Promised Land of the Jewish people: "There is a process of de-Palestinization, instilling of the Zionist narrative and minimizing of Arab culture," reported Dr. Mansour. Gender: The Israelization of Palestinian education has profound and specific impacts on both boys and girls within the school systems. Boys specifically experience higher rates of dropout, while the combined dropout rate of both boys and girls in East Jerusalem schools is as high as 36% by the 12th grade. Girls also experience unique disparities as a result of the controlled Palestinian education, such as early marriages (prior to 18), which remains to be a form of gendered based violence and limited self-determination. Protecting the right of education among East Jerusalem Palestinian schools diminishes the negative impacts that specifically target boys and girls in distinct ways, which exist due to the Israelization of Palestinian education. The disparity in the provision of services between East and West Jerusalem, can be highlighted by the following recent statistics from a report released in September, 2017 by the Air Amin, which paints a devastating picture of neglect, urban blight and underdevelopment in East Jerusalem, the historic heart of Palestinian life, all a result of nearly five decades of Israeli policies, with over 76.4% of Palestinians living below the poverty line compared to the national Israeli average of 22.2%.Though, Palestinians constitute 37% of the population of Jerusalem. Within Jerusalem, 84% of Palestinian children live below the poverty line, and thousands of children are considered to be “at-risk.” With a shortage of classrooms and striking discrepancies in resources allotted to Palestinian versus Jewish Israeli citizens. 2,400 children who remain unrecognized by the Jerusalem education administration.
