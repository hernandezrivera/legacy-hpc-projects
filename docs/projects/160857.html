Project Summary

This project will deliver of preventive and curative health care services through supporting Primary Health Care Units PHCUs and Mobile health services to conflict affected areas to vulnerable population 7,743 boys, and 9,756 girls, Women 5,124 and 2,184 Men in Renk during 2020. Based on the Hold the Child experience operating in the proposed areas through Provision of Education in Emergency project in Renk and with current based in Fangak, Hold the Child will ensure consistent coordination through the cluster and local authority.
With $150,000 appeal during 2020, this project will support the static and Mobile health facilities contributing to reduction of morbidity and mortality through provision of preventive and treatment, and ensuring increased access to essential health services during the project cycle.

1 - Needs

The survival, welfare and development of Children in South Sudan still interfaces negative impacts from the protracted national crisis that has been associated displacements of households, destruction of service facilities including health units, impended access to service points, economic melt down among others. The overwhelmed under resourced health service capacities in the country is characterised with high burdens of vaccine preventable diseases, high infant and maternal mortalities among others, amidst low service coverage, ignorance and low treatment seeking practices. Renk County (Upper Nile) has been reported to experience Measles Outbreak in week 23 of 2019 a signal of a very low service coverage; and coupled with conflict clashes associated population movements in search for safety, water and food among other services. In June 2019, REACH reports indicated that nearly one-third of host community children aged 6-59 months in Renk County (Upper Nile) are acute malnourished, a GAM prevalence of 32.1%, and SAM of 8.5%; and a proxy acutely malnutrition for pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) at16.8%. The report further indicated that 89% of deaths are due to illness such as Malaria, Diarrhoea, Fever, Respiratory tract infections, Measles and others; while 11% death attributed to trauma/injuries. With a GAM is more than double the WHO emergency threshold and shows an extremely serious situation upon which Childhood illness in the areas have attributed. Only half of caregivers reported seeking treatment from health facilities, signalling a relatively poor health seeking behaviours and low utilization of preventative health services. Only 8% vaccination confirmed by card, and trend far below the Sphere recommended 95% coverage and indication of inadequate EPI coverage in the county. According to REACH report, High GAM prevalence is both driven by and increases the risk of infectious disease outbreaks such as malaria, diarrhoea, pneumonia and measles due to compromised immunity. More than one-third of children were ill in the two weeks prior to data collection (36%) as reported by caregivers with the most common symptoms reported as fever, followed by cough/fast difficulty breathing, and diarrhoea. Fever is often treated as a proxy for suspected malaria, and the fact that nearly one-fourth of children were reported with it suggests malaria may be a main driver of morbidity in the population Based on the above described trends, Hold the Child a strategically placed national organization with recent activities in Renk (Education sector) proposes to scale up health service delivery through both static and mobile approaches to underserved and vulnerable population pockets with essential life saving health care during 2020 in accordance with the SPHERE standards and consistent coordination through the cluster and local authority
