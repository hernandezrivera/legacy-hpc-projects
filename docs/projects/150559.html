Project Summary

The project is an immediate response to ensure vulnerable women, children and men to have sufficient storage capacity of domestic and drinking water whenever it becomes available and have better access to safe water and safe sanitation facilities and public services through the rehabilitation, construction and upgrade of existing wastewater infrastructure and practice proper daily hygiene through increased advocacy and awareness raising.

Needs

The political instability and 11 years of blockade, have resulted in the lack of access to adequate WASH services and restricted movements for people and goods. Gaza’s population is subject to a chronic electricity crisis which is caused from the Gaza Power Plant that is regularly shut-down due to fuel shortage resulting from lack of funds; a 40% reduction in the electricity supplied by Israel following the Palestinian Authority’s decision to cut funds to Gaza; as well as the interrupted Egyptian lines. Outages of 18-20 hours/day have resulted and severely impacted the provision of essential services, particularly health, domestic water supply (40-50 l/c/d) and sanitation services. Gaza’s population relies on the deteriorated groundwater aquifer which is the sole source for all water use. Chloride and nitrates concentrations are higher than WHO guidelines, which makes the extracted water unsafe for drinking without treatment. The population has therefore become dependent on private and public desalination plants for drinking water: 68% of the consumed water is susceptible to biological contamination due to inadequate disinfection at the desalination plants, improper handling during distribution, and poor user storage habits. According to the CMWU, 23% of the Gaza Strip lacks wastewater systems, forcing households to rely on cesspits for excreta disposal that ood in the streets and houses causing damage to property, environment and have a signi cant negative impact on basic health, mainly on women and children. Vulnerable children are the most exposed to waterborne diseases such as diarrhoea and skin infections, and without proper sanitation facilities, their basic hygiene needs cannot be covered. UNRWA and Ministry of Health records indicate an increase in the cases of diarrhoea, skin infections and viral meningitis. This causes women to be unable of carrying out daily house chores and meet their personal hygiene needs (i.e. during menstruation). During the last 4 years, UNICEF led multiple interventions at household and community levels, inter alia with support from AAH. For instance, a joint assessment in September/October 2018 of 15,000 households from 26 localities, combined with the AAH data base, found that 9,769 households do not have domestic storage tanks or lack of sufficient storage capacity, and consuming <60 l/c/d, 6,754 households lack drinking storage tanks. Additionally, 3321 households lack totally of sanitation facilities and 2153 households are currently not connected to municipal wastewater networks. These key findings and interviews with the communities’ members demonstrate a significant gap in meeting the water storage and sanitation needs of the Gazan population. UNICEF and its partners are strongly committed to gender sensitive WASH programming, as well as engaging individuals with disabilities. Following the same principles defined in the project identification, design and implementation will be inclusive of women, girls and boys’ inputs at all stages. UNICEF will use a broad range of methodologies to ensure comprehensive collection of beneficiaries’ voices, including Key Informative Interviews, Focus group discussions, and Human Interest Stories. A feedback mechanism will be put in place in concert with community leaders to ensure that the beneficiaries’ voice, including women, children and individuals with disabilities, are duly heard and prompt follow-up actions are taken to address complaints.

Activities

The proposed intervention is an immediate response to ensure women, children and men have sufficient storage capacity of domestic and drinking water and have better access to safe water and safe sanitation facilities and services. It aims at further addressing the significant gap in humanitarian needs, especially targeting female heads of HH and war widows. Implemented activities will consider the needs of women and girls. Trainings will focus on the specific needs of men and women (e.g. menstruation and child care) with appropriate locations and timing. Separate training sessions with gender specific trainers may be required to ensure messaging is presented in a gender sensitive manner. All activities will be coordinated with CMWU and Area Focal Points: 1. Household Water Supply 1.1 Provision and installation of new 1,400 domestic 1000l water roof storage tanks equipped with all fittings installed for families whose water storage tanks were not filled due to deteriorated power crisis or are consuming less than 60 l/c/d. 2.1 Provision and installation of 2,500 250l potable water storage tanks, respectively, for vulnerable families who purchase their drinking water from private vendors; and currently store this water in jerry cans or buckets. Priority will be given to large families in critical condition; families with water storage tanks that have been damaged or destroyed; women headed families, and vulnerable families with children and disabled members.2. Household Sanitary Services 2.1 Rehabilitation of sanitation facilities for 1,000 HHs to meet SPHERE standards 2.2 Rehabilitation of wastewater connections for 500 HHs inMiddle area 3. Household Hygiene Promotion 3.1 In close collaboration with communities, to improve the quality of life in the targeted communities and public health, meetings will be conducted with communities’ representatives and other key informants, interviews with household representatives, focus group discussions with women from the areas. 170 public awareness and hygiene training sessions will be conducted through participatory workshops for HH representatives of target communities in order to promote safe water handling and storage, safe drainage of wastewater and good hygiene practices. Water tank cleaning campaigns will be organized to encourage women, children and men to practice positive behaviour.3.2 1500 HHs will be targeted with at least four home visits through the Communication for Development approach to discuss, negotiate and plan actions for behaviour change to improve their knowledge, attitude, and practices of key water handling messages, and menstrual hygiene for adolescents and women. Activities 1-3 shall be carried out by UNICEF AAH and the 5 CBOs 4. Installation of HWTS and training in 400 HHs Household Water Treatment and Safe Storage (HWTS) unit with Gravity Driven Membrane technology minimizes risk of cross contamination before consumption. Dedicated practical trainings will be carried out in 400 HH identified. 5. Collection and analysis of water samples in HHs covered: Water samples are regularly collected from raw water, treated water and at the point-of-use (at the cup), and analyzed in order to assess water quality according to biological and physical parameters. HH water consumption is analyzed through tap sensors. Small lab is installed and staff provided with adequate training. 6. Promotion of hygiene and sanitation practices with kit distribution, especially for HHs practicing open defecation: Promotional activities with behavior change approach will be carried out in a culturally appropriate manner to systematically analyze and tackle practices contaminating water. Specific sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers, children (5 18Y) and will be supported by the distribution of hygienic kits.Activities 4-6 shall be carried out by UNICEF and CESVI with support from AAH and the 5 CBOs.

Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will maintain close coordination with CMWU, related municipalities, WASH Cluster and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. UNICEF and its implementing partners, will supervise the projects on a daily basis to ensure timely and effective implementation. Women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. FGDs will ensure separation of women and men to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. A detailed survey, field visits and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor and evaluate the activities. A KAP survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the impacts of the activities on women, men and boys, girls, persons with disabilities and the elderly. UNICEF and its implementing partners will contribute to exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, and to be used for advocacy purposes. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation: the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism with well-known access by targeted beneficiaries. The following results will be achieved: - 3,900 households will improve the quantity and reliability of access to drinking and domestic water with proper and sufficient storage tanks (B.1: 11,800 people with improved access to safe drinking water- 7092 children, 2360 females and 2348 males)- 500 households will improve their access to sanitation with rehabilitated wastewater connections to the existing municipal wastewater networks (B.2: 2,800 people with improved access to sanitation- 1683 children, 560 females and 557 males)- 1000 families will improve their households’ sanitation facilities to improve personal hygiene (B.3: 1000 HHs that have a handwashing facility with soap and water available on premises, target 5,460 people - 3281 children, 1092 females, 1087 males)- 1500 households will receive awareness session to improve their knowledge, attitude, and practices of key water handling. Including 563 adolescent girls and women who receive specific messages around menstrual hygiene (B.4 : 8400 of people received hygiene materials/kits - 5049 children, 1680 females, 1670 males).
