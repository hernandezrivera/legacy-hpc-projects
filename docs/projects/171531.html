Project Summary

The continuation of protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining to Health. Approximately, 3.15 million Somalis are still in need due to insufficient access to healthcare in many districts, thus negatively affecting the country’s public health and the dignity of highly vulnerable and displaced communities.
Over 1.3 million IDPs and a further one million non-IDPs in Somalia are in need of provision of emergency health services; this population regularly faces significant barriers to quality service. The already-disrupted healthcare system is overburdened, fragmented, under-resourced and ill-equipped to provide lifesaving or preventive services. Those who are displaced or marginalized face high barriers to healthcare access and consequently, high exposure to disease. Measures to combat waterborne and communicable diseases, including immunization, are underfunded or simply not in place in several areas.
According to the UNICEF Somalia COVID-19 Situation Report No. 9, as of 22nd September, 3,588 COVID-19 cases were reported in Somalia with an overall downward trend.
On the other hand, the 2020 Deyr rainy season has started with moderate to significantly heavy rains reported. Flash and riverine floods continued to affect many parts of the country and it is estimated 191,800 people in Hirshabelle and South West states as well as Benadir region among others are affected. Among those affected, about 124,200 people have been displaced from their homes. Another 5,000 people are at risk of further displacement in Jowhar, Middle Shabelle. Cholera continues in several parts of the country including Benadir, Hiraan, Middle Shabele among others.
According to the Somali Health and Demographic Survey 2020, 68 percent of women did not make antenatal visits during their most recent pregnancy in the five years prior to the survey. The reasons for their low uptake of health care during pregnancy and child delivery include the lack of money, long distances due to the lack of health facilities and the need for Somali women to obtain permission from family members before seeking health services.
An inter-agency assessment conducted in May 2020 in Galgadud region shows an estimated 12,000 internally displaced people (IDPs) lost their livelihoods following a storm. There is no health facility nearby and the nearest health centre is approximately 5 km away while the hospital is approximately 15 km away.
CPD is proposing to provide lifesaving and emergency health services to crisis affected and displaced populations in up to 10 districts in Benadir region, Galmudug, Hirshabelle and South West States of Somalia with a target of 51,600 people (8,600 HHs) through fixed facilities as well as outreaches to serve hard to reach pastoral communities and IDPs.
Activities will be coordinated with other partners and clusters. Somalia Ministry of Health and WHO Covid-19 regulations and guidelines will be mainstreamed in programme design and implementation. GBV prevention and support measures, inclusivity of people living with disability and gender concerns will also be mainstreamed. Disabled groups will be consulted and all facilities will be disability friendly.

Project Activities

1) Provide consultations and lifesaving healthcare treatment and common conditions to 51,600 persons through 10 health facilities (one in each target district) and 20 mobile teams (two for each facility) on a daily basis (Essential PHC (EPHS) clinical care, Child health care, Communicable disease treatment, STD/HIV treatment and TB screening and treatment).2) Provide targeted supplemental vaccine services to prevent vaccine preventable diseases i.e measles to children under five and women of childbearing age and PLW (1,326 people)3) Procure and distribute essential drugs and equipment to fully operationalize the 10 health facilities and 20 mobile clinics. 4) Organise and conduct 30 community awareness session and education on common communicable diseases and health promotion targeting 50 community members per target district with special focus on IDPs and returnees with equal access to men and women at target locations. CPD plans to improve the capacity of the community members on AWD/Cholera and other communicable diseases in order to detect and refer suspected cases early enough to prevent devastating outbreaks, through Rapid response teams (RRTs), HCW training, monitoring (EWAR) and supplies. To do this, CPD will organise one day community workshops targeting 50 people per district where specific people will be trained. 5) Strengthen the management of clinical management of rape (CMR) for gender-based violence cases (target 7,096 people). This will be done by capacity building and stocking of drugs to treat the same. CPD will also mainstream Ministry of Health and WHO Covid-19 guidelines in the design and implementation of programme activities.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.): The project will mitigate this risk by: Establishing an information sharing protocol for survivors of abuse that avoids exposing her/him to further trauma and that all efforts will be made to ensure her/his confidentiality and wishes concerning the care provided. Communicating information through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels (e.g. health community workers, door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms). Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: The project will mitigate these risks by: Involving and consulting all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to their health needs. Collecting and using data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. Ensuring that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. - the most vulnerable groups in the community will be identified and prioritized. Exclusion and discrimination of marginalised groups will be avoided. Ensuring lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services. Making infrastructure adaptions to the health facility so that it is accessible to persons with impaired mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, older persons, bed-ridden individuals e.t.c.). Introducing special arrangements for persons unable to access health facilities e.g. mobile health services. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: The project will mitigate this risk by: Setting up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms that will not expose the beneficiaries for suggestions and complaints e.g. feedback box at health centre, phone lines e.t.c. Responding to complaints depending on urgency and take corrective measures. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: This risk will be mitigated by; Ensuring that patient consultations and documentation are confidential and private: Not Sharing information on the identity of patients unless informed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). Keeping lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use. Ensure all health staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.
