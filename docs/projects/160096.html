Project Summary

Many IDPs are currently residing in urban and pre-urban informal crowded settlements, with insuﬃcient access to basic services and poor shelter conditions that raise protection and health concerns. They remain in need of Shelter and NFIs support for protection from harsh climatic conditions including rainy season, overall improvements to their living conditions and improvement to their chances to access livelihoods and durable solutions. Majority of IDPs and returnees live in makeshift shelters, temporary emergency shelters, tents.
The proposed actions under this request are meant to complement ARC’s humanitarian efforts on addressing humanitarian needs of the IDP's and returnees who are living in deplorable shelter conditions in Kismayo, Mogadishu and Sool region (Guumays IDP, Cayanbo IDPs and Canjiid IDPs) by providing durable shelter units, life-saving and life-sustaining assistance to people in need, prioritizing the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls. Being the Shelter focal point in lower Jubba, the project will enable ARC to fulfill its mandate and address shelter gaps in majorly the IDP Settlements and returnees in Kismayo through construction of durable shelter, procurement and distribution of emergency shelter kits (ESK) and NFIs, installation solar of street lights and working with the state government of jubbaland to advocate on settlement planning and creating awareness on local housing, land property rights to the IDP populations and returnees in the settlements. The response will also seek to create awareness on settlement layout, with layout of the existing services to avoid haphazard settlement and the project will be tailored to address cross-cutting issues such as gender, protection, safety and security aspects. 50 stand alone Solar street lights will be installed in identified areas to provide adequate security and safety vulnerable persons with IDP settlement including persons with disabilities, chronically ill, children, and women at risk. The installation of 50 solar street lights in IDP settlements of Sool, Mogadishu and Kismayo will be accompanied by the training of a solar light committee on safety and protection of the solar lights. All distributions, selection and implementations will be gender-sensitive and will ensure overcoming of obstacles that might prevent vulnerable groups from receiving access to lifesaving services. The proposed intervention has already been identified in a recent IDP profiling report which this allocation seeks to address per the highlighted needs of the targeted IDP population.
In complementing the transitional houses implemented by ARC under UNHCR funding, ARC will target 100 HH (600 people) who are returnees with construction of durable shelter unit that consists of two rooms (4.4mx4.4m+4.1mx4.7m) with a veranda (4mx1.3m) while ARC will provide 1000 NFIs for (6000 people) in IDPs in kismayo, Sool region (particularly Guumays IDP, Caynabo IDPs and Canjiid IDPs) and Mogadishu. The same individuals will be provided with 1000 ESK.

Project Activities

In collaboration with Shelter Cluster, protection cluster and Jubbaland state government, ARC will identify vulnerable returnees in need of shelter, register them and construct 100 durable shelter units. Each shelter unit consists of two rooms(4.4mx4.4m+4.1mx4.7m) with a veranda (4mx1.3m). ARC will procure and distribute 1000 ESK and 1000 NFI kits to the IDP populations in Sool region, Mogadishu and Kismayo and will carry out registration of all targeted beneficiaries and identify the most vulnerable household and individuals with priority focusing on child headed family, elderly, disabled households, female headed house hold, families with high number of children under 5 years. This will be done in consultation with Shelter Cluster and other partners including the government. Installation of 50 stands alone Solar Street lights in strategic locations within the IDP settlement in Sool, Kismayo and Mogadishu will be done to provide adequate security and protection of vulnerable IDP populations. ARC will mobilize local authorities including IDPs and returnee’s leadership, government line ministry responsible for land and will organize consultation meetings to create awareness in the IDP settlements about housing land and property rights and policies; 3 such consultation meetings will be conducted drawing participants from the wider IDP community and returnees. After 2 weeks of the distribution, ARC will conduct a post distribution monitoring (PDM) to cover 90% of the households that have received the ESKs and NFIs to ensure accountability, appropriateness of materials and effectiveness of distribution and use of the distributed kits. Data will be dis-aggregated sex to evaluate whether specific needs of women, girls, boys and men are met. The results will be shared with the Shelter Cluster and the Donor for evaluation purposes.

Mitigation Measures

4a. ARC will work with local authority to avoid eviction and carryout land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including Statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. ARC will preserve the safety and dignity of the affected populations and provide durable shelter through a tailored design which respect privacy and cultural norms between men and womenby including partitions and door locks to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female headed-households, from risks of exploitation and abuse. 4b. ARC will work closely with Shelter cluster and other clusters partners to improve settlement planning, management – both Federal and Jubbaland governments to support physical mapping of settlement infrastructure through an integrated approach which ensures that beneficiaries have access to basic services (water, health services, schools, roads, markets, protection etc. 4c. ARC has an efficient Complaint Registration and Feedback Mechanism (CRFM) which affirms that our beneficiaries and communities have rights to complain if services and our commitments fell below satisfactory. 4d. ARC will use real time KUJA-KUJA online platform and set-up a hotline accessible available to target beneficiaries to Record suggestions and complaints related to Durable Shelter/NFIs distributions and other associated interventions. The hotline will be staffed by both male and female employee to ensure that it is culturally appropriate for callers of both genders. The hotline system will be designated to withheld information of the beneficiaries, their district they are calling from and deliberate attention will be given a regular feedback. The database generated, will be an integral part of the complaint and feedback tracking system. Further, documenting the nature of the complaint or feedback, it has required field tracking of how the complaint was resolved and the name of the staff member who communicated resolution to the complainant. The entry remains open until the issue has been resolved.

Please provide further explanation

ARC will continue to undertake Durable Shelter activities that empower the affected people, Shelter/NFIs management committees, local authorities, line ministries and other stakeholders through technical capacity building, with aim to return to their homes, continue rebuilding their livelihoods, and resume normal life. The proposed project will address Shelter/NFIs and protection concerns in all stages, from project identification, design and planning, to implementation and monitoring by involving direct beneficiaries and ensuring equal representation of both women and men. Solar power can also be harnessed and stored for use for lighting the neighboring settlements at night, improving the safety of women and girls. After successful completion of Durable Shelter/NFIs infrastructure, it will be handed over to a legal authority of the locality, and Shelter/NFIs committees formed, equipped and trained by the project, to instill community ownership and project sustainability. To increase the project impact, ARC will ensure coordination between existing and planned WASH, Nutrition, Health, Protection and Livelihood projects in the same regions. ARC will ensure all Shelter/NFIs distribution and facilities are sited and designed considering environmental requirements and disaster risk reduction measures. To enhance transparency and accountability, project beneficiaries and line ministries will be involved in all stages of project planning an implementation. ARC uses real time Kuja Kuja beneficiary feedback system (www.kujakuja.com). The system includes a mechanism to incorporate findings in project design. Through the adoption of "Do No Harm" approaches, efforts are made to ensure relationships with the project targeted population and local leaders are enhanced and programs are implemented to improvement relationships, reduce tension, and promote peaceful pathways out of Somalia conflict situation. ARC believes that understanding the situation and the context in which we operate is paramount to ensuring that interventions are successful and to avoid negative consequences in the operation area. In order to mitigate risks facing the targeted communities ARC proposes to ensure all issues that can cause conflict of interest are restricted, training and community gathering will be conducted in safe locations within the targeted communities to avoid risk associated with long travel distance and road blocks. Durable shelter facilities are constructed in safe and accessible locations. As a part of protection activities, community capacity building will be conducted to promote peaceful co-existence and conflict resolution.
