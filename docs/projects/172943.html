Project Summary

The project seeks to reduce the number of explosive hazards related deaths and injuries while increasing safety levels for people affected by contamination in Tripoli, Benghazi and Sebha. The project also seeks to empower communities and national actors with sustainable capacities to assess and address the risks posed by explosive remnants of war. From 1 January 2021-31 December 2021, DDG will deploy survey, clearance and explosive hazards risk education teams in Tripoli, Benghazi and Sebha, to reduce and remove the threat of explosive ordnance. This will enhance resilience of landmine/ERW affected communities and improved conditions for socio-economic development. In addition, DDG will focus on building sustainable local capacity twofold - by supporting the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC) and its regional sub-office operating in eastern Libya - the Regional Mine Action Centre (RMAC), and by assisting to establish a local civil society humanitarian mine action (HMA) capacity in Benghazi.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Tripoli and the ongoing insecurity remained the main drivers of internal displacement in Libya with over 343,000 people displaced at the beginning of 2020. Furthermore, the mine/ERW contamination in Libya remains a key issue affecting human security. The scale of contamination from explosive hazards in Libya is unknown, due to a lack of comprehensive nationwide survey and the additional layers of contamination resulting from ongoing conflict since 2011 and is understood to be beyond the Libyan authorities' capacity to clear. However, it is clear that urban areas are significantly contaminated impacting people’s safety, socio-economic opportunities and freedom of movement - the enabling factors required for legitimate stability in the longer term. As a result of 14 months of armed conflict, many areas in Tripoli have become contaminated adding a considerable burden to a population fatigued and a city damaged by war. This destructive environment causes a decrease in safety levels for civilians and a rise in insecurity and instability and hinders access to basic services. There are an estimated 97,522 internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Tripoli, a city of over 1,100,000 inhabitants, based on data from the last census in 2012. The intense clashes had advanced to densely populated suburbs of southern Tripoli with severe damage to critical infrastructure and civilian lives being put at risk from the indiscriminate use of explosive ordnance. The situation remains critical and the longer-term socio-economic impact is yet to be fully understood, but it is expected to be significant. At the same time, contamination by explosive hazards hinders the access to provide vital emergency assistance, remaining long after conflicts end, so there is a critical need for DDG to provide emergency EORE to those returning to former-front-line areas in Tripoli as well as to survey and map the extent of contamination.In Benghazi, many urban areas were damaged, and the infrastructure destroyed from heavy shelling and airstrikes during the 2014-2017 war between the LNA and militant groups. Due to its relative stability, currently, Benghazi is receiving the highest number of returnees (189,025) in Libya as well as hosting a high number of IDPs (36,915). DDG has been present in Benghazi since 2018 and began surveying the areas of Ganfouda, Gawarsha, Garyounis and Downtown before conducting battle area clearance (BAC) and EOD, as these areas have been identified as priority areas due to their location (such as Downtown, the historical old city of Benghazi), the high number of people still living there in spite of the contamination and the impact on key areas such as education, industry, employment, tourism, transport links and opportunities for income generation. As in Tripoli, the official number of victims and accidents reported in the Information Management System (IMSMA) for Benghazi is low, with only 54 victims recorded (94% of which were men and among them 83% were adults), mainly due to limited data collection resources in the past. Real casualty figures, however, are expected to be significantly higher. DDG is to increasing focus on the identification and reporting of EO casualties committed through its EORE and NTS teams and will advocate within the humanitarian community for more dedicated efforts on reporting. Sebha has remained neglected in terms of coverage and attention paid to the humanitarian needs due to security and high level of ERW contamination. Since January 2014, the region has been the centre of recurrent and intensified tribal confrontations and armed. The presence of HMA INGOs is highly valued since there is still a need to conduct survey and clearance of areas heavily affected by ERW contamination as well as provide EORE to at risk communities.

2 - Activities

NTS/TS: As part of the land release process, DDG focuses on comprehensive surveys of suspected hazardous areas to collect and analyse data about the presence, type, distribution and surrounding environment of explosive hazards contamination. This enables DDG to define better where contamination is present and to support land release prioritisation and decision-making processes through the provision of evidence. BAC: is undertaken for areas that have been the site of armed confrontation and where no mines are suspected, there often remain large areas that are contaminated by unexploded ordnance that needs to be cleared. These pose physical risks to communities in proximity and heighten risks to aid workers, since injuries can occur when people who want to make use of land move or tamper with explosive items. BAC can contribute to two outcomes: the release of cleared land for future use and development, and the reduction of casualties in circumstances where people were previously coming into contact with the EH in the cleared areas. EOD: In many cases, the contamination with explosive hazards is scattered and not found within defined battle areas. Thus, it is not always efficient to carry out widespread search/clearance and HMA programmes establish EOD teams to react to reports of individual items of EH found. The EOD teams do not release large areas by clearance but their work is critical as they provide a rapid response to endangered communities.EORE: activities that encourage safe behaviour around explosive hazards. Risk education is both a tool for preventing suffering and promoting security amongst populations, a process intended to reduce casualties by modifying behaviour around explosive hazards.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Survey and clearance will increase personal security by mapping contaminated areas and removing the threat, while also facilitating land release, allowing people to rebuild, access renewed infrastructure, social facilities and lands for agriculture/housing. Risk education will increase knowledge on dangers and the adoption of safe behaviour, thus increasing community safety levels.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

DDG strives to mainstream protection elements into all HMA activities, not only to ensure compliance with standard protection principles but also to facilitate meaningful access, safety and dignity for all the beneficiaries. Through the provision of survey and clearance beneficiaries can reclaim spaces and livelihood opportunities while through EORE sessions they will be empowered and able to avoid the dangers posed by explosive hazards. By recruiting and training staff from the most vulnerable communities, DDG maximizes the chances for members of local communities to actively participate in the implementation of all HMA activities that aim to address their needs, in a meaningful and effective way. As all activities will be planned beforehand with the relevant national authorities, there is potential for areas to be prioritized based based on where the needs are the greatest, thus targeting the most affected communities.DDG integrates conflict sensibility in its programming by ensuring different population sections are targeted, not left behind or completely excluded, therefore reducing the chance of exacerbating conflict among already vulnerable communities. All activities undertaken are performed in close cooperation with national, regional, and local authorities and with the participation of beneficiaries throughout the entire project cycle.DDG has zero tolerance for abuse and misconduct, including Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, and goes a long way to ensure the prevention of abuse and exploitation in the work place, especially regarding the asymmetrical power relationships between DDG staff and beneficiaries. DDG ensures that all beneficiaries are aware that all humanitarian activities implemented come at no cost for beneficiaries and that all people are aware of their rights.
