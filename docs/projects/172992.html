Project Summary

The project intends to provide improved access to WASH services to a total number of 250,000 persons (76,500 women, 73,500 men, 51,000 girls and 49,000 boys). Of them, a total number of 139,000 will be targeted In North Darfur (42,000 Tawilla, 61,000 Kutum and 36,000 Malliet), 29,000 persons in South Kordofan (15,000 Kadugli, 7,000 Eldiling, 3,000 Habila, and 4,000 Elreef-Elshar), 18,000 persons in North Kordofan (3,000 Bara, 3000 Garab Bara, 7,000 Sheikan, 2,000 Alrahad 3,000 Umruwaba) and 64,000 persons in White Nile (30,000 in Guli, 14,000 in Alsalam, 8,000 in Um-rimta and 12,000 in Edwiem). The project addresses the three sector objectives while focusing in adopting CLTS approach for sanitation. The project will also provide complete/integrated WASH response in IDP camps and other settlements as well as improving the sanitation facilities in nutrition and education institutions in order to contribute to curbing the morbidity and mortality amongst the targeted population group. The project duration is one year.

1 - Needs

Inadequate access to safe water supply, compounded by poor hygiene practices and inadequate sanitation services impact the lives of poor families in the project targeted locations in North Darfur ( Tawiala, Kutum and Malliet ), South Kordofan ( Kadogly, Eldiling, Habila, and Elreef-Elshargi) North Kordofan (Bara, West Bara, Shaikan, El Rahad and Umrwaba) and White Nile ( Guli, Alsalam, Um-rimta and Edwiem). The mentioned project locations are suffering limited access to clean drinking water and poor water supply infrastructure with many non-functional water systems and broken storage tanks, compounding the already deteriorated water and sanitation situation. The vulnerable populations are averagely travel for 3 to 4 hrs kilometres for fetching drinking water, particularly in North Darfur. While the existing water infrastructure fails to meet minimum SPHERE standard requirements. Among those most affected are women and children, particularly girls are the ones travels long distances for fetching water which affects their health, safety and wellbeing.Due to the lack of appropriate sanitation services, the vulnerable population including IDPs, refugees, returnees, host communities in the project targeted locations are severely exposed to vector and water-borne diseases. A high percentage of the population have no access to any form of sanitation facilities, which results in mass practices of open defecation and poor hygiene practises in the camps as well as among the host community. This is even worsened by the absence of solid waste management which contributes to vector-borne diseases. With nearly a third of households (over 10 million people) practicing open defecation, Sudan has the highest prevalence of open defecation in the Middle East and North Africa region, posing grave public health risks to transmission of diseases such as cholera, diarrhoea, dysentery, hepatitis A, typhoid and polio. Open defecation is a manifestation of multi-sectoral deprivations be it poverty, malnutrition, education and affects women/girls disproportionately. The unhygienic water management and handling practises such as not regularly cleaning the jerry cans and water containers as well as inappropriate methods of water storage are among the most reasons for the prevalence of waterborne diseases. WASH services and facilities in White Nile , North and South Kordofan states are suffering from low budgetary allocation and pressure caused by significant numbers of IDPs and South Sudanese refugees around the big cities in some localities. This situation has led to inadequate and improper water and sanitation facilities. In IDP camp in Fata Burno in North Darfur and Guli locality in White Nile, most of latrines are damaged and out of service exposing the displaced population to serious health risks. Vector breeding grounds in camp also result in high incidences of diarrhoeal disease, jaundice and malaria. The poor sanitation services in schools is also observed in all targeted locations which could adversely affect the education outcomes. The available sanitation facilities in the schools are not adequate for all students. The hygiene and situation services in even worse in girl schools as girls do not have access to menstrual hygiene management kits as well as gender-sensitive latrines. In addition, the school teachers do not have enough skills to facilitate school-based hygiene promotion activities. Moreover, there are several nutrition centres lack appropriate sanitation facilities including latrines and hand-washing facilities. Lack of sanitation facilities and poor hygiene also contribute to prevalence of COVID-19. To improve the health situation of the community and build their resilience with providing durable solutions, Plan International will adopt Community Led Total Sanitation (CLTS) method. It utilizes a zero subsidies approach on sanitation hardware, which has been applied in different contexts in Sudan and has achieved good results.
