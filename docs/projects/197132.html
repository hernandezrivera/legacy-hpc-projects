Project Summary

WFP’s Programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first1000days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months and PLW in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM rates in the North West and South West regions
Key project activities include distribution of specialized nutritious food, screening and referral of cases of acute malnutrition to relevant treatment programmes, social behavior change communication on infant and young child feeding practices. WFP ensures that its programmes take into account the needs of men, women, girls and boys, as well as people with special needs. Distribution points are located in locations that do not pose a particular risk to women's access. Practical procedures are taken to reduce queues at distribution points, and to give priority to pregnant women, people with disabilities and the elderly. WFP’s programmes are designed and implemented with due consideration of population protection concerns or barriers for people with specific needs for safe and dignified access to Nutrition services

1 - Besoins / Needs

It is estimated that in 2023, more than 230,700 people including children under five and pregnant and lactating women will need nutrition services in the North-West, South-West, West and Littoral regions. Up to 48,800 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in these regions including 11,600 with severe acute malnutrition. Populations living in remote areas, IDPs in urban/peri-urban areas, and households/communities hosting IDPs are vulnerable to malnutrition since they are exposed to aggravating factors (food insecurity, limited access to water, poor sanitary conditions, sanitation and hygiene services, and morbidity due to overstretching of basic social services). The nutrition situation in the North-West and South-West regions remains unclear due to the absence of a comprehensive nutrition anthropometric survey using the SMART methodology. Proxy data from rapid emergency food security assessments (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 revealed a precarious situation with a proxy GAM of 5.1 per cent and 7.1 per cent in the North-West and South-West regions respectively. Analysis from Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening data conducted by nutrition partners from July to December 2021, showed a proxy GAM prevalence of 1.5 and SAM prevalence of 0.4 per cent in the North-West and the South-West regions. However, these findings are not conclusive of the nutrition status in the two regions as there could be pockets of high nutrition vulnerability that are not yet identified. Prior to the crisis, the North-West and South-West regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36 per cent and 28 per cent in the North-West and the South-West respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5 per cent for the North-West and 58.3 per cent for the South-West, of exclusive breastfeeding rates. Proportions of children 6 to 23 months who met Minimum Dietary Diversity (MDD) was reported to be low at 31.8 per cent in the North-West and 20.7 per cent in the South-West. Approximately 40 per cent (44.3 per cent in the North-West and 41.9 per cent in the South-West) of children aged 6 to 59 months were reported to have anaemia and with the ongoing humanitarian crisis, it is very likely that the poor situation of MDD has worsened.WFP’s Programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with a focus on the first 1000 days of life. The Programme targets all children aged 6-23months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in a food-insecure household. With a package of activities including, distribution of specialized nutritious food, screening and referral of malnourished cases to relevant programmes for treatment and social behaviour change communication in favour of appropriate IYCF practices in an emergency.
