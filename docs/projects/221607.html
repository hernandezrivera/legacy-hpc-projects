Project Summary

South Sudan's protracted conflicts coupled with natural disasters and returnees' influx, left 9.3 million people in critical need of humanitarian assistance in 2025. Conflicts in neighboring countries of Sudan and Ethiopia have added overwhelming pressure to the already volatile situation in South Sudan. Despite efforts exerted by humanitarian partners and governments to reduce vulnerability in hotspots, the crisis is still anticipated to worsen in 2025 due to conflict escalations, natural hazards, epidemics, and climate volatility. Food insecurity and lack of basic services are expected to accelerate vulnerability. ISNA 2024, highlighted that there will be difficult access to lifesaving shelter materials and nonfood items in 2025. This is due to natural disasters, newly displaced persons, and the influx of returnees from neighboring countries. 4.2 million people will need emergency shelter and nonfood items in 2025 worse than last year.
From the year 2019 until this moment, Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) has been launching emergency responses in hotspots including hard-to-reach areas, through the provision of lifesaving shelter materials and nonfood items to the populations affected, in Fangak, Panyijiar, Rubkona, Mayendit, Panyikang, and Leer counties. Since 2017, Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) has been playing a critical role in humanitarian response, carrying out IRNA, multi-sectoral need assessments, PDM, household surveys, safety audits, and KAP in collaboration with static partners and OCHA in locations affected by conflict and disasters. The assessment findings therefore informed service deliveries to the most vulnerable populations, IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents. In 2025, Hope Restoration South Sudan(HRSS) will aim to provide living-saving emergency shelter and nonfood items, support returnees' integration, recovery, and resilience in Fangak, Leer, Panyikang, Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Rubkona counties. The total project beneficiaries in all locations targeted are fifteen thousand nine hundred seventy-eight (15978) individuals equivalent to 2663HHs and can be broken down as follows:

1. Needs

Displacements of the population propelled by conflicts and disasters have directly complicated humanitarian situations in greater Uppernile regions, namely Jonglei, Unity, and Uppernile states. These crises of conflicts and natural disasters such as floods and their harsh consequences have triggered population movements, resulting in vulnerability. Heavy rainfalls backed up by flash floods from the river Nile and its tributaries redoubled water volume, eventually devastating human settlements including shelters, killing livestock, and washing away the farmland. Household items such as NFIs and other essentials got lost during long walks to the highest grounds. Such displacements driven by multiple facilitators have weakened community coping strategies and fuelled suffering. ISNA 2024 highlighted that 4.2 million people will need lifesaving shelter and nonfood items in 2025. Sectoral-specific need analysis conducted by Hope Restoration South (HRSS) and ADA this year in Panyikang county has underscored the presence of newly arrived returnees, with up to 6000 HHs suffering in Tonga town due to a lack of shelter materials and NFIs. The humanitarian situation in Tonga is heartbreaking and worsened further by the pressure of the frequent arrivals of returnees from Sudan through Southern Kordofan.Finland Church and other partners in Fangak County conducted an IRNA between September and October 2024, and the report revealed that 80% of the total population had been displaced by fluvial and pluvial flooding to Northen Fangak's highest grounds with limited access to shelter materials and nonfood items. Also, incidents related to Gender-based violence (GBV) and protection risks orchestrated by poor sheltering were reported to have threatened the safety and dignity of women and girls in Fangak County. Additionally, Returnees coming from Sudan through Lierra in Southern Kordofan experienced acute shortages of emergency shelter materials and nonfood items. IRNA conducted by HRSS and other partners in Leer confirmed the displacement of 6611 HHs plus 47000 newly arrived persons through the Adok Port and Bentiu routes. Their shelter and NFI situation was seen as very critical.Furthermore, an IRNA report from Panyijiar County carried out in August 2024, established that 6545HHs were affected and became homeless following the shocking effects of flash floods. The armed conflicts and the influx of returnees have added pressure to the already fragile situation and compromised the safety of children, lactating and pregnant women, unaccompanied elderly 60+, and persons living with a disability in these counties. Diseases caused by harsh weather resulting from lack of proper shelter and NFIs have exposed populations to malaria, pneumonia, and colds. In a nutshell, the humanitarian situation facing populations in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states will be a humanitarian catastrophe if nothing is done. This requires urgent humanitarian intervention to support the persons affected by providing emergency shelter materials and non-food items such as; plastic sheets, blankets, mosquito nets, sleeping mats, kitchen sets, and bundles of rubber ropes. HRSS will further enhance community resilience that prepares them for reintegration, and recovery. We will form accountability to the affected population committee (AAP) in an attempt to increase community participation and establish community complaint feedback mechanisms to track beneficiaries' feedback on satisfaction felt.

2. Project activities

Activity 1.1: Community consultation meetings: (HRSS) will lead community consultations with representatives of population groups at large. Attendees will involve community representatives; women leaders, youth leaders, representatives of persons living with disabilities, religious groups, opinion leaders, cultural leaders, local authorities, and local chiefs. This meeting will serve as a focal and entry point to the project locations and project implementation. Activity 1.2: Formation and training of the (APP) Committee: HRSS will form 6 AAP committees with 10 members in each location and train 60 members of AAP in all locations. Members of (the AAP) will be drawn from community representatives taking into account gender equality in all selection processes. Activity 1.3: Conduct S/NFI Needs Analysis: HRSS will conduct a 7-day S/NFI needs analysis with the involvement of other static partners to establish genuine S/NFI needs and identify project beneficiaries. Shelters and NFI need analysis to justify the pipeline request (PLR). During need analysis, (HRSS) will conduct FGDs and KIIs with stakeholders drawn from each Payam. This exercise will help the project team verify the prevailing needs of the beneficiaries before the pipeline request. (HRSS) will recruit 36 enumerators with 6 selected from each location with the assistance of the APP committee and the project team to complete the need analysis in 7 days Activity 1.4: Establishment or strengthening complaint feedback Desk: The CRM desk will track beneficiaries' feedback about the level of satisfaction served and the modality employed. Activity 1.5: Identification, registration, and verification in all locations: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) in collaboration with the AAP committee, RRC, and local authorities, will conduct identification, registration, and verification of the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and residents through effective coordination with community representatives and other stakeholders involved in the process.Activity 1.6: Transportation and storage of the supplies to the project locations: HRSS will hire trucks or consider hiring flights or barges to complete transportation of the approved supplies to the project locations for timely distribution to the persons affected. Activity 1.7: Planning distribution: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will develop a distribution plan, which will involve the selection of a distribution site and establishing a distribution timeline before distribution. Activity 1.8: Distribution of emergency shelter materials and nonfood items: Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will lead the distribution of emergency shelters and nonfood items to the beneficiaries for 7 days with lapses flexibly considered during the distribution. Activity 1.9: Post Distribution Monitoring(PDM): Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) will conduct a matured Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) for 2-3 weeks after distribution and will adopt S/NFI tools and methodologies.
