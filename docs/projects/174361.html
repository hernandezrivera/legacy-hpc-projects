Project Summary

Pakistan has been prone to emergencies (natural disasters, conflicts and displacements). In 2020, Pakistan has been affected nationwide by the unprecedented global COVID 19 and heavy rains/floods, especially in Sindh, Balochistan and KP, disproportionately affecting the most vulnerable communities, children/adolescents and especially girls. The emergencies have accelerated the crisis of education, a longstanding challenge for access, equity, quality and governance. ITA, an established CSO with 2 decades of experience of working in 4 emergencies, assessments ,school improvement and accelerated, second chance learning from ECE-to Secondary education through hybrid and tech based approaches seeks to support the recent Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP)/Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) in 9 of the target districts specifically for COVID 19 affected communities.
COVID 19 is unprecedented in its impact on health, livelihoods and education globally. To date, (Dec 12, 2020) 435,606 COVID-19 infections and 8724 deaths have been recorded in Pakistan(NCOC). At its peak COVID 19 affected 91% of learners in 188 countries or 1.66 billion students (UNESCO 2020). In Pakistan, 40 million students were affected during school closures from mid-March 2020 to mid-September 2020. The institutions re-opened for 6-8 weeks (primary to secondary/universities) and were shut again on November 26th, 2020 until January 11, 2021. The Government of Pakistan, recognizing the challenges of learning and enrolment losses especially vulnerable groups encouraged all schools systems (public and private) to engage in online/distance/hybrid/print learning platforms cognizant of ‘no-low to high tech’ contexts. The call for action to support learning outreach to parents/homes is also encouraged through cell phones/SMS/Whatsapp mechanisms. The federation has responded through extraordinary coordination through the National Command and Operations Centre (NCOC), with all relevant ministries including Ministry/Depts of Health, Education, BISP and EHSAAS social safety and stabilization support for the poorest. The Education Sector guidelines interventions and resource mobilization have been boldly spearheaded by the Ministry of Federal Education and Professional training (MoFE&PT) and provincial counterparts diligently; closely supported by donors and local partners.
Safe Schools & Accelerated Learning with Community Engagement-
In 9 target districts/areas, in close coordination with the Departments of Education/CEO/DEO, fully aligned with the COVID 19 guidelines, rapidly undertake 12 months gender sensitive humanitarian interventions for children and adolescents through formal and non-formal service delivery covering # schools and local communities. The non-pharmaceutical interventions cover six concurrent areas:
a) Risk Communication and Community Engagement (C4D) outreach through mobilization and training of SMCs/SCs/PTSMCs/PCs
b) Infection Prevention and Control through WASH support, IEC training and behavior change
c) Psychosocial Support and Child Protection- for mental health, mindful exercises and social emotional learning initiatives embedded in life skills education
d) Building Resilient Health Systems through active linkages with health services at school and local levels
e) Continuity of Education and Learning -through light touch rapid assessments (ASER/ICAN Tools) of learning losses and accelerated 45 days Chalo Parho Barho (CPB) or Teaching at the Right Level camps up to grade 6 (Literacy/Numeracy) –;
Learning will be supported by optimized used of distance learning technologies (no-low-medium) for students, teachers and parents for varied learning options.
f) Nutrition support where possible through linkages/partnerships with industry/CSR/govt./EHSAS/ donor programs.
All interventions will be highly gender sensitive, embedded in COVID-19 guidelines and partnerships and innovative system interventions

1- Project Activities

For the Safe Schools and Accelerated Learning Program in 5 districts , the core activities will entail the following, flexible to the contexts of schools being open or closed beyond January 11, 2021 for home based/school based interventions that support children and adolescents especially girls. In each district/area ITA teams will be mobilized to undertake the following: - a rapid needs assessment will be undertaken to identify vulnerable population, schools challenged on WASH facilities, SOP trainings, SMCs/School Councils functionality; psycho social -emotional/life skills assessment, technology access and most critically learning losses along with teachers’ preparedness for COVID-19 and learning - ITA is well prepared with tools in all areas having completed surveys that cover all areas of COVID-19 SOPs; WASH-Hygiene, Health Services/Needs, learning losses SMC/Community preparedness and knowledge of SOPs and support for learning at home/school through hybrid approaches innovative and accelerated learning and technology readiness skills. A rapid assessment will serve as a baseline that will enable interventions to be designed of Three Types : Type I : Community Mobilization ; SMCs Training on SOPs and making of Safe School Action Plan for COVID 19 along with Back to Safe School Campaigns if applicable-targeting the most vulnerable likely to drop out including girls and the poorest children. Back to Safe School Campaigns and/or Home learning community campaign comprise of unique engagement stations set up in a cost-effective manner at the school/community spaces. These station allow parents,SMCs/SCs students, teachers to engage in Child Friendly activities of ‘safe hygiene, health, learning losses, activity based learning, CoVID-19 board games, storytelling, arts/crafts and visible ‘expectations’ from a safe school (written in colored post its)- This will be the basis for engaging them with lively supplemental materials/ TLMs- at home and schools – including SOPs for safe schools and homes through impactful IEC materials • Type II: Safe School Protocols (Infection Prevention and Control/IPC) including minor infrastructure maintenance and repair and in some cases low cost WASH equipment as per need; this category mainly involves non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) –toilet repairs, safe drinking water mainly brought from IPC Kits and RCCE /IEC Materials material in local language, and support to school based, and linkages to nearby health facilities; and (FOR RCCE materials and IPC Kits all previous /current information will be the basis for action to avoid duplication) Type III : Interventions will address Learning Losses through accelerated 45 day camps to catch up on learning as well as the latest distance learning options that are relevant in each district/province. This will be undertaken with teachers in schools/local communities- as well as educated parents/sibling together with students. Lifeskills to offset Social Emotional Learning, Technology readiness; Mindful Exercises and School Safety Behavior change activities will be part of the learning package. Teachers will be given orientation on use of all learning materials, accelerated packages of TARL and supplementary materials for SEL and Life skills. Training on optimizing technology TV/Radio/Cell/Computers/Apps will be extended to teachers/parents/SMCs for the best option of distance learning that is accessible in local contexts IEC materials will be cross-cutting for all interventions for behaviour change. The first port of call for IEC will be existing materials already produced in multiple languages during COVID 19 by UNICEF and other agencies, fully endorsed by the Govt. ITA has recently made board games and books on COVID-19 (Snakes and Ladders and stories ) that will be widely used. The activities will be undertaken strategically to be mainstreamed into education systems in close liaison with govt.

2 - Project Indicators

The program design for one year emergency program interventions embeds stakeholders to participate actively in the tools of assessment as well as in the mitigation measures for prevention and care. This includes, both health/hygiene, child friendly learning areas as is the scope of the project. All key stakeholders will become conversant with the tools and their purpose so that they are enabled to see the trajectory being one where the outcome and output indicators can be measured – at home and the school The interventions and impact will be measured through a rapid baseline and endline (midline only if there are funds available) evidence for indicators agreed with the donors/govt. The target groups in each school includes: Students, Teachers/HT, Non-Teaching Staff and Parents/communities Outcome Indicators • At least 80% of target groups are aware that the use of face masks, social/physical distancing and hand washing can reduce risk of Covid-19 infection (prevention knowledge)• 100% of the target group perceive themselves at risk of Covid-19 (Risk perception)• School staff understand how to rapidly assess children’s learning with a light touch /set of tools at the classroom /school level (ASER Tools) Output Indicators (Programme Monitoring)• 90 % of schools staff and students understand COVID-19 pandemic and preventive measures • At least 80% schools have functional SMCs /SCs who understand COVID 19 and its serious implications for school safety• 90% of the parents can self-evaluate health of children before sending them to school• Learning Losses can be mitigated in 50% schools through a rapid accelerated learning program of 45 days for primary schools called Chalo Parho Barho or Teaching at the Right Level (TARL) (50% as funds may not be sufficient for all) • Schools/Communities (teachers/students/parents) provided with teaching learning packs/guides/supplementary materials and lifeskills handouts together with animations – links to Teleschool- Taleem Ghar and other tech ed initiatives • At least 70-80% of intervention schools do not have any new cases of COVID-19 after school reopening (better prevention, care and decision making)• 90% Students, teachers and 80% parents understand technology options for online Health and Learning A Project Dashboard will be set up for project indicators and outreach in each district with clear phase wise updates on targets reached and being met.
