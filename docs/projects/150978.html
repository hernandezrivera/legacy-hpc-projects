Project Summary

The interventions will address acute needs of crisis affected communities and lack of access to basic services, health and protection of services to reach populations in Mudug region. The project will help to address important needs related to health, WASH, and Protection. The IDPs, Refugees and Host Communities will be targeted through provision of health and related services.
The most vulnerable groups identified by Pmwdo internal assessment in Mudug region are the elderly, children, women, pastoral drop outs, disabled and destitute individuals who make up about 60% of the population in need. Of this vulnerable group, 50% are children with a proportion making 20% being children under the age of 5. The immediate needs as assessed by pmwdo include; limited access to clean drinking water, medicine and medical services, hospitals, mosquito nets, high level of illiteracy, among others.
Pmwdo proposed intervention To improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for crisis affected aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality for crisis affected identified beneficiaries in the region. Pmwdo anticipate to support 50,000 people by undertaking the proposed activities targeting men and women, boys and girls, adult and elderly, etc.

Needs

According to (UNICEF Somalia Humanitarian Situation Report July 2018), Despite improvements in the first half of 2018, major humanitarian needs in Somalia persist, particularly among IDP populations, due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict and marginalization. 5.4 million people are still in need of humanitarian assistance throughout Somalia, including 2.8 million children; although this number may decrease in the second half of the year following the impact of above average Gu rains and sustained humanitarian interventions. Malnutrition rates across Somalia remain among the worst in the world. In total, about 1.2 million children under-5 are projected to be acutely malnourished in 2018 including, 232,000 expected to be suffering from severe acute malnutrition. Over 4.4 million people need humanitarian WASH services, with 3.5 out of 5 people without adequate safe water to meet basic needs. More than 5.7 million people require basic health services, including critical needs in maternal and child health, as one in eight Somali children die before the age of five. Disease outbreaks such as AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with 6,979 suspected measles cases (72 per cent being children under-5) and 5,704 suspected cases of AWD/cholera with 39 deaths reported in 2018. Over 3 million children, out of 4.9 million in the country, are estimated to be out of school. More than 2.6 million people have been displaced, including over 1 million in the last year alone and displacement flows continue at critical levels. Exclusion and discrimination of socially marginalized groups continue to exacerbate high levels of acute humanitarian needs. More than 76 per cent of recorded gender-based violence (GBV) survivors are reported to be from IDP communities. Grave violations against children continue at worrying rates with abductions, recruitment and use, as well as killing/maiming reported as the primary concerns.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main risks facing the targeted population are:-i) Security risks - These affects all the targeted project population as a result of their vulnerability to almost all issues. Since their rights have always been violated and neglected. Most of them are not even aware of their basic human rights and therefore not easy to defend or claim it from the law enforces or violators. These among others can be prevented by ensuring that these populations are protected by ensuring that they are mobilized, sensitized and trained on their basic human rights to be able to understand and defend them at all times. (ii) Financial risks - The targeted population is poor because of their vulnerability and therefore access to basic services remains a dream for them. For a long time, they depend on support and assistance from well-wishers and humanitarian aids which also does not come their way at all times. Its therefore as a mitigation measure necessary to identify the specific needs of these population and train them on entrepreneurship or business skills areas and also identify and initiate for them income generation activities to reduce dependency while making them self-reliant.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The main risks of exclusion based on gender and age are in the areas of:-(i) Education - Where priority is given to the boys over the girl-child among the targeted population. There is, therefore, need to sensitize the targeted population on the need to educate both the boys and the girls alike.(ii) Employment and other available opportunities - In most if not all cases, men are the ones considered whenever employment opportunities arise, women are left out to take care of the households. There is a need to also empower the women through education and training to enable them to participate actively in the decision making and development of their country.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The following are among the identified risks facing the targeted population:-- The presence of heightened political, economic, and social grievances - Inter-group competition and conflict - Preparedness and capacity (of at least one party) to use violence and violate human rights - The perceived absence of effective pathways for nonviolent conflict resolution (the lack of alternatives). The appropriate prevention measures include among others the following: Sensitization, Empowerment through capacity building and training to the targeted population and financial assistance.
