Project Summary

The IRC continues to expand its humanitarian support in the NW and SW regions, where the humanitarian situation will likely persist, if not worsen. The project will implement an integrated, adaptive programme responding to both the risks associated with crisis situations and underlying vulnerabilities, aligned with our SAP and the HRP/HNO. We will continue lifesaving programming centred around crisis-affected population’s protection and basic needs, combining direct service delivery, support to existing services and partners, multi-purpose cash assistance, access to essential services and referrals. IRC will ensure that protection needs of people affected by human rights violations, gender-based violence (GBV) and child protection issues are addressed whilst local capacities are strengthened for a safer and more protective environment. IRC will support the most vulnerable households to meet their economic wellbeing and water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) needs, with the cash modality weaved throughout when feasible, in order to avoid harmful coping strategies, and support their capacity to face shocks. IRC will continue to identify the most vulnerable to health challenges and provide health support assistance through referrals as well as building community awareness on SRH issues. The project will continue to target the most vulnerable and hard to reach populations, including internally displaced populations (IDPs), returnees and host communities; in particular the most at-risk including victims of human rights violations, GBV survivors, women and girls, children (girls and boys) at risk of violence, abuse and neglect, persons with disability, elderly and other vulnerable people at particular risk of protection. The flexibility of the funding will enable us to respond to the most urgent needs will support us with increased access and reach through deepening community engagement, capacity building with frontline staff, key stakeholders engaged with programming and scaling up our partnership strategy. IRC will continue build upon operational and programmatic learning from utilising the flexibility of the PBA, which has in the past few years enabled the IRC to quickly respond to outbreaks (such as cholera outbreak in the SW in May 2022), to start crucially needed child protection activities in the SW – which we have now been able to scale up through other funding in the NW. We will continue to support and empower communities to better analyse the risks they face, and to develop their own strategies to reduce exposure to and mitigate the effects of these risks.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The ongoing conflict in the NW and SW regions led to a severe and protracted humanitarian crisis, which has, compounded with structural vulnerabilities and poverty, severely eroded the population’s protective environment and ability to prepare to and recover from shocks. Continued insecurity, displacement, lack of access to livelihoods, and restrictions of movements have exposed the host and displaced populations to large scale protection risks and human rights violations. Women and girls as well as men are at risk of GBV. From January until October 2022, 8,623 GBV cases have been registered in the GBVIMS in the NWSW, among which 645 cases have been registered by the IRC. Women and girls are particularly exposed to protection risks outside of their homes. During the same period, 949 cases of male survivors have been registered in the GBVIMS, including 6 by the IRC in the SW. However, it is likely be under reported because of the taboo regarding sexual violence against men and inadequate services to respond, despite the increasing severity of needs (GenCAP report, July 2022, see Additional Annex folder). During the same period, the IRC identified and documented 1,769 in the NWSW, affecting in majority men and boys, with the main protection incidents documented related to theft and destruction of property, physical violence, extortion of property, murder, kidnapping, illegal arrest and detention. Protection monitoring demonstrated that male children are especially vulnerable to severe violence which could result in death. Lack of civil documentation has been identified as a key protection concern of men and women in the NWSW (HNO 2023), with increasing reports of human rights violations and abuses including arbitrary arrests and destruction of property, or illegal taxation at checkpoints, especially for boys and men, since lack of civil documentation fuels suspicion regarding their possible belonging to armed groups. In this context of constant perception of fear and lack of security, populations, especially the most vulnerable such as children, are faced with negative psychosocial impact and stress that can develop into depression and trauma-related disorders and may lead individuals to adopt harmful coping mechanisms such as self-harm, substance abuse, violence. Protection partners, including IRC, continue to report grave child rights violations, including abduction and school attacks, directly impacting children’s access to school, retention in school and sense of safety. 62% of children are amongst the total displaced population within the NWSW (HNO 2023). Rampant insecurity and violence, and the consequences of negative coping mechanisms adopted by their family, including severe neglect, violence, child marriage and child labour in its worst forms (WFCL), loss of access to education, and birth registration, amongst other forms of abuse, impact both children and caregivers’ mental health, resulting in toxic stress, emotional distress, and community violence. All of which can have lasting and long-term negative implications on children well into adulthood . Findings from an IRC assessment conducted in September 2022 (see Additional Annex folder) in 9 conflict-affected villages within Manyu Division (SW), indicate that as a result of school closures and caregivers’ loss of revenue, and inability to provide for their children’s basic needs, children aged between 15- 17 resort to negative coping mechanisms which have a detrimental impact on their healthy wellbeing and development. Widespread cases of child labour (farm work, domestic labour, sexual transactions) were mentioned by 92% of the respondents, and about 90% respondents indicated they have noticed changes in children’s behaviours (e.g., post-traumatic disorders) since the crisis started including increase in criminal tendencies and drug use.
