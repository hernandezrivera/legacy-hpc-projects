Project Summary

Even with the permanent ceasefire declaration and signing of the revitalized peace agreement by the different factions; Jonglei and Unity states have continued to witness increasing bureaucratic impediments, cyclical waves ceasefire violations compounded by inter-communal violence, counter-insurgency further undermining efforts to create the previous status quo prior. According to the latest IPC September 2018 projections, 5,712,000 people,(women, men and children) are estimated to face unprecedented levels of food insecurity and malnutrition in the first quarter of 2019 and these population will lack access to adequate WASH in 2019, a 7% increase compared to 2018. The recent SMART surveys conducted in Jonglei indicate 20.7 per cent GAM rates, way above the WHO emergency threshold of 15 per cent. This is attributed to the recent and renewed fighting, cattle related fighting incidents in Jonglei and Unity states; coupled with perennial flooding in Jonglei. This could potentially worsen the WASH and nutrition situation among vulnerable populations in 2019 (July 2018 Boma State Inter agencies Rapid Assessment).
Jonglei states’ Duk, Bor South, Uror and Twic East are predicted to remain at Crisis levels (IPC 3 and 4) in January –March 2019, and would worsen without humanitarian intervention. Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong link between poor WASH infrastructure and food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children.
A joint OHCHR-UNMISS report, covering Southern Unity during the period of April-May 2018, documented the killing of 232 civilians, including 50 women and 35 children captured while fetching water. It reported 25 women were hanged and 32 civilians were burned alive, while at least 120 women and girls, including pregnant and lactating women, and girls as young as four years old, were raped and gang raped. In addition, at least 15 incidents of abduction involving 132 women and girls subjected to sexual slavery were documented. All these abductions happen when the women are seeking water and other social amenities. The report further identified the main perpetrators as uniformed forces, armed groups and youth militia. This is further exacerbated by the rapidly deteriorating economy with inflation at (850 CPI), dysfunctional markets and flooding (WASH Cluster reports 2018).
Two thirds of the population in targeted states do not have access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 60-minute distance, with many relying on surface water or unprotected water sources. The recent September 2018 IPC projection showing at least 69% of the population in Unity facing emergency, crisis and catastrophe mainly due to continued ceasefire violations especially in southern Unity – Leer, Mayendit and Koch which experience cattle raiding, revenge killings and multiple displacements, killings and grave human rights violations. This is in addition to increased impediments to access displaced beneficiaries, attacks to aid workers and assets in addition to across Unity state. According to UNICEF-WHO 2017 Joint Monitoring Programme, over 74 per cent of the population of Unity and Jonglei states in rural and urban areas practice open defecation, and the few existing sanitation facilities are mostly in disrepair. The high open defecation rates are exacerbated by the internal displacement of over 1.94 million people. Institutional capacity is fragmented, and communities that are unable to afford clean water have reverted to using contaminated water sources. These has subsequently resulted in overall prevalence of GAM of 8.6 – 11.7 95% C.I. and the severe acute malnutrition (SAM) prevalence (WHZ<-3 and/or oedema) of 2.3 – 4.1 95% C.I. (Urban Food Security Assessments, 2017).
Jonglei and Unity states have an estimated population of 490,000 in Emergency and 35,000 in catastrophe classification, (IPC September 2018) these have been exercabated by sustained insecurity, sporadic humanitarian access since 2013, lack of freedom of movement and extreme depletion of livelihoods. Should these conditions persist, the food security and WASH situation is expected to deteriorate further. This calls for immediate and unhindered provision of humanitarian assistance and close monitoring. Acute malnutrition has worsened compared to the same period last year. Populations in Koch, Panyijar and Mayendit counties of Unity state have extreme Critical levels of acute malnutrition, while other counties show Critical levels of acute malnutrition. Increases in Acute malnutrition is attributed to severe food insecurity, widespread insecurity, displacement, poor access to services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene” [(Urban Food Security Assessments, 2017, Key IPC Findings March - September 2018]. This has made the country to suffer from cholera outbreak for the longest time in history (2016-2017) where a total of 21,438 cholera cases were reported with 478 deaths (case fatality rate of 2.13 per cent). The current protection Trends report indicate protection needs of boys and girls is exacerbated by conflict with women and girls continue to be at risk of sexual and gender based violence, while boys continued to face recruitment into armed groups (2018 Protection Trends Report; UNICEF South Sudan 2017 Situation Report).
Relief Corps Organization seeks to provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees; mitigate WASH-related GBV; WASH in Nutrition for famine prevention minimum package of interventions; and WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level. The project will target 5,000 girls, 5,000 boys, 2,500 men and 2,500 women in Unity, Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria States. The beneficiaries will include elderly persons, PLW, people with disabilities drawn from the most vulnerable households, children men and women form both the IDPs, host communities and returnees. Activities will focus on; Improving water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and facilitating hygiene promotion throughout the targeted communities to encourage behavior change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices. The proposed action are in line with HRP 2019 and the cluster objectives of providing timely equitable drinking, sanitation and hygiene services to the population affected by conflicts, diseases and malnutrition and food insecurity .
RCO has a static presence on the ground in Jonglei, Unity and Eastern Equatoria States with competent and experienced staff, facilities and logistics that will be rapidly deployed to respond to the needs. The organization currently has ongoing WASH interventions in the target locations; that will complement the funding with positive leverages on costs. RCO has built very good rapport and excellent community long lasting goodwill with local state ministries linkages, state leaders, strong local networks and coordination with the WASH Cluster, UNICEF among other agencies on ground which will complement effective and rapid implementation.

Activities

• Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected population and those at risk of disease outbreak through water points repair and rehabilitation of 100 safe and lockable latrines and equipping them with hand-washing facilities to ensure 15,000 targeted beneficiaries practice safe, sanitary, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation to institutions like Health, Nutrition centers and schools to prevent malnutrition and WASH related diseases. Provision of water purification items (PUR and Aquatabs),• Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities • Mobilize and train 300 health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level • Distribution of WASH NFIs, capacity building in terms of preparedness through (training of pump mechanics and provision of borehole spare parts, training of Water Management Committee for resilience) water quality tests, both at water points and community level. • Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among affected population to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases through promotion activities, particularly hand washing for displaced and vulnerable communities and institutions.• Mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, protection, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to affected population and committing to improve effectiveness of cooperation with communities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

RCO will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project; by ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project cycle. Beneficiaries (both male and female) will be dis-aggregated and involved -equal representation - taking into account the different roles and needs of boys, girls, women and men); local leaders among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in implementation, monitoring and especially evaluating the project. All project activities will be aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the beneficiaries and communities. Implementation committees will be selected among IDP/mobile communities with linkages with RRC and local leadership to ensure targets are met. Their roles will involve but not limited to beneficiary identification, selection and verification; including participation in trainings and cholera preparedness activities. RCO will meet with the authorities and community members at early stage of project design and implementation to discuss project scope. Focus group discussions with various groups to collect feedback on the needs which is related to providing information on activities carried by RCO. Complaint/feedback system will allow beneficiaries to approach our team member in case clarification or follow up is needed. This includes establishing help desk, supported by both men and women responsible for providing information to the community members. Interaction sessions during distributions, demonstrations and training. Using IEC materials for the promotion of key messages.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Some messages are general for all population, depends on items provided. Specific sessions for women, girls, elders, people with disabilities, will be taking place to ensure safety and security during activities and on the way to Nutrition centers. Women and girls as key caretakers will be addressed on the use of proper hygiene (hand washing, hygiene related to caring for children up to 5 years of age) and preparation of nutritious foods. Breastfeeding is message specifically targeted for women of productive age.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

RCO is committed to restoring relationships and building safe and secure communities. RCO has internal regulations and fully adheres to a code of conduct signed by all staff including consultants We seek to prioritize the protection of all - especially the most marginalised and vulnerable adults and children - from physical, social and psychological harm. We will take steps to assess risks and avoid any adverse effects of our work which might expose people/ children to danger or lead to abuse. We believe that community members are the best actors in their own protection and will support their actions to stay safe, find security and restore dignity. The project will prioritize the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries including children and vulnerable adults in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm’. RCO will ensure non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and people with special needs. RCO will focus on mainstreaming crosscutting issues and also fully engage with the community among other relevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights while implementing various activities. This will be in addition to continuously identification of key protection concerns affecting children, boys, girls, men and women in the course of implementation including reporting to various Protection/Child Protection/GBV/FTR desks; and referrals to counterpart agencies implementing protection programs.

Various stakeholders including local line ministries, local leaders, beneficiary representatives, RRC among others will be consulted and engaged throughout all stages of implementation. Focus group discussions with women groups, HH visits and consultations on specific needs related to gender, age group, disability, also within various sectors. Individual interviews with community members, institutional users (Health facilities, Nutrition Centers, Schools, etc.) on needs, protection concerns, other. Community needs to be inclusive of a wide representation of people in an intervention. Equitable Representation must include: Men, Women, Elderly, Youth, Disabled, Ethnic minorities, the most Marginalized people, Boys and Girls.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

An active compliants and feedback mechanism will be in place at implementation sites where key issues will be listened through set up feedback desks propably manned by a third party. Key issues will be tackled in a transparent manner whilst involving beneficiary representatives, line ministries and local leadership. All our stakeholders can give feedback and make complaints, including direct and indirect beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries, partners, local leaders, group of persons, organizations, project staff or any person can provide feedback about the RCO program, activities, staff, implementing partners and project suppliers. Complaints are collected through paper based system on the field level, phones calls, emails, complaint boxes, during monitoring visits, established help desk, coordination/review meetings, recipient/complaint committees.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Communities members will be involved to actively participate in implementation including monitoring and evaluation of the project. Men and women are systematically asked to share information about needs and cultural preferences. M&E team is monitoring the community engagement through questionnaire designed to monitor safety of beneficiaries during assistance delivery and AAP. Community members are supporting repairs and rehabilitation of boreholes, construction of latrines and hygiene promotion campaigns.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

RCO is committed to mainstreaming protection and gender in all WASH activities. RCO will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification to ensure protection needs of different groups - IDPs, Host communities, returnees - men, women, girls and boys including youth, elderly and people with special needs are taken into account and to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, Nutrition and Education responses plan based on needs, access, safety, and gap findings, this will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and HH surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Barriers to access beneficiaries including impediments will be advocated for in collaboration with key stakeholders and counterparts to ensure unhindered access to targeted beneficiaries. The project has clear risk analysis and mitigation related to access, this includes logistic and security challenges. Ongoing coordination and communication with logistics cluster is done to support delivery of project items. NGO forum and OCHA is focal point for all discussion regarding access and permission. Support from Access Working group discussed where needed. In addition, RCO will prioritize needs and design all activities with the project interventions in line with the identified and verified needs. Protection assessments and analysis capturing information on access, safety, dignity, participation and power dynamics will be the key methodology where needs are analysed, prioritized with clear recommendations captured from the beneficiaries and later utilized to influence the intervention modalities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

The project will aim to mitigate all possible inequalities either attributed to gender, status among others are mitigated including ensuring an equitable targeting criteria is used, which will be transparent and culturally acceptable. RCO will conduct protection assessments and risk analysis, stakeholders mapping, and needs verification with clear AGD data to understand context dynamics, protection risks, vulnerability level and inform the WASH, S/NFI, FSL, and Education responses plan based on needs, access, safety, and gap findings, this will be done through focus group discussions, key informant interviews, meetings and house hold surveys with women, men, children, institution workers, community leaders, within IDPs, host community and returnee population. Awareness raising by use of IEC materials for protection specific messages. RCO will distributions at house hold level for people with disabilities, chronically ill.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Dignity of beneficiaries will be measured through consultative engagements, focus group discussions and interviews with beneficiaries, stakeholders, local leaders to ensure services and activities preserve dignity of beneficiaries and are culturally appropriate and will not in whatsoever, demean targeted people.
