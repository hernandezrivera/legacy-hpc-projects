Project Summary

INTERSOS intervention will focused on:
- Capacity building for GBV workers and health staff in-line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian response
INTERSOS provides protection staff with the capacity to identify, manage, safely document, and refer protection cases in a dignified way. Case management training and mentoring is conducted in all the phases of the project implementation to ensure provision of quality services to individuals and sharing lessons learned and best practices amongst protection practitioners. This will include strengthening the capacity of the protection staff on the safe and confidential identification of vulnerabilities; individuals risk assessment of persons in need of case management services; risk-rating to correctly assess the case-by-case urgency; safe and confidential referral of cases to specialized providers; and monitoring and follow-up of individuals based upon developed case planning.
- Community engagement to improve GBV services awareness, safe referral and work with opinion leaders to develop strategies for prevention of GBV.
INTERSOS supports the facilitation of protection help desks to serve as a hub for the affected populations where the community can access vital information, protection awareness information, and advice on services available, livelihood opportunities, feedback and complaints opportunities. The protection help desk is a space allocated in the health facilities - to avoid exposing people to stigmatization when accessing them - and serve as an entry point to improve community self-referral mechanisms.
INTERSOS intends to engage women, men, boys and girls of diverse backgrounds in informed events, information exchanges (including referrals). Appropriate IEC and BCC material for awareness and information-sharing among the community on the prevention of protection risks is being available for community members, including survivors.
- Confidential individual and group-based PSS interventions available for GBV survivors
INTERSOS intends to continue providing survivors with a safe space to gather, where case workers trained by the PSS/GBV specialist, will facilitate groups discussions and group counseling to confidentially and safely share their experiences and to start their recovering process.
The PSS intervention will be integrated in both the health and protection response.

Needs

The protracted nature of the conflict in the North East, Nigeria continues to reinforce the negative effects of pre-existing gender inequalities across the states, with women and girls being disproportionately affected. 54% of the displaced population are female; with 25% being of reproductive age needing access to reproductive and health services . Women and girls are most vulnerable to gender-based violence (GBV): 99.0% of reported GBV incidents between January- June 2018 were made by women .All forms of GBV are chronically underreported. Particularly, reporting an incident of sexual violence is deterred by the risk of stigmatisation, reprisals and/or marginalisation from the community as well as a lack of confidence in the judiciary system. Physical assault, denial of resources, and psychological and emotional abuse are among the violations most frequently reported, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners. Minors, particularly adolescent girls are also at risk of GBV which occur mainly in the context of child sexual abuse and early marriage.The targeting and abduction of women and girls by non-state armed groups as sex slaves and/or PBIED is a worrisome trend, at least 4,000 women and girls abducted/kidnapped since 2013. Whilst in captivity, survivors reported being subjected to rape and forced marriage amongst other types of GBV, which occurred in the context of possible sexual slavery and sexual exploitation. During the needs assessment, over 90% of people interviewed showed knowledge and understanding of issues related to gender based violence, however those were restricted to instances of physical violence (40%), verbal abuses (35%), harmful traditional practices (30%) (early and forced marriages) and sexual abuse (28%). Only a small percentage of respondents understood and reported instances of emotional (7%) and economic violence (8%), while 11% of the female respondents reported FGM practiced in their communities.When asked what instances of GBV were most commonly experienced by their relatives, peers and themselves, participants reported that physical violence (67%), sexual abuses (53%), verbal abuses (48%) and forced marriage (39%) were often heard of in their communities and were perpetuated particularly against women (81%) and children (40%). One of the most common answers during FGDs and KIIs given by both men and women groups, was related to lack of economic means, which leads women to sell their bodies. When participants were asked where they would usually report instances of gender-based violence against them or members of their families, they indicated that reporting to the Bulamas being the most common practice. Only 1% of the women interviewed mentioned reporting to NGOs instances of violence, including GBV.As well as specialized medical services for cases of clinical management of rape, life-saving services are not immediately available to survivors in many affected areas. In instances where they are available, health facilities often lack the necessary equipment, drugs, and trained personnel to provide adequate treatment. Survivors of rape require PEP for the prevention of HIV/AIDs to be administered within 72 hours of the incident, but clinical management of rape capacity is available to populations in only 8 of 27 LGAs in Borno state. Likewise, immediate medical assistance in cases of physical trauma, may be the difference between life and death for some survivors of GBV.Strengthened GBV prevention and risk mitigation mechanisms are also needed to support vulnerable persons particularly for households headed by females and unmarried women and girls in remote areas where the security parameter is limited to the LGA capital. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood opportunities will further mitigate occurrences of survival sex and sexual exploitation.

Activities

INTERSOS main goal is to provide protection to vulnerable and extremely vulnerable men, women, girls and boys, respecting the principle of equality and taking into consideration age, gender, and ethnical differences. Special attention will be given to individuals with specific needs, especially persons with disabilities, as they might be more exposed to violence and abuse.INTERSOS intends to provide safe and confidential identification, survivor-centered case management services for cases of gender-based violence including for groups at risks. This will include medical response in INTERSOS facilities when needed, for sexual, physical and emotional violence survivors in Dikwa, Ngala, Bama, Magumeri areas, while referrals will be confidentially made to other actors who provide medical care in Kukawa LGA. Case management services, emotional support and counselling and psychosocial support (in-line with IASC MHPSS standards - level 2 and 3 non-specialized support) is provided to the targeted population including assessment of needs and resources, development and implementation of safety and action plans, referral, appropriate follow-up including quality of referral check, and case closure (if and when appropriate) will be delivered. Emphasis is made on personal safety, high risk cases, and life-saving situations. These will be considered urgent cases and in need of immediate interventions, for whom individual data will be collected and immediately encoded to ensure confidentiality of information and promptly uploaded to the GBVIMS database.Referral to an appropriate specialized service provider for necessary services after consent (health, nutrition, legal assistance, safety and security, shelter, food assistance). – Coordination with other services providers through ad-hoc meetings for urgent cases. The Case Workers will ensure that at least for the first time, clients will be accompanied to the service to help with introductions and ensure the referral is understood by the agency receiving it. INTERSOS will conduct safety audit/monitoring to identify GBV risks and mitigation measures regularly in addition to conducting joint and/or partner safety assessments, monitoring and analysis.Post-distribution monitoring will be regularly conducted for clients receiving direct support from INTERSOS, while satisfaction surveys over services received by other agencies will be conducted to survivors receiving those. INTERSOS Case Workers will ensure that services provided by other organizations are safe and confidential for survivors to access them and clients will be referred to those only if found compliant with guiding principles for working with GBV survivors.
