Project Summary

The project aims to respond to immediate humanitarian needs identified in both camp and non-camp settings. The first outcome will be to improve state of health of conflict affected people in Fallujah and East Mosul. The second outcome looks at increasing knowledge and utilization of GBV services by IDP women, men, girls and boys residing in camps in Duhok.

Needs

1) In Non-camp settings (Fallujah and East Mosul):The proposed activities are informed by needs assessments and rapid gender analysis studies carried out by CARE and needs data triangulated from different sources:• Rapid Needs Assessment in Fallujah, Anbar, July 2018, with a focus on health: Structured interviews conducted with PHCC employees and representatives of the respective health directorate, augment by physical onsite inspections of PHCCs; • Inspections of PHCCs and hospitals in East Mosul during the first half of year 2018;• Focus group discussions conducted with displaced persons in IDP camps in Dohuk Governorate and discussions with cluster actors and BRHA, first half of year 2018;Meetings were held with the following knowledge carriers: 1) Senior representatives of the health directorate in Ninewa (especially in regards to East Mosul); 2) relevant Cluster Leads (Health Cluster, SRHR Sub-Cluster, Protection Cluster, GBV Sub-Cluster); 3) Community leaders, Mukhtars in East Mosul; 4) Camp managers and other employees of the Board of Relief and Humanitarian Affairs (BRHA) in Kurdistan Region of Iraq (for humanitarian needs in Dohuk Governorate) that are experiencing diminishing funding and projects winding down. Sexual reproductive and maternal health services are severely compromised due to lack of medical equipment and pharmaceutical supplies and the shortage of health professional’s particularly women gynaecologists due to combined reasons of insecurity and the government’s inability to pay full salaries to doctors and erratic medical supplies. Burdened with 524,736 returnees, Falluja’s already crippled healthcare sector is extremely overstretched and can barely cope with the demand for healthcare services. Despite the liberation of Anbar in June 2016, the government of Iraq, like the KRI government is struggling to cope with competing reconstruction/rehabilitation demands to restore basic level of services in education, water, sanitation, electricity, health, protection, etc. required by the returnees, IDPs and host populations. The situation is compounded by the diminished performance of the economy that has created rising unemployment, whilst expenditure on security has escalated in an effort to mitigate potential security threats. Discussions with the DOH during a recent rapid needs assess conducted by CARE in Anbar highlighted significant deficiencies in the health sector in Falluja. 2) In Camp Settings (Dohuk Governorate, Sheikhan, Chamishko, Essyan and Mamrahan IDP-camps):A Rapid Gender Analysis conducted by CARE in Chamisko camp in 2016, reported that two of the more prominent needs for women and adolescent girls health and safety were: • A lack of lights along the roads inside the camp. There are solar panels, but they only provide light for 20 minutes at a time; this is a safety issue particularly for women and girls moving at night.• Women reported vouchers for buying hygiene items are not enough for their menstrual hygiene needs. They need specific vouchers for underwear and menstrual hygiene items.

Activities

Output 1: Improved access to essential maternal, child health and primary health services in return areas (Fallujah, Mosul) Activity 1.1: Minor refurbishment of Al Wahda PHCC in Fallujah along with provision of a standby generator. Activity 1.2: Provision of essential medical devices, equipment and supplies for Al Wahda PHCC in Fallujah. Activity 1.3: Provision of essential nutritional supplements for pregnant women and newborns as well as contraceptives in Al Wahda PHCC, Fallujah. Activity 1.4 :Integrated awareness-raising sessions on reproductive health, pre- and postnatal clinical examinations, childhood vaccinations as well as nutrition for newborns and pregnant and lactating women and family planning sessions for women and men.Targeted health facilities are already operational and the interventions only seek to complement and address specific gaps to enhance quality of health services. The proposed reproductive and maternal health capacity training targets midwives who will continue to render services to women of reproductive age thus assuring sustainability of the investment and a smooth transition and exit beyond the project life period of 10 months. Sustainability is assured as the government will continue to support the health facilities in question, that are located in locations with a high demand for services. The selected PHCCs are already assigned to the DOHs and long-term operation is assured - it is expected that the financial capacities of the Iraqi government will develop positively in the long term after reconstruction and that corresponding resources will be available.

Protection Risk Analysis

Both the activities conducted in the PHCs and in and outside the camps will pay particular attention to the different challenges particularly faced by women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities in accessing basic health and protection services: 1. Movement restrictions: Placing a special emphasis on bringing services to women and girls. Women and girls have limited freedom of movement within the camp and have expressed similar challenges in East Mosul and Fallujah where they are required to travel with a man escort to access PHC services. In camps they often rely on moving in pairs or with a male chaperone. People with disabilities have even less freedom of movement. 2. Lack of Participation: Encouraging joint decision making in the household and regular consultations with women and men from the affected population to understand and adequately reflect their needs. Decision-making within the camp does not adequately ensure that women are consulted, nor their views and preferences taken into account. 3. Equal Access to Aid: Engaging men, community leader and camp management to ensure that women and men have equal access to entitlements and services. Rapid Gender Analysis assessments conducted in the areas of concern have revealed men tend to receive aid and generally control what is done with it. Special attention is needed to ensure that women have equal access to all aid and services.  4. Protection: Women, men, girls and boys experienced the conflict differently as a result of their perceived gender roles and therefore face different protection risks. Consulting women, men, girls and boys to understand what is the safest channel for them to benefit from primary health services, outreach initiatives and dignity kit distributions whilst simultaneously ensuring that information provision on GBV and protection referral pathways is a key component of all outreach initiatives.

Mitigation Measures

CARE implements certain measures to facilitate the access of women, men, girls, boys with and without disabilities of diverse ages and ethnic and language backgrounds by ensuring assessments are conducted by gender balanced teams familiar with the context, that questionnaires are culturally sensitives, that GBV related assessments are conducted by individuals with the required skillset and that all assessments include Key Informant Interviews (KII), Households visits and Focus group discussions (FGD) with a representative sample of the population. Women facilitators conduct the discussion with women and girls while men facilitators conduct the discussions with men and boys. When consulting people with disabilities the Washington Standards are employed. The main barrier and challenges are related to insecurity which can limit access to certain hard to reach populations.

How is your project accountable to affected population

All CARE interventions involve partners and community members during focus group discussions during assessment stage and collect relevant data from the communities and complement these with secondary data. The results are adapted to the project activities if needed, to avoid any negative consequences. A gender analyses is done to explore the consequences of project activities on women’s access to health facilities, access to basic services, resources, norms and traditions. Additionally a robust complaints and feedback mechanism will be implemented to ensure that affected populations are consulted, provided with feedback and are able to raise complaints throughout the project implementation. Awareness-raising sessions will be conducted to inform the affected population on the purpose of the hotline. CARE will deploy a focus on community based structures allowing for a broader dissemination of information about SRMH and to engage the community to identify context specific solutions to address protection risks and mitigate GBV including denial of women SRHR while reinforcing inclusion of PWD.CARE will also rely on IDPs Call center and use CWC, RPAs, monitoring missions, RNAs.
