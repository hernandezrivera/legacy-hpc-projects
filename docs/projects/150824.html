Project Summary

RRP through this interventions in the stated localities will work towards Strengthen and improve access to essential lifesaving health services aimed at reducing avoidable morbidity and mortality, reduction of maternal and child morbidity and mortality among and strengthen emergency preparedness and response capacity at all levels in order to mitigate and respond to communicable disease outbreaks in an efficient, coordinated, and timely for crisis affected and host populations in Beletweyne, bulaburte and jalalaqsi districts of Hiran region Somalia. As a way of working towards provision of Integrated life-saving primary health care services aimed at reducing the burden of morbidity and mortality; We will improve access to health care systems by providing health interventions to the residence within the target population to the nearest proximity. Health care services through consultation and management of childhood illnesses with an aim f reducing maternal and neonatal mortality will be given focus. ANC and BEmONC with referrals of the CEmONC cervices will be provided to the pregnant mothers. Staff and the community will be equipped with adequate information to manage emergencies and outbreaks that may arise from the dynamic natural and conflict related concerns. EDEWS reports shall be shared with the in line MOH and the cluster including proper coordination so as to respond to emergencies like AWD/cholera, measles, and some haemorrhagic fever outbreaks. RRP staff will be equipped with trainings on ANC and BEmONC, IMCI, Emergency response preparedness and CHWs equipped with skilled on community management and targeting. Community dialogue will be key in adherence with the AAP framework.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The Protection Risk Analysis highlights the extremely difficult situation in IDP settlements and collective centers across the country, in light of growing displacement, due to flood drought and conflict and continuing protracted and secondary/multiple displacement situations (including the particular challenges facing refugee returnees who get displaced again). The IDPs in these displacement locations frequently live in undignified and hazardous circumstances, where they face multiple protection risks/threats such as, unlawful evictions, overcrowded and unsanitary environments with limited access to basic services, exposure to explosive hazards, increased risk of Gender Based Violence (GBV), negative coping mechanisms such as child marriage and child labor, and tension with the host community. Children and young people (mainly male) in displaced situation, particularly those from Al-Shabaab controlled areas face risks of arbitrary arrest once out of those controlled areas, and therefore live in fear and sometimes isolation. IDPs continue to struggle to end their displacement and pursue voluntary, informed, safe, and dignified durable solutions based on their individual and household needs. By utilizing the reach, capacity, and weight of the whole humanitarian system, the safety, dignity, and well-being of these displacement affected populations (including the heavily burdened host communities) can be strengthened.
