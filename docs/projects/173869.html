Project Summary

As conflict continues in the Tigray region of northern Ethiopia, the number of refugees in Sudan continues to increase. As of 4th December, 2020, the number of new Ethiopian refugees was 47,960 and were, hosted in Kassala and Gedaref States. Registrations are continuing and people are being voluntarily transferred to the Um Rakouba camp site in Kassala, which is currently accommodating over 11,000 people. Access to clean drinking water, latrines, and waste management remains a critical challenge in all sites as the capacity of aid agencies is quickly becoming overwhelmed. Shelter provision cannot keep up with the current resettlement rate in Um Rakouba camp, and more communal shelters are required at the border sites. Without improving access to water in Um Rakouba camp, and the nearby villages, an expected increase in conflict between refugees and the host community will occur. ZOA is requesting 2,418,589 Euro to provide clean drinking water to 20,000 people in Um Rakouba, sanitary kits to +/- 7,000 people in Um Rakouba camp, support 2,000 household latrine constructions serving approximately 12,000 people, provide communal shelter to serve 1,000 people and provide household shelter serving approximately 3,000 people.

1 - Needs

WASH: This action will focus on Um Rakouba where significant WASH gaps are present. Um Rakouba campsite is being prepared to host 20,000 refugees as per the current planning scenario by UNHCR and COR and requires urgent construction and rehabilitation for latrines and existing water points. The planning scenario may increase, and estimations of a capacity up to 40,000 have been suggested. The WASH sub-sector working group has identified hand washing facilities, household and communal latrines, dignity kits, IEC materials for COVID-19 awareness messaging and hygiene promotion measures, plastic tanks, jerry cans, chlorine tablets and water trucking as critical needs. Many refugees are retrieving water from nearby Doka town. The short-term outlook must focus on improving access to water. UNHCR through the WASH sub-sector working group has signalled the need to shift from short-term water trucking due to high costs and risks in water quality. Water: Um Rakouba village is less than one kilometer away from Um Rakouba camp, which is split into Site A and Site B (about 1500 metres from each other). Doka town is about 5 kilometres away from Um Rakouba Camp. The current approximate population in Um Rakouba village is +/- 9,000 individuals and +/- 25,000 in nearby Doka town (not including surrounding sub-villages). Currently, water trucking is being provided to Um Rakouba camp by UNHCR with a 22m3 water truck provided by the host community in the main distribution tank in Um Rakouba and Doka town. Rour 5m3 water bladders are provided by UNICEF in Site A and B. Approximately 6 water taps have been connected to these water bladders in both sites, serving an average of 210 refugees per tap. There is no water point in Um Rakouba camp.Um Rakouba village currently has eight boreholes. Four are functional, one is collapsed and three have a very low water yield (as determined by pumping test, conducted by ZOA in November 2020). The power source for these two boreholes are in nearby Doka town, which often experiences power outages upwards of 8 hours a day. They require alternate power sources. ZOA, with UNHCR and IOM, consulted the State Water Corporation (SWC) for their technical recommendations for how to meet the water needs of Ethiopian refugees, host community and account for seasonal fluctuations in water use over the coming months. SWC recommends the drilling of 8 new boreholes at a suitable depth, in the Rashid basin, a water-rich area of Gedaref.Sanitation and hygiene: There is a critical sanitation gap in Um Rakouba camp with an unclear plan from the WASH sub-sector working group on latrine construction and/or rehabilitation. When the planning figure for Um Rakouba camp was 10,000 (this has been raised to about 20,000 and possibly higher), the sanitation/hygiene gaps were as follows: 200 household and communal latrines with slabs, 100 hand washing facilities, 50 cleaning materials tool kits, 200 shower stalls, 20 laundry facilities and approximately 1,000 IEC materials. The planning figure is now doubled, at 20,000. While some partners (WHH and UPO) are doing emergency latrines and latrine rehabilitation, the safe sanitation facility capacity of current implementing partners is not sufficient to meet the projected capacity of Um Rakouba now that the expected influx of refugees has doubled. UNICEF has constructed +/- 3 communal latrines, each with about 20 stalls. Hygiene will soon become a dire issue, which can lead to contaminated water sources and excreta-related, water-borne diseases. If cases of disease increase, this will overwhelm the already scarce health facilities available in Um Rakouba. With health and nutrition actions struggling to keep up with the influx, access to clean water and appropriate sanitation facilities must be prioritized. Shelter & NFIs: Shelter gaps remain at border points (communal) and camps. In Um Rakouba, the population is now exceeding 11,000.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of protection mainstreaming, ZOA and partners will ensure the following during the project’s design and implementation: Gender sensitive latrines and water cleaning points separate for boys and girls will be constructed in schools while in public social places, separate latrines will be constructed for men and women. Basic facilities including lighting will be installed in public places to ensure safety to women and children when using them even at night. Further, latrines constructed in public places through this project support will include special ones with ramps for safe and dignified access by PWDs especially those who are physically challenged. Latrines in public places and schools will be labelled to clearly demarcate those for females from males.In the water infrastructure rehabilitation and development activities, the project will ensure to put in place the following protection measures: Ensure that the water points will be at points unanimously by the community to avoid any tensions between different segments of a community; the water points are at a central place easily accessed by most users; and the timing for access; if regulated, is convenient for majority of users. Further, water pumps will be well positioned so that even children, old and pregnant women can easily use; separate watering points for animals;

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of accountability, ZOA has a complaints reporting mechanism in place in all its programming that is adopted by all implementing partners. The reporting mechanism is part of a broader three-pronged organizational integrity framework (IF) made up of i) code of conduct (coc)signed by all staff and consultants; ii) complaints reporting mechanism for staff, partners and beneficiaries and iii) Addressing complaints. Under the CoC are child protection policy, PSEAH and fraud & corruption, among others. Each new ZOA project develops a beneficiary reporting mechanism that is shared with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project through sensitization so that they know their rights on where and whom to report to incase they have complaints
