Project Summary

UNOPS proposes a project to repair and rehabilitate public schools through the rehabilitation of classrooms and school buildings, installing gender-responsive and disability-adopted WASH facilities, and provision of school furniture and supplies in order to support the vulnerable students, teachers and parents in targeted communities.
This project will target public schools in the locations with highest needs (severity 3/4/5 level) in Aleppo, Homs, Hama and Rural Damascus. The needs were identified based on the UNOPS technical needs assessments, and consultations with the authorities. The direct beneficiaries of this project are estimated to be 23,800 students and staff in public primary and secondary schools (13,227 girls, 9,978 boys, 339 women, 256 men, and 720 PWD).
In Syria, UNOPS is currently implementing infrastructure projects focusing on provision of renewable energy solutions in 10 PHCs and schools in Hama Governorate. Rehabilitation works, supply and works of solar photovoltaic hybrid systems and provision of a medical waste management system is also the ongoing project in one of the Homs Grand Hospital buildings (the Kidney Building). UNOPS also provides operational, HR services, project management support and administration to other UN agencies. In addition, UNOPS commenced the Nexus Response Mechanism (NRM) project, aimed at reducing communities’ vulnerabilities through programmes that increase their resilience through capacity development to prepare for future shocks, while addressing their immediate needs and challenges, across Syria.
Building on its extensive experience in implementing infrastructure and procurement projects in the region and in Syria, UNOPS’ proposed plan will contribute to the Education Sector’s Objective 1: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth. It is expected that UNOPS will address the needs of repair or rehabilitation of classrooms and schools buildings, provision of school furniture and supplies, improving gender-responsive and disability-adopted WASH facilities and provide solar power systems. Particularly, electricity shortage which results in impeding the availability and quality of education services, specifically overcrowding schools with double shifts. According to the assessment UNOPS took, on average, only two hours of electricity are supplied, so this hinders the quality of education services for the students in the second shift. For improved safety and security of access to schools, UNOPS proposes to carry out repairs of access roads to the rehabilitation schools with solar lighting (Note: Repair of access roads is planned under ERL sector), and rehabilitation of boundary walls for the schools. The facilities and the access roads have limited or no lighting, which can have significant protection risks on residents, returnees, IDPs) such as GBV, limited education opportunities and potential health-related concerns, particularly for children.
Given the above, it is expected that UNOPS will contribute to Activity 2: Support the provision of formal education to children and youth. The activity cost was calculated based on UNOPS’ ongoing project experience, the technical needs assessments conducted in 2023, and market surveys. The activity cost per school includes rehabilitation works, design and installation of PV systems for electricity supplies and related mechanical works (Note: the activity cost was calculated per school, not per beneficiary, with an assumption that 8 large-sized schools/21 classrooms per school, and 8 small-sized schools/9 classrooms per school).
To mitigate PSEAH risks, UNOPS plans to conduct gender-responsive needs assessments with female and male beneficiaries, including marginalized groups in order to identify their specific needs as well as perceived SEAH risks and reflect these in the design and implementation of the project.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

During the implementation, UNOPS will take measures to protect the security and safety of the beneficiaries, with particular attention to SEAH risk management through robust PSEAH training programmes to the project team and the selected contractors and strict adherence to UNOPS’ environmental/social safeguards measures that include PSEAH safeguards. Given the focus of the project on marginalized populations including children and PWD, it is crucial to include PSEAH measures in the project. PSEAH is particularly important in projects with high degrees of direct contact between humanitarian actors (including contractors implementing on behalf of the UN) and local community members (beneficiaries) as in the case of the proposed project. For the project design, protection risks have been identified and mitigation measures have been developed. The main risk would be related to potential exposure to violence and abuse to the beneficiaries, particularly for students (girls and boys) during the implementation, when the UN staff and contractors commence the work on-site. The mitigation action would be to conduct PSEAH awareness training to the project team/contractors, and design the work plan to avoid or reduce contacts with the beneficiary (e.g. the work schedule is planned for after school hours, school holidays,etc.). UNOPS will also put grievance mechanisms in place, including hotline, complain box in the project sites, and take remedial actions. According to the Project Risk Analysis, the identified risks are as follows: 1) fraudulent activities occur causing UNOPS operations to be suspended, 2) delays in design works (if original drawings of the building structure are not available/provided to UNOPS), 3) delays in receiving the necessary permits/approval from the relevant authorities, 4) lack of awareness of contractors on health and safety measures at the project site, which may trigger incidents on site, 5) deteriorating security situation and kinetic environment that experience frequent incidents which might hinder accesses to project sites. UNOPS takes mitigation actions against these risks, which are 1) implementing a robust internal control framework with oversight mechanisms to mitigate the risk of fraudulent activities. Regular monitoring, quality assurance, reporting systems in place. Vendor due diligence and performance monitoring in place, 2) coordination with the relevant authorities for their continuous support to provide the required technical information, 3) active stakeholder management through Project Board for agreement/endorsement. 4) mandatory HSSE training and inclusion of HSSE requirements in the tendering documents, and the selected contractors will be guided by the UNOPS team. Any incidents on site will be treated as per UNOPS HSSE procedures. 5) close monitoring of the situation and coordination with UNDSS and the relevant stakeholders, and operational readiness and position staff to support alternate activities.
