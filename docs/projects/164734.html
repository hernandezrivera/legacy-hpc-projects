Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 11.06 million (49.8% male and 50.2% female), of which 4.6 million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.1 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 341,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 480,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. More than 70% of the population in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria.
Overall, 7.9 million people are estimated to be food insecure (including 0.57 million severely food insecure and 7.3 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 45,500 small-scale herding and farming households (equivalent to 273,000 people) in Syria. The objective of the proposed action is to improve food security and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable herders through restocking, the provision of animal feed and capacity building. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Production assets of 10,500 vulnerable, crisis-affected households restored through restocking.
This output aims at restoring the productive capacity of crisis-affected households through the provision of small ruminants (two female ewes/sheep per household, in line with sector response package recommendations for livelihoods)
• Output 2: Livestock productivity for 35,000 vulnerable crisis-affected breeders is maintained.
This output aims at improving the availability of animal feed for the purpose of asset protection for 35,000 vulnerable households (400 kg of feed per household, in line with sector recommendations) as well as boosting milk availability through enhanced livestock production.
• Output 3: Improved skills and livestock management capacity of 45,500 households
This output aims at supporting all project beneficiaries to enhance their livelihoods through strengthened skills in livestock production and management.
It is expected that the introduction of agriculture-based livelihood protection support in the current protracted crisis will increase the local availability of food, improve self-reliance and food and nutrition security of Syrians, especially the most vulnerable.

4 - Needs

As the crisis protracts, the capacity of small scale livestock keepers to generate income and to access food erodes. A yearly joint WFP and FAO Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) conducted since 2016 estimated sheep,goats,cattle and poultry losses up to 40%, 30 %, 40% and 55% respectively. A further reduction in sheep and cattle population were reported during 2018/19. In the most affected areas by insecurity and active fighting farmers were forced to leave their villages and farms for their security and safety and hence leaving behind their livestock. Furthermore, General Poultry Association (GPA) estimates that 80 to 90 percent of poultry farmers have left production operations due to lack of operator confidence in the recovery of the sector. The continuous devaluation of the local currency is constantly affecting the animal feed price which has soared since 2011 and farmers cannot afford the required quantities for their animals compelling them to sell their livestock at giveaway prices. Asset loss will specifically affect women as traditionally they are the caretaker of small ruminants and have less capacity to access resources for the replacement. The constraints for livestock production include prevalence of common epizootic diseases, low successs rate of artificial insemination, high livestock feed prices. The overall impact is high prices of livestock and their products which generally showing an upward trend compared to previous years. According to FAO damage and needs assessment of the overall agriculture sector for the period 2011 2016, the cost of damage and loss to the livestock sector is estimated to be USD 5.5 billion. The livestock sector faces two principle challenges related to animal production and animal health. The protection of the remaining livestock assets is essential and together with restocking of livestock for poor herders (women headed households in particular) who have partially/totally lost their herds.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiaries selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population examples IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of assistance/assets. Mitigation measures: Establish a community based beneficiaries’ selection and community based monitoring.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2. Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities, conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3. Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: likely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework. Diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders) through more coordination with the hub in Damascus.4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major. Mitigation measures: Continuous advocacy and negotiations with Government and counterparts for partners to have access to targeted areas. Establish agreements with counterparts to facilitate the implementation of project activities. 5. Risk: Deterioration of the relations between IDPs and host communities in the period/area concerned. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Assistance is balanced and not directed only to one specific group.
