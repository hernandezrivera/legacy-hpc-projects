Project Summary

The project refers to the following strategic and cluster objectives:
SO2. Respond to the protection needs and strengthen protection of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, with due regards to international norms and standards.
PCO2. Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs.
and
SO3. Improve the resilience of conflict-affected population, including IDPs, and strengthen national/local response capacity to ensure their access to quality essential services in collaboration with development actors in areas where feasible.
PCO3. Improve social cohesion and resilience of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, through community-based protection and identification of durable solutions.
PIN has been providing PSS services to individuals affected by the conflict, and promoting activities, which enhance community cohesion and resilience. For PSS services, PIN sends mobile teams of psychologists and social workers to the conflict zone to provide free psychological counselling and individual protection assistance to adults and children in order to promote psycho social well-being. PIN also manages a free, 24-hour psychosocial hotline, and provides trainings to local PSS practitioners and other protection partners. The team of PSS specialists, focusing on area along the contact line, alleviates, mitigates, and averts life-threatening physical and psychological harm or threats to people directly affected by the conflict. While PIN PSS services target all affected people who needs such support, two key groups are being prioritized in terms of vulnerability specifics, such as children and people with disabilities.
PIN is responsible in front of the beneficiaries in terms of the main following accountability commitments and prioritizes:
- Accountability to affected population: PIN involves people affected by crisis in key decisions and processes that affect them, and have effective communication and feedback channels that engage all sectors in a community.
- Leadership/Governance: compliance response mechanisms that are integrated into project proposals, strategies, monitoring and evaluations, trainings and performance tracking management, partnership agreements etc.
- Information Provision: accessible and timely information to beneficiaries on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its beneficiaries over information provision.
- Beneficiaries’ Feedback: actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.
- Non-discrimination: PIN ensures that all vulnerable groups of women and girls, men and boys, have access to the assistance.

1 - Needs

Conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to influence individuals' behavior. The need for psychosocial support is often viewed as not visible and not measurable as other material needs, but it needs special attention. Health-related risks (including psychosocial conditions) impact people in different ways, depending on a range of factors, including age and gender. 78% have applied negative coping strategies (the highest level since April 2016) (HNO, 2019). With almost five years of violence, the mental health and psychosocial wellbeing of conflict-affected people continues to be a key challenge. The consequences of displacement and conflict such as family separation, loss of livelihoods, lack of access to services, and anxiety about the future are some of the psychological distresses. Lack of mental health and PSS is a key gap in protection and health in conflict-affected areas, particularly near the ‘contact line’. A recent World Bank scoping study found that one of the key gaps in programming in eastern Ukraine is PSS support. PSS was one of the top five protection needs identified by people living along the 'contact line' in the Area-Based Assessment (ABA).Two-thirds of HHs do not know where to access PSS services and 17% of rural HHs report that there is no place to access services in their settlement. Psychosocial issues have been reported amongst all age groups, including children, the elderly, and working-age men and women. Physiological signs of distress, such as difficulty sleeping, and emotional difficulties, including grief, fear, and crying for no reason, were also reported in a recent Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) assessment. In particular, there remains a gap in MHPSS support for working-age people. In NGCA the situation is even more dire, with only a few psychologists and there are many areas where there are no functional social services at all. One-third of older people report feelings of isolation and loneliness, which is compounded by the lack of social services for those living close to the 'contact line' in GCA and many parts of NGCA. There is a clear need for psychosocial support to all categories of conflict-affected populations in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. Also, there is insufficient coverage of people and families in need of social case management in line with limited local capacities (e.g. two-thirds of social workers have left Donetsk oblast since 2014). PIN proposes to fill this gap by individual protection assistance and case management.The children’s morale and psychological state are low, which affects their school education negatively. As hostilities affect the social and economic lives of the children’s families, in turn, parents’ emotional behavior involves improper parenting and negative practices causing even more harm to children. Schools lack qualified staff, and taking into account the mean age of the pedagogical staff. PIN protection team confirms the UNICEF’s findings that the need for psycho-emotional support is still not fully satisfied. The primary focus of the PSS implementation actions will be on the most vulnerable (women and children, IDPs, people with disabilities, retired large families, single mothers, low-income people, victims of the military conflict) and those most at risk living in the areas along the ‘contact line’, where daily fighting has severed their access to basic services and access to healthcare. To effectively address the needs, PIN will include women in decision-making at all levels, promote and support women’s leadership and increase support for local women’s groups. Through its work, PIN will promote gender equality, strengthen the participation of women and men in the implementation, and promote the enhanced protection of women and girls from SGBV.

PCO2. Improve social cohesion and resilience of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, through community-based protection and identification of durable solutions.5. Capacity building for PSS practitioners (psychologists, educators, social workers, local activists/volunteers, representative of international organizations, etc.) Rationale: As an extension of 2018/2019 programming, PIN will continue to provide psychosocial support for children and parents, adults in vulnerable communities in the form of mobile teams of psychologists and social workers, which will bring such PSS services (awareness raising sessions, individual/ group consultations/events) to the field primarily across the LoC. The cognitive and psychosocial programs will be directed to stabilize and improve the psycho-emotional state of children and adults, reduce the risks of adopting any kind of self-inflicting coping strategies and reduce the negative consequences of the military conflict (anxiety, aggressiveness, neuroses, imbalance, fear, apathy and etc.), form anti-stress coping strategies, skills of coping behaviour in crisis situations, skills of self-regulation of psycho-emotional state, formation and development of resilience.Based on identification and referrals from local communities (incl. from local social workers), PIN will provide direct, non-cash individual protection assistance (IPA) to individuals with specific protection needs and in difficult circumstances. Conceptualized as an emergency fund, this is meant to address some of the most urgent needs of individuals living in frontline communities in a timely and diversity-sensitive manner. In-kind assistance may include wheelchairs or walkers for persons with disabilities, the upgrade of shelters to meet special needs, the provision of essential medicines, etc. PIN experience shows that although many families are able to cope on their own, there are risk factors which can provoke the emergence of crisis conditions with which families are unable to cope independently. It is important to identify families at risk in time to prevent negative coping strategies. PIN will support the such families in difficult life circumstances through individual case management.Strengthening local capacities is integral to PIN’s approach as it contributes to improving psychological wellbeing among conflict-affected populations. PIN will strive for building capacity of local partners/specialists as a means to increase access to aid and ensure meaningful transition and exit strategies, which are part of a process of moving from emergency to rehabilitation and development. This activity will target identified community specialists in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, especially along the line of contact in GCA. An assessment will identify their needs to improve their technical competencies concerning PSS services, including through workshops on PSS, Minimum standards for child protection in humanitarian actions etc. This will equip local staff/specialists to improve their own services and continue applying the newly acquired competencies independently, beyond the project intervention phase. Such improved capacity will have long-term effect for their further work and for the well-being of beneficiaries and reduce their dependency on humanitarian aid. This will support targeted communities in acquiring, developing, and engaging the resources they need to face an environment characterized by uncertainty.
