Project Summary

The city of Benghazi has serious need of clearance of the explosive remnants of war, so that critical infrastructure including schools, hospitals, electricity and water supply, as well as residential areas, to enable reconstruction and for those displaced to make a return. Both children and Adults require risk education to teach them the dangers of unexploded ordnance and how to adopt safe behaviour in such an environment. Additionally, medical professionals need support in victim assistance and their clinics lack a supply of equipment for victims of unexploded ordnance. DCA have worked in Libya since 2011 and have consistently had an office in Benghazi, from which national and international staff deliver a wide range of projects both through direct implementation and through our civil society organization partners. For this project; DCA will select and train 2 teams of deminers/searchers and then lead and mentor them in the clearance of UXO in priority areas of the city, so that essential services can be reopened and the citizens of Benghazi can access them safely; DCA will, through its CSO partner, deliver risk education to children and youth in schools, colleges and universities, and to adult workers, and female headed households; DCA will also continue to work with medical professionals involved in victim assistance and provide further support to them in terms of training and additionally provide walking aids and wheelchairs to specialist clinics and in coordination with the Ministry of Health and Libyan Red Crescent. In essence the project will address 3 critical pillars of mine action and benefit men, women, and children in areas most affected by conflict and suffering from contamination from UXO. The project will be delivered by DCA’s national and international staff in Benghazi and have a duration of 18 months.

1 - Needs

a. Clearance of the dangerous explosive remnants of war; Everyday there are accidents from the unexploded items that litter Benghazi following years of fighting. These items, causing injury and fatality, pose a barrier to accessing education, medical services, and essential utilities such as water and electricity. DCA’s propose action will enable access to these services, enable the re-instatement of utilities and contribute to reconstruction and regeneration, increasing not the just the safety or citizens but increasing their wellbeing and quality of life.b. Risk Education to increase safety and reduce injuries and fatalities; Unexploded ordnance (UXO) in Benghazi affects a wide range of people from curious children to youth, adult workers and returnees. Injury and fatality is commonplace, particularly amongst children playing, utility workers and returnees cleaning out repossessed houses. Through our civil society organization partner, DCA will deliver risk education in schools, colleges, universities, and in places of work. Additionally, our partners female members will be able to gain access to female headed households. An SMS message media campaign will also reach all sections of society. People will be better informed as to the dangers of UXO and know how to adopt safer behaviour in areas where contamination is present. c. Assisting the victims of the explosive remnants of war; Research by INGOs and the UN have borne out the critical lack of training and equipment to support those in Benghazi who have suffered injury from the explosive remnants of war. Through our CSO partner who specialises in working with people who have disabilities, DCA will provide training to medical professionals and enhance their knowledge on victim assistance in matters such as re-integration and psychosocial support. Additionally they will coordinate with the ministry of health and red crescent over the specification of required equipment and handle the distribution of much needed wheelchairs and walking aids. This will give victims mobility and increase their wellbeing and quality of life.

2 - Activities

a. Clearance of the dangerous explosive remnants of warTwo teams of deminers/searchers will be selected and trained to international mine action standards and mentored by international technical staff to clear priority sites in the city. The teams will be QAd and then accredited by the Libyan Mine Action Centre. Teams will be tasked according to the priorities of the local authorities and communities most at need. To that end, teams will conduct EOD spot tasks in response to items found by returnees and also battle area clearance in more substantial sites such as hospitals and schools, so that they may reopen. All reporting will be conducted to LibMAC and into IMSMA. The training of these individuals plus medics will leave sustainable capacity.b. Risk Education to increase safety and reduce injuries and fatalitiesOur CSO partner will receive refresher training and re-accreditation to conduct RE in the city. Training will be delivered by DCA’s international RE coordinator. Teams will design and print fresh RE collateral to distribute during sessions, including posters, leaflets and comic books. A risk education message campaign will designed and delivered via SMS to Libyana and Al Madar networks. M&E will be delivered by DCA’s Field Office in Benghazi.c. Assisting the victims of the explosive remnants of warDCA’s CSO partner will receive training for our VA Coordinator and will devise a needs assessment to collect secondary information, in additional to some of the more recent more substantial studies. Based upon these finding the partner will devise a project delivery plan for both the training of medical personnel and the procurement and delivery of the medical equipment. DCA will oversee the distribution of walking frames, walking sticks, and wheelchairs to specified clinics in coordination with the MOH and LRC.

5 - Mitigation measures

1. Robust security monitoring and reporting practices will be adhered to during the course of the project and DCA will maintain a minimum number of evacuation routes at all times.2. DCA teams will work with local authorities to ensure that suspect hazardous areas are clearly marked and will deliver Risk Education messages, in particular targeting returnees, and IDP sites, to educate them to the risks of ERW/UXO.3. DCA procurement and anti-corruption procedures adhere to Libyan national law, as well as DCA global procurement regulations. Measures are taken to prevent support of black-market practices, prevention of breaking financial sanction regulations, and ensure safe VFM practices. 4. National staff retain a low profile outside of work hours, including not wearing uniforms outside working hours. Staff uphold close relations with involved communities to ensure high levels of acceptance. All national staff are protected by Libyan labour laws, including healthcare and life insurance. All staff have received basic life support trainings in the event of accidents during and outside working hours. Movement of national staff only takes place after acceptance of local stakeholders. Staff are all vetted through international and national security systems.
