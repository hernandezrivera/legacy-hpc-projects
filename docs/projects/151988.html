Project Summary

This project is designed to focus on an increased access to safe, protective and quality formal and non formal education opportunities for conflict affected boys and girls in hard to reach LGA s of Borno State.
In response to the identified needs in most of the communities where ROHI has operational presence, the community members, teachers and the children, prioritized activities which ROHI would implement. The specific outputs are;
1. Rehabilitation of classrooms at primary school which have been dilapidated as a result of the crises, as well as associated toilets to decongest the existing classrooms and provide a hygienic environment respectively. ROHI identified that the children will feel safe in the classrooms if they are provided with furniture to sit on during lessons.
In hard to reach areas which has recently been reclaimed by the military, it is imperative to note that development activities may not start as soon as in other locations like the Southern parts of Borno State. Therefore, the construction of temporal learning spaces, will create a rapid enrollment for children to go back to school.
2. Enrollment of children boys and girls into non formal education to be taught literacy, numeracy and SEL. Due to the crises which has affected many children to flee their homes and become internally displaced persons at different communities, school years have been lost. In order to catch up there is a need for an accelerated learning in the form of non formal education for children who cant be newly enrolled into formal schools.
3. Training of volunteer teachers as facilitators who will provide literacy, numeracy and psychosocial support services for the children in the non formal classes, as well as the training of teachers for the formal schools on psycho social support while they learn to teach at the right level.
Provision of temporary incentives for the teachers will help to maintain there stay at the schools.

Needs

From the rapid needs assessment conducted by ROHI at Damboa in July 2018, only 8 functional schools are operational due to the crises that has ravaged the LGA. The needs assessment reavealed that some of the classes in the operational schools are dilapidated and as a result, the children are seen overcrowded in the available classes with over 200 children in a class as against the INEE minimum standard of 1 is to 50 children. ROHI observed that some of the primary school’s classes are occupied by the IDPs. For example, at the Central Primary school Damboa, 6 classes where occupied by the IDPs, thereby creating insufficiency of classrooms for the children. This action has also brought about children overcrowdedness at the classrooms. During the assessment, ROHI observed that all the schools have no furniture and the children were seen sitting on the floor on the broken window frames and broken furniture. The blackboards in some of the classes were seen broken and the ceilings were seen at the verge of falling, which poses a threat to the children. The assessment further revealed that only 3 schools have a functional toilet facilities, while the other schools have dilapidated toilets which are filled up and thereby the students and teachers practice open defecation. At Shuwari primary school, one of the dilapidated classroom is used as a toilet where the children defecate on the bare floor and makes the environment unsafe for learning. The Joint Education Needs Assesment (JENA) report of November 2017, confirms that many of the school’s facilities in areas of high security level like Damboa LGA, are dilapidated and at the verge of collapsing.The assessment reveals that the children enrolled in the schools have no school supplies and as such they come to the school without any writing materials thereby affecting their access to education. The teachers at the schools were seen without instructional materials, textbooks and teaching materials during their lesson period. Very few classes had chalks to write on the blackboards. Focused group discussion with the children at the school indicated that the lack of school supplies in form of exercise books, pencils and bags has reduced their zeal towards coming to the school. Some of the parents interviewed mentioned that the children are always going to the school but since they do not write what they learn, knowledge is not transferred. The assessment, through the focus discussion with the teachers revealed that the amount paid to them as monthly salary is low and insufficient for them to cope with their families. The lack of proper remuneration has brought teacher absenteeism at the schools. Some teachers were seen sitting under trees within the school premise and complained of the students being rough for them to handle. In some of the classes ROHI visited, the teachers were seen not able to cope with the number of the children in the classroom, lack of classroom control and management was displayed by some of the teachers. Members of the community interviewed by ROHI described how the government has refused to take care of the schools and as a result the students have destroyed the facilities present. Interaction with some of the community members showed that they do not to have the necessary skills needed to create awareness on children enrolement and retention. The needs assessment further reveals the presence of overgrown children who reside at the IDP camps and have never been enrolled to school, whereas some dropout. The children identified were above 9 years and have never been to primary 1. All of the needs identified at Damboa, where also seen in different locations where ROHI is providing a small scale education intervention. The needs also confirms the reports of the JENA 2017, more especially in areas of high security situation.In areas of low security situation, the need for a developmental intervention exist as most of the communities are calm.

Activities

Provide 40,000 learners and teachers with learning and teaching materialsProvide temporary incentives to support teachers in hard to reach areas; Establish temporary safe and protective classrooms with adequate basic WASH facilities for both formal and non-formal education, targeting 15,000 children; Train teachers on psycho social support and life skills to cope with the negative effects of the conflict; Empower communities through school management committees to protect education and assist in the establishment and maintenance of child friendly school environments; Support parents and guardians with cash based programmes to increase enrolment and retention rates; Provide sanitary kits to adolescent girls to encourage them to stay in school; Provide recreational materials to school going children and support peace/sports clubs in schools;Provide early childhood development materials to preprimary school aged children; Empower teachers and school management committee members with knowledge on referral mechanisms for issues related to GBV and child protection
