Project Summary

Through this intervetion, Silk Road will aim to implement direct ERL response to continue and expand itshumanitarian services to the vulnerable population in different communities in the targetcommunitie.
The 3 ERL packages will work to help vulnerable people to overcome the negativeeconomic and social results left by the spread of the Covid 19 besides the Syrian crisisthrough :
1- the provision of short-term labour opportunities to skilled and unskilled workers(Women) supporting 360 HHs who will mainly be responsible for producing around421200 reusable masks through the cash for work that will be implemented (Silk Roadhas already the basic equipment like sewing machines to produce reusable masks inaddition to the experience we got in a previous project).
2- Cash-for-work (CFW) activities will include wastes and garbage collection activitiesOperation of water stations and Performing the necessary periodic maintenance throughrecruiting skilled technicians and operators in the water stations we will operate,consequently, Silk Road will secure short-term labour opportunities to 240 skilled andunskilled workers (CFW for both gender) plus 120 technicians and operators under thedirect supervision of our team ( CFW for both gender). All WASH and ERL activities will beimplemented and provided according to WASH & ERL standards as well as they willprovide timely equitable access to the project services.
3- Morover, Silk Road will train and equip men, women and youth volunteers or throughthe CFW activity (Slight support) to provide temporary employment for public projects tothe most vulnerable segments of a population targeted through this project. Furthermore,the training will include best practices for each specifi c training subject, and explain whatsafety risks they may face in their work and how to mitigate them. Silk Road will target900 benefi ciaries of both genders including youth above 18 years old. Silk Road willrequest those 900 benefi ciaries to integrate themselves in small volunteer separate groupswhich will implement community initiatives in the targeted locations which include.
1- Rehabilitation or repair of basic, local economic infrastructure
2- Removal of debris and waste
3- Rehabilitation of education facilities
4- Rehabilitation of health facilities
5- Rehabilitation of access to basic utilities (electricity, gas, water, sewage) for around1300 HHs.
6- Basic housing repair support
7- Community initiatives support
One of the ultimate goals of this project is to provide the targeted benefi ciaries with thetools and resources to work towards self-empowerment and sustainability by providingeach trainee with, Knowledge, expertise and a full equipment kit to enable him/herstrongly enter the labour market. This could be through handing over some of the assetsand equipment of the project such as sewing machines and could be through thedistribution of the kit.
The priority will be given for the following categories:
- IDPs families with the focus on IDPs Families headed by women.
- Women/Boys-headed households.
- Families with large size above six members and do not have a breadwinner.
- Silk Road will take into consideration girls, separated or unaccompanied girls and boys, orphans, women heads of household, lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer/questioning (LGBTQ), and people with disabilities. As well as the criteria will focus on GBV survivors and Women, girls at risk of GBV.
Silk Road will strive to target all affected people applying aligned and reliable selectioncriteria to benefi t from the ERL activities in addition to the priorities that will be given forIDPs, vulnerable groups such as the elderly, people with disabilities, and women takinginto consideration the women and children exposed to violence based on Gender. Theproject will adhere to the "Do No Harm approach" & "Cash for Work MinimumRecommended Actions Guidance for NWS" especially on daily wage rate, th number ofwork hours per day, and project duration.

3 - Needs

According to 2023 HNO overview 15.3 M out of 22.1 M of Syrian population are in need for the humanitarin aid, in other words, almost seven out of ten people in Syria need assistance. All the population groups identified in the 2023 HNO are worse off in 2023 than in 2022According to the need assessmen conducted by Silk Road we found the followin results:All people are suffering from the lack of job opportunities and lack of agriculturalproduction inputs and the high prices, the high cost of agricultural and fodder inputs, andthe weak purchasing power of the population in general, whether the displaced orresidents.There is a lack of fi nancial resources, high prices, and limited purchasing power,which places additional burdens on the population, especially with the onset of winter andthe high price of heating and cooking materials such as gas and fuel.In light of thespread of COVID 19 which increased people's suffering, from the negative impact . Localcouncils have reported the need for rehabilitation of infrastructures, such as water andsewage networks, the lack of livelihood opportunities, small business projects, vocationaltraining and restoration of agricultural equipment and assets have been reported aspriorities. In agricultural areas needs are huge for the provision of livelihood kits inclusiveof tools, seasonal crops and rehabilitation of wells, powered by solar panels. Furthermore,water, sewage and energy systems in the villages need repair, restoration or upgrade .

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1-Security issues related to cash transfer. Mitigations measures: 1- The frequencyof payments will be weekly or monthly (This will be agreed upon with women andmen). 2- Payment mechanism: The delivery instrument that will be used to make thepayment is a direct cash distribution (We will encourage all women and men beforesigning the agreement to open a PTT account where they can receive all payments ontheir accounts put if it is too hard or inapplicable then we will deliver it in direct cash inthe workshop. 3- Payments must be made in the presence of the workshop supervisorand one of the monitoring and evaluation teams. Risk 2-Risks related to the limitationof the participation of the disabled people in the program cycle.Set up communitychannels for communication (e.g. focal points and committees) to ensure that theinformation goes through and reaches marginalized groups, including persons withdisabilities.Look for people with disabilities and include them in the lists ofbenefi ciaries and consult all of them.Risk 3-GBV survivors are easily identifi ed byexternal parties / Lack of clear data protection protocols. Mitigations measures: GBVsurvivors should not be labelled as such(to keep confi dentiality)- eligibility criteria willnot be limited to GBV survivors. GBV survivors will be included as 'individual recipients'or another non-specifi c category name. Institutionalize/incentivize data protectionpolicy/protocols in agreements and MoUs.
