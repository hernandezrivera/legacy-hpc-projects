Project Summary

Community mobilization project aims to support grassroots initiatives of vulnerable communities to increase resilience for future development. All project activities are based on the analysis of issues, and prioritization of protection risks and solutions in every particular community. Community mobilizing component seeks to empower communities to develop their own community-based coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, and to find solutions for the most pressing issues in their communities. Communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts.

Needs

The Community Mobilization component focuses on the needs of people living in close distance to the contact line who still suffer from the consequences of a military conflict. However, taking into account the fact that a large number of IDPs and residents of the contact line leave for other populated areas, and also, that humanitarian organizations are focused mainly on locations adjacent to the contact line, there is a need for such support in other settlements located outside contact line. The needs of the most vulnerable communities remain the same e.g. damaged infrastructure system: ruined roads, absence of transport connection between villages and regional cities, unstable water and electricity supply, absence of socially vital services such as medical treatment and cultural life in addition. Therefore, residents of these areas rarely travel outside of the village and live under constant potential threat to their lives and health . According to the communities` assessment needs (managed by PIN during the first phase of the community mobilization component), in majority of cases, the communities require a creation or restoration of public/unity spaces such as halls of cultural clubs for leisure activities and stress relief, central parks and avenues, as well as, health care centres, children’s indoor/outdoor spaces etc. Judging by a previously implemented phase of the Community component, among total number of the unique beneficiaries (650) the gender analysis shows that 46% of 650 beneficiaries comprise women (from 18 to 79 years), 36% - men of the same age group as women, 1%-elderly people (80+). 17% from the all age groups comprise young children up to 17 years old. Among children the number of girls represent a bigger part 58%, respectively boys - 42%. Traditionally, the population of 18 to 60 years old is considered to be the most proactive part of any society, and our age analysis proves this fact, as more than two thirds of all the beneficiaries belong to 18-59 age category among whom female gender exceeds a male one. PIN is responsible in front of the beneficiaries in terms of the following accountability commitments and prioritizes: • Leadership/Governance: compliance response mechanisms that are integrated into project proposals, strategies, monitoring and evaluations, trainings and performance tracking management, partnership agreements etc. • Information Provision: accessible and timely information to beneficiaries on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its beneficiaries over information provision. • Beneficiaries’ Feedback: actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.

Activities & outcomes / Деятельность & результаты/ Діяльність & результати Outcome 1: Affected people have access to adequate protection and means of livelihoods.Activities:1. Capacity building for activists of communityRationale: PIN will strive for building capacity of local communities as a means to increase access aid and ensure meaningful transition and exit strategies, which are part of a process of moving from emergency to rehabilitation and development. Under this activity, identified community activists in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, especially along the line of contact in GCA, will receive trainings on Community Mobilization. Community Support Initiative (CSI) includes a range of activities designed to mobilize and empower conflict-affected and displaced communities, building on the education, skills, and capacities of affected populations in order to strengthen their abilities to organize emergency response, protect themselves and identify solutions.Community Mobilization involves a training on skills and gaining experience on a variety of topics:training on community mobilization and empowerment; on (self) management, or support for the establishment of an inclusive leadership structure; a complaint mechanism, a platform for ideas exchange and a dialogue with the authorities or other relevant actors; a project design, from an idea till implementation and finalization process;Outcome 2: Affected people have improved access to quality and affordable services. Activities1. Promoting peaceful coexistence & durable solutions. 2. Individual Protection Assistance (Non-cash)Rationale: This intervention seeks to empower communities to develop their own community-based coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, and to find solutions for the most pressing needs/issues in their communities. Communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts. PIN will provide grants to 12 communities in the Donetsk and Lugansk Oblasts GCA for community-based projects which will be designed at the community level and meet needs identified by community representatives. An obligatory condition for receiving the grant was the community's own contribution to the project, including all direct activities for the implementation of the project.Examples of successfully implemented mini-projects in a previous projects such as centres for retirement age and people with disabilities; leisure activities and personal development; parks of the inhabitants etc. PIN proposes to include the territories adjacent to the area of war conflict exceeding 20 km distance, one of the examples of implemented mini-project in such territory is Sviatogorsk Centre for people with disabilities and IDPs from a previous DFID phase. Also under this activity, there will be the opportunity for communities to identify local individuals to directly benefit from targeted individual protection assistance. This is meant to be an emergency fund for the most urgent needs for individuals in frontline. PIN will provide direct assistance to the most vulnerable individuals identified by representatives aforementioned communities. Examples of such assistance include provision of a new wheelchair, walkers for persons with disabilities, upgrading a shelter to meet special needs, provision of essential medicines or medicament, provision of specialized food for sick children, etc.It is worth to focus a particular attention that other people seeking for IPA assistance not included into the IPA (non –cash) are not left behind. In this case, PIN automatically shares information about the needs of these people among PIN’s inter-sectoral approach. One of the examples of such transfer assistance can be a MPC (Multi-Purpose Cash) provided by the PIN’s distribution department. Also, the PIN transfers such kind referral request to other humanitarian NGOs.
