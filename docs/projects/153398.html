Project Summary

This Emergency Food Security and Livelihood Support to Vulnerable populations in Koch County, Unity State and Fangak County, Jonglei State. The project duration will be one year from January to December 2019. The project will serve 30,000 Households, 18,500 in Koch County and 11,500 in Fangak County. The project will adopt a year round programming with main season activities involving crop production and some fishing activities while dry season activities will involve vegetable production and fishing activities. The project will have three main components, livelihood kits support, training and conditional cash transfer. A conditional cash transfer modality is included to provide access to cash for food and other household needs with the main focus being creation/rehabilitation of communal assets. Collaboration and integration will be key tenets of this project. Working in collaboration with other stakeholders will reduce duplication. Integration with other sectors including nutrition, WASH and protection is meant to ensure greater impact at household level. The project also aims at ensuring gender parity by according both men and women an equal opportunity to participate in project activities. Right from inception, community involvement will be pivotal to project implementation to ensure transparency, accountability but also project sustainability through localization.

Needs

The conflict that erupted in South Sudan in 2013 has persisted in many parts of Unity State. Insecurity has been a major driver of food insecurity in Koch. Frontline locations have been too insecure to allow humanitarian operations. There has been an outright restriction by government to access areas considered as opposition sites. Humanitarian agencies have feared creating pool factors which would expose the civilian population to a possible attack. Such areas have therefore not been served and therefore exacerbating their food security situation. In Koch, conflict manifests as cattle raids between clans or direct combat between government and rebel forces. The civilian population has been constantly on the move in search for safer grounds, and livelihoods have been severely disrupted. Fishermen cannot safely access their traditional fishing grounds. Farmers cannot settle down to engage in any meaningful production. Persistent cattle raids have reduced the number of livestock in the area and denied children and women access to milk which is a staple source of protein in the county.The climatic conditions for 2018 have not been very favourable. Rainfall in Koch in much of 2018 has been less than the long-term average. Availability of wild foods which is an important coping alternative for this area will be reduced. This will only worsen the food security situation in the county. The hunger season will set in early, particularly from December 2018 when most of the areas start experiencing the dry spell. The dry season also means that grass and water will be in short supply. This will result in livestock migrating away from homesteads in search of water and pasture and this will reduce access to milk especially for children and the elderly. The market in Koch has exhibited some resilience to rejuvenate even amidst conflict. There are basic household supplies brought in mainly through Bentiu to Koch. This resilience and growth is expected to persist with improvement in roads during the dry season and if the peace process will hold. However in general, there has been an upward trend in staple food prices in the market and an increase in the cost of minimum food basket. There is a depreciation in terms of trade. Comparing price of one goat to price of one sack of 50kg sorghum, the price of sorghum has continued to increase while the price of a goat has continued to remain almost steady. Similarly, the terms of trade of wage to sorghum have continued to deteriorate. The increment in wage rate has remained low compared to increment in price of sorghum in the markets(September Monthly Market Price Bulletin, October 2018). Koch county has a population of 123,043 people. It is expected between October and December 2018, 22% of this population will be in either crisis, emergency, or catastrophic food security situation. The proportion of population in crisis, emergency or catastrophic food security situation will increase to 75% between January and March 2018. Koch County boarders Leer and Mayendit counties. These two counties are expected to have sections of the population with catastrophic food insecurity as early as October 2018(IPC June 2018). Given the constant interaction between populations in these counties, it is expected that there will be pressure on food resources within Koch. It is possible to have pockets of insecurity in Koch. For instance, IRRM screening conducted by UNICEF concurrently in Koch and Bieh shows GAM rates of over 15% in Bieh which is above the WHO threshold for intervention. WRSS Nutrition SMART survey conducted in March 2018 in Koch County has revealed high levels of malnutrition with a critical SAM rate of 4.3% (95% CI 2.9-6.4) which is higher than 4% used in the context of South Sudan to trigger an emergency.Peace is expected to offer hope for stability and wellbeing. However, with peace there is likelihood of population movement from the POC towards Koch.

Activities

The proposed activities will involve both input support and training. The project proposes to provide crop, vegetable and fisheries kits to beneficiaries to help them produce their own food to ensure self-reliance in the long run. Vegetables and fisheries provide quick impact in access to food while crop production will ensure the household continues to build its food resources over time. The bulk of the kits will be through the FAO managed pipeline with supplementary kits sought through other means, especially for local vegetables to be provided for dry season farming. The following activities will be implemented:• Support 30,000 vulnerable food-insecure households with crop and vegetable kits.• Support 12,500 vulnerable food-insecure households with fishing kits.• Support 30,000 vulnerable food-insecure households with agricultural hand tools.• Train 3,000 community leaders, lead farmers and extension staffs on improved agricultural practices including Fall Army Warm management.• Establish and maintain one demo farm per payam for training for 9 payams. • Train 1,250 fisher folk on improved fishing and fish preservation methods. • Train 1,250 farmers on vegetable utilization and preservation.• Conduct post-distribution, post-planting and post-harvest surveys. • Document on amount of fish caught. • Provide conditional cash transfers during the lean season to 400 beneficiaries. • Monitoring and reporting.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

During inception phase, the project will conduct mobilization and sensitization. This will involve community leaders and representatives from vulnerable groups including households headed by women, children, elderly and people with disabilities. during these meetings, the project team will discuss content of the project, what is expected from stakeholders and beneficiaries as well as what the project will be able to provide. During distributions, beneficiaries will be informed in advance of the venue and time of distributions. During monitoring visits, project team will involve community members and provide feedback on project progress.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Trainings will be tailored to include more demonstrations due to the low literacy levels in both counties of Koch and Fangak. During trainings on sexual and gender based violence and human rights, women, men, girls and boys will be separated to allow them openly discuss issues of concern without fear of victimization and lessons drawn from such segregation in trainings shared with all the groups as part of feedback. Given that these areas have been adversely affected by fighting, statements that would flair emotions will be avoided either during trainings, distributions or even on informal interactions with the beneficiaries and community

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

WR South Sudan’s ability to achieve our aims, in complex and insecure environments like theGreater Upper Nile Region, is linked to our service quality and commitment to ethics. Given thepotential for desperation among the beneficiaries, and the power dynamic of those bringing anddistributing relief items over the beneficiaries, World Relief (WR) will take a firm, zero tolerancestance towards any staff or volunteers taking advantage of beneficiaries financially, sexually, orphysically, or in any way attempting to divert resources or use the system for their personal gainin addition to their wages. The Country Director and Human Resource Manager educate all staffon the Code of Conduct content, which includes the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse(PSEA), which each staff member receives and signs upon acceptance of employment. A copyof the signed Code of Conduct stays with the employee and another copy is kept on record.Already employed staff benefit from a series of HR trainings organized annually to deepenunderstanding on a number of important HR and protection issues. Line managers explain thecontent if an employee, beneficiary or local authority have any questions or issues regarding theCode of Conduct or PSEA. In order to manage cases of a breach of conduct if they arise, World Relief will identify protectionFocal Points at the implementation site who will receive complaints from beneficiaries or thecommunity. Breaches of the code of conduct will be referred immediately to senior managementin Juba (CD and HR Manager) for action. In order to ensure beneficiaries and local communitiesare able to report complaints, a complaint mechanism will be developed and disseminatedthrough community volunteers in the project.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

With years of static presence in the target locations and existing long-term programming, World Relief in a unique position to implement the proposed project because of the resulting relationships with the communities, beneficiaries, and volunteers. Interactions and dialogues with local staff concerning similar past projects and the needs of their communities have helped to shape the proposed project. Local staff, as members of the community, have unique insight to help shape humanitarian programming. WR's recruitment strategy that prioritizes local staff first and then other national staff and building their capacity has resulted in 97% of employees being nationals, even in senior positions, such as Program Director.WR Staff regularly interact with beneficiaries through normal programming activities in these locations. Likewise, beneficiaries are regularly consulted through monitoring and evaluation activities in existing programming, where they provide their opinions and feedback as well as expressing their needs and those of the larger community. These formal consultations have taken the form of discussion groups, interviews, and surveys, in addition to informal consultations. This feedback has been used in planning the proposed project.Feedback is also regularly obtained through consultation with project volunteers. WR has an extensive network of volunteers in each location, such as Community Nutrition Volunteers, Lead Mothers/Fathers, Community Health Promoters, Water Use Committee Members, Parent Teacher Association Members, and/or Lead Farmers. As community members with specific commitments to humanitarian activities, these volunteers are able to provide learning that is used to tailor the project to the needs of their communities.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Right from inception, project will engage the local community and leadership in project activities. the project will establish local project management committees at Boma level. these committees will be involved right from identification of beneficiaries to final evaluation. this committee is also expected to receive all complaints and forward them to the project team. At payam level, beneficiaries will be sensitized to report all complaints involving project team or project management committee to Payam administrator who will in turn relay to the project team. At the county level, the South Sudan Relief and Rehabilitation Commission director will be the focal person to receive complaints and forward to project team and protection cluster where such issues are not handled at project level. At the project level, the protection officer and protection assistants will be required to document any complaints and address them together with project team or relay them to the senior management where senior management involvement is required.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Project team will establish a project management committee at Boma level. This committee will be involved in project activities right from inception. The committee will be in charge of recruitment of beneficiaries, monitoring of project progress and evaluation. There will be monthly monitoring visits. These visits will involve representatives from the different Bomas not only for visits in their locations but other locations as well as part of peer to peer learning. These representatives will then be facilitated to provide feedback to their committees and areas that they represent.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With years of static presence in these communities and multiple existing long-term programs in the target locations, WRSS is familiar with the protection risks in the population and regularly analyzes and tailors its programming. Existing programming addresses the needs of those with protection concerns.WRSS has established two layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community level volunteers working on health and nutrition. The volunteers will be involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-based committees, which are comprised of members of both genders, elderly, youth, people with disabilities, and of minority groups where relevant.The community level engagement is materialized through conducting regular meeting with the volunteers and community-based committees. WRSS sector staff and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting at community level and discuss progresses, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. Feedback is discussed with the management team in Juba and used to adapt the project to meet the changing needs.WRSS also interacts with specific protection actors concerning their findings in the target locations to make sure programming addresses the needs of all community members.Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

With similar programming in these locations, WRSS Staff are aware of the barriers to access that different population groups could face during the project. Staff will work with volunteers, local leaders, and other community representatives to ensure that all eligible beneficiaries are able to access and benefit from the proposed services and activities. These consultations will be very important during beneficiary selection/targeting.One of the most significant barriers to access in these locations is logistical, either through distance or terrain or due to restrictions on movement due to the conflict. WRSS will mitigate these barriers by attempting to reach beneficiaries as close to their own communities as possible.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

This project will measure access of beneficiaries to key activities. Known population estimates will be used in setting numerical targets and in the targeting of beneficiaries by geographical location. Known population statistics will be compared against the number of beneficiaries accessing the project’s activities. Access of beneficiaries will also be measured by beneficiary category, with monitoring disaggregated (as appropriate) by gender, age, disability status, vulnerability status, etc. Where the monitoring identifies insufficient access by a beneficiary group, project staff will adjust the project to ensure their inclusion.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of beneficiaries is of paramount importance to project implementation. World Relief has a security focal point. Working together with other protection players, a security risk analysis will be conducted to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. This will be particularity so for distributions which act as pool factors and have a potential of exposing beneficiaries to attacks and injury. The Boma management committees will be pivotal in advising the safest mode and venue for training and inputs delivery.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project team will serve beneficiaries in the most respectful and dignified way possible. This include respecting their culture and way of life. where change in practice is envisaged, for instance in use of different variety, mode of cultivation or consumption of other foods, this will be explained in the most respectful manner and beneficiaries allowed to make their own decisions whether to adopt or ignore what has been availed to them.
