Project Summary

The project aims to provide protection, child protection and GBV prevention and response services to the most vulnerable MWGB through community-based interventions and protection emergency response mobile teams in Northern Syria (North Aleppo) and Northwest Syria (West Aleppo, Idlib,) The project will cover WGSSs, CFSs, widows camps, community centers ,Mobile teams (most of those teams are emergency response protection mobile teams to response for the new displacement movement and to conduct the assessment needed to respond for those IDPs) covering most of Idleb governance, west and north of Aleppo. The main objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks and respond to protection needs through: * supporting the protective environment in Syria. * provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, psychosocial assistance. * Individual Protection Assistance targeting people with protection, CP, GBV concerns *support other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms to address particular protection issues/concerns as identified through protection monitoring. Provision of PFA support and structures PSS support, including case management. *Provide legal advice/counselling and legal representation on civil status documentation /registration as well as on housing/land/property issues. *Expand the availability and quality of protection" CP and GBV services" in targeted location accordance to strategic priorities of the 2020 GP, CP, MA and GBV. As well to ensure the protection of the population affected by the crisis is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and advocacy with duty bearers, Increased access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the child protection minimum standards in Humanitarian Action " by providing Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys. Provide quality specialized GBV services for survivors to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV, e.g., case management, psychosocial support and establish referral pathways. Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a particular focus on adolescent girls and to reduce the impact of explosive hazards. The project aims to strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors and duty-bearers at the national and community levels to assess, analyze and address protection risks and needs and to respond to child protection concerns in Syria and Build the capacity of GBV specialist and non-specialist to respond, prevent and mitigate GBV.
MEAL team has an excellent experience in monitoring and evaluation, The team has a balance of Male and Female field staff. We ensure, considering safety issues, that each site visit is conducted by a Male/Female team in order to eliminate gender bias.
MEAL field staff consist of 140 enumerators spread on 4 offices and cover wide geographical areas in Idleb and Aleppo governorates. In addition to 11 MEAL staff in Gaziantep office.

4 - Needs

According to Syrian Arab Republic February 2021 Needs and Response Summary, 13.4 Million people (48.7% male, 51.3% female) across Syria are in need; 45.4% Children, 50.4% adults, and 4.2% Elderly. Whereas 3.34 million of them are PwDs. The report indicates that 13.1 million people across Syria are in need of protection services. Restrictions on freedom of movement were reported in 65% of assessed communities, lack and loss of Civil Documentation in 61% of assessed communities, and Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues in 50% of assessed communities. These have cross-cutting impacts on ability of people to access rights, assistance, and ability to return. Based on SC-NW of Syria – Multi-Sectoral Need Assessment December 10, 2020, the most prevalent threats for children were reported as child labour 61%; school dropout 40%; diseases 21%; followed by additional risks such as violence, physical hazards, war remnants/UXOs and early marriage 10%. According to Voices from Syria 2020 Report and WoS GBV sub-cluster and GBV Trend Analysis Report Q3 2020, in NWS some of the types of GBV violence incidents’ occurrence is reported high prevalence of Psychological/emotional abuse 34%. Physical assault counts for 29% of reported incidents, whereas denial of resources and opportunities counts for 24%, rape and sexual assault add up to a total of 10% whereas early/forced marriages count for the remaining 4%. Based on the need assessment conducted recently by IRD in the most severe locations, there is a high need for protection services, along with interfering in the Widows’ Camps (WCs). IRD aims to cover the gaps regarding to need assessment by providing CP, GBV prevention and response, GP response services.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

IhsanRD adopts a zero-tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. And it is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within IhsanRD and within the framework of IhsanRD programmes and their beneficiary. To this aim, IhsanRD’s Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse:• Encourages prevention;• Promotes detection;• Sets out a clear investigation procedure;• Sets out a mechanism of sanctions IhsanRD fully upholds principles of freedom of speech and protection of victims of sexual abuse and exploitation. As such, IhsanRD has developed mechanisms that can contribute to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse such as:• Signing of the Code of Conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behaviour and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse;• Information to the beneficiaries, staff, and all stakeholders about the existing Complaints and Response Mechanisms - CRM which can be allowing them to send their concerns and complaints in a confidently way without and fear• Implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams, particularly for staff exposed to working with minors or the most vulnerable population;• Identify risk factors on an ongoing basis and in particular throughout the project cycle;• Put in place evaluation procedures for IhsanRD staff, partners and stakeholders by carrying out due diligence, particularly with regard to their ethical commitments and past behaviours.
