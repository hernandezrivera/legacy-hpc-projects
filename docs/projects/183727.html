Project Summary

The proposed project is in line with the education sub-cluster framework objective two aiming to increase equitable safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and aims to ensure that boys and girls have access to a protective and enabling educational experience in MoEHE schools in the Gaza Strip.
The project targets a total of (62,198) children (31,632 girls and 30,566 boys) including 59,517 children (30,285 girls and 29,232 boys) registered in classes from 1 to 3. Besides 581 children (297 girls and 284 boys) with visual disabilities in addition to further 100 children (50 girls and 50 boys) with disability in addition to 50 (25 boys, 25 girls) governmental schools who are benefiting from the conducted awareness sessions on rights of students with disabilities. Each session is expected to target 40-school student and those are expected to share the information they received among other school students and colleagues. In addition, the project is targeting 1000 parents mainly mothers through the conducted awareness campaign and the developed videos.
On teachers level, the project will target (105) teachers and 7 MoEHE experts who will benefit from project activities. These teachers will create inclusive and interactive online and offline self-learning cards following the Diamond Kite Project (DKP)* to be uploaded on MoEHE formal channels and to deliver to students in hard copies as alternative form of education when schools closure and to bridge the gaps that resulted from learning losses.
This project support target beneficiaries through:
1) Provision of capacity building program to teachers on creation inclusive and interactive online and offline self-learning cards following with developing new and/or enhancing existing self-learning cards for (1-3 grades) students in Arabic, English, and Math subjects. In addition, provision of tablet per targeted teacher besides distributing package of self-learning cards on all (1-3 grades) students in governmental schools. Besides, it designed at aiming of consulting mothers (the expected educators for children at home) in different phases of project implementation and designing through engaging them in FGDs. The project concluded with conducting an awareness campaign targeting parents manily mothers to increase their awareness related to the developed self-learning cards.
2) Provision of tablet for all students with visual disability registered in the governmental school; Supporting conflict-affected school children with disability with assistive devices and peer counseling services; and Conducting awareness sessions inside governmental schools to increase awareness of rights of students with disabilities
*The DKP is Educaid's toolkit for education in emergencies. it designed at aim of fostering active and inclusive education based on the Universal Design for Learning and centered on the four main access of experiential learning: I AM; I CAN; I SHARE; and I CARE. According to the DKP methodology and tools, CBOs teachers with support from Educaid educators will be able to respond to the psychosocial needs of targeted children, developed their life skills and learning process, increase the level of resilience in emergency and vulnerable context, and in the same time, promote the inclusion of CWDs.

1 - Needs

Please describe the needs your projects aims to addressThe COVID-19 pandemic has created the largest disruption of education systems in history. In early March 2020, COVID-19 hit oPt which triggered the declaration of a state of emergency and cause closure of all education facilities. In the previous academic year 2020-2021, 1.3 million children have lost at least three months of schooling and have not been able to engage in summer and extracurricular activities. 88 per cent HH in Gaza strip with school-aged children reported that their children were in need of catch-up learning programs because of the loss of learning due to COVID-19 related schools closure (HNO, 2022). COVID-19 has meant that, in the last school year, keeping up with classes from home was more challenging, particularly as only 35 per cent of Palestinian households having access to home computers (Statement by UN Humanitarian and Resident Coordinator in the oPt, Lynn Hastings, August 2021).According to PCBS 2020, the percentage of individuals (10 years and above) who used the internet from anywhere reached 65 per cent in Gaza Strip also data showed that the percentage of male internet users reached 72 per cent compared to 69 per cent of females. Given that, 49.1 per cent of female are with educational attainment of secondary and above which means that half of mothers are not qualified to play the teacher role (PCBS, 2017). Recently and following the last escalation on Gaza in May 2021, more families have been pushed below the poverty line undermining their capacity to afford for basic education supplies, transportation costs, adequate internet or IT equipment for distance learning and increasing the risk of school dropout. With 47per cent unemployment rate in Gaza and 64 per cent among youth which considered evidence of increased resorting to negative and harmful coping mechanisms on the part of vulnerable groups, particularly children, as shown in the rise in the school dropout rates, child labor and child marriage(Education Custer Strategy, 2020-2021). This imposes the necessity to find alternatives and complementary modules to E-learning program also learning losses threaten to extend beyond this generation and erase decades of progress, not least in support of girls and young women’s educational access. Accordingly, MoEHE has requested to provide more support to the low achievers, students with learning difficulties and students with disabilities boys and girls to enable them mastering the basic skills on reading and writing in addition to the numerical skills in Math. According to the academic yearbook (2021-2021), there is (59,517) children are registered in the governmental schools in grades from 1 to 3. In more details, there are (9,548 M and 9,951 F) children registered in grade one, (9,759 M and 9,986 F) children in grade two where (9,925 M and 10,348 F) children are registered in grade three. A study conducted by the MoEHE found that only 30 per cent of schoolchildren continued their study through the distance learning, implying that 70 per cent of students lost their E-learning opportunities. If not mitigated with solutions, those children are at high risk of dropping out of school. On the other hand, the situation is further aggravated for children with disabilities; according to the PCBS 2017, 20 per cent of the overall people with disability in Palestine are children of which 46 per cent between the ages 6-17 years old are not enrolled in schools. 71 per cent of the HHs in Gaza Strip who have CWDs reported that their CWDs are facing challenges in accessing education services (HNO, 2022). According to MoEHE, There is (1,907 Male, 1,347 Female) children with disabilities are registered in the academic year 2019-2020 including (581) children with visual disability. In March 2020, Educaid conducted a multi-sectorial assessment concluding to main findings showing that 75 per cent of children with disabilities are facing lack of accessibility to E-learning platforms while 45 per cent of respondents are facing lack of accessibility to curriculum materials.Recently and following the last escalation on Gaza Strip in May 2021, Educaid conducted a rabid assessment in July 2021 intending to assess the needs of PwDs during and after the escalation. Educaid had reached 570 PwDs (52 per cent Female) through conducting a survey. The results showed that among respondents, 72 per cent of CWDs, who are in grades 1-4, have mobility disability and 14 per cent of them have visual disability while 42 per cent of them are studding in governmental schools. The results show that only 14 per cent of CWDs participated in educational activities following the escalation while no one of them receive support with remedial/catch up classes. The results show, as well, 70 per cent of CWDs are more likely to turn into vocational training due to learning losses and low academic achievements. Additionally, according to the OPT- 2021 Multi Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), conducted by REACH intiative in close collaboration with OCHA, 75%, 100%, 86% of HH with children with a disability in Gaza, Deir Al Balah, and Khan Yunis respectively reporting challenges to access education services, The results shows, as well, that 90%, 65%, 57% of HH in North of Gaza, Deir Al Balah, and Rafah respectively reporting barriers to accessing education. Furthermore, the results shows that there is only 13%, 12%, 24%, 17%, and 38% of HH in North of Gaza, Gaza, Deir Al Balah, Khanyounis, Rafah respectively reporting access or knowledge of the complaint mechanisms. A Violence Survey conducted by PCBS in 2019 indicated that 20.6% of the boys and 13.7% of the girls under 11 were exposed to severe physical violence while 36% of children between 12 and 17 years old were exposed to violence from one or both parents. According to PCBS 2019, 38% of Palestinian women in Gaza reported psychological, physical, sexual, social or economic violence by their husbands

2 - Activities

This project will center around two main components in direct support of the strategic and cluster objectives of the 2022 HRP. Responsive activities will include 1) Provision of education supplies to students and teachers. 1.1) Establishing Self-Learning Cards Task Force (SLCTF) consists of 7 MoEHE experts one per educational moderate; 1.2) Establishing 7 teachers groups, one group per educational moderate that consisting of 15 teachers, who are teaching Arabic, English, and Math classes of grades 1 to 3. Each SLCTF will lead a teacher group according to his/her education moderate; 1.3) conducting focus group discussions with male and female prents mainly mothers (expected educators) in order to support in identifying the basic skills and knowledge that children lost; 1.4) In cooperation with Italian Experts, conducting capacity building program for the teachers groups and SLCTF members focusing on creating inclusive and interactive online and offline self-learning cards in light with Diamond Kite Project (DKP)*; 1.5) provision of tablet per each involved teacher and SLCTF member; 1.6) Along to four months, Identifying set of key missing skills and basic skills of grades from 1 to 3 in subjects (Arabic, English, Math) then creating new package of / enhancing existing self-learning cards; 1.7) Conducting coaching activities to involved teachers aiming to support the development process of self learning cards; 1.8) Printing out and distributing the developed/created offline self-learning cards among All registered students in grades 1 to 3 in the governmental schools; 1.9) conducting an awareness campaign through social media to inform parents and increase their awareness related to the developed self-learning cards; 1.10) developing and disseminating three awareness videos the close consultation from mothers (expected educators) aiming to provide mothers with directions and advices to use the self-learning cards while teaching their children 2) Provision of educational assistive devices to vulnerable students with disabilities; 2.1) provision of tablet per each students with visual disability registered in the governmental school; 2.2) Supporting conflict-affected school children with disability with assistive devices and peer counseling services; 2.3) Conducting awareness sessions inside governmental schools to increase awareness of rights of students with disabilities Additionally, in line with oPt PSEA Network Action Plan and with close coordination, on the time of implementation, with leading organizations including PNGO, WFP, OCHA, DCA/NCA, UNICEF, UNWOMEN, the project is expected to increase awareness of involved staff and team members in addition to the involved teachers for what related to PSEA trough engaging them in training sessions. These sessions will be conducted in order to ensure that every beneficiary has access to confidential, community-based complaints mechanisms. To reach this goal,• Project has adopted a safe complaint channel(s) for beneficiaries based on consultations with the beneficiaries and accessible to different groups. • Project has built in activities involving development and dissemination of PSEA awareness raising material including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries and that awareness raising targets all project sites. • Project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA, in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter-Agency Referrals.This project is involving the Independent Living Center to implement the second component related to support students with disabilities and increase awareness inside schools on disability rights and inclusive education. The Center mission and vision are focusing on empowering and supporting PWDs in multi-sectorial areas of intervention varied from rehabilitation services, economic empowerment, educational activities, and advocating their rights through provided services and valued interventions. The Center will follow the peer counseling approach where empowered persons10 with disabilities providing/implementing the services to increase awareness and serving students with disabilities. On the other hand, the project will be implemented in partnership with SDF, that specialized in digital tools and social engagement. The SDF will supervise and lead the process of developing the online self-learning cards as IT specialists and experts in contextualization/adapting some learning tools to Gaza context. In addition, the SDF will lead and inplemnt the activities of conducting the awareness campaign and developing the three awareness videos. *The DKP is Educaid's toolkit for education in emergencies. it designed at aim of fostering active and inclusive education based on the Universal Design for Learning and centered on the four main access of experiential learning: I AM; I CAN; I SHARE; and I CARE. According to the DKP methodology and tools, CBOs teachers with support from Educaid educators will be able to respond to the psychosocial needs of targeted children, developed their life skills and learning process, increase the level of resilience in emergency and vulnerable context, and in the same time, promote the inclusion of CWDs.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Educaid is regularly conducting capacity building to the staff at aim of increasing knowledge about core humanitarian standards, sphere project, human rights approach, IHL, IHRL, Gender, disability and protection mainstreaming and children rights EducAid is ensuring Do No Harm in two levels: 1- At strategic level The project activities identifying and responding to the most at risk beneficiaries (vulnerable school children and children with disabilities). The Activities not create any unintended negative consequences affecting beneficiaries or the community mainly during COVID-19 through setting different scenarios of implementation to ensure commitment to delivering services with full respect to government instructions during emergency. The project activities designed to promote meaningful access ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries through participatory approach that engaging them besides direct key actors in order to design the intervention based to their needs and with full respect to right to education 2- At operational level The implementation methodology ensure that the four protection mainstreaming principles mainly Do No Harm are integrated and it became part of each phase of the project life-cycle. the activities prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm mainly during the distribution of printed materials also during the implementation of capacity building programs. All activities take to consideration the social distance, prevention measures, appropriate distribution times and locations. Secure meaningful access: the project ensure assistance and services are delivered according to needs without discrimination. Services and assistance are provided in good quantity and quality, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the beneficiaries and are accessible by students with disabilities. Ensure accountability through enable the affected beneficiaries and communities to play an active role in the measurement of the quality of interventions that affect them, and put in place effective and easily accessible mechanisms for suggestions and complaints. there will be a complaint box in the Independent Living Center managed by Educaid M&E officer. Beneficiaries will be updated about the progress of dialing with their complaints or suggestions. Ensure participation and empowerment through fostering inclusive and effective participation of beneficiaries through building and maintaining a direct communication channel between project staff and beneficiaries in order to encourage them to raise voices, speak out and to claim their rights and empower themselves. The project related staff will be requested to conduct regular field visits (with respecting to governmental instructions related to COVID-19) to listen to beneficiaries and collect their feedback then back to them with a response. A complaint response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedbacks and complaints from the beneficiaries are reported confidentially, and that complaints are properly addressed

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Educaid will ensure engaging and participation of beneficiaries through conducting pre-assessment to group of representative sample of direct beneficiaries also through conducting interviews with teachers and MoEHE experts to collect in depth related and supportive information in early stages of the project mainly during breakdown activities. A clear image will be formed to design the implementation methodologies to ensure equal and impartial access to services. For example, the time of implementing capacity building sessions will be suitable to both male and female teachers through avoiding the time of doing house works. A special attention will be paid to children with disabilities through disseminating adapted printed self-learning materials. During the project implementation phase, monitoring visits will be conducted to make sure that the activities are on the right track, also a feedback and complaint mechanism will be accessible and adapted for all through allocating the complaint box and a direct call number who will be written in braille and large print to be accessible for all. Responding to the complaints, corrective actions will be taken, if needed, to ensure inclusion of all. At the end of the project, an evaluation process will be conducted and learned lessons will be collected for the future projects. Additionally, the M&E officer will make sure to collect disaggregated data by age, gender, disability, vulnerability, refugees, and others to ensure taking to consideration the different needs of different targeted groups
