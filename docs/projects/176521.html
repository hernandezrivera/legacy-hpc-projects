Project Summary

This proposed project aims to reach 5800 vulnerable IDPs and members of host communities affected by conflict and displacement in Lainya County to reduce protection threats and incidents; ensure safe, equitable, and dignified access to basic services; and enable vulnerable people to recover from crisis through building their resilience. To reach this goal within the sector of Protection, the project seeks to (1) ensure the provision of critical, specialized, and integrated GBV services for vulnerable people; and (2) prevent and mitigate GBV protection risks through preparedness and resilience.

1 - Needs

Between July 2016 and 2020, armed conflict and displacement have affected the lives of thousands of civilians in Lainya County. The impact of armed conflict and displacement coupled with the deteriorating economic situation and COVID-19 restrictions have exacerbated the risks and occurrence of gender-based violence. Through funds provided by UNICEF, IsraAID and its partner the South Sudan Health Organization (SUHA) have established Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) in 2020, provided case management and PSS services, and conducted GBV training for local service providers in some communities in Lainya, Logwili, and Kupera. The proposed project will fill the gaps that will be left by the end of the UNICEF-funded project at the end of March 2021. IsraAID and its partner, the South Sudan Health Association (SUHA) conducted a safety audit for gender-based violence in Logwili, Lainya, and Kupera, from 6- 13 March 2020. The findings from the assessment suggest that gender-based violence remains a serious concern in the three proposed project locations (Lainya, Kupera, and Logwili). Women and girls are particularly vulnerable to sexual exploitation and physical abuse in their daily chores such as the collection of firewood and fetching of water. Although there has been some improvement in water provision in the locations, the distance traversed to collect water is about 4 kilometers on average, exposing women to additional risk. Ongoing clashes between host communities and cattle keepers, and also the huge presence of armed forces pose threats to the safety of women and girls, especially threats of being sexually or physically abused. According to findings from the safety audit conducted by IsraAID and SUHA in March 2020, the prevalence and impact of gender-based violence, especially rape, child marriage, as well as intimidation and harassment of women and girls when they access water points, is further compounded by the gaps in the effectiveness of the referral pathway in many of the communities in the three proposed project locations. Through service provision, sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as physical violence, can be addressed to ensure the wellbeing of women and girls and the community at large. Gender inequities were apparent in the reported prevalence of early and forced marriage and truancy rates among girls of school-going age. Certain misconceptions, myths, stereotypes, and cultural beliefs about gender contribute to the high rate of early and forced marriages, and truancy, as well as other forms of gender-based violence in the communities. Thus, there is a need for raising awareness on gender-based violence in communities that the UNICEF-funded project did not cover, so gender inequities could be mended and gender-based violence reduced among women, men, girls, and boys. Protection structures in these communities are poor and highly dependent on the male-dominant committees and community leadership structures. This creates a dependency on traditional conflict resolution structures and justice systems inherited from a patriarchal society whose structures are unfair to women and girls. Access to health services in response to GBV over the last period has improved but the number of qualified local healthcare workers is still limited, thereby affecting the quality of services. An Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Assessment coordinated by the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (Central Equatoria State) in Lainya and Juba Counties from 24 February - 1 March 2021 was supported through a coordination role by IsraAID. The needs reflected in this proposal are based on the findings from this assessment and will subsequently inform the implementation of the proposed project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This project provides beneficiaries with information about PSEA during home visits, at outreach activities, on posters and fliers, and through community leaders. Clear information is provided on these mediums on where to seek help, and how to report cases in a confidential and safe manner. Information is provided to community member during home visits, outreach activities, posters and fliers

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Our project ensures feedback from the most vulnerable members of communities, including individuals with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women, and girls, through home visits. These sessions ensure that service provision is offered regardless of age, gender, vulnerability, and mobility. Home visits are informed by the community mapping process conducted at the start of the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IsraAID uses series of participatory approach to promote ownership, accountability, and sustainability of projects. IsraAID recognizes the rights of beneficiaries and community members to give feedback, and make complaints through the following mechanisms: community entry dialogue and planning information sessions with community members at the start of the project; establishment of community feedback and response mechanism groups such as the Community Emergency Council; regular meetings with community members; sharing contacts for follow- ups and complaints from the community.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback provided by beneficiaries will inform ongoing program implementation and shape intervention design. This is used specifically in project review meetings, compiled into action plans developed as a result.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This program ensures that children, women at risk, and GBV survivors have quality, timely, and inclusive protection services including medical care, legal support, safety, and mental health psychosocial support services by (1) disseminating referral pathways per each of these needs; (2) conducting multi-sectorial trainings of service providers; (3) holding county-wide coordination meetings with other sectors; (4) implementing joint awareness raising programs, for example, 16 days of activism and international women's day; and (5) offering integrated case management that includes referrals to relevant service providers.
