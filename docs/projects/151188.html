Project Summary

The proposed project will ensure protection and restoration of livelihoods associated through agriculture, livestock and income generating activities. This will be achieved through provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs, emergency livestock assistance, livelihood diversification, improved post-harvest practices and response to food chain threats. This is aimed at reducing overreliance on livestock products and reduction in livestock morbidity rate. Additionally, NAPAD will ensure women participation in community development and decision making by encouraging women participation in disaster response by empowering women and the youth through vocational education and providing an enabling environment for self-employment opportunities.

Needs

According to the FSNAU, 2 September 2018 post GU report, agricultural activities have improved due to above average seasonal rainfall. However, flooding in the low lying areas combined with the previous prolonged drought led to continued acute food insecurity for riverine populations. FSNAU September 2018 postGu technical release reported the agro-pastoral livelihood of Southern zones currently have little to no food stocks and limited farm labor opportunities. Livestock and water conditions have been extremely poor leading to livestock deaths in Gedo region and Abudwak. Populations of 81,000 in Galgadud, 189,000 in Banadir, 77,000 in Lower Juba and 30,000 in Gedo region are in IPC 3. Areas with IPC3 including most of the 13 IDP camps urgently need humanitarian assistance or livelihood support through December 2018. 294,200 acutely malnourished children including 55,200 children who are severe need urgent treatment and nutritional support through December 2018. Livelihood support is also required for communities categorized in IPC Phase 2. NAPAD’s September 2018 situational analysis revealed that the average cost of food per household for 7 days is 18.71USD, which is very expensive. The report revealed that the targeted regions of Gedo and Galmudug experience food insecurity. Households reported current food stocks (cereals) could only last about 3 days. The situation is similar to households in IDP camps. The targeted riverine districts of Gedo, Kismayo Mogadishu are considered as hotspot in need of urgent nutrition and health support interventions with critical prevalence of acute malnutrition of ≥15% GAM (FSNAU, September 2018). NAPAD through its vast experiences will ensure protection and restoration of livelihoods associated with food and income sources through provision of seasonally appropriate agricultural inputs, emergency livestock assistance, livelihood diversification, improved post-harvest practices and response to food chain threats.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project’s sustainability component is that its community led. All equipment and assets constructed including farming equipment, irrigation canals and demonstration farms will be owned, secured, maintained and managed by communities where they are located during and after period of project. After each project is commissioned, an MOU stipulating ownership, management and maintenance roles of each project will be signed btween all local elders, local administration and community groups. The selection of beneficiaries will be coordinated through the community structures. This will prevent conflicts based on purported discrimination. The engagement of women and youth self help groups to develop livelihoods will ensure that relevant business management and development skills will transferred to the selected beneficiaries. Empowerment of women and youth will be emphasized as they are change agents on economic empowerment and job creation for the most vulnerable. The targeting of mainly women, people with disability and youth among the IDPs and Host communities will ensure equal opportunities or all in economic development. The project purposes to develop the economic status and leadership capacities of women through training in leadership and governance as well as enterprenural skills development. Women in somalia have little to no access to startup capital and these projet plans to provide that opportunity to targeted women. This will be a step toward the growth of businesses increase in trade and devlopment of employment opportunities for other community members.This project will develop the agricultural sector in the riverrine farming communities thereby creating a food secure community, controlling malnutrition in the community and increasing household income from sale of surplus food production. The proposed project seeks to equip 400 riverine farmers with farming inputs and skills through demonstration farms as well as farming extension services. The project also seeks to diversify Abudwak community livelihood to include poultry farming. This will ensure that targeted household rear chicken for meat and egg purposes for sale and consumption

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The project targets both IDP and host communities as a strategic way of avoiding exclusion based on displacement. Assistance and services will reach both IDPs and the host communities. The location distances and access routes to distribution points will be accessible to all members of community . These locations will be central to ensure that beneficiaries should not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. NAPAD staff will ensure that all distribution points will be safe spaces from attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. Finally distributions should take place during so that all displaced beneficiaries will have sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. The communities and their leaderships will be actively involved is site selections to minimize disagreements that may lead to conflicts and eventual displacement. Communities will also be fully mobilized at the project start to ensure community buy in thus the community will protect the food security and livelihoods infrastructures to avoid take over by militant groups leading to increased displacements of most vulnerable.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Food security, humanitarian Aid and livelihoods programming of this proposed project will be based on dis-aggregated data by age, gender and diversity. In addition, a systematic process of beneficiary identification will ensure that pregnant and lactating women, under five children, older persons, and people with disability will receive quality assistance and services as any other groupings in the IDP and host community. For instance, female-headed households, child-headed households and unaccompanied children will be issued with food vouchers in their own names. This will ensure that they have direct access and control over their food voucher. NAPAD Staff especially during distributions will be gender balanced to ensure that women staff are available to address the needs of women and children. Women will get equal priority during all project opportunities. These include all cash for work during rehabilitation of canals and construction of dykes, identification and training of NRM committees and distribution of agricultural inputs. The livelihoods development component is designed to ensure that women, youth and able persons with disability will benefit either and from vocational training and business development. However, caution will be observed to ensure that women and children are not exploited or abused in order to receive any benefits. Additionally, during all distributions there will be separate male and female queuing areas, also giving priority to those who can’t stand for long in line such as the people with disability, older persons, unaccompanied children, pregnant and lactating women.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Poor engagement of community and their leaders from the inception of the project is a major risk factor that can lead to conflict that can escalate to violence. Comprehensive community and leadership engagement and orientation to the objectives of the project will ensure that the community owns the project. Discrimination, inequalities of targeting among clans as well as inaccessibility and competitiveness over natural resources is a risk factor that can lead to conflict and violence greatly impacting agricultural production and other small holder livelihoods. The impact of this conflict will be plundering of crops harvest and livestock discourage farming and business and ultimately contribute to displacement. This project seeks to engage the administration, local leadership and food security actors in target areas right from the inception of the project to the completion. This community leadership will ensure that there is will peace building and conflict management structures that will intervene in times of conflicts.
