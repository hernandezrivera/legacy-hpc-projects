Project Summary

These projects will target the most vulnerable children IDPs, Returnees and the Host- Communities at EJM Locality, SD State, Um Baru Locality, ND State and Rosseries and Gissan Localities of BN State with educational activities. The target groups will be children at basic education and their teachers and PTAs members from the most vulnerable and needy groups, namely the project will target the following schools:
SD State- EJM Locality schools:
- Dira Basic Mixed School with total of 1201 students (M 555, F 646)
- Deribat Basic for Girls with Total (100) students
- Korni Basic Mixed School with total of 837 students (M 419, F 418)
- Jawa Basic Mixed School with total of (845) students (M 493, F 352)
From ND State- Um Baru Locality:
- Um Baru Basic school for Girls with 540 total students
- Um Baru Basic school for Boys with 662 total students
- Garbara Mixed Basic school with 538 total students (M 238, F 300)
- Abu Laiha Mixed Basic school with 548 total students (M 268, F 280)
- Arkuri Mixed Basic school with 626 total students (M 355, F 271),
BN State- Rosserise and Giessan Localities:
- Jiwaria for girls with total of (690) students at El Roserise Admin Unit,
- Elimam Elmahdi Mixed School with total of 235 students (M 80, F 155) at Bados Admin Unit, El Roserise Locality,
- Anas Ibn Malik Mixed School with total of 163 students (M 78, F 85)
- Tajamoa Bakori Mixed School with total of 590 students (M 275, F 315) IDPs students at Giesan Locality.
All these schools need classrooms rehabilitation or TLS, WASH and Sanitation activities including Covid 19 awareness raising and hygiene promotion, training for teachers and PTAs, EiE materials for students and teachers, recreational materials and CFS activities. provide dignity hygienic kits to meet protection needs among students girls, Establish complaints mechanisms and orient the community about the humanitarian assistance laws and policies (e.g. PSEA), the importance of education especially for girls through community campaigns and awareness sessions.

1 - Needs

According to rapid assessment survey conducted at the SMoE- Refugees, IDPs and Returnees Unit Manager Mr. Abdallah Salih and Basic Education Unit Manager Mr. Elfadil Zakaria Adam, EJM had been established as a Locality composed of Deribat and Jawa Admin Units. The area has been broadly affected by insecurity and rotation of authorities since the beginning of the Darfur conflict 2003/2004; and witnessed movements of population to the internally displaced camps at Mershing, Nyala, Kass, Elfasher and Manawashei. The ongoing tensions and fighting between the Movements forced a large number of people to flee their homes seeking for settlement in different villages such as Dira, Kiba, Krony, Soro, Korbal, Baimadina, Sawani, Leiba and Jawa at the Jebel Marra mountains. The socio- economic structures of EJM had been broadly affected by the conflict as the entire displaced population sought refuge in satellite villages within JM mountains and some sought refuge in South and North Darfur IDPs camps, and therefore the social services are disrupted while the destruction of the scarce income generating sources devastated the general economic situation. In the view of general situation, population could be categorized into three groups; host- community, internally protracted displaced persons and returnees who returned home due to the improvement of the security situation within the territory. According to the above assessment, these two Admin Unit (Deribat and Jawa) have been affected by the Movements clashes that displaced civilians who should receive humanitarian assistance they urgently need. Um Baru Locality located far West North (240 Km) from Al Fasher Town. Surrounded by Meilet and Malha from the East, Kutum from the South, the Great Desert from the North, Chad and Altina from the Eastern direction. The Locality witnessed recent voluntary returns from in and out of the Country. This information confirmed by Mr. Hussein Adam Abdellah the X- Education Unit Director at Um Baru Locality till 2014 and who live now at the Locality. Blue Nile State has received recently an above average heavy rainy season resulting into flooding and related diseases which affected the whole State system including the education sector. According to the FMoE assessment a lot of basic schools at Blue Nile State have been differently affected by the heavy rains and flash floods (Emergency Committee statistics survey on September 2021). OCHA, UNICEF and the Government led joint efforts to assess and find out the humanitarian needs caused by flash floods, PBA Sub- Office team conducted a FGDs with key informants from the area and assessed the education needs of the people there and named some of the most needy schools to address their urgent assistance we could provide. The project will address the urgent needs of construction or rehabilitation of classrooms, provision of clean drinking water and hand- washing points, latrines construction or rehabilitation, seating, EiE and recreational materials for students and teachers, training for the teachers on core subjects and EiE, issues such as PSS and lifesaving skills, and management issues for PTAs members, awareness raising campaigns on the importance of education and enrollment, hygiene promotion campaigns and spreading a culture of peace among the target groups through CFSs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

- Rehabilitation and repair of 8 classrooms, construction of 15 TLS using environmental friendly materials (stones and durable materials to conserve the environment by reducing wood use in construction/ rehabilitation of classrooms. - Provide gender- sensitive WASH facilities at the 13 targeted schools including hygiene promotion campaigns, rehabilitation of 6 gender- sensitive latrines based on INEE and SPHERE minimum standards (30 girls/drop hole and 50 boys/drop hole- 6 seats each) based on WES design, 13 gender- sensitive hand washing facilities and 12 gender- sensitive safe drinking water points. - Provide gender- sensitive temporary safe drinking water points will be implemented at the 12 of the targeted basic schools. - Provision and distribution of education in emergency materials including school textbooks, exercise books, equipment, teaching aid and recreational materials for (7,575) children. - Conduct training workshops for enhancing PTAs capacities on EiE and school management plans for (3) days each. - Conduct training workshops on core subjects and teaching methods for (3) days each (in- service professional training) for 285 teachers - Provide seating desk/ bench for 1,012 students (253 sets),- Distribution of hygiene kits to (300) girls twice a year at the targeted girls schools, and - Provide schools improvement grant (SIP) for all the (13) targeted schools.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

PBA adopted Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to affected population (AAP) in setting standards and building capacity, communication, participation in programs, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve quality of partnership in principles of accountability. PBA is mainstreaming approach focusing on assessing and addressing the needs of all groups within communities where we work including men, women, boys and girls specially the most vulnerable groups such as groups with disabilities, minorities and all project’ stakeholders to ensure inclusive community consultation and participation, access to service by all, specific needs of extremely vulnerable groups and the safety of all project participants. To ensure that community engagement is maintained through various stages of implementation, and that key information is shared and collected by and with the integration of all these groups, PBA undertook the following: - Formulate Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) signed by all the project stakeholders to establish feedback and compliant and response mechanisms. - Sign with the project staff a contract with clear code of conduct steps to prevent all forms of abuse and sexual exploitation on affected population - Draft AAP guidelines for data collection and reporting. - Monitoring the project by all staff to ensure excellent and on- track implementation of the activities. - Organize community consultation meetings to promote participation and involvement of beneficiaries in the formation of community committees and planning the implementation schedule. - Community meetings held at the beginning of the project to orient the target groups and all stakeholders to ensure their understanding and acceptance as well as check the needs, guaranty the engagement and support of all groups and involvement of the target groups at all stages. . - Community Committees such as PTAs, CBCPNs oriented to organize and mobilize the beneficiaries. The Community Committee composed of 15 elected members. They help a lot at all assigned tasks during the implementation process such as following on construction and rehabilitation of classrooms, development of selection criterion of the training participants, transport and distribute the EiE, educational and seating materials, organization of the campaigns…. etc. - Appointed Community Mobilizers as feedback, complaint and response mechanisms for the targeted community. - PBA involved affected population at the Education Committees that composed of 10 members/ each school) which aims to participate on project implementation process e.g re-assessing the real gabs and priorities of education in schools at targeted communities at all Localities will conduct regular meetings and group discussion for follow up project performance.
