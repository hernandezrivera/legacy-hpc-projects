Project Summary

The project is designed to contribute to achieving the HRP response Plan strategic Objective # SO3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported”.
This project aims to protecting the livelihoods and enhancing the food security of the small-scale farmers and vulnerable livelihoods (with priority to female-headed HHs)in the Gaza Strip. and renewable power supply system for water wells and agricultural ponds for Greenhouses farmers (M/F) based on BBB approach to enhance resilience of agricultural sector. Furthermore, the project will work on promoting the rights of these groups through advocacy means and raising awareness activities to this end, 100 raising awareness sessions will be conducted with participants of 2000 farmers (94% M and 6%females). The project will target 250 HHs of farmers and poultry breeders (1,375 people:700 male,675 female). 500 poor HHs and 100 agri workers will also be targeted (3,300 people:1679 male,1621 female). In total 6,675 people will be benefited from the project different interventions.
The project will target 500 of dunums of open farms for 200 small-scale farmers (90% M and 10% F based on PARC experience) in order restore production and functionality by providing either full and/or partial land rehabilitation, fencing, piping and/or irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution based on the individual needs assessment of each farm. 3 Agricultural wells will be rehabilitated for the sake of rural communities; 90 farmers " MoA indicates that available Agri wells need rehabilitation to the north of GS to provide irrigation water to 30 farmers as average). 3 wells * 30 farmers = 90 beneficiaries in average". The wells will be supported with renewable power supply system (30 Kw each) to increase its functionality and durability to pump water during day time which will improve farmers access to water resources for irrigation purposes. The project will also rehabilitate 50 affected old agricultural ponds (concrete, plastering and plastic) as per the scoping study to examine available water ponds in GS (PARC/DKH July 2018).
The project will also provide rehabilitation works for 50 of small-scale poultry farms (70% M and 30% F/1000 checks capacity) and provide them with renewable power supply system (3 kw each) in order to make available the lighting, watering and ventilation required for the chicks.
In addition, the project will support the food security of 500 HHs poor and vulnerable Households (300 female headed ,200 male headed, in total 2,750 people) by providing them with 6 rounds of in-kind food parcels.
PARC is HAP certified Organization dedicated to “Do No Harm” to its targets and make sure that its activities are causing no harm to people. PARC has taken in into consideration the protection concerns raised by surveyed people and will make sure that its teams are able to identify protection and risks concerns, report them to responsible bodies and are dealt with as possible. PARC has a PSEA policy and will provide training to all its staff; that was planned to be conducted in coordination with PARC partners. PARC will also conduct awareness session to its target groups through specific sessions and awareness materials. PARC also has a code of conduct emphasis clearly on this. PARC will refer GBV cases to service providers through the agreed coordination mechanism.
Safety Standards of Operation SoPs have been developed for the safe implementation of activities with a commitment to Infection Prevention and Control measures and an enhanced mitigation approach to risks for both staff and the BENEs. The SoPs were jointly developed in cooperation with PUI France and UWAC. In case of movement restriction, the project will use the self-help modality for conditional cash as based on PARC pas experience.

1 - Needs

Already suffering from protracted conflict, restricted access to national resources, geographic fragmentation and intra-Palestinian divide, and decades of occupation, the implications of the C19 and subsequent economic crisis on the State of Palestine will deepen vulnerabilities and drive additional HHs into poverty, especially those surviving on income in informal sector. In Gaza, Beside C19, the deteriorating fiscal sustainability will have a serious impact on the ability to ensure social safety networks are sustained, including cash transfers to vulnerable HHs and the salaries and pensions of government workers, including front-line health workers. Furthermore, the pandemic came at critical time when food security in the Gaza Strip is already under straining. More than 80 percent of HHs in Gaza, depend on cash and in-kind assistance while 68% are food insecure as per last HNO. Beyond the health concerns and implications of COVID -19 pandemic, serious impacts are expected on food security and nutrition status in the Gaza Strip. C19 affected all four pillars of food security and nutrition: availability, access, utilization and stability. The most affected will be the poorest and most vulnerable groups and communities as they do not have resources to cope with the loss of jobs and incomes, the increase of food prices and the instability of food availability, accordingly have less ability to adapt to the crisis. OCHA C19 reports stated that more people have become food-insecure amid an unprecedented economic crisis. These include, among others, daily workers. In addition to many small-scale farmers continue to be affected. Furthermore, PARC Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) indicated that the income of the farmers and HHs decreased due to covid19 pandemic which has a bad implication on their livelihoods and food security. From other hand, increasing attention has to be given to Small-scale farmers (male-female) for being categorized as one of the most marginalized social groups and affected of food insecurity (UNWOMEN 2019) especially those whose access to land and agricultural inputs have been affected by the barrier, mobility restrictions and the blockade. As per latest updates of Ministry of agriculture, 15,000 dunum of open field are still in need for land rehabilitation interventions after the war 2014. Land rehabilitation in all governorates of the Gaza strip was set top humanitarian priorities for the Food security sector and the ministry of agriculture "HRP 2019". As per the needs assessment of PARC for the Organizational plan and latest scoping survey (PARC July 2019), land rehabilitation is still urgent and is high priority in the north governorates (1500 dunums) and Gaza governorate (2000 dunums). Power crisis as well continues to affect farmers ability to sustain their small-scale business: The continue energy crisis, though relative improvement, still imposes an additional burden on farmers who already suffer from an increase in agricultural input costs accompanied by low purchasing power by HHs due to falling economic situation, salary cuts and shortfall in Aid provided to Palestine. Reported in the PARC scoping survey July 2019. agricultural ponds: The bad quality of water in some areas, and power crisis to pump up water for irrigation purposes along with the drought challenges have decreased farmers' resilience and increased farmers’ vulnerability to food insecurity and poverty. This has also obliged farmers to resort to negative coping mechanism like selling the land and/or abandoning farming as profession, (PARC and Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe 2016). PARC has identified 2700 Agri ponds in the GS with different functionality conditions (PARC and DKH 2018). The scoping survey (PARC July 2019) has indicated that agricultural ponds continue to be a high priority. Moreover, and based on past experience of PARC 2013-2018 water drought resilience program DKH and Christian Aid, installing of renewable power supply system to water ponds, give the farmers ability to control the irrigation process/timing, as well as to provide assistance to surrounding farmers' community to maximize the benefit and enhance collaboration and social bonds. Agricultural wells: MoA has set the rehabilitation of agricultural wells as high priority in all Governorates as per HRP 2021. Based on the MoA, rehabilitation of agricultural wells in still high in the north and Gaza governorate where 35 and 48 agricultural wells are still in need for rehabilitation. As well, agricultural wells have been set as high priority intervention for PARC organizational plan 2019-2020. Poultry farmers are one of the most affected groups by the C19 pandemic, the results of PARC Rapid Needs Assessment indicated that this sector is badly affected by the low demand due to the reduced access to market and the decreased purchase power of the HHs in the Gaza Strip, which threatening the ability of the sustainability of this sector. In addition to the implication of C19, Poultry farmers are already suffering from a chronic deteriorated situation. According to MoA 700 broiler poultry farms are at risk due to the electricity shortage. As well, due to the deterioration of the economic situation in the past three years, most of animal farms were sold. This is also due to the fact that farmers are unable to sustain the costs of the business and have accumulated debts that they cannot repay, & agro-suppliers are not assets/agr-input on credit anymore. As part of PARC`s Rapid Needs Assessment, conducted in September 2020; the female poultry farmers, who represents 8% of the assessment`s respondents, expressed their need to renewable power supply. On the other side of the spectrum, the male farmers, (8% of the assessment respondents) said that they need cash support to help them to secure inputs they need which includes feeders, chicks, feed, wood shavings, water and veterinary costs.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

PARC has a mechanism regarding accountability and receiving feedback from beneficiaries; Handling complains and ensuring accountability measures are implemented since the design phase of the project until the handing over of all activities, Feedback is ensured via HAP mechanism that is adopted by PARC which ensures to provide a secure space for targeted population to express their dissatisfaction. This mechanism includes a well-designed complaint form that will be tracked through PARC’s MEAL officer and documented through the MEAL documentation system. All project activities will be followed by the MEAL officer who collects data to develop the lesson learned on two levels; during the implementation and after achieving the project in order to be taken in consideration for achieving the rest of the project in a good manner and the upcoming projects. In 2015, PARC developed its guidance to HAP policy systems with Christian Aid Britain, which includes participation, information sharing, and complaint and response policies. A HAP complaint mechanism will be elaborated at targeted communities and audience will be encouraged to claim their rights. The mechanism allows beneficiaries and community members to provide their feedback and report to PARC in a formal and confidential manner. This is promoted and implemented in coordination local committees (LCs). Hence, a complaint and feedback box will be introduced to VCs, placed in the targeted communities in addition to a generic e-mail, field visits and satisfaction survery, and is easily accessible to community members (males and females). Of course, PARC team will ensure that health and safety measures are taken into consideration throughout the project life cycle.
