Project Summary

The second year of the project implemented by Cesvi in partnership with IMC gives continuity to the
work already undertaken in 2020 in addressing the protection needs of vulnerable migrants,
refugees,host communities, Internal Displaced Persons (IDPs) and
Returnees, in urban communities located in Misrata and surroundings.
The expected outcome of the project is the improvement of the protection environment
of the targeted vulnerable population by (a) providing psychosocial and protection
emergency assistance services, inclusive of cash assistance and NFI distributions; (b) improving access of
vulnerable Libyans and non-Libyans living in urban areas to humanitarian assistance and
specialized protection and GBV services; (c) enhancing the knowledge of local
stakeholders on protection approaches, norms, and humanitarian and human rights laws.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis that Libya has faced over the past years, coupled with the Covid-19 outbreak, have significantly eroded the population capacity to revert to positive coping mechanisms and fulfil their basic needs. Political instability and successive waves of armed conflict across the country continue to result in displacement and humanitarian needs of vulnerable populations with the numbers of IDP’sincreasing from 343,180, as stated in the HNO last year, to 425,714 in June 2020. Violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian law against migrants and refugees remain a grave concern. Migrants and refugees continue to be subjected to torture, sexual violence, abduction for ransom, trafficking in persons, forced labour and unlawful killings throughout Libya. Discrimination in access to services and lack of documentation continue to prevent migrants and refugees from meeting their basic needs including healthcare, food, shelter, education and specialized protection assistance. So far this year, at least 900 people have drowned in the Mediterranean trying to reach European shores, some due to delays in rescue. Unaccompanied migrant and refugee children have also reduced coping mechanisms placing them atheightened risk. Durable solutions for refugee children also diminished, as opportunities to be resettled and reunified with families were greatly impacted by travel restrictions due to Covid-19. Moreover, GBV service providers continue to grapple with providing safely access life-saving assistance due to widespread social stigma, social exclusion, lack of freedom of movement and adequate service provision. Lockdowns have further restricted access to services and community safety networks, particulary for survivors living with perpetrators. Migrants and refugees, are unable to safely access life-saving health services and social protection networks. Lack of economic and livelihood opportunities and the sharp increase in unemployment rates, from 17% in February to 29% in June, are also impacting the most vulnerable. Migrants who rely on daily labor report being negatively affected by a significant decrease in casual labor opportunities. The poor economic situations and limited income continue to be one of the main factors driving and increasing humanitarian needs, particularly within vulnerable groups such as IDP’s and migrants challenging to meet their most basic needs including shelter, water and food.

2 - Activities

Screening for vulnerabilities and assessment/monitoring of the needs of the targeted population; Distribution of core relief items (CRIs) and gender sensitive kits; Provision of counselling, MHPSS and case management services, including for GBV survivors (PFA, MHPSS Individual and Group Support, Case Management including for GBV survivors, External Referrals); Awareness and sensitization on Protection and GBV principles and services; Community-based care arrangement for vulnerable migrants and refugees with a specific focus on women and children; Individual Protection Assistance (IPA) to assist vulnerable groups exposed to a wide range of protection risks, including IDPs, returnees and host families affected by armed conflict as well as migrants and refugees; Cash for Rent; Training on GBV and Health essential package to relevant service providers; Technical assistance and regular coaching to Ministry of Social Affairs (MoSA-Misrata Branch) in delivering quality psycho-social services to vulnerable women, including GBV survivors; Community led initiatives addressing protection challenges focusing on the engagement of women and girls; Organization of trainings and workshops targeting local stakeholders (CSOs members and frontline workers, and local authorities

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Based on the experience in the country and in the target location, Cesvi ensures an integrated approach that entails a set of activities seeking to reduce the risk of exposure to protection incidents and threats. To guarantee such approach, the project is being implemented using the socioecological model, where different layers are taken into account in all steps of the project cycle: the individual, the family, the community and the society. The socio-ecological model is thus entailed in both prevention and response related activities envisaged through: 1. Analysis and monitoring of the impact of the humanitarian crisis on risk and resilience factors at the four levels; 2. Consultations and involvement of individuals, families, communities, local authorities in the activities; 3. Awareness raising on protection and principles at the four levels; 4. Advocate to improve coordination among actors to prevent gaps and duplication of aid; 5. Implementation of capacity-building initiatives that include prevention actions led by families, communities and service providers (such as local authorities and CSOs); 6. Provision of specialized protection services, including case management and psychosocial support, at all levels of the socio-ecological model. Moreover, the response strategy aims to ensure a comprehensive package of activities to be tailored to the needs and profile of the affected individuals/groups: Individual Protection Assistance (IPA), Cash for Rent, Community-based care arrangement (hosting families) and distribution of NFIs that complement the provision of specialized services, enhancing their effectiveness. Concerning the specific risk 4.2 (Increased tensions and conflicts in the community due to perceived or actual unequal distribution of assistance) Cesvi has different entry points for assistance, chosen on the basis of their capacity to reach people in need among specific groups: for the host community and IDPs, MoSA and CSOs represents the main point of entry; while the engagement of Community Mobilizers as entry points in the migrant communities facilitates the reach of all groups regardless of their status. Moreover, our need-based approach vis a vis a target-based approach mitigates as well the emerging of possible tensions between host community, IDPs and migrant community. Concerning the specific risk 4.6 (Disclosure of sensitive personal data of affected persons, including safety and confidentiality of case management conducted in detention centres, leading to increased protection risks and further harm), Cesvi staff receives constant training on Confidential Information and Confidentiality to ensure buy in and ownership to reduce harm. Concerning the specific risk 4.8 (Lack of access to principled (safe, dignified, voluntary) access to durable solutions for persons in forced displacement), the case management approach and the and the use of Interagency Referral Mechanism and SOPs qualify as mitigation measures in order to complement other short and medium-term temporary solutions, that alone would create a void of durable solutions and a lack of sustainability in the intervention. Concerning the risk 4.14 (Exposure of individuals to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance), Cesvi has in place a safeguarding system designed to prevent Cesvi staff, operations and programmes from harming children and vulnerable adults, or from exposing them to risk of harm and abuse, and to ensure adequate response is provided.Safeguarding policies – PSEA Policy and Child Safeguarding Policy –set standards of conduct expected by staff to prevent any form of violence or abusive behaviour against those we serve. Cesvi Policies and Codes acceptance is integral part of contracts and agreements.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Cesvi has adopted a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism in order to to receiveand handle feedback and complaints for all services provided. Cesvi Complaints andFeedback Mechanism targets 3 kinds of communication regarding organization, staffand project, namely: feedback, operational complaints and serious complaints, applyingthe following categorization: • Feedback: it refers to positive, neutral or minor negativecommunications. They can be positive comments, suggestions, requests for information,requests for assessment, etc. but also negative comments, which do not fall under thecategories of operational and serious complaints, as described below. This positive,neutral or minor attribute implies the absence of an investigation phase, while keeps onensuring a response to the feedback provider. • Operational Complaints: communicationcharacterized by a negative connotation, which may refer to project, staff or organization.Operational complaints are limited to issues linked with operations, such as delay inactivities, low quality of provided products or services, low quality of staff performance,etc. • Serious Complaints: communication characterized by a negative connotation, whichmay regard project, staff or organization and refers to a breach of the principles and rulesestablished by Cesvi’s policies and prescriptive documents. These three categories ofcommunication are received and managed through the same CFM, but applying differentHandling Procedures (person in charge, maximum time to provide a response,investigation, etc.). All feedback received are recorded in a database that ishandled by one focal point channelling the feedback to relevant staff in order to providethe correct answer to beneficiaries. The CFM is also expected to contribute the lessonslearned approach, allowing the project identify concerns, needs and opinions of thecommunity.
