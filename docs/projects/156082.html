Project Summary

Deir al-Zour is a city located in the east of Syria, and one of the most affected areas by the Syrian crisis, with a damage exceeds 75% of the city including medical centers and related health sector facilities, with hazardous remnants of war scattered everywhere, such as landmines and unexploded ordnance. However, the area is now considered under government control.
Deir Ez-Zor including (Al Jourah & Al Qusur and Al Hamidiyyeh with neighboring countryside) is an area that has a population of over 500,000. Most residents are currently very poor and internally displaced after seven years of war and encircle, resulted in more than 105,300 of them need medical care & services. High percentage of the population are children and families who rely on women as their main breadwinners, and Dummar Youth Charity will provide its medical services to 10,000 of the aforementioned 105,300.
Due to the high population of Deir Ez-Zor including surroundings and the inability of many of them to leave the area (due to security/financial reasons i.e. medical compensations). The project aims at establishing Two Mobile Medical Clinics in Deir Ez-Zor area and surroundings to provide free medical services with referral system, and find job opportunities to jobless people of the area (mainly women).
The exact locations of the two mobile medical clinics in Deir Ez-Zor will be in Al Hamidiyyah near Sqaure and in Al Jourah near Law Faculty Square (respectively). And referral system will be executed in coordination with local hospitals in the area and with hospitals (to beneficiaries who are unable to travel) and in Damascus (to beneficiaries how are able to travel). And medical services do not include medication.

Needs

In view of the large number of inhabitants and the continued displacement to the region, which has generated increased needs. Deir Ez-Zor including (Al Jourah & Al Qusur and Al Hamidiyyeh with neighboring countryside) is an area that has a population of over 500,000. Most residents are very poor and internally displaced after seven years of war and encircle, resulted in more than 105,300 of them need medical care & services. High percentage of the population are children and families who rely on women as their main breadwinners. (More than 60% are women). The area is considered under government control.These people need local nearby health services including emergency health care. Patients who require periodical health care services (dialysis, diabetes, etc.) have highly critical situation since they are required to go the health centers outside their areas periodically. Most people in the area have no capacity to pay for proper health services due to their financial situation. The first step of the project will be the selection of proper locations in Deir Ez-Zor area and surrounding; the location will be strategic so it will serve two to three areas from neighborhood of Deir Ez-Zor area. Protection Risk Analysis

The risk of failure to provide full free service, and this will be addressed through permanent monitoring and daily contact with beneficiaries and by implementing active strict quality procedure and penalties.In referral system there is the risk of the non-availability of a specified medical service in the local area, and this will be addressed by transferring referrals of beneficiaries to nearby medical centers or to Damascus and provide beneficiaries with medical attendance cards to facilitate mobility.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk of continuous power outages and this will be addressed by providing generators to mobile clinics. The difficulty of reaching (security) from the nearby countryside, and here it is addressed by choosing strategic locations of the mobile medical clinics which are easily accessible by beneficiaries in addition to the fact that most villages in the area have returned to government control. The risk of lacking of commitment of some beneficiaries of continuing treatment process, and this will be addressed by daily follow-up medical system and conducting daily questionnaires to beneficiaries and closing each case separately.
