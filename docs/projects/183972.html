Project Summary

The goal of the project is to provide timely housing solutions and NFIs to assist returnees and IDPs in Khartoum affected by floods. Through a combination of approaches and direct intervention, reduced vulnerabil-ity, increased social resilience and improved living con-ditions for communities in need of humanitarian assis-tance will be achieved. The project includes technical training and capacity building , purchasing and place-ment of NFI kits and partial housing restora-tion/modernization kits in case of emergencies in the vicinity of Khartoum.
Within the framework of the project, CF “CF “HADC” will strive to implement an integrated approach to project implementation, creating interconnections between programs at all stages of activity, which will contribute to ensuring awareness, safety, protection of rights, access to social guarantees, solving issues with employment and housing.
In order to minimize humanitarian assistance in the future and develop long-term solutions for beneficiaries that will help transform them from social burden into social capital, within the framework of the project, the Charitable Organization “Charitable Foundation “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” (HADC) plans to involve social institutions in joint planning of work within the framework of the project. Also, beneficiaries who, within the framework of the training, prove themselves as people with an active civic position, who want to change the social situation, will be further involved in HADC projects as volunteers. In the foreseeable future, our organization plans to develop and implement a program to train social community leaders (agents of influence), who in the future will be able to independently develop solutions that improve the lives of IDPs and other vulnerable persons.

1 - Needs

Khartoum state is estimated to be home to 8.4 million people, of which about 19% are in need of humanitarian assistance, according to the 2021 Sudanese HNO. According to the Ministry of Irrigation and Water Resources, as of August 18, the water level in the Nile in Khartoum state reached 17.14 meters, higher than the flood risk level of 16.50 meters. As of September 29, heavy rains and flash floods affected more than 314 000 people in 14 of the 18 states of the country. More than 15,500 homes were destroyed and 46,500 homes damaged, resulting in the temporary displacement of about 100,750 people. Most of the displaced people take refuge with friends or family, or in public buildings near their homes, and some live in open, elevated areas. It should be noted that flooded water sources and power plants make life extremely difficult for affected communities. Displaced persons (new IDPs, long-term displaced persons and returnees) need shelter and NFIs such as plastic sheeting, plastic rugs, jerrycans, blankets and kitchen sets. These items help restore minimum self-esteem and reduce health risks by providing some privacy and security to those in need. Displaced women and girls are especially vulnerable without shelter and basic survival resources, essential elements that help them survive and maintain their own dignity. Meeting gender-specific needs and opportunities OF housing ensures an adequate standard of living for women and girls. Affected populations include various groups - including those displaced or affected by natural disasters and conflicts. It should be noted that pregnant and lactating women, chronic patients, the elderly, unaccompanied minors and people with disabilities are especially vulnerable. Supporting shelters and NFIs can help protect them and improve their physical and mental health. According to the 2021 Sudanese HNO, housing and NFI needs are expected to grow from 1.2 million in 2020 to 1.9 million in 2021. Based on detailed analysis of key indicators, approximately 43 localities will have acute needs that could be extreme or catastrophic (severity 4 or 5) In eastern Sudan and many parts of central and northern Sudan, severity is projected to remain low (severity 1). However, the population of Khartoum and several other states remains vulnerable to hazards such as flooding, and the current situation suggests that demand will continue to grow in 2022.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project involves the implementation of the fol-lowing targeted activities:1.1. Technical training and capacity building for 1500 beneficiaries in Khartoum in the following are-as (education and training):- methods of construction of shelters, restoration and modernization of shelters;- raising awareness about fires, floods, best prac-tices for mitigating the impact of natural disasters, rules of conduct and where to go for help;- information about the environment and improved management of natural resources, efficient use of en-ergy and environmental protection, solid waste man-agement;- the use of stabilized soil technology (SSB) as an al-ternative to fired bricks. Additionally, women will receive training in household goods production and food conservation (canning) techniques.The above training will automatically, not only pro-vide them with the means build and improve their eve-ryday mode of life, but will also contribute to employ-ment opportunities for a long-term livelihood. For groups of the population with access to the Internet, trainings will be conducted online, with the aim of re-ducing the number of people on offline trainings, to minimize the spread of Covid-19.To conduct trainings, training methodological man-uals will be developed, which in the future can be used by indirect beneficiaries for self-training, as well as other humanitarian actors, as part of similar project activities. Educational methodological manuals will contain not only text, but also an infographic part, in order to reach the illiterate population.- 1500 developed and published training manuals.The trainings will be conducted with the support of local authorities and will target community beneficiar-ies and community leaders.1.2. Improving living conditions by providing:- 500 partial housing restoration/modernization kits;- 1000 complete NFI kits.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” understands that communities living in countries affected by conflict are more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. Armed conflict destroys infrastructure, weakens institutions, impedes access to basic services and erodes social cohesion - all critical to human resilience and adaptation to climate change. Communities are on a survival rope in the face of these complex crises. Given that the protection of civilians remains the highest priority in the implementation of this project, it also takes into account environmental lenses that will contribute to the effective management of natural resources, which, among other things, contribute to the restoration of basic livelihoods. In the implementation of this project, the Foundation operates with one of the humanitarian principles – "Do no harm", which is part of the concept of reducing the impact on the environment.Thus, the training programs will include mandatory items that train the project beneficiaries in waste management, waste separation and disposal (including construction waste), training in cleaning their local area, careful attitude to natural resources (plants, water, land). Also, beneficiaries will receive training on raising awareness about fires, floods, best practices for mitigating the impact of natural disasters, rules of conduct and where to go for assistance; environmental awareness and improved natural resource management, energy efficiency and environmental protection, solid waste management; using stabilized soil technology (SSB) as an alternative to fired bricks.In the training centers, to train opinion leaders, a separate course on environmental protection will be developed and conducted, which includes hygienic aspects, cleaning and separation of garbage, careful handling of nature, water resources, etc.In the context of climate change, the carbon cost of international deployment will also be taken into account when defining the workforce. The project will employ mainly local staff, with a limited presence of international staff. Local staff will be trained in the principles of environmental protection. Also during the project, the staff of the Foundation will conduct a systematic analysis of the cost-effectiveness of the impact of this humanitarian operation on the environment.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

People affected by conflicts, riots and natural disasters are almost always exposed to high risks of violence (physical, psychological and sexual), rights violations, coercion and exploitation. Individual and community protection capacities, as well as national protection mechanisms, are often compromised or insufficient to cope with the protection risks posed by ever-emerging threats. Thus, the situation in the field of protection will be taken into account separately, including when interacting with stakeholders and government officials. When developing and implementing the project, the location of the facilities where the project is being implemented will be taken into account, the distance of routes from real or potential threats, such as: violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence and attacks by armed groups.Protection risks and violations will be determined by characteristics such as minority status, gender, sexual orientation, age and other diversity factors. Thus, the central role of protection in this project is, together with the communities, to identify groups at high risk in the field of protection, to find out the types and causes of such risk, and also to analyze these features, taking into account external factors that affect the protection mechanism itself. In addition, this approach involves identifying specific weaknesses that give rise to such risks, taking into account the unique experiences of men, women, girls and boys, such groups as the elderly, people with disabilities, LGBT and other groups.A wide range of representatives of the local community will have the opportunity to participate in the decision-making process in an inclusive and non-discriminatory manner that has a real impact on the provision of a humanitarian response. The CO “CF “Humanitarian Aid and Development Center” in this direction will use communicative approaches, assuming the presence of clear and direct feedback between the staff who develop and control the program, and the communities themselves. The project also plans to improve protection by differentiating the risks (needs) of the local community, which will allow for more targeted assistance. This will help, among other things, identify the underlying drivers of conflict and thereby improve the protection of the local community, while avoiding the harm that humanitarian action could otherwise do.
