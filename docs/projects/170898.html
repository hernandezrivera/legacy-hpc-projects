Project Summary

Information, Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) Project aims to contribute to protection and promotion of the rights and durable solutions for Palestinians in Gaza in accordance with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights law (IHRL). Within this goal, the specific objectives are to: (1) strengthen the respect for international humanitarian and human rights law and principles, including promoting accountability for violations thereof; and (2) ensure that a greater number of the most vulnerable Gazans have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. ICLA will target 3,000 Palestinians in Gaza identiﬁed as affected by protracted protection crisis and conflict-related violations, while prioritising individuals, families and communities in dire socio-economic situation affected by freedom of movement restrictions, especially medical patients, families separated between Gaza and West Bank, students and others in need of travel for humanitarian reasons; vulnerable female-headed households, widows and divorced women; children at risk of resorting to negative coping mechanisms such as school dropout and child labour, persons with disabilities; as well as families at risk of eviction, IDPs and refugees from the region who lack documentation and face challenges in accessing humanitarian assistance.

1 - Needs

2020 brought a deterioration in the severity of protection risks in Palestine driven by the outbreak of COVID-19 and related movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic distress. The administrative vacuum, because of the PA halt in coordination with Israel, has introduced additional challenges. UN-OCHA estimates that at least 2.1 million Palestinians, 60 per cent in the Gaza Strip and 40 per cent in the West Bank experience, or are at risk of conflict, violence and forcible transfer, and will be in need of protection assistance in 2021. The most recent NRC legal needs assessment in Gaza was conducted in late 2019 early 2020. The main findings of the report include; an estimated 10,000 individuals in Gaza without Palestinian ID cards, including thousands whom Israel considers ineligible for receiving IDs. 40 % of persons surveyed had difficulty obtaining a civil identity document, yet only 8.4 %, amounting to 16 individuals pursued legal assistance, with the majority seeking this assistance through UN agencies. Travel and movement are among the biggest concerns for Gaza’s residents. The majority of the respondents (51.9%) do not have a document to travel or move freely for a variety of reasons, primarily due to the lack of permits, IDs and passports. 67.3% of those that do not have the document indicated that they do not know the criteria for which permits are granted, while 61.2 % indicate that they do not have easy access to secure the necessary documents, of which 39.2% indicated that they do need legal assistance. NRC’s legal aid needs assessment showed that 24.4 % of survey respondents indicate that they have a family member with a disability with the vast majority of those having motor disabilities (83.8 %). Moreover, 71.2 % of respondents indicated a family member with chronic diseases, 5.4% elderly over 65 years of age and 9 % have suffered injury due to the GMR, of which only 16.2 % received legal assistance to challenge Israel’s practices causing their injuries. Of those who did not receive legal assistance, the vast majority either believed that it was not a case that could be addressed through legal intervention with the Israelis (45.9 %) or did not know that legal assistance was available (35.1 %). With high rates of poverty, food insecurity and living in conflict zones, medical needs are undoubtedly high. Of those who needed medical referral outside of Gaza, 45.8 % did not receive it because they did not know the procedure to obtain a permit to exit Gaza (37.9%) or they did not have assistance to be able to obtain one (30.3%). For those who receive medical referrals, 21.1 % were still unable to travel. Even if a permit was obtained, 72% of patients did not travel because their escort could not obtain the permit. According to those interviewed for the NRC legal aid needs assessment, 13.1 % indicate that they own a house, land or property inside Access Restricted Areas. Of those interviewed, 37 % indicated that their inability to access their houses, land or property inside the ARA negatively impacted their income. Of those affected, 48.1 % declared that they have become dependent upon aid, while 40.7 % lost access to their homes. As regards legal assistance, the vast majority, 74 % did not seek any assistance while those who did sought assistance with UN agencies and with local NGOs. Those who did not seek assistance declared that they did not think a case could be made (43.7 %) while others (64.6 %) did not know how to contact legal aid organizations. In addition, 37.5 % indicated that they did not believe that there is benefit from obtaining legal assistance. This may highlight insufficient outreach by NGOs towards target beneficiaries. A majority (53.5 %) of respondents who sought legal assistance approached private lawyers, (66.7 %), followed by the government (20 %) and NGOs and international NGOs, each being sought after by 6.7 %. The overwhelming majority of those interviewed in the course of NRC legal aid needs assessment indicated that they did not work (71.4 %). Of those who reported that they worked, 53.6 % indicated they had a formal employment contract. Knowledge of rights, benefits and terms of the contract was roughly 77.8 %, but overall knowledge of the general framework of labour rights under Palestinian law was less, with only 44.8 % indicating that they have knowledge. For those who faced legal issues, (6.8 %) only 12.1 % went to court while the remainder reported that they resorted to alternative dispute resolution methods (family, 75 %, labour union, 12 % and formal mediation 37.5 %). An estimated 9,356 families in Gaza were at risk of eviction in 2019 over their inability to pay rent. Respondents indicate that in the past two years 10.6 % of them have been exposed to eviction. Respondents also indicate that 6.7 % of them have been evicted in the past two years. For those struggling to pay rent, 75 % obtained humanitarian assistance while others opted to leave the house (6.3 %), moved in with relatives or had others move in with them. Of those surveyed, 64.1 % have children in school (80.4 % in elementary school and 19.6 % in secondary school). Fifteen % of those surveyed have a child who dropped out of school whilst 85 % of those surveyed do not. Of the reasons for withdrawing, there were two primary reasons: economic (45.2 %) and poor achievements (35.7 %). There is a slightly higher %age of university withdrawals (17.5 %), all of whom left university for economic reasons. Interviewees were also asked about children’s exposure to violence. Of those interviewed 11.1 % declared that some of their children were exposed to violence. Of those exposed, 73 % were exposed to psychological violence while the remaining 26.2 % reported that they were exposed to physical violence. Of those exposed to violence, there were two main sources: school (53.1 %) and home (16.3 %).

2 - Activities

Whilst the Israeli system remains inadequate for protecting HLP, freedom of movement and legal status rights under international law, legal interventions to engage Israeli and local authorities can mitigate the effect of current violations and prevent future violations. In accordance with the Protection Cluster objectives 1 & 2, ICLA will provide: (i) Legal aid to secure freedom of movement for patients in need of medical care outside Gaza; (ii) Legal aid to most vulnerable HH/groups displaced or at risk of displacement (Housing, Land, Property rights); (iii) Legal representation for individual victims of IHL/IHRL violations – accountability; and (iv) Awareness raising on accessing legal aid in context of IHL/IHRL violations, freedom of movement and displacement.In addition, ICLA will continue to coordinate activities between International and Palestinian legal aid providers through the Legal Task Force (LTF), and will ensure active participation in fora such as HCT, Protection & Shelter Clusters. ICLA will also contribute to advocacy in relation to legal issues related to HLP (incl. for women), freedom of movement, lack of legal identity and accountability for IHL and IHRL violations through: (i) monitoring and documentation of violations and related trends, focused on IHL and IHRL violations and conflict related violence; (ii) evidence generation and advocacy on the impact of violations on children including grave violations; (iii) advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers. An integrated legal response will be provided through PC and partner organizations. PC Shelter/Wash, ICLA and UDOC staff will work together to respond to the multiple needs of beneﬁciaries when feasible. Furthermore, ICLA with UDOC will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. Emphasis will be placed on an integrated approach among PC programmes, in order to ensure strong coordination and synergies. In addition, PC’s advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blockade through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the involved individuals while upholding the do no harm principle. PC will work with strategic, project and lateral partners to ensure the beneficiaries have access adequate standard of living, including livelihoods, effective and accessible mechanisms to protect housing, land and property rights, access to personal and other documentation, access to services including health and education, and freedom of movement. The support to civil society actors and community initiatives will enhance local participation and ownership, as key factors of sustainability. The role of potential implementing partner organizations will be: (i) court representation, mediation & arbitration, cooperative dispute resolution mechanisms (with focus on women’s rights), awareness sessions for mukhtars and community leaders; (ii) monitoring and documentation of Israeli violations, especially in the ARA and legal aid to seek justice/redress on behalf of victims; legal aid to secure freedom of movement, including of patients in need of medical care outside of Gaza; (iv) monitoring, documentation and legal aid on access to facilitate freedom of movement, including of patients in need of medical care outside Gaza. (v) legal counselling and assistance on issues related to freedom of movement in front of Israeli authorities. PC will provide partners with technical support on IHL violations, housing, land and property and access restrictions through in- house lawyers. ICLA programme is designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians especially people with disabilities. While there are no special activities are designed to address PWDs under the project, the project prioritizes inclusion of PWD as one of the most vulnerable groups subject to human rights violations in Gaza. The project will ensure access to PWD to report on violations and to request assistance as needed. NRC and partners will make special arrangements to accommodate beneficiaries with special needs such as PWDs, those who suffer from specific medical conditions, and elderly people. For example, beneficiaries who cannot reach the NRC and partners offices or locations will be visited by the team at their places to mitigate difficulties associated with movement. NRC will ensure that all government protective measures related to COVID-19 are implemented at NRC level as well as with all partners under the project. NRC has several policies in place to allow the continuity of operations in a safe environment for both staff and beneficiaries. The policies include Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) to operate in the office and the field environments under such circumstances, and it aims to mitigate any Covid 19 related risks. NRC policies are in line with the instructions of the Ministry of Health and international standers. These protective measures include strict hygiene practice such as avoiding handshakes and sharing utensils, regular hand wash and sanitization with alcohol sanitizers, maintaining physical distancing, wearing protective gear (facemasks and gloves) at all times and using sanitizers before entering NRC locations. NRC SOPs also provide limitations on number of individuals in same room per size. Strict measures will also be enforced to ensure safety in the field (physical distancing, number of beneficiaries in one location, and protective masks). NRC and partners will also use telecommunication with project beneficiaries and will limit direct contact the lowest level possible. Alternatives to direct communication will include utilizing social media platforms such as Facebook, and websites as well as messenger, WhatApp and regular phone calls.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In order to prioritize safety and dignity of refugees over other considerations, ICLA will conduct protection risk assessments (Do no Harm), adopt conflict and context sensitive approach in dealing with beneficiaries, ensure informed beneficiary consent and acting on instructions of beneficiaries, securing confidentiality and data protection. In addition, ICLA will use additional protection technical expertise to supplement its interventions; coordinate with specialised protection actors to assist with conducting protection risk assessment of ICLA beneficiaries, carry out combined ICLA/protection campaign around protection issues; in collaboration with gender / child protection actors, conduct joint legal and protection research around a particular protection issue or risk in order to inform programming or recruit staff with expert protection skills in NRC. For example, although the legal framework might enshrine certain rights and entitlements that NRC would ideally like to pursue for women, NRC will not do so, if the process of accessing justice were assessed as likely to do harm to the claimants.NRC’s Protection Policy, Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and guidance notes and tools including Core Competency Quality Assessment tools and the Protection Resource Pack all contribute to reinforcing and providing guidance on how to action the protection policy. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners and associates (e.g. contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff. At the community level, ICLA will support multi-sectoral, sustainable, and integrated programming with the NRC UDOC, Shelter/WASH and Education Core Competencies and with other humanitarian actors, which will allow people to preserve or increase their resources and opportunities, thus making them more resilient, and more likely to find durable solutions. ICLA and UDOC will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. ICLA will conduct needs assessments, service mapping and where relevant, refer beneficiaries to other NRC core competencies or other partners providing specific services. NRC ICLA will support NRC UDOC programme and OCHA in the assessment, dissemination of information and implement referral pathways in order to ensure coordinated prevention and response to emergency. ICLA will actively and regularly participate in the Protection Cluster, lead Legal Task Force and collaborate with other Clusters and actors (e.g. UNRWA) on awareness raising, sensitisation and information dissemination activities to ensure that people have meaningful access to information and feedback mechanisms. NRC Advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blocked through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the individuals involved, while upholding the do no harm principle.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

ICLA programme is designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to-reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduce their vulnerability and build resilience. ICLA activities will be complementary to other existing Protection activities in order to avoid duplication and ensure holistic protection interventions. Information dissemination and counselling: ICLA’s main priorities will be to empower women, men, girls and boys to have equal access to information and services. ICLA will develop information and communication products to support IDPs/refugees and host communities’ access to basic services, rights and entitlements. Accurate, timely and relevant legal information and awareness-raising messages will be disseminated through outreach activities, including face-to-face information and counselling by trained field staff. ICLA will also use a community-based and participatory approach to identify and train members of the communities to disseminate information, recognise protection concerns and refer cases to specialised service providers. Legal assistance including representation and/or collaborative dispute resolution: ICLA may use judicial and administrative remedies, customary or religious mechanisms and collaborative dispute resolution methodologies to support access to justice for the most vulnerable including women, children and people with disabilities. Approaches and procedures will be selected on a case-by-case basis to allow for a customised response that meets the specific needs and interests of (a) beneficiary(s). In this respect, carefully selected and trained fe/male officers may provide legal information and advice, facilitate negotiations, and if relevant, provide legal representation. Legal assistance will be delivered through NRC and partners’ legal aid clinics including mobile legal aid clinics to ensure widest possible outreach for people with limited mobility. Flexible and creative approaches will be used to address barriers to access to justice such as: health, education, costs, distance, etc.Capacity Building: ICLA will also provide technical support (sensitisation, training, orientation, etc.) to local NGOs, youth and women leaders and community representatives, as well as humanitarian actors with a view to strengthening their capacity to ensure protection for IDPs/refugees and support host communities. Particular emphasis will be placed on IHL/IHRL, rules and regulations about the rights and issues of women and other vulnerable groups. Subject to needs assessment, ICLA may also provide financial and organisational support to implementing partner to strengthen their capacity and ensure sustainability of the intervention. Advocacy: Research and technical support on legal status, freedom of movement, access to rights and services and adequate standard of living, including housing will be used to inform the major protection actors, donor community, and other relevant stakeholders, with the respective analyses of the current protection situation, strengthen the networks and seek allies in order to influence policies and decision making in relation to the targeted population.
