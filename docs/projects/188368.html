Project Summary

It became very clear during that that good education must intersect with good psychological and health readiness in children, which in turn is reflected on their behavioral status, as conflicts, whatever their form, negatively affect children in a very deep way.
From this point of view, the "Striving" project seeks to work to participate in bridging the gaps in the educational environment surrounding children, especially those who have been exposed to the most severe types of conditions that hinder learning.
The project is a sustainable educational and developmental center in the Quneitra area, Al-Khishniyeh district, one of the most affected areas during the years of war. The municipality of Al-Suwaisah, the village of Ain El-Tineh, was chosen to establish the center as an easy access point from all other areas and the safest one.
The project targets children who have dropped out, who have difficulty accessing schools, or who have poor education in their schools, as well as the teachers involved in the educational process, as well as the community surrounding these children.
The project adopts a diversity, gender and age approach an integrated approach to support learning for children that includes several dimensions to improve the quality of their learning:
The health dimension: which allows the child to learn to take care of his health in its comprehensive concept (mental, psychological and physical) as a basis for obtaining high quality learning.
The personal dimension: building capabilities, developing life skills, and acquiring healthy lifestyles that take care of the child as an individual interacting with himself and his educational environment.
The social dimension: through educating the local community and building its capacities to be a supporter of the educational process for children. In addition to training the female breadwinners in a range of professions that guarantee them an income that enables them to send their children for education.
The educational dimension: building the appropriate capacities of teachers to advance the educational process in the target area.
The protective dimension: securing the environment and safe and supportive spaces for the child to be able to learn.
The technological dimension: working to provide educational opportunities through learning to employ technology and to access, search for, and benefit from learning resources.
The project was designed to suit the target area’s need for a supportive and easy-to-access educational space by equipping the center with all the necessary equipment at the spatial level, and qualifying a work team with the skills necessary to work with children during the first year of the project’s life, in addition to targeting 1920 beneficiaries, including 60 teachers who will be trained On how to deal with children and carry out educational support tasks, 1,000 children in educational support activities, 800 children in psychological support activities, and 60 women in vocational training activities to be able to send their children to learn. In addition to a set of life skills activities as an accompanying part to all other activities, as a basis for learning all other skills, and series of activities and events targeting the community in general will also be held with the aim of raising awareness and strengthening partnership with it.
The cost of the project during the first year is $325,000, which includes the preparation of the place, the costs of activities, training, rehabilitation and supplies. In the following year, it excludes the costs of preparing the place.

4 - Needs

UNICEF placed Al-Khashniya at severity level 3. According to its statistics, the total number of children in need is 3,322 children and educational staff, including 1,538 males and 1,784 females.After conducting a field assessment, the results indicated a sharp decline in the pace of education due to the war conditions, and the displacement of the majority of the population.The area’s recovery period has only exceeded three years, but despite all the efforts made in all sectors, especially education, services are no longer sufficient under the current economic conditions, as she explains:Lack of teachers, especially specialists.Children drop out of education.Teachers are switching to other professionsWeak belief in the importance of education.Lack of early childhood services.Some children lag behind their peers because they did not enroll in their classes at school age, and some of them are still muffled, or because they studied different curricula for several years.Increasing the number of female breadwinners who are unable to work economically.The complete absence of professional centers, training institutes, or educational support spaces.The absence of secondary schools in all their branches and their limitation to a comprehensive secondary school within the village, which hinders students' access to and receiving an education.Children learn a curriculum other than the government curriculum for several years.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented according to a pre-prepared schedule, starting from the activity of preparing the center until closing the project, through: An experienced and specialized team Use of project management applications such as (MS project) Building a detailed database of community members in the area Dividing the tasks and defining the responsibilities of the team members according to a work matrix (Work Breakdown Structure), in which each activity is mentioned and who is responsible for its implementation. Develop a logical framework for project activities Develop a follow-up and evaluation system to ensure that the project is progressing as planned in terms of time, quality, performance and cash budget. It includes a set of tools such as participatory assessment tools (interviews, questionnaires .... etc.)The selection and preparation of the appropriate building will begin, and then the project will be presented to the local community through a series of meetings with community leaders and members of the community to ensure acceptance. In parallel, the center’s work team will be rehabilitated with a set of trainings, and teachers will be qualified to carry out educational support activities. And then move on to start providing services within a specific program and time frame.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

There is a range of risks to beneficiaries or employees related to sexual abuse and exploitation, such as:Harassment within the work environment.Requesting sexual services from children or their dependents in exchange for money or to facilitate access to services.Sexual behavior by one of the parties stems from a sense of force.The strategy of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse is an essential part of the institution’s approach to community work, and therefore it has developed a policy of its own. The Foundation will also conduct awareness campaigns on the need to report any cases that may fall under the name of sexual exploitation or abuse, with full commitment to maintaining confidentiality. The Foundation is also committed to training its staff on the policy.
