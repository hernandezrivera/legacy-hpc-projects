Project Summary

Ensure equitable access to WASH essential services, in safety and dignity, to the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys located in unconnected communities, thus contributing to the achievement of WASH cluster objective no.1. The project addresses immediate needs as well as the underlying drivers of those needs, in line with the water master plan for Area C developed by GVC and AAH, in cooperation with PWA, as well as the PWA National Water Sector Strategic Plan and Action Plan (2017-22). The intervention encompasses: - A responsive measure, comprehensive of water access massive interventions (pipeline, filling point, HHs connection) and storage water intervention (rain water harvesting cisterns); - A remedial measure, through the provision of sanitation facilities The initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of people in need increasing water access for unconnected communities in area C and guarantee a safe level of sanitation for the most vulnerable groups in the West Bank. The initiative will connect 6 communities (3,980 people) in Area C with safe water domestic connections. 3 communities (425 people) will increase their storage capacity through construction of rainwater harvesting cisterns while 4 (445 people) will see improved their level of sanitation and hygiene. The action aims to support gender and age -sensitive relief provision and gender and age-responsive protection as a means to meeting specific vulnerable groups’ material and safety needs, ensure gender and age responsive assistance and equitable access to humanitarian goods and services, prevent/mitigate potential negative effects of the action on different gender/age groups and ensure adequate participation. The action aims at responding to the differentiated vulnerabilities of all community members, considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Analyses and strategies utilized throughout the project take special consideration of the vulnerability and needs of women, children, people with disabilities and the elderly. Needs analyses, activities, outcomes, and indicators consider the diverse needs of each community member and vulnerable groups. All the interventions will trigger a positive effect on the reduction of vulnerability and the increase of resilience of the community members, especially the most vulnerable groups as boys, girls, women and men. The action has been endorsed by PWA and in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) –, and is in compliance with the water Master Plan for Area C. These activities are in line and part of the 2017-22 PWA Strategy Plan, and the priorities set for 2019 by the Joint Service Councils, West Bank Water Department and PWA. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.

Needs

The most vulnerable people living under occupation in Area C are in urgent need for an increased sustainable access to essential WASH services, ensuring sufficient, safe, acceptable, physically accessible and affordable water for personal and domestic uses. This project lays its basis on 3 recent thorough assessments conducted by GVC which allowed identifying the most unconnected communities and groups in terms of WASH services and lacking proper coping mechanisms to withstand the protracted threats and shocks, with high risk of displacement(i) Water access indicators survey conducted on 2018 in 56 communities, in the framework of the UNICEF GVC water trucking programme; initiated in 2014 in 90 communities, this assessment has been updated in a yearly basis.(ii) Water Master Plan for Area C for 247 vulnerable communities, published in 2017 (iii) Community based Protection Approach conducted by GVC (and the other agencies of the West Bank Protection Consortium) for the development of the Protection Community Profiles and Responses Plans – including a specific analysis of the WASH sector of 163 communities located in Area C and H2, initiated in 2015 and updated on yearly basisThese communities are dependent on the direct purchase of water for around 7 m/y, failing to match WASH Humanitarian Benchmarks (HB) of 60 l/c/d, and even the critical minimum HB of 30 l/c/d/ for 25% of the population. Water expenses can account for up to 50% of the family monthly expenses with a negative impact especially on women who prioritize the water use for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, and men who prioritize the use of water for their livestock rather than themselves. According to the WASH Cluster parameters 100% of water trucked sources are unsafe, 86% of the communities are located in Area C and over 54% in sensitive Area C. These trends generate a coercive environment with specific protection impacts increasing the risk of displacement of the populations, and the construction of water facilities stands as the humanitarian response to improve their access to basic services improving their living conditions. Particularly, these communities are characterized by: A chronic erosion of income for the most vulnerable groups, depleting their livelihood and affecting the overall communities’ resilience (division of the income within the family critically affect the needs of young and women since that the main amount is addressed to purchase water for drinking, domestic and agricultural/herding purpose)Increased exposure to settlers’ attacks and IF interventions, while fetching water from distant water sources. Men and young boys are specifically affected when using mobile tankers, while women and girls while fetching water from cisterns located in unsafe zones.Constrained access to domestic/drinking water has direct consequences on the general wellbeing of the population. Children are particularly affected due to their higher vulnerability, and women and girls being responsible for the wellbeing of the family members. Lack of water and sanitation facilities affects specifically hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation, and leads to extra workload and stress, being they in charge of all house works. The sense of privacy and personal safety of women and girls is critically jeopardized. Most of the communities targeted do not dispose of functioning latrines for all the inhabitants, which means open defecation practice is still wide used. This produces direct negative consequences on protection and privacy of most vulnerable groups, such as women and adolescent girls and on health supporting the propagation of water born disease, extremely dangerous especially for the infants children. Women and girls are the most affected group based on 3 dimensions: 1) Quality of life 2) Feeling of Protection 3) division of duties

Activities

All the initiative will encompass, when feasible, the adoption of GVC Community Implementation. This methodology encompasses a creation a Project Committee composed of representatives of the communities/ CBOs (gender balanced), which are the local implementing partners. CPCs are responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of the infrastructures (construction of rain water harvesting cisterns and pipelines and provision of mobile latrines). CPCs will receive 15-20% of the direct cost, through conditional cash transfer modality. The entire amount of direct costs (which includes material and cost of labor) is therefore paid to them upon receipt of a payment request with a progress report (prepared by the sector responsible and verified by Technical Coordinator and Project Manager) that verifies the work accomplishment along with the payments amount eligibility. Capacity building and monitoring will be done by GVC along the intervention. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee will be conducted. Specific SoP are followed during implementation to minimize/mitigate risks. The Community Implementation will allow to reduce the risk of confiscation and other potential obstacles related to the coercive environment as Area C, and to enhance the commitment and ownership of the beneficiaries. The project will benefit 13 communities located in 5 Governorates in Area C of West Bank (Bethlehem, Hebron, Jericho, Nablus and Tubas). Activity 1. Needs assessments: at the beginning of the action a need assessment will be carried out to verify any changes of needs and/or objectives, as well as to assess the security situation to enable the interventions to be conducted according to plan. The need assessment will be tailored to analyze the needs, the role and the dynamics of the beneficiaries through the following Implementation of the Community Protection Approach: a contextualized sectorial protection analysis (including WASH) including Focus groups/ Individual Interviews/ Observatory walk/ Daily and Seasonal calendars/ Risks and Resources Maps/ Legal Mapping involving women, men, boys and girls separated for all the steps. Activity 2: Community Implementation: creation a Project Committee. Prior to any activity, a risk analysis, including legal consultation with the lawyer of the community and the Project committee will be conducted Activity 3. Improvement of water access through domestic water connections: • Construction of transmission pipeline from a filling point to the community : Jourat Al Jamal 2.5km (steel); Khureise 3.5km (steel); • Expansion of the existing transmission pipeline; installation of house connections: Wadi Al Ghrous 2km (steel) Al Masara 2km (steel), Khirbet Tanna 2.4km (HDPE), North Al ‘Auja 2.2 km (HDPE). Activity 3. Improvement of water storage capacity through construction of cisterns: • Construction of rainwater harvesting cisterns (70m3) to increase water availability and storage capacity (locations and n. of cisterns): Arab Ka’abneh 7; Al Rahnia 20; Kheroshewesh Wal Hadedeyah 20. Activity 4. Improvement of sanitation services through the installation of latrines: • Provision of mobile latrines (locations and n. of latrines): Al Hadidiya 12, Al Qaffaf 11, Hammammat al Maleh al Meiteh 6, Khirbet Tell el Himma 7. The project implementation is based on the principles of protection, safety, and dignity of the beneficiaries, principle of the “Do No Harm”, while considering, community participation, vulnerability concerns, non-discrimination, security and protection. Proper coordination will be done between PWA, WBWD, the Governorates, local village councils, Joint Service Councils, Municipalities and all other major stakeholders active with WASH in the targeted areas. Coordination with other organizations working in this sector has been carried out to avoid overlapping (e.g. ACF, PHG, CISP, etc..)

Indicators

The overall intervention aims at preventing the forcible transfer of targeted communities by improving access to basic WASH services, and the fulfillment of the fundamental human right of access to water in sufficient quality and quantity, safely physically accessible and affordable. The action is targeting the most vulnerable communities in Area C, close to settlements or military zones, facing restrictions for development and affected by or under the threat of forced displacement. All the beneficiary communities fall within the priority geographic locations and within the vulnerability benchmarks identified by the WASH Cluster and the Palestinian Authorities. All the interventions aim at reducing the distance to access to water for people, increasing their resilience and reducing their probability to be forcible displaced. The beneficiaries will benefit of an increase in terms of water access through two main initiative: the construction of pipeline, HHs connections and filling points; and the increase of storage water capacity through implementation of rainwater harvesting cisterns. The targeted people will benefit of an increase of availability and an improvement of quality of drinking water. This will be reflected in a decrease of water costs and purchase of truck water influencing directly the management of the family’s income and the livelihoods. Especially men and boys will benefit from a sensitive improvement of security from the reduction of the distance driven to collect water with tankers. In communities where women and girls were tasked to walk to collect water, women and girls will be the most positively affected beneficiaries. In addition, the beneficiaries will benefit from an increase of access to sanitation through the provision of mobile latrines aiming to reduce the risks of infections and water born disease typical of a coercive context when most of the HHs practice open defecation. In particularly, young children, girls and women will benefit from reduces workload and risks of water contamination with direct impact on the health, especially of the young children and adolescents. Through the Provision of safe drinking and domestic water services, the action will achieve the following HRP Indicator: 4,850 people with increased access safe drinking, domestic water and sanitation/solid waste services Specifically, the action will achieve the below targets: - 4,405 people (1001 women, 1054 men, 1119 girls, 1178 boys, 26 elderly women, 27 elderly men) have improved their access to water through HHs connections and rain water harvesting cisterns. - 445 people (101 women, 106 men, 113 girls, 119 boys, 3 elderly women, 3 elderly men) have increased access to safe sanitation services. - 1102 adult women have reduced their daily workload due to increasing of water access and improvement of sanitation services. - 47 families have increased storage capacity of beneficiary households. An ongoing monitoring against the initial disaggregated risk analysis will be carried out as part of the continuous process of analysis, combining both quantitative and qualitative data systematized through a series of outputs. The action encompasses a participatory approach that includes focus group and individual interviews (with men, women, girls and boys, with and without disability separated) to assess the needs and identify activities that take into account the need and dynamics of the beneficiaries, analyzing the different effect of the initiative on different age and gender groups. After the completion of the intervention the qualitative impact of the initiative will be recorded through FGs to collect the feedback of different vulnerable groups (women, men, boys, girls, elderlies). Through the Community Implementation approach, the feedback of the beneficiaries will be collected along the WASH infrastructures.
