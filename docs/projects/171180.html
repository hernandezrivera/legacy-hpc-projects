Project Summary

According to FSNAU technical release in 30th Sept 2020, As a result of combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)1 or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance and government support are currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million. Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2020 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes for 2.1 million people. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2 or higher).

Project Activities

Unconditional cash transfer (UCT). Project will deliver monthly cash to 6,000 households in eight districts in Somalia. Each household will be provided to 60 USD for a period of 4 months to enable them meet their short term food needs. Conditional cash transfers (CCT). Project will provide cash-for-work (CfW) opportunities for 4,000 households (HHs) who have experienced severe economic shocks prioritizing female headed households who might be at risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV). This temporary employment will have a “double impact” by providing cash to families as well as improving infrastructure to revitalize local market functioning. Beneficiaries will work for 3-4 hours a day for a total of 15 to 20 days per month, but only during seasons which are not disruptive to other seasonal demands for labor. Daily rates will be aligned with the recommended rate in the latest cash TWG and FSNAU-FEWSNETUCT and CFW will target the most vulnerable households affected by the recurrent shocks including drought, desert locust, floods and COVID19 who face significant or extreme food consumption gaps whose levels of malnutrition are acute. Their lack of conditionality will ensure that recipients can respond quickly to their own greatest needs, while the cash injection should stimulate the local economy. Project will deliver monthly UCTs and CCT of US$ 1.2 million over 9 months to 60,000 households. The context of COVID-19, CARE Somalia has developed Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) guidance note which explore different ways to mitigate the spread and impact of COVID-19 through ongoing CVA and inform on the adaption of CVA including promoting sensitivity to changing market dynamics and prices. CARE will continue to 100% UCT which adopted to the COVID -19 and will revert to CfW when the curve flattens. CARE will work with reputable cash transfer companies with clear procedural agreements to ensure the protection of beneficiaries and other standards. Cash will be delivered safely via the robust Mobile Money transfers (MMTs) system as recommended by the Somalia Food Security Cluster and the cash working group- so as to ensure beneficiary privacy and reduce risks to staff and beneficiaries. UCT and CfW recipients will also be given Sim cards and CARE will use a kobo collect for the beneficiary registration system during this period of COVID 19.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

According to FSNAU technical release in 30th Sept 2020, from October to December, food insecurity is expected to deteriorate among poor households with limited livestock or low capacity to cope with harvest losses thus the use of cash transfer will empower the crises affected communities to meet their most basic needs. Damage from Desert Locust could exacerbate the impact of a below-average 2020 Deyr season. Desert Locust will likely continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture and crops across Somalia through late 2020, and the situation requires continued close monitoring and scaling up of control measures. According to results from 37 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in July and August 2020, there has been some improvement in the overall nutrition situation among rural and urban populations compare to previous seasons. However, the nutrition situation among IDPs has shown no improvement. For population groups that registered a high prevalence of acute malnutrition, contributing factors include high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices and food insecurity. At the national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10-14.9%) over the past three seasons (11.8% in 2020 Gu, 13.1% in 2019 Deyr and 13.8% in 2019 Gu). Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. Unconditional and conditional cash transfer will unable vulnerable households to buy food during this critical time in order to reduce hunger and malnutrition

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Project will transfer cash on monthly bases, households will buy food and essential items on monthly bases, we have functional markets in the project target sites are functioning and affected vulnerable populations are able to access the food which is available and accessible. According Somalia joint market and supply chain updates in Oct 2020, there were increases of retail prices of most staples and essential items in the main markets of Bossaso, Qardho and Garowe. For instance, rice, wheat flour and sugar, which are most, consumed food items increased by 8% - 13% compared to previous weeks due to devaluation of Somali Shillings against the USD.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities; CARE will continually monitor the situation of the targeted communities and provide updates to the donor and the cluster members. CARE has developed a multi-hazard emergency preparedness and response strategy, including “current scenario, most likely scenarios and the worst case scenario”, the populations and regions forecasted to be affected. It has put in place deliberate effort to scale up its humanitarian response to reach out to new beneficiaries and geographical areas2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities; The project staff will continuously assess vulnerabilities to identify most vulnerable members of the community, such as ethnic minorities and the disabled persons. Members of the marginalization groups will be invited in the consultation meetings. Community awareness campaigns will be conducted in order to address the barriers. The safety of the needed people covered by the project will be prioritized. Through CARE ‘s complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM), the project will provide a safe and confidential ways of receiving, managing and responding to the excluded and the marginalised communities. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms; CARE will involve committees/local leaders, local authorities in the monitoring of activities. Meetings will be held with communities to explain project purpose and the target groups. Most vulnerable groups which include women and girls will be consulted. Community leaders and the clan elders will be given full project orientations and seek their support for providing the assistance to the most needed people. CARE will also ensure community engagement on issues that are pertinent to project implementation and solicit their feedback to ensure community ownership of the project. CARE will inform the communities of the available mechanisms to provide feedback so that beneficiary concerns are taken into consideration for quality service delivery.4. CARE uses standard selection criteria which is devoid of influence and ensure the community leadership is involved in the selection of the beneficiaries. CARE will undertake physical verification of beneficiaries selected and facilitate open forums for vetting
