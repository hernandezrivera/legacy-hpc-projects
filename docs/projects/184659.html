Project Summary

According to HNO 2022, Conflict, drought, COVID-19, and flooding are some of the main shocks that drive displacement and largely affect at least 2.9 million internally displaced persons in Somalia. As per the CCCM Cluster and partners, the IDP's have settled in 2,400 sub-standard and unplanned IDP sites in urban, semi-urban and rural areas across the country. The IDP's are in need of multi-sectoral support services including protection from harsh climatic conditions, improvements to their living conditions and improvement to access livelihoods and durable solutions.
The extreme drought condition in Juballand may continue to perpetuate more displacement in 2022 and present more protection concerns on displaced persons, including women and girls resulting an alarming levels of SGBV in IDP settlements. As per Juballand Ministry of Humanitarian and disaster management over 60% of the animal in Juballand had perished due to the prolonged drought and huge pastoral dropouts together with those fleeing from the AS conflicts are now overcrowding in Afmadow and Kismayo cities. As per the last IDP site verification exercise in Kismayo on May 2021 conducted by the CCCM partners indicates there are 146 verified IDP sites hosting 66,051 Individuals. Of these 31% and 40% are facing extreme risk of eviction and flooding respectively. The IDPs in Kismayo are concentrated in sub-standard sites which are either privately owned land or government owned without any formalized land tenure agreement. This precipitate frequent evictions and continued displacement that contribute to protection risk particularly women and girls. There is a strong need to assess risk and observe vulnerability of displaced populations in Kismayo and Afmadow. The Shelter conditions of the IDPs are made up of locally made materials and old pieces of clothes that expose harsh climatic conditions and infringe privacy. Limited knowledge on fire prevention and awareness, poor drainage systems and lack of protection emergency items such as solor lamps, poor physical structure such as fence remains priority as part of routine site maintenance and site improvement activities.
Through this project, SFH aims to address immediate needs and improve the living conditions of the newly displaced and the protracted IDP's living in a deplorable conditions in Kismayo and Afmadow districts of Lower Juba region by strengthening protection referral pathways, support and participate coordination mechanism that advocate for provision of essential social services to IDP's, and contribute to community-driven site maintenance activities including infrastructure rehabilitation and safe spaces, build existing governance structures on CCCM principles and participate safety audits assessments in the target sites while working towards transitioning to durable solutions.
SFH will train 40 camp management committees and Key local authorities representatives on HLP (Housing, Land and Property) to improve their knowledge and understanding of HLP issues as well flood and fire prevention techniques. SFH will also train its CCCM staff on site planning and layout to ensure that all site maintenance projects implemented in the target district meet the required standards. Assistance will be provided in accordance with minimum humanitarian standards of service delivery and protection while ensuring every member of the IDP community participate in the activities directly or through inclusive representation including persons with disability. SFH will conduct regular monitoring to track population changes and communal facility inspections to ensure that the camp is properly maintained and/ or upgraded to meet Sphere standards and the changing needs of the IDP's. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. Referrals will be established with GBV AOR. SFH will established responsive and reliable CFM.

1. - Projects activities

Camp management ensures Assistance and Protection to the camp dwellers in accordance with the relevant laws and minimum humanitarian standards while ensuring inclusive participation and equitable access to assistance without MINORITY exclusion. In line with CCCM cluster strategy SFH will coordinate with WASH/Protection/Health and Shelter Clusters to ensure realization of objective 1 of the cluster through active participation of the cluster operations and joint advocacy on the needs of the IDPs. SFH will improve the living conditions of the displaced populations through site care and maintenance activities that will enable them access income through conditional cash for work approach involving rehabilitation of communal infrastructures, safe spaces, back-filling drainage systems and participating paid communal clean-up and Solid waste management activities. SFH will contribute to site level actor’s effort to address the increasing needs for the displaced families. SFH will also participate site identifications, mapping, monitoring and help de-congest site and relocate the IDP's where applicable. Through routine monitoring exercise and coordination, SFH will help increase CCCM coverage through monitoring the displacement conditions and provide baseline information on multi-sectorial issues and needs of the displaced population which will be captured in the master database that will be shared with the humanitarian actors for planning and response. Where feasible SFH will support the new site development (preparation, improvement and expansion) in Kismayo to de-congest sites and mitigate furthers risks in protection and health. To enhance protection and ensure physical and material security of the Minority and disable persons. SFH will conduct safety audits to identify the specific needs of the women and girls and locate routes associated with high risk of GBV. SFH will Coordinate with protection and GBV partners and establish strong referral pathway. Organize inclusive sensitization session with SMC, Community leaders, men, women and person with disability and disseminate information about availability of humanitarian assistance at the sites including Clinical management of rape, Psycho-social and mental health counseling and availability of dignity kits. SFH will Install 20 public Street solar lights at risk prone locations identified as result of the safety audit assessment and provide Household Solar lanterns to 1000 most vulnerable households that are far from street light and at high risk of GBV. Post distribution monitoring will be undertaken after every distribution round. Establish responsive and accessible complaint and response feedback mechanism that will enable beneficiaries provide timely and confidential feedback. To enhance the capacity of the existing governance structures, SFH will Conduct 3 days capacity building on CCCM modalities and related principles for SMC and government officials, this will involve: Humanitarian principles, actors coordination mechanism, camp management house( assistance, protection, law and standards and participation-which are all essential throughout the 3 phases of camp life (set-up, care and maintenance and closure), roles and responsibilities, data collection and information management systems, prevention, monitoring and referral of protection risk. SFH will conduct regular monitoring to track population changes including WASH and HEALTH facilities to ensure that the camp is properly maintained and/ or upgraded to meet Sphere standards and the changing needs of the IDPs. Particular attention regarding needs will be paid to women and girls who also have to content with GBV and protection concerns within camp settings. General activities includes Mapping services at the IDP sites, strengthening protection referral pathways, support coordination mechanism at the site level that highlight gaps and service delivery. Minority and Persons living disability remains prioritised by default.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. Mitigation measure:- a) Targeting will be guided by needs, access and vulnerability and the vulnerable groups include women headed household, child headed households b) Inclusive representation of the committees will be prioritized to ensure persons with disability or special needs are represented and their data made available. c) SFH will make special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits. they will be assisted and their safe arrivals ensured. 4.2 - Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. Mitigation measures:- a) All SFH staff and suppliers are required to sign PSEA policy and its violation will lead to disciplinary measures including termination of contract. b) SFH will ensure appropriate gender balance and composition of registration and distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community. c) We will establish separate male and female queuing areas and ensure female staff presence at distributions so that they are accessible to women and girls.4.3 - Exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements. Mitigation measures:- a) Advocate for improved shelter through advocacy with the shelter cluster. b) Install solor light along the route to the water and sanitation facilities c) Provide solar torch to women to avoid risk of GBV at night d) Ensure the latrines are lockable from inside e) Advocate and support site de-congestion and better security of tenure f) Conduct safety audits to identify the most critical safety gaps.4.5 - Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area. Mitigation measures:- a) Advocate with the local authorities to provide alternative lands and tenure for de-congestion. b) Coordinate with mine action actors at the site level to conduct mine and explosive risk education including procedures of reporting and collection. c) Training of CMC on HLP to equip them on the knowledge of land tenure and rights.
