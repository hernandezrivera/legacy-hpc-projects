Project Summary

1: The EORE component of the action will target 30,880 direct beneficiaries, with a main focus on girls and boys, including persons with disabilities. The team will train 540 Community focal point persons (270 women and 270 men), 850 PREs (Peer Risk Educators) (425 girls and 425 boys) to keep the messages within different age groups including the persons with disabilities (PWD). The EORE component will be implemented in Juba County, Central Equatoria as well as Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria State.
2. The target hazardous areas during the 2023 project period will be 50 and that would be used throughout the project proposal. Beside the HA to be surveyed or cleared, the action will be that DCA will handover at least 600,000 sqm of land released from ERWs for a minimum of 12 HA surveyed /Cleared of 50,000 sqm each. And actual number of HAs will depend on upon the UNMAS tasking and prioritization. An estimated 16,760 people will benefit from the land release.
In summary, the overall goal of the proposed project is to increase the civilian security by protecting the lives and property through the removal of explosive Remnants of War and sustainable Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) as the main objective for the project. The team will also be focusing on the returnees currently coming home from refugee settlements in Uganda and beyond within Magwi County especially in Nimule, Pageri, Magwi, Mugali and Pajok Payam through Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, and NTS/Clearance to make the land safe for the returnees to settle and safely cultivate land for farming. The Multi-Tasking and Mechanical team will conduct Non-Technical Survey/Clearance while Explosive Risk Education Team will focus on the sensitization of the local community about the dangers of ERWs and what to do in case they come across the explosive devices, they EORE team will further train some community members as focal points (CFPs), and Children both at school and community level as Peer Risk Educators to educate their peer on the dangers of ERWs and landmines.
DCA propose this activity to scale up its current Mine Action intervention in terms of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and Clearance/NTS in Juba County, Central Equatoria and Magwi County, Eastern Equatoria State.

1 - Needs

Decades of conflict has littered vast areas of land in South Sudan with mines, ERW, and cluster munitions. The risks of these ERW prevent people’s safe movement, including those fleeing from violence or trying to return to their homes; preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid; and inhibit socio-economic development by injuring community members and making local land, resources and infrastructures inaccessible. The conflict have left most of the fertile lands contaminated with Landmines and Explosive Remnants of War creating obstacles for human settlement and livelihood activities like farming and other economic activities. In addition, the decades of long civil war, the recent conflicts which started in 2013 between the Government of South Sudan and the SPLA-IO has added more threats to the local communities as most of the local communities within Magwi County fled their home areas due to fear for their lives and their areas occupied by the military personnel seen in Pageri payam along Juba-Nimule Road. With the withdrawal of the vast majority of these soldiers to other parts of the country, there are a number of local community members voluntarily returning home, they would be the focus for this project where they need safe land and environment/protection as put in the project objects. This action can be achieved through direct Explosive Ordnance Risk Education and Clearance/NTS. DCA plans to reach all the Payams under Magwi considering the severity of the level of contamination of the areas affected by the Explosive remnants of War and Landmines. Additionally, Central Equatoria State continues to be among the most contaminated areas in South Sudan affected by ERW as a result of recent fighting and decades of conflict. Contamination includes both minefields, BACs with multiple cluster munition strike areas. Contamination near populated areas have resulted in 27 registered ERW accidents until October 2022, totalling to 5221 accidents registered since 2004, with children being among the groups most at risk (IMSMA, October 2022). Within Central Equatoria State, Juba County continues to be one of the counties with the highest contamination of mines and ERW. Thus, the need to provide comprehensive, sustainable and innovative risk education in the area is significant.

2 - Project activities

In line with the overall strategic objective to ensure that “Vulnerable people are exposed to fewer protection threats and incidents, and those who are exposed have access to safe, tailored, timely and dignified access to appropriate services through integrated and inclusive humanitarian action”, DCA is well-positioned to carry out mine action clearance and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE). The action shall include two main components, namely land handover to the affected communities through land release and clearance of suspected hazardous areas and EORE awareness sessions and trainings based on the inputs from the contamination severity Maps provided by UNMAS, beneficiary vulnerability feedback from the past needs assessments during the field surveys in highly suspected areas of contamination. During the project period in 2023, DCA plan to reach a total of 47,640 beneficiaries with handover of 600,000 sqm of land released from ERW threat through mechanical and manual demining and through Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE). The crucial part is community liaison and EORE which will be tackled by the EORE team as an entry point tool for the successful implementation of the project activities as per the proposal. The team will meet and liaise with different stakeholders before, during and after the implementation targeting all the age groups and the gender inclusive. Out of the Explosive Ordnance Risk Education target of 30,880 girls, boys, men and women, the EORE team will train 540 CFPs (270 women and 270 men) and 850 PREs (425 girls and 425 boys) both at school and community level to pass safely messages to other community members while identifying and reporting all the spotted hazardous areas to DCA, Mine Action Partner or UNMAS. That would justify that participation and involvement of the beneficiaries as per the overall objectives. The CFPs and PREs will continue to disseminate EORE messages after the project ends. DCA, given its long standing static presence in Magwi County and Juba County, will continue to follow up and continually serves as a point of contact for communities, through trained CFPs, for any spot tasks, ensuring removal of identified explosives. DCA has ongoing relationships with many CFPs throughout Magwi and Juba County and this long-term commitment has proven very valuable. However, to measure the impact of the intervention of the action, DCA will conduct follow up visit to the trained CFPs and PREs and there will be Baseline and Edline survey conducted to assess and get feedback of the direct EORE sessions conducted from different age groups and gender.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

DCA will pay specific attention to the feedback given by the groups at risk and the vulnerable during the and after the project implementation. In this sense, a dedicated effort will be placed to ensure all the groups are able to equally access the project activities which will be implemented in line with the humanitarian principals and standards. Hence all the above individuals, including persons living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women, men, girls, boys, and elderly persons.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

DCA will ensure that accountability towards the community is fully enhanced through beneficiary’s engagement during monitoring, Evaluation and learning activities such as pre- and post-knowledge, Attitude, and behavioral change (KABP analysis, interviews, and post impact assessment. The beneficiaries and the local community representative will be given opportunity to provide feedbacks regarding activity performance and the overall impact of the action, achieving two objectives; first collect data to improve the team’s targeting and performance, secondly ensure that accountability of the implementation of the activities is registered and shared with the relevant counterparts and Mine Action stakeholders. All information and feedback and complaint mechanisms will be provided in the local language.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Although DCA will not be directly implementing PSEA, however the team will always be connected with other implementing partners to make sure any siting sighting of any abuse and sexual exploitation in the areas of the operation are reported to the concerned focal point persons that concerns with PSEA.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All the feedbacks from the project beneficiaries will be treated vital especially during the pre KABP survey which will help the adaptability of the response from the analysis got from the survey conducted before starting the project. Hence this would help the team to adapt appropriate methodology to achieve the project objectives as per the overall goals of the cluster.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

The release of cleared land allows the targeted communities to use it for productive purposes. This means that local food production is increased. At the same time, released land provides space for safe and sustainable returns from neighboring countries, predominantly Uganda. Returnees will require access to land and food to settle. As this will initially increase the burden on the host community, it is important to provide opportunities for food production, preventing an increase in the population facing IPC 4 and 5.
