Project Summary

JDF seek to strengthen the livelihoods resilience of IDPS, returnees and host communities and especially the vulnerable persons in Askira/Uba and Monguno to be able to withstand current and future external stresses and shocks, considering the the recent security bridge which led to the displacement and movement of many persons from their ancestral homes and communities. Communities are considered to be resilient if they are able to take charge and manage economic activities on their own and in a sustainable manner. JDF will as a priority implement the following activities to ensure that the above can attained within the two local government areas of Borno state
1. Distribution of farm inputs 2. Livestock support 3. Group/village savings and loan association
Livestock: Jireh Doo Foundation has the ability to receive and distribute livestock to beneficiaries. JDF has the ability to receive and quarantine livestock, supervise the feeding and care of the livestock as well as provide mobile clinic. JDF also has proven capacity to provide support and monitoring of beneficiaries.
Group/village Saving and Loans Association. Jireh Doo Foundation has extensive past experience in implement the Village Savings and Loans Association across many communities in and outside of the north East. JDF implements the VSLA as a strategy for achieving sustainable livelihood and socio-economic resilience for vulnerable communities.
Distribution of farm inputs: JDF will distribute farm inputs as part of efforts to prepare the most vulnerable persons identified, to prepare them for the planting seasons , train and strengthen their capacities to produce and provide for themselves.

1 - Needs

In order to strengthen the livelihoods resilience of its beneficiaries, JDF is seeking to implement livelihoodeconomic development and financial inclusion support to affected/displaced persons in all the two local government areas of Borno state. JDF has the capacity to directly implement GSAL methodology and agribusiness to new and existing beneficiaries. Savings and Loans groups as well as agribusinesses and cooperatives are a sustainable way ofstrengthening the resilience of beneficiaries as they are autonomous self-managed by thecommunities. The savings group and agribusinesses are a good exit strategy to all thecommunities that have been assisted by JDF and other humanitarian actors and gives assurance of continuity of the initiated projects.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The proposed project will ensure Complaint Response Mechanisms (CRM) which will be established early in the life cycle of the project to ensure that all complaints on the project are captured and addressed. Jireh Doo Foundations approach to protection is governed by the SPHERE Core Standards and Protection Principles, the Core Humanitarian Standard and the Do No Harm Approach In line with a “do no harm” and conflict-sensitive approach, JDF will carefully engage all relevant stakeholders within the community. JDF will work to establish feedback mechanisms open to individuals with different communication needs and abilities (including illiterate and disabled beneficiaries). JDF will also follow up to ensure that the beneficiary feedback cycle contributes to programmatic and service decisions, particularly in case management. In contexts where there are extremists operating, work that is explicitly focused on gender or on women can invite unwanted scrutiny or interference. JDF agrees on appropriate language and protocols for communicating about the project and associated services within the communities, so as to safeguard project security and reputation.
