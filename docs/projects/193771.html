Project Summary

This project aims at providing tailored food security and livelihood assist vulnerable households engaged in farming, fishery, or animal breeding but do not have the financial capacity to acquire the needed production inputs and maintain their livelihoods. In addition, it aims to provide life-saving support to acutely vulnerable or shock-affected households with limited capacity to cover basic food needs in the Gaza Strip with a special focus on the access-restricted area (ARA). 390 vulnerable farmers, fishers, and herders, affected by the longstanding blockage, recurrent IHL/IHRL violations, market fluctuations, deteriorating economic situation, floods, and/or other types of natural and/or man-made shocks, will receive conditional livelihood support (i.e. cash assistance, rehabilitation through service delivery). It will allow them to maintain their production capacity and protect their livelihoods against potential shocks, while the most acutely vulnerable households of 200 daily-wage laborers, engaged in farming and suffering from limited access to food and income opportunities, will benefit from 50-day temporary job opportunities (CFW) to cover their immediate basic needs.
PUI will also seek to address the infrastructural challenges and implementing prevention measures for protecting agricultural livelihoods in the ARA, through the installation of solar photovoltaic (PV) systems for 2 agricultural water wells and the implementation of 40 water ponds serving at least 240 farmers. The Action will enable the vulnerable households of farmers, fishers, breeders, and daily-wage laborers to maintain their livelihood activities, enhance coping capacity to future shocks, and meet basic food needs. It is worth mentioning that the planned intervention was built on the priority needs identified through the different assessments carried out with the affected population as well as the agricultural priorities and focus matrices prepared by food security sector (FSS) in consultation with the ministry of agriculture (MoA). Furthermore, the action was fully coordinated with MoA, FSS, and the key actors working in the same intervention areas (i.e. ACTED, CARE, AAH, Oxfam, PARC, UAWC, NDC, SC, GVC, MC, PAAPD, ANERA, ESDC) to ensure responding to the real needs and avoid the overlapping of the intervention areas. It is also worth mentioning that extensive consultations were held with all the main stakeholders to discuss the different aspects of the proposed action, and ensure prioritization of the most urgent needs.
The project will contribute to the achievement of the following overarching country strategic objective (SO3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. Specifically, it will contribute to the following food security sector objectives: The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced (CO1) and Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs (CO2).
PUI will implement the proposed action with its two strategic partners, namely Palestinian Agricultural Relief Committees (PARC) and Union of Agricultural Work Committees (UAWC), to benefit from and build on the added-value and specific experience of each organization. The partnerships with PARC and UAWC have proven to be successful and effective in the joint projects implemented during the last 3 years. Therefore, PUI will rely on the strong and extensive experience of both organizations to ensure the quality of its intervention knowing that the distribution of roles and responsibilities among partners will be done based on the specialization as well as the experience in the intervention area.

1 - Needs

• Open-land (OL) and greenhouse (GH) farmers: According to HNO 2023, the recurrent hostilities further reduced the profitability and sustainability of agricultural activities for many farmers, driving them to continuous losses and risking their farming practices. Agricultural investments are expected to decrease, leaving most vulnerable farmers at risk of having to abandon their activity. The last hostilities in August 2022 hit tens of agricultural lands, GHs, livestock, and poultry farms, causing damages and losses estimated at $1M (MoA), with 900 dunums of OL farms and 600 dunums of GH in Khan Younis and Rafah needing rehabilitation. In August 22, PUI, through its local partner CBOs and UAWC, carried out FGDs with 105 small-scale vegetable OL/GH farmers (including 22 women) from North Gaza, Gaza, Khan Younis, and Rafah. Farming was reported as a sole source of income with an average monthly income ranging from 800 to 1,000 ILS for OL farmers, and from 800 to 2,000 for GH farmers. Challenges and gaps included limited financial capacity to invest in agriculture; limited access to irrigation water due to power cuts; high cost of and/or unavailability of some inputs; limited access to lands especially in the ARA; local market instability; frequent price fluctuations; export restrictions; debt accumulation (leading to risks of imprisonment). Priority needs identified were conditional cash/in-kind assistance for livelihoods restoration; rehabilitation of water ponds and wells; and solar energy for water wells/ponds. • Fishers: In June and October 22, PUI/UAWC conducted FGDs with 46 fishers from Gaza, Middle Area, and Rafah. The vast majority reported fishing as a sole source of income with a monthly income ranging from 800 to 1,500 ILS (71% below 1,000 ILS). Challenges and gaps included unavailability/high prices of fishing equipment, inputs, and/or spare parts; fluctuation of fish prices; reduced fish catch; high prices of fuel; debt accumulation; access restrictions; continuous IHL/IHRL violations; import restrictions. Priorities were in rehabilitation of boats through cash or in-kind assistance; distribution of fishing nets; cash/vouchers assistance for basic needs and debts repayment; and temporary job creation for daily-wage workers. 135 boats are still in need of urgent rehabilitation (MoA/DoF, Sep 2022). • Poultry/sheep breeders: HNO2023 needs analysis confirmed that the poultry production sector is very vulnerable to shocks, leading to price increases with negative consequences on food security. According to MoA (Sep22), 1,080 poultry farms and 1,750 sheep farms do not meet the standard specifications, are very vulnerable to harsh weather conditions and therefore are in need of critical rehabilitation interventions. Four FGDs were conducted with 49 poultry and sheep breeders (11 F; 38 M) from Rafah and Khan Younis in September 2022. In addition, FGDs were conducted by UAWC with 19 (6 F, 13 M) breeders from middle area in May 2022. Challenges identified included monopolization of market by big traders; high price of inputs, especially fodder; extreme weather conditions; high death rate due to inadequate farm structure and poor ventilation system; low quality of sheep breeds; low selling prices of milk; lack of gas for warming the poultry farms; acute power cuts; frequent prices fluctuations especially for chicken; high cost of vaccines, and high vulnerability to harsh weather conditions. Priority needs were in introduction of new varieties of sheep; rehabilitation of poultry/sheep farms; PV solar energy, and cash for livelihoods. The preferred assistance modality was cash assistance (57%); in-kind (34%); and vouchers (9%). • Daily-wage laborers in agriculture: According to the MSNA2022, 36.7% of Gazan HHs were relying on daily-wage labor as a main source of income; 60.3% reported a member of their household being unemployed and looking for work; 83.4% reporting having existing outstanding debt; 81% of HHs reported financial difficulties in meeting basic needs; 68% of the HHs employed crisis or emergency livelihood coping strategies. In addition, high unemployment rates among agricultural laborers and the very low wages were highlighted in some recent reports as well as during the discussions with farmers and local partners. 91.8% of HHs reported a preference for cash/voucher assistance. 29%, 19%, and 18% of HHs respectively reported lack of opportunities for women, lack of consent from husband/guardian, and unavailability/unaffordability of childcare as main obstacles to their female members finding job opportunities. According to MSNA2022 crosscutting report, 22% of female-headed HHs (compared to 13% of male-headed HHs) reported having spent more than 75% of their household expenditure on basic needs (including food). 60% of FHH reported NGO or charity assistance as their primary income source (compared to 50% of male-headed HH) and 39% reported community support as a primary income source (compared to 17% of male-headed households). This indicates the higher vulnerability of female-headed households. • Access to irrigation water: Limited access to irrigation water was raised during FGDs with farmers from various governorates. In addition, PV solar systems were proposed by farmers as a solution which would improve access to water and mitigate risks that farmers are exposed to. According to PARC’s assessment conducted in 2017 and updated in December 21, the total number of GH is of 8,779 and of water ponds (WP) is of 2,805. Subsequently, WP were established by PARC for 300 farmers but there is still a significant gap, as the water and electricity issues are acute, so solar systems for WP are still direly needed. According to MoA (Sep22), 40 water wells and 170 WP in Khan Younis and Rafah are in an urgent need of solar energy systems to ensure farmers’ regular access to water.

2 - Activities

PUI will collaborate with UAWC and PARC to implement the activities proposed in the framework of this project. PUI and its partners will seek to implement a “build back better” (BBB) approach, encourage the purchase of environment-friendly agricultural inputs, and promote the use of renewable (zero-emissions) sources of energy in all its proposed activities to enhance resilience and ensure the sustainable impact on the beneficiaries’ lives, livelihoods, and the surrounding environment. PUI and its partners will use two different methodologies to identify the individual beneficiaries for the activities: open registration for vulnerable caseload, and based on the official lists from MoA for shock-affected caseload. • Result 1: The livelihoods of farmers, fishers, and breeders are protected, restored, and enhanced through providing conditional support. • Activity 1.1: Rehabilitation of open-field farms and green houses for vulnerable and shock-affected farmers using build back better (BBB) approach (PUI+UAWC). 120 Open-field and greenhouse farmers (672 individuals; 331females, 341 males, 29 PwDs) will receive conditional cash assistance amounting to 3,000 ILS (910 USD) in two installments (30%, 70%) to relaunch, and/or enhance their livelihood activities. The amount of assistance covers the average cost (based on previous experience) of agricultural inputs to cultivate two dunums with vegetable crops (i.e. potato, tomato, onion, spinach, beans, peas, string beans, eggplant, etc.) or carry out critical repairs for one dunum of greenhouse. The rehabilitation of OL and GH farms will be done under the direct supervision of experienced agronomists and field monitors from PUI and its partner to ensure the quality implementation of works according to the global standards taking into account the sustainability, and climate change issues. Small-scale farmers (up to 5 dunums for open-field farms, and 2 dunums for greenhouses) from Khan Younis and Rafah governorates will be targeted based on a set of eligibility criteria prioritizing female-headed households (FHH). • Activity 1.2: Tailored livelihood support to vulnerable and shock-affected fishers (PUI+UAWC). 50 vulnerable and/or shock-affected fishers (280 individuals; 138 females, 142 males, 12 PwDs) will receive conditional cash assistance amounting to 3,000 ILS (910 USD) in two installments (30%, 70%) to carry out essential repair works and/or replace the affected/worn-out fishing tools and equipment including but not limited to boat engine maintenance, boat body repair, and fishing nets. Registered small-scale fishers with engine boats from Gaza and Deir Al Balah will be targeted based on a set of eligibility criteria prioritizing the most vulnerable households (HHs). • Activity 1.3: Rehabilitation of poultry and sheep farms (including provision of PV solar systems) (PUI+PARC). 220 vulnerable and/or shock-affected breeders (1,232 individuals; 608 females, 624 males, 53 PwDs)will benefit from the critical rehabilitation works and/or provision of PV solar systems to poultry (120) and sheep farms (100). The average cost of rehabilitation works (including PV) is 2,500 USD (8,250 ILS) for poultry farms and 3,500 USD (11,550 ILS) for sheep farms knowing that the rehabilitation works will be implemented by a contractor under the direct supervision of PUI and its partner. Registered small and medium-scale breeders (up to 2,000 birds for poultry, 20 heads for sheep) from Khan Younis and Rafah governorate will be targeted based on a set of vulnerability criteria prioritizing FHH, HHs with PwDs and large HHs. • Result 2: The living conditions of the daily laborers in agriculture sector are improved through creating temporary job opportunities and improving access to income. • Activity 2.1: Creating temporary job opportunities for daily-wage laborers in the agriculture sector (PUI+UAWC). 100 female and 100 male unskilled agricultural laborers from Khan Younis and Rafah (1,120 individuals, 552 females, 568 males, 47 PwDs) will benefit from the creation of temporary job opportunities in the agriculture. Each BNF will work for 50 days divided into two rounds with a daily wage of 60 ILS (19 USD). The agricultural workers will support small-scale farmers in planning crops/trees, removing harmful weeds, and the harvest of both vegetable and olive crops. An open registration will be launched to identify the beneficiaries based on a set of vulnerability criteria prioritizing FHH, HHs with PWDs, HHs with chronically-ill persons, and large households. • Result 3: Safe and continuous access to irrigation water is ensured through implementing critical repairs and provision of solar systems for agricultural water facilities. • Activity 3.1: Rehabilitation of agricultural water wells and ponds (including provision of PV solar system) (PUI+PARC). PUI will carry out the critical maintenance works and provide solar systems to two large-scale agricultural water wells (30 HP, PV: 30 KW/well) and 40 individual water ponds (3HP, PV: 3 KW/pond) serving at least 240 farmers’ households (1,344 individuals, 662 females, 682 males, 58 PwDs) from Rafah and Khan Younis. The cost of essential maintenance works and PV systems (30,000 USD for wells, 4,000 USD for ponds) was estimated based on the actual bill of quantities (BoQs), current market prices, as well as the previous experience of partners in this field where they provided PV systems to a number of water wells and ponds during the last years. The activity will be coordinated with all actors implementing similar interventions. Priority will be given to the wells and ponds which were affected by different shocks during the past years as well as those serving the highest number of small-scale farmers with a specific focus on ARA farmers.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

• Service delivery: Based on the experience of PUI and its partner (PARC) during the last years, contractor-driven approach is the most appropriate option for implementing the rehabilitation works for the poultry and sheep farms as it allows for more control on the quality of the implemented works. Besides, the use of this approach enables the partner to ensure that the farms are rehabilitated in accordance with the principles of build back better (BBB) approach as well as the specific technical specifications of model farms. • Cash: Cash modality is recommended by the affected population and the humanitarian actors as long as the markets in the affected areas are functioning, the financial service providers are operating with an adequate capacity, and goods are available. According to MSNA (July, 2022), 91.8% of households reported a preference for cash and voucher assistance in any modality (physical cash, mobile money, bank transfer, prepaid card, food and non-food vouchers). In Gaza Strip, goods are available in the local market, but the affected population has lost the means to buy them. This is why cash transfers can be safely provided to the acutely vulnerable households who can choose how to use the money to cover their priority needs in a dignified manner. Evidence showed that cash transfers, in GS and other contexts, are often more efficient and effective than other forms of aid. The cash transfers modality provides affected people with the means and flexibility to decide and priorities their life-saving and recovery needs. Moreover, it also supports the local markets, contribute to boosting local economy, and lays the foundations for communities’ recovery and resilience. Cash transfers are effective in addressing negative coping strategies such as poor diets and debts.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s complaints and feedback mechanisms is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people, ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop,” progress is being made. It provides ‘unique and invaluable sources of information to be used for better project management and outcomes. It can help identify and address mistakes, shortcomings, improving the quality of the intervention or project. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. • Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews (KIIs), surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include protection mainstreaming considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • SEAH is a GBV issue that might negatively impact all aspects of protection mainstreaming and have lasting consequences in the lives of survivors. To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support (PSS) services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed (i.e. the Protection Cluster Service Directory) and complemented by other resources identified by PUI if needed. Furthermore, all PUI field staff have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEA and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partners, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, the Action aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle. The Action’s participatory assessment is specifically designed to involve vulnerable groups traditionally excluded from decision making. For each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI and its partners will analyze possible barriers and enablers in cooperation with the community, CBOs and beneficiaries with a specific focus on age, gender and diversity. Furthermore, the Action will pay particular attention to identifying beneficiaries most at need by attributing special attention to inclusiveness of vulnerable groups and people with specific needs. The planned action will target women as well as men with and without disabilities as income providers. For various activities, FHH will be prioritized. In the same way, households headed by or counting with PwDs, persons with chronic illnesses and older persons will be targeted in priority. Obstacles to as an example female headed households’ economic participation will be tackled, through engaging actively with the involved beneficiaries on challenges and possible solutions. Here, as it was identified in the needs assessment that women face specific barriers in access to work opportunities (due to lack of consent from husband/caretaker, to difficulty in accessing childcare services, or various other socio-cultural factors), the accent will be put on engaging with women and men in the targeted communities to encourage the participation of women, notably in Cash-for-Work activities, as well as in others. As such, awareness-raising sessions, either door-to-door or in groups may be organized, specific communication material designed (leaflets, posters, etc.). This topic will be brought up regularly in orientation sessions, during the selection process, during field visits, etc. For FHH, specific considerations will be made to ensure that they can take part in the activities without constraints or barriers (please see above in section 10.1). PUI and partners are also working to ensure that programs and services are available and accessible to PwD. Therefore, they work on a non-discriminatory approach, where a person with a disability is able to exercise their protected rights on an equal basis with other members of the community. PUI is also committed to protect Rights of Persons with disabilities and inclusion principles and practices of the most vulnerable. PUI ensures that persons with disabilities and their families are involved and consulted at all stages of the project cycle. For instance, during the needs assessment phase, PUI includes persons who identifies with one or several of the Washington Short Set of Questions to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities. As a result, PUI and partners will identify and work to remove any barriers that may prevent persons with disabilities from effectively and safely using services and participating in projects as well as build on identified enablers. During the planning phase, PUI ensured that the disability-related findings and recommendations from the assessment stage have been carried through into the project design. PUI then, make adaptations when necessary to ensure accessibility and inclusiveness of project services. Further, during the implementation phase, service delivery mechanisms can be adapted to improve access for persons with disabilities. The protection and gender analysis will be updated regularly to reflect new emerging needs associated with any enforced changes in the project delivery modalities. These enforced changes in modality will also be part of regular consultations with the communities to identify and assess how they might contribute to the access in humanitarian service provision of different groups of people.
