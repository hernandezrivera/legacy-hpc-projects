Project Summary

The project aims to provide secondary health care and surgical services in Idleb governorate, in the districts which received a huge number of IDPs, the project will improve access to secondary health care services for people with traumatic injuries and medical conditions demand surgical interventions. As well as securing access to dialysis sessions in Kafartakharim subdistrict ( which serve about 45 cases monthly in addition to 1 or 2 new cases monthly), and establishing Burn Unit in Idleb City linked to Idleb Surgical Hospital in Idleb city.
Idleb surgical hospital provides general, urology, ENT, maxillofacial, plastic and orthopaedic surgeries, in addition to emergency, admission, laboratory, pharmacy, blood bank and radiology services, and contains 5 operation rooms. The hospital is located in Idleb district and covers many surrounding towns, such as Maaret-Masrien, which is a highly crowded area and contain many camps with a high percentage of IDPs.
Waseem Husaino hospital provides general, urology and orthopaedic surgeries, in addition to emergency, admission, laboratory and radiology services. The hospital contains 6 surgical wards and located in Harim district - Kafartakharim and covers many surrounding towns, such as Armanaz and Salqin, which is a highly crowded area and covers many camps, villages, and small towns with a high percentage of IDPs. SEMA will continue support Jisr Al Shughur Hospital (the only health facility in Jisr AL Shugur) as long as the needs exist in the area, the hospital is providing emergency surgical intervention and Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care services. Complicated cases are being referred through the central ambulance system to the close specialized hospital.

4 - Needs

The proposed facilities are all considered as critical facilities in Northern Syria, as the medical situation in Syria is turning into a humanitarian disaster; it is continually deteriorating at a rapid rate. Hospitals, field hospitals and medical clinics, doctors and medical personnel have become regular targets in Syria, Most doctors have fled the region out of fear to be killed or kidnapped. There is an increase in attacks on health facilities since the middle of 2019, where about 47 hospitals and health centers in Idleb governorate and north of Hama went out of service.Based on above mentioned facts, SEMA constantly proposes to keep running trauma facilities in the mentioned locations in order to provide essential secondary health care as well as surgical and emergency services for the most vulnerable people affected by conflict. Furthermore it aims to reduce morbidity among the beneficiaries, and advocates for the establishment of a strong health care system, which is severely necessary after 6 years of conflict. This can be proven through the effective coordination and collaboration between SEMA and all the stakeholders (health directorates, local councils, other actors and partners).On the other hand, the gaps in funding health facilities could cause an interruption in the necessary medical services for the beneficiaries, thereby diminishing the impact of projects carried out by humanitarian organizations over the past years, and weaken the potential for health sector governance, which is still considered to be under the emergency projects due to non-stop conflict and lack of stability in the region. In Idleb city the static indicates to be 900,000 people in the city (250,000 as a host & 650,000 as an IDPs) and In Kafar Takarim city the static indicates to be 65000 people in the city (40,000 as a hosts & 25,000 as an IDPs) in addition to displacement about 700 families at the beginning of 2020.

5 - Implementation of activities

SEMA will support the hospitals with the needed running costs and with staff salaries, in addition to providing the needed technical support and tools. Furthermore, the provided activities will be as following:Outpatient clinics: including orthopaedic, neurosurgical, ENT, ophthalmology, urology, vascular, plastic surgery, maxillofacial, and general surgical clinics. Inpatient Department: Where major and minor surgeries are implemented, in addition to the hospitalization of patients.In addition to ICU and ER departments services were all trauma cases are received and provided with emergency services, then referred to the needed speciality. Laboratory and Blood Bank: The hospitals contain a well-equipped lab to perform the needed tests in the hospital, in addition to a blood bank that collects blood donations to be used inside the facility. X-Ray Department: The hospitals contain a well-equipped x-ray department with a variety of different services including Mammography.Please check HeRAMS code for each facility:Idlib Surgical Hospital: G0700032Waseem Hsino Hospital: G0703021Jisr Ash Shughur Hospital: G0704013SEMA MEAL team will conduct different activities to monitor the work progress in each facility including the following tools: Monitoring visits-Bimonthly. Facility assessment-Quarterly. FGDs-Satisfaction surveys-indicator tracker. Furthermore, SEMA team applies SAFE mechanism in order to improve accountability. The system facilitates receiving complaints and suggestions through a hotline, WhatsApp, email, and complaints box. SEMA coordinates with active actors on the field and on the cluster level including the health directorates, the local councils, and the active organizations. Specifically to this project, it will be supported through different donors including, IRC, WV, WHO, SCHF, and SEMA private fund.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In traditional communities, social mores expect women to maintain modesty and chastity, and anything perceived to threaten these values is regarded as a threat. This has resulted in a gender gap that has been exacerbated by the conflict. Women and girls now face different layers of vulnerabilities ranging from the heightened risk of physical, sexual, and gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, early marriage, restricted mobility that limits access to services, and other forms of negative expressions of masculinity resulting from displacement and insecurity. SEMA, in general, implements its activities by upholding individual rights in accordance with the humanitarian principles. In that regard SEMA implements all of its activities, taking into consideration doing no harm to the target communities, and providing all services and activities to beneficiaries regardless of their gender, religion, sect, ethnic, political or regional affiliation. In addition, SEMA works to improve gender equality in terms of recruitment, considers protection mainstreaming in all of its projects, provides PSEA training for all staff, provides self-care sessions for staff in order to help them feel more comfortable when dealing with beneficiaries, and introduces staff on how to refer CP and GBV cases to active protection programs whether implemented by SEMA or other active organizations. Also, the staff capacity was improved in regard to providing integrated health and protection programming; by conducting various training for the staff on how to provide clinical care for GBV survivors and on how to detect and refer potential cases, as well as, protection mainstreaming, CMR, and SOPs for GBV case management. Also, in all the three facilities SEMA always works on improving access to people with disabilities, and the most vulnerable groups. In addition, to upholding gender equality and humanitarian principles.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: • The local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it. (likelihood: weak, the impact: mid) Mitigation: • Intensive advocacy, negotiation, and coordination with the local council, stakeholders, and main actors will be launched and followed up. Risk 2: • The ever-changing security situation may badly affect the response, and resulting in mass influx of new IDPs including children and children with disabilities. (likelihood: weak, the impact: high) Mitigation: • Safety and security measures and emergency plans will be set and considered before and during the implementation period. Project activities will be designed flexibly to be modified appropriately with the changeable security situation. Implementation sites could be located to safer places as needed in coordination with OCHA. Risk 3: • The one-off emergency case funds to enable immediate response when needed may be used by the caregiver for another irrelevant purpose. (likelihood: weak, the impact: high) Mitigation: • The project team will draw attention to this issue while conducting the project activities.
