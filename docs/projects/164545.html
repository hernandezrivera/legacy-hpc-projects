Project Summary

Prior to the conflict, the water and sanitation resources in Syria were under pressure due to population growth and urbanization. Syrian water resources faced significant managerial challenges and had been prioritized by the Syrian government for modernization. Water utilities were heavily subsidized and as of 2006 were only covering 55% of their operational costs and were unable to gain funds for investment. The sector was further challenged by its complex management structure with different Ministries and sectors having overlapping responsibilities and functions. The protracted conflict has significantly worsened the situation. Unreliable electricity, damaged water infrastructure, the influx of IDPs, threats from militant groups inside Idleb, and households with few financial resources are all challenges facing the water sector.
NGO X has been supporting the operation of four Water Units serving the conflict-affected population of Idleb Governorate since 2014. An estimated 676,225 individuals rely on the water pumped by 50 water station within the four units for their households’ drinking and hygiene needs. The programme will continue to provide inputs and technical expertise to allow for the continuation of services while strengthening the financial and management capacity of local stakeholders to manage the utility and continue on a path towards sustainability. In addition, NGO X will provide emergency assistance to recently displaced households to allow them to meet their basic hygiene needs. This activity is funded until 31st March 2020 and NGO X is working with the donor to secure new funds for the coming year.
In June 2019, NGO X procured emergency funds to support an additional 14 water stations that provide water to approximately 141,174 individuals in Idleb governorate. As a result, NGO X is currently supporting 64 water stations by providing water to approximately 817,399 individuals in Idleb governorate. This activity is funded until January 2020 and NGO X is working to secure new funds to continue to support 14 water stations until June 2020.
NGO X is planning to replace two generators at the two pumping stations that serve Idleb City (estimated population 175,000).
The desired outcome of this project is that community water systems are efficiently and effectively managed and the community is informed, invested and supportive of local institutions and has sustained access to safe drinking water and hygienic conditions.
Key activities include:
• Providing inputs (fuel, oil, HTH, salaries) to enable the stations to pump water for over 107 villages.
• Intense organisational capacity building to ensure water units and stations have the financial, administrative and management skills necessary to manage the stations in a transparent manner that prioritises accountability to customers, the community and donors.
• Stations develop realistic and achievable cost recovery targets based on financial analysis and communicate the approach to communities.
• Continue using the modality of ‘Infrastructure Stability’ fund that water stations can apply to for upgrading or investing in their stations and water networks. Maintains resources to enable emergency repairs conducted by NGO X.
• Supporting recent arrivals into Idleb with hygiene kits and hygiene promotion.
NGO X is also planning to support one camp in northern Aleppo with a comprehensive WASH response which will include water supply, solid waste management, sanitation and hygiene promotion for 3,500 individuals.
NGO X has secured 34% of the funding required for this project and is awaiting donor approval of the remaining sum.

4 - Needs

The WASH HNO 2020 assessment showed an estimated 10.7 million people in Syria who require WASH assistance, 3 million of whom are considered in acute need. Idleb and Aleppo host the second and third highest numbers of displaced populations (IDPs and returnees) representing between 40 and 50% of the total population at governorate level. This presents an enormous challenge in terms of providing a WASH response that mitigates the impact of displacement and the burden placed on host communities. 42 per cent of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, (2019 WASH Household Survey (countrywide)) at least 80 per cent of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems aren’t functional, (Ministry of Water Resources, 2018)26 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities (2019 WASH Household Survey (countrywide)). WASH services in IDP sites require significant and continuous investments. Interviewees in IDP sites often reported water insufficiency (37 per cent) or having barely enough water (22 per cent) (Ministry of Water Resources 2018). 31 per cent of assessed households’ drinking water samples didn’t show any traces of water treatment at the time of the assessment. In September 2019, NGO X contracted a third-party monitoring firm, CARFAX, to conduct a survey on its water provision programme. Two villages were randomly selected from a list of communities supported by NGOX’s water programme. A total of 328 community surveys, 23 focus group discussion and two local council interviews were conducted., A significant minority, 43% said that water from the network was insufficient to meet their household needs and 31% indicated the water tanker is one of their main sources of water or that they rely on water from water trucks. This indicates that water from the water network is insufficient to supply community needs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Continued access to targeted areas, supported by continued adherence to NGO X's acceptance strategy and the joint operating procedures (JOPs), while security conditions remain sufficiently stable- Sufficient/ timely access to inputs through procurement, reliable supply and available transportation, and border crossing remains open- Strong coordination and collaboration with local authorities and humanitarian actors in the AoO prevails- NGO X's strategies and mechanisms to prevent armed and sanctioned actors from interference in programmes that violate the JOPs remain effective- NGO X can continue to support cross border activities.Risks:- Remote management presents the risk of international and Syrian staff losing legal status; sudden border closures disrupting operations; other regulatory changes which could affect the way NGO X operates cross-border- There is a risk of fraud including banking, cash management, procurement, transport, and programming- Tools/repair supplies not available, thus repairs not able to be completed on time - Fuel theft or misuse at water stations- Aid diversion - NGO X loses access to some areas in Idleb or Aleppo governorate as a result of shifting political and military control on the ground. Mitigation:- Safety and Security Evacuation Plans (SSEP) and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) in place, and an experienced security team- Ongoing communication with actors operational across the operational area, and continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of NGO X’s programming- NGO X has a strong reconciliation system that is reviewed by Senior management on a monthly basis. Any suspected cases are reported by the WASH team directly to area management team and to Senior Management Team - NGO X works with suppliers to ensure that aid is not diverted - Active engagement in Cluster and completion of 4ws, work with Councils on local communications
