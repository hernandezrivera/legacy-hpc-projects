Project Summary

The targeted sub-districts in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates are on a severity scale of 3-5. An estimated 2% of targeted beneficiaries may have a disability. Total funds received in 2021: around 2.6m USD. The number of beneficiaries reached in 2021 is over 63.000 individuals. The project is partially funded at this stage. Considering the needs and in line with the No Lost Generation Initiative, context analysis, and demographic trends, the intervention aims at re-establishing basic education services in the targeted communities, which are return, underserved, and newly accessible areas. The Action aims at increasing access to safe, inclusive, and quality education for children and adolescents, relying on WW-GVC expertise in NFE (Non-Formal Education), solid reputation, and knowledge of the areas. The total number of expected beneficiaries is 56.903 individuals for 2022 and a further 27.161 beneficiaries for 2023.
Over the course of the 2 years project, WW-GVC will implement the following activities. Firstly, in order to increase availability and access to education:
-Rehabilitation of at least 30 schools, 18 ECD rooms, benefitting at least 11000 students; When rehabilitation is not feasible, the project will consider implementation of pre-fabbricated classrooms or the use of other public spaces for educational purposes;
-Rehabilitation of 50 WASH in-school facilities to ensure access to safe water and sanitation to at least 13.000 students;
-Back To Learning (BTL) campaigns targeting 30,000 individuals, including door-to-door visits to identify out of school children (OOSC) and the main barriers to education access; interactive theatre events with OOSC; Focus Group Discussion with community members on the importance of inclusive education; distribution of awareness materials;
-Self Learning Program for 6.750 OOSC; Remedial Classes for 7.000 students (RCs); both including the distribution of school materials;
-Vocational trainings and distribution of start-up kits, benefitting 700 students, including the production of materials conducive to a safer school environment, such as reusable PPE, desks, chairs, other school furniture, training on solar systems and the implementation of solar panels; furthermore, vocational trainings will prioritize households with OoSC in order to increase income and favour re-enrolment;
-Distribution of furniture, small repairs to existing structures, school supplies (including the distribution of vouchers), School in a Box kits, and schools bags, benefitting at least 25.000 students;
In parallel, to improve the quality of education, WW--GVC will:
-Train 1600 teachers in Active Learning (AL), PSS, Hygiene Promotion (HP) and Risk Education (RE);
-Deliver school awareness campaigns in HP, RE, and inclusive school model for 42.500 students;
-Distribute recreational materials benefitting at least 5.000 students;
-Distribute incentives for teachers to support the transportation costs, benefitting at least 550 teachers working in rural areas;
-Carry out life-skills development activities throughout SLP and RCs programs, supporting the wellbeing of 7,000 OOSC and students at risk of drop out.
Finally, to improve the capacity of the education system of the targeted areas, WW-GVC will:
-Provide training to DoE staff on data management and IT, targeting at least 100 DoE staff,
-Implement PTA at community level, targeting at least 15 communities.

4 - Needs

Intervention sub-districts are selected based on the Sector’s HNO, OCHA severity scale, assessments conducted by WW-GVC in autumn 2021 including infrastructure analysis and an evaluation of completed projects, and secondary data (e.g. DoE, other INGOs, MoE, etc). Main problems for the incoming two-year period include: lack of safe learning spaces, high rate of non attendance (26% in Aleppo and 25% in Deir-Ez-Zor), significant decrease in attendance for children older than 12yo, high percentage of children lamenting unclean and unsafe WASH facilities (88% at national level), child labour, presence of mines/IEDs, overcrowded classrooms (62 children per class in Aleppo and 52 in DEZ). During the most recent BTL campaigns, WWGVC identified more than 1500 OoSC in rural Deir-Ez-Zor and more than 2000 in Aleppo. The main reason for the lack of school attendance is economic, with 33% of families citing that they cannot afford to send children to school and 28% that their children are working. Further assessments identified a high rate of drop-out among CwD, both due to social stigma and physical barriers. Discussions and KIIs with education staff highlighted the lack of materials, lack of trainings, and difficulties in covering the cost of transportation, especially when working in rural areas, leading to understaffed schools in the most vulnerable communities. Finally, lack of facilities and materials for secondary and TVET education is leading to drop-outs and loss of livelihoods.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is preliminarily discussed and approved by the Ministry of Education (MoE), carried out in cooperation with the Directorates of Education (DoEs) and Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC), and constantly monitored by WW-GVC in each governorate thanks to WW-GVC presence in the field with local offices in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. The MEAL Department in Damascus manages monitoring and follow-up tools to ensure supervision and evaluation of activities’ impact, informing about possible ongoing adjustments of modalities and contents. In particular, the MEAL team is composed of a MEAL Manager and a MEAL Officer based in Damascus, and two MEAL Officers based in Aleppo and Deir-Er-Zor respectively. Rehabilitation works are supervised by WW-GVC Engineers and assistant Engineers, ensuring that selected companies deliver quality and timely works; possibly, rehabilitations will be conducted out of school time to avoid affecting the regular and safe implementation of classes. NFE activities will be conducted by trained teachers, using contents/time schedules and modalities agreed at national level (by MoE and Education Sector). School awareness campaigns will rely on standard contents made available by national and international duty-bearers (e.g. DoH/SARC/UNICEF/UNMAS). For the distribution of incentives, close cooperation with DoE and monitoring of the beneficiaries is key. Across all the Action, WW-GVC will collect data of beneficiaries in databases for analysis and monitoring.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including: • placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations • reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor • planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children • avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children’s well-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include: • AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff is introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy. • TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to this Policy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events. • RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization’s operations. • SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.
