Project Summary

In 2022-2023, COOPI aims to respond to the main protection concerns identified throughout protection program implementation and need assessment with local communities, IDPs and returnees. The response will be focused on 4 strategic Objectives: 1. Improve protection of population affected by the crisis through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions and through advocacy with duty bearers; 2. Improve access to quality and life-saving GBV response services for GBV survivors and women and girls at risk and put measures in place to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV; 3. Minimize the impact of explosive ordnance for communities most at risk; 4. Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the CPMS in Humanitarian Action. COOPI response will be based on a community-based and participatory approach involving the affected population in actively participating to the decision-making process, focusing on the establishment of community committees, that, with the guidance of COOPI staff, will work on the identification of protection risks within their communities to address them appropriately. To reach an equal access for boys and girls (0-17) to quality child protection interventions, COOPI will support community-based child protection responses for girls and boys in targeted locations, including provision of MHPSS services for girls, boys and caregivers, parenting programmes, awareness-raising and establishment of groups/committees to ensure the community’s active participation to prevent and respond to child protection issues. The combination of these activities, aims to develop a long-term positive practice through the targeted populations with effects on the whole community. Psychosocial assistance will be also provided in order to cope with stress relief/PTSD. For women and children will be provided quality and life-saving response services, including case management, and COOPI will contribute to reinforce referral pathways. CVA modality will be considered to meet specific protection needs, and to mitigate risks related to GBV, child labor, child marriage, and disability. CLIs will be also promoted and implemented as more efficient tool for protection risk’s prevention and response. Our team will work through mobile team interventions to guarantee assistance also to the most vulnerable populations in rural, underserved areas. For example, therapists will reach out for home-based rehabilitation and distribution of medical in-kind assistance to the most vulnerable PwDs and survivors of landmine and ERW, to provide physical rehabilitation through COOPI specialized therapists, to reduce the physical and psychological implications of EO traumas and overcoming impairments, economic loss, social marginalization, limiting the enjoyment of their basic human rights. Mental therapists will provide individual counselling and to provide a lifesaving support. In addition, COOPI response will have a focus on risk education, due to the presence of EO that our team has assessed in several areas targeted. Awareness and campaigns will be conducted in the communities to reach all beneficiaries’ age and sex groups and to allow them to reach economic, social and health development, mitigating the likelihood of exposure to accidents leading to permanent injuries or loss of life. Intervening in multi- sectoral response, COOPI will ensure protection mainstreaming across all sectors, with particular attention to safe distribution modalities, gender, age and diversity approach and focusing on highly vulnerable groups in the definition of eligibility criteria and in targeting the assistance. Moreover, the SEA will constitute a cross-sectoral priority to be addressed and eradicate, while ensuring COOPI staff and third parties to be trained, to recognize and to report such violations, to do no harm and to protect people affected by the crisis.

4 - Needs

In terms of severity of needs, of the 23 sub-districts targeted, Atareb and Maadan are ranked 5, Zerbeh, Maliha, Darayya, Nawa and Jaramana are ranked 4, further 15 sub-districts are ranked 3 and Abul Thohur is ranked 2. Through previous field assessments in the targeted locations and ongoing/past protection programs implemented, specific needs have been detected in the areas targeted, reflecting also the findings of HNO 2022.Particular protection risks such as child marriage, child labor, exposure to explosive ordnance and movement restriction imposed by spousal, family, community or due to security threat and lack/loss of civil documentation have been identified. In the last 2 years it was observed an increased number of vulnerable women-headed HHs, whose capacity to meet needs and enjoy basic rights is hampered by socio/cultural norms, exposing them to further GBV risks or heavily relying on humanitarian assistance. Moreover, survivors of GBV encounter numerous barriers to seek protection, from lack of services available such as safe houses, to cultural reasons, and lack of information about their rights. Presence of EO and movement restrictions further limit the chances of community recovery. Moreover, it was identified that the population struggle to access quality care, especially for PWDs and/or chronic diseases in rural areas and protection services responding to widespread neglection of elderly and PwDs, discrimination and social stigma.

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the needs identified above, the most vulnerable are identified according to internally established SoPs on beneficiaries targeting and selection criteria, that has been adapted to the constraints experienced in the Syrian context. Activities are carried through mobile units to provide protection services in hard-to- reach areas and/or through static facilities and include all CP, PSS, GBV programming package. Protection support is provided firstly through individual assistance (CP case management, PSS individual and group counseling) while COOPI will continue advocating for the implementation of GBV case management, currently extremely challenging in Syria. Community mobilization and sensitization to protection risks prevention and response is ensured by CP, GBV and PSS awareness sessions, EORE, as well as by the establishment of community-based protection structures (committees) and the implementation of community-led initiatives. These activities are in place to meet urgent needs and to increase capacity building (through the communities for the communities) adopting a culturally and socially appropriate approach to generate ownership, participation, sustainability, using local capacities.Structured assistance is also delivered through parenting and life-skills programs, and COOPI-specific physicals, speech and hearing therapy services targeting PwDs, and Victim Assistance, complemented by material assistance in-kind (i.e., MIKA, dignity kits) or vouchers.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance. LL: Medium. Impact: High. MM: Establish formal and informal complaint and feedback mechanisms in each area of intervention; Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA, according to COOPI Global PSEA policy in place; Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services; SoPs are in place for submission and receipt of complaints, reporting, investigation and victim assistance. PSEA Focal Points have been nominated and trained in COOPI sub-offices, Syria CO and HQ and all staff members receive training and information on preventing and responding to acts of SEA. When intervening in new areas, COOPI nominates and trains a dedicated PSEA FPs in charge of supervising the use/dissemination of PSEA reporting tools among communities, staff and third parties. COOPI sensitizes the beneficiaries, requiring them to report any SEA cases in written (COOPI reporting tool) or at presence of the PSEA FP, that guarantees anonymity and confidentiality. At the HQ level, it is available a dedicated e-mail address: help@coopi.org. Monitoring actions are envisaged to check the process of reporting misconduct through a register. To maximizing risk reduction, all agreements with partners, suppliers and staff contracts make specific reference to COOPI Code of Ethics, which violation constitutes reason for terminating the partnership/collaboration.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R: Direct access to the target areas and beneficiaries is limited, suspended or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. A: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas/COVID spread. MM: Maintain constant communication with agencies, GoS and in-country security monitoring networks. All staff receive security and remote management training. R: Fraud/identity theft, voucher sold or exchanged for unauthorized items. A: misuse of vouchers causes negative impact on protection outcomes. MM: vendors’ selection involves community stakeholders. Random quality controls of suppliers. Add unique serial numbers and laser tags. Crosscheck of voucher. PDM including random monitoring at HHs’ level the quantity and quality of the goods.R: Explosive incidents. A: Lack of knowledge about risks related to EO and adoption of unsafe behavior. MM: Contribute to service mapping and to incident reporting; Utilize sector agreed key messages and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging is required. R: delay in project approvals/access, challenges in technical capacity, data protection issues and lack of AAP. A: Extensive involvement of national stakeholder in the project implementation. MM: Advocate to maintain the direct implementation of specific activities as case management to ensure the respect of minimum standards. Advocate with donors for the inclusion of specific requirements in implementing and monitoring specialized services; set up feedback and complaints mechanisms.
