Project Summary

This is 12 months Project which seek to provide access to quality and lifesaving basic education for internally displaced persons (IDPs), Returnees and host community children and youth affected by conflict and crisis. The project will be implemented by Touch Africa development organization (TADO) in Twic and Tonj East County of Warrap state, that will utilize the static presence of TADO in Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Twic, Tonj East and Tonj South counties in Warrap state for the ongoing education in emergency EiE interventions Funded by UNICEF and health through funding from SSHF.
Access to education has been hindered by inter Communal Conflicts in settlement areas, food insecurity, economic crisis, COVID – 19 pandemic and flooding which took place last year causing displacement of population, loss of livelihood, damaged most of the education facilities in Warrap state as a result of heavy rains. Floods caused by torrential rains worsened the living conditions of people in the Twic and Tonj East of Warrap states in the past months. The heavy rains destroyed shelters, crops, water sources, public infrastructure like schools and health facilities which increased the protection risk of water-borne and other diseases among communities, and rendered many roads impassable, constraining access to affected areas including schools.
This intervention seeks to improve learning condition and address protection concerns of the affected IDPs, spontaneous Returnees coming back to south Sudan from the neighboring countries such as Ethiopia ,Uganda and Sudan as a result of signing of the revitalized peace agreement in 2018 that led to the formation of the new government of national unity that has given hope for peace and host community children in Warrap states. This will be an integrated Education intervention that will create synergies with the health, GBV and WASH clusters through Health, GBV and WASH implementing partners such as World vision ,PCO, ADRA ,NRC and UNKEA with in Twic and Tonj East counties of Warrap state
This intervention intends to target 8 primary schools in Twic and Tonj East county of Warrap state to provide access to quality emergency learning to the affected IDPs, Returnees and host community children.
TADO intends to target 3430 (2262 Boys 1000 Girls 103 Men and 48 Women) direct beneficiaries comprising of vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities’ children and youth with disabilities who have limited access to education through the provision of the following services:
Enrolment of learners and out of school children
Establishment of temporary learning spaces (TLS) and classrooms
Rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms
Establishment of gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, Rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion, Procure and supply Sanitation and Hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to students, Procure and supply sanitary and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to teachers, Provision of quality education and recreational materials to the conflict and crisis affected children , youth and teachers ,
Training of teachers in EiE pedagogical skills and Child-centered methodologies to improve teaching -learning process for emergency-affected children, Train and update teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms
Training of SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management / governance and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
Printing of communicable diseases including and distribution of appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e., posters, leaflets) of communicable diseases including COVID 19 prevention and mitigation measures The project will use an integrated approach to address the issue of GBV and problem faced by the CWDs in using WASH facilities .

1 - Needs

According to the South Sudan humanitarian overview (HNO) Report 2022, an estimated 3.3 million girls and boys between ages 3 and 17 in conflict and crisis affected areas are estimated to have inadequate or no access to education in 2022, while more than 98,000 teachers and SMC members are in need of humanitarian assistance. Increasing climate change, food insecurity, and localized conflict exacerbate existing barriers, including inadequate school infrastructure, costs associated with accessing quality education, untrained education personnel and irregular and insufficient teacher payments. Needs are particularly dire and may worsen in Warrap state (ENA) report 2021.Most learners do not get a meal before school and over 60% of ENA assessed schools do not provide food for learners. Hunger is a key barrier to education, resulting in poor academic performance , inconsistent attendance and eventual dropout. In South Sudan, 7.2 million children and adults are severely food insecure and education settings can be one of the only places where children have access to food, making it a key intervention opportunity. One in three schools in the ENA reported having no access to a functioning safe water source and less than half of schools have clean water and soap available, this increases the risk of illness resulting in irregular attendance of learners and teachers. A lack of Menstrual Hygiene Management is also a significant barrier for female learner retention and progression. Over 70% of schools reported having a washroom for girls and half said they had never received dignity kits for female learners.Over 80% of Education Needs assessment (ENA) conducted assessed schools reported a rise in child protection cases during COVID-19 school closures and the number of pregnant learners nearly doubled between 2020 and 2021 in some schools. Schools can be key institutions to identify and refer children requiring protective services and case management. Over a third of schools assessed said they had no referral mechanisms in place, and less than a quarter of schools reported having a general protection referral mechanism.According to the IRNA Report October 22nd 2021, Due to heavy rains and over flow of river banks, Twic County experienced extensive flooding situation that affected all 6 Payams. This caused population displacements and affected livelihood in 2021 more than it had been recorded in the last 13 years. The key findings of the IRNA were that 61 schools had been affected by floods ,8573(4235 Boys and 4338 Girls) school children were displaced /affected by the flood, Low enrollment of learners in schools ,most of the schools collapsed due to heavy rainfalls and flood, most of the schools are under the trees ,some of the schools are being used as shelter by IDPs ,WASH facilities in some schools have collapsed due to the water logging crated by the flood .Since January 2021 No IRNA has been conducted by the education partners in Tonj east due to the persistent inter communal conflict and flooding which has made the county in accessible to conduct IRNA in the county in 2021 .However flood report summited by UNOCHA reported large number of people displaced by conflict and flood in Tonj East . Some schools had been occupied by host community who had been displaced by the flooding interrupting learning. The affected schools needed rehabilitation as structures were destroyed by the flood. ,Segregated WASH facilities were also lacking in the schools and learning and teaching materials were soaked in flood waters.Since July 2021 inter communal conflict and Flooding has significantly impacted on education infrastructures like classrooms and WASH facilities in schools particularly in Tonj East and Twic counties of Warrap state which are the priority locations for this intervention.

2 - Project activities

The following activities listed will target 3430 ( 3262 Learners , 66 teachers and 85 education committees ) which will target children of internal displaced persons ,returnees , host community , children and youth with disabilities in the targeted counties and the strategy is in line with the education cluster objectives through the below actions • Enrolment of 3262 learners (2262 Boys and 1000 girls) in the 7 primary schools in Twic and Tonj East County including out of school children in order to allow them access basic education • Enrollment of 3262 children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) access and attend schools in affected areas to provide the learners with basic and quality education.• Establishment of 4 temporary learning spaces established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy 2019 which will provide safe and protective learning condition to the affected children to continue with their learning in the schools • Rehabilitation of 2 temporary learning spaces to the learners to improve the learning environment and quality of learning in the affected schools • Establishment of 4 and rehabilitation of 2 gender segregated / sensitive WASH facilities in line with INEE and sphere standards. (Unit of measurement: 1:60 for boys, 1:30 for girls) to improve the water and sanitation situation in the affected schools and improve the learning conditions of the learners more especially girls in term of attendance and retention in the schools • Provision of learners with sanitation and hygiene materials more especially for girls to improve their hygiene and sanitation situations in the schools and improve their attendance and retention in the schools • Provision of recreational materials/ supplies in the schools to help to cope up with the psychosocial problems and improving the wellbeing during the extra curriculum activities • Provision of teaching and learning materials to the learners and teachers in the schools to improve the quality of teaching and learning in the supported schools and the affected children • Training of 66 teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-cantered methodologies to improve the quality of teaching and learning in the schools • Training of 66 teachers on PSS, CP and referral mechanisms so that they can help children and psychosocial problems and child protection related issues • Training of 85 community education committee members and local education officials on school management, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction in Education (DRR) so that they can effectively manage and governed the schools in accordance with the set education laws • Provision of appropriate IEC prevention and mitigation information and materials to children, teachers and PTAs in order to create more awareness on the communicable diseases including COVID - 19 mitigation measures.• Conducting 6 school campaigns organized on back to school/learning to raise awareness and community mobilization on education.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

TADO regularly shares timely information, consultation and dialogue between service providers and affected populations and improves the quality and efficacy services to humanitarian response. It also strives to ensure, that appropriate inclusion of expressed priorities, needs and views of the affected population of different age and gender are considered , particularly women .children and persons with disabilities in regard to protection and solutions. The project will encourage strengthen accountability to the affected population and adapt coherent approach to AAP through existing community structures. The project will regularly undertake consultation with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls ,children and persons with disabilities and prioritizes them while planning education interventions. SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders will also continue to be involved in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for construction of TLS and monitoring teachers and pupils’ attendance through the PTA. Setting-up a simple, safe and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA and protection of school children will be enhanced and strengthened. A community based approach for protection of school children will also add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanism by incorporating local solutions into the overall SEA reporting system. A successful complaints mechanism will ensure the full participation of the community, through integrating formal and/or informal community structure. Project staff shall provide Hotline and suggestions boxes in the supported schools for the beneficiaries to either call or write any information which is important and send to the project management team and their complaints will be analyzed and address accordingly regardless of gender and age group in order to redesign, adjust or review the project based on the information provided by the affected population or beneficiaries

TADO will have focus group discussions with most vulnerable community members such as women ,girls , and person with disabilities of the affected community and key informants interviews in order to get feed backs from the most vulnerable groups in the community .There will be suggestion boxes placed in the schools to get feed backs on the project implementation , monitoring and recommendation from the affected population .

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

TADO commits to ensuring protection mainstreaming in all its humanitarian responses. Throughout this project, TADO will ensure affected populations are protected against harmful practices. IASC principles, and guidance by the GBV sub Cluster have been adhered to in designing the project and will be practiced during implementation. Protection risks will be mitigated by introducing efficient GBV awareness sessions and control measures. Targeted beneficiaries will be informed about the program- process, safe learning spaces will be established or rehabilitated with gender segregated WASH facilities for boys, girls and children with disabilities. This will emphasize the Do No Harm principle in a humanitarian setting. Equal opportunity shall be given to all beneficiaries, to emphasize impartial access to assistance, with considerations set up for Children with Special needs in collaboration with protection actors. Adolescent girls and young girls have been identified as the most marginalized and neglected group of the affected population requiring urgent interventions. TADO will further strengthen communication channels with IDP communities, Host Communities and Returnees. Through PSS and Life Skills trainings, self-protection capacities will be built amongst children. TADO plans to provide a multi – sector response utilizing schools as platforms for humanitarian interventions. TADO has provided new measures for prevention and handling staff misconduct in its HR policy with all staff signing a new code of conduct to reduce abuse, exploitation, harassment of beneficiaries etc. Additionally, TADO has carried out staff inductions to acquaint staff with AAP standards
