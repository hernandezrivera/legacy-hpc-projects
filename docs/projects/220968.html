Project Summary

Since April 2020, AAH delivers health, nutrition and MHPSS activities through mobile clinics in Meme Division.The project aims to reduce morbidity and mortality among the affected population in Meme Division (SouthwestCameroon) through an Integrated multisector emergency response in WASH, Nutrition, Health and MHPSS-P to children and adults affected by the crisis.
Activities are designed to ensure the continuum of care of the vulnerable population (children under five, pregnant and lactating women, elderly above 50 years old and people living with disabilties) through preventive and curative activities in four primary health care (PHC) facilities and the Kumba Regional Hospital Annex – herein after Kumba Hospital. Preventative activities at the community level with active CHW participation includes active screening for acute malnutrition, Family MUAC, community-based surveillance, and health and hygiene promotion. At the PHC level, antenatal care and vaccination of common childhood illnesses will be provided through the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI).
Meanwhile, curative activities that will be supported by AAH at the PHCs are:
• Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI)
• Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)
• Out-patient Therapeutic Program (OTP)
• Maternal and newborn care
• Contraception and family planning
• Adolescent sexual and reproductive health
At Kumba Hospital, we will be supporting the following:
• Peadiatrics
• Post exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for rape victims.
Other health strengthening services that we will implement:
• Reimbursement of referrals from the primary health care (PHC) to the referral hospital, Kumba Hospital.
• Deployment of Mobile clinics (MCs) to respond to emergencies.
Through MHPSS, mental health care will be provided as part of the general health services package and as part of clinical care for survivors of sexual violence and intimate partner violence. WASH in Health Care Faciltiies will also be supported. Furthermore, Action Against Hunger will mainstream protection principles and promote universal, meaningful, dignified and safe access to quality healthcare.
Action Against Hunger will mainstream protection principles and promote universal, meaningful,dignified and safe access to quality healthcare. 57,458 individuals are targeted for this 24months of project (2023-2025), including 30,662 female, 26,796 male, 15,168 U5, 2,772 ANC, 412 U5 with MAS, 11,492 IDPS, 575 Returnees.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Southwest region (including the the Northwest) has been the hot bed of the Anglophone Crisis also known as the Ambazonia War which started in 2016. This has led to the displacement of 583,112 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) (MSNA NWSW, February 2023) and 459,605 Returnees (former IDPs and refugees) ( MSNA, NWSW, February 2023). Many civilians have been targeted directly, others were caught in crossfire or killed/injured by improvised explosive devices (IEDs). Fighting continued in several areas between State security forces (SSF) and non-State armed groups (NSAGs), and among NSAG factions. This resulted in some of the displaced populations hiding in the surrounding bushes or forests, while others found refuge in neighboring villages. Many of these displacements were pendular, with people returning to their place of origin once the situation had improved and some displacements again when violence occurred. In the 2024 IOM MSNA, 95.7% of the IDPs interviewed cited armed conflicts as their main reason for leaving their place of habitual residence. The population continues to suffer from human rights abuse, including killings, destruction of property, kidnappings for ransom, arbitrary arrests, and extortion. According to Tthe 2024 IOM Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) with respondents who are either internally displaced (IDP) or returnees, has identified access to clean drinking water (18%), health services (8%), hygiene and sanitation (2%) and protection issues (1%) as the priority needs of the affected population. The 2023 MSNA reveals that most of the assessed households rely on unimproved sanitation facilities , These facilities are reported to be neither safe nor readily accessible for children, women, and people living with disabilities (PLWD), highlighting a critical sanitation and protection situation. Meanwhile, 83% of water is consumed without treatment and 79% use water without soap for handwashing . The impact of the crisis has put increasing pressure on the WASH infrastructure and further increased the risk of water related disease outbreaks. According to the MSNA Round 10 conducted in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) in July/August 2024, the primary source of water are unimproved water sources with a high risk of contamination like public taps or standpipes (37%) and surface water (37%) from lakes, ponds, rivers, and dams. The latter is 4% higher than the percentage reported last year during the same period. The same survey also revealed that the top three reasons for challenge in access to the waterpoints are distance (42%), limited water points (39%), and long lines (21%). All these scenarios increase the exposure of the population to the risk of water borne diseases such as cholera and potentially leading to a public health crisis. This situation is exacerbated by recurrent climatic phenomena affecting these regions, coupled with floods, landslides, and outbreaks, particularly mpox and cholera. According to Sphere, the percentage of population that can access primary healthcare within one hour’swalk from dwellings should be at a minimum of 80%. However, 60% of those interviewed says that it takes them more than 30 minutes of walking one way to get to the nearest functional health facility. This highlights the need to bring health services closer to the community. Another issue reported was the low vaccination rate of children 12 to 59 months. Less than 50% of the household (HH) surveyed possess a vaccination book. Only half of the children above six weeks have received Pentavalent vaccine and have it captured in a vaccination book while the rest reports to being vaccinated but without any documentation. The main challenges cited were the lack of awareness, lack of vaccine and vaccine services and no trust in vaccines. This highlights the need for community awareness and sensitization on Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI).
