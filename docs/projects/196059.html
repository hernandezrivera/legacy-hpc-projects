Project Summary

"The project aims to continue strengthening and improving a wide range of Camp Coordination and Camp Management as per 3 sector specific objectives; 1) to enhance coordination between camp stakeholders for timely and appropriate delivery of services, 2) to support safe living conditions through rationalized and participatory site planning, promote inclusive representation through feedback mechanisms, and work towards environmentally conscious construction and site maintenance initiatives and 3) to ensure emergency preparedness and response activities to natural hazards while protecting and rehabilitating ecosystems.
In partnership with the RRRC, IOM, site management and site development (SMSD) sector, EETWG and other sectors, UNHCR will work towards strengthening coordination structures, site management and development at the camp level. UNHCR will continue supporting the government in strengthening and promoting governance structures and civilian administration of the camps, while its partnership with MoDMR and RRRC remain key to UNHCR’s engagement in the camps.
UNHCR, through the Capacity Sharing Initiative (CSI) will continue to prioritize modular training to camp management actors (RRRC, CiC and partner staff) to enhance understanding and application of camp management standards.
UNHCR will prioritize improvement of site management facilities, ensuring continuous access to services, promoting community participation, promoting adherence to camp management standards and practices, enhancing site development including improvement of settlements’ infrastructure, site planning that address vulnerability to natural hazards and natural resource management.
UNHCR’s response will focus on engaging more closely with refugees to ensure interventions respond better to their needs; capacity building and support to the government; enhancing decision-making structures based on inclusive refugee representation and strengthening linkages between refugee preferences and available assistance; and promote the sustainability of interventions in collaboration with partners while continually searching for comprehensive solutions.
To improve participation and ownership of critical infrastructure and other community assets, UNHCR will continue working with refugees and Partners to implement community-led projects under care and maintenance where communities select and implement projects through Cash for Work (CFW) and material support from UNHCR. In addition, UNHCR plans to expand for communities to maintain WASH and Energy facilities as well.
Community and protection assessments have shown that refugees, specifically women and girls continue to face various protection risks and challenges such as poor lighting at night. Increase in threats targeting female volunteers have also added to the sense of insecurity in the camps. UNHCR will strengthen initiatives to reduce tensions and promote peaceful co-existence between the refugee and host communities and will design programs based on community consultations and feedback received from refugees and host communities including the rollout of the Common Feedback Platform.
As Bangladesh is particularly vulnerable to climate change, UNHCR will continue enhancing resilience of both refugee and host communities against seasonal natural disasters such as monsoon and cyclones as well as anthropogenic hazards such as fire. It will also continue to strengthen preparedness and ensure timely activation and deployment of personnel, continue supporting them with adequate equipment and relevant DRM trainings, drills, and simulations, while continuing to preposition essential relief items ahead of disasters.
UNHCR is currently piloting mechanisms to improve safety through continuous maintenance of solar streetlights through community engagement. Maintaining mini grids and other camp infrastructures with a community-based approach will also be reviewed.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Strengthening the management of the camps remains a priority, including enabling relevant actors to have adequate knowledge and tools to ensure the effective delivery of services in line with agreed standards. Over the years, camp management functions established in partnership with the RRRC, UNHCR and camp management partners continued to be improved. UNHCR’s focus is to consolidate these functions to the extent possible to enhance refugee participation and representation at camp level as well as strengthening the capacity of RRRC team both in Cox’s Bazar and at the camp level.Due to frequent change of CiCs and complexity of this response, there is a continuous need for supporting RRRC, CiCs, with site management and site development capacities; strengthen camp level coordination; ensuring all actors understand roles, and responsibilities and participate in decision-making; increase and strengthen refugee representation (with gender parity); ensure strong Disaster Risk Management (DRM) for effective emergency response.Increased government role in camp management will require the RRRC to ensure harmonized exercise of authority and consistent practices by CiCs across all camps. Through the Capacity Sharing Initiative, UNHCR will continue to work with IOM to provide direct technical support and training to RRRC team and partners. It is supporting CiC staff and will make concerted efforts to ensure checks and balances in the governance model in all camps. UNHCR and its partners will continue to design and provide information products that reinforce the coordination system enabling humanitarian actors to make informed decisions.From the partners progress report and field monitoring findings, there is a need of small-scale site improvement and DRR works in camps. The difficult topography of most part of the camps, refugees (especially the persons with disability, children and pregnant women) continue to face difficulties with safe physical access to services. Although the camps have evolved significantly, the partners report and field monitoring reports show that there is still need for provision of adequate access that could function as emergency exit/rescue routes during a disaster incident.In addition, most of the site infrastructures (pathways, bridges, drains, slop stabilization works) will require regular maintenance as these were developed using non-durable materials. Therefore, UNHCR and partners continue to work on improving access, mitigating environmental risks, and installing and maintaining essential infrastructure like foot paths, roads, bridges, walkways, steps and slope stabilization, among others. Eco-disaster risk reduction and infrastructure investments have been integrated with environmental protection and rehabilitation activities, including nature-based solutions such as drain cleaning and restoration of natural drainage systems and plantation for slope stabilization to reduce soil erosion and flooding. A monsoon season of four months with heavy rain and two cyclone seasons in April/May and October/November exposes the camps to recurring emergencies. In collaboration with CiCs and relevant partners, UNHCR will continue to invest in the capacity of Safety Unit Volunteers (SUVs) and Site Management Volunteers (SMS volunteers) through training and simulations. These refugees will continue to be the first responders to natural/ human induced disasters and other emergencies, and as a community-based response mechanism they will play a key role in saving lives.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

"UNHCR has one partner per camp for its site management as well as site development programmes and continues to facilitate synergy among SMSD partners. As part of the rationalization approach, UNHCR is processing and rationalizing partnership arrangements for site management and community-based protection (CBP) sectors aligning with the implementation of both programmes in the same camp. This combination contributes to effective coordination at the camp level, efficient service delivery as well as strengthening strategic relationships with different stakeholders including the camps-in-charge (CiCs) and refugees. In coordination wtih other stakeholders, the SMSD sector facilitates service monitoring, incident reporting, joint assesments and engages with the affected population to inform alignment to the needs. UNHCR conducts regular needs assessments and Post Distribution Monitoring to identify assistance required and prepare for providing support considering age, gender and diversity (AGD) and prioritization of persons with specific needs. UNHCR also ensures regular risk assessments and adjusts implementation as required to optimize the achievement of desired outcomes in the projects. UNHCR has a multi-year approach to its partnership arrangements, while the maintaining of annual partnership agreements is subject to an annual performance review. This allows service continuity and the opportunity to capitalize on expertise, cost efficiency, community engagement, bulding relationsips with government officials for development of site infrustructure and their sustatainable management. The UNHCR SMSD programme will continue contributing towards the ""one camp approach"" that would facilitate efficient and equitable services for its Persons of Concern."

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Protection mainstreaming is at the core of SMSD interventions ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, safety, and security, alleviating suffering, providing dignity for women, girls, boys and men, particularly marginalized groups such as older people, people living with disabilities, and ethnic groups. UNHCR and its partners will use gender-sensitive quantitative data analysis to track beneficiaries’ satisfaction, participation, acceptance, knowledge, and practices along with PSEA trainings. UNHCR will continue working with refugees including all ages, genders, and diversity (AGD) and Partners to implement community-led projects under care and maintenance where communities identify and implement projects and are part of the implementation through CFW with material support from UNHCR.

3.2 - Gender

The provision of safe site management services to address the specific needs of refugees is one of the most fundamental principles of protection and gender-based violence (GBV) risk mitigation. Assessment of the needs of the various communities will focus on gender-based needs to determine appropriate refferal and response in coordination with various service providers. Assistance to persons with special needs and risks associated with gender, age and diversity will be assessed through participative and inclusive risk mapping focused on the provision of basic services, as well as safety audits. UNHCR will ensure ongoing information sharing between site management, shelter, protection and GBV field staff in the targeted areas, and ensure appropriate training of the partners and community volunteers on gender and age equality and GBV mainstreaming.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Children have the same right to site management services as everyone else. At the same time, UNHCR recognizes that children may have specific needs. Improperly built and poorly maintained facilities especially in crowded refugee camps may expose children to risks of sexual exploitation and abuse. Therefore, UNHCR in coordination with Child Protection AoR and community-based child protection structures will ensure that shelter facilities are safe and related information provided through communication interventions, including on safety, is disseminated in formats that children, adolescents and caregivers with disabilities can understand and also promote child safeguarding.

3.4 - Diversity

UNHCR's service delivery is based on a diversity analysis to ensure that all persons of concern have equitable access to the support required. To improve participation and ownership of critical infrastructure and other community assets, UNHCR will continue working with refugees including all ages, genders, and diversity (AGD) and Partners to implement community-led projects under care and maintenance where communities identify and implement projects and are part of the implementation through Cash for Work (CFW) with material support from UNHCR. In addition, UNHCR plans to expand community maintenance to enable communities to maintain WASH and Energy facilities as well.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in the design of the projects are key steps in achieving quality outcomes. UNHCR will engage affected populations throughout the project cycle; in assessments, design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of projects; establishing open channels of communication for feedback, complaints and information sharing (CFRM); and facilitate a participatory process for decision making and mutual learning. The CLPs and other initiatives have enabled the community to identify, priorities and contribute to the improvement of community infrastructure and enhanced ownership for sustainability. Community volunteers are first responders and have been equipped with skills on emergency preparedness and response as well as community awareness. UNHCR, through site management focals and partners, will conduct regular meetings with community representatives to inform and update communities on projects and receive feedback from beneficiarie

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNHCR continues to implement its activities while considering existing climatic and environmental risks and vulnerabilities, given that refugee camps are prone to climate related disasters. A follow up LPG assessment conducted in 2021 by UNHCR’s partner found 79 percent reduction in deforestation as a direct impact of the sustainable use of LPG and an ongoing improved cooking technologies pilot points to initial indications of some 30-33 percent reduction in energy use. Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), access to safe cooking energy, reafforestation and climate change actions contribute to the overall protection of the populations and sustainability. UNHCR will work to ensure implementation of the Climate Action Plan to further the greening of the camps and is investing in bamboo plantations to mitigate the impact of excessive bamboo use for shelter and infrastructure construction. Through continuous training and simulations, the communities continue to be equipped on DRM and DRR elements
