Project Summary

In order to address the urgent humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host community members affected by the conflict in Borno and Yobe States, Northeast Nigeria, Action Against Hunger is proposing a cash-based transfer program. The aim is to prevent and mitigate the shocks of the lean season on food-insecure households and build resilience as well as economic recovery. The lean season months in Borno and Yobe include May to October (with peaks in July, August & September) Hence, the proposed intervention will prevent deterioration and contribute to the improvement of the food and nutrition status for the most vulnerable people in the highly food insecure areas of Borno and Yobe. It will also start them on a journey towards income resilience through conflict-sensitive and context-specific economic empowerment initiatives, while at the same time improving the consumption of nutritious food and biomass production in the community through community agroforestry using tree seedlings raised under cash for work intervention. The proposed intervention places gender, protection, COVID-19 prevention, conflict sensitivity and environmental consideration at the forefront in the programming.

1 - Needs

Over a decade now, the conflict in northeast Nigeria has taken a heavy toll on the population. Approximately 2,191,193 individuals (445,852 households) remain displaced in North-East Nigeria. According to HNO 2021, the projected figures for people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 in the BAY states is 8.7 million people (2.4M boys, 2.7M girls, 1.9M women and 1.7M men). Of the people in need, 1.9M are IDPs, 4.8M are host community members, 0.9M are people with physical disabilities. 5.1M people are in need of food security and livelihoods assistance. The majority of the people in need are in Borno State, which hosts 81% of the IDPs, of whom 54% stay in IDP camps (HNO 2021). One in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children are the most affected by the crisis constituting 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. Borno State is the worst hit and hosts the highest number of IDPs (1,633,829). In Yobe State there are 162,394 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 37, 2021. According to the latest CH report (October 2021) in the projected situation (June to August, 2022), there will be about 18,030,674 persons in the combined critical phases of food and nutrition insecurity. This figure is higher than 12,831,034 persons in the same period in 2021(June-August, 2021). The COVID-19 pandemic and the associated containment measures disrupted the household income-generating activities (IGAs) and led to sub-optimal livelihood functionality, reduced income sources while most livelihood activities showed deterioration (FEWSNET, October, 2021). It has been projected that several households in Borno and Yobe states are likely to resort to emergency coping strategies that may result in a further collapse in livelihoods in the absence of conscious livelihood intervention stimulus packages. Currently, about 45% of the population in Nigeria live in poverty, 76.8% of this population is in the Northeast. The unemployment rate in Nigeria is projected to reach 40.1% by the end of 2021, while the unemployment rate in Yobe state is around 52% and 50% in Borno state2 . In Yobe and Borno states respectively, the working-age population stands at 2,117,760 and xxx while 1,538,643 are not in the labour force according to this NBS report. The Essential Needs and Nutrition Assessment (ENNA) conducted in October 2021 by WFP indicates that COVID-19, insecurity and other shocks had led to severe losses in income-earning opportunities for IDP and host population across the BAY states.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure safe and accountable programming AAH shall mitigate the potential of conflict between host community members and IDPs or project beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in the location of intervention through strong community engagements and consultations and project monitoring. The project shall target vulnerable host community members and IDPs as part of measures to ensure that no tension is created between host population and IDPs. Community stakeholders shall have representatives from host community and IDPs. AAH shall mitigate risks associated with cash and livelihoods by selection and siting of distribution points in close proximity with beneficiaries' homesteads or camps. Distribution shall be done only during the daytime and on market days. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to travel in groups to serve as a deterrence measure to any form of risk when alone. Security assessment of distribution points and location of intervention shall be conducted prior to commencement of the intervention. AAH will undertake constant monitoring of the context and review.The proposed intervention will seek to address household decision-making and resource management through the engagement of men and women in the intervention. The intervention will consider active participation of men and women in project meetings, monitoring, selection, trainings, project activity review and feedback.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Action Against Hunger (AAH), ensures that participation, feedback and complaints mechanism are integrated in all strategies including Food Security and Livelihood sector, plans and programs. It also provides a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication with affected populations and use them to inform affected populations about procedures and processes that affect them so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. This is in line with IASC principles and commitment. AAH considers sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers as an act of gross misconduct and therefore perpetrators will face termination with immediate effect. Irrespective of any form of consent, if the sexual activity with children (anyone under the age of 18 years despite the customary or legal definition in the country) by humanitarian workers is prohibited regardless of explanation. Moreover, sexual exploitation as a mode of exchange for benefits are completely prohibited not only with beneficiaries but also within humanitarian workers. Apart from this, any form of exchange whether it is monetary or physical for providing humanitarian assistance to the beneficiaries including sexual or other degradable forms of favors are strictly prohibited. The workers, vendors or community volunteers who were caught in this act will be immediately suspended from the program/ mission and if proven guilty after investigation, they will be terminated. AAH will also ensure to create and maintain an environment that will prevent such sexual exploitations and abuse and promote the implementation of AAH code of conduct.
