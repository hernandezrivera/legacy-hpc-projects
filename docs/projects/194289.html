Project Summary

South Sudan literacy levels remain very low. According to UNICEF, only 23% of the total population in the country have had formal education and this has affected the access to education to millions of young children (UNICEF 2020). It is even worse for the children with disabilities who have no access to education services and schools are not accessible. The COVID-19 pandemic has greatly affected the school calendar in South Sudan with a lot of schools being closed during the pandemic and that greatly affected education services to the vulnerable and affected populations, especially persons with disabilities. Conflict and displacements have led to destruction of the education infrastructure leaving students unable to learn. The capacity of teachers to teach in schools is very low. Availability of disability inclusive learning materials, for learners with disabilities, is also quite limited. This has left learners with huge learning gaps and especially learners with disabilities.
In line with the education cluster strategic objectives, Light for the World will improve equitable access to safe and protective education services for 2000 emergency-affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children with disabilities. The project will improve learning conditions by supporting the learners and teachers with education materials, building the capacity of teachers and the school community to support safe learning and improving coping mechanisms and resilience.
LfTW will use Disability Inclusion in Community Development (DICD) which is a key approach used to support our inclusive education work. It combines rehabilitation with capacity building at community level. LfTW also uses Disability inclusion facilitators (DIFs) approach that empowers youth with disabilities on the one hand and targets hard to reach children on the other. Taken together, these two approaches will ensure that DICD workers in the communities are able to identify neglected children with disabilities in the communities and link them with education actors to support inclusive education for vulnerable children, including girls, with disabilities.
DIFs are highly motivated and competent youths with disabilities that received extensive training to support disability inclusion among mainstream organizations. They are changemakers and ensure that mainstream organizations are disability inclusive. They also act as role models on how inclusion is a reality. They provide training on disability awareness, inclusive education, inclusive TVET and humanitarian action. They are also involved in on-the-job coaching and changing community mindset on issues related to gender and inclusion.
Keeping a child-centred approach: in Education in Emergency (EiE). LftW will use this approach in implementing IE in 3 IDP camps and other locations.
We are grounded and connected to the daily reality of parents, teachers, school management and children with disabilities.
Light for the World offers a comprehensive package of expertise and support within the South Sudan context and is also able to draw from our global expertise in the area of inclusive education and humanitarian action.
LftW has vast experience in disability inclusion and supported South Sudan’s ministry of education in initiating and drafting the disability inclusive education policy, which was signed in July 2021. This project will disseminate the policy to key stakeholders in an effort to realize disability inclusive education. LftW has a physical presence in the target counties and has vast experience in implementation of disability inclusive education programmes in South Sudan. LftW recruited 32 youth with different types of disabilities as facilitators in four states to make sure that they become role models for advocates of the right to education for children with disabilities. The organization is a model for other organizations by giving job opportunities for people with disabilities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been in political conflict since 2013 and this greatly affected service delivery in the country. A lot of school infrastructure was destroyed during the fighting among the conflicting parties. It is estimated that over 2.2 million children were out of school by 2018 (GPE 2020) and, with the county having one of the youngest populations in the world, this puts pressure on the limited education resources in the country. The countries literacy levels are one of the lowest in the country, with UNICEF estimating that only 23% of the population are literate and this is even worse for persons with disabilities. This is due to limited access to education services, disability inclusive learning environments and materials. According to the 2022 humanitarian needs overview, a total of 3.6 million people, of which 97% are children, are in need of disability inclusive education services. This is an increase from 3.4 million people in 2021 and 3.1 million people in 2020. Over 139,000 children who are displaced are in need of education services. The COVID-19 pandemic has had a negative impact on the education calendar and to the learners due to prolonged periods of school closure. Before the COVID 19 pandemic, a total of 2.2 million children were out of school and this has increased to over 2.7 million children.This includes children with disabilities who lack access to education services, thus reinforcing the urgent need for provision of disability inclusive education. According to the 2021 Education Needs Assessment, education service delivery in South Sudan has faced great challenges. School infrastructure and the enrollment of children with disabilities in learning institutions, have been negatively impacted by 3 years of recurring flooding, insecurity around the schools and , lack of disability inclusive materials for teachers, both teaching and learning. Additionally, the lack of payment of salaries and delayed incentives to the teachers, have led to low teacher turnout and teacher motivation to attend to learners. This has had a detrimental effect on learning of children, especially children with disabilities. Lack of segregated and disability friendly WASH facilities, menstrual hygiene management kits and hand washing facilities in the schools have also led to girls, including girls with disabilities, absenteeism in schools.Girls with disabilities are vulnerable to gender based violence that affect their attendance to school, Light for the world will therefore identify the severe cases in schools and link them up to care in order to improve school retention rates for girls with disabilities.Early and forced marriages among young girls have had a huge negative impact on education services for young girls that has led to relatively low numbers of girl enrolment in schools. Therefore, urgent intervention is needed to ensure that children, including those with disabilities, have access to quality, disability inclusive education. This is key to improving literacy levels in South Sudan as well as empowering the most vulnerable in the community.

2 - Project activities

Enrollment of 2000 children with disabilities in primary schools in Bor South County of Jonglei State and Wau County of Western Bar Ghazal State ● Establishment of 4 disability inclusive temporary learning spaces (TLS) and classrooms in Juba IDP camps and in Bor South and Wau Counties, 2 TLS per county ● Establishment of 6 gender segregated disability inclusive latrines with water points and in Wau and Bor South Counties, procure and supply sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) to students and teachers ● Provide assistive and mobility devices for children with disabilities to support access to education institutions ● Provision of quality disability inclusive education recreational materials to the conflict and crisis affected children and youth in the two target counties ● Training of 80 teachers in disability inclusive Education in Emergency pedagogical skills and disability inclusive Child-centered methodologies to improve disability inclusive teaching and learning processes ● Train 80 teachers and education personnel on disability inclusive referral pathways on protection/GBV, COVID-19 and health, and nutrition ● Training of 100 SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on disability inclusive school management and governance, Psycho Social Support (PSS), and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) ● Awareness Creation on COVID-19 prevention and mitigation measures in all the project locations (print and distribute appropriate Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials ● Conduct back-to-learning campaigns, 4 per county in Bor South and Wau Counties ● Identification and registration of children with disabilities in an emergency situation ● Conduct home-based rehabilitation - therapy, mobility and orientation training, sign language, etc.,to enable children with disabilities to be ready for schooling ● Awareness raising on inclusive education in education in emergency. ● Recruitment and training of DICD field workers to work home to home to rehabilitate children with disabilities through different life skills training ● Recruit and train youth with disabilities as disability inclusion facilitators (DIFs). DIFs are role models and relationship builders with organizations for meaningful inclusion. ● Train mainstream actors on disability inclusive education in emergency through the trained DIFs and Light for the World experts. ● Train OPDs/Organizations of people with disabilities to advocate for an inclusive education in emergency in government, donors and education oriented organizations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Light for the world will ensure that the feedback from the most vulnerable in the community is received through robust mechanisms that will be put in place. The organization has a pool of disability inclusion facilitators that will be key in getting feedback especially from the persons with disabilities.Light for the world will have representatives of marginalized groups and most of the materials will be translated in to local languages to ensure that they are able to reach the most vulnerable in the community.There will be a dedicated line for people to call and give feedback on the project implementation through out the project cycle and focused group discussions and key informant interviews will be conducted with the vulnerable groups to get their feedback on the project implementation and act upon the it appropriately.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Light for the world will ensure that the communities in the target locations are fully involved in the implementation of this project.This will be done through continuous engagement with the community leaders and communicate to them the project activities and ensure that they are involved in selection of beneficiaries. Light for the World will form beneficiary accountability committees that will be key in giving feedback to the organization on the implementation of the programmes.Light for the world will have feedback mechanisms put in place and will have a dedicated email address and phone number to receive feedback from the community and act upon the issues raised within the shortest time possible.There will be constant engagement with the community through out the implementation period and light for the world will use the disability inclusion facilitators to build the capacity of the community leaders on disability inclusion.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Light for the World has non tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse. This policy will be given to all the project staffs to ensure that they read and understand on how to present sexual exploitation and abuse during project implementation. Every staff will sign code of contact that has policies in sexual exploitation and abuse.Cases on sexual exploitation and abuse reported will be taken seriously and investigated and the culprits brought to justice. LFTW will establish referral pathways for survivors of sexual exploitation and ensure that they are protected against the perpetrators and where possible access to legal aid. this will be done in collaboration with protection partners and health partners in the project implementation sites.Teachers who engage in sexual exploitation will be interdicted waiting investigation and the victims will be protected from further abuse.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Light for the World will regularly share timely information, consult and dialogue with the community, service providers and affected populations to improve the quality and efficacy of services to humanitarian response. It also strives to ensure, that appropriate inclusion of expressed priorities, needs and views of the affected population, particularly children, with regard to protection and solutions. The project will encourage strengthen accountability to the affected population and adapt coherent approach to AAP through existing community structures. The project will regularly undertake consultation with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls and children with disabilities and priorities them while planning education interventions. SMCs, PTAs and Community leaders will also continue to be involved in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) with the aim to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for construction of TLS and monitoring teachers and pupils’ attendance through the PTA. Setting-up a simple, safe and accessible Community Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA and protection of school children will be enhanced and strengthened.Feedback received during project implementation will be key in improving performance during project implementation.
