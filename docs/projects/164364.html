Project Summary

Economic livelihoods across Syria have been severely impacted by the crisis. This impact is particularly apparent in the agriculture and livestock sector. Increases in input and production costs, and disruptions in value chains, coupled with reduced availability of power and water resources are all factors leading to decreases in production levels, poor availability of locally produced foods, rising sector-related unemployment, and placing added strain on already weak social structures and communities in general.
Taking into consideration the above trends, the Communities to Market (C2M) project will contribute to strengthened livelihoods of crisis-affected populations and improved social cohesion among local communities through the promotion/adoption of socially inclusive business models. The project will aim to create 650 durable, sustainable, and green direct and indirect jobs in the dairy value chain through the creation of two pilot community production clusters in Hama and Rural Damascus as well as two pilot co-operative and business governance structures in the same locations. Using environmentally-friendly technologies which are designed to work off-grid, the community production clusters will integrate all economic activities related to livestock development, dairy productions, cheese production, and other economic activities within a closed loop model. Direct job creation will be managed and governed through the co-operatives responsible for ensuring that employees benefit from quality income opportunities, social safety nets such as health insurance, and skills enhancement. Vocational training will also be provided to direct beneficiaries in the areas of system operation, quality management, product development, and life skills training. Indirect jobs will be supported across the dairy value chain including in the areas of food retailers, transportation, logistics, etc. The project will prioritize job creation for women and persons with disabilities in rural areas given the high levels of vulnerabilities this segment of the community faces in accessing resources and income generating opportunities.
The project will achieve its goals through five implementation-output streams:
• Two community production clusters established within Hama and Rural Damascus Governorates
• Economic livelihood opportunities created particularly for female-headed households
• Labourers received training in system operations and livestock production
• Linkages with markets established for cluster-based products
• Community-level business entity with a co-operative structure and governance framework established within the project locations.

4 - Needs

An estimated 7.90 million people in Syria are food insecure and a further 1.9 million are at risk of food insecurity. The main drivers to food insecurity continue to be conflict and protracted displacement, loss or lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity and economic sanctions on the country (HNO 2020).The HNO 2020 indicates that around 70% of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65% of their expenditure, over 37% of food insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57% of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies. Based on AKF Syria’s internal study, in Salamieh district alone, 80% of HHs minimized expenditure on non-food items; 51% skipped payments for rent or debts; and 48% took out more loans and fell further into debt to afford food and medical care. Female headed households are mostly vulnerable due to female high unemployment rate, and high dependency on social welfare. HHs exhausted economy is further complicating elderly and PwD health situation, as they cannot afford medicine.Surging input prices, abandonment of productive lands and pastures, limited access to essential grazing lands, disruptions in domestic supply chains, a poorly functioning market, weak institutional and community-based support systems and lack of access to new technologies and practices are rapidly degrading and eroding the livestock sub-sector placing immense strains on both urban and rural households (HNO, 2020). FAO (2019) estimates a decrease in sheep numbers of 40 percent compared to 2010 with 69% decrease in Hama and 6% decrease in Rural Damascus. While cattle numbers experienced a decrease of about 40 percent compared to 2010 with 2% decrease in Hama and 60% in Rural Damascus. As per FAO (special report, 2019) recommendations, considering cooperative arrangements are advised due to the financial advantages of cooperative purchase of inputs and the marketing of produce, and of collective credit.

5 - Implementation of activities

To increase access to direct and indirect jobs for rural-based HHs, focusing on women and PwDs, two pilot community dairy production clusters will be established in project locations. The clusters will integrate all economic activities related to livestock development, dairy production, cheese production, and other activities within a closed loop integrated model. The specific cluster components are organic feedstock production unit, cattle farming unit, algae production unit, milk processing unit, and cold storage unit. The output of the cluster will include cream cheese, and eventually high-quality meat products for both domestic and international markets. Cream cheese will be packaged and sold to local retail markets and adapted based on local tastes. About 70% of net profits will be used to cover the direct and indirect costs of the two clusters. The remaining 30% will be distributed across different usages including health insurance, training/capacity development, a community investment fund used to upgrade community assets such as schools. In collaboration with community stakeholders, a community-level business entity with a co-operative structure and governance framework will be established within the proposed project locations to ensure that decent jobs are maintained, and health insurance is made available. Vocational training will also be provided to direct beneficiaries in the areas of system operation, quality management, product development, and life skills training. Process and outcome monitoring activities implemented under the project include baseline and end-line assessments of the target groups, case studies, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring, household surveys and Focus Group Discussions with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Social cohesion within the district breakdown due to increased sectarianism within the community. Impact: Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Through CBOs, AKF will focus on inclusion of all stakeholders, and promote communal benefits of the project within local communities.B: Female workers may be subject to discrimination by other workers/traders due to negative cultural perception associated with some types of work. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Low. To mitigate such risk, community organizations will expand awareness of work type to identify vulnerable individuals who are willing to undertake such type of work. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting system will help to identify and investigate any cases of exploitations and take actions against it.C: Gender related cultural and traditional norms and roles may limit ability of women to participate in some types of work. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Women workers will be asked preference of specific work/task in order to ensure their comfort. AKF-Syr will maintain regular monitoring of women working environment along with active complaint reporting system, and facilitate gender equality awareness raising to facilitate positive interaction and appreciation of female participants. Teams will also be established with mixed genders with female leaders for each team whenever applicable.D: Community representatives/ Cooperatives’ Board of Trustees might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries, Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium. AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precis implementation based on the selection criteria.C: GBV and job associated hazards. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. Workers will be provided with protective equipment; close monitoring of working environment, and ensure safe and easy access; consequently, minimizes the risk of gender-based violence, reduces time burdens, and facilitates family care responsibilities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Reduced productivity of cow’s milk and meat due to the spread of pathogens. Mitigation: Cows will be fed on organic feedstock produced by the organic feedstock production unit, ensure healthy environmental conditions, and contract with competent and well-trained veterinaries to provide animal health services. Risk2: Lack of adherence on the part of MOSAL to deliver the required permits which results in either delayed or protracted approval processes for the legalization of community co-operatives. Mitigation: AKF has already communicated with MOSAL and got an official permission to establish a cooperative/foundation. Risk3: The transfer of funds to implementing unit may take longer than expected due to the Syrian sanctions and modification in banking procedures. Mitigation: AKF in collaboration with AKF’s head office in Geneva regularly monitor the amount of time transfers take, and bridge financial resources to ensure minimal disruption to project implementation. Risk4: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project. Risk5: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in successfully procuring required items for delivery to Salamieh. Mitigation: AKDN has extensive experience in rural livelihoods programming in extremely difficult environments. AKF will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement tendering offers to within the Governorates. Risk6: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed a high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is safe for travel and implementation.
