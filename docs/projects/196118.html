Project Summary

"Reaching People in Need Bangladesh (RPN)’s education in emergencies (EiE) program started in 2018 and, based on the learning’s into the response, will continue in 2023 to support across the 4 objectives of the Cox's Bazar (CXB) Education Sector's JRP, corroborating equitable access to inclusive, quality learning opportunities and community engagement for girls, boys and youth in camp At Ukhiya. In 2023, RPN will continue to support equitable access to culturally and age appropriate care, learning and development in a safe, protective and inclusive environment, spanning early childhood care and development (ECCD) to functional literacy, numeracy and life skills for older children, adolescents and youth. In camps, RPN will Improve to establish a community-based learning facilities (CBLF) with modality. Meanwhile, RPN will continue to support education in on its long experience with Education sector across the camp, and collaboration in RRRC over 2017, particularly in ECCD.
Structured trainings for learning facilitators in camps and teachers in HC will aim to strengthen the quality of teaching and learning process for Girls &Boys across the age spectrum targeted by CXB Education Sector, with regular monitoring of quantifiable and qualitative progress to feed into regular mentoring and on-the-job coaching. PIB adopts a pairing modality of learning facilitators from camps with HC Mentors, bridging the distance between both communities through capacity building initiatives. HC Mentors and education officials will be trained to provide better mentoring and supervisory support to learning facilitators and teachers for inclusive, quality education.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"Key findings in Education, ref. to Needs and priorities of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017. Access to education for Rohingya children in the camps remains challenging. COVID-19 mitigation measures resulted in the closure of LCs and schools, in camps and Bangladeshi communities, halving the number of children able to access learning in 2021. Access to education has improved in 2022 but remains difficult for some Rohingya children, particularly older children. School closures and the challenges of implementing remote learning in both communities during the pandemic reduced education access in 2020–2021.Barriers for older Rohingya students persisted, as they faced a less structured curriculum and significantly fewer opportunities to have an education facility in their camp. Only 3% of the LCs offered services for children above 14 years of age. The main barriers to education reported by boys included irrelevant or age inappropriate learning (reported by 52% of boys). The barriers reported by girls were those related to cultural norms and families’ tendency to withdraw girls from public life after puberty. Factors affecting the latter involved concerns around girls bringing shame to the family or ruining their chances of marriage, perceptions that there is limited utility to educating girls in terms of the low livelihood of them getting jobs, and concerns about being harassed by men and boys.Involvement in livelihoods and domestic work are additional impediments to educational quality for older children (REVA 2017; ISCG 12/11/2020 b; REACH et al. 29/03/2021). Remote learning has been challenging for children in Rohingya camps given limited resources. Impediments include limited internet and mobile network connection, a lack of learning materials, the unavailability of teachers, a lack of guidance from teachers, and the unavailability of educated household members to support children with their studies (REACH et al. 29/03/2021; J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). In 2020, 62% of households reported facing challenges in supporting their children’s remote study (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020). Households also reported older children getting involved in child labor and child marriage, putting many (both boys and girls) at risk of not returning to school at all (REVA 2020; J-MSNA Rohingya 2021). In 2021, Rohingya refugees expressed discontent with the LCs, specifically about the poor quality of education, disinterested Bangladeshi teachers, and a lack of progressively increasing difficulty levels upon finishing each year of study (ACAPS/IOM 28/04/2021). It is difficult to accurately understand the proportion of eligible children covered because of differences in the age groups used in the demographic breakdown of refugee population figures. To give an approximate idea, UNHCR figures indicate that there were 64,415 girls and 69,504 boys in the 12–17 age group in the camps as at 31 July 2022 (UNHCR 13/08/2022). While the introduction of the Learning Competencies Framework and Approach and the Myanmar curriculum are likely to improve education quality for all children regardless of age and prepare them for future employment, opportunities for them to work and improve their quality of life remain severely restricted (J-MSNA Rohingya 2020)."

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

RPN has designed this project based on the Principles of Rationalization for the Rohingya Refugee response in Bangladesh, prepared by the ISCG in Cox’s Bazar. Necessary activities and strategies have been adapted ensuring that all targeted Rohingya Refugees in Cox’s Bazar have equitable access to basic education services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner, through a standardized and cost-effective approach to providing equitable education services to refugees where 3% learners will be Children with Disability. RPN will take necessary steps on inclusive education which will be appropriate for the Children with Disability learners. While RPN is delivering the quality education (MC & LCFA) along with other need based supports to the targeted Refugee children in light of Child safeguarding policy and PSEA maintaining necessary coordination with education sector and respective CiCs and above all, following the sector prescribed guideline and principles. RPN is also providing monthly 5w reports and other necessary reports as required by the sector and other stakeholders regularly. RPN has experienced manpower who have received different training by Education sector and will be involved in capacity building initiatives by the sector to ensure a minimum standard of service delivery.
