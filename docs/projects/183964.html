Project Summary

Despite promising reforms, incidents of violence and violent conflict remain an everyday reality for many communities in Darfur. As UNAMID is withdrawing its presence and both UNITAM’s and the Government of Sudan’s capacity to protect civilians is limited, there is a significant protection gap that requires an urgent response by local and international actors. Unarmed Civilian Protection is one way in which civil society can contribute to closing this protection gap. It is an approach recommended in UNSCR 2579 (2021) on the transition from a peacekeeping mission (UNAMID) to a special political mission (UNITAMS) in Darfur) and requested by many of the 80 Darfuri young men and women that Nonviolent Peaceforce trained online in 2020 with the support of UNAMID.
NP is moving into Darfur in 2021 to initiate a civilian protection programme in El Fashir (North), specifically in Kabkabiya, Tawila and Kutum. The proposed action contributes to reducing the identified protection gap by focusing on direct protection efforts in the most vulnerable areas in North Darfur. It seeks to enhance safety and security of high-risk communities in Darfur by: (1) enhancing existing and emerging local community protection efforts: (2) providing direct protection by mixed teams of national and international teams of Nonviolent Peaceforce; (3) improving the responsiveness of actors that have a mandate or responsibility to protect civilians.
The Action goes beyond the enhancement of safety and security by fostering a culture of nonviolence. It seeks to reduce threats by bringing conflicting parties together and opening channels of communication, humanising conflicting parties, building trust between conflicting parties, engaging with antagonists, and expanding options for response. It also opens the door for women and youth to participate in security processes and challenges the notion that weapons or physical force are a prerequisite for providing safety and security. Its use of nonviolent methods shows communities that strengthening social relations, trust, and social responsibility can be both a method and outcome. This is particularly relevant to the current context of political transition in which armed peacekeeping is phased out. The action also acts as a counterforce to the drivers of violence within Sudan and the broader region. As the country emerges from violent conflict, Ethiopia is facing severe and violent internal conflict with humanitarian and security consequences that could jeopardise the fragile political transition of Sudan.

1 - Needs

Sudan’s 2019 nonviolent revolution initiated promising reforms nationally, but demands for responsible governance, justice, and security have yet to be realized. As an already thinned out UNAMID has drawn down as of 31 December 2020 and withdrawing its presence entirely by 30 June 2021, seasonal migration disputes, inter-ethnic tensions, and gender-based violence continue unabated and community violence in certain locations has increased. Proliferation of weapons among the population and a lack of capacity of police and military further contribute to insecurity, while significant military forces (e.g. Abdul-Wahid al Nur) continue to operate outside of Sudan’s national peace process. The increase in violence, destruction of property, and numbers of newly displaced is traumatising communities and undermining an already fragile peace process. While UN and other humanitarian agencies operate across Darfur, a lot of protection needs remain unaddressed. Moreover, none of these actors provides direct physical protection. The new UN Special Political Mission in Sudan, UNITAMS will have a primarily Khartoum-based presence. UNITAMS’ proposed 'Office of Civilian Protection and Rule of Law' requested under fifty staff to cover human rights, child protection, Woman Peace and Security, and protection for the entire country. The Government of Sudan has established an office and produced a national plan for the protection of civilians, but it does not contain concrete benchmarks. Observers also point out that the GoS does not have the resources, nor the trust of the citizens, to meet protection needs for the almost two million people in IDP camps and the entire population of 9+ million in Darfur alone. This means that a lot of the immediate protection burden rests on the shoulders of local authorities, human rights defenders, youth groups and other civil society actors.NP’s interlocutors in Darfur, including Sudanese people we have trained in Unarmed Civilian Protection methods (2020), UNAMID staff, UNCT members, and counterpart NGOs have indicated the need for additional and stronger modalities for protecting civilians and enhanced skills among civilians in de-escalating tensions and protecting vulnerable individuals and groups in their communities. Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) is an approach recommended in UNSCR 2425 (2020) on the establishment of UNITAMS in Sudan and strongly recommended by a number of local actors as a method that can contribute to closing the identified protection gap.Closing the protection gap, breaking the cycles of violence, and strengthening the drivers of peace in Sudan is urgent. The spirit of the nonviolent revolution and the confidence that a more peaceful society is within reach is still strong among large segments of the Sudanese population. Using the momentum to assist the Sudanese people in meeting the existing and emerging protection challenges can further invigorate the drive for peace in Sudan and boost confidence in their self-protection capacities. A successful nonviolent political transition will not only benefit not the people of Sudan, but may also act as a counterforce to the emerging violence in the region.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate action is an important part of NP`s global strategic plan for 2021-2025. As members of the Climate Action Accelerator group, NP is also committed to reduce its own carbon footprint with 50% by 2030 and replicate such actions within our spheres of influence. Sudan is NP`s first programme opened under our reduced carbon emissions strategy. NP is currently in partnership with Energy Peace Partners (EPP) that leverages climate and finance solutions to promote peace in the world's most fragile regions. NPP and EPP are looking at ways to address the intersection of energy poverty, conflict risk and climate vulnerability in Sudan. Climate change is a major factor in conflict dynamics in Sudan. Traditional mechanisms known as Judia are eroding and not functioning due to the shorter and sharper rainy season. Various organisations have set up mechanisms such as Community-Based Reconciliation Mechanisms which are attempting to build upon the traditional systems like Judia, but without addressing the relevant climate factors they are doomed to fail. The proposed action will approach protection action through a climate lens. NP is not providing any material aid and thus has a very light carbon footprint. Moreover, through the application of Unarmed Civilian Protection NP promotes and models a shift from armed to unarmed forms of direct protection. In this way it contributes to reducing the tremendous strain of the military-industrial complex on the climate, integrating the protection of people and planet.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

All project activities are designed to protect civilians and reduce violence, and thus inherently prioritize their safety. NP’s principle of ensuring the “primacy of local actors” ensures that the beneficiaries remain the central participants in all of NP’s programming, and promotes their self-protection capacities. Activities such as creating safe spaces for conducting strategic/protective accompaniments for civilians to access authorities or security actors, are prime examples of this methodology. Such an approach prioritizes the dignity of beneficiaries, treating them as partners in implementation. The proposed programming is designed to enable equal and impartial access to services. Vulnerable subsets of the community, such as women having to travel long-distances, older persons or persons with disabilities who are less able to protect themselves, minority ethnic groups who can easily be overlooked in other types of aid are the starting point of specifically tailored activities. NP also engages with community leadership and advocates for the inclusion of the concerns of these groups, or when possible representatives of these groups to be included in leadership structures. The protection mainstreaming component of the program helps to support vulnerable groups in obtaining meaningful access to assistance and services provided by other actors. NP carries out in-depth context analysis and community consultations about the impact and potential effect of our work, prior to and throughout program implementation. Field staff are trained in protection mainstreaming. Furthermore, while NP will be directly involved in ensuring the centrality of protection at the distribution, the Team will also support humanitarian actors in protection mainstreaming, such as identifying and prioritizing vulnerable individuals, analysing the areas where distribution sites are being set up for protection concerns, participating in negotiations with local authorities about distributions as well as providing technical assistance in the form of training and agreement making for inter-cluster response missions (i.e. helping partners think through the impact of and commitments to hiring casual laborers, introducing protection as a cross-cutting component, identifying the activities and indicators that might put communities at risk, etc.). Trainings will target national implementing staff and volunteers of agencies and local community structures to enhance knowledge and practical capacity in protection mainstreaming. Training curricula includes definitions of protection, international standards, including “do no harm” and principles of humanitarian aid, confidentiality, ensuring the safety and security of beneficiaries in all activities and a planning session for how agencies can improve the way protection is mainstreamed within agencies and local community structures.
