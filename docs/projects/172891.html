Project Summary

The proposed intervention links with and contributes to 2021 HRP Strategic Objective 1: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity and Strategic Objective 2: Improve vulnerable people’s access to livelihoods and life-sustaining basic services by supporting displaced populations, returnees and host communities to meet their basic needs and/or access to essential basic services in West Darfur, West Kordofan, South Kordofan, Kassala and Gedaref States. This intervention will also contribute directly to the first FSL sector strategic objectives by addressing life threatening needs and addressing life sustaining needs. In addition, it will indirectly contribute to FSL sector strategic objective 3, protection. In the five-targeted states, a lack of investment in agriculture, protracted displacements, frequent insecurity, impacts of climate change, lack of investment in social services have condemned a large number of people to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities. The continued insecurity in the state means that access to agricultural land remains limited as farmers abandoned their farms due to insecurity. The latest IPC report (Nov 2020) indicates that there are 1,715,282 persons are in need of food and livelihood security assistance. The project will support the people with agriculture, livestock, IGA, MPCA and vocational training intervention to improve food security and provide opportunities to earn incomes
This project will target 63,750 persons that include, IDPs and vulnerable host community members at a total cost of $ 4, 143,750, which is about 1% of the $426,480,000 needed to support 80% of the persons in need in 2021. The budget has been arrived at using the FSL cluster estimate of $65/beneficiary.
In the five states, the project will support vulnerable households affected by manmade and natural disasters, IDPs, returnee communities with limited access and availability of adequate, sufficient and nutritious food and small-scale farmers who face production and market constraints as projected by HNO 2021 report. There will be deliberate focus on women, beneficiaries as they are marginalized and socially excluded and thus bear the greatest burden of food insecurity.
The purpose of the project is to support food insecure households to access food, improve food production, increase access to incomes, enable households meet their basic needs and provide livelihood opportunities and strengthen their resilience to further shocks and stresses. The intervention seeks to provide food assistance and cash assistance, material support (inputs) and increase the knowledge and skills of the targeted households in agricultural production, income generation and livelihood opportunities in order to increase household food availability, improve food diversity and have diversified income and livelihood opportunities

1 - Needs

Food insecurity and malnutrition remain alarmingly high in the Sudan due to a number of factors. Firstly, as a result of the protracted conflict and displacement since 2003 that is still lingering. The situation is further compounded by the existing economic downturn and higher inflation rates, in addition to food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. The prevailing humanitarian situation deteriorated rapidly in the first six months of 2020, increasing the numbers of rising hunger across the country. In 2020, the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) estimated that more than 9.3 million people, almost a quarter of the entire population of the Sudan, are in need of humanitarian assistance; an increase of 75 percent compared with the 5.2 million in need in 2015. The humanitarian landscape in Sudan is complex and is a result of long standing protracted crises from repeated and ongoing displacements (due to conflicts, climate related shocks and stresses), poverty, the impact of covid-19, deteriorating economic situation and the influx of refugees. According to the latest IPC report update of October - December 2020, an estimated 7.1 million people, representing 16% of the total population, are expected to face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) through December 2020 and are in need of urgent action to save lives, protect livelihoods and reduce food consumption gaps. The five-targeted states have a combined population in need of 1,715,282 persons, which is 24% of the total number of people in need. In some of the localities in the states targeted, some of the people in need are already engaging in negative coping mechanisms to get food such as, increased cutting down of trees for sale as firewood and burning of charcoal, borrowing money and selling productive assets in order to access food. Levels of debt are escalating and affected populations will need to access cash as soon as possible to repay their debts and avoid increased food insecurity.The draft 2021 HNO report identifies IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities as the most vulnerable groups based on various food security assessments. The project targets those facing food crises and those in emergency situation IPC phases 3 & 4, respectively. This also includes the prevention of diseases through vaccinations.o Lack of cash to meet the most basic household needs, especially of the most vulnerable households. o Vocational training skills for employment. o Livelihood opportunities such as small business and food processing targeting women. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Objective 1: addressing Life Threatening Needs - Provide timely multi-sectoral lifesaving assistance to crises affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity.Activities: • Distribution 143 MT of short duration and drought tolerant assorted seeds of Millet, Sorghum, Sesame, vegetables, legumes and groundnuts to 5,750 farmers through seeds fairs and direct distribution based on the particular context. • Distribution of assorted hand tools (digging and weeding hoes, sickles etc.) for cultivation to 5,750 farmers through direct distribution.• Provision of MPCA to 5,150 to IDPs, People Living with Disability (PLWD), the old and terminal ill and vulnerable women. The MPCA will be based on the MEB that will be revised in 2021 and will be preceded by a market assessment. • Vocational training to 350 vulnerable youths to acquire market-driven skills for employment.• Training and capacity building of 1,850 lead farmers in improved agricultural practices, including climate smart agriculture, pest and disease control and soil fertility management. Objective 2: addressing Life Sustaining Needs - Improve vulnerable people’s access to livelihoods and life-sustaining basic services to enhance resilience • Distribution of livestock (chicken and goats) to 2,700 households through livestock fairs using vouchers.• Vaccination of 12,000 livestock, focusing mainly small livestock, goats, sheep and chicken. The vaccination of livestock will be implemented in close partnership with the department of veterinary services in the Ministry of Production and Economic Resources (MoPER)• Livelihood skills training in small business and provision of supplies to 2,650 beneficiaries upscale & start small business for income generation in urban areas. • Provision of vegetable seeds and drip irrigation kits, donkeys, donkey and hand tools to 3,150 women in Mother Support Group (MSG) to engage in small-scale irrigation schemes during the dry season.• Distribution of tree seedlings and support the establishment of tree nurseries targeting 1,900 households. The project will work closely with the department of forestry to acquire seedlings and support groups with training, seeds and equipment to establish tree nurseries. Objective 3: Protection - Mitigate protection risks and respond to protection needs through humanitarian actions • providing information to beneficiaries and staff about COVID-19, • implementing physical distancing and• taking precautions with high-risk populations.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not have direct significant impact on the environment. However, it is acknowledge that farming practices that the project seeks to support sometimes have negative impacts on the environment. For instance, in some of the localities where the project will be implemented, environmental degradation is a major concern. This is caused by cutting down of trees, poor agricultural practices and over-grazing, among other factors. The project seeks to improve agricultural techniques and practices such as soil and water management practices will be promoted. In addition, the project will promote planting of trees through distribution of seedlings and organizing the beneficiaries in groups to establish tree nurseries. The tree nurseries will become community resources to provide seedlings beyond the life of the project. Moreover, environmental education will be mainstreamed in the training of farmers and is estimated to reach 1,950 farmers. Lack of viable alternative livelihood strategies also lead to wanton cutting down of trees for firewood and charcoal and by seeking to provide agriculture and livelihood opportunities, this project will directly lead to the cutting down of trees.Climate change adaptation practices will be prompted through different climate smart agriculture practices such as the use of certified short-cycle and drought tolerant seed varieties, tree planting and drip irrigation practices among others.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of rapid context analysis, the project will identify the most common protection risks, the high risk group and then design mitigation measures. However, the project will take measures to ensure that women and children are protected and are not put in a situation that may increase their protection risks. For instance, the distribution of supplies will take place within the communities, awareness raising will be done, MPCA will involve both men and women. Lack of food in the household can sometimes lead to protection risks such as GBV and child labour and by increasing availability and access to food and livelihood opportunities, the project will indirectly be reducing these risks.
