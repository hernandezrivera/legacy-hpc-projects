Project Summary

This project will support the delivery of quality, integrated child protection and psychosocial support (PSS) services to the most vulnerable boys and girls aged 4-18 in Gaza Strip, especially those affected by the May 2021 escalation, as well as COVID-19. The escalation has affected all aspects of life in the Gaza Strip, including child protection needs and services.
The project will ensure that 12 family centres and 30 outreach sites in Gaza are fully operational, responding to the pressing mental health and psychosocial and child protection needs of 23,440 children (55% girls) and 5,480 caregivers (77% women).
Service delivery will be directed to the most affected locations and the most vulnerable children through a coordinated continuum of services in Gaza. Each family centre will be connected to a community-based child protection committee that informs the family centre of community child protection needs and acts as a focal point for identifying vulnerable children for referral. Family centres will operate through established Community-Based Organisations (CBOs) and outreach sites and will assess/screen and refer children for further services through:
1) a case management approach for the worst affected;
2) referral of complex child protection cases to child protection services (relevant stakeholders and Civil Society Organizations (CSOs); and
3) referral of children with further psychosocial support needs to more specialized psychological support services.
Services will also strengthen outreach capacities to detect and link up children in need with service providers while providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) through the family centres. A total of 23,440 children (55% girls) and 5,480 caregivers (77% women) will be reached through the project.
This project aligns with and contributes to the Protection Cluster Objective Two, which calls for putting in place protection response mechanisms to prevent and mitigate occupation and conflict-related violence.
Ensuring compliance with PSEA principles and procedures, project beneficiaries will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse. They will receive information on UNICEF email and other interagency channels, including SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp to report on PSEA.
At least 70% of the project budget will be split equally between the two project implementing partners to ensure timely services are provided to children with the protection and MHPSS needs. The money will be transferred in line with UNICEF Harmonised Approach to Cash Transfer (HACT), which ensures close follow-up on expenditures, where only project-related activities are covered by the fund. Partners are normally micro-assessed and spot checks are regularly conducted to ensure compliance with the agreement.

1 - Needs

The May 2021 escalation resulted in large-scale loss and human suffering, aggravating the already fragile situation preceding the conflict. 242 Palestinians, including 67 children (44% boys), were killed, and 2,200 people were reportedly injured (685 children). The escalation significantly impacted the mental health and wellbeing of children and families. Displaced families, persons with disabilities, and GBV survivors are exposed to increased threats of psychological distress. The Child Protection Area of Responsibility estimates that some 678,000 children (345,800 boys, 332,200 girls) across the State of Palestine are in need of child protection and MHPSS, including 122,000 (80,0000 in Gaza) in need of focused non-specialized support or specialized services. More than half (53%) of all children in Gaza are in need. A Terre des Hommes assessment conducted post-escalation found mental health to be the second major issue, with high levels of distress evident across society, especially for women and girls. The assessment noted the heightened level of anxiety, fear, insecurity, and lack of sleep, and recommended providing MHPSS support to children, families, teachers, and frontline workers. Other recommendations included raising awareness of caregivers on positive parenting practices during emergencies and conflicts, responding to MHPSS needs of men and boys, and providing case management (CM) to children with protection concerns. Boys and girls whose primary caregivers were hospitalized or died due to the escalation may also face neglect or lack of support, particularly emotional, particularly young ones from fellow household members. Orphan boys and girls remain among the most vulnerable. In Gaza children’s exposure to violence remains widespread with some 73 per cent boys and 63 per cent girls aged 1-14 experiencing physical punishment. Out of this, 23 percent boys and 17 per cent girls experienced severe physical violence by their caregivers (MICS 2020). Around 77.6 per cent of the children violently disciplined were between 3-9 years of age. On the child labor front, 7.6 per cent of Gazan children aged 5-11 (12% boys, 5.5 % girls) were involved in economic activities for at least one hour in the past week of the study, while 11.9 per cent children aged 12-14 years (17% boys, 9.5% girls) were involved in economic activity less than 14 hours a week. This percentage goes up to 16.9 per cent for children aged 15-17 (28.5per cent boys, 6.6 per cent girls) working 43 hours over one-week period. On the child marriage front, of women aged 14-49 in Gaza , 2.3 per cent were married before the age of 15, while 24.4 per cent were married before the age of 18 (MICS 2020). The COVID-19 crisis has further undermined the humanitarian situation already affected by a longstanding closure with intensified access and movement restrictions, recurrent violence including the aftermath of the Great March of Return demonstrations, ongoing political tensions, and fuel, water, and electricity crises. According to the SAWA helpline, suicide attempts increased during COVID-19 by more than 400% mainly in Gaza, which on its own counted 33 cases of girls. Men and boys face higher threats when it comes to the risks of the Explosive Remnants of War (ERW). The proposed project aims to address protection, resilience, and PSS needs of children affected by the recent escalations, through the provision of case management and referral to available services (such as education, social protection, WASH, etc.), psychological first aid, group, and individual counseling, life skills education, in addition to other indirect briefing and awareness-raising on positive parenting and protection from ERWs. Case management of CP cases are undertaken by trained case managers, who assess the needs and address child protection risks. Recommended response varies depending on risk level, with cases referred to the Ministry of Social Development or family centres as per Gaza referral pathways. Unless critically under-resourced CP services are funded, children will remain at significant risk. Between January and September 2021, Family centres reached 48% and 70% of all Protection Cluster child beneficiaries for PSS and CM in Gaza respectively, reflecting their vital role. However, intermittent funding to the family centres limits their capacity to meet the spikes in needs. Beneficiary consultations will be undertaken at various stages of the project to ensure their participation in the planning, design, implementation, and evaluation of the project; and information about the channels to report on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be communicated to the beneficiaries, to ensure compliance with accountability to affected population principles and prevention and response to SEA.

2 - Activities

The proposed project aims to address the protection and PSS needs of children and caregivers, mainly those affected by the May 2021 escalation. The project covers activities for children including the provision of case management and referral to available services (such as education, social protection, WASH, etc.), psychological first aid, group, and individual counseling, as well as other indirect debriefing activities such as expressive arts. The project also includes activities that aim to build the resilience of children and increase their self-worth including life skills education and child-parent interaction programs. Caregivers will also receive awareness-raising on child protection and positive parenting. Awareness-raising ERW risk education will also be provided to both boys and girls and male and female caregivers. The project fits under the second strategic objective of the Humanitarian Response Plan; ensure acutely vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank have access to essential services; and the Protection Cluster specific objectives of: 1. Delivery of emergency responses to mitigate effects of violence, especially for children and vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities.2. Reducing deaths and injuries caused by ERWs. 3. Individual protection needs of both girls and boys (4-18), especially adolescents aged 14-17, will be addressed. through, but not limited to, case management of children affected by PSS distress, domestic violence, and child labor. Priority will be given to support children impacted by the May escalation. In-house counseling manuals developed by the partners and reviewed by UNICEF will be used and the I-deal manual will be used for life skills. Case management will help address the gender-specific needs of boys and girls, in particular older boys affected by child labor, and younger age boys and girls subject to PSS distress, neglect, violence, and harmful practices. To support children with Disabilities (CWD), implementing partners will target them in coordination with disability organizations they outreach to or partner with. In 2021, UNICEF supported more than 350 children with disabilities through these twelve centres. During COVID-19 lockdowns, activities were conducted remotely. This modality has been used in the family centres in 2020 and 2021 and has shown success in attracting children to the centres’ activities. Special attention will be paid towards CWD to ensure their smooth access to the centers, taking into consideration physical distancing to minimize close interaction with others and allow access to hygiene materials including hand sanitizers to minimize their need to go to the washrooms. In 2021, children coming from vulnerable families with no access to the internet were reached through home visits, involving the distribution of recreational and awareness kits for use by parents with their children. Project outputs and activities include: Output 1: Holistic child protection services equally provided to boys and girls aged 4-18 through 12 family Centres. Activity 1.1: Provide structured child protection and psychosocial support programs, especially for children with disabilities. Activity 1.2: Provide case management and referral to appropriate services (including but not limited to social protection, WASH, health, and education services) for boys and girls with protection needs. Priority will be given to children who have been directly affected by May 2021 escalation through coordination with MoSD, especially those who lost caregivers. Output 2: Integrated child protection services supported to strengthen parental capacity to respond to children's needs, including those with disabilities. Activity 2.1: Organise awareness-raising sessions for male and female parents on gender-specific child protection issues and positive parenting (including a session on ERW awareness). Output 3: Capacity for professionals working on MHPSS support strengthened. Activity 3.1: Conduct training on child protection and MHPSS guidelines for field and emergency workers including non-CP professionals. UNICEF's two implementing partners will support the Children MHPSS need assessment following the May escalation. They will contribute to the implementation of the community engagement activities and provide technical support during the design of the activities. UNICEF partners will monitor and supervise the progress of the works daily and share issues and concerns with UNICEF. UNICEF developed an online platform for monitoring the projects to oversee the implementation closely. All partners have been trained and have to upload the information in the system. The partners will coordinate responses with each other, the Ministry of Social Development and other members of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility, and MHPSS and GBV Working Groups. Information about the channels to report on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) will be communicated to all project beneficiaries, to ensure compliance with accountability to affected population principles and prevention and response to SEA. All 12 family centers will be covered by PSEA communication materials including how to report SEA-related complaints.

3 - Indicators

In order to track project deliverables, the following indicators will be used; • Indicator 1.1: Number of children (50% boys and 50% girls), including the injured, and children with disabilities aged between 4-18 benefiting from structured child protection interventions including life skills, group and individual counselling, and child-parent interaction programs by December 2022 (Target 20,560, 50% girls, including 500 CWD). • Indicator 1.2: Number of children (45% boys and 55% girls), including those affected by the May escalation including children with disabilities, benefiting from case management support by December 2022 (Target 2,880 including 60 CWD). • Indicator 2.1 Number of caregivers (20% males) benefiting from awareness-raising on child protection & positive parentingand community initiatives by December 2022 (Target 5,480, 77% Women, 100 people with disabilities) • Indicator 2.2 Number of children and caregivers benefiting from awareness sessions on ERW risk education by December 2022 (Target 10,000, 70% females, 100 people with disabilities) • Indicator 3.1 Number of MHPSS and child protection workers and non-protection professionals (50% females) trained on child protection and /or guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support by December 2022 (Target 100, 10 working with people with disabilities). UNICEF and its implementing partners will closely follow up on the project implementation on daily basis to ensure timely and effective execution of project activities. Field visits will be organized by the partner project management staff to family centers, ensuring they receive at least one visit weekly, helping to capture challenges and delays to ensure they are timely addressed The partner will be requested to develop the family centers monitoring and evaluation framework which us normally checked the followed up by UNICEF Monitoring and evaluation officer, as well as the child protection officers. Monitoring procedures include regular programme visits to the field where implementation progress, achievements of results and means of verification are systematically checked against the agreed framework including the projects indicators. Recommendations in response to the field visit findings, including bottlenecks are documented and systematically followed up on. UNICEF will ensure monitoring and evaluation of the project on the basis of outputs delivered, including the number of referrals to emergency services, child protection case management, and psychosocial support services. Monitoring the adherence of serviceproviders to the case management standard operating procedures will also be included, as well as the use of resilience-building approaches. The project will be monitored using the UNICEF regular monitoring framework. Project implementation will be reviewed as part of the Annual Programme Review jointly conducted by UNICEF and project implementing partners. Regular field visits to project sites will be conducted, where feasible considering COVID-19 restrictions. Beneficiaries will be asked if they have received any orientations on PSEA and methods they can use to report abuse. Also, UNICEF and its partners will abide by the three mandatory indicators on PSEA.Partners will submit quarterly progress reports as a component of the project monitoring mechanism. In addition, UNICEF will provide technical support to partners through regular coordination. Partners will assess benefits to children, caregivers, and professionals through pre-and post-questionnaires, as well as focus group discussions. UNICEF is committed to reporting towards the Humanitarian Response Plan periodic reporting and the updating of the OCHA Financial Tracking System.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The safety of children, caregivers, and family centres’ staff will be ensured through the selection of safe and child-friendly spaces. In the context of COVID-19, hygiene materials related to disinfection of the workplace and personal hygiene in line with WHO guidelines will be allowed in the budget. To ensure the dignity of beneficiaries, staff will be observing the way they approach beneficiaries, which will respect their privacy and ensure the confidential handling of their information. Additionally, the familycentres will use context-appropriate manuals for life skills education and group counseling. The child protection case management Standard Operating Procedures will govern the work of the case managers in the family centres, which prioritize the needs and the best interest of the child and takes into consideration their holistic welfare and protection without compromising their safety. The principles of inclusion of children with disabilities will also be taken into consideration when designing the various family centres' activities. Within the children corners and caregivers awareness-raising sessions, all project beneficiaries will be oriented on sexual exploitation and abuse and will receive information on UNICEF email to report on PSEA. They will also be made aware of the other interagency channels including the SAWA Free toll number, social media platforms, and WhatsApp.UNICEF safeguards beneficiaries’ rights to safe and dignified interventions. Consent of beneficiaries is usually considered and obtained through partners and implementing agencies joining training and community interventions. UNICEF ensures applying international standards, for working with children. UNICEF developed feedback mechanisms from beneficiaries to ensure accountability toward affected populations and to mitigate any risks identified by end-users, including SEA. UNICEF also conducts end-user monitoring field visits to ensure the safety and quality of services and supplies provided.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Individual protection needs of both girls and boys (4-18), especially those directly affected by the May 2021 escalation and COVID-19 crisis, and its repercussions on families’ protection and wellbeing will be addressed through the project. This will be done through the provision of psychosocial support services to girls and boys impacted by the escalation, as well as case management of female children who might be affected by violence and early marriage and boys affected by violence, child labor, and injuries by Explosive Remnants of War (ERW). Both men and women will be targeted through the project, making sure that they understand their contribution to their children’s wellbeing and ensuring their safety and protection, especially during a lockdown. Both male and female case managers will be available to provide support either face to face or remotely over the phone or through social media platforms.
