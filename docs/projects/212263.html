Project Summary

In 2024, a staggering 16.7 million people in Syria, comprising 8.4 million females and 8.3 million males, require humanitarian assistance, witnessing a rise from the 15.3 million recorded in 2023. This dire situation is exacerbated by the aftermath of a series of earthquakes in February 2023, causing significant displacement, loss of lives, and severe damage to already fragile infrastructures. HNO for 2024 reveals a critical scenario where 5.5 million people, including over 2 million residing in last resort sites, are displaced. The earthquakes resulted in nearly 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 injuries in Syria, further straining a population already grappling with prolonged hostilities and economic crises. Families lost breadwinners, adding to the vulnerability of millions unable to meet basic needs. SIF's proposed interventions directly align with the Shelter Sector's objectives 1 and 2 in Lattakia, Homs, Daraa and Aleppo, focusing on both accessibility and the critical need for shelter assistance post-including earthquake. Proposed locations were identified according to their level of accessibility, SIF accessibility and inter-sector severity of needs (2 to 3), targeted locations were strategically chosen based on SIF's multisector and multiyear approach, aiming to enhance sustainability, community self-reliance, and resilience. Through consultations with local communities and authorities, SIF aims to directly address the urgent needs identified by affected populations. Targeting the rehabilitation of 225 damaged houses in Lattakia and Daraa (benefiting 1,125 People in Needs) and carrying minor repair for 150 houses in Aleppo and Homs (benefiting 850 PiN), the intervention is tailored to restore normalcy and offer comprehensive support beyond individual housing units. Customized interventions, informed by social and technical assessments, will address specific needs identified by SIF's MEAL and technical teams. For damaged houses, this involves installing doors, windows, locks, and fixing WASH facilities and light electrical repairs. The standardized Shelter Packages include doors, windows, and basic WASH kits, targeting cost reduction and easing approval processes, especially for those lacking Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) documents.
The proposed project complements the activities of SIF and its partners in the target regions (including those with UN partners in 2024), particularly in the WASH (water reservoirs and sewage system rehabilitation), FSA (irrigation canal and agriculture kits distribution) and Education sectors (school rehabilitation and NFE).
The inclusivity of Persons with Disabilities (PwD) in the interventions, coupled with a focus on local capacities and community empowerment, exemplifies SIF's commitment to creating sustainable solutions. By encouraging contractors to recruit locally, SIF not only enhances the effectiveness of its intervention but also fosters a sense of ownership and collaboration within the communities served. Guided by safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach and adherence to SPHERE standards, SIF ensures an ethical and effective project cycle. SIF's commitment to humanitarian principles ensures accountability, transparency, and community empowerment.
SIF will implement this project in close coordination with the MoLAE, Shelter sector, NGOs, SARC, local committees in order to bridge gaps in available services and ensure better access of People in Needs to services. SIF will closely follow Shelter Sector guidelines. In 2023, SIF targeted 15,635 individuals through shelter interventions.
In 2025, SIF plans to expand its operations to address rising humanitarian needs in new areas. Committed to identifying and responding to urgent needs in communities facing significant challenges, this expansion aligns with SIF's core mission of providing timely and impactful assistance to vulnerable populations, ensuring no one is left without support.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

SIF will submit project approval through the sector. Selection of locations and PoCs will be conducted in collaboration with sector, donors, SARC and municipalities. Selection of beneficiaries will be based on sector criteria. Results of the technical assessments conducted by SIF in selected households will be endorsed by an appointed Committee composed of representatives from Municipality, governorate and SIF. The Committee will be also responsible of monitoring the implementation of activities and issuing handovers documents. SIF will rely on a contractor to carry out the rehabilitation work selected based on SIF SoP which include a transparent and objective tender process. To ensure sustainability, a guarantee period will be provided to each household in case some repairs are needed after implementation. Maintaining transparency and inclusivity, SIF engages continuously with affected populations through participatory approaches, ensuring structured dialogues with diverse groups to address protection concerns. Regular feedback mechanisms, community consultations, and grievance redressal processes are integral to SIF's accountability framework, incorporating community needs into project planning and implementation, fostering ownership and empowerment among beneficiaries. The risk of sexual exploitation is addressed by implementing comprehensive measures. These include integrating PSEA indicators into the monitoring framework, conducting rigorous vetting during recruitment with a focus on application and reference stages, ensuring the signing of a code of conduct that strictly prohibits SEA for all individuals associated with SIF, including staff, volunteers, and contractors. Additionally, contractors sign the SIF Code of Conduct at the time of the purchase order. SIF employees undergo PSEA training, and each targeted governorate has a designated PSEA focal point. Awareness sessions for community members cover the definition of SEA and reporting/whistleblowing channels. Community-based complaint mechanisms are accessible in all response locations, and SIF has developed a structured process, steps, and timeframe for the investigation of SEA allegations. SIF faces various risks, each with a likelihood, impact, and mitigation strategy. Measures include dialogue and follow-up to mitigate delays in local authority approvals, donor coordination and BoQ adjustments to manage inflation-driven price hikes, monitoring and preparedness to address security deterioration risks, financial controls and beneficiary verification to mitigate fraud and aid diversion risks, prevention measures and testing to address COVID-19 and cholera spread risks, and monitoring and planning to mitigate fuel shortage risks.
