Project Summary

194,000 children with moderate acute wasting and 51,000 children with severe acute wasting are at risk of deterioration and death. Children with severe wasting are 12 times more likely to die than healthy children (HNO 2022-2023). Recent data by MOH reveal that12% of CU5 are stunted. The national prevalence of acute malnutrition in pregnant and lactating women, based on Mid Upper-Arm Circumference is 4.2%, which is 2.5 times higher than children with acute malnutrition, and highlights the vulnerabilities of PLWs in Syria, particularly to poverty, inadequate diet, heavy workload, and harmful coping mechanisms such as prioritization of food for children. Mothers need outreach support to properly care for the newborn at home, including feeding practices for infants and young children. Adolescent girls are particularly vulnerable in the northeast and northwest where early marriages are common. The project aims at improving equitable, safe and dignified access to nutrition services targeting PLWs and U5 children by strengthening the local institutional primary healthcare and activating community-based initiatives. Emphasis will be placed on ensuring a comprehensive approach to primary care services including malnutrition assessment, prevention and management integrated with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). AAH will implement a two-pronged approach to meet the priority needs focused on immediate lifesaving support together with sustainability efforts, integrating nutrition services strengthening and accessibility to ensure local capacity can meet the different needs of women, men, girls, and boys and people with disability. Five governorates are on AAH’s map for 2022 and 2023. Aleppo, Hama, Hasakeh, Idleb and potentially Dar’a. Aiming at increasing sustain access to quality nutrition services in rural areas, AAH will identify rehabilitations of health facilities to reinstate safe and dignified condition of services. COVID-19 Infection prevention and control measures will be promoted in the supported facilities. Mobile medical service will also be mobilized to reach beneficiaries with primary healthcare and nutrition services in hard-to-reach areas. Adolescents and young mothers visiting health centers will benefit from IYCF and MHPSS services and participate in recreational activities for an integrated mental and maternal health response. Gender responsive counselling will be promoted, while applying gender lens through all activities, by understanding the context and building on the existing gender roles. Capacity building and on-the-job training will be implemented for healthcare staff to ensure implementation of national protocols and guidelines consistent with quality nutrition care delivery. Finally, AAH will integrate community-based mental health, psychosocial, maternal nutrition, CMAM and IYCF support provided through community health workers and women’s groups. A comprehensive MEAL plan will support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with proposed interventions and is undertaken directly by MEAL in collaboration with health and nutrition department. Systematic collection of information on risk environment, gender roles and responsibilities, gender relations and decision-making dynamics, access to and control of resources, and social norms will be done with a conflict sensitivity approach. As part of our accountability towards affected population, qualitative interviews and satisfaction surveys will complement monitoring activities. AAH will ensure that there is a gate for feedback in every activity whether through focus group discussions, key informant interviews and hotlines, through program cycle. AAP is an integral part of the response to ensure community voices are at the core of the response. The total beneficiaries targeted includes activities not featuring in the provided sector's list.

4 - Needs

With 14% increase in PiN from HNO 2021, nutrition needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient and inequitable access to services due to a combination of fuel and transportation shortages, damage of health facilities and insufficient human resources. Findings from AAH assessments revealed an increased vulnerability to disease outbreaks, especially in Northeast Syria, a serious scarcity of resources and negligible presence of health actors in key areas of intervention covering a large catchment population. Rural areas in Hama and Aleppo are at particular risk for nutrition insecurity due to the volatile economic and hardship situation and reduced funding interest. While HNO 2021 reveals that in North-west Syria, close to 60% of children of the age 6-24 months are not receiving diets of acceptable quantity and diversity, in Hassakeh alone, results of mobile malnutrition screening by AAH teams were alarming with 227 cases of CU5 were diagnosed with MAM and 68 with SAM out of 459 total CU5 screened since September2021. Additionally, 208 MAM cases were reported in PLWs from a total of 428 PLWs screened. AAH assessments also revealed that the risk environment is creating suboptimal community engagement, especially in Idleb, with rampant poverty, limited access to healthcare resources, stigma and transportation challenges all impacting communities’ ability to engage in health and nutrition care, and women’s health seeking behavior and decision-making as well.

5 - Implementation of activities

AAH will activate community structures targeting PLW and CU5 for meaningful access to public services to prevent undernutrition and restore essential health systems. Capacity building will be provided to scale up early identification of malnutrition through community-based and family-based MUAC screening. Referral of detected cases for treatment will be facilitated through supported CMAM clinics. Mobile medical services will be activated to provide health and nutrition services in hard-to-reach communities. Micronutrient supplementation will be provided through the supported facilities for anemia prevention and management. AAH will strengthen awareness on integrated MHPSS-IYCF practices by supporting provision of consultations at PHCs and community levels. On-the-job training will be applied to ensure implementation of guidelines consistent with quality care delivery and adequate reporting. To ensure sustainability, activities will be integrated with comprehensive WaSH and Health interventions, including hygiene promotion, provision of medicines, and rehabilitation of facilities. Periodic satisfaction surveys and beneficiary feedback will be collected during activity monitoring to ensure accountability towards affected populations. Beneficiary data will be disaggregated by sex, age, and marginalized groups, including disabilities. A comprehensive MEAL plan will support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with proposed interventions.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: nutrition facilities could be attacked. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: apply the designated de-confliction mechanism for health and nutrition facilities. Continue reporting attacks against health and nutrition facilities. Continue advocacy efforts with all parties. Risk: gender inequality and GBV. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: train all IYCF counsellors on how to supportively engage with GBV survivors and provide information in an ethical, safe and confidential manner. Ensure referral mechanisms to specialized GBV support, health care, MHPSS, and child protection. Ensure locations, times and methods of nutrition services are safe and accessible for women and other at-risk groups. Risk: not equal provision of and access to nutrition services for vulnerable groups. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age and disability. Implementation of nutrition services in line with global and nutrition sector guidance, including improved quality reporting and monitoring. Review existing informed consent procedures. Ensure that complaints and feedback mechanisms are accessible and facilitate inputs for all persons.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: children receiving nutrition services are over-exposed to the risk of harm by nutrition personnel, organization staff or related entities. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place, regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed. Sufficient safe, clean and sex segregated bathroom facilities should be provided. Risk: aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies in place, practiced and monitored. Polices address risks at the organizational level and the program level. The organization’s staff and communities have access to safe/effective reporting systems. Risk: any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc.). Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: compulsory trainings on organizational ethical policies. AAH mission has a policy for PSEA and a reference person against harassment at country level.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: security situation allows safe response; access to target areas and beneficiaries is granted; partners are willing to collaborate on timely manner; availability of suppliers and items in the market, prices remain stable; no major displacements occur. Risk: security situation is unstable and/or worsens, AAH has no access; there are additional administrative procedures imposed on D-INGOs, and restrictions on access to beneﬁciaries; lengthy processes to obtain approvals. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AAH coordinates with local authorities, Ministries, and stakeholders to monitor the situation, to maintain access to target areas and beneﬁciaries, to develop relationships and mitigate restrictions. Security rules and plans are in place and regularly reviewed. Re-planning of activities to recover eventual delays. Advocacy and coordination with the humanitarian community. Risk: significant devaluation of EURO/ USD against SYP; International bank transfers are not ﬂuid and timely. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: signiﬁcant changes in the value of the Syrian currency will be monitored and, if materialized, communicated to donors and options discussed. Risk: aid diversion, financial fraud, and corruption. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: strict policies and procedures in place for procurement, financial management and risk management; internal controls set up. Monthly monitoring of financial spending.
