Project Summary

Responding to WASH Needs in Gaza is a project that Anera and its local partner, Youth Empowerment Center (YEC), will implement in partnership with the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA), the Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU), and the WASH Cluster. The project responds to the WASH Cluster’s Strategic Objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.” The project will also respond to Strategic Objective 3: “The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crises, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.”
The WASH sector in Gaza is one of the most severely deteriorated sectors. Over two million people face grave challenges and limited accessibility to WASH services at the community and household (HH) level, which creates serious environmental threats including health hazards and pollution.
In response to these challenges, Anera works with local partners to identify gaps in the WASH sector and address needs on the community and HH levels to enhance access to sustainable and clean water and sanitation services to improve overall health and wellbeing. The project will serve 150,660 individuals in different part of Gaza and will include the following interventions:
Installation of wastewater networks in different neighborhoods in Al Karama area - Western Jabalia,
Installation of wastewater networks in different neighborhoods in Eibad Alrahman - Western Jabalia,
Upgrade of water networks in different neighborhoods in Al Karama area - Western Jabalia,
Upgrade of water networks in different neighborhoods in Eibad Alrahman - Western Jabalia,
Installation of stormwater network at Al Attatra area - Western Beit Lahia,
Rehabilitation and upgrading of the pumping stations facilities in Khan Younis,
Rehabilitation and upgrading of two municipal waters in Al Saffa Complex il al _Shijaiah.
Awareness program for 1,000 women from the most vulnerable HHs on hygiene and health standards.
Anera’s team, through its local partner, YEC, and in cooperation with the relevant stakeholders, will conduct assessments of identified target areas through field visits during the first two months of the project period to identify and approve the exact rehabilitation needs of each area in terms of necessary repair/construction works and materials to support their connection to WASH facilities. Anera and YEC will review and confirm HH beneficiary numbers to identify the cost of HH connections. Anera’s team will review selected project locations and cross-check them with the relevant key players in the WASH interventions to ensure they are not receiving duplicate assistance from other parties.

1 - Needs

The ongoing blockade on Gaza created a compounding negative effect on the development and the capacity of several sectors. Most local institutions lack the financial and operational capacity to provide basic services to Gaza’s population. The WASH sector in Gaza is one of the most severely deteriorated sectors. Over two million people face grave challenges and limited accessibility to WASH services at the community and household level, which creates serious environmental threats including health hazards and pollution. According to the UN WASH Cluster, the longstanding closure of Gaza significantly impacts the technical capacities of WASH service providers due to restrictions on the entry of construction material and tools into Gaza, which impedes the sector from operating at the scale required to meet the needs of the growing population.The hostilities on Gaza in May, 2021 resulted in widespread destruction that exacerbated Gaza’s chronic infrastructure and electricity struggles. Consequently, some infrastructure damage from the conflict resulted in a decrease of clean water and sewage treatment services and power shortages ranging from 18-20 hours a day. According to UNICEF’s most recent evaluation, only 10% of Gazans have access to safe drinking water. The water in Gaza is extremely polluted, and around 98% of the groundwater is unfit for drinking. Purchasing tankered water is often the only option for accessing clean water in Gaza, but due to the increased poverty levels, most people cannot afford to do so, forcing them to consume polluted water. Wastewater networks in Gaza are particularly old and damaged. Poor wastewater infrastructure and import restrictions on construction material resulted in old and exhausted sewage networks across most of Gaza. Financial challenges prohibit local institutions from upgrading wastewater networks to meet the needs of the growing population. As a result, the poor infrastructure affected the transmission of sewage to treatment plants and increased the amount of sewage discharged into the sea. On the community level, lack of access to wastewater networks and household connections to the networks increases the risk of sewage flooding and contamination, which creates health and environmental hazards.American Near East Refugee Aid (ANERA) and the Youth Empowerment Center (YEC), in coordination with CMWU, have conducted a rapid assessment in order to identify the water sanitation and hygiene issues that were identified as follows:• 30% of residents of Western Jabalia have no sewage networks, including scattered neighborhoods or streets in El Karama and Eibad El Rahman areas that located in western area in Jabalia city.• There is no sewage network in some side streets and neighborhoods in the targeted area, in addition to some street that have old sewage lines that need to be resized and replaced.• An increase in the number of cesspits and septic tanks in the neighborhoods that lack sewage networks. • The water network in some side streets and neighborhoods in the targeted area, in addition of some streets have old steel water lines that need to be resized and replaced. • There are neighborhoods that are affected by floods, which results in property damage and prevents movement especially in El Karama Area. • The poor WASH infrastructure leads to pollution of the aquifer water, increase diseases, especially for children and the elderly because of the increase of the septic tanks, impact on movement especially in winter season, an increase in expenses due to the difficulty of obtaining and purchasing water from suppliers and soil collapse in some areas as a result of water leakage in the water old lines.Suggested neighborhoods and streets in need of sewage networks and water networks include El-Ashqar Boora line , El-Shanti street , El-Saftawi Street , El-Safina Street , Qassem street – western , Gaood street – western , El -Rawda street , Laborer Union street , Artists neighborhood , Abu Amra neighborhood , El Shanti street - Abu Naji neighborhood , Abu Ouda street and Nasseba School street.Suggested neighborhoods and streets in need of rehabilitated water networks include El Shanti neighborhood, Ouda Block, Potato neighborhood, El Boshra neighborhood, Ajour neighborhood, Ziad neighborhood, El Shantawi neighborhood and El Helou western neighborhood.During the rainy winter season, the western side areas of Beit Lahia including Al Attar area suffer from continuous flooding with rainwater coming from the eastern side of Beit Lahia, and the accumulated stormwater often reaches 50-70 cm high and floods the main streets, houses and other properties. The Coastal Municipalities Water Utility (CMWU) uses mobile pumps to reduce the amount of water, but this is expensive, timely and often ineffective. Installing a storm water network will prevent flooding and carry all surface drainage by underground pipeline to the existing stormwater basin. The CMWU identified pump stations facilities in Khan Younis governorate that needed for upgrading works including replacing the old and damaged electro mechanic fittings. Anera has successfully secured the find to conduct maintenance work including electro-mechanical, civil and electrical works. In response to these challenges, Anera works with local partners to identify critical gaps in the WASH sector and address needs on the community and household levels.

2 - Activities

2.1. Beneficiary Selection: Anera and its local partner, YEC, will review the final list of areas of intervention with the CMWU, PWA, and WASH Cluster. 2.2. Procurement:. Anera’s team will identify the bill of quantities (BOQ) and technical specifications for procured material, which will be verified with the relevant partners. The team will select the contractors based on a transparent bidding process based on Anera's procurement policy. 2.3 Training on PSEA: Anera will conduct training on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) for relevant partners and contractors.2.4 Rehabilitation Work: 2.4.1 Installation of Wastewater Networks in Different Neighborhoods in the Al Karama Area - Western Jabalia: The installation will include installing UPVC sewage pipelines with different diameters, installing main concrete manholes, and installing house connections. 2.4.2 Installation of Wastewater Networks in Different Neighborhoods in Eibad Alrahman - Western Jabalia: The project will install UPVC sewage pipelines with different diameters, install main concrete manholes, and install house connections. 2.4.3: Upgrade Water Networks in Different Neighborhoods in the Al Karama area - Western Jabalia: The project will install UPVC water pipelines with a 4 inch diameter, install water manholes including gate valves and other fittings, and install 3/4 inch house connections. 2.4.4 Upgrade Water Networks in Different Neighborhoods in Eibad Alrahman - Western Jabalia: The identified urgent interventions include installing UPVC water pipelines with a 4 inch diameter, installing water manholes including gate valves and other fittings, and installing 3/4 inch house connections. 2.4.5 Installation of a Stormwater Network in the Al Attatra Area - Western Beit Lahia: The CMWU uses mobile pumps to reduce the amount of stormwater in this area, but this is expensive, timely, and often ineffective. Installing a stormwater network will prevent flooding and carry all surface drainage by underground pipeline to the existing stormwater basin. The identified intervention includes installing concrete pipes with a 100 or 120 cm diameter, installing main concrete manholes and gullies, and reinstatement works for the roads. 2.4.6 Rehabilitation and Upgrading of the Pumping Station Facilities in Khan Younis governorate: The CMWU identified pump station facilities in JKhan Younis that need upgraded works including replacing the old and damaged electro mechanic fittings. The project team will conduct the final technical assessment to identify the detailed interventions during project implementation to conduct the works for the targeted facilities. 2.4.7 Rehabilitation and Upgrading of the Al-Saffa water well complex in Gaza City. 2.5. Monitoring and Evaluation: Anera ensures the quality and quantity of repair works to each beneficiary through follow-up and assessment visits to ensure that HHs have received the needed services to support their living conditions and dignity. During the first month of the project, Anera will prepare a monitoring matrix, where indicators at every level of the results chain and means of verification will be identified, as well as the frequency and responsibility of measuring indicators. 2.6 Handover: The local councils will sign the handing over certificates after the contractor completes the works with the approval of Anera, PWA, the CMWU, and YEC, which Anera will share with the WASH Cluster and relevant authorities. The municipality will issue a commitment for the regular running and maintenance of the renovated/new networks. 2.7 Evaluation: Anera will prepare a pre and post questionnaire to measure the needs according to the WASH standards, socio-economic conditions of the HHs, and eligibility of HHs against the cluster standards. The post questionnaire will also measure the quality of work and satisfaction of beneficiaries with the quality of work and the improvement in their living conditions.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

To be Anera puts in place complaint mechanisms for beneficiaries to provide feedback and address concerns throughout the project period. Anera informs its partners and beneficiaries of the available mechanisms and how to access them in the initial phase of the project. Complaint mechanisms will be made available to beneficiaries through three methods that include: 1. direct line (text or call) to the local partner organization which identified them for the project, 2. Direct line to the Project Coordinator at Anera, and 3. Locked complaint box at partner organizations and Anera where beneficiaries can submit complaints anonymously and confidentially. The first two methods are the most useful for registering a complaint or asking questions as they are immediate and can be dealt with in real time, whereas the third method of written complaints will be responded to within 48 hours of receiving them. Anera’s Whistleblower Policy provides further protections for beneficiaries, staff, and contractors who express concerns in need of being addressed. Anera will oversee the collection and analysis for each complaint. Additionally, all feedback and complaints are considered to be confidential. In order to submit feedback or complaints, individuals are encouraged to contact Anera’s outside counsel, as posted on our website (https://www.anera.org/whistleblower-policy/).

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Anera’s Accountability to Affected Populations (Operational Framework) summarizes the key concepts for making programming at the field level more accountable to affected populations. Anera is committed to strengthening its accountability to affected populations by committing to the following principles: program design and implementation are implemented in collaboration and with the support of the local community; conflict and gender sensitivity; the implementation of child protection policy; and producers and systems to address fraud and corruption risks. Prior to implementing any program, Anera works with local communities and families to discuss the challenges and needs in the community. Anera hires its staff from the local community where it operates and has strong networks that help to provide information, identify participants, and inform program design and methodology. Anera’s team selects its program participants and beneficiaries in consultation with community members and leaders. In addition, it selects many programs, especially infrastructure projects, in partnership with the local government and relevant ministries. Anera uses a mapping process to identify the most vulnerable communities lacking access to needed services. This mapping process pays special attention to women, youth, and marginalized/special needs members of the community as a main element in project development, planning and design, implementation, and function. Anera puts in place complaint mechanisms for all beneficiaries to provide feedback and address concerns throughout the project period.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Anera’s “Do No Harm” policies are woven throughout all of its program designs based on its principles of Accountability to Affected Populations document. For this project, Anera ensures that all of its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves and monitors access and accountability from the project planning phase to implementation and monitoring, through regular field visits and assessments. This includes ensuring adequate representation of vulnerable groups in the project design and monitoring. All Anera staff, including project staff, have been trained on PSEA to ensure gender-sensitivity, protection, and detection of abuse or gender violence. Anera operates a hotline for complaints and issues from beneficiaries, stakeholders, partner staff, or other concerned parties, which are dealt with as quickly as possible by Anera staff and escalated for further response when necessary. The principles of “Do No Harm” are included in the project as follows: 1. Anera’s needs assessment during the design phase includes addressing any challenges, dividers, and connectors in the community and planning activities to address these challenges or to ensure that they do not cause harm on the community level. Anera also conducts a risk analysis prior to the implementation of any project to identify mitigation methods and limit any potential risks on beneficiaries. 2. Anera’s local team and its close coordination of activities with local partners, local authorities, and international bodies and organizations ensures better understanding and consideration of local needs and increases the depth of interventions’ impacts. 3. Anera and its partner will provide targeted communities with information about the projects and their impact on the community and provide them with safety guidelines during the implementation of construction activities. 4. The data of all beneficiaries involved in the activities in the targeted communities is protected and kept confidential. From the design phase, Anera works with local partners to identify the most vulnerable areas in need of interventions to increase their access to WASH services and prevent them from the risks of seasonal flooding. Based on these assessments, along with the WASH Cluster’s and other humanitarian organizations’ assessments and reports, Anera develops beneficiary criteria that includes the number of HH members (above 5 family members); families that have children, elderly members, or persons with disabilities (PWDs); and financially vulnerable HHs and communities, with priority given to women-headed HHs, communities affected by the recent military escalations, and beneficiaries living in inadequate WASH conditions that make them prone to health and environmental risks. Anera selects beneficiaries in coordination with local partners, including community-based organizations (CBOs) and the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD), to ensure that they meet the selection criteria and are not receiving similar assistance from other sources. Throughout the project design, planning, and implementation, Anera conducts assessments and surveys with a representative sample of beneficiaries (inclusive of age, gender, and persons with disabilities [PWDs]) to inform them about the project, hear their feedback and concerns, and ensure that the activities are responsive to their needs. Anera employs a gender-sensitive approach throughout the project cycle and applies a gender-integrated approach from design to evaluation. Anera will conduct a training on PSEA for relevant partners and contractors.
