Project Summary

This project aiming to increase equitable access to humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining health services for those most vulnerable and in need,. The project is targeting Blue Nile, Central Darfur, North Darfur, West Darfur, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, North Kordofan and Red Sea, River Nile and White Nile. This project will strengthen health sector capacity to prepare and deliver timely response to affected population by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks, and strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and enhance risk mitigation measures The project will deliver package of primary health care services including the treatment of communicable and non- communicable diseases, ensuring the availability of essential medicines, provision of MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health, strengthening the expanded program of immunization EPI, infection prevention, health education, and mental- psychosocial support in addition to supporting referral to the secondary care services. The project will contribute to strengthening surveillance system and responding to disease outbreaks, including training and supporting Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), ensure weekly surveillance reports, supporting CTUs in the areas of high risk of cholera outbreaks. Drinking water in the targeted facilities will be chlorinated and monitored regularly. The project will contribute to building capacity of health care workers by conducting trainings to strengthening skills of staff to implement quality services. The project will be planned, implemented and monitored jointly with targeted communities and Ministry of Health. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $ 8,938,320

1 - Needs

The capacity of the fragile health system to respond to different emergencies in the country have been significant ally affected by cycles of humanitarian crises including conflicts, civil unrest, floods, diseases outbreaks and deteriorating economy. On September 2020, WHO classified Sudan as grade 2 acute health emergency.During 2020, the cost of health services has significantly increased by 90 per cent according to the Central Bureau of Statistics.By 30 November 2020 the total number of confirmed COVID-19 cases across the country is 18,254, with 1256 deaths since the first positive cases was confirmed in March 2019. Throughout the ongoing response to the pandemic, lack of designated facilities, health staff, and necessary equipment affected the outcome and contributed to the high fatality rate. The availability of health cadre and healthcare workers is still a challenge in Sudan hindering the capacity and efforts to scale up the response. The lowest ratios of medical professionals per 1,000 people, were reported in White Nile, West Kordofan, East Darfur, Northern, and Central Darfur.The DPT3 vaccine coverage during 2020 estimated at 63 per cent and the coverage of measles vaccination was only 71 per cent. The decline was reportedly due to the deteriorating economy's impact on vaccination programs as well as, COVID-19 contingency measures such as restriction of movement, and reallocation of the healthcare workers to assist during COVID-19 response.In August 2020, the Federal Ministry of Health (FMOH) declared an outbreak of Vaccine-Derived Polio Virus type 2 (cVDPV2) after the report of positive samples from patients with Acute Flaccid Paralysis (AFP), environmental samples, and healthy children. By mid-September total confirmed cVDPV2 among AFP patients reached 22 cases across 10 of Sudan’s 18 states indicating a wide spread of the virus.By the end of week 34 of the 2020 epidemiological year, 900,000 malaria cases had been reported. This accounted for 13.3% of all patients visits to health facilitiesAcross Sudan, around 7 million women of reproductive age lack access to both Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (CEmONC). Around 70% of delivers occurs at home with Clinical management and psychological support to gender-based violence survivors, including clinical management of rape, is still impacted by a weak referral system and lack of qualified staff at the facility and community level. Children under five years of age are exposed to vaccine-preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization and a high prevalence of malnutrition. It is estimated that 1.5 million people above the age of 60 are among the most vulnerable and in need of access to regular health services. Around 765,000 out of 1.8 M of IDPs in Sudan live in areas with a severe lack of health service and need life-saving health activities. The 2020 rainy season and associated floods put further pressure on the fragile health system, increasing the risk of vector-borne diseases for more than 2 million of the population. Estimated 15 per cent of the total population are people with disabilities.According to the 2020 Multi-Sectorial Needs Assessment (MSNA), 55% of those seeking health assistance had to pay for medical consultations, 74% had no access to free medicines, 23% reported shortages in the availability of the needed medical treatments, and 63% could not find medicines they needed. Transportation is a major issue hindering access to health services with 27% of people reporting lack of accessibility due to the high cost of transportation to reach health facilities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will respond to the basic needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities while increasing their self-reliance and improving their health, status and resilience. The planned activities to be implemented under this project will include the treatment of communicable and non- communicable diseases, ensuring the availability of essential medicines, provision of MIPS for RH to support reproductive, neonatal, and child health, strengthening the expanded program of immunization EPI, infection prevention, health education, and mental- psychosocial support in addition to supporting referral to the secondary care services. The project will contribute to strengthening surveillance system and responding to disease outbreaks, including training and supporting Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), ensure weekly surveillance reports, supporting CTUs in the areas of high risk of cholera outbreaks. Covid-19 pandemic response including PPE, RCCE and considering adaptation on program operation. Drinking water in the targeted facilities will be chlorinated and monitored regularly. The project will contribute to building capacity of health care workers by conducting trainings to strengthening skills of staff to implement quality services. The planned activities will be implemented in equitable manner to ensure access to services by all targeted population mainly the most vulnerable like women, children, the elderly, and people living with disability

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Environment will be considered as an integral component for this project, and a number of factors will be essential for improving the humanitarian response such as coordination with different actors at the communities, prioritization and awareness raising amongst practitioners, and effective policy frame work. The plan mitigation will through effectiveness of existing disposal mechanism as preventive measure for disease caused by insufficient or unsafe waste disposal. Plan the location of emergency waste disposal sites with local authorities to avoid contamination of water sources, disease vectors and odors. Healthcare and other forms of hazardous waste will be disposed of using appropriate methods, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Awareness raising for targeted beneficiaries will be conducted on availability of GBV services and importance of timely access to the service. GBV, survivors will have safe access to health facilities, and health providers will be equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and CMR will be conducted in coordination with the protection and GBV. Further, information sharing of violence, IDP movements, and GBV incidents will be shared among sectors to ensure timely response

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches covers the following: • Regular, timely and accessible information sharing • Participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together • Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints • Generate data and evidence for continuous learning SCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard, and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will pprovide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: • Who we are and the principles we adhere to; • Project interventions and what SCI intends to deliver (e.g. what people are entitled to receive and when); • Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); • How communities can be involved. This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targeted communities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities. During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their views and influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities.
