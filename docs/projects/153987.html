Project Summary

The objective is to provide access to up-to-date, reliable and comprehensive information about IDP and returnee populations in Iraq, as well as their needs and vulnerabilities. This is achieved through the implementation of the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Tool and timely dissemination of results to government authorities, UN Humanitarian Country Team partners, NGOs, think tanks, media, academics and other stakeholders.

Needs

The crisis in Iraq saw the occupation of large areas of the country by ISIL followed by Government-led military operations to re-take ISIS controlled areas. The armed conflict led to massive internal displacement, with more than 5.8 million people displaced following the emergence of ISIL in 2014, the spread and scale of the violence resulted in country-wide and even regional instability. This has meant that it is essential for the Government of Iraq, the Kurdistan Regional Government, and the international humanitarian actors to have access to accurate, timely and comprehensive data on displacement in Iraq. The scale of displacement across all regions of Iraq has limited the ability of the government and humanitarian actors to effectively track and monitor ongoing population movements, necessary to identify, analyse and prioritize needs of those affected by the Iraq displacement crisis. Therefore, IOM has taken the lead within the UN Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) to regularly track and disseminate national displacement data. The DTM collects data through its Rapid Assessment and Response Teams (RARTs) –composed of 130 field staff– and using an extended network of over 9,500 Key Informants present across Iraq. In addition to information collected from key informants, the RARTs also visit and directly assess locations particularly affected by the displacement crisis to collect more detailed information on IDPs, returnees and host communities. The DTM Emergency Tracking mechanism can ensure a real-time monitoring of the key displacement and return trends of any medium to large-scale crisis including conflict or environmentally-induced displacement. Since the declaration of the end of the war in December 2017, the number of returnees has exceeded that of IDPs. As of 31 October 2018, IOM’s information management tool, the Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), has identified 1,866,594 displaced individuals and 4,113,624 individuals who have returned to their home location. However, the rate of return has been slowing down: in October 2018, the number of IDPs only decreased by one per cent. Therefore, as well as continuing regular data collection on population displacement figures, in response to the change in displacement dynamics and the change in context from emergency response to stabilization and durable solutions, IOM DTM has designed a new tool, the new Return Index. This composite indicator is designed to evaluate the conditions in areas of return giving governmental and humanitarian actors important information to inform their programming in these areas. At the same time, to assess reasons why displaced people are not returning to their places of origin and improve the understanding of achievement of durable solutions, the DTM is also investigating the phenomenon of protracted displacement. Continuation of the DTM, the primary source of information on IDPs and returnees across Iraq, is therefore of utmost importance for the Government and the Humanitarian Country Team to maintain a sophisticated understanding about the ever changing IDP and returnee populations as well as their needs and vulnerabilities. As the Iraqi context keeps evolving and new complex population movements are reported thanks to its countrywide presence, IOM’s DTM can provide figures to support operational planning and response.

Activities

Output 1: Increasing access to displacement information through the distribution of DTM products• DTM methodologies developed and adapted to changing security and displacement situation. Data collection and analysis carried out.• Design of DTM Information Products: IDP and Returnee master lists, Location Assessment, Emergency Tracking, Returns Index and Protracted Displacement study, to target difference audiences and cater to different info needs.• DTM reports, dashboards and datasets released online and shared with government institutions, HCT partners, NGOs, think tanks, academics, media and other interested parties.• Participation in IMWG and ICCG meetings to ensure that partners are aware of and able to use displacement tracking information to respond to needs and inform programming.• Participation in inter-sectoral/inter-agency assessments• Regular coordination and support provided to the Returns Working Group. Output 2: Enhance regional and national government authorities’ data management capacities• Capacity building, technical assistance, and IM support provided to governmental institutions (KRSO, JCMC, MoMD, CSO and other institutions who routinely analyse and report on humanitarian data)• Technical capacity of MoMD strengthened to carry out registration of returnees through trainings and secondment of IOM staff to MoMD offices Output 3: Improving access to data for Clusters, including protection data to relevant cluster partners, and other relevant parties• Protection-mainstreamed Integrated Location Assessment conducted once a year to identify risks and needs of IDPs, returnees and host communities• Dissemination of protection indicators including but not limited to SADD, GBV risk, child protection risks, figures of vulnerable persons, risk of UXO, landmines, and contamination, social cohesion tensions, and discrimination to the Protection Cluster and its sub clusters.

Mitigation Measures

• IOM has over 1300 staff operating directly in all governorates within Iraq, providing significant access to at-risk populations, including in areas of high insecurity. IOM has established relationships in GoI and KRG with key government focal points and maintains strong relationships to ensure necessary bureaucratic processes for support. IOM can rely on existing access and coordination arrangements in all governorates to support access• DTM Data in Iraq is collected at aggregate level, therefore there is no personal information involved, nor personal identifier (with the exception of KI names and contacts). All information is stored securely and anonymously, and so shared. Sensitive information i.e. Protection-related data - is password-protected and only accessible on demand following completion and acceptance of a request form. IOM Iraq follows IOM global data protection policy.• Co-ordinating with UNAMI, to enhance protection and facilitate delivery of assistance; Advocacy at all levels, including with agreed-upon UNCT/UNHCT/OCHA actions for access; Scoping missions and advice from local staff on the ground, other actors conducting assessments and security sector actors.• Staff are trained on following do not harm principles in interventions and signed a code of conduct. They are receiving PSEA training and have a reporting system in place.
