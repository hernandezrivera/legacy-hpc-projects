Project Summary

As insecurity continues in many places across South Sudan, food insecurity and increasing humanitarian needs has closely followed. The Integrated Phase Classification Technical Working Group (IPC TWG) in September 2018 estimated as many as 5.2 million people in South Sudan were in IPC Phase 3 or worse, with as many as 36,000 people in Phase 5 Humanitarian Catastrophe, specifically Leer/Mayendit, Yirol East and West, Canal/Pigi, Panyikang, and Greater Baggari in Wau. Furthermore, catastrophic outcomes are additionally forecasted in the next 6 months for Leer/Mayendit, Pibor, Panyikang, and Greater Baggari (IPC TWG, September 2018).
As humanitarian needs have spread across the country, it has become increasingly critical to improve monitoring of humanitarian needs to support real-time decision-making and provide evidence-based support to improve planning for the worst-case scenarios. REACH has been collaborating closely and feeding information into the humanitarian architecture in South Sudan to support in tracking humanitarian needs, primarily this has been conducted through its REACH Area of Knowledge remote monitoring and subsequent information sharing and collaboration with the clusters, the Inter-Cluster working Group (ICWG) and the Integrated Phase Classification Technical Working Group (IPC TWG).
In order to support this improved monitoring for decision making, REACH will (1) expand its current implementation of the Area of Knowledge remote monitoring to new locations, (2) further develop and maintain an integrated needs tracking system for both real time tracking of needs and earl warning, and (3) conduct supply chain and market vulnerability monitoring.

Needs

As insecurity continues in many places across South Sudan, food insecurity and increasing humanitarian needs has closely followed. The Integrated Phase Classification Technical Working Group (IPC TWG) in September 2018 estimated as many as 5.2 million people in South Sudan were in IPC Phase 3 or worse, with as many as 36,000 people in Phase 5 Humanitarian Catastrophe, specifically Leer/Mayendit, Yirol East and West, Canal/Pigi, Panyikang, and Greater Baggari in Wau. Furthermore, catastrophic outcomes are additionally forecasted in the next 6 months for Leer/Mayendit, Pibor, Panyikang, and Greater Baggari (IPC TWG, September 2018). As humanitarian needs have spread across the country, it has become increasingly critical to improve monitoring of humanitarian needs to support real-time decision-making and provide evidence-based support to improve planning for the worst-case scenarios. REACH has been collaborating closely and feeding information into the humanitarian architecture in South Sudan to support in tracking humanitarian needs, primarily this has been conducted through its REACH Area of Knowledge remote monitoring and subsequent information sharing and collaboration with the clusters, the Inter-Cluster working Group (ICWG) and IPC TWG. In early 2018 at the Famine prevention workshop, FSL, WASH, Health and Nutrition clusters endorsed the development of an Integrated Needs Tracking System (INT), designed to support the clusters in prioritizing emergency areas for famine prevention responses. REACH has been engaging throughout 2018 to begin developing this system and REACH proposes the following activities to strengthen evidence-based decision making and ensure the humanitarian response in South Sudan is reflective of the changing needs and perceptions of South Sudanese.

Activities

Activity 1. Shocks monitoringa. Research capacity to conduct assessments (in accessible areas) to verify remote monitoring of shocks, 6 food security county profiles as deliverables. b. Expansion of remote monitoring through 2 new sites: New Fangak and Rumbek. Deliverables include monthly data for Ayod, New and Old Fangak, Rumbek Centre, Rumbek North and Cuibet, 12 monthly FSL factsheets, and 4 quarterly Situation Overviews for Lakes and Jonglei respectively. Activity 2. Integrated Needs Tracking Systema. Funding for academic input and ground-truthing of the early warning conceptual framework and development of the “decision-tree” for thresholds for warnings.b. Develop and maintain the Integrated Needs Tracking system. Deliverables include 1 Online dashboard with maps for “real time need tracking” and “early warning”. Activity 3. Supply Chain and Market Vulnerability Monitoringa. Market profiles for 1 critical market in in each of 21 target counties.b. Monthly market vulnerability monitoring report (12 total).c. Open-source tools and methodologies that will be made available to the entire humanitarian community.d. A set of best-practices for identifying coping strategies for market disruptions, and engagement events that return knowledge to affected communities.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

REACH conducts quarterly community feedback sessions, in which information collected is shared back by REACH teams in 10 of our field sites. REACH further shares all information products online making them available to the wider public.
