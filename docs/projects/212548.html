Project Summary

The NGO and its partners will implement a 12-month Education and Child Protection project with integrated MHPSS and WASH components. The intervention aims at supporting 14,937 beneficiaries through all areas of intervention (NES, NWS), by increasing access for conflict-affected girls and boys to protective quality learning environments. Through this project, children will receive integrated education and child protection services in learning spaces across the target locations. NFE programmes can help to fill the education gap and provide a foundation for children to transition into the formal education system. NFE programmes can also help to build the confidence and motivation of children to continue their education. Through NFE programmes, children can gain a sense of achievement and a desire to continue learning, which can motivate them to enrol in formal schooling and continue their learning. NFE programmes can also be delivered in safe and child-friendly spaces, which can create a positive and supportive learning environment.
Needs: The education needs across Syria, resulting from both the protracted conflict and the more recent shocks created by the earthquake, remain extremely high. Lack of physical access to educational centres is compounded by high levels of insecurity. Furthermore, in 2023, 6.3 million boys and girls who are at heightened risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, require child protection assistance. Insecurity, economic hardships, repeated displacements, and psychosocial distress make it difficult for children to cope with everyday situations and remain resilient to further shocks.
Monitoring and evaluation :During the planning phase, MEAL team will collaborate with the project team to develop a MEAL plan that ensures proper tracking of progress, measurement of outcomes, and systematic learning throughout the project's duration. The plan will guide data collection by the field team, including activity reporting and monitoring through methods such as training reports, activity reports, and attendance lists this will done by using Primero to collect data where appropriate to ensure higher quality data collection and analysis, while paper-based data collection will only be used if it is the most appropriate option for the context, The MEAL team will update the Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT) monthly to monitor progress and compare it to targets, with all data disaggregated by sex and age. The MEAL officer will verify the achieved results against Means of Verification (MoV) to ensure accuracy and transparency of targeting.
