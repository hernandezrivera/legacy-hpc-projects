Project Summary

In response to food insecurity and malnutrition driven by recurrent and overlapping sub-national violent conflict and climate-related shocks, including extreme flooding and severe drought), CRS will deliver the USAID/BHA funded Sustained Humanitarian Action to Reduce the Effects of Disasters (SHARED) Project: a robust, multi-sectoral program of flexible humanitarian interventions to meet life-saving needs in Akobo and Duk counties of Jonglei state, and Budi and Kapoeta North Counties of Eastern Equatoria State.
CRS’ interventions will support crisis-affected households in Payams where CRS’ BHA-funded Pathways to Resilience (P2R) currently implements to restore and maintain food production through the end of 2024, helping to ensure they access adequate nutritious food. This will contribute to preventing hunger and malnutrition, as well as preventing further deterioration of food security and livelihoods for target HHs until the harvest season of 2024. CRS will provide farmers with key inputs, training, or support they need to: 1) ensure a strong harvest of wet-season vegetables and field crops in late 2024, 2) produce nutritious vegetables and/or utilize small livestock during the dry season to access a diverse and nutritious diet in interim months before harvest, especially during lean season when food security conditions will be most severe. 3) In Duk and Akobo East counties where the risk of flooding is high and with the potential to disrupt the livelihood of agro-pastoralists CRS will provide participants with fishing kits and training to engage in fishing and help meet their immediate food and nutrition needs. These interventions are primarily aimed at ensuring food access to meet nutrition needs in order to save lives, reduce suffering, and prevent harm to vulnerable communities and families. These interventions will also contribute to protecting resilience gains made under the P2R project and encourage early recovery of livelihoods through restoration of productive assets and income.
CRS will build on learning and success of the P2R Project and leverage existing farmer-producer groups and fishing groups, as well as Field Extension Agents (FEAs) trained under P2R in the project areas. The project will benefit from CRS’ strong relationships with key stakeholders, established presence in the target communities, and strong understanding of each local context. These interventions will complement emergency food assistance interventions programmed by World Food Program (WFP), as well as the emergency response programming of FAO.

1. Needs

IPC Projections for lean season 2024 (April-June 2024) indicate that all four target counties will experience IPC Acute Malnutrition Phase 4 (Emergency) conditions. (IPC predictions released 6 Nov 2023). Given the high likelihood of conflict, flooding, or drought to impact food security in 2024, the severe degradation of livelihoods due to recurrent shocks over multiple years, and the poor macro-economic conditions (including a steady trend of inflation of food prices and devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound), there is every reason to anticipate food security conditions will represent a severe emergency again in 2024. Food insecurity is driven by complex factors: in the greater Jonglei area including Duk and Akobo Counties, four successive years of severe flooding and persistent localized violent conflict have repeatedly displaced households, disrupted livelihoods, and resulted in the loss of productive assets, while also hindering the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In Kapoeta North and Budi counties, localized long dry spells in multiple successive years, coupled with cattle raiding and other incidents of violent communal conflict have led to poor harvests of staple crops and depletion of pasture and water resources critical for livestock. The detrimental effects of conflict and disaster related shocks continue to hinder the gains made in the implementation of the P2R activity and other humanitarian and development activities across South Sudan. Across the project areas, these successive severe shocks have resulted in poor agricultural production, resulting in immediate food insecurity, interrupting the livelihoods of target communities, and undermining the ability of these communities to absorb and adapt to these shocks. However additional challenges include poor agronomic, farming, and pastoral practices including poor crop storage techniques; lack of extension services and access to new skills for crop production; lack of access to key inputs in the target communities due to lack of purchasing power, lack of availability of those inputs locally, and challenges to accessing markets. These key challenges are underlying drivers of poor agricultural production, reducing communities’ capacity to meet their own food and nutrition needs, and leaving them highly vulnerable to the climate-driven and conflict-driven shocks that disrupt their livelihoods and exacerbate food insecurity. Based on a Seed System Security Assessment conducted by P2R project, CRS feels that provision of seeds is critical to ensure productivity among the farmers who have been affected by climate-driven and conflict-driven shocks for multiple seasons, and is a cornerstone intervention to improve food security and nutrition in these vulnerable HHs and communities. As an emergency intervention, distributing seed from the FAO pipeline can be effective to save lives through improved food security, as long as the right seeds are available and can be delivered within the needed timeframe. A more ideal solution is to facilitate local farmers to produce high-quality seed: and where P2R, RFSP, or other CRS projects have developed farmer producer groups who are effectively implementing seed multiplication systems with quality improved seed varieties, CRS can procure from these local sources or facilitate seed fairs for distribution of these local seeds.

2. Project activities

CRS will provide livelihood support for 5,200 HHs (27,200 individuals to meet their food and nutrition needs). This will include supporting 3,000 HHs to produce adequate nutritious food throughout 2024; supporting 1,200 HHs to have improved production of small; and supporting 1000 HHs for improved fisheries production in Akobo and Duk counties. Specific activities will include: Distribution of Seeds and Tools for cultivation of wet-season field crops: A wet season crop production kit accompanied with sound agronomic training and mentoring will enable HHs to grow and harvest enough nutritious food and meet their basic needs for at least seven months after harvesting. CRS will carefully assess risk of flooding to ensure strong chance of crop maturity and successful harvest. Distribution of dry-season vegetable farming kitsVegetable farming kits and training will help ensure early-maturing, nutrient dense crops to promote dietary diversity and availability of food during lean season 2024. CRS follows SOPs for local seed procurement and quality assurance developed under P2R and approved by BHA. To empower farmers to sustainably produce local seed, CRS will include training on seed production/storage to FPGs and provide seed multiplication training to selected groups. Increased income among farmers will stimulate demand for seed in turn, strengthening the market for local seeds. Field Extension Support CRS will work with seconded Field Extension Agents (FEAs) and livestock LFEAs who will be coordinated by CRS and supported by the State-level Ministries of Agriculture to improve climate smart agricultural and livestock production practices through training and mentoring. Training Farmer Producer Groups: CRS will work with 150 existing Farmer Producer Groups (FGPs) established by P2R, and support training these FPGs by FEAs on climate-smart and nutrition-sensitive agriculture topics. CRS may organize farmer field schools. CRS will emphasize improved Post-Production and Storage Techniques, promoting the use of hermetic Purdue Improved Crop Storage (PICS) bags. Small ruminant restocking through livestock fairs CRS will re-stock small ruminants to households affected by conflict and flooding, primarily female-headed vulnerable HHs. CRS will utilize voucher-based livestock fairs to support the local livestock market, give participants more agency, and allow CRS to respond quickly. Training Livestock Producer Groups CRS will organize target HHs into 120 Livestock Producer groups (LPGs) established by P2R during FY 23. The LPGs will be trained on, and in some case CRS will organize pastoral field schools to promote, climate smart livestock production practices. LPGs will be trained by the LFEAs who will be trained and directly supported and accompanied by CRS staff based in the field. Distribution of Fishing Kits CRS will distribute fishing kits to conflict/flood affected HHs in Duk and Akobo (nets, hooks of approved standard by the line ministry) to kick start fishing activities. The immediate benefit will be improved food and nutrition status, with potential to positively impact the whole community, as the sale and trading of fish in the target counties of Akobo and Duk will contribute to the functioning of local markets. Training of Fisher Producer GroupsCRS will organize fisherfolk into 50 existing Fisherfolk-based Producer Groups (FBPGs) established under the P2R project to sustain their current productive activities which are essential to food security in these communities, as well as to continue to build their nascent capacity. CRS will provide basic training on improved fishing methods as well as fish handling, processing, and storage, including fish preservation skills training Reducing loss and waste will have a greater impact on food security, and may also increase revenue from excess fish, to only to meet basic food needs but also to restore other productive assets.
