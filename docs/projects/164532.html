Project Summary

Improving the access for the newly displaced people in NES to NFIs and emergency shelter, where 1233 HHs will be provided by tents and NFI kits. The project will target 6,165 IDPs (1233 HHs) in total, who were displaced due to the last conflict in NES with tents and NFIs kits distribution in two sub-districts (Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh) as follow:
In Ar-Raqqa : 3665 IDPs (733 HHs)
In Al-Hasakeh: 2500 IDPs (500 HHs)
The kits content will be as follow:
 5 x Mattress
 1 x Tarp
 1 x Rope
 1 x LuminAID Packlite Nova (solar light)
 1 x LuminAID Max Charger (solar light)
 1 x NFI003 Packed Box, including:
• 5 x High thermal blankets
• 1 x kitchen set
• 2 x SB blanket
• 2 x 10lt jerry can

4 - Needs

The shelter situation has continued to deteriorate due to the cumulative impact of the crisis. A significant portion of the population continues to experience protracted displacement. At the same time, returns have begun to some areas while new IDPs swell already overburdened communities, resorting to living in unsustainable settlements with poor access to basic services. Meanwhile, improvements to markets and access to non-food items (NFIs) is undermined by the widespread economic vulnerability.Shelter needs in Syria are widespread and acute resulting in significant living standard consequences and affecting physical and mental well-being and protection of affected populations. Overall shelter severity has increased across Syria, with a corresponding increase in PiN from 2019 by 20% to 5.65 million people. Of this, 3.8 million (69%) are in areas of severity 4 or higher. Geographically, these areas are concentrated in Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Raqqa city and camps in the north-east. Up to one million IDPs reside in last-resort informal and spontaneous settlements. Up to one million IDPs are residing in last-resort sites in the north-west and north-east, a significant proportion of whom are in improvised shelters which will need replacement/ repair in the next year.However, NFIs continue to be essential and life-saving particularly for those newly displaced of forced to relocate. While market access appears to be gradually improving, many life-sustaining, basic items continue to be.According to the HNO 2020 there are 236,297 PiN; 187,983 of them in acute need in Ar-Raqqa sub-district and 188,488 PiN; 173,733 of them in acute need in Al-Hasakeh sub-district.

5 - Implementation of activities

The area of intervention will be targeting 6,165 recent IDPs (1,233 HHs) in North East areas of Syrian Arab Republic, Specially the IDPs whom will be relocated from collective centers (CC) to the newly created camps.Beneficiaries of this project will be identified and provided with tents/NFIs kits in accordance with the beneficiaries’ selection criteria that Bahar adopts through its projects which aim to target the most vulnerable families with humanitarian aids. Focusing on the families who recently displaced in NES.Bahar organization will start with the assessment of the locations in need and the selection of the IDPs according to the selection criteria in coordination with the local authorities and the SNFI working group. After that the beneficiaries will be informed about the distribution of NFIs and tents schedule in order to have the beneficiaries accommodated with furnished tents. The distribution of the NFIs and installation of the tents will be done by Bahar's core staff and daily labors. After the items being received by the beneficiaries, Bahar M&E will conduct PDM for beneficiaries whom received the items from Bahar in order to see their opinion and have their feedback for the received services, this will be in addition to the onsite motoring that is provided by the M&E. Also in order to provide transparency and to get the feedback from the targeted beneficiaries, Bahar will share its AAP mechanism and provide contact details to receive any complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries of the project.The Project is targeting 733 HHs in Ar-Raqqa sub-district and 500 HHs in Al-Hasakeh sub-district

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A) Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members; Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigating Measure: 1. Agree to minimum assistance package across sector. 2. Assess and validate any changes before they occur. 3. Identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions.B) Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion); Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigating Measure: 1. Sector partners ensure that their staffs have read/areware of the Code of Conduct. 2. Sector partners develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. C) Risk of increased community tension due to different assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance to the host community; Likelihood: High; Impact: Medium; Mitigating Measure: 1. Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. 2. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and is clearly communicated widely within a community and among different groups. 3. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. 4. Establish complaint mechanism at community level. 5. Training to humanitarian staffs.D) Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation; Likelihood: High; Impact: High; Mitigating Measure:1. Advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites. 2. Provision of lighting around shelter. 3. Work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Border closing, Mitigation: Speed up sending the shipment to Syria since the border is open for the time being and try to find an alternative solution for shipping the items from another side.Assumption: Significant increase of IDPs in NES coming from NWS, Mitigation: Keep the high and clothe coordination with the local authorities and the working group in addition to the other NGOs working in the area in order to target the most vulnerable IDPs and cover the gaps.
