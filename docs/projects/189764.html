Project Summary


The effects of conflict in South Sudan continues to affect the humanitarian response as the effects of widespread violence and the deteriorating economic situation and will be aligned to Nutrition Cluster specific objectives, namely 1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2022. 2 To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2022. 3 To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2022. 4 To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2022. The project will complement the ongoing intervention in selected priority Counties, narrowing the nutrition care provision gaps in the most remote sites and for the most neglected groups for what concerns MAM/SAM treatment but also prevention and timely detection of acute malnutrition. Nutrition services and related activities will be informed by the most recently updated guidelines, considering COVID19 prevention and containment measures. Different services delivery modalities will be considered, from static to mobile/community based, as a proactive and articulated strategy to overcome the accessibility issues affecting current nutrition care coverage and to reach both hosts and displaced population. The proactive involvement of local communities and health authorities will facilitate the selection of the most responsive, feasible and acceptable option, while orientating the intervention on the most serious and urgent gaps, promoting better outcomes and ensuring the due accountability to beneficiaries. Nutrition services will be provided at the Health Facility level or through mobile team and community-based initiatives in their catchment area and under their supervision and guide, resulting in highly comprehensive, as addressing both health and nutrition conditions and the interrelated complications. In this way, the project will contribute to enhancing the institutionalization of management of acute malnutrition into the health system as suggested by the Cluster. The cornerstone of the proposed nutrition intervention will be its integration with other sectors as well. WASH & FSL will be given high attention, with the result of the applied strategy to be wide enough to tackle some of the key acute malnutrition drivers, as poor hygiene and access to clean water, limited food availability, low knowledge. Being aware of the higher exposure to malnutrition of certain population groups, whose peculiar conditions (disabilities, mental health disorders, exposure to SGBV) makes them particularly vulnerable and their access to services more challenging, besides requiring specific response routine interventions are not used to provide, the project will guarantee adequate integration with the Protection sector, through the opening and sustain of reciprocal referral channels for people to be offered both nutrition and clinical/psychosocial support. In general, a people/right centred approach will be adopted and mainstreamed to guarantee sector-wide responsiveness and acceptability. The quality of provided care will be strengthened through technical assistance and supportive supervision and monitored through an enhanced data collection and analysis, functional to the informed decision-making process to improve the current situation. In an area where GAM above 15% emergency threshold and more than 50% of the population are in IPC Phase 3.

1 - Needs

Help-Hilfe Zur Selbsthilfe has been present in Yirol East since 2016 following vulnerability and needs trends and constantly monitoring WASH & Nutrition Data of the population. This monitoring has been coupled with a nutrition pre-harvest SMART survey in June 2018 in Yirol East and the result showed increased food insecurity at the household level and high rates of acute malnutrition with of GAM rate of 22.6% and SAM rate of 6.5% among children 6-59 months. Acute malnutrition prevalence among PLWs measured by MUAC (<23 cm), was 25.7%. The latest 2021 National Nutrition cluster proxy GAM rate projects Yirol East 13.6%. Help proposes Multi-Dimensional Nutrition sensitive programming integration of WASH, In the first HNO projection period of May 2022 to July 2021, an estimated 8.3 million people will likely face Crisis or worse acute food insecurity, with an estimated 2milliom people likely to be in acute malnourish. A total of 26 counties have reported GAM rate of above 15% above emmergey threshold. Help approach will be: - Prevention, detection and care of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) amongst children under 5 years old and of malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women, through 12 OTP's and 3 Outreach posts; - Dissemination of Community-based Management of Malnutrition (CMAM), ensuring community-based MASS MUAC Screening, MIYCN awareness-raising and Mother MUAC Strategy. - Reinforcement of a Community Outreach Network to ensure Protection monitoring, referral mechanisms and awareness-raising of the local population on protection rights and needs, health and hygiene; protection mainstreaming within Help’s humanitarian operation and capacity building in protection, PSEA, AAP and GBV of Help’s staff and nutrition personnel. The present request is justified with a thorough knowledge of the area of intervention and its needs. As a whole CNW and CNV’s will integrate Help’s outreach component to ensure closeness with the community by diffusing key messages. CNV’s will particularly focus on PLW’s and Mother Care Groups animation while CNW’s will ensure the whole outreach component. Yirol East county, health and nutrition services are predominantly manned by people of lower cadre (CHWs, untrained semiliterate Nutrition Assistant CNWs, or CNV’s with much lower qualification to provide quality services), with CHW/MCHW accounting for about 44%. Help will recruit staff with requisite skilled staff in capacity on nurses and clinical officers for the position of nutrition supervisors, officers and managers. These important needs require a multi-dimensional response which includes early identification and treatment of cases through active screening of SAM children, community sensitization on prevention of malnutrition, promotion of breastfeeding and hygiene practices. Aiming for a sustained impact, this multi-dimensional response must be part of an integrated approach tackling WASH, food security, nutrition and centrality of protection. As projected in 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan people in need, Yirol East County expected caseloads are disaggregated as SAM U5’s: 1,332; MAM U5’s: 4,706; MAM PLW: 6,934; MIYCN: 17,320; VAS: 38,970 and Deworming 36,083.

2 - Project activities

The intervention will strengthen Acute Malnutrition management institutionalization into the Health System with CHD and communities’ involvement. It will complement the ongoing support to the 12 Nutrition sites with creation of 3 mobile sites. Activity 1. Staff Training & Capacity Building Nutrition teams will receive pieces of training, and regular refreshers from Help’s supervision staff. Regular field visits by Nutrition Program Managers & field Coordinator. Activity 2. Management of complicated cases and Referral System: a. A tailored referral system will be an essential component of the PHCC, and Mobile sites. Patients in need of urgent care will be internally referred to SCs. b. Help teams will carry out door to door visits to analyse the overall vulnerability situation of the households in regards to barriers to access to services. Activity 3. Nutrition Education and Promotion at CMAM Sites, promotion sessions will be implemented in all health facilities. Awareness of key community persons will be built on major health and hygiene topic as well as information on the availability of Nutrition services. Activity 4. Community outreach and mobilization. a. CNV’s will oversee the door to door visits, screening and sensitizations while Community Nutrition Workers will oversee animating and following up the Mother Care Groups and ensuring IYCF messages. During the community outreach and mobilization, preventive messages on malnutrition will be passed as well vitamin A supplementation and deworming will be conducted at household level. b. Community awareness sessions will be provided gathering all key messages and including protection awareness. Besides, Help outreach teams will study the possibility to implement mass awareness sessions at the community level. Activity 5. Infant feeding assistance and complementary feeding initiatives and breastfeeding promotion. Caregivers at OTP’s level will be trained in inappropriate feeding practices. Nutrition education sessions will be implemented by health promoters at Static and Mobile OTP’s level and will be organized weekly. Activity 6. Management of Outpatient Treatment Programs Contributes to cater to the needs of children with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition cases (SAM) which either come spontaneously to the facility or referred by others (and particularly by the outreach team). Activity 7. Awareness Sessions and provision of IEC materials: The CNW's will organize two types of sessions. Door-to-door sessions, meeting the community directly in their houses to provide health, hygiene, sanitation and nutrition advice. Activity 8. WASH Key messages and engage CNV's to disseminate COVID-19 messages, provide personal protective kits including facemasks, and limit crowd at distribution points following COVID-19 protocols. 1) Handwashing; 2) Latrine maintenance and management; 3) Latrine usage; 4) Food preparation. Activity 9. Protection mainstreaming training: Following the Global protection cluster guidelines, Help's CNV teams will receive training from the Protection and Nutrition Project Manager on how to incorporate PSEA and GBV.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will ensure protection mainstreaming through the implementation of its activities across all sectors. Special attention will be provided on the identification and referral of the most vulnerable and at risk. Some parts of the population (Persons with disabilities, older persons, women and children) face additional challenges to access humanitarian support and are “invisible”, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE teams will be trained in order to identify and prevent potential risks while responding to the specific needs of the most vulnerable. o All HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE staff in South Sudan are required to sign the code of conduct (See Annex E) and its additional policies pertaining to 1) Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), 2) Prevention of Fraud and Corruption and 3) Child Protection. In addition, brings are provided ensuring a good understanding of core humanitarian values and beneficiary protection. HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE has tried to ensure a balanced recruitment process in terms of gender and ethnicity, particularly for health and protection teams to ensure accessibility of services. Furthermore, all members of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will received protection mainstreaming training including a strong component of Psychological First Aid (PFA) allowing them to provide good listening, identify potential risks and either internally or externally refer. HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is aware of the importance of including gender equality to increase the efficiency of the humanitarian assistance and recognize that men, boys, girls and women have specific needs and constraints. In NE South Sudan, Displaced Women and Girls have been particularly exposed to violence, for this a particular referral pathway ensuring confidentiality and protection of the beneficiary will be put in place and explain to relevant staff. With strong protection and outreach community component, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE will foster the protection environment by enhancing awareness, information and identifying individuals at risks requiring an immediate response. Protection Outreach teams will be specifically trained to assess basic and specific needs (with a health approach) and to analyse, with a holistic vision, the vulnerability of the individual or Household and to refer to the relevant HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE department to receive an adequate response. For instance, in link with the food security department of HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE (financed by USAID-FFP), particularly vulnerable households identified as facing protection risks because of the lack of sufficient food will be referred to FSL teams for a sectorial assessment to establish the need and the response in parallel, HELP-HILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE is ensuring that complaint mechanisms are in place. In Yirol East a hotline has been available for beneficiaries since the beginning of HELPHILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFEs intervention. Through this project, teams will enhance the diffusion of the hotline number to ensure beneficiaries and nonbeneficiaries have access to it. In Yirol East, with low telephone coverage, a complaint box will be available for beneficiaries to communicate with HELPHILFE ZUR SELBSTHILFE and receive treatment to their complaints.

Protection Outreach teams will be specifically trained to assess basic and specific needs (with a health approach) and to analyse, with a holistic vision, the vulnerability of the individual or Household and to refer to the relevant HELP department to receive an adequate response. For instance, in link with the food security department of Help-Hilfe Zur Selbsthilfe (Financed by German Foreign Affairs-GFO), particularly vulnerable households identified as facing protection risks because of the lack of sufficient food will be referred to FSL teams for a sectorial assessment to establish the need and the response. In Yirol East, with low telephone coverage, a complaint box will be available for beneficiaries to communicate with Help-Hilfe Zur Selbsthilfe and receive treatment to their complaints.
