Project Summary

In the post revolution and post peace agreement environment, returns of displaced people is on the increase. As enshrined in the Juba Peace Agreement (JPA), the transitional government will apply all possible efforts to ensure return of displaced people to their areas of origin or choice. Large numbers of returnees are intending to re-establish their lives starting with building houses that was destroyed by the long conflict and years of negligence. Under this scenario, there will be a crucial need to assist returnees with durable shelter options to contribute in making their return a success and reduce their dependency on humanitarian aid while they gradually make it into self sufficiency. from other perspective; 2020 witnessed the most disastrous floods ever with the highest recorded water levels that left hundreds of thousands homeless (data from EoC and OCHA). most of the floods affected population also lost their means of livelihoods and their belongings and life savings. Its of a paramount importance to assist this neglected portion of community to restore their household shelter to be able to resume productivity and reclaim their livelihoods. many of the flood affected population are still living in temporary shelters or scrambled with relatives in no conducive environment that can't allow for social distancing putting them in real danger of catching COVID-19.
Objectives:
1. Increase resilience of returnees in communities of return through provision of durable shelter.
2. Provide support for to address urgent needs affecting flood affected population through provision of durable shelter to the communities in the most affected areas.
Environmental safeguards will be incorporated in all aspects of the proposed interventions to reduce vulnerability to climate change-induced risks. Interventions will seek to promote improved natural resources management, efficient energy utilization and environmental protection. the use of stabilized blocks in durable or transitional is expected to significantly reduce the environmental footprint of the burned red bricks on both short term while rebuilding shelter for the returnees and the flood affected and on the longer term through introducing of new building technology that has less environmental impact.

1 - Needs

As per the 2021 HNO, there in an increase of needs from 2020 to an estimated 1.9 million people across Sudan. Estimated 43 localities or 22% of total localities in eight states hosting IDPs and Returnees where the severity of needs remains acute have catastrophic level of needs (severity 5) and extreme (severity 4) while in Eastern Sudan and many parts of central and north Sudan the severity remains low (severity 1). However, populations in Khartoum, Blue Nile, Gedaref, Gezira, Kassala, Red Sea and Sennar states remain vulnerable with potential to be affected by hazards such as floods. As the rains subside and flood waters are receding in Sudan, Inter-agency assessments were conducted to assess the urgent needs of people affected by heavy rains and flooding. Needs identified through assessments included emergency shelter and household supplies, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) assistance – including access to clean water, sanitation and health services, and drainage of, often contaminated, water – food and agricultural assistance, health, and vector control. Since the start of the rainy season, humanitarians provided emergency shelter and household items such as kitchen utensils to more than 150,000 people who have lost everything when their houses collapsed or where washed away by floodwater. The Government's Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC) has activated a national Flood Task Force led by the Ministry of Labor and Social Development to coordinate the response with all partners. Sudan declared national state of emergency in response to the flooding and asked for life-saving assistance to people affected.

2 - Activities/Outputs

In an effort to support the transition to recovery and support the transition from dependency to self-reliance, UNDP will provide affected communities with Durable Shelter and cash for work to provide income generating and livelihood activities such stabilized Blocks, woven grass mats, and sleeping mats. Based on finding from the 2020 Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments (MSNA), shelter needs - particularly roofing and shelters walls - are a major gap. Improving construction technique and methodologies will be prioritized while ensuring decongestion of shelters can mitigate health risks including COVID-19. Investing in Shelter will support households to transition from the emergency phase to recovery increase community resilience, and mitigate impact of floods and other hazards in the future.Activities will include: 1. Distribution of durable shelter using stabilized blocks to protracted vulnerable households in the returned or integrated communities and to the most vulnerable households in floods affected communities2. Community training on durable shelter construction3. Flooding related activities and community work with vulnerable households to ensure no or minimal conflicts over shelter land allocations while ensuring gender equality and rights of minority groups during engagement in shelter construction and distribution 4. Distribution of Cash for Shelter to protracted vulnerable households in the returned or integrated communities and to the most vulnerable households in floods affected communities to contribute to the construction through clearing the sites, digging and other hand works. 5. Training community members with focus on youth and women on basic construction techniques as a skill set to assist in income generating. 6. Replenishment of life-saving non-food items (NFIs) to vulnerable households to complement shelter and as an additional incentive to contribute to the shelter construction efforts. 7. Provision of Cash and/or NFI to vulnerable households as an additional incentive to contribute to the shelter construction efforts. 8. Hazard mitigation as part of preparedness support to communities at high risk of natural disaster in floods prone areas the will include community embankment and earth movement works through cash for work.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

the project will ensure protection principles are adhered to including ensure access to impartial assistance, Protection from violence, and access to remedies and recovery, ensure safety and dignity for the beneficiaries are employed during the planning, and implementation phases. It will also ensure leaving behind community and locality level structures that can monitor and ensure protection of vulnerable people and protection from abuse while linking with locality and state protection and Rule of Law level mechanisms to ensure rights based approach is strictly followed. One important component of protection is making sure that there is a conflict management system in-place to handle smaller flares or conflict triggers related to shelter, land allocation and prevent that from escalation into ethnic or community level conflicts that produces protection concerns. Do-No-Harm principles will always be put forward during all stages of project planning and implementation All protection concerns are to be reviewed for all participatory institutions and beneficiaries, ensuring that rights of all beneficiaries are respected and promoted, A comprehensive review of any potential discriminatory approaches, including gender based discrimination will not be tolerated. UNDP is integrating the PSEA principles agreed by the PSEA network.
