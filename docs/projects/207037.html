Project Summary

Following RAGJ's Justice and Rule of Law (JRoL) procedures (establishing the Legal Aid Clinic), the GBV One-Stop-Shop is aimed at providing mobile, on-site and online legal and subsidiary services for GBV mitigation and prevention in 20 sub-divisions in Northwest and Southwest regions, of Cameroon. Hence provide capacity-building, sensitisation, consultations and counselling sessions, as well as setting up early warming mechanisms in communities. To build resilience and ensure adequate protection services, safe spaces will be created, not just for girls/women or boys/men, but also for families and persons with disabilities in need of protection. A referral system will be established across all 20 project sub-divisions, GBV cases in need of legal and non-legal assistance will be identified for specialised legal, socio-legal, MHPSS, health, medical, and other services.
Over 5,000 persons will be sensitised and/or trained in GBV (types, causes, school-related GBV, etc.), the changing dynamics of the family setting amid conflict, the legal implications of GBV, the practicality of legality vis-a-vis multiple scenarios, denunciation of GBV perpetrators, and exploring alternative ways of getting justice. Given the critical need to address prolonged trauma faced by affected persons as a result of the crises that began in 2016, and especially by GBV survivors, both specialised and non-specialised MHPSS sessions shall be held with approximately 5,000 persons. Persons in need of medical assistance and healthcare shall be supported through health centres and medical facilities in and around their communities. Dignity kits, post-rape kits, and cash to mitigate protection risk (multipurpose, shelter, NFIs, etc.) shall be provided to survivors.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Since 2016, the Northwest and Southwest regions have experienced an ongoing violent conflict and political tension that have caused both displaced persons and host communities to face multiple injustices and human rights violations in both public and private spaces ranging from, harassment, arbitrary arrest/detention, the lack of civil documentation, limited freedom of movement, and access to services such as education, health services, and employment. MNSA R9 records that 12% of respondents were affected by safety, security incidents or protection incidents, which were physical violence (53%), armed attacks (45%), and killings (19%). Women and girls remain the most affected. GBV remains a predominant security concern, particularly for women and girls. Intimate partner violence, sexual violence and emotional violence threaten women and girls' security amid a society characterised by patriarchal values. The 2022 National Strategy to Combat GBV indicates that more than 50% of women experienced domestic violence, 39 % experienced physical violence committed mostly by the husband/partner (64%), 13 % of women experienced sexual violence, and 47 % experienced various forms of emotional violence. Despite life-threatening consequences, GBV survivors lack the means to access services and resources to mitigate their insecurities, which further increases their vulnerability to negative coping strategies such as exploitation, survival sex and forced marriages. Besides, GBV service provision is inadequate and sub-standard in most cases, mostly due to limited financial and technical resources. Many health facilities experience a shortage of PEP kits. Denunciation of GBV perpetrators remains challenging as there are no protective measures for survivors and impunity is often experienced in the justice system. Lessons learnt from RAGJ's 2023 experience with providing legal and socio-legal assistance to GBV survivors in the Northwest and Southwest regions reinforces the need to strengthen the referral system and coordinate with other GBV actors to ensure adequate servicing to GBV survivors, especially on MHPSS, health, Shelter, NFIs and cash to mitigate protection risks. It is in this light that the GBV One-Stop-Shop is aimed at providing mobile, on-site and online legal and subsidiary services for GBV mitigation and prevention in 20 sub-divisions in Northwest and Southwest regions, of Cameroon. Hence provide capacity-building, sensitisation, consultations and counselling sessions, as well as setting up early warming mechanisms in communities. To build resilience and ensure adequate protection services, safe spaces will be created, not just for girls/women or boys/men, but also for families and persons with disabilities in need of protection. A referral system will be established across all 20 project sub-divisions, GBV cases in need of legal and non-legal assistance will be identified for specialised legal, socio-legal, MHPSS, health, medical, and other services. Over 5,000 persons will be sensitised and/or trained in GBV (types, causes, school-related GBV, etc.), the changing dynamics of the family setting amid conflict, the legal implications of GBV, the practicality of legality vis-a-vis multiple scenarios, denunciation of GBV perpetrators, and exploring alternative ways of getting justice. Given the critical need to address prolonged trauma faced by affected persons as a result of the crises that began in 2016, and especially by GBV survivors, both specialised and non-specialised MHPSS sessions shall be held with approximately 5,000 persons. Persons in need of medical assistance and healthcare shall be supported through health centres and medical facilities in and around their communities. Dignity kits, post-rape kits, and cash to mitigate protection risk (multipurpose, shelter, NFIs, etc.) shall be provided to survivors.
