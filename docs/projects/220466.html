Project Summary

"UNDP proposes to provide support to the affected islands with infrastructure construction/rehabilitation for water capture and storage systems. The project will target the most vulnerable groups and first provide immediate support to shelters and healthcare facilities. The project will engage community residents under a cash-for-work programme to fill in some gaps considering the livelihoods of the residents were significantly affected by the hurricane.
1. Loss, Damage and Needs Assessments
Validation of Rapid Response Needs Assessments to confirm needs.
2. Infrastructure Construction/Repair
- Construct or Repair: prioritize the construction or restoration of communal rainwater capture for health facilities, schools, domestic uses.
- Innovative and Resilient design: Incorporate resilient and innovative design concepts to improve the sustainability of water infrastructure against future disasters, such as ferro cement tanks.
3. Water Quality Management
- Water quality and advisory programme improved for the target countries and communities. This should include testing water sources for contamination to ensure water safety and education on treatment solutions to ensure safe drinking water.
4. Community Engagement
- Public sensitization: Gender sensitive public education and awareness campaigns on water management and conservation and hygiene practices pre and post disaster.
- Participatory Implementation: Engage communities, grassroots organisations, vulnerable groups in the planning and implementation.
5. Governance Structure
- Establish management structure/mechanisms for parties directly involved with water management to include government departments and civil society groups. "
"• Debris Removal and Cash-for-Work Program
The Cash-for-Work (CfW) program is designed to provide immediate financial support to individuals affected by Hurricane Beryl, specifically through activities related to debris removal. This program will employ about 2,000 individuals from affected areas, prioritizing those whose livelihoods in agriculture and fisheries have been impacted. It will ensure equitable participation across genders and will focus on including vulnerable groups, such as persons with disabilities, ensuring that the program's activities are inclusive and accessible to all.
Participants in the CfW program will be compensated for their labour through effective and established national cash transfer mechanisms. This method ensures transparency, timely payment, and the use of a system already familiar to the beneficiaries. The tasks involved will include the consolidation of debris using handheld tools and the coordination with operations that utilize heavy machinery for the larger-scale removal and transportation of debris. UNDP will also support local stakeholders in the identification of potential storage and/or dumping sites and solutions, including off-island options, determine if they will be temporary or definite sites and support the development of longer-term management plans for the sites.
The program will also involve training and up-skilling of participants in the safe use of tools and machinery, emphasizing the importance of safety standards, especially for those handling heavy equipment. The deployment of both handheld tools and heavy machinery is designed to maximize the efficiency of debris removal efforts, allowing for thorough clearing and cleaning of affected areas.
The expected outcomes of the CfW program include the swift and effective removal of debris to restore access to essential infrastructure, the reduction of environmental and health hazards, and the promotion of economic recovery among the affected population. By providing income through established cash transfer mechanisms, the program supports not just immediate recovery needs but also contributes to the longer-term economic stability of individuals and communities impacted by the hurricane and reinforces national systems. This involves the systematic collection, sorting, and repurposing of debris to minimize environmental impact and support reconstruction efforts. This will be undertaken as a separate, but connected element of the debris removal exercise and special emphasis will be continuing to be placed on inclusion and employing vulnerable groups, including ensuring adapted methodologies and the participation of women and persons with disabilities.• The process will begin with training participants on how to safely handle and categorize debris, ensuring awareness of relevant environmental and safety protocols. This training will also focus on the specific needs of women and vulnerable groups, ensuring that all participants are equipped and empowered to contribute effectively.
