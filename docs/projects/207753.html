Project Summary

The ongoing conflict in the North West (NW) and South West (SW) Regions of Cameroon, which commenced in 2016, has disproportionately affected the most vulnerable members of society, with children being particularly at risk. This crisis has led to serious violations, including gender-based violence, heightened risks of violence, exploitation, and psychosocial distress, especially for children in rural and remote areas. Lack of access to basic social services, including child protection services, compounds the challenges faced by these vulnerable children (PAID-WA, Jan, 2023).
According to reports from the Pan African Institute for Development, West Africa (PAID-WA) in January 2023, despite efforts to establish community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM), the current systems are insufficiently robust to address the multifaceted challenges faced by children in these conflict-affected areas. Critical issues contributing to the inadequacy of existing CBCPM include insufficient training and resources for local community leaders, caregivers, and frontline responders, a lack of comprehensive knowledge about child protection rights and available mechanisms within communities, and fragmented coordination among stakeholders, hindering effective collaboration and impacting the overall impact of child protection efforts.
In Fako division and Mezam Division, the challenges are particularly pronounced. Local community leaders, caregivers, parent-teacher associations, religious leaders, and frontline responders in these divisions struggle to provide timely and comprehensive protection for children, as reported by PAID-WA in September 2023. The overwhelming scale of the crisis compounds the difficulties faced by these community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM).
In response to these challenges, PAID-WA seeks to address these needs through the following project activities: (a)strengthen the capacity of 300 members of CBCPM, who will comprise of community leaders, religions leaders, parent teachers associations, caregivers/caregivers association and gate keepers of on child protection principles and child rights, positive parenting and trauma-informed care via a 12, three days training workshops (6 per North West , 6 per Southwest) ,( b) established 12 child friendly safe spaces , strengthened the capacity of 12 child friendly safe spaces facilitators, for provision of psychosocial support to 300 children (c) coordination and provision of case management services to 150 children, (d) establish a hotline and install suggestion boxes as feedback community based complaints mechanism (e) conduct monitoring and evaluation to assess the effectiveness of project interventions.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The ongoing conflict in the North West(NW) and South West(SW) Region of Cameroon has disproportionately affected the most vulnerable members of society who are children. In these regions, children faced serious violation such as gender-based violence and heightened risks of violence, exploitation, and psychosocial distress including family separation. (PAID-WA, Jan, 2023). Lack of access to basic social services including child protection services, remains a key risk factor, especially for children in rural areas and most remote localities. (PAID-WA, Jan, 2023) Among the displaced children reported in 2022 in the NW/SW conflict affected Regions, 2,919 were unaccompanied and 19,092 separated, with little alternative care solutions in place (MSNA, NWSW, OCHA, August 2022).Despite efforts to establish community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPM), the current systems are insufficiently robust to address the multifaceted challenges faced by children in these conflict affected regions. In Fako division and Mezam Division , the following critical issues contribute to the inadequacy of the existing Community- Based Child Protection Mechanisma: Local community leaders, caregivers, and frontline responders lack sufficient training and resources to identify, prevent, and respond to child protection issues, Communities lack comprehensive knowledge about child protection rights and available mechanisms, Coordination among various stakeholders, including governmental agencies, non-governmental organizations, and local community leaders, is often fragmented (PAID-WA Jan, 2023). The lack of effective collaboration hampers the overall impact of child protection efforts and limits the ability to provide a unified front against child rights violations.Under these circumstances and Overwhelmed by the scale of the crisis, the following CBCPM: local community leaders, caregivers/ caregiver associations, parent teachers association, religious leaders and frontline responders, struggle to provide timely and comprehensive protection for children in Fako division and Mezam Divisions (PAID-WA September 2023). Addressing these challenges is paramount to ensuring the safety, well-being, and development of children in the NW/ S/W regions of Cameroon. Building community awareness and engagement is essential to creating a protective environment that prioritizes the safety and well-being of children. It is against this background, PAID-WA seeks to address the needs through the following project activities: (a)strengthen the capacity of 300 members of CBCPM, who will comprise of community leaders, religions leaders, parent teachers associations, caregivers/caregivers association and gate keepers of on child protection principles and child rights, positive parenting and trauma-informed care via a 12, three days training workshops (6 per North West , 6 per Southwest) ,( b) established 12 child friendly safe spaces , strengthened the capacity of 12 child friendly safe spaces facilitators, for provision of psychosocial support to 300 children (c) coordination and provision of case management services to 150 children, (d) establish a hotline and install suggestion boxes as feedback community based complaints mechanism (e) conduct monitoring and evaluation to assess the effectiveness of project interventions.
