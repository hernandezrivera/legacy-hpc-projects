Project Summary

The main goal of the proposed project is to enhance the resilience of most vulnerable groups through lifesaving and resilience building. The intervention majorly targets Afrin. Proposed intervention has 2 components: (1) Alongside the displaced populations, the vulnerable groups in rural areas are the most affected, especially female-run households. There is a continuous need to support employment and income generating activities for the most vulnerable segments to mitigate the consequences of the crisis. The aim of the project is to support employment creation and promotion of income generating activities for the vulnerable groups affected by the crisis. CfW activity to address the risk of spreading C-19 by a preventive intervention where 20 women produce reusable fabric masks and winter clothing after receiving a training on how to produce/sew mask and clothes. Social cohesion will compose a crucial part of this action where 1 site will be set up in IDP camp and one village nearby in Afrin City to include host community members. During the activity, women will visit the other site to observe their work, exchange their experiences and discuss about their work. Further, there will be some social events to bring them together to enhance their interaction. The produced fabrics will be distributed in schools, health facilities, clinics camps and selected facilities. Women will receive $90/month equivalent of TL based on Turkish Central Bank bid rate or agreed exchange rate for 6 months. Additional 20 PwD will be included in this activity to assist during packaging, ironing and/or knitting processes. (2) Provision of Small Business Grants (SBG) to 50 women to enhance their resilience towards external shocks in Afrin; each grant will be worth of $900 equivalent of TL based on Turkish Central Bank bid rate or agreed exchange rate to launch their own businesses after receiving their business trainings. As complementary to the SBG activities, women will be receiving multi-sectoral cash worth of $90 in TL per month for 4 months through banking transfer in PTT. The activity aims to contribute to creating employment and generating income for the beneficiaries with special focus on women. Potential areas of businesses are textile, handcrafts, livestock, agriculture and others. The women will receive a training that educates the beneficiaries in (a) basic profit/loss accounting and (b) marketing. WHH will further assist with market analysis. To avoid that the grant is used for ineligible own-consumption purposes, the women will be assisted for eight months with cash voucher in line with the activity introduced above.

4 - Needs

The following assessments were taken into account for this proposal: 17/12/2021-WHH-Multisectoral Need Assessment (RNA); 28/12/2021 WHH Gender Analysis; 31/12/2021WHH-Rapid Market Assessment; 31/12/2021 Household Economic Analysis. In additionfindings from HNAP population assessment, Reach NWS Market Monitoring Report-November 2021 are also considered. The whole region is currently rocked by a wave of C19, that spreads extremely fast and leads to test-positivity-rates of around 30% in the whole of NWS according to the health cluster dashboard (19/11/2021). As the majority of IDPs, the winter season will further worsen the already dire living conditions and will put people into an even more vulnerable situation. The economic situation in NWS further exacerbates humanitarian needs as prices of basic items further increase and the devaluation of the Turkish Lira, that is mostly used in Afrin, puts more people at risk. According to the latest Needs Assessment (11/2021) conducted by WHH, 90% of HHs in the surveyed locations in Afrin report either no income or an income below 50 USD which means that the living standard is way beyond the poverty line. Furthermore, the assessment found that 45% of households in the surveyed locations were female headed, while 3% were child headed. This issue will be addressed by the livelihood activities because the pandemic has been affecting the entire socio-economic system and has led to a dramatic loss of livelihoods across the country.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH focuses on applying protection mainstreaming (both through the principled approach and in the technical areas of operations), primarily following the guidance of the Global Protection Cluster (GPC). While implementing protection mainstreaming throughout project cycle, WHH is pursuing four elements: prioritizing safety & dignity and avoiding or at least mitigating the effects of (internal or external) physical and psychological threats faced by affected population when accessing humanitarian services, arranging for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e. g. discrimination), setting up appropriate accessible mechanisms, through which affected populations can evaluate the adequacy of interventions and address concerns and complaints in a participatory or involving way and finally supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. WHH adheres to the Core Humanitarian Standards(CHS), the Sphere Technical Standards, the Code of Conduct of International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and NGOs in Disaster Relief and to other international standards and commitments. The aim is to improve the quality and effectiveness of its humanitarian assistance and to support greater accountability to communities and people affected by crisis. WHH's mandate is to end hunger.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) is unacceptable breach of fundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassment of fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations. Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. It also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions, and prevents SEA and protects victims. To mitigate the risk, staff will be trained on PSEA and signed Code of Conduct, PSEA banners, hotlines are set up in activity sites, BNFs receive awareness campaigns. There are PSEA Focal Points and update the Program/Project in coordination with PSEA Network, they attend the regular meetings. WHH designed a video and poster to inform the people.
