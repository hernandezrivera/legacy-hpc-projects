Project Summary

The project aims to the specific needs of boys, girls and children with disabilities in different area both in the refugee camp and host community. Learning facilities construction for the refugee community address proper drainage, waste management system and space allocation based settled standard. The project is gender sensitive and address the age group 3-24. The total targeted beneficiaries are 20,430 where at least 50% are girls. Having the consideration of gender issue, gender segregated wash facilities has been included. The enrollment in all the learning center is limited to 50% minimum for the girls. A participatory approach to teaching and learning material development is applied to ensure the content and approach are gender, cultural, and conflict sensitive.
Awareness session is another important part of the project to sensitize the community on different issues such as gender, environment, attendance & others issue to eliminate the pre-existing socio-cultural hindrance for education.
Operational support in Pre-primary, Non-formal primary Centre Operation, Adolescent Centre in the host community will strengthen the existing facility as well as improve the number of school going children and create income generating opportunity for the adolescent.
Some of basic training, refresher training, Teachers Learning Circle (TLC) and other capacity building activities in the project will ensure the quality of teaching.
Project also emphasis on monitoring through continuous data collection and field visit through different levels of project staff to ensure the delivery of quality education and in time project activities implementation.
BRAC will be concern and ensure the preparedness regarding any natural calamities in accordance with contingency plan.
Brac is also concern about the land crisis. To mitigate this challenge, BRAC initiated Two(2)- stored LC & is the pioneer in the Rohingya Camp of doing this.

Needs

Needs of refugeesDespite significant progress made these last two years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, 69 % of the households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not attending any learning opportunities (JMSNA, 2019). Furthermore, an alarming 89% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old don’t have access to any educational activities.The issue of gender-inclusiveness is yet to be addressed, especially due to the lack of menstrual friendly WASH segregated facilities. Children with disability have the additional barriers of accessibility (ramp, road, geographical terrain) and lack of inclusive teaching-learning materials and are less likely to attend learning facilities (10 to 40% less than children without disability). Pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%) are the main factors preventing children and youth to fully access education opportunities (J-MSNA, 2019).Although 71% of the community (REACH/ENA 2019) consider that the education situation has improved in the camps over the last 12 months, children and parents are reluctant to pursue their education, especially as the Learning Competency and Framework Approach levels 3-4 are yet to be approved and around 30% of the children currently enrolled in level 2, will be enrolled at the level 3 in 2020.Needs of host communitiesThe rapid influx of August 2017 has stressed the communities and schools of Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilla, whereby enrolment and retention are among the lowest in the country. Barriers to education for children and youth are spanning from being engaged in household chores or economic activities due to the high inflation in the area (32% - JMSNA 2019) to increased sentiment of insecurity (criminality, road traffics). As for the youth, only 26% are attending formal education during this current academic year.Beside the direct impact to children’s retention or enrolment, the quality of education has also significantly decreased due to the defection of the community para-teachers seeking a higher-paid occupation in the camps with the humanitarian community.

Activities or outputs

Currently, BRAC ensure access to education for 61,185 children through 759 Learning center and 1,539 Adolescent through 25 adolescent club and 450 children through 30 community-based learning facilities in 26 different camps. The organization seeks to expand its program activities in 2020 to ensure meaningful equitable and quality education for refugees. The project aims to engage the community in overall project cycle that includes; planning, monitoring and evaluation process. BRAC ensure gender and protection mainstreaming through organizing training on MHPSS, PSEA, CP, inclusive education and DRR for all the teachers and educators. The training will be facilitated by its expert staffs from the specific sub-sector to ensure proper mainstreaming as well as referral mechanism. BRAC always contributing in sector level capacity building through organizing workshop, actively participating in sector meeting and workshop, leading discussion in technical meeting as well as supporting sector level colleagues as liaison between sector and government officials. The project aiming to establish community-based learning center for ECD to mitigate the space shortage as well as ensure community ownership within the program. Moreover, the project will ensure below mentioned activities; Activities of Objective 1:1.1. Provision of early learning and basic education for Rohingya and host community children and youth (3-24 years, at least 50% girls), through 30 new LC construction & 36 existing LCs continious oprtation, new 40 ECD & 30 adolocents center. 1.2. Provision of life-skills supports through engaging youth in Rohingya and host community (10-24 years, at least 50% girls) through adolescent activities.1.3. Capacity development of mainstream schools in host community through engagement with teachers, students and community (at least 50% girls).1.4. Create life long learning opportunities for host community youth and adults. Activities of Objective 2:2.1. Arrange learning material to Rohingya and host community children, adolescents and youth (3-24 years, 50% girls). 2.2. Provision of teaching-learning material to the teacher of host community and Rohingya community. organize capacity development initiatives (training, mentoring, coaching etc.) for teachers to delivery the LCFA at respective levels.Activities of Objective 3:3.1 Organize forums and committee to ensure participation, ownership, and the improvement of practices on girls education, child protection, care giving, life skills, disabilities, social cohesion, DRR. 3.2 Co-ordinate with other sectors (WASH, Child Protection, Protection, Shelter, Food Security etc) and agencies to ensure effective and efficient implementation of activities.
