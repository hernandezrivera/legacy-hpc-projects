Project Summary

The “ Enhancing Sustainable and Inclusive WASH Services for Both Refugee and Host Communities in Teknaf Collaboratively Streamlined with Local Partners” project aligns with the strategic WASH sector priorities for 2025-26, with a strong emphasis on enhancing sustainable and inclusive WASH services for both refugees and the host community in Teknaf. In partnership with DSK and NGO-Forum, Oxfam aims to coordinate with relevant agencies to ensure equitable access to quality water and sanitation facilities.
(1) Oxfam’s WASH interventions in Camp 22 and surrounding communities, such as Hnila and Whykong, are rooted in inclusivity. Utilizing a CLTS and “Social Architecture” approach, the project upgrades existing latrines and constructs new facilities designed to be accessible and appropriate for women, girls and people with disabilities. In Whykong and Hnila, we support borehole construction and maintenance. For long-term sustainability in Solid Waste Management, a tariff collection model has been established for the host community, facilitating a transition to local operators and encouraging community-driven management.
(2) Oxfam’s WASH innovation in Cox’s Bazar focus on transitioning from emergency to sustainable hygiene promotion. The innovation aims to examine models for achieving cost-effective and sustainable hygiene promotion services within displacement contexts. The model will explore transition from relying on incentive based workers to community engagement for sustaining responsive hygiene behavior in longer term in cost-effective way.
(3) Oxfam will conduct multiple studies and assessments to analyse long-term efficiency of past WASH projects after seven years of response. Planned activities include a comprehensive review of existing SWAT (surface water treatment) on its operation & technical challenges by drought, flood and climate change impact. Study on in-house bathing uses that practices by over 90% of the shelters/HHs. Based on recommendations, Oxfam plans to:
(A) advocate with relevant AFA for funding to upgrade SWAT facilities
(B) Conduct an assessment on In -house bathing to identify the need for and usage of private washing facilities in shelters, with a particular focus on how families utilize these spaces. The study will explore the concerns of women and children regarding comfort and safety when using these facilities. The ultimate goal is to identify key issues and propose practical & environment-friendly solutions.
(C) support WASH sector agencies and authorities in evaluating FSTPs and sludge collection systems, focusing on their efficiency and financial sustainability to inform future investments.
(4) As expert human resources may leave due to funding constraints, building capacity among new and existing staff in FSM, SWM and safe water chain management will be crucial. Oxfam will support the WASH sector by organizing targeted training sessions and workshops, ensuring that local partners are equipped to design and implement projects effectively. By maintaining a strategic presence in the Cox’s Bazar WASH sector, Oxfam will continue to excel in sustainable water supply and FSM development through community-led solutions.
(5) Hygiene promotion priorities include functioning CBO’s and linked with ward and union WATSAN committees in host community, CLTS approach with community Based Action Groups, community-oriented solid waste management, cost-effective and sustainable hygiene promotion, adoption of the Community Perception Tracker, and active participation of representatives from different levels in decision-making.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Critical water and sanitation gaps were identified through the Inter-Sector Need Assessment (ISNA) in Teknaf, particularly in Whykong, Hnila and Baharchora, where water scarcity, poorly managed sanitation and inadequate solid waste management are prevalent. These challenges, coupled with a significant lack of menstrual hygiene support for adolescent girls and women, have a severe need for a gender-sensitive WASH response.Beneficiaries received WASH hygiene items at less than half of the SPHERE standard due to funding constraints. Menstrual hygiene kits are essential for both health and dignity. Adolescent girls and women often face challenges related to menstrual hygiene, which increases their risk of infection and social exclusion. Therefore, gender-sensitive interventions are crucial to address the specific needs of women and girls and to reduce their vulnerabilities.Reduced funding also impacts water supply and sanitation services. Existing WASH facilities in the camps are deteriorating, creating a new latrines or WASH blocks and urgent upgrades to SWAT. In relation to SWAT operation, which collectively deliver 450,000 liters per day to drought-affected camps, during the rainy season, turbidity levels in canal water exceed 1,000 NTU, additional aluminum dosing affect taste and color. After eight years of operation, latrine infiltration rates are reducing and latrine pits are filling with dense-thick sludge and sandy clay, diminishing latrine efficiency. Distributed handwashing stations across the camps are also becoming non-functional. Although there are 160 FSTPs in operation, many struggle to meet effluent discharge standards. However, budget constraints have prevented potential investigations to address these issues.The host communities around camp-22—Whykong, Hnila, and Baharchora—lack a faecal sludge collection and treatment system and rely on surface water sources highly susceptible to contamination. Integrating these communities with the newly built 120 m³/day FSTP-3 in Teknaf could help close these gaps, though challenges in developing a sustainable operational model persist. A tailored faecal sludge collection model is essential to secure safe sanitation and environmental protection for these areas.Oxfam has continued the Solid Waste Management (SWM) intervention in Whykong, Hnila, and Baharchora host community locations following UNDP's withdrawal in June 2022. The need had some up for implementing a market-based tariff collection system as it could foster a self-sustaining framework, supporting ongoing waste management and reducing pollution from unmanaged waste.Oxfam's role is crucial in addressing these issues to prevent further degradation of WASH services, improved service actor’s capacity to maintain the service standard and mitigate the risk of disease outbreaks. Without adequate support, host communities may experience increased resentment toward refugee communities due to disparities in service access, especially as emergencies from disease outbreaks and natural hazards are frequent in this region. Oxfam’s continued commitment aims to stabilize and enhance these essential services, ensuring that both refugees and host communities benefit from dignified, reliable, environment-friendly and gender-responsive WASH solutions.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Oxfam uses a community-based, safe programming approach to prioritize the safety and dignity of all participants, especially vulnerable groups. The program actively prevents actions that could cause harm. A thorough risk assessment is done with partners and stakeholders to identify and reduce protection risks linked to project activities. Oxfam promotes meaningful access by making sure services and assistance are available to everyone, including people with disabilities, without barriers or discrimination. Dedicated staff members focus on safeguarding, disability inclusion and protection monitoring to support inclusion throughout the project. Accountability is strengthened through tools like the Community Perception Tracker and Community Feedback Mechanisms, which allow for two-way communication and ensure transparency. An inclusive communication strategy creates safe, accessible ways for reporting and feedback. Staff and community members receive training on protection principles, safeguarding, and data management to support this.Oxfam’s protection mainstreaming strategy aims to make all its interventions safe, inclusive, and responsive to participants' needs. By prioritizing safety, ensuring access, building accountability, and identifying risks, Oxfam’s approach creates an environment where all individuals, especially the most vulnerable, are empowered and protected.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project is centered around ensuring inclusive access to Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services, with a focus on all age individuals, while also integrating child protection measures. The primary goal is to prevent discrimination, abuse, neglect, violence, and exploitation. Staff members undergo training to effectively identify and refer at-risk children to community-level child protection systems.Moreover, the program adopts a twin-track approach to disability inclusion, incorporating it into all outcome areas and implementing targeted activities for individuals with disabilities. Following the guidelines set by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC), the project places a strong emphasis on meaningful participation, barrier removal, empowerment, and data disaggregation for monitoring. Inclusion within the WASH components, people with disabilities actively participate in discussions and contribute to various committees. This ensures their inclusion in decision-making processes and accessibility initiatives.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations is a fundamental pillar of this response. Implementing partners commit to practicing downward accountability by involving communities and local authorities in all aspects of project design and implementation. Key strategies for ensuring accountability and involving communities in design and implementation will include:• Community Consultation and Feedback: The project design process has involved and will continue to engage affected populations in constructive dialogues, using mixed, disability, age and sex-disaggregated focus groups and interviews. This approach ensures all voices within the community are heard, fostering safe environments for people to voice their priorities and concerns. • Community perception tracker in WASH interventions can uphold accountability to affected populations• Community Ownership: To support greater community ownership in host areas , OXFAM and its partner will hand over the management of small-scale infrastructure to local communities, including women's groups and WASH structures. By entrusting these responsibilities to local populations, the project aims to promote local investment and engagement in project outcomes as well as challenging gender norms where appropriate.• Feedback and Complaints Mechanism (FCM): This mechanism provides safe and secure channels for community members to raise issues and concerns. FCM channels include project engagement, hotline telephone numbers, and feedback and information centers, including mobile outreach and household visits to ensure access for marginalized and vulnerable groups in camp and host communities.Monthly reports on the FCM (disaggregated by disability, sex and age) will aid in identifying and addressing gaps in feedback and complaints.All of these strategies will work in synergy to ensure that the project is responsive to the needs and concerns of the affected populations.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the OXFAM project incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) elements as part of its strategy. Oxfam employs the Social Architecture approach, involving communities in designing resilient Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) infrastructures. This approach integrates DRR/DRM by empowering communities to actively participate in decision-making, particularly involving women in households. The community's responsibility includes developing preparedness plans and mitigation measures for recurring disasters such as monsoons, cyclones, droughts, and Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) seasons.The project emphasizes community involvement in building resilient WASH infrastructures using locally available materials. The long-term repair and maintenance of these infrastructures, including addressing post-disaster impacts, are the community's responsibility. This approach instills a sense of ownership and resilience among affected people, enabling them to address their needs using existing capacities when disasters strike.Furthermore, the project demonstrates consideration for environmental issues by promoting the use of locally available materials, which contributes to sustainability and climate change mitigation. The focus on community empowerment aligns with climate change adaptation measures, fostering self-reliance and preparedness for environmental challenges. The project will work further on resource recovery from FSTP’s ( Co -composting / biosolid ) , assessing product values in host communities, FSM and SWM inputs, biogas digester , scrubbing H2S and utilization for electricity generation at small communal digester.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

The Oxfam project incorporates elements of Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures, particularly through community engagement and the integration of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategies. Communities are actively involved in developing preparedness plans for recurring disasters, such as monsoons and cyclones, which fosters resilience and self-reliance. By empowering local populations, particularly women, to take responsibility for the maintenance of WASH infrastructures, the project enhances their capacity to respond to emergencies. Additionally, the project includes provisions for emergency funds and a rapid response capability, ensuring that assistance can be mobilized within 24 hours during emergencies. For instance, the project successfully responded to recent flash floods, demonstrating its commitment to timely and effective action in crisis situations. While specific EPR elements like formal business continuity plans are not explicitly mentioned, the overall strategy reflects a strong commitment to building resilience and preparedness among affected communities

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the Oxfam project engages youth aged 15-24 as part of its community empowerment efforts. The project recognizes the important role that young people play in promoting resilience and sustainability within their communities. Youth involvement is facilitated through various initiatives, such as training programs that enhance their skills in disaster risk reduction, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) practices. By participating in these activities, young people gain valuable knowledge and are encouraged to take leadership roles in community decision-making processes, particularly in relation to preparedness and response to disasters. This engagement not only empowers youth but also helps to foster a sense of ownership and responsibility towards the WASH infrastructures and disaster management strategies being implemented in their communities. Moreover, involving youth ensures that their perspectives and needs are considered, making the project's efforts more inclusive and effective

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, our project in the Rohingya camps is centered on promoting self-reliance and resilience among refugees. Through community-based WASH- initiatives, we empower refugees to take ownership of their sanitation needs. We establish and train WASH committees, composed of refugees, to manage and maintain facilities, ensuring skills transfer and long-term upkeep. Additionally, we introduce sustainable water sources and treatment methods that reduce dependence on external aid. By involving the community in decision-making and training them in technical skills, we enhance their ability to manage resources independently. Our project also integrates -flood-resilient WASH infrastructure, ensuring communities are prepared for natural hazards.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, our project activities in the host community are closely aligned with Cox’s Bazar district development priorities, such as those outlined in the District Development. We prioritize inclusive WASH infrastructure and community-led solid waste management systems that integrate with local development goals, addressing environmental sustainability and public health. By collaborating with local government bodies like DPHE and Cox’s Bazar municipality, we ensure our WASH initiatives are responsive to district-level planning, particularly in water resource management and climate resilience. Our project incorporates sustainable livelihood support, training host communities in waste management, biogas utilization and water conservation, which contributes to economic stability and aligns with district goals of environmental management and growth.
