Project Summary

This project seeks to support 5 mobile outreach and 9 static health facilities to reach out most vulnerable women men, boys and girls through provision of health services in 4 districts including Bardhere, Elwak, Doolow and Garbaharey to populations including IDPs and hard to reach communities , conflict and drought affected people by ensuring access to Universal Health Care covering primary and secondary health care through 8 facilities, 3 in Bardera District which is Bardhere District Hospital, Ali cato MCH/ Geriley MCH IDP,4 facilities in Elwak which include Elwak District Hospital, Dhamasa MCH, Geriley MCH and Husqurun, one facility in Dollow which is Doolow MCH/Kabasa IDps and Garbaharey –Buurdhuubo Health centre MCH in Garbaharey District through the provision of services including outpatient consultations for both males and females (boys and girls included), child health services, immunizations for both children under five and Women of Child Bearing Age; Reproductive health services including Family planning promotion and integrated method mix counseling and service delivery, Focused Antenatal Care HDC proposes to undertake a life-saving emergency primary and secondary healthcare services to Disaster-Affected Communities (DACs) IDPs, returnees, and host communities in Baardhere, Ceelwaq, Garbaharey, Dollow in Gedo and Banadir Region. The humanitarian situation in Somalia has remind Fragile; due to wide-spread insecurity, lack of resources and fragmentation of the health sector in Somalia particularly in Baardhere, Garbaharey, Ceelwaaq and Dollow, makes it difficult the availability and access of emergency live-saving primary and secondary healthcare services to crisis affected populations. The current impact of the new threats of Covid-19, locust infestations and floods compounds an already dire situation. Furthermore, Maternity services are lacking in 74% of IDP settlements. In particular, displaced and marginalized groups face barriers to healthcare and higher exposure to diseases. Somalia has a high mortality rate for children under 5 years old (U5MR) at 27.2 per 1000 live births; the leading causes are reported to be pneumonia (24%), diarrhea (19%) and measles (12%), as well as neonatal disorders (17%), which are predisposed by malnutrition. In addition, Somalia has less than 40% of its target of two health facilities per 10,000 population and has only 19% of its target skilled health workforce. Proposed project location usually experience cases of gender based violence mostly against women and girls. There is heightened gender based violence (GBV) risks for displaced women and girls in Ceel Waaq, Baardhere, Dollow, Garbaharey and Banadir districts who are currently living in makeshift shelters and have no adequate food, water and sanitation services. The major GBV risk to IDP women and adolescent girls is a threat of sexual violence. Health services for GBV services is only available in the main towns which is distant from the IDP locations where the recently displaced population live. Major gaps in GBV services includes lack of safety and security services; safety facilities are cited as being unavailable. Priority needs are safe shelters, gender segregated and designed to reduce risks of violence for women and girls. Access to health GBV responses need to be improved through increased community awareness of existing services and referral transportation provision. Furthermore, high stress situation caused by poverty, violence such as rape and abuse against women, lack of jobs and use of drugs and stimulants such as khat causes mental illness in project target areas. Men, women and the youth are pre-disposed to this distress situations. HDC will provide treatment and psychosocial support and counselling to affected men, women boys and girls. Mitigation Measures

HDC will provide refresher training to all its staff on PSEA involved in project implementation on preventive measures against sexual exploitation and abuse/ protection mainstreaming and ensure they sign the code of conduct on physical sexual exploitation and abuse as a commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients. HDC will ensure the selection/targeting criteria is shared with local communities/IDP management committees while staffs will ensure that the selection criteria’s adhered al time. The committees must be selected based on AGD criteria to ensure all affected groups are involved in the interventionHDC will involve all the stakeholders and target beneﬁciaries at all stages of the project so that there are sense of ownership and inclusivity right from the beginning. HDC will involve local leaders, women, men, youth and community elders during beneﬁciary identiﬁcation, selection and registration. This will allow the community to do vetting and identify the most needy community members. It will also reduce risk external interference as well as exclusion of marginalized community members. Community will shared with complains and grievance feedback mechanism as well as opportunity to engage the organization to identify most suitable and friendly way of raising their complains
