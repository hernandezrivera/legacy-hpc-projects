Project Summary

This project aims to address the urgent needs of Limb Reconstruction patients in Gaza, especially those who suffered from complex LR injuries since the start of the Great Marches of Return (GMR) events.

This project is in rhythm with Strategic Objective #2 "The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.",
and Health Cluster Objective #1": Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building".
A year and half later after the start of GMR, many of those injured are still struggling with the devastating consequences of their wounds. They are stuck in painful waiting while the effects of their injuries take an ever greater toll on them and their families.
However, this category of complex limb injuries has not been prioritized by organizations and have placed impossibly high demands on the Ministry of Health to deal with as these cases require years of treatment and are financially overwhelming. Plus, the skills' set available in Gaza are not able to meet such great numbers and complexity of injuries.
Until recently, MAP has been the only active INGO since 2014 establishing LR services in Palestine (until eruption of GMR events) and have had huge successes in terms of LR interventions, this project is designed to meet the continuous needs of the very recent and late-complex limb injuries of the GMR as these far exceed any local capacity that exists in Gaza. According to the recent MOH report (September 2019), the number of live ammunition injuries in the GMR events reached 7,594; 87% of them are limbs injuries coming to a figure near 6,600 injuries (HC report, August 2019).
Of these, and according to latest estimations from health cluster, there are still around 1,700 patients who need LR treatment (HNC 2020 Monitoring Framework).
As stated previously, this project will aim to reduce the suffering of the LR patients via increasing the frequency of the medical missions and the number of UK LR surgeons that MAP brings to Gaza and also via the supporting the local teams in Shifa and EGH with the needed supplies to perform the LR interventions locally.

1 - Needs

Due to the enormous rise in casualties from the ‘Great March of Return’ (GMR) demonstrations at the Gaza fence, with injuries having surpassed those recorded during the 2014 Gaza hostilities reaching till 7th of Sep this year 34,282 injured; 18,642 of them (54.4%) have attended hospitals including 6,606 with the lower limbs injuries (HC report August 2019). Out of these, according to several resources (MoH, MAP, WHO, HC) the number of complex LR injuries is to be around 1200 to 1700 patients. This huge number of casualties reflects the surge in demand on the already overburdened health sector in Gaza either in terms of drugs, supplies, human resources, equipment, or training. This all has to be added up to the drastic financial and timely needs of LR patients.According to the National Screening Committee of MOH, who were able to identify 371 NEW complex LR injuries out of 899 patients screened; the number is more likely to increase with time for people who might develop complications if not addressed swiftly. It should be mentioned also that the treatment of the 371 patients has only started in August 2019 using the MAP resources in Shifa and EGH, with an average of 5-8 surgeries done per week consuming the stock we have at both locations dramatically.MAP, in 2019 has added to its database more than 250 GMR complex LR injuries, 245 of those are still receiving care, only 5 discharged!Adding numbers 250 (MAP) + 371 (MoH) = 621 patients, leaving about 580 – 1,100 patients yet to be treated!MAP is currently the only organization dealing with late complex limb reconstruction injuries in Gaza, in coordination with MOH and Health Cluster and in partnership with IDEALS (our UK partner for technical staff). MAP supports LR work in Shifa and EGH hospitals via specialised teams that the organisation has trained. The two locations in two major hospitals ensures geographical access for patients from all Gaza as our cohort of patients is from all over the coastal enclave.In 2019, MAP’s LR team has received referrals from MSF France, HI, and ICRC which indicates the important role we play in providing the most complex care in Gaza for LR patients. MAP is planning to scale-up its response by providing 8 ortho/plastics missions (lower and upper limbs) in 2020 with more medical members to come per mission, and by increasing the local cadre of LR skilled surgeons and health practitioners in Gaza. Having said all of the above, it must be said that the needs are still greater than all the stakeholders’ capacities combined and that support and donor approvals are key to addressing the needs of LR patients in Gaza. Please note that while MAP works at Shifa and EGH, as based on meetings with MoH and other stakeholders, MAP will also support the Naser LR unit once it is fully operational.

2 - Activities

Activities:- Conduct 8 medical missions- Support Outpatient Clinics by the medical missions and the local team- Perform surgeries for complex LR patients by the medical missions- Support MoH to perform surgeries for complex LR patients using the donated LR items (Shifa and EGH/Naser)- Conduct ward rounds after each mission - Conduct 8 medical MDT symposiums for ortho/plastics related health care providers- Procure the needed LR items and donate these to LR units at Shifa and EGH/Naser- MAP to follow up with the cases using EQ-5D-5L assessment form- Maintain the project’s database- Continue hiring two project assistants for the support of the LR units at MoH M&E:Please note that MAP has its own Database in collaboration with the MoH and that the patients use EQ-5D-5L assessment form so that we can evaluate their health. The EQ-5D-5L form is a self scoring form of two pages where the patient can assess their health in five dimensions: mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort and anxiety/depression n addition to scoring their overall health at the time of the assessment. It is a European standard well known worldwide and used in similar services in the UK. The measures are taken before and after treatment to compare results. In addition, MAP hires two project assistants who always attend the local clinics and are in constant touch with the local teams and the patients for any feedback. They also perform as monitors in the field and report back to the officer in charge and the programme manager any issues or concerns. Good links with the MoH enables MAP to communicate any issues and demand solutions. Disability:In this project, MAP tries to sustain limbs and prevent long term disability to retain functions. However, it should be noted that MAP has recently started a link with HI in Gaza to refer patients who are in need of specialist support. Protection:MAP is committed to GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation), this means that all patients’ data saved by MAP are encrypted and restricted. Even, not all MAP's staff have access to the data. Also, MAP is utilizing a specific consent form for every patient and avoids any photos showing the patient face to enhance protection if these materials were to be used online or publicly. MAP also is very active on CHS, and is part of the alliance globally. One of our colleagues in Gaza who is responsible for CHS has recently had focus group discussions with LR patients, LR local teams, and also the missions to try and increase the accountability and enhance the feedback mechanism. The feedback was also used to redesign some small aspects of the project and to assess any further needs that may arise. Gender Sensitivity:MAP provides the LR treatment to both male and female patients and all ages according to their specific needs with no discrimination. Also, the in-patient physio services are provided to both genders considering the cultural settings via a male and a female physios. The project is addressing the needs of all patients regardless of their ages or gender. When there is a mission and the outpatient clinic is ongoing, if a patient is female, then the MAP team asks my female colleague to enter with them for relieving the patient and also translating whatever the mission has to say, in addition to the presence of the female physio. The opposite is also true for male patients. This is for cultural considerations.

9.1 - Do No Harm

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

We support the LR service through the MoH and unlike other organisations , we do not have an exclusion criteria. To add , the two hospitals Shifa and EGH/Naser are major and are accessible to most of Gaza Strip as specialized centers which provides access to all. The GRM patients now are one of the most vulnerable groups in Gaza and this project addresses not only them, but will also not exclude the normal trauma patients who may come to the local clinics. Cases will be treated for the injury, not what caused it. Having said that, majority of LR patients now are GMR related.
