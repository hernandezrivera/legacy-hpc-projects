Project Summary

Nearly 3.5 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3)[1] or worse outcomes through the end of the year, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. The key drivers of acute food insecurity in Somalia include the combined effects of poor and erratic rainfall distribution, flooding and conflict. (FSNAU September 2021)
Moreover, approximately 1.2 million children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 213 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Likely, sustained, large-scale humanitarian food assistance and government support since January 2021 have mitigated the magnitude and severity of food insecurity. Despite minimal damage in early to mid-2021, Desert Locust will continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture availability and crop production across Somalia. Available forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most of the country, which would adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes. (FSNAU September 2021)
2021 Gu season cereal production in southern Somalia is estimated at 48 900 tons, which is 60 per cent below the 1995-2020 average, mainly due to poor and erratic rainfall, insecurity, river floods and shortage of farm inputs. In the Northwest, 2021 Gu/Karan cereal production, which will be harvested in November, is estimated at 14 500 tons, which is 63 per cent below the 2010-2020 average, mainly due to poor and erratic rainfall.
In Bay Region, 108,340 IDPs in Urban populations, are classified in “Crisis” IPC phase classification and 30,960 in ‘Emergency” through October – December 2021. For IDPs in Rural, 43,390 are in “Crisis and 14,460 in Emergency. (FSNAU IPC Interactive Population Table)
In regards to markets in the Southwest, in Xudur, there is a scarcity of fruits, and vegetables such as tomatoes, potatoes, onions, cabbages, bananas, lemon, and mangoes due to insecurity limiting supply from rural areas.
Due to scarcity, prices of local cereals increased slightly by less than 5% in Xudur compared to the previous week. However, fuel prices (diesel and petrol) have been stable for the past four weeks, yet remain high. In most markets in the South West, the exchange rate of the local currency (SOSH) against the US dollar remains stable. For example, the local currency to the USD exchange rate in Xudur is 25,000 per and reminded stable. (VAM Market Weekly Monitoring October 20210
In response to this, READO in line with the FSC & HRP 2022 objectives aims to improve households’ immediate access to food through conditional and unconditional cash
transfers in Baidoa and Dinsoor districts in the Bay region. Also, READO will protect and restore livelihoods, related food, and income sources through
seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs providing livelihood inputs/training and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings.

1. - Projects activities

Objective 1: Improve households’ immediate access to food through the provision of conditional and unconditional assistance in the Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor districts in Bay region. Activities:- READO will prioritize unconditional cash transfers especially for 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) in the IDP population for four months. READO will prioritize cash in areas where markets are viable; are physically and socially accessible to all segments of the population. Cash transfers will be done in line with recent Somalia Cash Working Group and FSNAU/FSC guidance. Throughout the year, READO will coordinate with the cash working group to determine the value of cash transfers. Cash transfers will be done through mobile money as it will not only ensure the safety, dignity, and integrity ofrecipients but limit fraud and diversion of funds. Activities:- Provision of conditional cash transfers (cash for work), which will target key infrastructure of community assets rehabilitation during the lean seasons to 9000 vulnerable people (1,500 HHs: 900 male, 600 female) for four months. The transfer will be conditional for completing water catchment rehabilitation work in Bay regions. For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labor-intensive work, specifically female heads ofhouseholds with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers. READO opted for cash transfers instead of in-kind because cash transfers support the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs, provide recipients with more choice, support their dignity, support livelihoods, strengthen local markets and promote financial inclusion. Objective 2: Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through the provision of seasonallyappropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings. Activities: Provision of livelihood specific inputs READO will distribute the seeds package to the 1,000 HHs (600 male, 400 female). In line with FSC guidance, each household will receive 40-kg which includes: Sorghum (25kg) Cowpea (15kg) and hand farm tools (Knife, Hoes, and Axe). Before the distribution of the livelihood inputs, the farmers will receive GAP training to improve their yield. The livelihood inputs will be done seasonally. Activity 3.2 Livelihood support through Vocational Skills training: READO will train 500 youth (300Female 200male) IDPs in vocational skills (masonry, traditional weaving, tie & dye, business entrepreneurship, tailoring, and carpentry, etc.) so that they can boost their income levels through increasing theiremployability. READO will also conduct a Post Distribution Monitoring after the distribution of the cash transfers and livelihood inputs. The PDM will also be disaggregated by persons with disability analysis of barriers faced by persons with disabilities and any instances of exploitation or other protection risks created through the delivery of assistance. In light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings and consider staggering of the distribution of livelihood inputs between 5-10 days. The distribution sites will set up a supervised hand washing area with an adequate supply of hand wash solution and each site will allocate areas for health screening/ body temperature checks by READO staff in coordination with MoH. 2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Firstly this project is using cash modality instead of in-kind because markets in Baidoa, Hudur and Dinsoor are functioning as explained further in the below section. Also, in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings at distributions site by using mobile money transfer as the modality of cash transfer which is safe and quick and will minimize contact and prevent the spread of the virus. Furthermore, cash transfer through mobile money modality will mitigate the risks associated with a cash project which include: corruption, diversion of assistance, delays in providing assistance etc as the beneficiaries will receive the cash directly on their mobile handsets. In regards to protection risks, READO will use mobile money transfer to avoid forced taxation/extortion to beneficiaries. Moreover, cash transfer through mobile money modality will prevent discrimination when targeting marginalized groups, for example, the elderly, people with disabilities, female-headed HHs will have equal access and receive their entitlement with ease, safety and dignity. Moreover, READO opted for cash transfers instead of in-kind because cash transfers support the local economy, enabling households to meet other priority needs, providing recipients with more choice, supporting their dignity, supporting livelihoods, strengthening local markets and promoting financial inclusion.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

As far as protection risks are concerned, READO through security mapping will ensure that the location, distance and access routes to the distribution sites of livelihood inputs and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. READO will, in turn, avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, cross􀂦re or violence. Also, READO will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming.For the minority of participants, especially those who are unable to partake in labour-intensive work, specifically female heads of household with young children, the disabled and the elderly, the cash transfer will be unconditional cash transfers Furthermore, READO will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to food security needs. READO will consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women, and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for food security target locations, design, and services. In regards to protection risks, READO will use mobile money transfer to avoid forced taxation/extortion to beneficiaries. READO will also establish a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for accountability. READO will use information boards to display CRM phone numbers during training sessions activities/beneficiary selection. Beneficiaries will be able to call or text this number to register their complaints. READO will ensure this information, which could potentially be sensitive, remains internally. All complaints will be documented and reviewed regularly. Mitigating protection risks Insecurity: READO will ensure that the distribution of livelihood inputs takes place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time totravel home before nightfall. In light of COVID-19 pandemic, READO will avoid large gatherings and consider staggering of the distribution of livelihood inputs between 5-10 days. The distribution sites, will set up a supervised hand washing area with an adequate supply of hand wash solutions and each site will allocate areas for health screening/ body temperature checks by health officials in coordination with the MoH.During distribution, READO will also allocate specific areas for the elderly, pregnant and lactating women, people with disabilities and individuals with pre-existing/chronic underlying conditions & give priority. The CFW and UCT cash will be distributed to the beneficiaries via mobile money transfer which will minimize contact and prevent the spread of the virus. Also, READO will ensure that beneficiary lists are confidential and private. READO will not share information on the identity of beneficiaries unlessinformed consent has been given (e.g. names, addresses, or traits and characteristics about the case that can lead to identification). READO will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by identifying and prioritizing the most vulnerable groups in the community and preventing discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. For instance, READO will use mobile money transfer which will ensure females, the elderly, disabled and others who face exclusions benefit. Besides, READOwill identify the power dynamics in the area which includes who has access to foodservices and who can influence decisions on the provision of food services. READO will, in turn, use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. In regards to avoiding tensions between the host and displaced communities, READO will employ the DO NO HARM approach.
