Project Summary

In 2025, Acted will scale its efforts to improve food security and livelihoods for 226,296 beneficiaries across the targeted regions of Kapoeta (EES), Jur River (WBeG), Nasir, Renk, (Upper Nile) and Aweil East (NBeG). This intervention aligns with the Objective CLFSC/CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5) of the FSL Cluster Strategy and reflects Acted's commitment to addressing the complex and overlapping vulnerabilities affecting South Sudanese communities.
The project focuses on a comprehensive response to food insecurity, recognizing the multifaceted challenges posed by years of conflict, climatic shocks, and economic instability. Acted’s activities will include direct food assistance to alleviate immediate hunger, alongside resilience-building initiatives aimed at fostering self-reliance and sustainability. By prioritizing counties identified as most at risk—those with IPC 5 populations and IPC 4 areas with acute pockets of hunger, violence, and environmental degradation—the project will deliver targeted support to mitigate the most severe needs.
Key to the success of this initiative is Acted's integrated approach. Livelihood support will involve training in good agronomic practices and sustainable techniques such as integrated pest management, targeting smallholder farmers and farmer groups. Investments in rural infrastructure, including the construction of medium-scale agro-warehouses, shallow wells, and flood control dykes, will bolster productivity and safeguard communities against climatic shocks. Additionally, tailored support for value chain development, including the provision of hives for beekeeping groups and processing facilities for honey and fruit, will enhance livelihoods and reduce food loss.
Recognizing the importance of inclusive economic growth, in areas of IPC 3 and 4 programs will support micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) through grants, training, and access to business services. Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs) will be expanded to empower farmer groups with better access to financial opportunities. Meanwhile, interventions such as conditional cash assistance will address the urgent needs of the most vulnerable households, ensuring they meet their basic food requirements in line with the South Sudanese Minimum Expenditure Basket.
Acted’s commitment to resilience is further demonstrated by the inclusion of disaster risk reduction (DRR) measures, with a focus on flood-prone areas like Jur River. Early warning systems for floods and climate information dissemination will empower communities to prepare for and mitigate the impacts of climatic shocks.
Meanwhile in IPC 4 and 5 areas – Acted will support access to in-kind food assistance at border crossing points and access to MPCA to address immediate food insecurity for critically food insecure households.
By combining emergency assistance with robust resilience-building initiatives, Acted’s 2025 project aims to not only address food insecurity but also empower communities to adapt to evolving challenges. This holistic approach ensures that the 126,296 beneficiaries receive tailored support that fosters sustainable development and enhances their ability to withstand future shocks.

1. Needs

Years of conflict, climate change, and systemic gaps have left over 2.2 million South Sudanese internally displaced and 9.3 million in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024. According to the latest IPC analysis (September 2023–July 2024), 46% of the population faces high levels of food insecurity, with 4.15 million people classified as in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), 1.63 million in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and 35,000 in catastrophic conditions. The situation is projected to worsen during the lean season, with up to 56% of the population requiring food assistance from April to July 2024. Factors such as unproductive agricultural techniques, flood damage, displacement due to conflict, and restricted access to Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights contribute significantly to the crisis. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has compounded South Sudan’s food security challenges. By October 2023, 347,249 individuals had crossed the border, 84% of whom were returnees and 16% refugees, adding strain to the already limited resources in northern regions. Trade disruptions, combined with demographic pressures, have further impacted food supply chains. Vulnerable groups, including new arrivals, face heightened challenges, with 88% experiencing severe hunger and 99% resorting to coping mechanisms like meal skipping. The WFP anticipates 6.8 million people will need food security and livelihoods support in 2024, though funding constraints mean only 3.96 million can be reached. Women, girls, and persons with disabilities remain disproportionately affected, requiring targeted interventions. In response, Acted combines immediate assistance with resilience-building efforts to address structural vulnerabilities and foster sustainable solutions. Focusing on counties not prioritized under the intersectoral framework, Acted supports long-term agricultural practices, market rehabilitation, and flood mitigation. These measures, alongside community-based interventions, aim to break dependency cycles, mitigate food insecurity, and prepare communities for the anticipated arrival of a significant number of returnees in 2025. This integrated, multi-sectoral approach prioritizes sustainability and equips communities with the tools to adapt to ongoing and future challenges.

2. Project activities

To address the FSL needs in the specified areas of Jur River, Renk, Nasir, Kapoeta and Aweil East counties in 2025, Acted's planned activities focus on providing emergency food assistance in-kind and via MPCA assistance compounded by complimentary assistance strengthening food security, improving livelihoods, and building resilience. As Jur River is a priority 3 county, activities will have a six month timeline. These interventions align with the multi-sectoral needs across South Sudan and aim to address challenges including limited agricultural productivity, market accessibility, infrastructure deficits, and vulnerability to natural disasters. Key activities include constructing medium-scale agro-warehouses to enhance storage capacity and reduce post-harvest losses. Swale construction and flood control dykes aim to improve water management, mitigate flood risks, and enhance agricultural productivity. Road construction is critical for improving market access, facilitating trade, and connecting farmers to essential services. Support to micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) and Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) through training, grants, and business services will strengthen local economic activities. Price and commodity information dissemination, along with hosting expositions and fairs, will enhance market transparency and competitiveness. Agricultural advisors and extension workers will deliver technical support to small-holder farmers and Farmer-Based Organizations (FBOs), fostering improved farming practices and productivity. Efforts to build resilience include constructing shallow wells to address water scarcity, granaries for better storage, and market rehabilitation to enhance economic infrastructure. Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLA) will provide financial inclusion opportunities for vulnerable households and enable investments in livelihoods. The activities will also focus on climate-smart approaches, including swales and irrigation to address water scarcity and improve crop yields. Support for agro-industrial value chains, such as through granaries and market rehabilitation, will reduce post-harvest losses and enhance food security. Disaster risk reduction components, particularly flood control dykes, aim to protect livelihoods from climate-related shocks. Locations such as Jur River will receive particular attention for flood resilience activities, integrating disaster risk reduction into livelihood programs. In Nasir Acted will work with CSOs - development will leverage the areas’ potential as South Sudan’s breadbasket. Eastern Equatoria locations, including Kapoeta, will benefit from enhanced water management infrastructure and support to pastoral and farming communities. Acted's interventions will be implemented in coordination with local authorities, community organizations, and sectoral partners to ensure comprehensive and sustainable impact. Special attention will be given to vulnerable groups, including women, youth, and persons with disabilities, to ensure inclusive participation and benefits from the programs. Lastly, as one of the main critical responders in Aweil East County supporting the Sudan crisis border response, Acted will aim to provide immediate life-saving in-kind and/or cash assistance to households in critical need of immediate food assistance. These modalities will be compounded by multi-sectoral assistance and will focus on tackling malnutrition and households at risk of access mortality due to adverse impacts of critical food security.
