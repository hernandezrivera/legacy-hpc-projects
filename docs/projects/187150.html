Project Summary

The HLP Sub-Sector will coordinate and strengthen HLP response for the target communities by organizing meetings and engagement events. Similarly, logistical support will be provided to dispute resolution centers/structures and the land ministry to facilitate their work. The project will also enhance capacity of local authorities and community leaders on HLP and collaborative dispute resolution so they can uphold basic human rights of IDPs, returnees and host communities. These set of actions respond to increasing HLP needs. Furthermore, the absence of civil administration in the LGAs pose a challenge in terms of accessibility especially for the physically disabled. Meanwhile gradual return and spread of COVID-19 in all the BAY States have resulted into return-related conflicts, including ownership disputes and secondary occupation of land among other issues. Yet, the dispute resolution structures are not well equipped to address arrange of disputes that arise. With the spread of COVID-19 and related measures that has exerted economic pressure on IDPs, more cases of evictions have been reported, and it is likely that the trend will continue into 2022, with the most vulnerable households at risk of eviction and other related HLP right violations.

1 - Needs

The challenges of access to HLP rights for the different population groups revolve around lack of access to farmland, evictions, living in poor housing and crowded conditions, and conflicts over land, among others. Some return/relocation locations lack the basic housing facilities and farmland, and the affected communities are unable to claim HLP rights, especially in garrison towns in Borno State. Evictions are severe, with female-headed households having difficulty in accessing and paying for rental accommodation, especially more in Borno than Adamawa and Yobe States. Eviction exposes women to significant risks, including sexual exploitation and abuse. The situation has deteriorated since the spread of COVID-19 andhas led to increase in the number of eviction cases from an average of 6-10 households per month to over 100 cases per month. More of the eviction cases have been reported in MMC, Jere, Mobbar, Gwoza, Bama, Monguno, Dikwa LGA of Borno State; and Mubi and Yola in the Adamawa States. While eviction cases continue to grow, the cases identified can only be viewed as proportion of the cases, as many remain undocumented and unresolved. The challenges are generally compounded by lack of awareness, illiteracy and restrictive religious and cultural practices that affect different members of the community. The traditional dispute resolution structures in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe are existent but require support forcapacity in order to adjudicate justice in an efficient, fair and transparent manner

2 - Activities

Established local coordination points through local partners most especially in the areas of return/relocation. Sub-Sector is also planning to strengthen the capacity of the existing dispute resolution mechanism (both formal & informal) in material and technical capacity to be able to respond to HLP disputes arising in the places of return and relocation, Provision of information/awareness on HLP rights and legal counseling services, Support in rehabilitation of damaged structures in areas of return/relocation. Also planning to support with processing of HLP documentation i.e certificates of occupancy for returnees, IDPs with land allocation to secure their land title and prevent future dispute on those land. Special consideration shall be given to those benefiting from permanent shelter construction by Shelter partners, as this will assist in the achievement of durable solution and Provision of cash for rent assistance/rent subsidy to emergent eviction issues particularly to those affected by High inflation rate, flood, and fire disasters in return/relocation areas where there is probability of secondary occupant being evicted by original land owners. Sub-sector shall keep a close monitoring of the 146 informal settlements and any likely additional to mitigate against surprising eviction incidence.• Strengthen the capacity of line ministries through provision of material and technical support to enhance its capacity to be able to response to issues. Also intending to established and donate a dispute resolution center to the line ministries

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

NRC will adhere to the core guiding protection mainstreaming standards throughout the project. NRC will ensure that the proposed intervention ensures the safety and dignity of target populations, as well as avoids causing harm. NRC has a dedicated protection team that works together along with the programme teams to map out potential protection risks and advise the project staff on how to mitigate them. NRC will also ensure that all demographic groups can be part of the community engagement and governance process, to ensure a better representation. Hence, woman participation but also youth, elderly of persons with disabilities’ engagement will be highly encouraged and supported. NRC will thereafter work in close coordination with these community representatives to identify needs and conduct referrals to appropriate. Services. NRC will also mainstream protection and GBV in the site improvement and maintenance activities, by ensuring that specific groups of the population have an appropriate access to communal services. Infrastructures and services will be improved to integrate accessibility and inclusion facilities. NRC has developed detailed protection guidelines and will deliver training to project staff, to increase its staff capacity to recognize and deal with sensitive protection issues. NRC will ensure that the Community-Based Workers are trained on basic protection principles, as part of the induction process.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standards definition of accountability and understands AAP as a process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaints and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes complaints and suggestion boxes at project sites. NRC will ensure that these sites and CFRM are accessible for people with disabilities. NRC’s CFRM mechanism will also include complaint and feedback hotlines, email, and face-to-face sessions between staff and beneficiaries. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. A dedicated team, which is responsible for ensuring multiple channels, delivers this CFRM and tools for complaints and feedback are available for the affected population. The M&E team, as well as technical program staff will manage complaints related to NRC services. CFRM staff reporting directly to senior management provides additional accountability toward targeted communities and ensure feedback and complaints are discussed on a senior level within NRC.NRC encourages face-to-face meetings with senior managers for all site visits, as well as ensuring a practical ratio of female field programming staff. Beneficiaries will therefore have the opportunity to express their grievances, feedback and report challenges with accessing the assistance directly. NRC’s M&E department will carry out post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the progress of activities towards achieving the project objectives.
