Project Summary

The proposed project will increase child protection activities that will work to consolidate
efforts that ensure services reach the most deserving and vulnerable children’s groups.
This project is aligned with the UNICEF child Protection strategy note and will contribute to
the overall goal of “2022 - 2025, children are protected from all forms of abuse, neglect and
exploitation. It targets the most vulnerable in society including UASC and at-risk ones and
the strategies proposed will not only mitigate risks, but will also work towards resilience
building, and sustainability through investing in community structures CBCPCs knowledge
building. ARC has expertise in protection and currently leads the Protection Sub Regional
Cluster in Sool and Burao of Somaliland with close coordination of UNHCR and NDRA and
chairs the GBV Working Group Sub Cluster in Kismayo and co-chair the regional protection
cluster in Jubbaland in collaboration with MOWGFA AND UNHCR in hosting all protection
meetings with the Ministry in Jubbaland. ARC have strong programmes, community
acceptance and accessibility to the proposed locations. The intervention will also include
activities for adolescents between the age of 14 and 17 years old in order to address the
unique needs of this group. Despite adolescence being the most important stage for
children in terms of making critical choices in life, interventions available for this group
remain limited across Somalia. As a result, early parenthood, dropping out from school,
recruitment into armed groups and making un-informed choices in life are predominant
challenges. 1,200 IDP children (650 girls; 550 boys) drawn from the above IDP camps will
benefit from psychosocial and life skill development activities at safe spaces. 360
children will benefit from case management; 500 adolescents (300 females; 200 male)
identified from the above IDP camps will benefit from mentorship programs. 10,000 IDP
children (6,400 girls; 3,600 boys) from the above IDP camps will benefit from improved
child protection environment as a result of improved awareness on child protection,
monitoring, reporting and referrals among the wider community in their camps. 15,000
adult IDPs will benefit from awareness campaigns and sensitization initiatives on child protection. child protection networks created/enhanced across the locations of
intervention. 20 child protection service providers including INGOs, local NGOs, formal
schools, Qur’anic centers and Maternal and Child Health Clinics workers will be trained. ALIGHT will deploy trained Psychosocial Counsellors at the Child Friendly Spaces to support the social workers deliver one on one daily counselling services to the traumatized children affected by Tigray crisis and have lost social connections and parents. Children will be provided with appropriate conducive, confidential, compassionate environment to express their emotional stress and trauma. 1200 Children under the age of 18 will be targeted with structured PSS sessions and parent and foster parents will be engaged in specific designed session to enhance their mental wellbeing and reduce the chances of the critical mental health issues as a result of the crisis. Through adult structured sessions, daily therapy sessions will be coordinated under the Psychosocial sub sector under GBV. ARC will endeavor to ensure a standardized approach for case management (including IDTR for UASC), PSS and reporting within the targeted Tigray child protection network. This effort will be beneficial for designing more appropriate and timely responses for children, benefit data collection and analysis, advocacy work and in general strengthen coordination and complementary of actions among partners who are engaged for the protection of children in Tigray. ARC, will coordinate mapping of where children have come from, building linkages with the stakeholders there for reunification will be implemented.
