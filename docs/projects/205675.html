Project Summary

ALIGHT nutrition response will focus on preventive and curative nutrition interventions in Morobo, Kajo Keji and Aweil West Counties. The response will primarily target children under five years and PLW among the host community, IDPs and returnees.
The proposed interventions include prevention of acute malnutrition through behaviour change communication to improve MIYCN practices in the community; prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns targeting children under five years; CMAM through the Stabilization Centre, Outpatient Therapeutic Programme and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme and community mobilization where the community nutrition volunteers will be engaged to identify and support enrolment of children and PLW with acute malnutrition before their condition deteriorates. ALIGHT will work in coordination with other sectors such as WASH, Protection, Education, FSL and Health to strengthen integration and linkages of services with the goal of preventing acute malnutrition and to enhance recovery of beneficiaries undergoing nutrition rehabilitation. The proposed interventions seek to reduce morbidity and prevent mortality associated with acute malnutrition and subsequently improve maternal and child health.
The proposed interventions are in line with the nutrition cluster objectives which seeks to (a) increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents and women; (b) increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW; (c) Enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information, and (d) strengthen coordination and integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women by the end of 2024.
ALIGHT will operate 31, 9 and 4 nutrition sites in Aweil West, Kajo Keji and Morobo respectively. The nutrition sites will be integrated within the health facilities. The project aims to reach 7,812 children aged 6 to 59 months with SAM (Aweil West: 7,017, Kajo Keji: 427, Morobo: 368), 17,771 children aged 6 to 59 months with MAM (Aweil West: 14,480, Kajo Keji: 1,789, Morobo: 1,502), 16,718 PLW with acute malnutrition (Aweil West: 14,604, Kajo Keji: 1,539, Morobo 575), 67,610 caregivers/mothers with children under two years through individual MIYCN counselling sessions (Aweil West: 50,537, Kajo Keji: 11,384, Morobo: 5,689), 95,780 children aged 6 to 59 months with Vitamin A supplements (Aweil West: 71,594, Kajo Keji: 16,127, Morobo: 8,059) and deworm 78,878 children aged 12 to 59 months with Albendazole tablets (Aweil West: 58,960, Kajo Keji: 13,281, Morobo: 6,637). The proposed interventions will be implemented in accordance with the Ministry of Health's CMAM and MIYCN protocols.
ALIGHT will make efforts to ensure the work that the organization does is sustainable by building the capacity of the CHD by working closely in every phase of the project design and implementation as well as working within the health facilities to strengthen health and nutrition programming. This will ensure the CHD is able to continue the project or use the knowledge and skills they would have acquired. ALIGHT will build the capacity of the County health director and nutrition officer on nutrition service delivery, human resource management, nutrition information system, management of nutrition supply chain, finance, and leadership and governance. ALIGHT will also continue to ensure meaningful involvement of the communities in the implementation of the nutrition project to foster ownership and leadership on the project and ensure the exit does not have a detrimental effect on the children and communities.

1. Needs

Acute malnutrition remains a public health problem among children and women in Aweil West County in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) State. The key drivers of acute malnutrition include elevated levels of food insecurity owing to unsustainable livelihoods, high inflation resulting in exorbitant food prices and climatic shocks (severe dry spells & flooding) which have resulted in poor harvests. This is consistent with the results of the IPC report published on November 2023 where Aweil West County is projected to experience serious acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) between October 2023 to March 2024 and the situation is projected to worsen from April to June 2024 and the population is likely to face critical (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In addition, Aweil West County is projected to experience serious acute malnutrition (IPC Phase 3) from October 2023 to March 2024 and deteriorate to critical acute malnutrition situation (IPC Phase 4) from April to June 2024. According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System – Plus (FSNMS+), Round 27 survey report released in June 2022, other drivers of acute malnutrition in NBeG State which Aweil West County is part of include high prevalence of diseases; poor hygiene and sanitation conditions; sub-optimal maternal, infant and young child feeding practices; lack of safe water and limited access to quality health services.Kajo Keji and Morobo Counties in Central Equatoria State are among the most affected Counties in South Sudan by the continued localized conflicts that has led to massive displacement of the population within these Counties and to the refugee camps in the neighboring Countries, however, recently, according to the UNHCR Overview of Spontaneous Refugee Returns report, September 2023, the number of returnees has increased owing to the improved security situation in South Sudan, testified reduction of food ration offered in refugees camps, unemployment and livelihood opportunities, and limited access to basic services in the Countries of refuge. An increase in the returnees is likely to overstretch the existing basic services and food reserves among the host population, leading to poor household food security. In addition, the population in these Counties are exposed to the risk of acute malnutrition due to food insecurity attributed to high inflation leading to high food prices, recurrent conflicts and invasion of cattle keepers from neighboring Counties that has disrupted normal livelihoods coupled with other aggravating factors such as inadequate safe water, poor sanitation facilities, lack of proper shelters, and limited access to health care services, all common during displacements as per the findings of UNOCHA led joint assessments in Kajo Keji and Morobo in 2021. Though the results of IPC report released on November 2023 projected Kajo Keji and Morobo Counties would experience IPC AMN phase 1 and IPC AMN phase 2 respectively between October 2023 and March 2024 and both Counties would experience IPC AMN phase 2 from April to June 2024, the population especially children and women are at a higher risk of acute malnutrition due to the aggravating factors mentioned above. Malnutrition can have long-term negative effects particularly during the first 1,000 days of a child’s life, from pregnancy through to two years of age, therefore, prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five years, and pregnant and lactating women through an integrated multi-sectoral response programming remains a significant need in the targeted locations. Furthermore, the protracted conflict has over the years weakened the government infrastructure and systems and consequently the ability to respond to the burden of acute malnutrition thus the need to build and strengthen the capacity of the County health department in nutrition programming.

2. Project activities

• Prevent acute malnutrition through behavior change communication approach by conducting MIYCN individual counselling and group education sessions at the nutrition sites and community, cooking demonstration sessions to mothers/caregivers with children aged 6 to 23 and facilitation of Mother-to-Mother Support Groups (MtMSGs). • Promote intake of quality diets for young children through rearing of chicken and goats, production and consumption of groundnut butter, and production of dark green leafy vegetables using climate smart farming techniques. Households with children under five years will be targeted.• Prevent micronutrient deficiencies through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming campaigns for children under five years.• Conduct nutrition screening of children under five years and, PLW in the community through CNVs and strengthen family led MUAC screening approach to enable early detection of malnourished cases and referral to nutrition sites.• Implement CMAM programme (SC, OTP, TSFP, community mobilization) to treat acute malnutrition among children under five years and PLW.• Conduct routine support supervision and monitoring of nutrition activities to identify capacity gaps, and subsequently conduct on the job training and mentorship. The senior management in the Country office and the nutrition specialist will carry out quarterly support supervision of nutrition programme activities. • Establish Complaints and Response Mechanism to provide feedback on nutrition interventions and promote community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery.• Participate in the FSNMS at the County level and conduct MIYCN barrier analysis to determine barriers to optimal uptake of maternal, infant and young child feeding and care practices.• Collect, compile and submit monthly nutrition reports to the nutrition cluster through the NIS and DHIS2. • In collaboration with the WASH cluster, provide WASH kits to children with SAM, sensitize the community through CNVs and programme beneficiaries on the correct use of aqua tabs and optimal hygiene and sanitation practices.• In conjunction with the health cluster, provide LLITN for malaria prevention to children and PLW with acute malnutrition, test and treat malaria among children under five years with SAM and MAM, referral and management of children with diarrhea with the zinc and ORS and immunization of children in the program.• With support of the FSL cluster, establish and maintain kitchen gardens at nutrition sites and in the community through MtMSGs, CNVs and beneficiaries discharged from the CMAM programme. In addition, link beneficiaries discharged from the OTP and TSFP with cash and voucher assistance programs, and train CNVs and MtMSGs members on climate smart agricultural practices including food preservation methods.• Integrate child stimulation and ECD in provision of care for children with SAM and MAM by equipping the nutrition sites with child play materials.• Coordinate with the Education sector in raising awareness on optimal nutrition practices including the importance of kitchen gardens in schools.• Train nutrition workers on gender and GBV including safe referrals for GBV survivors to facilitate identification of GBV survivors at the nutrition sites for support. Conduct GBV safety audit in each nutrition site to identify and address protection risks.• Strengthen the capacity of the CHD by working closely in every phase of the project implementation and integration of nutrition into the health facilities, training health facility staff in nutrition and coordination of nutrition meetings.• To curb the sale of nutrition supplies and equipment, ALIGHT will work with the government authorities to enforce laws banning sale of nutrition supplies and equipment, conduct community engagement meetings to foster ownership of the nutrition project, and strengthen stock control management system to ensure supplies are not diverted from the stores for sale.
