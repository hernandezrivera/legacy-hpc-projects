How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed project centres on increasing communities’ resilience to short-term and chronic emergencies by strengthening health systems and improving the overall health of the beneficiaries. The program will address the immediate need to improve health and nutrition outcomes while working towards the longer-term goal of sustainably integrating nutrition into the health system framework, thereby attracting government strategic and financial commitments that will enable continuous provision of quality health services. The project will implement activities alongside or directly through MOH staff to ensure that the capacity and responsibility for the provision of health and nutrition services remains within government facilities. Targeted trainings and supportive follow-up will strengthen local partners’ (e.g. CHVs, health workers, community leaders) capacity to deliver quality services in the long-term. The project will specifically invest in CHVs as these individuals will remain in their communities to further health and nutrition education and link communities to services.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Gender disparities are visible in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. The project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender mainstreaming has been considered in the design of this project at all levels. All targeted beneficiaries will be registered, profiled and data disaggregated by gender and age as for the following categories: 1. Women and pregnant women 2. Children under 5 years 3. Population under 18 years 4. IDPs resident in IDP camps 5. Access of men to transmissible diseases services During implementation and monitoring of the services a participatory and gender sensitive approach will be adopted to allow equal opportunity to all. The hygiene and health promotion interventions will involve hospital staff, both health workers and support staff, patients, relatives and visitors with specific sessions addressed to mothers of patients in pediatric ward and women in obstetric/gynaecology department. Gender specific group discussions and interaction are organized. Hospital staff recruitment will respect gender equality ensuring opportunities to all genders on the base of the professional qualification.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

COOPI will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, COOPI will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that project stakeholders understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. COOPI will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the project are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social groups. All implementing staff will be trained in the Do-No-Harm approach that will enable them to perceive dividers and connectors within communities that can serve to harm or help in situations of conflict. Understanding the unique challenges and opportunities in each target area will allow staff to avoid negative impacts and emphasize peace-building initiatives. COOPI will design conflict sensitivity activities based on lessons learned from recent experience as part of a DFID-supported conflict sensitivity.
