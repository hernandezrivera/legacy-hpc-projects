Project Summary

This project aims to contribute to the protection and rehabilitation of livelihoods for the most vulnerable populations, including conflict- and flood-affected IDPs, host communities, and returnees in Rubkona, Guit, Koch, and Leer counties in Unity State. The project will target 24,500 beneficiaries, including men, women, boys, and girls from IDP, returnee, agro-pastoralist, and host communities. Special attention will be given to women-headed households, persons with disabilities, and families with special needs, ensuring a gender-sensitive approach to beneficiary selection.
The project is designed to address the immediate food security needs of populations in IPC Phase 4 and 5, targeting communities facing severe food insecurity. The response activities are aligned with the FSL priority areas, geographical scope, and seasonal context of the region. It is built on identifying and capitalizing on windows of opportunity to ensure sustainable recovery and resilience building.
The primary activities of the project include providing support for livestock, crop, vegetable, and fish production, incorporating resilient livelihood training for beneficiaries. Additionally, the project will train 100 Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) on livestock treatment, vaccination, and disease surveillance. A total of 100,000 livestock will be treated and vaccinated, which is essential to maintaining the health and productivity of local herds.
Throughout the implementation, the project will adhere to the Do No Harm principle, placing transparency, accountability, and Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) at the core of beneficiary targeting and support delivery. This ensures that the needs of vulnerable groups are met, while safeguarding their safety and dignity.
Humanitarian Aid for Development’s extensive presence and experience in Unity States, combined with its deep understanding of the local conflict dynamics, will ensure that project activities are tailored to the unique needs of each community. This localized approach will help mitigate risks, reduce potential negative impacts, and prevent protection issues, ultimately promoting the long-term well-being of the targeted populations.

1. Needs

South Sudan is grappling with intersecting crises, including conflict, subnational violence, economic challenges, and climate change, which have severely impacted food security. These challenges, compounded by displacement and limited access to essential services, have left 7.7 million people across the country projected to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse from September 2024 to July 2025. Unity State is among the worst affected, with severe food insecurity persisting throughout the lean and post-harvest seasons. According to the IPC analysis for December 2024 to March 2025, approximately 748,000 people (64.5% of the population) in Unity State are expected to face IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse, including 204,000 in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). This situation will worsen during the lean season (April to July 2025), with 830,000 people (71.6% of the population) projected to experience IPC Phase 3 or worse, including 255,000 in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Counties such as Guit, Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, and Rubkona are expected to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) conditions, while Abiemnhom, Koch, Mayom, and Pariang/Ruweng will remain in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Key drivers include depleted household stocks, high food prices, climate shocks, displacement, and cattle raiding, which have eroded coping capacities and left households reliant on markets that are increasingly inaccessible due to damaged roads and infrastructure. The ongoing conflict in Sudan has further compounded South Sudan's food security crisis. As of September 30, 2024, 821,000 refugees and returnees, including 413,000 children, had crossed into South Sudan. Many arrived in dire need of humanitarian assistance, further straining already limited resources. Women and children, in particular, face heightened vulnerabilities, with many requiring urgent nutrition and protection assistance. Returnees have primarily settled in Unity State and other border regions, adding pressure to local resources and services (UNICEF Report 2024). Livestock is a key asset for agro-pastoral households in Unity State, yet its productivity is being undermined by disease outbreaks, inadequate veterinary services, and cattle raiding. FEWS NET reports an increase in livestock diseases across Unity, Upper Nile, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal due to competition over grazing resources and disrupted migratory patterns caused by Sudanese pastoralists fleeing conflict. These challenges have diminished livestock's contribution to household food security, further straining coping mechanisms. Crop production in Unity State faces significant challenges, including depleted food stocks, high food prices, climatic shocks, and conflict. Households in counties like Rubkona, Guit, Koch, and Leer face declining food consumption, particularly during the lean season, as staple food prices continue to rise. The FSNMS 2024 report highlights how these rising prices have reduced purchasing power, especially among vulnerable populations such as returnees, IDPs, and host communities. HAD’s project in Rubkona, Guit, Koch, and Leer counties aims to mitigate these challenges through an integrated approach to improving food security and livelihoods. The project will provide agricultural support, including crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits, and agricultural extension services to restore food production capacity and diversify livelihoods. Training in improved agronomy practices and fish handling/preservation techniques will help enhance productivity and reduce post-harvest losses. The project will also address livestock challenges by providing vaccinations, disease surveillance, and treatment services to safeguard livestock health and support agro-pastoral households. These efforts will help stabilize household food security and rebuild critical livelihood assets.

2. Project activities

1. Promote Emergency Food Production: Support both main and dry season food production for households in Rubkona, Guit and Leer counties while providing Lean Season response to households in Koch county. This will in include provision of livelihood kits that comprise fishing equipment, vegetable seeds, crop seeds and tools in order to enable the communities boost their food security.2. Pest and Disease Management: Train farmers in Integrated Pest and Disease Management (IPDM), including Fall Armyworm (FAW) surveillance, detection, prevention, and eradication techniques to protect crops and increase yields.3. Livestock Health and Disease Control: Conduct livestock vaccination campaigns for disease prevention and provide treatment and disease surveillance to safeguard livestock health and enhance the resilience of agro-pastoral livelihoods.4. Value Chain Development: Support value chain addition by providing training and resources for milk, fish preservation, and marketing, enabling households to improve income generation from agricultural and livestock products.5. Capacity Building and Skills Training: Strengthen on-farm production capacity through adaptive training models such as farmer field schools and demonstration plots. Provide income generation skills through business training and vocational skills development to support diversification of livelihoods and improve economic resilience.
