Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to improve protection, safety and security and enhance access to justice and peaceful coexistence between refugees and host communities in 10 camps in Cox’s Bazar, with particular attention to vulnerable groups such as youth, women, elderly, people with disabilities . The project will provide a forum through which refugees and host communities will have improved access to protection services and conflict resolution mechanisms, as well as access to legal assistance and victim support services. The expected results will reinforce the capacities of actors that are currently working on community safety and security, justice and protection in both refugee and host communities, focusing on addressing crime, promoting women’s empowerment, and preventing and responding to SGBV in a more inclusive and sustainable manner in accordance with national legal and local administrative structures and refugee protection standards. The interventions proposed are based on the findings of a community safety pilot project carried out in two camps in 2020, which emphasized the need for increased police presence in the camps and involved training on humanitarian law, protection principles and community safety. The pilot also focused on establishing community safety forums in the two camps to enable partnership and dialogue develop local solutions to problems identified jointly by refugee and host communities and strengthen different forms of social accountability. The project will be jointly implemented by UNDP, UNHCR and IOM in close collaboration with the RRRC and Camp in Charge officials, APBn, District Police and local government institutions in Cox’s Bazar. Further the project will work closely with UNHCR and IOM Protection sector and staff in the camps, SGBV staff and Camp Management. The multi-stakeholder approach will focus on achieving refugee protection through more predictable and sustained support to both refugees and the communities hosting them.

1 - Identified Needs

Nearly all Rohingya refugees living in the camps in Cox’s Bazar depend on humanitarian services for their survival. They are confronted with limited opportunities for livelihoods, complex & intersecting financial pressures despite efforts by the Government of Bangladesh (GoB) & humanitarian agencies to provide basic needs. They face a range of safety issues including theft, lack of lighting, drug use & drug trafficking, human smuggling & trafficking in persons, disputes over aid distribution & gender-based violence. Through a pilot project the three agencies conducted a community safety baseline FGDs in 2 pilot camps, which emphasized need for 24/7 presence of the police, different perceptions of safety and security, risks and mitigation measures between refugee men, women, boys, girls, camp leaders & camp administration. (e.g. women & girls face additional layers of violence as a result of social culture of practices & lack of adequate response & crime report mechanism.) The GoB has made concerted efforts to curtail the spread of COVID-19, yet the negative economic & social impacts of the pandemic reverberate across the camps, as is the case globally. Security measures undertaken by the GoB have increased in the camps since 2019, but gang violence in the camps and throughout Cox’s Bazar district, particularly related to drugs trafficking, continues unabated, while conflicts among refugees & between refugees & host communities are increasing. The Cox’s Bazar Analysis & Research Unit (CARU) of UNDP reports a total of 225 homicides between January & November 2020, of which 129 were murders & 86 were “gunfire deaths”. CARU estimates that in the first four months of 2020, 4.44 million yaba tablets were seized and 176 people were arrested in anti-narcotrafficking operations. In 2018 and 2019, police arrested more than 1,000 Rohingyas for various crimes including smuggling, human trafficking, & prostitution. In parallel, host communities in Cox’s Bazar are also facing socioeconomic and safety pressures. Host communities indicated a feeling of being ignored by humanitarian organizations, rising labor competition, deforestation, price increases & damage to physical & natural resources. GBV is on the rise in the camps, including intimate partner violence, physical assault, emotional abuse, & denial of resources. There is also rising anxiety among locals about being outnumbered, & a widespread perception that kidnapping, theft and armed robbery have increased since the 2017 influx. Through the project, efforts will continue to be made alongside law enforcement agencies & the judiciary to ensure refugees & host communities live with reduced fear of crime and to ensure reporting mechanisms are accessible, safe & in place to allow refugees to report incidents of crime and violence, & to do so confidentially and without fear of reprisal. In terms of participation and empowerment, the project combines top-down processes & institutions, including supporting police & justice actors, good governance, strengthening accountability & social justice instruments, with a bottom-up focus in which inclusive & participatory processes support the important roles of individuals and communities as actors in defining and implementing their essential freedoms. For disaster preparedness, agencies will work with a range of partners, including other Sectors, community-based protection networks in the camps & relevant authorities as appropriate to the situation to identify the threats & support the people in need. Agencies will verify the needs of EVIs, coordinate services, & support other partners to mitigate harm during disaster response. The agencies will work closely with protection staff & partners to support outreach sessions for Rohingya & host community members, camp authorities & partners to raise awareness on COVID-19 & impact on available protection service.

2 - Activities or outputs

-IOM, UNHCR will continue constructing and/or refurbishing police posts for APBn police based on the facilities needs assessments in camps to strategically consider the best use of the community assets, safety and security priorities and conflict dynamics between host and refugee communities. UNDP will carry out needs assessment for refurbishment of two police stations in Ukhiya and Teknaf with the aim of enabling easier access to police for local populations and refugees. HRDD Assessment will be conducted for the law enforcement actors supported by the three agencies to ensure do no harm principle is applied. -IOM, UNHCR, UNDP will continue capacity building of law enforcement authorities, particularly APBn, in humanitarian law, refugee protection and community safety through a ToT curriculum in collaboration with partners within the Protection Sector. In parallel, UNDP will continue with capacity development of District Police in the area of criminal investigation, gender sensitive policing and community policing. Training platform for police on humanitarian law, refugee protection and community safety will be developed and integrated into the curriculum of the Police Academy-Support the establishment of records keeping systems and records management for criminal offences reported at the camp level and further processed at relevant district police stations. Establishing such system will enhance feedback and trust into community police services.-UNHCR, IOM, UNDP in cooperation with GP, GBV, CP will support establishment and capacity building of Community Safety Forums in selected camps. The agencies will apply gender, age and diversity markers when establishing Forums and enable inclusive participation. Capacity development will focus on community safety, conflict resolution and mediation, promoting peaceful coexistence. Skills gained through training programs will be utilized for development of safe community plans jointly with police and CiCs, recognizing different gender and age safety and risk perceptions and addressing the vulnerabilities facing women and youth. UNDP will revamp existing Community Policing Forums (CPFs) in host communities adjacent to the camps to contribute to overall peaceful co-existence.-Capacity development of community leaders in conflict resolution, & youth peer-to-peer education on violence & crime prevention, including SGBV and SEA. Activity will promote positive attitudes & behaviors & offer skills & knowledge to promote peaceful coexistence, protection from violence & crime, sexual exploitation & abuse & minimize potential secondary victimization. -Improving the reporting of crimes & police response, to GBV, SEA & other crimes affecting women, children & other vulnerable groups, & expanding legal assistance services, including targeted awareness raising activities within the refugee communities on access to formal & informal conflict resolution mechanisms. The Agencies will apply an integrated approach to Protection, & increase coordination with GP, GBV, CP different Protection sector groups.-Provide targeted legal and social awareness trainings to women in camps to capacitate them on their legal rights & access to legal remedies. UNDP will work on enhancing the capacity of Union Parishad Legal Aid Committees in host communities through trainings and awareness raising on case referral mechanisms to support the most vulnerable & poor, especially rural women & girls.-UNHCR & IOM will build capacities of refugee community structures (women, men, youth, elderly, people with disabilities) & camp leadership committees on aspects of alternative dispute resolution & mediation through trainings to support peaceful coexistence. In parallel, UNDP will support local level host -communities mediation forums in Ukhiya & Teknaf through skills development & awareness raising. Good practices identified through this mechanism in local communities can be extended to mediation mechanisms within the camps
