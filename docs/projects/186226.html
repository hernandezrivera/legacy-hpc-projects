1 - Besoins / Needs

Since late 2017, the two Anglophone Regions of Cameroon are sunk into a violent conflict that has had more than 3,700 confirmed casualties (Armed Conflict Location Event Data project event database), half of them were civilians. This has created displacement for over 700,000 people (UN OCHA Situation Report August 2021), pushed 60,000 to become refugees in Nigeria, and shattered access to basic services. Inhabitants of the rural areas live constantly between the forest and the village, seeking refuge from military raids in self-settled communities with no social service. The conflict and the nature of these hard-to-reach communities isolate the population and make it difficult for them to interact with those of the urban areas, and to access services.Along long stretches of the South-West, more than 200,000 persons live with virtually no social service, including water. These are historically neglected communities, and water supply was already a problem before the crisis. The inhabitants rely on local streams and untreated water that is mixed with fertilizer, faeces, and dirt. This lack of water infrastructure affects the population, women and children have to move long distances to collect water, and this water is untreated and unsafe to them. Cholera Outbreaks have been declared since 2019 in Bakassi, Ekondo-Titi (twice), Tiko (twice), Limbe 3 (Mabeta) and Buea. The International Office for Migration (IOM) published a multi-sectoral need analysis in February 2021 covering the two Regions. Access to drinking water decreased from the 2020´s standards, as infrastructure keeps deteriorating without rehabilitation. Only in 38% of the assessed villages everyone was satisfied with their access to water. In 14% villages, almost nobody has sufficient water to meet their needs. In 39% of villages, community members had to walk between 16 and 30 minutes to access water, and in 9% of villages, more than 6%. In 24% of the villages, community members complained of non-functional water points. This 2021, Reach Out has received more than 10 alerts from community leaders in Tombel (Koupe Manengouba) and Muyuka and Mabeta (Fako) about the dire need for water supply, and several appeals to develop water infrastructure in these localities.
