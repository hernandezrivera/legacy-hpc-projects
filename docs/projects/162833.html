Project Summary

Since 2016, WRS has been implementing lifesaving projects in the Health, Nutrition, FSL and WASH in Jebel Marra localities, Azum and Umdukhun (central Darfur) and West
Darfur especially in Krenik, Kulbus, Sirba and Geneina, leveraging resources from donors including SHF, OFDA, WFP and UNICEF. The health and nutrition actions proposed here aims to address the immediate and medium term needs of more than 193,371 returnees, internally displaced people (IDPs) and host community members in both States. Amid this year 2019, WR expanded its presence in Zalingei, Bindisi and Umdkhun (CD); an in Habila and Beida localities of WD to reachout the vulnerable people. Furthermore, WR has recently conducted assessment in South Kordofan and Blue Nile in order to serve the vulnerable population in both states.
This project is designed based on WRS’s assessment done, such as KAP, SMART nutrition surveys in 2018 in Central and West Darfur. All the reports show that returnees, IDPs and some resident communities are suffering from food shortage, malnutrition, lack of clean water, poor hygiene and sanitation, epidemic diseases, and lack of access to markets and other social services.
Furthermore, the reports indicate that people are still facing critical food shortage resulted in malnutrition. Additionally, the continued currency devaluation and tremendous increase of the SDG/$ exchange rate which was brought on by internal and external factors, has drastically escalated the price of food and non-food items in targeted states.
This increase significantly impacts the nutritional and health status of the community.
The project will support 20 health facilities in Central Darfur (5 in Golo, 3 Nertiti, 3 in
Rokero, 2 in Umdukhun, 3in Zalingei, 4 in Bindisi and 1 in Azum) and 23 health facilities in West Darfur (5 in Habila, 3 in Beida, 3 in Sirba, 7 in Kreneik and 5 in Geneina); 2 in Blue Nile and 2 in South Kordofan with integrated approach, where people will get health and nutrition activities in the same facility. These include for Health; curative consultations for common ailments and a focus on maternal and child health, and community level case finding and reporting and improving community health seeking behavior. Regarding nutrition, focus will be in reducing and maintaining a mortality level below the emergency threshold. This will be achieved by implementing CMAM and IYCF activities in order to improve the nutritional status of the communities, especially in children under five years and PLW.
All the health facilities mentioned are currently supported by WR except those in South Kordofan and Blue Nile States WR is planning to expand in 2020.
All interventions will be done in partnership with government line ministries, community based committees, local authorities, community leaders, and beneficiaries themselves.
The interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries while employing approaches that increase community ownership and thus sustainability.
The project is planned with full participation of vulnerable people, community leaders, and the stakeholders. The community members have developed beneficiary selection criteria to screen eligible targets which included, Returnees, IDPs, vulnerable host communities. This project follows, a community based, gender sensitive, conflict sensitive, and environmentally friendly and sustainable approach. Effort will be made to build future resilience capacity of the targets besides the lifesaving assistance. To ensure environmental sensitivity of the project training on safe management of medical wastes and community awareness on environment are planned

1 - Needs

WRS conducted a KAP & SMART survey in May 2018; which gathered data related to health, nutrition, & WASH in all Jebel Marra localities. Additionally, WRS under DFID WASH project conducted a KAP survey in Kreneik, Sirba and Geneina localites of West Darfur state. Nutrition: The deteriorating macroeconomic situation in Sudan is worsening household food & nutrition insecurity in the country. WFP data shows a large decline in the proportion of people who can afford the local food basket in 2018. Data from WRS’s existing nutrition sites in Central Darfur and West Darfur shows that admission to OTP increased by 10% from 2018 to 2019, while cases admitted to the TSFP increased by 10.9%. WRS’s Nutrition SMART shows a GAM rate of 20% that includes a SAM rate of 4.6% & a MAM rate of 16%, which are above the WHO emergency thresholds. Health: Communicable diseases are a major cause for morbidity & mortality in our operational area, & the country remains prone to measles, meningitis, AWD & most recently 2018, cases of chikungunya & dengue fever were reported in WD. According to the WR 2019 KAP survey report, diarrhea incidence 2 weeks prior to the survey was found at 30% especially for under five children. These epidemiological data reflect similar data from health facilities in WD, whereby the top three reasons for consultations are ARI, malaria, & diarrhea diseases respectively. The impact of the problems discussed above more pronounced on women, girls & children. According to the information from FGD sessions, women & girls overburdened with domestic work such as housekeeping, childcare, food preparation, fetching firewood, collecting water from distant areas, purchasing food items. Women & girls who have to travel long distances in order to accomplish these tasks or to seek services are at greater risk of sexual & GBV, which is also heavily stigmatized & often unreported & untreated.

2 - Activities / Outputs

Activities are designed to address the most critical needs presented above to save lives & enhance resilience. Gender & protection are mainstreamed throughout, and activities are implemented in accordance with national guidelines and in collaboration with relevant authorities and line ministries. Health: Support staff, running costs, and drug supply management at 47 health facilities; Mobilize and train CHPs; Enhance reproductive healthcare services; Provide curative consultations; Conduct communicable disease surveillance, work with UN agencies, MoH, local & international NGO. In this project emphasis will be given to ensure that vulnerable including children & women are protected and served with dignity. Nutrition: TSFP & OTP services in 47 sites including screening, admission, treatment, and follow-up for children under five & PLWs; IYCF services including mobilization of CNVs, Mothers’ Support Groups, & promotion of local foods.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

World relief will ensure protection and GBV are well mainstreamed in each sector interventions do positively contribute to the right and dignity of children, women/girls, the disabled and other people with special needs. Each sector ensures that all activities will be implemented towards improving women’s access to the services, facilities and they will have an equal voice in the decision-making process. In Sudan, Women and girls are overburdened in domestic labor including preparation of food, fetching water, collecting firewood, agricultural labor during planting and harvesting seasons, marketing domestic supplies, looking after livestock, preparing meals and responsible for child care in the household. Besides, women have almost had no right to make a decision on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of refugees, returnees, IDPs and especially the most vulnerable groups such as women, girls, and minority groups. Mobile health services will be conducted to reaching out to populations who are unable to access the static facilities, thus ensuring that individuals afraid or unable to move to the static facilities are not denied assistance or services. This is particularly important for elderly and disabled beneficiaries who may be unable to walk the required distances. Besides, the strategic choice of service locations, the strategic choice of staff can also strengthen protection of beneficiaries. The project will also promote conflict sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and various tribal groups have equal access to project services by respecting their choice to agree on and areas accessible to all so as to minimize conflict due to competition over social services. In addition, the M&E system intentionally segregates data by groups to clearly measure and analyze how the project’s impact on marginalized groups.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

WR established three layers of community engagement in the project proposed locations. The first layer of engagement with the community is through community-level volunteers working on health and nutrition. The volunteers involved in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities in their respective locations. The second layer is through strengthening and working with community-level health & nutrition committees. This committee closely works with health facilities & oversee WR engagement in health/nutrition and WASH committees. The third layer is working with community-level projects management committees. The community level engagement will be materialized through conducting a regular meeting with community-level committees and volunteers. Sector officers and area coordinators regularly conduct meeting at the community level and discuss progress, challenges, future plan and opportunities, community concerns and risks. The monthly meeting feedback discussed will also be conducted on a monthly bases at States WR offices and Khartoum level with the respective program managers and Program Director. In each facilities level, WRS displays hotline reporting mechanisms so that the community members can report their level of satisfaction on the services provided by WRS and partners to relevant staff in the organization. The information obtained from the hotline report will be verified by WRS’s team and for serious allegations, a committee will be established to carry out a detailed investigation. Furthermore, WR will continue using the existing suggestion box and the new one in new sites to be placed at each health facilities in collaboration with the state ministry of health to get the feedback from beneficiary communities on health facilities services. The beneficiary community will be informed to put their feedback anytime in the suggestion box and WR health coordinator will collect information from the suggestion box on a quarterly basis and discuss with program manager, Area managers on improvements of some of the beneficiary complaints. Also, the findings from the beneficiaries will be shared with SMoH for taking collective action.
