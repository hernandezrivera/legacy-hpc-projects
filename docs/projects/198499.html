Project Summary

The 2020-2023 Pilot Programmatic Partnership (PPP), funded by the European Commission, aims to meet the immediate needs of vulnerable forcibly displaced populations both in and out-of-camp in 9 target countries: Burkina Faso, Central African Republic, Democratic Republic of Congo, Iraq, Nigeria, Somalia, South Sudan, Uganda and Syria. In Nigeria, ACTED, in collaboration with Terre des Hommes and IMPACT, has been providing life-saving assistance to vulnerable, conflict-affected populations through the delivery of critical CCCM, Shelter, NFI, HLP, child protection and Education in Emergencies services since 2020.

1 - Needs

The ongoing crisis in North Eastern Nigeria continues to have a profound impact on the population, leading to displacement both within and out of the region and high rates of unmet needs among internally displaced persons. The sudden influx of arrivals add strain to the existing infrastructure of the humanitarian response, resulting in high rates of unmet needs, overcrowding in camps, and exposure to protection risks for vulnerable groups. Borno state remains the epicentre of the crisis, containing over 80% of the region’s over 2 million internally displaced persons (IDPs), with a majority living in urban host communities or informal settlements further complicating service delivery. Indeed, regarding the CCCM sector, the camp management tracker reports indicate that over 60,000 households live in makeshift shelters, up to 1,000 are living in the open with no shelter, while over 2,000 are sharing shelter. Furthermore, the REACH MSNA shows that over 60% of Northeast Nigeria’s estimated 2.1 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) reside out of camp, in host communities. Due in part to their dispersion over large geographic areas, displaced populations residing in host communities can be more difficult to identify, access, and target than those in camps. As a subset of host communities, informal sites and settlements (ISETs) are particularly vulnerable. Residents of ISETs live in hyper-dense areas characterized by extreme poverty and marginalization, with limited access to basic services, and are often not receiving humanitarian assistance. In addition, many vulnerable displaced persons face difficulties in accessing humanitarian assistance due to security and access constraints, which is seriously curtailing shelter and NFI partners from providing assistance required. Furthermore, there are 235 informal camps in the BAY states, and 42% of these sites remain without dedicated or mobile site facilitation. In Borno, 83% are without facilitation, 20% in Yobe, and 4% in Adamawa. Among the formal camps, only 44% have facilitation in Borno and 2% in Adamawa. In terms of shelter’s needs, the report reveals that 44% of the IDPs are living in emergency shelter, 35% in makeshift, 11% in collective communal shelter, 9% in public facilities and1% in transitional shelter. Furthermore, 10% of shelters are damaged, 89% of HH are in a form of shelter only, and 1% of HH is sharing shelters. The Prolonged closure of schools due to COVID-19 has shown to negatively impact the well-being of children, and to weaken their protective environment. Although the huge majority recognized education as being a strong concern, the main challenges identified for children attending school vary significantly. Indeed, male adolescents and SBMC staff reported a lack of support from parents and caregivers, while women mentioned COVID19 as a significant hindrance, along with poor infrastructure (e.g. tables and chairs). As for adolescent girls, it has been reported that many children attending school resort to hawking.

2 - Activities

ACTED-IMPACT's REACH initiative support to an evidence-based humanitarian response will enable actors to plan and implement an appropriate crisis-wide response to the priority needs of displaced populations in target countries. Building on its experience with the COVID-19 Child Protection (CP)/ Education in Emergency (EiE)strategy, Tdh will continue to implement a flexible approach retaining the good practices REACH information management activities include among others:- Provide assessment, information management and analysis support to relevant humanitarian coordination structures-Joint Market Monitoring (JMMI)- Emergency Prioritisation through Rapid Assessments - Population Movement Assessments and Research

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED and IMPACT will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. All ACTED and IMPACT staff will uphold protection principles at all times, as signatories to ACTED’s Code of Conduct, including ACTED’s Policy on PSEA. Accountability will also stem strongly from the CRM, ensuring timely and appropriate response to complaints

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED’s established Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM),mainstreamed throughout all ACTED-led interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. ACTED’s CRM enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. CRM mediums include complaints boxes, complaints desks, a telephone hotline and email. ACTED’s independent AME Unit will maintain responsibility for collecting and responding to all reports, addressing complaints through protection-centered case-management support in a timely and transparent manner.ACTED’s CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as ACTED continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. All ACTED staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct.
