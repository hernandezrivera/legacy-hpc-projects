Project Summary

According to the findings from protection return monitoring survey and participatory assessments targeting Somali returnee families and students conducted in areas of return in 2021, access to education remains one of the key protection challenges affecting students’ retention in schools and the sustainable reintegration of returnee children. This is further exacerbated by the adverse impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. Fifty per cent of the returnee children who participated in the post return monitoring survey reported incidents of high school drop-out. This was particularly true for girls due to early and forced marriage, lack of ability to pay fees, engagement with house chores and administrative challenges/costs associated with equating academic documents.
Both boys and girls are forced into child labour to generate income for families and are also engaged in illicit and illegal activities. Boys continue to suffer from forced recruitment as reported by the monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM 2020). According to the Protection Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), 593,000 people were displaced in Somalia between January and September 2021, among whom 420,000 were displaced due to conflict and 98,000 due to drought. Among those displaced this year, an estimated 237,200 (40%) are school-aged children.
In addressing the key gaps and needs of returnee children, UNHCR interventions will be guided by the UNHCR Education Strategy (2020 –2022) and the National Education Strategic Sector Plan. UNHCR will support a total number of 65,847 returnee children including 23,500 girls as well as children with living with disabilities for one-academic year , UNHCR will reinforce access to education through rehabilitation of schools, provision of education grants and scholastic materials (books, uniforms, stationery, etc.) to each refugee returnee child enrolled in school. Additionally, parents will receive a cash grant for school-going children through mobile money transfers one-time of $35 per child in south central,$25 for Somaliland and $50 for Puntland per academic year . In addition to individual level support, UNHCR will support existing schools in areas of return to increase their absorption capacity through provision of teaching and learning materials and rehabilitation of school infrastructure (classrooms, construction of gender specific latrines, playgrounds and provision of sporting facilities/items, water points/hand washing sinks, etc.)
UNHCR will also conduct teachers' training and awareness raising for community education committees on child protection, PSNs, SGBV/PSEA and rights of refugee returnees. In coordination with the Ministry of Education and relevant partners, UNHCR will also support specific interventions aimed at improving school attendance and retention of students, in particular of girls and children with disabilities. Example include providing sanitary and hygiene materials and ramps for disable. Inclusion will be key to UNHCR’s approach; as such, UNHCR will support the Ministry of Education in integrating returnee children into the national public school system to prevent the continuation of NGO-managed parallel systems. Overall, UNHCR will work closely with the Ministry of Education and the Education Cluster, adopting an area-based approach, where the emphasis will be placed on supporting and bolstering existing systems to increase reintegration capacities. UNHCR will work with partners to support training of teachers and school management committees on inclusive education, provision of assistive devices and psychosocial support to vulnerable learners. Lastly, UNHCR will support Ministry of Education to advance the implementation of commitments and pledges made during the Global Refugee Forum as well as the IGAD/ Djibouti Declaration and translate them into concrete actions.
UNHCR will ensure that targeted children are engaged in identifying needs and right based approach are impeded in project circle.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The aim of using cash is to restore and maintain access to safe and quality education, particularly supporting out of school children to quickly enter or return to quality learning opportunities. Given the magnitude of needs in Somalia, these objectives will also help to minimize the impacts of crises on the right to education and children’s learning. In addition, this will also support interventions aimed at greater school attendance and retention of students, in particular of girls. UNHCR will assist returning refugees mainly from Kenya, Yemen and Djibouti in accessing education through provision of education grants and scholastic materials (books, uniforms, stationery, etc.) to the parents of each refugee returnee child enrolled in school, parents will receive a cash grant for school-going children through mobile money transfers one-time of $35 per child in south central,$25 for Somaliland and $50 for Puntland per academic year. This will be paid in one or two instalments.UNHCR through partners will use the attendance of the pupils to pay the cash transfers to the parents, also after verification of the accuracy of the attendance by the partners education officers and MoE quality officers than the schools will be paid through their cooperate accounts.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

UNHCR applies the Age and Gender diversity approach and vulnerability criteria for the selection of beneficiaries for all its activities, which considers specific needs that are related to gender and age dynamics. Application of the vulnerability criteria ensures an equitable approach, while also addressing specific risks of exclusion. For example, provision of sanitary kits to girl pupils to encourage girl-child education and minimize the number of girls dropping out of schools and absenteeism thus enhancing retention and completion of school cycle. Also, UNHCR supports the construction and rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines and water points/hand washing sinks and sporting facilities in schoolsExamples of mitigation measures includes:• Conducting regular safety audits and rapid gender analysis to identify context-specific GBV risks and vulnerable groups in the camp/site• Regular coordination with protection cluster and GBV AoR, education cluster and their partners• Training of CCCM staff on GBV risk mitigation, core concepts and guiding principles, as well as identification of risks and mitigation measures, Codes of Conduct, PSEA and AAP• Ensuring CCCM teams have trained female staff working on sites• Systematically collecting and analysing sex and age disaggregated data to identify differential needs and access to assistance• Ensuring diverse groups of women are included in community governance structures and consulted on CCCM program design • Establishing feedback and complaints mechanisms through which community members can relay their concerns related to site infrastructure or service provision• Supporting GBV coordination mechanisms and development/updating of service mapping and referral pathways• Supporting CCCM staff to safely and ethically link survivors of GBV to available specialized services or referral pathways in case of disclosure of an incident
