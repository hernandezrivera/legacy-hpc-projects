Project Summary

Recognizing the heightened vulnerability of women and girls to safety and security incidents, UN Women aims to strengthen the leadership role of women in promoting peace and security while fostering social cohesion through targeted initiatives across 10 locations in Cox’s Bazar. This includes 8 camps (Camps 1E, 1W, 3, 4, 4 Extension, 5, 14, and 18) and 2 host communities (Ukhiya-Palongkhali Union and Teknaf-Whykong Union).
Aligned with the Protection Sector's Objective 6 under the JRP 2025, the project ensures that women and girls play an active role in advancing peace and security, while building their confidence in such efforts. It fosters trust-building between refugee and host communities and elevates the role of women leaders in community resilience and protection through women peacebuilders and self-organized women’s groups. These approaches reflect the Protection Sector's vision for 2025-26, emphasizing local women-led initiatives. Drawing on UN Women’s expertise in gender-responsive programming, the project will be implemented through three interconnected approaches:
To promote peace and confidence building through community engagement, 150 women peacebuilders (100 Rohingya, 50 host community) will lead community-driven initiatives, including structured peace dialogue to address resource disputes and inter-community tensions in camps and host communities facing resource competition. They will facilitate participatory drama and arts-based activities to build trust and mutual understanding. "Peace Cafés" will amplify the voices of women leaders, share practical solutions, and advocate for sustained coexistence and social cohesion. Forty community volunteers (25 women, 15 men) will conduct outreach, delivering peace messaging through household visits and community sessions, while supporting early warning systems to address potential conflicts and foster trust-building.
To support women-led initiatives on peace and security, self-organized women's networks will empower Rohingya women leaders through practical leadership training and peer mentoring, building on UN Women's established presence in 8 camps. Supported by WRO partners, these networks will address barriers to peace & security, map service gaps, facilitate community dialogues, and advocate for equitable decision-making in camp governance and service delivery.
To enable evidence-based programming, the project will produce gender and security analyses informed by community consultations and assessments. These analyses will guide improvements in service delivery, ensuring alignment with the specific needs of women and girls, especially those with intersecting vulnerabilities.
By positioning women as leaders in peacebuilding activities, the project will contribute to safer, more resilient communities across Rohingya and host populations. These efforts will reinforce the humanitarian and civilian character of camp settings and promote sustainable social cohesion.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The protection landscape in Cox's Bazar has reached a critical point in 2024, marked by escalating organized crime, increased violence, and deteriorating security in both refugee camps and host communities. This context disproportionately impacts women, girls, and gender-diverse individuals, and those with intersectional vulnerabilities. Joint Protection Monitoring Reports (Q1-Q3 2024) document concerning trends: physical assaults involving gunshots, extortion, forced recruitment and abductions have driven many men to flee camps, leaving women as primary protectors. Now responsible for household security, women face heightened risks of violence, trafficking, and exploitation, often unreported. Lack of empowerment severely restricts women's access to protection services including GBV. Restrictions on women’s movement underpinned by regressive gender norms are reflected by ISNA 2024 data, which reveals only 46.2% of female-headed households report income, compared to 75% of male-headed ones. The burden of unpaid care work falls heavily on women, with 48% spending over seven hours daily on caregiving compared to 15.2% of men. Economic dependency, coupled with limited mobility and restricted decision-making roles, creates cycles of vulnerability that increase women’s risk of protection related harms. Community safety concerns have intensified as well. Protection Monitoring (Q3 2024) indicates that intimate partner violence now constitutes 76.4% of reported GBV cases, underscoring the need for expanded survivor-centered services. ISNA 2024 further reveals: 31.77% of women feel unsafe using camp facilities at night. The recent influx of new arrivals has intensified competition for limited resources, escalating tensions, particularly affecting women and girls who rely on community networks for protection. Moreover, awareness of complaint mechanisms remains low among women (65.9% compared to 78.5% for men), indicating significant barriers to accessing protection services. These dynamics underscore the urgent need for targeted social cohesion and peacebuilding initiatives. Existing protection mechanisms remain insufficiently gender responsive. The systematic exclusion of women from community-level decision-making undermines protection effectiveness. Despite the National Action Plan on Women, Peace and Security (2019-2025) emphasizing women's participation in peacebuilding, their voices remain marginalized in community governance and conflict resolution. The success of existing women-led initiatives, including women peacebuilder groups, demonstrates the untapped potential of women's leadership in peacebuilding and protection responses. Protection data is often incomplete, lacking gender and intersectional insights (GiHA WG analysis, 2024). The analysis highlights that the absence of sex, age, and disability disaggregated data obscures the specific protection needs of women, girls, and marginalized groups, hampering targeted response planning. These compounded challenges necessitate a strengthened, gender-responsive protection approach that positions woman as leaders in community safety and social cohesion. Evidence demonstrates the effectiveness of women's leadership in building community resilience and enhancing sustainable protection for all. The pressing protection context in Cox’s Bazar demands expanded support for these proven approaches while strengthening institutional capacity for gender-responsive service delivery.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Safety, dignity, and Do No Harm: Safeguarding standards are implemented across all activities, including partner management. All implementing partners maintain PSEA Network membership, trained PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points, and required government permits. (ASK will be phased out by December 2024, and new WROs will undergo UN Women's IP PSEA Capacity Assessment to ensure PSEA compliance.) Safeguarding standards will guide the interventions, with dedicated training on PSEA for all staff, including GiHA camp focal points and community volunteers. Each member will receive training in humanitarian principles and survivor-centered care, emphasizing harm prevention amid rising insecurities. Meaningful access and non-discrimination: To ensure accessible and non-discriminatory service delivery, the project will implement community outreach and appropriate referrals with specific focus on reaching women, girls, and those with intersectional vulnerabilities who face barriers to protection. GiHA focal points will provide tailored gender sensitization trainings based on emerging needs and coordinate closely with protection actors to ensure accessible and inclusive services. Accountability and participation: Participatory approaches and WRO partnerships promote local ownership in program design and implementation and women’s equal participation in decision-making structures. Periodic assessments by community volunteers create feedback mechanisms for continuous improvement. The project builds sustainable local capacity through training on PSEA and safeguarding protocols. Transportation support and background checks for volunteers reinforce safety and accountability. Protection integration and joint responsibility: The project will collaborate closely with UNHCR and ISCG to align with the Protection Sector’s objectives. Women-led networks will serve as active agents to promote gender-responsive leadership and social cohesion between refugee and host communities.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The UN Women project prioritizes the "leave no one behind" principle, targeting age- and disability-inclusive interventions within refugee camps and host communities. The project will address the unique vulnerabilities of gender-diverse persons, elderly women, and women and girls with disabilities, who face compounded protection risks due to mobility limitations, isolation, and limited access to services. GiHA camp focal points and community volunteers will be trained on age and disability inclusion, equipping them to provide tailored outreach and support. Each community volunteer is trained on disability inclusion, enabling them to meet diverse needs within the community and conduct outreach to those who face barriers in accessing protection services. These volunteers are also trained to conduct household visits and offer direct support to women, girls, and elderly individuals who experience mobility challenges, ensuring accessibility. The project collaborates with local women-led and women’s rights organizations, enhancing their capacity to contribute to gender-responsive, rights-based protection which pursues an intersectional approach, addressing the specific challenges faced by elderly women and women with disabilities. These local partners refer those individuals to appropriate, rights-based protection services and support. The project emphasizes the collection of disaggregated data by age and disability to inform program design and implementation. This data-driven approach enables the identification of gaps and the tailoring of services to meet the specific needs of different groups, ensuring that interventions are both effective and inclusive.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project upholds AAP by integrating feedback mechanisms, ensuring meaningful participation, and fostering adaptive programming. UN Women prioritizes crisis-affected women and girls in decision-making processes throughout the program cycle, aligning with the IASC Revised Commitments on AAP and PSEA. UN Women emphasizes incorporating a gender analysis that considers intersecting identities throughout all phases of humanitarian programming. This ensures programs are responsive and inclusive, addressing diverse needs related to diverse SOGIESC, disabilities, and migratory status. To reinforce AAP principles in 2025, UN Women will support women leaders and self-organized women’s groups, enhancing direct engagement and support aligned with AAP Strategic Objective 5. By involving affected communities directly in program implementation, the project fosters empowerment and promotes sustainable, community-driven protection and resilience efforts in both communities. The project implements accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms, providing safe, confidential channels through suggestion boxes, direct reporting to trained staff, and anonymous options. All staff and volunteers are trained in PSEA and safeguarding protocols to manage feedback ethically and uphold confidentiality standards. Culturally appropriate information on protection services, rights, and complaint mechanisms is disseminated, considering literacy levels, language needs, and accessibility requirements to ensure inclusivity, particularly for individuals with intersectional vulnerabilities. UN Women partners with local WROs to build sustainable accountability practices, strengthening local capacities to manage feedback effectively and upload service delivery. Trained Rohingya community volunteers conduct household visits and community group meetings to gather feedback and share protection messaging. This participatory approach reinforces accountability and enhances resilience.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ASK will be phased out by December 2024, and new WROs will undergo UN Women's IP PSEA Capacity Assessment to ensure PSEA compliance. UN Women prioritizes protecting individuals in all their diversity from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through robust governance frameworks and operations. A survivor-centered approach aligned with the IASC's core principles promotes compliance with humanitarian standards and advances for gender equality, women's empowerment, and protecting women's rights in humanitarian action. UN Women is an active member of the PSEA network, Protection Sector, GBV Sub-Sector, Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector, Accountability for Affected Populations Working Group, and GiHA Working Group, ensuring it is well-informed of each sector's discussions and strategy development. UN Women will continue to ensure, in coordination with the PSEA Network Coordinator, that all staff, partner personnel, and volunteers are trained on PSEA principles and codes of conduct, ensuring compliance with inter-agency standards. UN Women PSEA focal points and reference points are linked to the inter-agency PSEA network, and the focal point information is displayed in the office and the MPWCs. During the fieldwork, partners, and volunteers closely assess the gender situation in the camps and host community, including PSEA, and report and escalate issues following the reporting lines. UN Women also established the PSEA feedback and complaint mechanisms at the MPWCs, available in written and verbal forms through the box or the hotline number, and ensured client confidentiality and safety while processing the cases. UN Women ensures survivor-centered service assistance by providing MPWC-based Psychosocial and SRHR services for SEA survivors. It also ensures service linkages with rehabilitation/safe shelter for survivors through an inter-agency referral pathway. MPWC is the available economic empowerment hub for SEA survivors.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UN Women strives for a resilient and empowered community, emphasizing skills building of vulnerable groups of women and girls in disaster-prone areas, conducting capacity-building at the community level, strengthening skills for gender-responsive disaster preparedness among women leaders and local women’s rights organizations in the context of the Rohingya humanitarian response. In 2025, as part of its support to strengthen the leadership of women and women-led organizations, UN Women will invest in developing the skills and knowledge of women leaders in Rohingya camps and host communities in disaster-prone communities for disaster risk management and emergency preparedness. This includes skills in related needs assessments, capacity development for emergency preparedness, and distribution of emergency kits for 100 refugees and emergency preparedness items through a local WRO partner. In addition, UN Women plans to strengthen the capacities of CiCs to enhance their sensitivity to the specific needs of women and girls impacted by disasters in the context of the Rohingya refugee response.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

This project embeds EPR measures aligned with inter-agency standards and cyclone/fire SOPs to address natural disasters, security incidents, and health emergencies. 140 female volunteers will receive two-day trainings in DRR and EPR, including gender-sensitive approaches to ensure inclusivity. A ToT will be conducted for WRO partners, ensuring broader outreach and preparedness. Emergency supplies, including dignity kits and essential materials, will be strategically pre-positioned at MPWCs to ensure immediate access for women and girls with intersectional vulnerabilities. To mitigate climate-induced risks, women’s networks and peacebuilders will be trained to implement anticipatory actions and promote climate resilience within their communities. These measures, integrated with ISCG’s EPR guidelines, enhance community-driven preparedness. UN Women collaborates with women-led organizations and community women's networks, reinforcing gender-responsive decision-making in emergency preparedness and response. SADDD data will guide tailored interventions, ensuring the needs of vulnerable groups are met. Through participatory approaches, women leaders will co-design and implement emergency plans, strengthening their decision-making roles in disaster preparedness committees. These measures ensure the meaningful representation of women and girls across communities, shaping protective, resilient communities during emergencies.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project actively engages youth, aligning with the IASC Guidelines on Working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises and the UN Women Youth and Gender Equality Strategy. Through the targeted outreach, the project engages young men and boys and amplifies the meaningful participation of young women (voice), enabling them to advocate for gender-responsive community protection initiatives, and increasing the representation of young women in community decision-making platforms. These efforts align with the IASC’s focus on youth-sensitive program design and implementation, ensuring that youth are active contributors to social cohesion. By prioritizing young women’s perspectives in prevention and response strategies, the project fosters environments where they can advocate for their specific protection needs. Recognizing youth in all their diversity, the project positions young women as meaningful agents of change, promoting safer, more inclusive communities.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project promotes refugee long-term self-reliance and resilience through capacity building of women leaders in protection and peacebuilding. Women's networks lead community-based protection initiatives, fostering a sense of ownership and self-confidence, reducing dependency on external actors. Through leadership development, women peacebuilder groups and female volunteers acquire transferable skills in community mobilization, and early warning systems. The project strengthens social resilience through inter-community dialogue and joint initiatives, while building women's confidence to engage in decision-making processes. By fostering women's meaningful participation and representation in protection and social cohesion efforts, the project reduces vulnerabilities while contributing to sustainable, community-driven solutions.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

In host communities, the project aligns with Cox's Bazar District Development priorities by strengthening local protection and justice mechanisms. The engagement of 50 host community women peacebuilders in peacebuilding initiatives supports district-level social cohesion objectives while building local capacity, engaging with local security actors. The project collaborates with local WROs to strengthen sustainable protection mechanisms, fostering host community resilience and contributing to long-term development goals.
