Project Summary

The South Sudan Education Cluster (SSEC) HNRP 2025, seeks to respond to a target of 676,727 children and teachers with EIE interventions for learning continuity. This project aims to address urgent education needs of vulnerable learners affected by crises, ensuring that children and youth have access to safe, inclusive, and quality education. Focusing on three key SSEC objectives—Access, Quality, and Capacity—we strive to create a supportive learning environment that promotes resilience and educational continuity for those most affected children by natural and man-made emergencies in Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang in Upper Nile, and Tonj North in Warrap State. The project targets a total of 4016 beneficiaries, including 3900 learners, 82 teachers, and 34 PTA/SMCs from the SSEC target, with a focus on reaching 40% female participants within the overall target. Key activities align with the education cluster's objectives and adhere to minimum standards set by MOGEI and the Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE), with a strong focus on child safeguarding principles to ensure the safe delivery of education services.
1. Access: This focuses on improving access to education for vulnerable children and youth by establishing and rehabilitating safe, inclusive learning spaces. Key activities: Establishment/Rehabilitation of Learning Spaces: Establish 4 new and rehabilitate 6 existing learning spaces to provide safe, inclusive learning environments. Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH): Rehabilitate 6 sanitation facilities and conduct hygiene promotion campaigns in learning spaces to ensure a clean and healthy environment for students and teachers. Enrolment and Registration: Conduct 3 "Back to Learning" campaigns within communities to encourage school enrolment. Psychosocial Support: Provide psychosocial support for children and teachers to cope with trauma and stress, fostering their ability to learn and thrive. Child Safeguarding Policies: Train 100 teachers on child safeguarding policies and the teacher code of conduct to ensure a safe and protective learning environment. Provision of Assistive Devices: Provide assistive devices (e.g., wheelchairs, hearing aids, braille materials) to 40 children with disabilities to facilitate their full participation in learning. Distribution of Hygiene Kits: Distribute 5,000 hygiene kits to learners to promote personal hygiene and prevent the spread of diseases in emergency settings.
2. Quality: This focuses on improving the quality of education by providing essential learning resources and teacher support. Key activities; Provision of 70 Early Childhood Development (ECD) Kits with age-appropriate learning and play materials to support the cognitive, social, and emotional development of young children during emergencies. Learning and Teaching Kits: Distribute 40 kits essential learning materials, including books, stationery, and other educational tools, to enhance the teaching and learning experience. 82 teacher training on Pedagogical Skills: Offer professional development programs, focusing on emergency-responsive pedagogy, child-centred teaching methods, and trauma-sensitive approaches to address the needs of children affected by crises.
3. Capacity: This focuses on building local capacities for disaster risk reduction (DRR) and ensuring the long-term sustainability of education interventions. Key activities; Parent-Teacher Association (PTA) Training: Provide DRR training to 34 PTA groups to help communities better prepare for future emergencies and mitigate the impacts of disasters on education.
Expected Outcomes: Improved access to safe, inclusive, and protective learning environments for children in emergencies. Enhanced learning outcomes through the provision of quality educational materials, teacher training, and psychosocial support. Strengthened community resilience and capacity to respond to future crises through active participation in disaster risk reduction.

1. Needs

Since gaining independence in 2011, South Sudan has faced severe social, political, and economic challenges, worsened by man-made and natural disasters. Vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, and children, continue to endure high protection risks, including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) and child protection concerns. Food insecurity has been exacerbated by climate change and conflict, with flooding and dry spells disrupting agriculture, and insecurity limiting access to farmlands. Inflation has further strained families’ ability to meet basic needs. The latest IPC data (Sept 2024–July 2025) estimates 6.3 million people in IPC Phase 3 (crisis), 1.71 million in Phase 4 (emergency), and 41,000 in Phase 5 (catastrophe). This has led to rising malnutrition, with 2.1 million children aged 0-5 expected to suffer from acute malnutrition. The country also faces inadequate access to essential social services, including education and healthcare. In the 2023/2024 fiscal year, only 8% of the national budget was allocated to healthcare, and just 8.3% to education, hindering service delivery amidst ongoing insecurity and environmental challenges.South Sudan Education System continues to face significant challenges in providing safe, inclusive, and quality education, particularly in emergency contexts. According to the 2023 Annual Education Census (AEC), despite a 12% increase in learners’ enrolment from 1,974,667 in 2022 to 2,215,494 in 2023 (1,176,239 boys and 1,039,255 girls) at the pre-primary, primary, and secondary levels, access to education remains severely constrained. This is especially true for an estimated 2.8 million out-of-school children, as reported by the South Sudan Education Cluster Strategy (SSEC 2023-2025, p. 8). One of the key challenges is the non-functionality of schools, with 16% (1,269) of the total 7,737 schools reported as non-functional (AEC, p. 10). Climate change-related events, such as floods and strong winds, have destroyed classrooms and worsened access constraints (AEC, p. 21). Additionally, a shortage of adequate infrastructure, delays in teacher salaries, school occupations, and ongoing conflict continue to hinder the education system. Another significant challenge is the shortage of classrooms, with 23% of classrooms still operating under trees (SSEC, 2023-2025). This lack of conducive learning spaces undermines students' ability to learn effectively. Moreover, learners with disabilities face substantial barriers to education due to the absence of disability-friendly infrastructure, which would help facilitate their learning experience. Gender disparities are also evident within the teaching workforce, where only 20% of the 49,536 teachers are female. Furthermore, 37% of the teaching workforce consists of volunteer teachers who lack the professional skills necessary to provide quality education (AEC, 2023, p. 40). This issue is compounded by an inadequate supply of learning materials, with a learner-to-textbook ratio of 6:1, indicating a critical need for more educational resources. Protection risks, such as those resulting from conflict, remain a significant concern for education in South Sudan. According to a June 2024 protection analysis update by the Protection Cluster, gender-based violence (GBV), denial of services, child separation, and marginalization are the highest protection risks in South Sudan. Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang, Gogrial West, and Tonj North experiencing high food insecurity levels, are particularly affected by high levels of protection risks. Early/forced marriage and gender disparities, further exacerbate the situation, especially for children and women. In summary, South Sudan's education system faces numerous challenges including infrastructure deficits, a shortage of qualified teachers, gender disparities, and the impact of conflict and climate change. Addressing these issues is crucial to ensuring access to quality education for learners in emergencies situations
