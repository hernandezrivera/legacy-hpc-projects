Project Summary

The situation across South Sudan remains fragile with insecurity, socio economic conditions, and natural disasters driving the needs in the worse affected areas. The proposed project for the provision of Safe and Protective Education in Emergency in the conflict affected Areas of Manyo, Makal and Panyikang Counties that has been severely affected by the closure of schools as result of widespread insecurities which have limited access to learning activities, exacerbated by the socio economic conditions of the communities
This project is an opportunity for a total of 14,222 people, (150 education officials and 14,072 children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) to enroll in schools in the conflict affected areas Manyo (2,493 M & 2,811 F), Panyikang (2,752M & 3,116F) and Makal (1,500M & 1,400F) respectively. • Established/rehabilitated 18 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS): 6 TLS in Panyikang, 8 TLS in Manyo and 4 TLS in Makal Counties respectively in line with INEE's Minimum Standards for Education and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy • ROAD will also construct 34 block of ( one for ladies and one block for men) gender sensitive WASH facilities 12 in Panyikang, 16 in Manyo and 6 in Makal Counties respectively in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard • 127 teachers will be trained on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills in both Panyikang (9F & 43M), Manyo (8F & 37M) and Makal (5 F & 25 M) Counties respectively • ROAD will distribute 15 learning and recreational material to children and youth (6-17 years) Panyikang, Manyo and Makal Counties respectively • ROAD will establish 23 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and will trained education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)
ROAD is responding to the challenges face by school going children and education sector as a whole in Manyo, Makal and Panyikang Counties respectively to support the above mentioned education activities including teacher’s incentive. Support to teachers have been limited and is not commensurate with what is being offered by partners within humanitarian sector leading to most of teachers abandoning teaching to seek for greener pasture. The inconsistency of pay and lack of alignment of salary since the economic collapse has greatly affected teachers. The learning environment where girl child is not secure, limited sanitary support for adolescent girls, WASH facilities and school distance play role and have hampered efforts to keep children especially girls in schools

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face multiple compounding shocks and stressors. Insecurity, sub-national violence, regional and national macroeconomic challenges – intensified by the global economic downturn, the impact of climate change and the ongoing Sudan conflict – all adversely affect the food security of South Sudanese families. As of 16 October, 317,993 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan since the onset of the conflict in April. Overall, half of the arrivals are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 88.5 per cent of all arrivals. The estimated 3.4 million girls and boys between 3-17 years of age are in need of education. The school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures since 2013 have resulted in an increased number of children out of school. The inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need (PiN) revealed that there are needs in every county of the country. Of the 9.4 million people in need, an estimated 56,325 people will experience catastrophic need in Panyikang County. Moreover, there will be 7.6 million people experiencing extreme need in 66 out of 78 counties and 1.7 million people in severe need in 10 out of 78 counties. Upper Nile and Western Equatoria States will contain the highest number of counties in critical need. ROAD will provide essential EiE services in Upper Nile State to 14,072 children, adolescents and youth (3-17 years old) who will be enrolled in schools in affected areas of Manyo (2,493 M & 2,811 F), Panyikang (2,752M & 3,116F) and Makal (1,500M & 1,400F) respectively. • Established/rehabilitated 18 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS): 6 TLS in Panyikang, 8 TLS in Manyo and 4 TLS in Makal Counties respectively in line with INEE's Minimum Standards for Education and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy • ROAD will also construct 34 block of ( one for ladies and one block for men) gender sensitive WASH facilities 12 in Panyikang, 16 in Manyo and 6 in Makal Counties respectively in line with MoGEI/Education Cluster standard • 127 teachers will be trained on EiE, subject matter, and pedagogical skills in both Panyikang (9F & 43M), Manyo (8F & 37M) and Makal (5 F & 25 M) Counties respectively • ROAD will distribute 15 learning and recreational material to children and youth (6-17 years) Panyikang, Manyo and Makal Counties respectively • ROAD will establish 23 school management committees (SMC) members, Parent Teacher Associations (PTA), and will trained education authorities on school management, governance, climate change and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) to assist the children affected by the crises (IDPs, returnees and host communities) to transition back to school and reduce their risk of dropout ROAD will make use of its long-term operational and static presence in Upper Nile State where the organization is currently implementing UNICEF project on Construction of Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) to Increase enrolment and retention of out of School Children (OOSC) in Makal County in line with Education cluster objectives and strategies
