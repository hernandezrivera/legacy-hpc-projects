Project Summary

UNICEF WASH core pipeline supplies project will reach 800,000 beneficiaries through provision to people (IDPs and communities affected by other emergencies) with immediate WASH needs through planning, procurement, prepositioning, distribution, and management of critical WASH supplies. The project will contribute to Cluster objectives 1, 2 and 3 through provision water treatment chemicals, spare parts for hand pump rehabilitation/repair, latrine slabs (squatting pans), plastic tarpaulins for latrine construction, latrine digging kits, hygiene kits (jerry-cans, buckets, soap, menstrual hygiene health materials, and dignity items). UNICEF will procure the WASH supplies and in coordination with the WASH Cluster pre-position them in strategic locations throughout South Sudan. Supplies will be released to partners through the approval of the WASH Cluster, and the receiving partner will be responsible for the distribution of the supplies. The WASH supplies will be accessible to all WASH partners currently operational in South Sudan through WASH Cluster structures at national and state levels. The project will distribute WASH supplies as per Sphere and South Sudan WASH Cluster standards and in coordination with other clusters (Health, and Nutrition). Through this project UNICEF will focus on delivering rapid assistance in all counties and populations affected by a sudden or rapid-onset disaster and targeting at the county level will be done through WASH Cluster updates or using various assessment findings (IRNAs, NAWG recommendations, WASH severity classification). Follow-up monitoring and capacity building of partners will be conducted throughout the project period to ensure proper management of supplies at various warehouses. UNICEF in coordination with beneficiary partners will conduct end user monitoring of the supplies to ensure that supplies distributed are reaching the vulnerable communities and are being used properly.

1. Needs

Poor access to clean water, hygiene, and sanitation services combined with high levels offood insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudanand contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Lack of access towater has led to tension and conflict between communities over competition of alreadyscarce resources. The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are inUpper Nile, followed by Jonglei and Central Equatoria. Every fifth person is unable tocollect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water forhandwashing. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecuritycontinue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the highprevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rateshave also high WASH needs and are prioritized by the WASH Cluster for multi-sectoralresponse for famine prevention. Mothers and caretakers of malnourished children lackbasic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domiciles, jeopardizing the benefitsgained in nutrition programs. Additionally, continued insecurity, disease outbreaks,nutrition crises and other natural emergencies combined with economic crises and poorinfrastructure throughout the country highlight the need for UNICEF and humanitarianpartners to continue providing emergency services to vulnerable communities. UNICEF isthe Cluster lead for WASH and manages part of the core pipeline supplies on behalf of thecluster partners. UNICEF will continue to work in coordination with Nutrition and HealthClusters in delivering the services. Targeting at county level will be conducted by partnersthrough assessments.
