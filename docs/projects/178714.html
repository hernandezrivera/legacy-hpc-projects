Project Summary

In line with the acute needs and the Sector strategy for 2021, PIN plans to respond to the WASH needs in Northern Syria by delivering humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people. PIN will focus on both distributions of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion and improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services. Interventions will primarily target newly displaced/returned populations in informal settlements, host communities, and camps, based upon coordination with relevant cluster partners to maximize response coverage and minimize gaps. Technical design and proposed level of services provided through these interventions will follow acceptable standards such as SPHERE and those established by the Northern Syria WASH Cluster and consider varying needs among different groups in the community, such as women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Capacity building for local councils will be provided by PIN staff through field coaching sessions focusing on operational aspects and management of water systems and solar power plants.
PIN is planning to implement WASH activities in Schools and Health facilities, taking into consideration the following locations: Locations with WASH infrastructure damaged by war, locations with high IDP population, locations with non-functioning WASH infrastructure indirectly affected by war - unstable electricity supply, lack of maintenance, lack of skilled staff for maintenance, etc., locations where non-existent or malfunctioning WASH infrastructure presents a significant risk to the environment and public health.
PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups through employing a wide spectrum of different activities, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable, and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PIN’s programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels from cross-border, to hub, to district and village levels. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of projects.

4 - Needs

With the Syrian conflict verging on its eleventh year, the humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate, impacting all life aspects of the Syrian families and further increasing their struggles to meet their basic needs. As indicated in the 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), WASH systems suffered from damages due to direct or indirect attacks, years of functioning at high capacity, limited to no maintenance, effects of climate change, and poor water resources management.PIN has conducted a needs assessment in Idleb governorate in March 2021. One of the findings is that in all assessed locations, the functionality of water and sanitation WASH services relies entirely on humanitarian assistance. In villages, lack of financial capacity shortage does not allow local authorities to cover the operational cost for more than a few months. This forces communities to depend on paid water trucking, which is a significant barrier as the vulnerable families cannot afford to pay the costs. Similarly, for sanitation, camps suffer the absence of a functional sewage network which poses several risks such as exposure to diseases. The overall findings align with the 2021 HNO report, which states that 27% of interviewed HHs in IDP sites in northern Syria indicated they are forced to purchase water to meet or complement their water needs PIN conducted a technical assessment of February 2021 in NES that reinforces WASH and general rehabilitation needs in schools. Particularly in Manbij, all assessed schools had no latrines for students’ use. Furthermore, schools had no consistent water supply, and the schools within the camp had no reliable water supply; only water trucked to the camp.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN plans to implement activities such as distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion. Hygiene kits are based on WASH cluster guidance, including soap, sanitary products, shampoo and dish soap, etc. The distributions are always followed by PDM conducted by PIN’s (MEAL) team to ascertain satisfaction levels. PIN will also undertake hygiene awareness activities and specific messages tailored to the context, such as COVID-19 mitigation measures, prevention and treatment of diarrhea; proper handwashing procedure; potable water safety; human/solid/HH waste disposal; Leishmaniosis identification and prevention; and oral hygiene. PIN aims to provide maintenance and rehabilitation of water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems. The targeting criteria include vulnerable war-affected communities, including schools and health facilities, where intervention can significantly improve access to basic WASH services. PIN will provide training/mentoring to institutions and assist in maintenance and technical/management support to promote sustainability of services. In villages with funding needs to support the ongoing operations of water pumps/networks, PIN will provide subsidies to the service department to ensure the uninterrupted operation of the rehabilitated infrastructure and stimulate community fee collection. After completion, PIN and respective LC will conduct follow-up monitoring visits to ensure they fulfill operations and maintenance duties and support water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems. PIN will also establish a solar power system for water stations to provide drinkable water in villages and camps connected to the same water station.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feeds into developing a protection risk and mitigation matrix. The following risks (R) and respective mitigation measures(MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)(MM): needs based targeting criteria communicated with communities/LCs; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the LCs. FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, advertisements and communication channels for CFRM, 2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs 3)R:PSEA, 3)MM: Staff training; gender-balanced teams, PDM contains protection questions, clear process to address PSEA cases swiftly, posted signs in project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: Internal distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with security teams for timing and place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered areas, seatings (if possible); queues for M/F, home assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties; kits are grouped to lighter boxes for easier transportation. 5)R: WASH infrastructure is not accessible/safe, 5)MM: lights installations, locks on sanitation facilities, WASH facilities are designed to be safe for PwD (platforms, ramps) pregnant women, children and elderly, separate facilities constructed for M/F, possibility of disposal of personal hygiene items.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch toremote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff,offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding byarmed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. Ifspecified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry outinternal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, informrelevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek torelocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line withprinciples. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize onprinciples of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to othertarget areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe andprincipled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy withlocal authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums,and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. Ifsuch efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acuteneeds; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If bordercrossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods,PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitateaccess; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming.
