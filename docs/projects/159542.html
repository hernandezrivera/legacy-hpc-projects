Project Summary

HALO will conduct risk education, survey and clearance operations in eastern Ukraine, to respond to the needs of mine-affected communities across Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, focusing on high-priority areas within the 15km buffer-zone from the line of contact. HALO will also provide training and capacity building sessions for the key mine action stakeholders including the newly established mine action authorities in order to contribute to an effective and efficient mine action sector.
Risk education sessions are a preventive measure that will inform on the threat related to mines and other explosive items and decrease the probability of accidents. Survey work will establish the extent and nature of mine contamination in the region, while clearance will permanently remove these threats, improving the physical safety and livelihoods opportunities of those affected. HALO aims to clear two million square metres of land contaminated with explosive hazards during 2020.

1 - Needs

Five years after the start of the conflict in eastern Ukraine, the humanitarian needs of the civilian population on both sides of the contact line continue to be serious and wide-ranging. In 2018, 43% of civilian casualties of the conflict were attributed to mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW), and for four consecutive years Ukraine has been reported the country that has suffered the most recorded anti-vehicle mine incidents in the world. Civilians do not just face the threat of injury but often also lose access to their land depriving them of subsistence and income-generating activities. Mine action in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions of Ukraine provides conditions to allow for sustainable socio-economic development after land is cleared. Landmines and unexploded ordnance have taken a heavy human toll in eastern Ukraine, causing more than 2,080 casualties since the start of the conflict in 2014. In 2019 only, 67 casualties caused by mines or ERW were reported, of which over 70% were civilians. Around 55% of civilian victims are adult men. This may be a result of traditional gender roles in Ukraine meaning that men are more likely to work outside, and in the agriculture sector, as much of the mine contamination is located on agricultural land. However, it is not just men, who are affected –over 120 women have been killed or injured by mines since 2014. More than 130 children have been victims of mine or ERW accidents during the same period, killed or injured while playing, walking in the fields or travelling with their families. The ‘buffer zone’, the area within 15km from the contact line, is where the vast majority of mine-related accidents take place. Civilians do not just face the threat of injury but often also lose access to their land; depriving them of subsistence and income-generating activities. Additionally, the heavily contaminated areas around checkpoints present a further risk to both civilians and to humanitarian organisations providing support to the conflict-affected communities. Humanitarian mine clearance will not only improve the physical safety of its beneficiaries, but also provide opportunities for renewed economic productivity. Mine and ERW contamination brings a high economic cost, preventing cultivation of agricultural land, use of forests and rivers for wood collection and fishing, fruit and vegetable production, and the development of land for industrial or commercial purposes. Rapid assessments conducted in government controlled areas of Donbas since 2016 have noted 214 settlements in which land is thought or confirmed to be contaminated by landmines, cluster munitions or explosive ordnance. Non-technical survey (NTS) in these settlements has so far identified almost 19 million square meters of contaminated land however, the survey is still ongoing and the area requiring systematic clearance will probably increase even further. At the political level, a Mine Action Law became effective in April 2019. It is a positive development for the mine action sector in Ukraine as the law establishes a legal framework in line with international standards. The National Mine Action Authority and the National Mine Action Centre have not yet been established however, it is probable that they will draw from the institutional capacity already present in the sector. HALO regularly contributes to the capacity development within the sector by providing technical knowledge, experience and guidance to key mine action stakeholders.

2 - Activities & outcomes

HALO began mine clearance in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts in April 2016, and is the largest humanitarian demining organisation working in the region. Since the start of operations, HALO has cleared almost 4.5 million square meters of land, with almost 83,000 people benefitting so far from completed or ongoing tasks. Current capacity comprises more than 400 staff in 25 clearance teams, 4 mechanical teams, 2 technical survey teams, 3 non-technical survey teams and 2 MRE teams. In 2020, HALO aims to continue risk education, survey and clearance operations in government controlled areas of eastern Ukraine including along the contact line, and is investigating the possibility of starting work in non-government controlled territory. HALO is looking to increase its capacity to over 500 staff next year, and to this end, plans to train 5 new manual clearance teams and 2 mechanical support teams. HALO will ensure that female staff, who currently make up 15% of operational staff, are recruited as part of these new teams, by targeting job advertisements to encourage female applications and by ensuring a comfortable working environment for all genders. Natural curiosity often leads both children and adults to touch hazardous items, collect them, or even try to destroy them, which can have tragic consequences. In order to prevent such accidents, HALO visits local government buildings, schools, factories, or households in settlements impacted by the threat of mines or other ERW. The MRE teams will use various methods, depending on what is appropriate for the audience, in order to teach safe behaviour and increase awareness of the dangers of mines and other ERW. HALO already uses innovative methods to target a larger and a more diverse range of people through the production and distribution of various MRE materials, including fridge magnets, car stickers, badges, colouring and story books, to provide long-term messaging and visibility. NTS teams will continue to identify, map and mark hazardous areas across both regions and deliver MRE sessions in settlements near mine-contaminated areas. This will continue to improve HALO’s understanding of the mine threat especially in the buffer zone. NTS teams will also facilitate explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) through the State Emergency Services (SES), in instances where civilians report the presence of hazardous items in their settlements. All clearance and technical survey teams will be deployed onto areas identified as high humanitarian priority during non-technical survey. Much of this clearance will take place in the buffer zone, provided security conditions allow demining to take place safely. Mechanical capacity will expand even further after the successful trials and deployment of various mechanical machines throughout 2019. Mechanical teams will clear abandoned military positions and trenches along with critical infrastructure sites, all of which are difficult and slow to clear manually. Mechanical support teams will prepare land for follow-up detector assisted clearance by cutting the thick vegetation in areas with items attached to tripwires. This will allow land to be released back to the communities more quickly than through manual clearance alone, and ensure that items of infrastructure, such as water pipes and electricity pylons, can be safely maintained. As the new Mine Action Law became effective in April 2019 and a new National Mine Action Authority (NMAA) is to be formed, HALO believes it is crucial that the organisation and its members will be equipped with knowledge, skills and procedures in line with international mine action standards. Additional specialised training for the key mine action stakeholders and government representatives will support an effective mine action sector with national authority that is capable of coordinating activities across eastern Ukraine and respond to the humanitarian needs of the civilians.
