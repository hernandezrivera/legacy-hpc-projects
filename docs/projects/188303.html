Project Summary

The project is in line with the education sector response framework in Syria 2022, aiming to contribute to the improvement of access and remain in basic educational services for the most vulnerable girls, boys, and PWDs in the targeted areas targeting 1500 persons (1400 girls and boys & 100 female and male teachers and education personal) following two main pillars 1) Access: Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children, 2) Systems: the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response strengthened, the project is targeting the most vulnerable areas based on the last updated severity scale from the education sector (100% from targeted areas having the severity scale of 3,4, or 5), Rural of Damascus Duma and Alzabadni, Dara'a, and the project will be conducted through non-formal education services in a non-formal setting, mainly remedial classes, and homework support sessions for children who are at risk of school dropout and home visits for people with disabilities, in addition to School bags which contain the basic materials for distribution for the most vulnerable children will target the mentioned areas under the pillar (Access) with the following activities and targets;
o Conduct Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns, and after finishing the required producers in each school awareness events will be conducted to increase the awareness of the community members (children and caregivers) about the importance of education.
o Provide students with school supplies: the project will provide all the children in each school with school bags including the basic stationery (Based on MoE standards)
o Conditional Voucher for educational support (stationery, school Bags) for the most vulnerable families of registered children in school-aged between (12-17) aiming to reduce the risk of dropping - out of schools and to protect the children from the risk of child labor and early marriage.
- Conduct all the Remedial classes in a safe place for the participants in the education center of Wared association, if any of the participants' data showed that he/she is disability Wared will conduct all the activities as home visits searching for the best interest for the participants.
The project will target the mentioned areas under the pillar (Systems) with the following activities and targets;
• Build the capacity of teachers and education personnel:
- the project will conduct eight main capacity building training for 100 teachers (the training will be covering policies, planning, data collection, INEE MS, main CP principals, and basics of inclusive education)
- the project will conduct 2 advanced training on inclusive education for 40 special education teachers from the schools in the targeted areas,

4 - Needs

According to OCHA 2022, a total of 6,609,775 people are in need of education services, including 73% that are living with 4/5 severity needs areas. a huge percentage of children are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement and crisis, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and basic services, with a huge number of children in age school (5-17) A huge percentage children in Syria are estimated to need education support or even get into school, with a further more than 1.3 million at risk of dropping out. Displaced communities remain significantly underserved, and a lot of schools are damaged or destroyed, while others are used for purposes not related to education such as shelter for displaced people. According to the last education need assessment in DEC 2021 in Duma and AlzabadniDara'a conducted by Wared Association,the results showed that the total percentage of children (6-17 years) who dropped out of school is increasing, and The economic prices are a huge problem that was reported as the main challenge making the children dropping out of schools because their families are not able anymore to afford them with their needs of (clothes, stationery, transportation, etc.).Furthermore, there is a lot of child labor and early marriage cases which were reported as a reflection of the economic crisis, One of the results of the FGDs was that a huge amount of the education staff left schools searching for new opportunities
