Project Summary

NRC will provide shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) support to vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities through the following modalities: in Tripoli, NRC will distribute NFIs and rehabilitate communal shelters which are hosting IDPs, while in Benghazi, Ajdabiya, Derna, NRC will rehabilitate communal and household-level shelters in IDP camps as well carry out shelter rehabilitation in urban environments (Benghazi/Derna) through the provision of upgrades/repair of sub-standard buildings.

1 - Needs

1.3 million people, including around 241,000 children, need humanitarian assistance in Libya. These include IDPs, returnees, conflict-affected communities, refugees and migrants. According to IOM, in June and July 2019, the number of IDPs identified in Libya increased from 268,629 to 301,407, and it is anticipated that this trend will continue throughout 2020. With an estimated 292,000 people in Libya in need of NFI/shelter assistance, shelter is considered a top priority by populations at risk, evidenced by both sectoral and NRC assessments. Protection against weather conditions, privacy, security and dignity are the main protection concerns of the shelter target population. In addition, UNHCR has registered 45,600 refugees and asylum-seekers. There are also an estimated 655,144 migrants (defined by IOM as non-Libyans) from more than 39 countries. Across Libya, the most severe needs for shelter assistance are concentrated in areas where conflict is still ongoing and in areas of return such as Tripoli, Benghazi, Derna and Sirt. In addition, there are an estimated 22,000 people in host communities in these affected areas that require support to afford a safe and dignified setting. Benghazi and Derna continue to witness high number of returnees with 188,625 and 34,270 respectively (IOM, July 2019). Throughout 2019 the number of new IDPs has increased due to the protracted conflict and it is anticipated this trend will continue throughout 2020. Although a large percentage of IDPs are able to reside in private rented accommodation, a significant number of IDPs (new and protracted) are obliged to live in improvised shelter or temporary collective centers until they have identified an alternative and therefore lack adequate safety & security, privacy, dignity, climatic protection – particularly during winter, and access to services (including WASH). Camps and Collective Centers are typically the shelter option of last resort for IDPs who have exhausted all other possibilities. The majority of camps/informal settlements in Libya are self-settled. As such, their layouts and service provision are often ad-hoc and do not meet international humanitarian standards. Most of these camps/settlements lack security and communal facilities, and are subject to high risks of flooding. Based on NRC’s assessments of settlements, the two main settlements locations in Benghazi and Ajdabiya still lack key adequate infrastructure (especially communal lighting, wat/san and gravelling), which are directly linked to protection concerns in the settlements. There is a high risk of forced evictions of those IDPs that settle in private lands, where rents are provided by local authorities or residents.

2 - Activities

In Tripoli, the following activities will take place:Distribution of kits: NRC will distribute 400 NFI kits to 400 vulnerable newly displaced families in need of basic NFI assistance. The distribution of NFIs will form part of NRC's first-line response and serve as an entry point into hard-to-reach areas, bolster community acceptance and access to populations. Due to the volatile nature in the areas of operation in conjunction with the security situation, blanket distributions in the collective centers may be deemed most appropriate as opposed to targeted distributions. NRC will aim to conduct, where feasible, targeted distributions to extremely vulnerable populations and refer individuals internally or to other actors who require specialize services.Rehabilitation of collective centres: NRC will identify and support collective centers hosting IDPs living in sub-standard shelter conditions in the Tripoli area. The rehabilitation of the centers will reduce protection and health-related vulnerabilities through upgrades of household’s shelter units or living spaces in the collective centers, and improve existing water and sanitation communal facilities. In Benghazi, Derna and Sirt, NRC will carry out the following:Shelter rehabilitation in IDP settlements: Shelter components of this result will reduce protection and health-related vulnerabilities through upgrades of household’s shelter units/rooms, and improving layout and/or communal facilities (e.g. wat/san, lighting, etc.). Household-level assistance may also be provided to newly displaced households and/or those living in over-crowded of physically sub-standard shelter units in the centers. Urban environments: NRC will strengthen the resilience of vulnerable households in east Libya (Benghazi/Derna) through the provision of upgrades/repair of sub-standard buildings. will focus on household-level light rehabilitation works to ensure climatic protection, safety, security, privacy and dignity.

4 - Protection risk analysis

Presence of multiple armed groups in and around project locations hamper the safe and principled delivery of humanitarian assistance and protection to target beneficiaries and the implementation of shelter activities and staff safety. Risks include aid diversion, staff and beneficiary harassment, restricted access of humanitarian assistance and staff to urban and camp areas.

5 - Mitigation measures

In coordination with other humanitarian agencies operating in the sites, definition of commonly agreed red lines establishing triggers for suspension/termination of the operations in the site.Advocacy vis-à-vis civilian and military authorities through OCHA and UNHCR in an effort to sensitize them about the importance of preserving the civilian character of the project sites.In line with humanitarian principles, NRC will continue to conduct humanitarian access negotiations and principled communication and engagement with all parties to the conflict (leadership and at the field level).NRC will continue to communicate with local authorities and beneficiaries throughout implementation to manage expectations of assistance delivery.
