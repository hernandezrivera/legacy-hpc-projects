Project Summary

Plan International’s mobile programming has been effectively used in its German Federal Foreign Office (GFFO) funded project in 6 LGAs of Borno State. Plan proposes using the mobile unit approach in the four targeted LGAs of Borno. The mobile response ensures the deployment of the mobile team within 72 hours of a specific request, or of a report from relevant humanitarian clusters groups of acute needs. In cases of first response, the mobile unit will be deployed for a period of 6 and up to 12 weeks to provide urgent lifesaving assistance including provision of cash transfer. The mobile team currently is based in Maiduguri and serves LGAs within Borno state. They are being deployed in Kaga, Damboa, Bama, and four different locations of Maiduguri Metropolitan City-Jere, Madinatu, Bolori and Teachers village camp. The team can also be stationed in other field locations like Mubi, Dikwa, Pulka or Gwoza and be deployed from there. The mobile protection team will conduct a participatory rapid needs assessment, engaging the affected population to identify immediate needs. The outcome of the rapid assessment will be used to determine the line of response and also to identify existing community based services. Based on the findings of the assessment a rapid referral pathway will be developed to guide the provision of protection services to the children and adolescents. The referral pathway will be disseminated among stakeholders and will be used to coordinate actors providing services on the ground especially during the first line of response which will include provision of direct case management services.
In addition, the mobile team will set up rapid complaints and feedback response mechanism to address issues of protection of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as well as advocate for services that are needed and not available. Communities will be engaged to identify the most suitable and safe mechanisms to raise complaints and provide feedback on both services and operations of the mobile team and other available community services. The mobile team will provide targeted response for high-risk children including children with no suitable caregivers, children at risk of abuse in their current care arrangements, and UASC

1 - Needs

Currently entering its tenth year, the conflict in North East Nigeria as of August 2019 has internally displaced 1,980,036 people in the Northeastern states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe. 92% of these people have become internally displaced persons (IDPs) due to insurgency. At least 800,000 remain out of reach. Over 80% of IDPs are in Borno State and over 60% are living in host communities, putting additional pressure on already stretched resources. According to UNHCR, since the end of May 2019 an estimated 130,000 people have been displaced due to insecurity triggered by the armed conflict. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) Nigeria reports an estimated 7.1 million people are currently in need of urgent lifesaving interventions. Deteriorating and widespread violations of human rights and limited access to basic social services are increasing food insecurity and resulting in severe malnutrition, particularly among children. Up to 3 million people are estimated to be suffering from food insecurity, resulting in additional protection risks in the lean season of June-September 2019. A recent Vulnerability Screening shows an increase in female-headed households, with 44% of households headed by widows in newly accessible areas, who are vulnerable to sexual exploitation. IDPs report incidents of arbitrary arrest, restrictions on freedom of movement, cases of exchange of foods for sex, forced family separation, and physical and emotional abuse of children resulting in a rise in child beggars/hawkers. Those staying in displacement sites often live in congested shelters or isolated, insecure or inhospitable areas, and are therefore susceptible to all forms of exploitation and abuse. An estimated 5.2 million persons are in critical need of protection. This includes: 1.81 million IDPs (79% of whom are women and children); 1.64 million returnees (IDPs and refugees, of whom 54% are female); 823,000 civilians in inaccessible areas; and 0.9 million individuals from host communities. Female-headed households and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, and adolescent boys and girls face heightened protection risks, including child recruitment, and sexual exploitation and abuse. Groups with specific needs include: the elderly, the chronically ill, persons with physical and mental disabilities, and pregnant and lactating women (HNO Nigeria 2019 Report). The violence and military operations have shrunk the protective space. Women and children, including adolescent girls and boys, are particularly affected by increased and child protection incidents, ranging from forced and early marriages to rape, physical, mental, and sexual assault of girls and recruitment of boys and girls by armed groups. Over the two years of response in North East Nigeria, particularly Borno state, Plan International has identified the following gaps especially with the respect to protection. These gaps and needs are further validated by various assessments and humanitarian situation updates presented by UNOCHA.

2 - Activities

Result 1: Children and adolescent boys and girls in hard to reach and underserved areas receive urgent child protection services in a safe and confidential mannerActivities:1.1 Identify unaccompanied, separated and vulnerable children: Plan will coordinate with agencies such as IOM and UNHCR to set up protection desks in existing reception centers to identify and document children including case-by-case management and referral of boys and girls significantly affected by violence, neglect and exploitation1.2: Conduct rapid family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children1.3: Provide emergency alternative care for unaccompanied children1.4: Conduct awareness on prevention of family separation as well as child protection. Plan will use training, posters, and community/mass meetings to conduct awareness on the risks of separations.1.5: Provide dignity and safety kits. Provision of items will be discussed with the affected population to take into consideration their needs in what they require in terms of dignity and safety kits.Result 2: Children and adolescents adopt positive coping mechanisms and have improved psychosocial wellbeingActivities:2.1: Provide psychosocial support to children. The mobile team will conduct non-specialized community based psychosocial support in line with the IASC guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support2.2: Conduct sessions for parents and caregivers on positive parenting and psychosocial support for children. The mobile team will select sessions on childcare and roll it out with parents and caregivers especially for foster parents providing emergency alternative care for unaccompanied and separated children.2.3: Train community leaders on child protection issues risk mitigation and support them to strengthen/establish community based protection mechanismsResult 3: Strengthened community based structures that respond to the needs of UASC, other vulnerable children and GBV survivors fostering a supportive community environment free of protection risksActivities:3.1: The project team will conduct a community based service mapping exercise which will inform the strengthening and establishment of community based protection structures and the type of support required to make them functional3.2: Plan International following the service mapping exercise will coordinate with the communities to establish or activate and strengthen community based child protection committees where they exist.3.3: Plan International will establish complaints feedback response mechanism in the project communities. the mechanism will ensure beneficiaries are aware of their entitlements, understand the project and have safe methods to provide feedback and complaints.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Plan International works in all project phases according to the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approach. The AAP approach is an active way of using power responsibly, taking into account, reporting to, and reporting on the communities being supported. In line with the approach. The project team willa) Involve the community members throughout the project implementation, for example, communities were involved in identifying the needs of different target group including women, girls, children, men and boys at the rapid needs assessment stage. Further, they will be involved in project monitoring during the implementation stage of the action. b) Provide community members with information on the objectives of the action and on the project itself c) Set up a functioning feedback and complaint mechanism at community level, enabling the team to timely respond to cases that have negative consequences or could lead to conflicts in the target communities; and d) That information is transparent and responsive to feedback and complaints from community members. With a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation system, Plan International will work to ensure project progress is measured while also monitoring the overall context in which the project is being carried out. Due to the high mobility of the target groups, monitoring and feedback mechanisms may have some limits. In order to increase the complementarity of the project, Plan International will work closely with other organizations that can provide services to the affected population in other areas such as education, nutrition and health. Plan International will continuously see that no discrepancies or tensions occur through project activities, project means and project-based resource transfers, team's behavior or information dissemination
