Project Summary

Northeast Nigeria is facing a landmine crisis as a result of the ongoing conflict between non-state armed groups and the Nigerian Army. MAG’s research shows that from January 2016 – August 2020 there were 698 confirmed incidents related to victim-activated explosive devices, explosive remnants of war and other improvised explosive devices, and 1,053 casualties, with over 408 deaths. Over a third of victims were civilians. Nigeria has one of the highest explosive ordnance casualty rates in the world. Through the provision of Mine Risk Education sessions to internally displaced persons and host communities in Borno State, and the collection of information on the level of contamination, MAG proposes to continue its life saving mission started in June 2017. Besides, MAG will conduct Explosive Ordnance contamination assessments through Non-Technical Surveys, Remote contamination baseline assessments and data collection on incidents and accidents across northeast Nigeria. Priority will be given to returnees and areas of return.

1 - Needs

MAG has collated data on incidents involving explosive items from different sources, such as INSO and UNDSS, and analysed it to better understand the situation. Based on data covering the period from 1 January 2016 to 31 August 2020, the database includes 698 incident reports and 1,053 victims (both killed and injured). More than a third were civilian. According to MAG’s incident database, the 5 LGAs with the highest number of incidents, Bama, Gwoza, Konduga, N’gala and Dikwa are all part of Borno State. However, also LGAs in Adamawa such as Numan, Hong, Madagali, Michika, Mubi North and Yola registered incidents. UNHCR also reports that 39% of households in Dikwa, Ngala and Munguno LGAs in Borno State, have witnessed or heard reports of mines or unexploded devices around their current displacement sites. In addition to improvised items deployed by Boko Haram, contamination from conventional weapons (mortars, projectiles, grenades etc.) is also likely to be found in these areas, as a result of clashes between government and coalition forces. MAG has found through its ongoing activities that on average, only 32% of conflict affected communities can identify a mine or ERW before receiving RE sessions, indicating the low level of knowledge of the threat they are exposed to.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 - Procurement of Risk Education materials. These materials will be used during Risk Education sessions, including for children, and include banners, posters, and lea􀂧ets, in addition to child specific RE materials such as flipcharts, picture and colouring books. For the broader community, MAG will use permanent billboards. For humanitarian workers, materials used consist of MAG’s Explosive Hazard Awareness Aide Memoire. Activity 2 - RE sessions for conflict affected individuals. CL teams will be deployed to IDP camps, host communities, and schools across Borno State to hold RE sessions to reach 94,000 beneficiaries (50% children and 50% caregivers). Each CL team will facilitate at least two sessions a day on average. These individuals are accessed through schools, child friendly spaces and community focal points including LGA Chairmen, Wards Heads, IDP Camp Chairmen, Bulama (and other local leaders), and civil society organizations. Activity 3 - RE sessions for humanitarian staff. MAG will provide scheduled mine and ERW safety briefings for interested agencies at regular intervals throughout the project period. Using established coordination networks amongst humanitarian actors such as sector working groups, the HCT and INGO Forum and government bodies such as SEMA for NNGOs, MAG will effectively ensure that actors are aware of the available safety sessions for their staff. Activity 4 - CL monitoring plan.MAG's Community Liaison Manager will closely monitor the progress of the CL teams. Specifically, the monitoring plan will include weekly field visits and quarterly refresher training. Furthermore, MAG employs tools such as surveys to identify changes in RE beneficiary knowledge, immediately before and after RE activities. For MAG, it is vital that impact-monitoring activities remain gender sensitive, taking into account the different effects of contamination on women, girls, boys and men. Activity 5 - Non- Technical Survey (NTS). NTS is practically undertaken by the CL teams using a ‘Dangerous Area’ form. The information collected in these forms is later analyzed. MAG’s NTS activities support wider planning processes by identifying the location, scale and type of Contamination that is present, providing data on the impact this contamination is havingon local populations. NTS requires access to the location that is surveyed. MAG's ability tocarry out this activity will be determined by the accessibility of potentially contaminatedareas. Activity 6 - Remote Baseline Contamination Assessment (RCBA). In areas that areinaccessible, and therefore unsuitable to carry out NTS, MAG will gather information oncontamination using focus group discussions among IDPs having fled from the samearea and by triangulating information that they give on contamination they haveencountered before or while fleeing from their former place of residence. This informationwill be recorded and used as a baseline for NTS once these areas become accessible.Activity 7 - Analysis of collected data and production of contamination assessments andmaps. All the information gathered by CL teams will be analyzed by MAG and will be usedto populate a global database, and create a contamination assessment per targeted LGAwhere MAG teams will be deployed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

All of MAG’s activities must be approved by the relevant local authorities at different levels. Community liaison teams also conduct meetings and focus group discussions with members of the community (always including separate meetings with women, community elders, and youth; other groups are added as relevant to the context). MAG has a well-established Feedback and Complaints mechanism, by which community members and beneficiaries can raise any concerns—related both to MAG’s performance towards in the goals laid out at the community entry stage as well as other concerns. Group discussions for mapping purposes ensure that all communities are included and receive RE. Short RE sessions are provided to group discussion participants, so that they can identify contamination during the mapping exercise.
