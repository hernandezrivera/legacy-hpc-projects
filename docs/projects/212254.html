Project Summary

DRC will rehabilitate 200 Partially Damaged Apartments (PDAs) in Aleppo, Daraa, Idleb, Homs, and Rural Damascus governorates that are indispensable to the survival, protection, and recovery of vulnerable households, expected to benefit a total of 1000 individuals. DRC intends to cover 50 PDAs in each target sub-district, including Jabal Semaan of Aleppo (50); Kherbet Ghazala of Daraa (50); Maarat al-Numan of Idleb (50); Mahin of Homs (50); and Beit Sawa of Rural Damascus (50). As shelter is considered an essential element of humanitarian programming and has operational links with other sectors, PDA rehabilitation will be closely integrated with other activities to maximize impact. Protection risks and vulnerabilities will be carefully considered during the beneficiary selection process, with the intention of strengthening protection outcomes by providing access to safe shelter and living conditions. This will include prioritizing households where poor housing conditions may exacerbate unique vulnerabilities and protection risks, such as women and elderly-headed households and households with members with disabilities, mental disorders, chronic illnesses, and large family sizes. To mitigate risks of causing harm or tension as a result of assistance, DRC will ensure careful due diligence of HLP rights; and in cases where DRC identifies HLP issues, households will be referred (upon obtaining informed consent) to SARC, as one of the few entities authorized to deliver legal aid.
Rehabilitation works will be conducted by selected contractors under strict technical supervision of DRC to ensure timely and quality implementation and adherence to approved BoQs, sector minimum requirements, national standards and regulations, and environmental mitigation strategies. This will include daily technical monitoring visits by DRC engineers, in addition to the preparation and sharing of weekly progress reports (including site photos) with the Technical Coordinator based in the Country Office for review and corrective action, if needed. Shelter works will consist of those necessary to bring partially damaged shelters to Sphere minimum standards, within the constraints of a repair project (i.e., repair or upgrade of existing structures, but will not involve heavy construction activities to, for example, increase the size of shelters). Based on DRC's experience implementing similar programs, this will involve the installation of doors (with locks), windows, and internal and external walls, tiling works, plastering for insulation and weather resistance, and the rehabilitation of WASH facilities and electrical networks inside the apartments. The works will slightly vary according to the type and severity of damage of each PDA, while special measures will be incorporated into the BoQs where additional protection or disability considerations are essential to ensure accessible, safe, and dignified living conditions. Shelter designs will be sensitive to the local context and adopt an environmentally friendly and sustainable approach, such as maximizing the use of local materials and labor, improved solid waste management, and reducing the requirement for winterization support. Throughout implementation, DRC will ensure households are not unduly burdened by rehabilitation activities, and are able to remain in their homes or have temporary, safe arrangements with relatives. Upon completion of the works, DRC engineers will advise households on how their shelters can be best maintained beyond the response and provide guidance on how to conduct light maintenance and minor repairs in a cost-effective and quality manner. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection: As part of DRC’s commitment to a protection-centric approach, Protection teams funded under complementary projects will conduct periodic FGDs and KIIs in the response locations to monitor protection risks and trends to inform a risk-sensitive design. Gender-segregated FGDs will provide qualitative insights into the perceptions, attitudes, preferences, and priorities of participants on various protection-related topics. Meanwhile, KIIs will allow DRC to capture more in-depth information of protection risks and needs in the target areas, without the social pressure or power dynamics of a group setting. Risk Mitigation:1) DRC will conduct security monitoring and analysis to anticipate access challenges. Mitigation measures will be taken in accordance with DRC’s Safety and Security Plan. DRC will remain abreast of the political situation at national and local levels, and will conduct Access Workshops between safety, programme, and support staff to identify key constraints and actions to be taken to overcome barriers. Clear and transparent sensitization of selection criteria will be central to all engagement. 2) All costs associated with the project will be carefully budgeted and reviewed on a regular basis. Procurement practices will follow best value-for-money principles per DRC SOPs. BoQs for PDA rehabilitation will include an amortization for inflation; while maintaining alignment with Sector standard unit costs. 3) DRC has Anti-Corruption and Fraud Management policies and has developed a contingency plan which takes the risk of corruption and aid diversion into account, with detailed measures to be taken should the situation arise. DRC has a policy in place to verify and conduct background checks for staff and contractors as part of Procurement and Financial procedures in Syria. An established and well-functioning CFM is in place which captures community concerns on the diversion of funds.4) Safeguarding measures are embedded in DRC policies (CoC, PSEA, Child Protection), procedures for recruitment of staff, CFM mechanism and reporting & investigation routines, as well as in the roles/responsibilities of senior management through to field staff. Staff are aware of their obligation to report suspected misconduct and to direct all SEA-related cases to the HQ-based CoC team for investigation and management. DRC will ensure refreshers and trainings are provided to all staff and contractors and will have signposting on reporting channels at project sites.
