1 - Needs

The Child Protection humanitarian needs analysis for 2020 falls solely under the Physical and Mental Well-Being Consequence. According to report by Indicative analysis from Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS) 2019. An estimated 1.8 million children and adolescents (0.96million girls and 0.84million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of child protection and specialized support services. At least 7,778 girls and 7,473 boys have been affected by protection risks including abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect and violence; analysis of the case management cases recorded indicates that children living with vulnerable caregivers has carried the higher proportion of children affected by protection risks1. Whilst there are no statistics available on the needs of children with disabilities, the narrative from the response has highlighted that there is a need for deliberate interventions to address protection risks for children with disabilities. More than 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been associated with armed groups, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Psychosocial distress has mostly affected IDP children and there is a clear need to assess the specific MHPSS needs for children and caregivers affected by the conflict. The ability of an estimated 110,700 parents and caregivers to care appropriately for children has been negatively impacted, putting children and families at risk of negative coping mechanisms including child marriage and dropping out of school. Overall, there are gaps in monitoring and analysis child protection needs that need to be addressed in 2020.

2 - Activities

This project is going to be implement using community based child protection networks/structures to prevent and respond to the protection needs of Unaccompanied Minors, separated, Orphans and Vulnerable children, Children at risk, Children Associated with Armed Group, Survivors of GBV, and those experiencing other Forms of violence against Children in IDP Camps and Host Communities This will be achieved through a combination of strategies including: trainings of community based child protection networks/structures on community­ based case management and referral mechanism, life skills, awareness and Counseling on child rights and effective parenting support through Psychological First Aid training for caregivers, provision of psychosocial support services, livelihood support services to adolescent, and vulnerable caregivers, support with school enrollment, community based awareness messaging on Children right and prevention from grave child right violation.The awareness will have strong focus on women, girls, and Youth support groups, also and men as the key decision makers at the household level and in the society in their capacities as decision makers in the local community leadership structures and mobilizers for political causes. For instance, Ullamas, Community and religious leaders, village elders who are respected in the society would have influence in the community based structures like wise men at the household level. The needs of other vulnerable children, especially girls, orphaned and disabled children will be addressed through adequate selection in order to achieve gender equity in targeting.The beneficiaries will be selected through a participatory community process, using specially constituted selection panels representing the interest of the vulnerable groups (men, women, boys and girls, including the disabled) following a criteria agreed upon beforehand with key stakeholders. Vulnerable children from vulnerable IDP women led households UASC, will particularly be prioritized during selection. The intervention will pay special attention to the protection concerns of displaced Children, girls and women as they are exposed to risks ofsexual violence. and in need of awareness messaging on life skills and information of referral services. All vulnerable children of school going age with no access to education will benefit from pilot schools program, a newly introduced but relevant concept to fill the gap in education needs of children.This approach is intended to deliver a multi­sector assistance to the targeted children beneficiaries.
