Project Summary

In Northwest Syria (NWS), the winter months are predicted to make things worse for the local populace. Severe weather conditions, such as predicted snowfall, low temperatures, high winds, rain, and possible flooding, seriously endanger inhabitants' lives and well-being as well as their ability to access necessary services. More than 2.87 million people are still affected in NWS at this time, of whom about 1.99 million live in roughly 1,527 internally displaced persons (IDP) sites. Regrettably, these locations frequently lack enough infrastructure, shelter, and essential services.
The project aims to provide a multi-sectoral support dignified shelter solution to 443 HH (4.686 individuals) the most vulnerable IDPs in Idleb sub-district in the following camps. The targeted areas were identified by selecting from the regions identified as priority areas by the SNFI Cluster.
1. Hawaa Al- Janouby = 32 HH (CP CODE: CP000920)
2. Al Zeyara (Eman Billah) =288 HH (CP CODE: CP000694)
3. AlRabiee-Almaqlaa =123 HH (CP CODE: CO002158)
In the field analysis conducted in line with TRC, and SNFI Cluster data, it was identified that the current targeted areas are in high need and face HLP problems. Transition projects could not be realised and integrated processes could not be ensured. Therefore, those in need could not become resilient in the shelter sector.
The areas targeted by TRC to ensure risk mitigation within the framework of the do-no-harm approach and to prevent protracted displacement will be relocated to a new centre where the shelter problem has been solved within the framework of the project. Before this process, TRC held the necessary discussions with local people in need, authorities and community leaders and obtained the necessary approvals. Additionally, HLP Due Diligence processes finalised
TRC's response plan showed innovative environmentally friendly and dignify solutions. The provision of physical safety and security for the displaced communities, while maintaining the accessibility and inclusion of IDPs and vulnerable groups, would improve measures and light humanitarian infrastructure at the IDP site. The TRC team will be led by pertinent cluster priorities and safeguarding and protection principles, including the Do No Harm approach, across all phases of the project cycle as it aspires to continue providing these services and all activities to 443 HH and 2,215 people in 3 camps. The IFRC/ICRC, SPHERE standards, and humanitarian ideals are fully embraced by TRC.
In order to ensure Cost-effectiveness, concrete shelter units will be built in the area to be transported and will be delivered to the families in need at the end of the project. Shelter units will be delivered to all of the targeted needy people.
TRC is planning to respond to three main GAP with this complementary project;
• To upgrade IDP sites to transition IDPs living in tents and other inadequate living conditions to dignified shelters with the improvement of Basic Humanitarian Infrastructure.
• To promote integrated multi-sector site planning including drainage, sewage, water, roads, lighting, electricity, early recovery and livelihoods, education and health.
• To ensure that affected people are central to all planning and advocacy efforts.
The dignified shelter unit consists of 3 rooms (1 room 8,8 square meter, 1 room 8.8 square meter, 1 room 7 square 1 room 8,75 square metres), 1 kitchen (4,2 square metres), 1 living area (5 square metres), 1 toilet and bathroom (4 square metres), total 45 square meter (37,8 net) square metres. Turkish Red Crescent will implement the project as a direct implementing partner with AFNS.
