Project Summary

The project objective(s) is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock; reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in floods and conflict-affected communities in Rubkon, Panyijiar and Mayendit counties, Unity state. The IRC proposes to provide a sequence of food production to support to most severe food-insecure and malnourished households in the main season farming of May to July and dry season of Nov to March. To strengthen households’ resilience, the project will mainstream gender, conflict sensitivity, disaster risks reduction to support at-risk households focused on improving vegetable, crop, livestock, and fisheries production and bolstering resilience towards recurrent shocks and stresses. In pursuit of the project objective(s), the following activities will be implemented:
(a) The IRC together with the complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM/AAP) committee will mobilize, engage community members to inform the needs of the most vulnerable households and selection criteria for registration together with Nutrition partners.
(b) Preposition and distribute emergency (short-term maturing) agricultural inputs (Cereal & Vegetable seeds, and tools) and fishing kits to 56,400 people. The IRC anticipates FAO will provide complimentary in-kind inputs to support the project.
(c) To improve nutritional practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under 5 years with MAM and SAM. Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds will be provided, establish demonstration gardens at nutrition facilities close to water points, provide basic training on vegetable production, harvest, and conservation techniques. The IRC Nutrition team will conduct nutrition-sensitive awareness, food cooking demonstrations showing how to enrich households’ meals with locally available foods.
(d) The IRC will identify 200 potential seed producers, provide foundation seeds, establish seeds multiplication centers and plots. The seed producers will receive at least 200 USD cash grants, training on quality seeds selection, quality seeds production techniques, and crop management practices. Seed producers will be supported to form VSLA to increase access to finance for seeds production and access to markets will be facilitated.
(e) The IRC with the County Agriculture Department, State Ministry of Agriculture, and FAO will contribute to livestock disease surveillance and monitoring, facilitate vaccination campaigns, mass vaccination exercises and treatments to livestock to be led by CAHW.
(f) Facilitate capacity building to community-based extension agents (CAHWs, AEA’s), crop producers, pastoralists, and fisherfolks on sustainable production skills and provision of proximate extension services on production, post-harvest handling through demonstration gardens. Also, fisherfolks to receive basic training on fish preservation and kit repairs to enhance household nutrition and income.
Overall, the project will reach 5% (188,000 people) (i.e 56,400 people, 9,400 HHs) in emergency or severe (IPC 4 & 5 for 2023) in Rubkona, Mayendit, Panyijiar counties. In Particular, Rubkona (36,000 people, 6,00 HHs), Mayendit (9,600 people, 1,600 HHs) and Panyijiar (10,800 people, 1,800 HHs) equivalent to a total of 56,400 people (5,640 Male children, 8,460 Female children, 13,536 Adult male, 20,304 Adult female, 3,384 elderly Male, 5,076 elder Female) people. By program type: 56,400 livelihoods inputs distribution (Crop, Vegetable, Tools & fishing Kits), 9,400 individuals with production skills (Crop, Vegetables, fisheries skills (Fishing kits repairs, fish preservation etc) training, and 200 seeds growers/producers

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to experience severe food and nutrition insecurity, recurring inter-communal conflicts and flooding. Accordinig to the Integrated Food Security Classfication Phase Classification (IPC Report, 2023), the food and nutrition insecurity in South Sudan is expected to worsen with an estimated 7.7 million people expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the lean season (May – July, 2023). In particular, an estimated 188,000 people will experience emergency or Catastrophe food insecurity (IPC 4&5) in Rubkona (120,00 people), Panyijiar (36,000 people) and Mayendit (32,000 people) The 2022 seasonal flooding has compounded humanitarian needs in South Sudan, OCHA estimated 900,000 people have been affected by floods 29 counties across South Sudan and in the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area. Unity state, which is among the worst affected state, Rubkona (52,247 people), Mayendit (17,004 people) and Panyijiar (30, 125 peole) accordinging to OCHA Flood Tracking Octber, 2022). The severely affected population are the most poor and vulnerable displaced persons without kinship support including elderly, disabled, children under five years of age, widows, and female-headed households. The impact of the floods in 2022 was severe with cultivated land destroyed due to floods, livestock diseases and dead increased and many livelihoods affected. The floods waters are likely not to receed in the early 2023 which may increased displacement caseloads in Rubkona, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties having a continued negative impact on people’s physical and mental wellbeing, and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate, with poverty, food insecurity, and lack of livelihood opportunities forcing many families to resort to negative coping strategies. Some are harmful to children such as early/forced marriage and child labour. Furthermore, some of the vulnerable women and girls turn to exploitative survival sex in exchange for basic needs. Thus, the needs to provide emergency food and livelihoods response to the flood and conflicts affected people who are in there needs.

2 - Project activities

(a) At the inception phase, the IRC will establish and train CFM/AAP committee on their roles and responsibilities, using WFP’s/FAO standard operating procedures. The APP will include local authorities, CAD and RRC, women and youth leaders (b) The CFM/AAP will validate beneficiary selection criteria, participate in the beneficiary registration process to ensure equal representation of vulnerable groups including beneficiaries from the nutrition sector. The targeted beneficiaries are flood and conflict-affected households, including IDPs, returnees, and host communities including pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs), and caregivers with children under five with SAM and MAN, Women GBV survivors and PWD. (c) To enhance access to nutrient-dense food and improve nutrition and caregiving practices for pregnant women and caregivers of children under five with MAM and SAM referred by the nutrition partners. Nutrient-dense vegetable seeds will be provided to caregivers, established demonstration gardens at nutrition facilities close to water points for training on vegetable production, harvest, and preservation techniques. (d) The IRC will conduct nutrition-sensitive awareness and cooking demonstrations, on how to prepare nutritious meals, particularly for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 using vegetables from gardens and other locally available foods. Nutrition-sensitive messages will be shared on hygiene promotion to caregivers.(e) To build local adaptive and absorptive capacity of target households to enhance food production, IRC will identify and train AEA’s who will cascade training to beneficiaries and extension services. The Farmer Field school training will be conducted at the demonstration sites by AEA using FAO training manual. The farmers will receive training on climate-smart agriculture practices including composting/manure management, mulching, pest management, and post-harvest handling. (f) The IRC will identify potential seeds growers, provide foundation seeds and cash grants to establish seeds multiplication centers and demonstration plots to be use as training grounds for the seed producers. Trained on quality seeds selection, quality seeds production techniques and crop management practices. Additional skills training on post-harvest handling techniques, storage, and marketing to be provided using FAO manual on Seeds production. Support the formation of VSLA groups to increase access finance, establish Farmers Marketing Association (FMA) and facilitate linkages with seeds within and outside communities. (g) Support livestock mass vaccinations, treatment, and surveillance through CAHWs: FAO through the Ministry of Livestock oversees the coordination of livestock disease surveillance and response with partners. In contribution, the IRC will engage the FAO, and train CAHWs and county agriculture and livestock department, to contribute to livestock disease surveillance and monitoring, facilitate vaccination campaigns during disease outbreaks, and planned mass vaccination exercises and treatments for livestock. (h) The IRC will deploy a team of technical staff to directly implement and monitor the project activities in the field. The project will engage beneficiaries and local authorities for participatory joint monitoring on a quarterly basis. Project M&E systems will include periodic data collection and tracking through standard tools as provided by FAO. The IRC will engage relevant stakeholders (CAD, RRC, beneficiaries, and AEA’s/CAHW) to document lessons learned that can be used by the IRC and partners in designing others projects.

3 - Project indicators

The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and checkpoints to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated by age, sex, nationality, disability and residency status. This will further inform access and utilization of project assistance by vulnerable groups in the community. Data will be collected at the service delivery sites using standardized tools used by the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster and reported on a monthly basis to the Cluster. Post distribution monitoring will be conducted with beneficiaries receiving cash assistance and agricultural inputs. Focus Group Discussions will be conducted with beneficiaries to understand their needs and perspectives and the effect of project assistance. This will support county-level planning and data-driven decision-making for the Food Security and Livelihoods sector. Monthly review meetings will be held at the service delivery and field office levels where project staff, AEAs, CAHWs and other partners/stakeholders will review data on all indicators and use the data to make critical decisions about how to best deliver on the project outcomes and make necessary adjustments. Quarterly joint monitoring visits will be conducted with County Agricultural Department to engage stakeholders on the progress of the project. Monthly project cycle meetings will be held to discuss project progress across service delivery sites, share experiences and learnings, and make high-level evidence-based decisions geared towards strengthening the project delivery mechanisms and success pathways. The below are indicators that will be prioritized: Number of HHs (female, child & male-headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits Quantity & variety of seeds distributed Quantity & variety of crops produced (outcome) Number of animals vaccinated & treated; Number of livestock vaccine cold chain established/ maintained animal disease surveillance reports Number of CAHWs trained/ supported through cost recovery Beneficiary satisfaction with information, participation and CFM;  % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish); Number of SAD people trained by (e.g. improved nutrition/ land conservation/ reducing post-harvest losses/ improved farming practices); Number of functional saving & loans groups (SAD) Number of members of VSLAs etc. (SAD) Number of households benefitting from market access interventions Value of cash received by SAD beneficiaries

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The conditional cash modality was informed by the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) in Rubkona, Mayendit and Panyijiar counties. The IRC participates and collects data monthly for market price and commodity in the Cash Working Group Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI) hosted by WFP through REACH which informs the design of cash interventions across the country. In Mayendit and Panyijiar counties, the local markets are functioning for seed producers to purchase the required inputs for the establishment of the seed multiplications farms.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The IRC will provide conditional cash assistance to potential identified seed producers in the farmer field schools. The conditional cash assistance will offer seed producers the opportunity to procure locally available materials for the seed multiplication in the local markets which may be most preferred by the producers than in-kind assistance. The cash grants gives them purchasing power to choose what they need and enables them to purchase locally thus boosting local markets. The conditional cash assistance adopted to boost local production of quality seeds as a sustainable strategy to address the challenges of limited access to quality seeds. The IRC will provide the Seed producers with foundation seeds, they will use the cash assistance to procure additional inputs including fencing materials, labor for cultivation, storage bags among others. Before the distribution of the cash grants, the seeds producers will receive training on business plans development for seeds production. The business plans developed by the seeds producers will determine the amount of cash grant to be supported to each seed’s producers a maximum of 200 USD will be provided to each seed producer reaching 200 seed producers.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IRC will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status, and other diversity factors. We will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account. We will conduct gender-sensitive Focus Group Discussions, Key Informants Interviews and community meetings with vulnerable groups to ascertain their unique safety needs. IRC institutes measures to mitigate and address safety gaps that exist such as setting up temporary shades for mothers and children to rest and breastfeed during distribution, training sessions, and provision of sanitary materials for women and girls through integration with the protection program. Beneficiaries (men and women) will be consulted to decide on the suitable time and locations for project distribution exercises and activities. This allows for women to be involved in activities and distributions while having ample time to attend to their household chores. A relative measure of beneficiary safety will be conducted through tracking numbers of all beneficiary gender, complaints addressed through the feedback mechanism, and the number of safety cases recorded and addressed under the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders, including VSLA groups and Farmers Marketing Association, will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedback are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision-making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The IRC will undertake sensitization and awareness-raising to beneficiaries on the rights and responsibilities of all project parties. Beneficiaries are made aware of their entitlements, freedom of choice, and how support is provided. Stakeholders and project staff are sensitized to exercise and abide by the core humanitarian principles of impartiality, neutrality, humanity, and independence while providing and accessing support. additionally, the project staff, volunteers, and beneficiaries will be sensitized on PSEA and reporting channels to ensure beneficiaries' safety

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services such as setting up temporary shades for mothers and children to rest and breastfeed during distribution, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other sectors such as the provision of sanitary materials for women and girls through integration with the protection program, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 Mitigation MeasuresThe project will ensure the safety and protection of beneficiaries during registration/distribution. The project team and community stakeholders AAP committee, Payam chiefs, etc will map out safe locations within communities where the registration/distribution will take place. In locations that are not fenced/protected, local ropes will be used to demarcate the registration/distribution points to ensure the safety and protection against any physical violence including beating, kicking, punching, or the use of objects or weapons. The CFM/AAP committee will be engaged in crowd control during registration/distribution.8.2 Mitigation MeasuresIRC will ensure distribution points are close to beneficiaries’ homes to mitigate exposing beneficiaries, especially women and girls to sexual violence including rape. The CFM/AAP committee will sensitize beneficiaries especially women and girls before and after distribution to report or submit their complaints using the suggestion box and AAP complaints desk that will be placed and set up at distribution points.8.3 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC has an existing child safeguarding policy, the project will adopt the policy and ensure all protocols are followed. The IRC will train AEA’s /CAHW’s and CFM/AAP committees who will be supporting the implementation to ensure adherence to child safeguarding and PSEA8.4 Mitigation MeasuresThe IRC will exercise program integration with the protection sector to sensitize and provide protection assistance to survivors and ensure referral pathways including during beneficiary selection through referrals, data triangulation and sharing, reporting, and follow-up of beneficiaries receiving integrated support.8.5 Mitigation MeasuresThe project will be inclusive in targeting the most vulnerable groups.
