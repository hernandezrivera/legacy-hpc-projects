Project Summary

UNFPA will continue to promote, coordinate & provide accessible, confidential, survivor-centered GBV services in Rohingya Refugee & Bangladeshi host communities within Cox’s Bazar in line with the Inter-Agency standards for GBV in emergency programming. This will be done with special considerations on value for money, accountability of affected populations, & inclusion.
UNFPA supported 39 Women Friendly Spaces (WFS) & 13 health facilities provide integrated GBV-SRHR services which include GBV case management, MHPSS services, clinical management of rape (CMR), voluntary family planning, STI testing & treatment, alongside emergency referrals. UNFPA also supports 10 Women Lead Community Centers (WLCC) which promote & advance gender equality & women’s empowerment. WLCCs are open to women, girls, men & boys, to support PSS, life skills development, self-reliance activities, GBV prevention & awareness programming & referral systems.
UNFPA also provides specific programming for adolescents & youth (A&Y) age 10-24 within the WFSs & WLCCs such as” Girl Shine”, empowering girls to acquire skills & knowledge to identify various forms of GBV & seek services when needed, along with sessions on bodily autonomy, gender equality, MHPSS, SRHR, menstrual health & positive social/emotional skills. This is done in tandem with a boy specific intervention called “Champions of Change” which aims to create a safe space to reflect & redefine masculinity while becoming more supportive, inclusive, and compassionate members of society.

UNFPA works to include particularly vulnerable groups within the camp & host areas (gender diverse populations (GDP), older persons, & persons with disabilities PWDs) by adopting special measures in programming & developing partnership with specialized organizations. UNFPA will also work to capacitate partners on the intersectional needs & vulnerabilities related to GBV to enhance programmatic support and case management services.
The project will ensure community engagement & awareness raising aimed at mobilizing communities alongside enhancing caregiver roles with males & females to challenge gender norms & harmful GBV related practices. This includes the tested SASA! Together methodology to address negative social norms & to prevent & respond to GBV by creating social norm change & cohesion between refugee & host communities through group activities and joint sessions. This also includes structured, community-level sessions for caregivers to complement awareness building activities for A&Y to challenge existing gender-biased social norms which perpetuate GBV in camps & host communities.
As aid workers and particularly beneficiaries are both at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, UNFPA will continue to advocate & provide technical guidance alongside capacity development in accordance to capacity development plans for the year to IPs in coordination with ISCG PSEA network to ensure safeguarding for beneficiaries and that PSEA referral, reporting mechanisms & policies are in place.
Under the leadership of UNFPA, the GBVSS comprises 67 active partners managing the inter agency response to GBV in Cox’s Bazar. GBVSS will enhance engagement with other sectors & government for integration of GBV prevention, mitigation & response programming for quality services using Inter-Agency Minimum Standards. The sub-sector will expand comprehensive GBV prevention & response programmes for women & girls, focusing on case management & multi-sectoral referral systems. Capacities on the IASC GBV Guidelines will be enhanced to sector & sub sector leads, government stakeholders & implementing partners fostering effective coordination in planning, implementing & monitoring essential GBV risk mitigation actions in all humanitarian response & services. This will be supported with regular community based GBV risk monitoring & joint safety/service audits.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

August, 2022 marked five years of the Rohingya response since the 2017 influx and although services and referral networks have been established and coordination structures have continued to support the response, GBV continues to be a social, economic & public health crisis both for the Rohingya refugees & the Bangladeshi host community. As 85% of incidents of violence were perpetrated by intimate partners, while 10% by a family friend, caregiver, & neighbors (1), many Rohingya refugees are at high risk of IPV, and are simultaneously adjusting to factors which contribute to risk of GBV. This includes increased overcrowding, stress due to the uncertainty of the protracted situation, limited privacy & lack of lighting across camps (2). This is coupled with constant vulnerability to SEA & inaccessibility to complaint & feedback mechanisms (8). Further emphasis & concerns are extended to intersectional vulnerable groups such as PWDs, older persons, religious minorities & GDPs as they face discrimination & particular barriers to accessing services. During disasters, 71.6% of Bangladeshi women were subjected to violence (3). Disaster related impacts in the country also include loss of livelihood activities leading to increase in harmful practices including child marriage, alongside drug abuse amongst men which directly correlates to increase in violence (3) women & girls inaccessibility to essential supplies to manage their menstrual health. Given that Bangladesh is one of the most disaster prone areas in the world (3), making women & girls more vulnerable, thus there is a need to further capacitate PERU services to provide acute response to extremely vulnerable persons. Rohingya & Bangladeshi A&Y are one of the most marginalized groups & face particular vulnerabilities to child marriage, trafficking & gender based violence (7). A&Y comprise 22% of the Rohingya population, & experience lack of targeted programming, disrupted families, harmful social practices & lack of access to specialized services (6). When attempting to seek support for GBV and SEA related issues, Rohingya refugees still refer to Majhis & CiCs (8) (4). As they are non specialized GBV actors, this hampers the ability to provide time sensitive referrals to services such as CMR and MHPSS. This highlights the urgent need to ensure GBV survivors have access to quality comprehensive GBV services within UNFPA support spaces. Upscaling awareness-raising on GBV services & safe referral points & intensifying behavioral change programs to address socio-cultural barriers remains a priority. The 2022 GBVSS fact sheet indicates that to address the prevalence of IPV, GBV actors must prioritize prevention activities. As 92% of survivors are married, findings reflect the need for community based GBV prevention interventions such as the SASA! Together Methodology. As over 35% of perpetrators are unemployed (1), lack of livelihoods may have a correlation to increased incidents of GBV within the Cox’s Bazar camps. WLCCs, which offer self-reliance activities alongside SASA! Together remain vital as a GBV prevention strategy. References: (1) GBVIMS Quarter 4 Fact Sheet, 2022;(2) Needs & Priorities of Rohingya refugees & Host Communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017: What has Changed? 2022;(3) Study on Women & Girls’ Participation in Community Disaster Risk Management in Bangladesh, 2021;(4) Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (Host Community) (J-MSNA), 2021;(5) Protection Implications of Polygamous Marriages in Rohingya Camps, May 2022;(6) JRP population data, 2022;(7) Age- and gender-based violence risks facing Rohingya and Bangladeshi adolescents in Cox’s Bazar, 2020; and(8) PSEA Bangladesh- Cox’s Bazar Data Collection, 2022.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Prior to establishing any GBV program, UNFPA refers to GBVSS & relevant working groups to ensure there is no duplication of services, & the programming is aligned to the JRP alongside current sectoral objectives & Principles of Rationalization in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh. GBVSS data indicates; amongst partners, UNFPA covers 73% of GBV service facilities & leads in preventive, risk mitigation and response activities in 82% of the locations; refugee camps and host communities. For all GBViE activities UNFPA IPs are GBVSS data gathering organizations & provide updates to GBVSS 5W actor mapping. Accordingly, this project has been formulated in consultation with facility & service mapping by GBVSS. UNFPA’s integrated programs are critical. Both WLCCs & WFSs provide PSS activities & services including 1 to 1 case management, GBV prevention & risk mitigation, & life-skills development, & A&Y programming. The WFSs also provide SRHR services. WLCCs is a coordinated space; other actors can provide life-skills activities. To ensure predictability & continuity of programs UNFPA prioritizes multi-year funding. UNFPA ensures that all its IPs are part of the current JRP process, possess relevant approvals by the Government of Bangladesh, & are active members of relevant coordination groups. UNFPA also works with IPs to ensure sustainable programs including multi year programs in selected camp locations. As 90% of UNFPA IPs are national & engaged in long term partnerships & capacity development for sustainability. UNFPA also monitors quality assurance on technical & programmatic working areas. UNFPA will consider its footprint in certain camps given the GBVSS rationalization guidance, this includes camp 4, 22, & 3. Leading to closing duplicate spaces in 2023 & providing more integrated services such as Case Management in Youth centers & WLCCs targeting men & boys, capacity development as listed above, and extending SRH services within relevant centers.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

This project will continue to ensure protection mechanisms and principles are in place by; - Applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits, & related IASC protection standards. - Providing lifesaving services incorporating the protection mainstreaming principles & approaches. - Applying the “do no harm” principle to affected people within facilities & outreach activities. - Applying survivor centered approaches in programming and specifically through case management services. - Provide GBV related data to GBVSS/Protection sector while abiding by GBVSS “Information Sharing Protocol” and data protection protocols. - Apply a holistic approach to promoting the engagement of rights holders at individual, community, & institutional levels through community-led social mobilization; SASA! Together. - Provide comprehensive referrals to specialized protection related services for legal assistance, safe shelters, and advocacy.

3.2 - Gender

Following contributes to SDG 5 - “Achieve gender equality and empower all women and girls” - WLCCs are established to contribute to the advancement of gender equality & facilitate empowerment of women & girls through life skills development, gender- GBV & women’s leadership. - 10 women-led “Self-help Groups” will continue to contribute as consultation experts to inform programing and promote women's empowerment in the community & WLCCs. - 39 Women Support Groups, 39 Community Watch Groups, & 4,040 SASA! Together Community Activists and Leaders continue working to ensure women’s leadership and participation in the decision making process. - UNFPA will consider GBV case management & SRHR services for men and boys in all WLCCs. - All community based groups will engage more on inclusion of PWDs through recruitment & special accommodations - Social Welfare Officer (trained Case Worker) in WLCCS will support disclosures & ensure PSS & emergency referrals, targeting men and boys and PWDs.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

- Under the SASA! Together approach, A&Y will continue as community activism & ensure peer-group support for one another and their communities. - “Girl Shine” and “Champion of Change” programme for A&Y will provide targeted sessions to equip participants with a clear understanding of gender equality, safety,child marriage, ASRHR and empower them to act as a change agents for the community. - WFS will continue the life-skills GEMS curriculum alongside the regular sessions on GBV, SRHR, PSEA, DRR & emergency referral pathways which are tailored to the specific needs of A&Y. - The Young Mother Support Group will expand to WFSs to capacitate & build upon the peer-group support for the adolescent girls in their community. - GBV case management service provision for adolescent girls will be strengthened with special focus on caring for older persons & child survivors, &survivors of child marriage.

3.4 - Diversity

Review from UNFPA’s 2021 Vulnerability & Infrastructural GBV Assessment, & through the guidance our Disability Inclusion Specialist, UNFPA will - Strengthen partnerships with PWD & GDP focused organizations to ensure accessibility to response services & prevention interventions for all PWDs and Gender Diversity. - GBV awareness & WFS service information sharing sessions will be organized with caregivers of the PWDs. - Dedicated “tom-tom” referral services & referral money are ensured for PWDs & caregivers. - WFSs maintain a working group to teach WFS staff and volunteers basic sign language. - Programmatic improvements are made in consultations with ethnic & religious minorities (FGD). - Pictorial IEC materials are utilized in programming & PSEA IEC materials are currently piloted in WFS & WLCC and will launch in all spaces. - UNFPA will enhance capacities of IPs on GBV, for PWDs & GDP. - UNFPA will improve data gathering by capturing GDP, PWDs, & ethnic minorities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

- UNFPA ensures program planning includes needs assessments, FGDs & consultation with relevant stakeholders when developing programs. Any curriculum or IEC materials in development involves consultations with and meaningful participation of targeted community members to ensure relevancy & accessibility. - Tools are used to ensure feedback for vulnerable groups (PWDs, GDP, aging pop etc): community feedback Mechanisms, Grievance Response Mechanisms, Client Satisfaction Survey, PSEA complaint boxes. - Based on the feedback, actions are routinely taken to improve service within facilities. Leading to capacity development with case workers & volunteers to address service & programmatic gaps. - UNFPA conducts FGDs on a regular basis with affected populations and minority groups in WFSs & WLCCs to gather feedback on programme & security concerns within prospective camps and host areas. - 1:1 MHPSS/case Management engages in Client Satisfaction Survey to gain feedback & improve services.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

- UNFPA will ensure adherence to DRR/DRM standards including the Bangladesh government National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. - GBVSS contributed to the revision of the Emergency Preparedness & Response Protection Plan (EPRP) including preparedness planning with 67 partners & identifying GBV focal points for PERU. - UNFPA Social Mobilizers are trained in responding to disaster related emergencies supporting their community members during the acute & post disaster phase. - UNFPA's IP staff are actively engaged & well presented in the PERU & receive training by UNFPA including GBViE. - UNFPA IPs preposition & store customized Dignity Kits to be utilized in the acute response of an emergency & reduce vulnerabilities for affected women & girls. - UNFPA will continue community based awareness programs on climate change through art based activity & audio-visual content, & provide DRR training for programming staff.
