Project Summary

In light of the growing needs and on-going displacements across Syria, as a part of the First Line Emergency Response (FLER), PIN will distribute Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) to respond to the urgent needs of the newly displaced households. Activities will be underpinned by emergency preparedness and response plans developed in coordination with relevant clusters, working groups, and partners – including the Cash Working Group (CWG), Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), WASH, S/NFI and CCCM clusters – to promote timely, efficient and joined-up responses that seek to maximize geographic and sectoral coverage and minimize gaps.
PIN employs MPCA as the preferred rapid response activity for sudden-onset displacements (mostly conflict-induced) during which individuals are deprived of community support, Shelter, WASH and income-generating activities and thus are in urgent need of all types of assistance in support of meeting their basic needs. Cash provides flexible, cost-efficient, and market-driven assistance, which enhances the dignity of vulnerable populations. Post-distribution monitoring data and needs assessments consistently highlight that cash assistance is the most preferred form of assistance by beneficiaries. PIN’s MPCA transfer value is fully aligned with the recommendations of the FSL Cluster and CWG, and currently covers 100% of SMEB (see needs assessment summary section above) for one month for a HH of 6 members, to enable IDPs to meet their essential needs in the first month following displacement. CWG partner members regularly review and harmonize both target criteria as well as unit rates of multi-purpose cash assistance being provided in the same target regions, in order to ensure maximum synergies and ‘do no harm’.
PIN is conscious that markets need not only to be present but require a certain level of functionality and accessibility to serve the population without excluding certain segments of society. PIN’s assessment from 2019 shows the following results - regarding modality preferences to meet basic needs, 15 of 27 villages in Idlib and 23 of 41 villages in West Aleppo ranked cash for work as a top 3 preference for humanitarian support. When asked to provide justification for rankings of the priority needs of their communities, representatives in 33 of 41 villages in West Aleppo referred to an overall lack of household finances, such as food basket support due to lack of adequate household income to meet their food needs. Additionally, PIN conducts monthly market analysis and also contributes to REACH's data shared with other NGOs.
Distribution of one-off MPCA is part of PIN’s First Line Emergency Responses (FLER) in support of vulnerable HH in the immediate aftermath of sudden-onset crises, including conflict-induced displacement. The most vulnerable households, including those meeting specific criteria outlined above, will be supported with up to an additional two months of MPCA assistance, and subsequently wherever possible be transitioned into activities such as food vouchers and winterization cash. Where referring beneficiaries internally is not possible, PIN will strive to refer needs to relevant clusters, including the FSL cluster, S/NFI cluster, and WASH cluster. These activities are anticipated to be largely focussed on Idlib and Western Aleppo governorates; however, PIN will retain the capacity to launch rapid responses in NES as required.
To ensure the relevance and appropriateness of cash assistance, PIN carries out market price monitoring and market functionality assessments across all areas of operation monthly. This data feeds both into REACH datasets, and into PIN’s own assistance design, ensuring that the MPCA value is set – in consultation with the CWG – to enable households to meet 100% of their basic needs as per the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket. PIN’s recent market assessments across NWS and NES have demonstrated functionality and no recorded shortages of food items.

4 - Needs

Unprecedented numbers of displacement have meant that almost two-thirds of the Syrian population has been forced out of their homes causing a detrimental effect on the availability and access of food. With an increase of 8 percent in the people in need since last year, food security and poverty remain to be deeply intertwined as economic deterioration becomes more persistent over time (HNO draft,2020). In Northwest Syria (NWS), 400,000 individuals, of whom 76% are estimated to be women and children, were displaced from May-Oct 2019 seeking to escape violence and reach essential services to survive (UNOCHA,2019). New IDPs – particularly those who have faced multiple displacements – are particularly vulnerable, as sudden-onset displacements disrupt livelihoods and result in loss of assets and social support networks. Newly-displaced populations supported by PIN’s ongoing emergency responses in 2019 demonstrated severe resort to negative coping strategies and food insecurity at the baseline phase. IDPs demonstrate extremely high resort to negative coping strategies, with an average baseline level of 35.3 in NES and 46.2 in NWS. 95% were food insecure / at risk of food insecurity. Female-headed HH demonstrated worse scores than male-headed HH in both regions, highlighting the extreme vulnerability of such families. Based on the recent PIN’s assessment conducted in 2019, the most common negative coping strategy for HH to meet their basic food needs is to purchase food using credit. The monitoring of existing activities indicates that this modality is amongst the most successful in improving FCS and CSI score of beneficiaries. Qualitative FGDs and secondary data highlight that the chronic nature of the crisis and the continued deterioration of resilience broadly amongst target communities further decreases the impact of assistance, with beneficiaries in some cases citing the need to spend provided cash on other priorities such as re-payment of debts and medical bills.

5 - Implementation of activities

Given the acute needs of recently displaced IDPs, responses will be launched within 48 hours of notifications triggering the emergency response plan from clusters, partners, PIN staff and/or local authorities. PIN usually first distributes hygiene kits and RTERs. During the distributions, beneficiaries will be registered, verified, and referred for a subsequent distribution of MPCA. MPCA is carried out according to PIN's Cash Distribution SOP, following a rapid needs assessment, targeting and verification. Market and protection assessments are done prior to launching MPCT in each area, to ensure that financial, security, and protection risks are rigorously reviewed prior to distributions. Financial Service Providers (FSP) under supervision from PIN staff carry out distributions. Distributions generally take place in community hubs including local council premises, camp management committee offices, etc., but door-to-door distributions are utilized when central distributions are deemed unsafe based on risk assessments. MPCA transfer value is anticipated to reach approx. $130, however, this is subject to change based upon market and currency fluctuations and any updated recommendations from the CWG.SPHERE standards are employed at all times, with particular focus on accessibility for the most vulnerable. Following the preliminary round of FLER assistance, the most vulnerable HH will be supported for up to a further two months with MPCA. PIN will collect baseline data through a HH survey on a representative sample of MPCA beneficiaries, and carry out PDM within one month of assistance using the same sampling methodology in order to capture the outcomes of assistance provided to target populations. Indicators used will include outcome indicators such as FCS and CSI. Robust CFRM mechanism is also available through MEAL staff directly present or distributed cards/signpost showing different channels of how to contact PIN in case of any complaints/feedback.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with PIN's Protection Mainstreaming Strategy, protection risk assessment and matrices, the following risks (R) and mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: needs-based targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs;100% verifications of the beneficiaries; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, wide advertisements for PIN’s CFRM,2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of different assistance packages to both host communities and IDPs .3) R: PSEA, 3)MM: Training of staff; Gender balanced teams, PDM with protection related questions, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case,Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: Internal distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with Security and Access teams for timing and place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked;bypassing queues,sheltered waiting areas are selected, with seating (if possible); Different queues for men/women, PIN home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties; 5)R: Distribution staff do not treat beneficiaries with respect, 5)MM: Screening and vetting of all new staff, including reference checks; Training on staff on key policies (CoC and PSEA/Safeguarding). 6)R: Cash not being equally shared within the HH/increased tensions in a household 6)MM: female, child, and elderly headed households and households with disabilities are prioritized as some of the most vulnerable groups within the community, strong community sensitization and involvement of the beneficiaries in project design; conducting protection risks assessments and FGDs to understand the possible negative impact of MPCAs on intra-household dynamics.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, local partnerships. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices/warehouses are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, relocate resources to other areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors to restore free and unhindered access. If unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible, and shift funds to Cash-based programming. (A6/R6): PIN is able to retain registrations in respective countries (MM6): If PIN’s registration or visas in cross-border response hubs, PIN would adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal. (A7/R7):Significant inflation or exchange rate fluctuations (MM7) Revision of targets, switch from cash to in-kind (A8/R8): FSPs face liquidity/access issues, (MM8): selection of alternate FSP, suspension of cash modalities, monitoring of the situation.
