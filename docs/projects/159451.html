Project Summary

The need for emergency shelter and non-food items (NFI) intervention continues to be required in Somalia and in specific areas in south central region that DKH intervenes. While conflict, drought, flooding and evictions remain to be the key drivers of humanitarian shelter and NFI needs, armed conflict, forced evictions and recurrent drought are the main reasons of internal displacements. Populations are in need of shelter and NFI assistance due to inadequate conditions in existing shelters and economic hardship. DKH will target the most vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) for provision of emergency shelter and NFI kits. The project will also target affected IDP settlements for improvement of living conditions through rehabilitation of community infrastructure assistance. The project intends to deliver the rehabilitation of community infrastructure like improvement of access routes within the settlements through Cash for Work activities (CfW) to provide these households with temporary employment to cover their urgent basic needs. DKH and its implementing partners aim to provide standard cluster recommended emergency shelter and NFI kits to IDPs affected by conflict and natural disasters in response to their emergency needs. The project will be implemented in the following locations; Banadir, Hiraan, Galgaduud and Mudug regions. In all stages of the NFI and emergency shelter response, DKH and the partners will ensure gender and age awareness including the dis-aggregation of data and the targeting of specific vulnerable categories which are; female-headed-households, children-headed-households, persons living with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women and the elderly persons. The project will incorporate gender dynamics and the specific needs of different groups into needs assessments and response recommendations. Built on previous experience, shelter and NFI kits shall be distributed through the modality of flexible vouchers where the list of eligible items for redemption is not fixed but gives flexibility to prioritize individual and gender-based needs in the household. The Shelter and NFI voucher includes core relief items for daily household use. Shelter items like plastic sheeting, timber, tools, nails, binding wire, door-frame, door-blade with hinges, latch and padlock, curtains for privacy protection, mats and mosquito nets. The list of items for NFI includes pots, pan and household utensils and also hygiene and other WASH items such as reusable sanitary pads, soap, jerry cans, solar lamps. The flexible voucher is limited by the fixed amount in USD that cannot be overspent. It is an unconditional and restricted voucher that will be handed out to new IDPs (including newly evicted IDPs) and the beneficiaries have the choice to prioritize items to choose from the list. Depending on the sectoral combination the voucher can either be a wallet in an electronic voucher, e.g. from RedRose whose data is protected by EU laws, or a paper voucher in a stand-alone shelter and NFI project.
The intervention will be implemented while mainstreaming protection as a core component in the targeted IDP camps. The beneficiary selection criteria applied will focus on most vulnerable crisis affected IDPs.
The project targets 10,500 IDP households for emergency shelter & NFI kits and 55 IDP settlements for rehabilitation of community infrastructure. The project will meet the following objectives;
Objective 1: To ensure that crisis affected IDPs have protection from weather elements and privacy through provision of life-saving emergency Shelter and NFI kits
Objective 2: To improve the living conditions for crisis-affected populations in targeted settlements through rehabilitation/improvement of community infrastructure
Objective 3: To improve the quality and accountability in delivering Shelter & NFI assistance through enhancement of existing complaints and feedback mechanisms.

Project Activities

Activities for objective 1;A1.1: Assess and register new arrivals and protracted IDPs in settlements. Electronic questionnaire to include questions about the intentions of the respondents. A1.2: Conduct assessment in settlements to establish existing gaps among the IDPs. This will inform on the gaps that exist and in coordination with the CCCM mechanisms share with the relevant stakeholders for the services needed by the residents; A1.3: Conduct market assessments for shelter materials and NFI in the target area, take appropriate numbers of vendors under cooperation agreements for the redemption of vouchers. A1.4: Provide restricted flexible vouchers to crisis affected IDPs for Emergency Shelter and NFI Kits. Both vouchers together have a ceiling value of 120 USD (targeting criteria developed by DKH will strictly guide beneficiary selection). A1.5: Organize demonstration sessions for the construction of one shelter per 100 beneficiaries with the materials listed on the voucher. Activities for objective 2;A2.1: Site Planning and settlement layout clearly demarcating access roads, fire breaks, drainage systems and common service areas; A2.2 Installation of solar lamps in an effort to improve site safety and security at night Activities for objective 3;A3.1: Enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of the existing complaint and feedback mechanism applied in the project. A3.2: Conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM): PDM will be carried out after each cycle of distribution. The effectiveness of the Shelter & NFI items will be built into the PDM questions to inquire on whether the needs of the women, girls and persons living with disability is met.

Project Indicators

For Objective 1:- Number of crisis-affected IDPs that have their NFI needs met- Number of crisis-affected IDPs provided with timely humanitarian life-saving shelter support For Objective 2:- Number of IDP settlements provided with rehabilitated access roads- Number of IDP settlements provided with lighting in public/community spaces- Number of IDP settlements with clearly demarcated fire breaks For Objective 3:- 50% of targeted beneficiaries make use of a functioning complaints and feedback mechanism.- Number of PDM lessons learnt applied for the next emergency Shelter & NFI action

Mitigation Measures

3C. Mitigation:1)The project will ensure the location, distance and access routes to distribution points minimizes the safety risks for displaced women and girls. The project will try as much as possible through community participation to select vendors that are closest to the camp sites to avoid the women and girls walking long distances to redeem the vouchers. The project will support in improving community infrastructure like the access roads and lighting to provide women and girls with access to distribution points and/or vendor locations with ease and reduce their risk of sexual violence. 2)The project will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to sexual violence arising due to crowded shelter conditions. 3)The project will enhance the capacity of community Shelter/NFI workers and committees to monitor, report and refer protection concerns in accordance with standard operating procedure. 4)The project will ensure the transfer modality and the timing of distribution will minimize safety risks for women and girls. Applying redeemable vouchers and avoiding exchange of physical cash, setting up the distribution timing during the day and not in the evening to give the women and girls ample time to reach home before nightfall. For women and girls living with disability, the HH will nominate a family representative to act as an alternate where the beneficiary cannot make it to the distribution point or Cash for work activities 5)The project will consider gender balance of distribution teams & committees to ensure that there are enough women available as contact points for women & girls in the community.4B ~ Mitigation: 1)The project will ensure selected location for registration/distribution services is safe with enough space for orderly registration/distribution. The project team will identify central locations where beneficiaries will not have to travel long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. 2) The registration/distribution centers will observe culturally accepted values; adopting separate queuing areas for male & female and guarantee female staff present and accessible to women &girls 3)The project will work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent & respond to physical violence arising during registration/distribution; an accessible, confidential and well understood complaints and feedback mechanism will be established with the beneficiaries to allow them to register their complaints. 4) The project will ensure shelter/NFI staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with the targeted beneficiaries understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and beneficiaries rights 5)The project will rotate distribution teams so no one team constantly visits the same settlements.4C. ~ Mitigation: 1)The project will provide clear information to distribution actors and affected community explaining that beneficiaries are not expected to pay for or give favors/services to anyone in exchange for receiving shelter/NFI assistance. The project will ensure this information is communicated to women and girls. 2) The project will ensure that the beneficiaries know how to obtain shelter/NFI assistance, when and where the distribution will take place, what they will get and for how long it should last and how to use the items. Information will be shared through various communication channels to reach broader community and to account for different literacy levels. 3)The project will issue distribution cards in their own names to female and children headed households. 4)The project will keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use. Effective data protection will be applied 5)The project will apply modalities that will reduce risks of theft of humanitarian assistance no physical cash exchange modality .

Please provide further explanation

An adequate standard of living, including at a minimum access to adequate food, water, housing, and basic education is a means towards achieving durable solutions. Enabling communities to create their solutions is key to achieving durable solutions. This project intends to improve community infrastructure in targeted settlements by improving the drainage system, access roads, communal lighting and fire breaks in between settlements. This will involve capacity building of the CCCM committees to enable them to plan and organize for this and through Cash for Work activities carryout this work and in the process obtain temporary employment to economically empower the community to meet their urgent basic needs. The project will link this activities with relevant state actors to achieve buy-in and involvement of the state. This project will link with DKH’s programmatic design in other sectors namely; WASH, Education, Food security & livelihoods projects. Provision of employment and livelihoods that is achieved through the IGA (Income Generating Activity) and TVET training projects, the targeted households and settlements achieve some level of financial freedom since they are involved in income generating activities like soap making, tailoring, briquette making among others that allow these communities to have sustained income. Access to effective mechanisms that support these communities in restoring their housing and livelihoods will be achieved through; consultations with the displaced persons and relevant settlements to map out their obstacles to and proposed solutions for durable solutions, Capacity building to achieve self-reliant and resilient communities, linkage of the interventions to existing government plans and structures to ensure sustainability, complementarities with other humanitarian actors and coordination and timely engagement with different actors. The processes supporting durable solution will be inclusive and involve, on the basis of full equality, all parts of the displaced population, including women, children (in accordance with their age and level of maturity), persons with special needs and persons who are potentially marginalized.
