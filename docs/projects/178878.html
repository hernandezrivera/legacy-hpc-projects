Project Summary

Economic livelihoods across Syria have been severely impacted by the crisis. This impact is particularly apparent in the agriculture and livestock sector. Increases in input and production costs and disruptions in value chains, coupled with reduced availability of power and water resources are all factors leading to decreases in production levels, poor availability of locally produced foods, rising sector-related unemployment, and placing added strain on already weak social structures and communities in general.
Taking into consideration the above trends, the Communities to Market (C2M) project will contribute to strengthened livelihoods of crisis-affected populations and improved social cohesion among local communities through the promotion/adoption of socially inclusive business models. The project will aim to create 680 durable, sustainable, and green direct and indirect jobs in the dairy value chain through the creation of two pilot community production clusters in Hama and Rural Damascus as well as two pilot co-operative and business governance structures in the same locations. Using environmentally-friendly technologies which are designed to work off-grid, the community production clusters will integrate all economic activities related to livestock development, dairy productions, cheese production, high protein value bars, and other economic activities within a closed-loop model. Direct job creation will be managed and governed through the co-operatives responsible for ensuring that employees benefit from quality income opportunities, social safety nets such as health insurance, and skills enhancement. Vocational training will also be provided to direct beneficiaries in the areas of system operation, quality management, product development, and life skills training. Indirect jobs will be supported across the dairy value chain including the areas of food retailers, transportation, logistics, etc. The project will prioritize job creation for women and persons with disabilities in rural areas given the high levels of vulnerability this segment of the community faces in accessing resources and income-generating opportunities.
The project will achieve its goals through five implementation-output streams:
• Two community production clusters established within Hama and Rural Damascus Governorates.
• Economic livelihood opportunities created particularly for female-headed households.
• Labourers received training in system operations and livestock production.
• Linkages with markets established for cluster-based products.
• A community-level business entity with a cooperative structure and governance framework established within the project locations.

4 - Needs

The situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated, with an estimated 14.2 million people, 68.6% of total population, in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance. At least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure. (HNO 2021).The agriculture sector is severely impacted, with half of the national grain requirements met through imports. High cost and limited availability of agriculture inputs, damage to the irrigation infrastructure, degradation of livelihoods and income-earning opportunities, regional economic and banking crisis affecting trade in Syrian produce, effects of COVID-19 on food security and agriculture sector, devastating wildfires and other climate-induced shocks have all contributed to the worsening situation in Syria (HNO, 2021).Based on AKF Syria’s 2020 Rapid Need Assessment conducted in Salamieh District, expenditure on Food needs (SYP 54,000/US$ 20.0), Payments of loans/debts and instalments of rotated saving (SYP 45,000/US$ 16.7), Health (SYP 32,000/US$ 11.8), Education (SYP 23,000/US$ 8.5), Shelter (SYP 20,000/US$ 7.4), Social occasions (SYP 17,000/US$ 6.3). The shortage and high prices for poultry, cattle, and sheep fodder, coupled with the fragile veterinary services, remains a challenge for livestock keepers. 5 - Implementation of activities

To increase access to direct and indirect jobs for rural-based HHs, focusing on women and PwDs, two pilot community dairy production clusters will be established in project locations. The clusters will integrate economic activities related to livestock development, dairy and cheese production, high protein value bars and other activities within a closed-loop integrated model. Specific cluster components are organic feedstock production, cattle farming, algae production, milk processing, protein bar production and cold storage units. Outputs of the cluster will include cream cheese, protein bars and high-quality meat products for domestic and international markets. Cream cheese will be packaged and sold to local retail markets and adapted based on local tastes. High nutrition protein bars will be directed to ongoing nutrition programs such as school feeding. 70% of net profits will be used to cover the direct and indirect costs of the two clusters. The remaining 30% will be distributed across different usages including health insurance, training/capacity development and community investment fund used to upgrade community assets. A community-level business entity with a cooperative structure and governance framework will be established in target areas to ensure decent jobs are secured and health insurance is available. Vocational training will be given to direct beneficiaries in areas of system operation, quality management, product development and life skills. Process and outcome monitoring activities include baseline and end-line assessments of the target groups, case studies, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries, post-project monitoring, household surveys and Focus Group Discussions with project stakeholders.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Reduced productivity of cow’s milk and meat due to the spread of pathogens.Mitigation: Cows will be fed organic feedstock produced by the organic feedstock production unit. Healthy conditions will be ensured and well-trained veterinaries will be contracted to provide animal health services. -Lack of adherence on the part of MOSAL to deliver the required permits can result in late or protracted approval processes for the legalization of community co-operatives.Mitigation: AKF contacted MOSAL and got official approval to establish a cooperative/foundation. -The transfer of funds to implementing unit may take longer than expected due to the Syrian sanctions and change in banking procedures.Mitigation: AKF in collaboration with AKF’s head office in Geneva regularly monitor the time transfers take, and bridge financial resources to ensure minimal disruption to project implementation. -Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact on the project. -Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties procuring items and delivery to Salamieh.Mitigation: AKDN has vast experience in rural livelihoods programming in extremely difficult environments and will seek to source from local suppliers and expand procurement tendering offers to Governorates when needed. -Deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors regional and local security situation. If the mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the activities implementation until the situation is safe.
