Project Summary

The project was designed based on SADAD's needs assessment in 2018, in coordination with (OCHA).
This project will target 7 Sub-districts in the Idleb and Aleppo which are classified among the areas most of the need according to the Food Security Sector Report. The percentage of IDPs in the targeted areas reached 64.12% of the total population.
SADAD aims to improve the food security of people suffering from food insecurity and provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable displaced families, focusing on areas with high needs.
The beneficiaries will be provided with a one-time food basket during the project, which will contain basic food items. The weight of each basket will be 29,350 kg, which consequently will contribute to alleviating the financial burdens and costs of living on the targeted families in the project.
SADAD logistics team will purchase food items from inside Syria exclusively to be packaged in the warehouses in Sarmada.
The baskets will be transferred to the targeted areas in Aleppo and Idlib after identifying the spots of IDPs especially in open-lands, collective centers and informal settlement in the targeted locations on a daily basis according to the distribution plan and under the supervision of the Field Project Manager, the Monitoring and Evaluation team, the Logistics Team, and the Security and Safety team and then deliver it to the beneficiaries.
SADD's team will cooperate and coordinate with the local councils in accordance with the needs assessment conducted previously by preparing lists of beneficiaries that will be approved by the local councils according to predefined selection criteria:
- Displaced families (a key priority for choice, especially for newly displaced families)
- Family income must be less than $ 50 per month.
- The breadwinner is a woman whose husband is lost, injured or disabled.
- Poor families that have (elderly or young children under 15 years or widowed women).
The Monitoring and Evaluation team in collaboration with the field team will verify the names of the beneficiaries who will be provided with cards containing (the village code and beneficiary code) certified by the organization before starting the distribution.
Field Distribution Teams will begin the distribution process according to the beneficiary name lists and under the supervision of the distribution supervisors within a pre-defined comprehensive distribution plan for the targeted villages.

Needs

Needs:Now in its eighth year, the humanitarian crisis in Syria continues and thus the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming and remain high. Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2019), indicates some 11.7 million people in Syria require humanitarian aid and assistance, of them, 5 million considered in acute need in addition to the increased number of IDPs to reach 6.8 million people.Based on recent assessments, the Food Security and Agriculture sector estimates that a third of the population in Syria is food insecure with pockets of acute and chronic malnutrition persisting in certain areas. HNO 2019. Food Needs in Syria are driven by many crisis related factors such as lack of financial access, as high food prices, low purchasing power, and lack of means of income or livelihood opportunities which badly affected the economic and living situation of people and led to food insecurity and this has caused heavy reliance on negative coping strategies, a lack of dietary diversity, and significant gaps in food consumption.Most of these families suffer from many obstacles such as difficulty in accessing food and relief due to poverty, lack of livelihoods and income-generating sources, lack of adequate financial resources, increasing prices of food items in general, lack of jobs and income. These families’ needs to food baskets in the current situations to alleviate the suffering and ensure the existence of food security for them and to ensure the provision of basic human needs to maintain the safety and life, where most of them have fled their homes due to the escalation of hostilities within Northern Hama and Southern Idleb.The project areas, like other areas in Northern of Syria, are experiencing very difficult economic and social conditions, with the increase in the number of "poor families" headed by a woman / husband who is missing, a detainee, a deceased or a husband with special needs who cannot work, where estimated to reach more than 53,875 households about 43.5% of the total population as indicated through the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment which Sadad have participated in to support Humanitarian Response Plan 2019.More than 50 percent of Syrians are currently unemployed, and the unemployment rate among young people is close to 78 percent. As well as the destruction of property and infrastructure and the deterioration of economic and social conditions and resorting to negative coping mechanisms and strategies that increase risks which they face such as selling assets, depending on relief aid, early marriage, and child labor, reducing meals consumptions per day. The lack of basic food items, absence of income resources and rising food prices led to the deteriorated situations of those families in their last resort where they seek refuge in camps, open lands and in collective centers.

Implementation of activities

•Sadad field team will start the implementation of the project as soon as obtaining funding from the King Salman Humanitarian aid and Relief Center, the project will provide 32000 food baskets for almost 32000 families of IDPs in the targeted villages to 192000 persons of IDPs, the distribution will be through several points of distribution in targeted villages. The proposed food baskets will be enough for families who consist of 6 individuals which were weight 29.350 kg and is composed of 14 items from (bulgur, rice, lentils, sugar, chickpeas, white beans, tomato paste, vegetable oil, dates, halva, noodles, and canned cheese).•The logistics team will announce the need to contract with food baskets' suppliers inside Syria, obtain bids from them and select the most appropriate price with the quality assurance of the product during June, July, and August.•The food baskets will be purchased from selected suppliers within Syria through a written agreement and agreement setting out all the conditions necessary to ensure the quality of the products and delivery and payment mechanisms (after samples have been examined by specialists or by the Evaluation Committee approved by King Salman Center).•the process of packing and packaged food baskets in boxes with the project's logo placed on the food baskets so that the packaging and packing duration will be during the first half of June.•The logistics team will prepare banners to be placed on the distribution sites and vehicles that will be loaded with food baskets, as well as the distribution teams wear vests during distribution to ensure the accountability for the affected population and prevent fraud and exploitation.•Memoranda of understanding will be signed with local councils explaining all project activities and objectives.•Verification of beneficiary lists through field visits by the M&E Teams.•The logistics team will also print cards for the whole targeted beneficiaries, 32000, and distribute them to the beneficiaries at least 3 to 5 days in advance, specifying the date of distribution, time and location, making them be able to receive the food baskets from the distribution points safely and easily.•The distribution supervisors will be supervising the work of the distribution workers, monitor the progress of the distribution process, prepare reports and send them to the field project coordinator on a daily basis.•Once the distribution process is completed, the M&E team will conduct visits to ascertain the satisfaction of the beneficiaries.•There will be media reports about the daily documentation of all project activities through the organization's media outreach, as well as the dissemination of project activities through the audio and video media.•SADAD will implement and manage the project through its offices inside Syria in the Idleb, and these offices operate fully under the supervision of the central administration of Turkey.

Protection Risk Analysis

Sadad during implementing the activities of the project may face these risks according to the project's activities. Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. Likelihood: high / Impact: High. Mitigation: Sadad will priorities the most vulnerable female-headed or males headed with PSNs HHs, considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. Sadad has partnered with local leaders (to make them aware of the whole project’s activities and interventions) in designing the project to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Protection risk 2: Risk of harassment of women and girls by the humanitarian workers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: establishing a confidential complaint mechanism and enhancing accountability mechanisms besides training the staff to deal with beneficiaries in addition to providing workers with training on humanitarian standards and signing code of conduct.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Risks of insecurity situation comes from airstrikes, clashes, the attack of regime forces, etc. Likelihood: high / Impact: High Mitigation measures: The security situation within the targeted villages is estimated to be safe to ensure best implementation and provision of baskets especially as the areas are considered to be safe after the spread of Turkish monitoring points. However, there will be a continuous risk assessments and a safety and security plan and activities may be suspended and delayed if there is shelling in addition to putting in place an emergency response plan and a contingency plan which allows alternative solutions to implement activities in emergency situations. 2 - Risk of military offensive in the areas of the project. Likelihood: medium / Impact: high. Mitigation measures: there is a risk of a military offensive in the project's locations and if this happened the project's activities will be relocated to another safe area and there will be a plan be to move the project's activities and services to another safer area after identifying the needs and situations on the ground.
