Project Summary

This project aims to provide basic education to 900 Rohingya refugee children (3-5 years old: 300; 6-14 years old: 600) through Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs), with capacity development initiatives for their teachers and learning facilitators to deliver high quality teaching and learning to the children. Community participation will also be ensured through TLC Management Committees, which will include representatives from the Rohingya Community.

Objectives

Strategic Objectives:[1] Expand and strengthen immediate access to equitable learning opportunities, in a safe and protective environment, for crisis-affected refugee and host community children and youth (3-24 years); [2] Improve the quality of teaching and learning for refugee and host community children and youth, aligned with Education Sector standards, and increase teaching-related professional development opportunities.

Needs

Currently 518,400 children and youth (3-24 years) from the Rohingya refugee community at Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh are in need of education and 76% of them have been targeted under JRP 2019. To ensure continuous and high-quality learning opportunities for the Rohingya children, a pool of trained teachers and learning facilitators equipped with necessary skills and knowledge are needed as well, along with the necessary infrastructure. Agencies involved in Rohingya education programs currently train their teachers using in-house resources and methodologies, which often vary in terms of quality. Thus, professional training opportunities for teachers and learning facilitators for Rohingya children are highly needed. Friendship has been running its primary and secondary education program in remote northern "Char"s (shifting river islands) and southern coastal belt of Bangladesh since 2006, to provide high-quality learning opportunities to students from disadvantaged communities - even in times of natural disasters and emergencies (for example, considering the danger of chars being washed away in times of seasonal floods, Friendship's schools are made with prefabricated materials so that they can be moved to new locations when the islands break away). More than 80% of Friendship's students achieve high Grade Point Averages (GPAs) in annual public examinations, which is often well above the national averages. In addition to its regular education program, Friendship is also implementing a project in partnership with UNICEF to provide learning opportunities to Rohingya refugee children through 52 Learning Centers (LCs). One of the key elements of Friendship's education program's success is strengthening the teachers' capacity through pre-service and in-service training. That is why, Friendship envisions to address the need of quality teachers to ensure quality education for Rohingya children by consolidating its inherent strength of teachers' capacity building.

Activities or outputs

Specific Objective 01: Providing basic education to 900 Rohingya refugee children (3-5 years old: 300; 6-14 years old: 600) through Temporary Learning Centers (TLCs), with specific emphasis on enrolling and retaining girl students. [1.1] Refurbishment and maintenance of TLCs in Rohingya Camps; [1.2] Provision of high-quality learning and education for Rohingya children through TLCs, with specific emphasis on enrolling and retaining girl students; [1.3] Collection and distribution of learning materials for Rohingya children; [1.4] Formation of TLC Management Committees (with inclusion of women, girls and boys) and arrangement of community engagement activities (including monthly consultations and feedback sharing meetings). Specific Objective 02: Develop capacity of 18 teachers and learning facilitators to deliver high quality teaching and learning to Rohingya children. [2.1] Development of a holistic capacity development program for teachers and facilitators, including foundational teaching and content, psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) prevention and/or referral pathways; [2.2] Provision of pre-service and in-service training to teachers and learning facilitators (foundational and refresher training). Classroom observation sessions and community engagement will be ingrained within teachers’ capacity development program. Also, relevant government officials and community leaders will be invited to share their thoughts and suggestions with the teachers.

Key project/performance indicators

Number of Rohingya children enrolled in temporary learning centers (by age/sex) [Target: 900]; Number of teachers and learning instructors (by age/sex) from refugee and host communities trained on foundational teaching and content, psychosocial support and SGBV prevention/referral pathways [Target: 18]; Number of in-service professional development sessions organized for teachers and learning instructors [Target: 12]; Percentage score of post-training evaluation of teachers and learning facilitators (by age/sex) after completing foundational training [Target: at least 70%].
