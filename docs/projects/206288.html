Project Summary

Climate change, conflict, deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, and the global food crisis are driving increased food insecurity across much of the country, according to the IPC report. Unprecedented multi-year flooding is limiting agricultural production and access to markets, while the depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound. The flooding and persistent violent conflicts continue to displace households, disrupt livelihoods, and resulting to loss of productive assets across the country, while also hindering the delivery of humanitarian assistance (South Sudan: Complex Emergency, USAID 18/11/22)
IPCS is planning a child protection response in Yei and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria State. The two (2) Counties have experienced violent incidents over the past years, resulting in significant displacement of people in and out the counties, which significantly disrupted existing family and community structures. People’s coping mechanisms has weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopting negative practices and children continue to be expose to psycho-social distress, family separation, abuses, neglect, exploitation, sexual exploitation, forced labor, child abduction, child re/recruitment, malnutrition, communicable diseases and physical violence.
In responding to needs in Yei and Lainya Counties, IPCS will identify of vulnerable 183 girls and 224 boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities who are at risk will receive individual and specialized case management services that meet their contextual or specific needs. IPCS will also identify unaccompanied and separated children (girls 71 and boys 86), document them and carry out family tracing and reunification services (UASC). Among the activities to be implemented, 11,143 girls and 13,620 boys including children with disabilities will be provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services. Adult beneficiaries, 5,365 women and 2,889 men will receive psychosocial support and knowledge through parenting sessions. Communities will be reached age and gender specific messages through awareness raising activities in child protection benefitting females 23,915 and males 12,877 and finally, 130 women and 70 men will be trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including case management, PSS, etc).
IPCS have operational presence in both Yei and Lainya Counties.

1. Needs

Climate change, conflict, deteriorating macroeconomic conditions, and the global food crisis are driving increased food insecurity across much of the country, according to the IPC report. Unprecedented multi-year flooding is limiting agricultural production and access to markets, while the depreciation of the South Sudanese Pound. The flooding and persistent violent conflicts continue to displace households, disrupt livelihoods, and resulting to loss of productive assets across the country, while also hindering the delivery of humanitarian assistance (South Sudan: Complex Emergency, USAID 18/11/22)The South Sudan Food Security Outlook Update August 2022 projected that South Sudan will continue to face one of the largest food security emergencies worldwide, with many households likely to face large to extreme food consumption gaps, indicative of Emergency (IPC Phase 4) or worse outcomes. While food security outcomes are expected to improve somewhat during the October to January period with the availability of the harvest, ongoing conflict and forecast flooding will limit production prospects. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Snapshot between December 2022 and March 2023 (3rd Nov 22), an estimated 2.90 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The IPC projections also estimated that an estimated 7.8 million people (63% of the population) will likely face Crisis (IPC Phase 3). Both Yei and Laniya Counties will Crisis IPC Phase 3 conditions between December 2022 and March 2023. Yei and Lainya Counties of Central Equatoria State has experienced a number of violent incidents over the past years, resulting in significant displacement of people in and out the counties, which significantly disrupted existing family structures. These unstable and difficult living conditions have resulted in trauma for children; the counties also have a high a caseload of unaccompanied and separated children, which are in need to be reunited with their caregivers. The children are under constant risk of being separated from their care givers, particularly with the outbreaks of violence and displacements, which is expected to continue during as counties are experiencing brutal armed conflict between NAS and SSPDF, restriction of civilian movements by government security forces, as well as the destruction of properties, tortures and killings and other abuses by armed cattle keepers. Due to the widespread poverty in these remote areas children are seriously neglected and deprived of their most basic needs. Forced marriage of girls under 18 and often much younger is a common practice, as families see this as the only way to generate some income and escape poverty at least temporarily. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices and children continue to be expose on psycho-social distress, family separation, abuses, neglect, exploitation, sexual exploitation, forced labor, child abduction, child re/recruitment, malnutrition, communicable diseases and physical violence. There are several unaccompanied and separated children in Yei and Lainya counties that are in need of support. The assessment in Lainya County which can easily be extrapolated with the scenario in Yei showed that there are several child head of households. There were reported cases of sexual abuse and harassment of adolescent girls by the armed forces and armed groups. Also reported are cases of mental health and psychosocial disorders was strongly mentioned with children presenting with signs and symptoms of trauma such as nightmare, unusual crying, isolation, anger, arrogance and talking alone some times. From the CPSC estimations for People in Need (PIN) of humanitarian assistance in Lainya and Yei Counties including the limited targets for assistance in 2022 are as follows. The PIN for children with specialized needs in Lainya is 288 children

IPCS will also identify unaccompanied and separated girls and boys, document them and carry out family tracing and reunification services (UASC). Among the activities to be implemented are, girls and boys including children with disabilities will be provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services. Adult beneficiaries, women and men will receive psychosocial support and knowledge through parenting sessions. Communities will be reached age and gender specific messages through awareness raising activities in child protection and women and men will be trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including case management, PSS, etc).
