Project Summary

This project will provide lifesaving emergency primary healthcare access to vulnerable populations in Patit and Lablabni in Koch County; Sector 2 PHCC and Sector 5 PHCU in Rubkona County; and Tiep, Wicmuon and Buom in Fangak County focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality. This will be through the modality of supporting two PHCUs in Koch; one PHCC and one PHCU in Rubkona; and three PHCUs in New Fangak to serve the catchment areas, all of which are not currently served under existing HPF or World Bank projects. Four health facilities will be renovated at Patit, Tiep, Wicmuon and Sector 2 while Lablabni and Buom will need basic construction. The project will target a total of 39,925 direct beneficiaries, particularly women and children, who would otherwise not have access to basic health services.
Once established, the facilities will be supported to provide the full package of care for a PHCC or a PHCU, including maternal and child healthcare services, immunization programs, disease surveillance, and infection prevention. Services will particularly focus on addressing the top three morbidities of malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), and acute respiratory infection (ARI), as well as prevention of epidemic-prone diseases like measles, EVD, and other outbreaks. With established OTP and TSFP services in these locations, as well as Food Security and WASH projects in the county, World Relief will be able to easily integrate the responses through other projects, those of WR and other actors.
In line with the purpose of the Standard Allocation for 2020, this project will implement health sector activities in the prioritized locations of Koch, Rubkona and Fangak Counties. The objectives of the project align with those of the health cluster and the fund’s strategic objectives.
The project will deliver a frontline health response meeting the cluster’s objectives of improving access and scaling-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations and reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities; strengthening local capacity to prevent, detect and respond timely to outbreaks and public health risk events; and integrating activities with WASH, Nutrition and Protection sectors to provide timely and promptly lifesaving interventions to vulnerable population in the prioritized locations. Strong referral mechanisms will be established or enhanced toward the care of vulnerable people with specific needs like SGBV survivors and SAM children with medical complications. To establish WASH activities at health facilities will be complementary to hygiene and sanitation messaging to improve disease control and infection prevention systems.
Capacity building for staff will be an important component, with trainings provided on key topics to include diseases outbreak prevention, IMNCI protocols, CMR protocols, and medical supply management strategies. Outreach interventions will be organized to harder to reach locations from the PHCUs. All these activities will be monitored through well-coordinated supervision strategies together with the Ministry of Health and UN Agencies. Activities will be monitored by Area Coordinators who will receive support from Juba office. The reports will be shared to the health cluster and the Ministry of health through the County Health Directorate.

1 - Needs

According to February 2019 “Inter-Agency Monitoring Mission report in Koch, Mayendit and Leer Counties," the accessibility to health services is very critical in Koch. The main drivers of the health situation for the country as a whole are also true for these areas in Koch County and have worsened this year. The Greater Upper Nile region, specifically Unity State have been the center for major conflict since it erupted in 2013. Large-scale displacement, targeting of civilians, and immense food-insecurity have all compounded the situation. Lablabni is a boma in Jaak Payam of greater Koch (former Koch County now Liech County) located about 20 kilometers from Koch Town. Through its Extended Program of Immunization, WHO has estimated the population of Jaak Payam to be around 26,296. Since there is no a health facility in Lablabni, all the populations in surrounding villages of Tongdol, Lablabni, Mayeik, Mar, Dejuol, Lolgat, Jahjah and Guor must rely on health services at Koch PHCC, a distance of about 4 hours by foot. The care of pregnant women and children under-five is among the major deficits and the immunization programs will only rely on outreach campaigns sometimes hampered by logistic challenges. For Patit which is located in what is now Gany county is under SPLA IO control, the only health facility which was operational before has been destroyed during the crisis. This has left an estimated population of 12,549 people in the surrounding villages having to seek health services at Koch Town, approximately 20 kilometers away. Organized robberies and crimes in Rubkona POCs have also increased the morbidity related to traumatic injuries and mental health illnesses are becoming an issue, given the vulnerability to post-traumatic stress disorders after the prolonged civil war. The settlement patterns in IDPs camps highly expose the population to sexual and gender based violence with high risk of rape and other domestic violence. Cases of measles continuously occur in the POC due to influx of returnees who end up getting into the POC given the porous nature of the setting, people get in at any time as they seek for better service delivery. HIV/AIDS continue to be a serious burden as the overall programmatic approach is constrained by many issues. A survey conducted in May 2018 by ACF International in Fangak County indicated the retrospective morbidity counted for 41.7% and the three most prevalent diseases were found to be malaria (56%), ARI (26.9%), and AWD (34.3%). Immunization coverage is very low for Measles (44.4%), Vit A supplementation was estimated to be very low (15.6%). All of these are worsened by poor health seeking behavior with up to 32.4% of mothers/caretakers not attending health facilities when their children are sick. Among the many challenges are the lack of well integrated strategies, lack of referral mechanisms in place, weak supply chain mechanisms, community approaches, referral mechanisms and case management activities. Laboratory settings are also challenged by lack of supplies and a limited technical capacity for the staff to diagnose and handle laboratory wastes. Maternal, newborn and child health needs are also very high. Unsafe deliveries, very low ante natal and post-natal care services coverage, limited knowledge on family planning and the lack of technical capacity in BEmONC are the limiting factors to accessing adequate and efficient maternal and child health services. The waste management and the lack of WASH facilities are also some of the neglected areas where strong and well-structured mechanisms to handle all biohazards and to avail safe and clean water in these facilities is lacking,flooding in the rainy season poses a real threat of epidemic diseases outbreak. Adding to all these challenges are also weak and dysfunctional disease surveillance system, prompt and timely reporting, as well as monitoring mechanisms from county health departments.

2 - Activities

• Establish two PHCUs in Lablabni, Patit, and Buom where services are not currently functioning. In Lablabni and Buom, new construction is required, while in Patit, rehabilitation of some existing infrastructure is needed.• Rehabilitate three PHCUs in Patit, Wicmuon, Tiep and Sector 2 PHCC.• Provide primary healthcare services through the five PHCUs including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services including antenatal care (ANC, assistance with deliveries, postnatal care (PNC) and immunization services.• Train health facility staff on integrated management of childhood illness, (IMCI), basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC), community-based management of malnutrition, (CMAM) and clinical management of rape (CMR).• Provide running costs including cleaning supplies, recording keeping supplies and water for the PHCUs.• Mobilize Community Health Volunteers to provide health education in communities and refer people who are ill to the health facilities for treatment. Health promotion activities will be generated from the weekly disease epidemiological trends of health centers. CHVs are also mobilized to do active case finding and early referral to health facilities. Additionally, they are able to conduct community follow-up where it is needed.• Conduct supportive supervision at the two PHCUs in Koch and three in New Fangak. The Health Officers will provide technical support, will conduct refresher and on to the job trainings and will collect and analyze data to feed into national health information system. They will visit each PHCU on site at least weekly. To facilitate these activities, supervisory staff will use available check lists and they will identify areas that can be strengthened by the supervision and areas that require additional trainings. They will provide updates on new protocols and guidelines and will make sure that all those tools are available at the facility level.• Procure supplementary drugs, obtain EHK, RH kits from core pipeline and maintain pharmaceutical supply chain. Biweekly requisitions from the facilities will be submitted to the medical store at the World Relief Koch and New Fangak bases for replenishing the stocks in the dispensaries of the respective facilities. All dispensing points shall be issued with dispensing registers for continuous monitoring and tracking of the consumptions of the medicines.• Train PHCU health staffs on Pharmaceutical Supply Chain Management and Rational Use of Medicines.• Train PHCU health and nutrition staffs and nutrition volunteers on epidemic prone diseases and outbreak surveillance.• Provide routine measles vaccinations to children 6 months to 15 years at the PHCUs and targeting returnees during out reaches.• Report on any vandalization of PHCU.• Establish emergency early warning and response systems for the early detection and response to outbreaks of epidemic prone diseases. • Report any attack on health care staff.• Identify and appropriately refer of patients with mental health problems for treatment and psycho-social support.• Provide clinical management of rape cases and referral for psycho-social support. • Do community mobilization and Sensitization on SGBV.• Training the staff on infection prevention, control and waste management.• Avail hand-washing mechanisms in consultation rooms, maternity and waiting areas. • Avail cleaning materials at the health facilities.• Organize health education sessions focusing on hygiene and sanitation, use of latrines, hand-washing.• Identify and refer patient with disability.• Establish a waste management area at the facilities and implement standard precaution procedures for infection prevention and control.• Renovate the perimeter fence at the Bentiu PoC PHCC and PHCU.• Fabricate an incinerator for waste management.• Recruit vaccinators for the PHCU, a Clinical officer for the PHCC.• Recruit three Health Officers to oversee the response in each of the three counties.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

WRSS maintains ongoing coordination with government line ministries, local Relief and Rehabilitation Authorities, the County Commissioners, and with camp coordination in Bentiu PoC. Capacity-building activities, especially for line ministry staff are key to program implementation and long-term sustainability of project activities. WRSS reaches communities directly through needs assessments and surveys as well as through community meetings and feedback mechanisms at project sites. Community members continue to stress the need for assistance with a commitment to participate in project activities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

All project activities are designed in accordance with national guidelines where they exist (for example in nutrition and education) as well as with UN agency guidelines where relevant. Anticipating additional IDP and returnee beneficiaries during the project period, the project will follow all relevant national strategies for these population groups.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The ongoing peace process within the country is expected to create a more stable environment for implementation of humanitarian activities. WRSS works closely with local authorities to ensure humanitarian access, negotiate safe access to project sites, and to report and address violations when they occur. WRSS also links with Protection actors in all project locations and trains staff on pathways through which to refer beneficiaries who are victims of SGBV or other violence.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Protection will be mainstreamed across this project to mitigate any risk, harm, abuse and exploitation faced by the target population. Consultation with local authorities and line ministries as well as other stakeholders will determine the locations of service implementation, taking into consideration safe spaces that are accessible to the largest number of beneficiaries. Minimizing the distances that people have to travel to receive services minimizes the risk of beneficiaries, especially women and girls being targeted with sexual or physical violence by armed groups on the way to and from accessing services. Gender-based violence has unfortunately been increasing in South Sudan, having been sanctioned by certain armed groups as a tactic of war. WRSS is serious about the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), including the demands for sex in exchange for goods or services. All staff are trained on PSEA and must sign on to the organization’s established policies and code of conduct. Staff are also trained on referral pathways for beneficiaries who identify as survivors of gender-based violence.
