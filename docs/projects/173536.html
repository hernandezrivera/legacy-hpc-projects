Project Summary

The overall objective is to provide integrated protection services to 35,000 most vulnerable conflict affected populations including IDPs, resident communities and returnees in Darfur, South Kordofan and Gadaref States. The project aims to achieve general protection activities through provision of legal assistance, psychosocial supports, strengthening of community base structures, cash assistance for emergencies, advocacy and awareness raising on human rights and protection principles, protection assessment and monitoring as well capacity building for service providers and supporting PNS and extremely vulnerable peoples. to support the community capacity to respond to the protection concerns . Under GBV sub-sector, UPO aims to reach 20,000 most vulnerable women and young girls affected by conflicts in Kass, Beliel, Alradom, Kirinik, Elgineina, Kadugli, Ede'en, Yassin, Umdukhun, Malliet,Zalinges localities with provision of dignity kits, awareness raising, capacity building , construction and rehabilitation of women centres to help increase their protection, the project will also focus on the provision of PSS, GBV community networks, livelihood start-up kits for the most vulnerable, training GBV front line services providers as well as GBV referral support system. UPO also commits the project activities will be well coordinated with GBV subsector across the target states including sharing regularly the ¾ W. For child protection sub-sector, UPO aims to reach 20,000 children with CBCPNs PSS, CFS, capacity building, identification of UAC, referral supports , distribution of dignity kits, alternative cares, case management , assessment and awareness raising to the public on child protection. UPO commits to strengthen the coordination both at the field and Khartoum across the protection sector and the sub-sectors.
Over the ten per cent of the activities cost of the project will be implemented through cash assistance approach for the PSN and extremely affected populations. This CASH will directly be distributed to the target populations. Each family will be receiving cash support for 12 months , $150 per month to help support essential needs of the families.
The majority of the activities are designed in way that it should be durable and sustainable for long term solutions for the IDPs, returnees and host communities. i.e. women and community centres will be constructed from permanent materials that can last for over 10 years, Community structures/protection committees/ networks with strengthened and supported to continue taking their roles beyond project lifetime. Women and adult youth and GBV/Child protection service providers will be capacitated through capacity building and training to continue tacking, preventing and addressing any protection concerns beyond the project duration

1 - Needs

The Jebel Marra massive straddles Sudan’s North, Central and South Darfur states. As such this area is still subject to significant civilian displacement, not only as a result of the fighting between the armed group of SLA AW, but also due to environmental factors. Despite the overall reduction of violence in Darfur and SK, there are still major concerns about intercommunal violence and general insecurity; as the situation remains volatile, more displacement is certain. This disproportionately affects women and children and restricts their access to basic services right to participation, development and their ability to realize their potential. In conflict contexts, women and girls bear the brunt of the violence and brutality as rape, sexual abuse, exploitation and other gender-based violence is common. The forms of violence and abuse encountered by minors include child marriage, child labor, child recruitment by armed groups, unaccompanied and separated children, female genital mutilation, lack of birth certificates and child trafficking. Jabel Marra have only became accessible to humanitarian workers as of 2017, and its population are therefore severely underserved. According to UPO assessment in (Nov, March, June 2020) in the localities of Jebel Marra area, ND, SK and South Darfur, there are significant numbers (PSN) in need of protection services. The assessment revealed that very little aid has been directed to the IDPs, most of the support coming from the hosting communities. The needs include provision of shelter, PSS support for both adults and children, health services, especially to pregnant women, mitigating measures to reduce prevailing threats to women and adolescent girls they likely to encounter on their way to water collection points, fire wood collection and local markets. There is no active CBPN and the existing traditional community platforms are weak. there is many reported smuggling and human trafficking across the border with Ethiopia ,Sudan and Eritrea and many protection issues reported specially child protection in Gadaref with little response. There is an urgent need to respond to these identified needs as well as to continue the registration and family reunification of unaccompanied/separated (UASC) and missing children, and to reinforce and establish CFS, to prevent violence, exploitation, abuse and other protection risks. GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern for the newly displaced Jebel Marra and SK, women and girls continue to face risk of different types of the GBV, insecurity, economic challenges and lack of livelihood opportunities, as well as a lack of community awareness on women rights. GBV remain a big gap specially in East Jebel Marra as there is recently ongoing conflict between the faction of the rebels and resulted to over 70,000 IDPs according to the interagency assessment conducted in Nov 2020, the intervention is hampered by lack of accessibility to conflict affected areas, as well as the cultural acceptability of the issue, which affects the ability to provide a timely response to the needs of vulnerable women and survivors. There is also lack of access to social services and legal redress in the worst impacted areas, which means that the most vulnerable often do not receive the necessary support services due to cash liquidity and funding issues.
