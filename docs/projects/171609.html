Project Summary

Médicos del Mundo Spain (MdM-S) will contribute through this project to the achievement of Strategic Objective No. 2 of the Protection Cluster Strategy, i.e. (2) “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”.
In particular, this project will tackle protection for the mitigation the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence, risk of forcible transfer in the West Bank, impact of blockade in Gaza, settler violence, etc. through an increased access to quality enhanced public Mental Health services and MHPSS outreach services at the community level. The project will specifically target population, and in particular children, affected by the conflict in the West Bank and Gaza. This will be done incorporating the IASC MHPSS core principles: human rights and equity, participation, do no harm, integrated support to systems and building on available resources and capacities; and the enhancement of a coordinated and effective response to critical incidents. MdM intends to pursue the following activities between 2021 and 2022: •Provision of Child Protection response, with special emphasis on safe spaces for responsive interventions in emergencies, including case management and PSS for children and families and PWD, adopting appropriate measures to mitigate risks related to COVID19 •Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial services (MHPSS) for adults and youth, with special emphasis on gender equality and PWD. •Capacity building of public Mental Health Services to address children and adolescents needs following appropriate guidelines and standards, using online training when needed to mitigate COVID19 related risks. •Advocacy initiatives among the international community that aim to address protection concerns; prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHRL and IHL by all duty bearers. • Contribute to coordination and participation in the Protection Cluster, Education Cluster, CPMHPSS subcluster and progressively increasing contributions to GBV subcluster. MdM signs the MoUs directly with the MoE and MoH; and at the same time, we strengthening the exchange of good practices, lessons learned, methodologies and resource guides among the different local COBs with responsibility in Protection in the areas of intervention.
ACCESS
1.MHPSS services
Long experience in direct implementation in the Bedouin communities. Trust and acceptance built: the communities share their main problems, concerns, and challenges. Special attention to children and adolescents for managing emotions, detecting cases with special needs, appropriate referral and close follow up of the cases. Strengthening the resilience of the most vulnerable groups to provide PSS, applying the emergency protocol designed by MdM in collaboration with other national and international actors (PCC, YMCA, UNRWA).

Strengthening capacities of local focal points and schools through integration of MHPSS in education, through training and improvement of infrastructure (safe spaces). In WB the project is implemented with PCC and in coordination with the MoH and MoE
MdM and PCC began their partnership in 2017 strengthening skills in Psychological First Aid (PFA) of school counselors. Since then, have worked systematically to strengthen the psychosocial care capacities of school counselors in the Governorate of Jericho and to improve CP mechanisms through training of teachers and parents.

1 - Needs

MdM-S has focused its needs analysis on the most vulnerable people living under occupation in Area C (Jericho and Ramallah Governorates) and E1 Area, and under blockade in the Gaza Strip. In the last year, several assessments have been conducted in the framework of current actions, identifying humanitarian needs and response gaps, in coordination with MoH, MoSD, MoEHE representatives, first responders (school counselors, community mental health professionals, primary healthcare professionals, communities and CBO mobile clinics health staff), as well as focal group discussions with target population. In addition, this project is aligned with 2021 HNO needs identification and the Main humanitarian trends and analysis 2020, and 2020 HRP Protection Cluster Strategy. A specific needs assessment, strategyc discussion and lessons learnt session has been conducted with MoE (WB) and both MoE and MoH in Gaza. During the Covid outbreak, specific needs assessments have been conducted by MDM field team, in order to actualize the needs and capacities of the target population, and ha gender analysis has been made, assessing the differential impact of the crisis for women, and men, the impact en Gbv and the capacities to address and cope with this situation. With the Bedouin communities a KAP assessment has been done to inform their main needs and community coping mechanisms during this crisis that worsened by the increase of political violence and risk of annexation. Based on these assessments, the main humanitarian needs identified are: *Since cutting funding for UNRWA’s emergency programs (including mobile health clinics and MH assistance for Bedouin communities), over 220,000 West Bank Palestinians lost access to permanent clinics. They will face critical challenges in accessing primary health and MHPSS services, according to OCHA*Need to increase in Gaza and Area C/E1 access to MHPSS services (especially outreach), primary health care and GbV survivors tailored services due of a shortage in service provision. *Need to increase the quality of MHPSS services and its integration into general health services and connection with the community services. *In the oPt the successive conflicts and the blockade/occupation created high levels of psychosocial distress and eroded infrastructures, negatively impacting children and their families, therefore making it essential to MHPSS in emergencies to foster the wellbeing of children exposed to conflict related violence. Moreover, the Mental Health (MH) services are insufficiently child-friendly. *Conflict related violence, risk of forcible transfer, settler violence and the impact of blockade and restrictions of movement makes it necessary to strengthen first responders self protection and safety, according to the Inter Agency Steering Committee (IASC) MHPSS Guidelines, including the provision of tools to mainstream gender perspective. * Need to tackle duty bearers according to International Humanitarian Law, through advocacy with International community, to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL. *Need to tackle Responsibility bearers and first responders to sensitize about the different aspects of the Right to health, and in particular, non discrimination, stigma (MH/GBV), and confidentialityMdM has been implementing mental health projects in Gaza since 2007. It has specialized in the training of personnel SMAPS service provider to the child and youth population because they are the most vulnerable population and a large group of the population (2/3 under 25 years). The Ministry of Health (MoH), the primary provider of SMAPS services through the Community Mental Health Centers and school health teams. The Ministry of Education (MoE) who has responsibilities for the detection and psychosocial approach of the student's moderate mental health conditions through the guidance staff and the Central Units of Orientation .MDM has been working directly with MoH and MoE, sharing needs assessment, listening to their priorities and strengthening their structures and capacities, in order to do not duplicate or substitute the responsibilities of the Ministry. MDM signs the MoUs directly with the ministries only; and at the same time, we strengthening the exchange of good practices, lessons learned, methodologies and resource guides among the different local COBs with responsibility in Protection in the areas of intervention.According to the needs analysis made by MdM and shared by the Ministry, these areas and unserved and in a urgent need f support and reinforcement. It has been accord with the M to start in this area, in order to make a comprehensive and integral intervention, that will continue expanding to other areas in Gaza, according to the needs and capacities analysis.

Advocacy initiatives that aim to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL by all duty bearers.• 2. COORDINATION • MdM participates in the Clusters system and other coordination mechanisms to ensure coordination, alignment, efficiency, and to avoid overlapping, as well as to contribute an participate in Advocacy task forces, and in particular in the Protection Cluster, Education cluster, Child Protection and MHPSS subcluster, and GVB subclusters, as well as the Health Cluster. MDM participates in the regular meetings proposed by MH department, and the MHPSS coordinator is part of the TAG MHPSS group (providing guidance to Education, Protection and Health clusters in MHPSS integration). MdM is also directly active in advocacy campaigns in Spain and is part of international platforms, including the international network of MdM associations. In the oPt MdM Spain, France and Swiss are present and coordinated in advocacy iniciatives. MdM will compile a lessons learned document with quali/quantitative data on impact of intervention, with attention to needs of women and girls, and gender dynamics. All MdM technical staff in WB has been trained in SRH and GbV in crisis settings and GbV data management. Plus SRH and gender expert capable of training specialists. A strong PSEA policy has been developed in the organization. Policies on information sharing in social media: Code of Ethics (MdM Network), Confidentiality commitment, Communications code of ethics (Spanish NGOs Code) [includes informed consent gathering], IT access manual, Advocacy Strategy, Communication and Advocacy Plan, Corporate identity manual. Manuals used: mhGAP-IG, IASC Guidelines MHPSS in Emergency Settings, IASC Guidelines Integrating GbV interventions in HA, UNFPA Clinical Management of Rape Survivors. WHO Psychological first aid: Guide for field workers.MDM is one of the humanitarian organizations that has not stopped intervening during the COVID19 crisis. We have operationally adapted in the field to be able to continue the interventions remotely. For psychosocial interventions with Bedouin communities we have established a call system, including not only the focal points but also the adolescents and children groups. For the trainings, MdM has adopted telematic methodologies to continue the trainings in online format, both in Gaza and the West Bank. The coordination with other organizations and institutions has been done by phone. MdM is prepared to adapt its intervention with the best possible effectiveness in case of another possible lockdown due to COVID19.In emergency response in Area C the sessions will last from 1 hour to 1.5 hours and will be adjusted to the needs and specific situations of the communities. In case of an emergency, such as a demolition, MdM's team will travel to perform the care within the first 72 hours, and the care will last as long as the person or family requires to receive quality psychological first aid. MDM has developed a specific emergency intervention protocol that applies in these cases. Coordination with OCHA and referral to humanitarian or health care actors involved in the care will be provided.In terms of capacity building, at least 6 sessions of 6 hours each will be develop in order to ensure the quality of the content of the training. Plus, at least 3 meetings of follow up will be conducted by MDM technical team, to make supervision and ensure that the knowledge is being applied appropriately.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MdM adopts an approach centred in the community and people affected by the crisis,and the response is always adapted to the specific needs, capacities and priorities ofthe people. MdM has local staff in the field and is part of several networks with localand international organizations, as well as coordination mechanisms and diplomaticfora to ensure a good understanding of the context of the operations. Moreover,information on the interaction of MdM actions and the contexts is gathered in severalways to be able to anticipate and correct negative impacts and maximize the positiveones. In particular, relations with systems and institutions, attitudes, actions, valuesand interests are analyzed and integrated in the strategic and operational levels. M&Etools are in place to follow up on the effectiveness and efficiency, and to informaccountability, both to the local communities, authorities, donors and the internationalcommunity through various tailored means, including informative briefings, factsheets,website/social media, and annual reports. The project is based on the needs identifiedin a participatory way, and integrating the humanity principle (action focusing in themost vulnerable groups, differentiating capacities and vulnerabilities of the differentgroups, including age and gender, and engaging with total respect for culturalparticularities). MdM incorporates the Neutrality principle while at the same timeraises its voice in face of human rights violations, operating independently from anypolitical, economic, or military power. MdM incorporates advocacy componentsaddressing root causes of humanitarian needs. All MdM Staff are subject to a Code ofConduct, a Protocol for Sexual Harassment and Gender and Gender IdentityHarassment, an Anti-Fraud protocol, and to Humanitarian Chart. Moreover,continuous training and self reflection sessions are in place for all MdM staff inrelation to Core humanitarian standards, Gender perspective and diversity, GenderBased Violence, and Negotiation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In direct interventions in MHPSS impartial assistance is ensured via enhancingtransparency and proactively providing information to all the population, ensuringavailability of safe, confidential and private services, and by approaching people withreduced mobility at the household level. In capacity building, all professionals in thearea of intervention participate. If needed, transportation means are facilitated. In alltrainings, specific time is dedicated to debate and reflect on equality and impartialaccess of populations, specially considering financial, cultural, gender and genderidentity constraints. Efforts to combat stigma in MH are done at community, healthand education levels.
