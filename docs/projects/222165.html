Project Summary

Despite ongoing efforts to maintain peace in South Sudan, displaced populations, particularly returnees affected by the Sudan crisis, face heightened risks of gender-based violence (GBV) due to unsafe environments. Women and girls are disproportionately affected, experiencing rape, physical assault, forced marriage, and sexual exploitation, while men and boys are at risk of killings, abductions, and forced recruitment in conflict settings. According to the South Sudan HNRP 2024/2025, an estimated 2.75 million people will face GBV risks in 2025, with GBV identified as the main protection risk in 38% of 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. Additionally, 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, and 53% of households face barriers in accessing critical services.
To address these challenges, CINA, under the Protection Cluster and GBV Area of Responsibility (AoR), plans to implement a comprehensive GBV response and prevention project. The initiative focuses on GBV prevention through community engagement, addressing harmful norms, and involving men and boys in promoting gender equality. Risk mitigation measures include distributing dignity kits, fuel-efficient stoves, conducting safety audits, and integrating GBV actions across sectors. Response services will include psychosocial support, GBV case management, establishing referral systems, and creating safe spaces for women and girls.
The project will support 15,400 vulnerable individuals, including women, girls, boys, and men, with 15% being people with disabilities or the elderly. It will provide in-kind support, cash assistance, and capacity building for frontline GBV service providers, ensuring inclusivity and accountability through community feedback mechanisms and complaint systems. All interventions will adhere to the "do no harm" principle, with a strong emphasis on inclusivity for persons with disabilities and other marginalized groups.
CINA is currently implementing GBV projects funded by SSHF in Pibor and Nasir under SA1 2024. It will leverage existing resources and collaborate with the Protection Cluster and GBV AoR to integrate the Protection Risks Monitoring System (PRMS) into its programming. CINA will work with sectors like health, child protection, FSL, WASH, shelter/NFIs, and HLP to create synergies and support the Flagship initiative at the local level, ensuring beneficiaries, communities, and local authorities lead the humanitarian response. The approach will promote coordination between humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding actors to mitigate and address GBV risks effectively. CINA will regular report through 5Ws the implementation of GBV activities and funds mobilized through the Financial Tracking System (FTS).

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan remains critical despite ongoing donor support. Gender-based violence (GBV) is widespread, fueled by harmful social norms, systemic gender inequality, and gendered power imbalances, severely affecting the safety and rights of women and girls. Physical violence, rape, and other forms of GBV are pervasive, with many cases unrelated to conflict. An estimated 2.75 million people, mostly women and girls, are projected to face GBV risks in 2025—a 9% increase compared to 2022. Factors such as conflict, recurring floods, and drought contribute to rising GBV risks. For instance, massive flooding in the Upper Nile region has forced women and girls to undertake longer, riskier journeys for livelihood activities, exposing them to rape and other forms of GBV. Similarly, conflict in Leer has led to an increase in sexual violence. The Protection Risk Monitoring System identified GBV as the main protection concern in 38% of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. In the 10 counties of Canal Pigi, Pibor, Bor South, Twic East, Duk, Awerial, Nasir, Renk, Malakal, and Raja, GBV risks are alarmingly high. The ISNA reported that 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, and 53% of households face barriers to accessing essential services. A survey of GBV experts also revealed limited availability of core GBV services in 35% of counties. These increased risks, combined with funding challenges, underline the urgent need for GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and expanded response services. Proposed interventions include integrating GBV actions into sectoral responses, conducting safety audits, and providing dignity kits and fuel-efficient stoves. GBV response efforts prioritize improving access to services such as case management, psychosocial support, legal aid, safety, and protection, as well as increasing the availability of safe houses and GBV referral pathways. Strengthened coordination and data analysis through tools like GBVIMS will guide programming and advocacy. Key priorities also include engaging men and boys through initiatives like "Accountable Practices" and the "Community Cares" program, conducting community awareness campaigns, and enhancing GBV programming across sectors.

2. Project activities

In accordance with Protection cluster strategy for HRP 2025, This project is going to focus on the following interventions: Provision of dignity kits to women and adolescent girls as a means of GBV risks mitigation. Provision of appropriate GBV case management and referral services including coordinated care and support. Establish and / or strengthen GBV referral pathways and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, wellbeing and empowerment. Provision of livelihood and economic support including through Cash and Voucher Assistance to vulnerable women and girls and GBV survivors as part of a multi-sectoral GBV response. Training and Provision of Fuel Efficient Stoves to women and adolescents girls as a measure to mitigate GBV. Conduct community engagement and awareness raising activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle. Provide capacity building on GBV guiding principles and response services(Case management, PSS etc). to staff and frontline workers Conduct GBV awareness raising, including prevention messages using approaches such as SASA,EMAP, etc. GBV risks mitigation and recreational activities to foster harmony; Timely, GBV activities monitoring , coordination and assessments will be conducted within the deep locations of the prioritized counties using inter-agency tools to inform GBV response and advocacy as well as providing guidance to appropriate multi-sectoral interventions. CINA will make use of existing operational GBV tools including revised Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, GBVIMS as well as ensuring regular 5Ws and FTS reporting.
