Project Summary

As the conflict in Syria enters its eighth year, 13.1 million men, women and children are in urgent need of humanitarian assistance in Syria. 6.3 million people are internally displaced. In many cases, households have been displaced multiple times in response to endless new waves of conflict.
Takaful Alsham in this program will aims to target:
Total 2100 beneficiaries as (640 women, 600 girls, 600 boys and 260 men) both camp and off-camps In Azaz, Al Bab, Dana, Maarat Tamsrin, Bennsh, Saraqab, Ariha, and Taftnaz sub-districts. Through three main interventions:
First: The establishment of mobile teams that will visit schools and camps to provide PFA, PSS and life skills for those in needs and the most vulnerable. The teams will assist vulnerable people by referring cases to service providers.
In addition, TAS will work on forming and training a protection committee from the local actors in order to take part in case referral, communicate service providers, facilitate the needed sessions, and contribute to solve the beneficiaries' problems.
Second: We will target children and adolescents with life skills programs and psychosocial support activities in addition to implementing community initiatives.
Emphasis will be placed on supporting children who dropped out of school by providing education to raise their competency, as priority will be given to drop out and child laboring, children who are prone to recruitment.
The intervention will focus on life skills sessions, personal character building and educational support.
The available services will encourage children and adolescents to accept themselves and gain the ability to deal with all forms of stress that they may face on a daily basis so that they can cope with the new environment.
There will be case managers to refer the most vulnerable children to the services they need.
Third: Raising girls’ awareness through providing them with sessions from “My Safety My Wellbeing” curriculum alongside referring them to service providers in case they were exposed to gender based violence through referral pathways received by GBV-specialized service providers.
At the beginning of each project, Takaful will hold meeting with local community and local actors to ensure their participation in the project, explain the project goals and take their suggestions and notes to develop the project activities and implementation mechanism.
Takaful will coordinate with the Protection cluster and other organizations who work in the same sector and field to reach the right beneficiaries and avoid any duplication.

Needs

Protection issues and needs in Syria remain prevalent and continue to affect all population groups. The protracted nature of the crisis has led to the emergence of increasingly complex and inter-connected protection issues and needs. Women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. They also face specific challenges in accessing assistance (feelings of shame and risks of sexual harassment). Boys are also understood to be at risk of sexual violence. Men are particularly affected by restrictions of freedom of movement and forms of violence directly associated to the conduct of hostilities, also during situations of hostilities and displacement, persons with a disability are at increased risk of separation from their families or carers, or the assistive products which support their independence(According to MSNA 2019). Takaful Alsham Charity conducted multi-sector need assessment in August 2018 included protection sector, 183 Key informants and 1252 HHs were interviewed in 13 sub-districts in Idlib and Aleppo governorates. The most frequent protection risks in the targeted communities in the last three month were cases of sexual harassment were counted by 19 %, the percentage of early marriage 8% and 7% family violence, and the rest 17% divided to child labor and recruiting them in illegal activities. The most problem faced female and male adolescents from 12 to 17 years are 84% early marriage, separation from their parents and 58% concerns of bad security situations. While the most women's needs were 88% leaning provisions, 87% literacy courses and 79% psychosocial support. As such needs, TSCO focuses in its protection response to contribute in improving the crisis affected people’s to cope with the shocks and to reduce the impact of the crisis by targeting adolescents, Implementation of activities

1. Capacity building activities targeting humanitarian actors and national/ local authorities to improve quality provision of protection services. • Provide training on PSS, PFA, FGDs, GBV, CP basics, PSEA principles, and self-care to the team and humanitarian workers. 2. Provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, PSS, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centers and outreach mechanisms.• Since our team is not ready to provide a case management service, we will assist the affected people by referring them to organizations through service mapping which includes service providers and the available services in addition to communication data and the communicating mechanism. Then, we will coordinate with these organizations to make sure that they can receive the referred cases and deal with them properly. We will work to ensure that the beneficiaries have access • PSS sessions for IDPs and HC who have specific protection needs including support sessions for disabled, children who have fear, and women and men who have stresses. • Awareness Sessions for women and men including how to deal with children in difficult circumstances • Forming local protection committees, after selecting from local actors, who can identify the most vulnerable situations. The committee will receive training about the minimum principles of protection, PFA, cases referral, and facilitation skills. 3. Community-based child protection interventions are available for girls and boys in targeted locations: Choose children and adolescents who are prone to child laboring, dropping out, and recruitment • Provide life skills • Provide scientific support 4. Specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys are available in prioritized locations: case managers are trained to assist the most vulnerable children, refer them to service providers, and to work out solutions with the help of local councils. 5. Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities raising girls about being teenagers by adopting “My Safety My Well-being” curriculum; taking into account that activities are inclusive, preventive, involved in society. Our organization does not currently have the ability to manage cases; therefore, we will use a gradual approach to respond by networking with organizations that provide GBV services through referrals. Since TAS has signed SOPs, it can refer potential GBV cases that girls might be exposed to through referral pathways, which are in accordance with Do no Harm principle.6. Engage and train humanitarian actors (non-specialist) to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialized services ensuring that our staff is trained on the basics of the GBV by agreeing with other organizations such as UNFPA and others, as well as attending any similar training provided by GBV Sub-Cluster. 7. Provide livelihood opportunities to empower women and girls and strengthen their resilience. • Providing vocational training in community centers to empower women and girls. We will apply the M&E system through designing Pre - Post assessments with coordination with Takaful's protection team in order to measure the progress of the training activities provided to children, adolescents and women, who will receive life skills, PSS and GPV training. The monitoring system will include IPTT to track performance achievements in addition to monitoring field visits reports to training centers to monitor applying protection criteria. The FGDs will be applied to assess the affected population of protection risks in addition to evaluate Takaful's response at the end of the projects, the FGDs also will be conducted with local authorities and within FLA activities to identify the appropriate protection activities.
