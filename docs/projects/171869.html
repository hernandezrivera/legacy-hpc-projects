Project Summary

In cooperation with the local authorities of Novoazovsk for the NGCA of Donetsk (Local MP + MOH of Donetsk) and the Network of Primary Healtchare of Stanisla Luhansk + the Hromada administration*. The project is aimed to install 2 equipped Modular Prefabricated Buildings in Valuiske (Luhansk GCA) and Patriotyshney (Donetsk NGCA), where the medical service is not available. The project team will select and train the medical staff available onsite, and will provide medical equipment, medicines and consumables and will take in account the COVID-19 situation. These places are intended to provide professional medical and preventive care to 14,500 residents with low income living in 15 villages isolated by the conflict. The available data suggests, that about 70% of people targeted by the project are estimated to be women and 45% have existing morbities. According to REACH and local authorities, 26 similar installations are needed due to destruction as a result of the conflict. The two locations were selected for the following reasons: 1) These areas show a critical situation that needs a real and sustainable medical local team. 2) The areas will be able to cover a large number of patients. The two Primary Healthcare Units in the villages of Patriotychne (Donetsk NGCA) and Valuiske (Luhansk GCA) are considered critical for a large number of villages around. The majority of people in these villages are elderly and disabled people. 3)The medical staff living in the area do not have adequate working conditions for the last 5/6 years, while also being active and ready to change. 4) We evaluated the possibility of alternative support by mobile clinics or home visits by official doctors, however, this option does not seem feasible due to low density of population and the distance from the nearest hospital (26 to 58 km). 5) The projects are located in safe areas without major security accidents for more than two year. 6) Local authorities in these location are fully supportive of the project (requests from authorities received and MoU prepared) 7). No similar projects have been planned by the MOH for the next two years. According to the OCHA Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020, 50% of people in need of assistance are over 60. Many of the most vulnerable conflict-affected older women and men are at risk of being excluded from the system. Along the contact line, they face mobility limitations, and many have disabilities or chronic illnesses. The REACH report of August 2019 underlined the necessity to engage humanitarian actors to rebuild the little medical facilities destroyed along the frontline with the priority for the most isolated villages. According to the report of WHO of July 2019, the conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to have a devastating impact on the health and well-being of all affected communities. The conflict has a lack of rural health facilities in the Government-Controlled Area (GCA) and in the Non-Government Controlled Areas (NGCA) isolated from major health centers located in urban areas. The insecurity and curtailed access to referral hospitals and pharmacies have undermined the health care system’s ability to cope with both new and pre-existing health care needs, especially for people living in rural areas. Following the assessment undertaken by AICM in GCA and NGCA with the MOH local authorities in Summer 2018 and February 2019, the Foundation AICM worked on the most critical needs of villages located in less than 5km from the frontline. Some similar projects are being implemented in other regions in Ukraine, but not in Donbas. According to the WHO (July 2019), humanitarian actors and authorities have to be concerned to provide meaningful access to health care in conflict-affected regions, particularly for those living near the ‘contact line’ in GCA and NGCA. Because of the situation, this project also includes the provision of a water filtration system, a tank and a generator in case of cuts.

1 - Needs

The two Primary Healthcare Units proposed were selected for the following needs : 1) These places are considered a critical need for a large number of villages around populated by 14500 people a majority of elderly and handicapped people but also IDPs in GCA. 2) The Regional Authorities are concerned and take part in these projects due to the situation of the quasi totality of citizens composed of pensioners and disabled. 3) There is no communal transport, so the inhabitants, including many handicapped people, are not able to come to the nearest hospital to receive medical assistance (from 26 to 58 km) ." For the villages targeted by this project, the nearest hospitals are Stanisla Luhansk for Valuiske in Luhansk GCA and Novoazovsk for Patriotychney in Donetsk NGCA. There is no accessibility for ambulances and the mine risk is still very high. 3) The long-term implications for morbidity and mortality from untreated illnesses and injuries caused by this protracted conflict will not only diminish the sustainability of future reconstruction and development efforts but will also potentially have intergenerational implications. Following two AICM assessments (Summer 2018/Winter 2018-2019) in many villages and little towns on both sides of the conflict with the support of local health authorities, we focused a part of our survey on the healthcare access for isolated villages and we saw that due to the conflict, a military border very poorly distributed and a lack of access to the main hospitals for certain parts of the territories of Donetsk and Luhansk, there is a need to reinstall many primary healthcare units (PHU) along the contact line for the villages isolated from the rest of the regions on both sides. The few mobile clinics are not sufficient to assure a normal service. The medical doctors and nurses still living in these areas can't help the people. The need for pre-hospital health care in rural areas is determined by the low population density essentially composed of elderly people (in contrast to the compact urban population) but also the very poor health conditions of people living there. New PHUs need to be located in the most remote areas from hospitals, making medical care for their residents more accessible especially for districts with bad roads, without public transportation and which are isolated during the winter period. At this moment, many PHUs buildings are unsuitable due to destruction as a result of military operations, which makes it very difficult to provide medical assistance to the people living in remote, especially in front-line areas. These two places are selected because of their immediate impact on a large number of people for a low-cost investment. AICM created similar buildings and services in Ukraine since 2014 respecting the European Standards Modular buildings of the PHUs are intended to provide quickly professional medical and preventive care to residents living in isolated areas with a very low income with the support of local authorities and other local volunteers. AICM is in charge of the medical part of the project USIF" Promoting Social Infrastructure Development - Improvement of Basic Rural Health consisting in the installation and renovation of 40 Primary Healthcare Units in the Oblasts of Kharkov, Luhansk (GCA), Donetsk (GCA), Dnepropetrovsk and Zaparojie.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

Strategic Objective 1. Provide emergency and time-critical assistance and ensure access to basic essential services, to people affected by the conflict, exacerbated by COVID-191.1 Provide life-saving assistance for people affected by the conflict, including IDPs.1.2 Improve living standards through ensuring people’s access to essential services.1.3 Expand and secure humanitarian access to all people in need. Strategic Objective 2. Respond to the protection needs and strengthenActivities: Installation of PHUs near the frontline in isolated villages; Provision of an adequate stock of essential medicines during the project; Selection, training, and support to the medical staff on the primary healthcare specificities and MHS. Information about the project: 1. Block-modular technologies can accelerate the construction of PHU premises, making them accessible in 24 weeks which will cover the 2021 period. 2. Acceptable cost: the main difference between the proposed solution with the construction or rehabilitation of an existing building is between 3 to 7 times cheaper than a brick construction. The module proposed will enable approximately 3 to 5000 consultations per year. It is composed of 2 consultation rooms, 2 rooms for medical procedures, 1 room for disinfection and laboratory, 2 toilettes, 8 sinks, 1 shower and an inside waiting corridor for 8/10 people. According to the authorities, they will provide for each two medical teams of 2 to assure a maximum period of service paid by the local administrations. 3. Expansion and mobility: if necessary, such modular buildings can be expanded by additional modules and can be moved quickly to new places. 4. Specific for medical use: the proposed facilities can be used in difficult climatic zones, given that the Donbas region has very low temperatures in winter and very high in summer. 5. Durability: the Block-modular are designed and manufactured in Western Europe and compatible with EC and ISO standards with a minimum durability of 20years. 6. Reusability: the PHU can be also viewed as temporary and can be reused in case of urgent need along the frontline 7. Green technology: All three suppliers contacted in Western Europe respect European Ecological Norms. Basic equipment proposed as standard allocation: 4 examination chairs including one gynecological, 1 observation bed, 1 portable ultrasound machine (replace the need of Xray machine), 1 single-dose hematology laboratory machine with 10/12 parameters, 1 100-liter autoclave for disinfection, 1 oxygen concentrator, 2 mobile semi-automatic defibrillators type Heartstart (immediate use and monitoring), 1 Electro Cardio Gramme type Array, the kits of small consultation equipment, trauma, and emergency intervention (including neonatal kit) +miscellaneous furniture, two computers /printers and internet equipment, VHF radio for connection with local emergency and security services. Similar buildings: http://bc-pmbud.com/fap-bodenki, http://csbblok.ru/meduchrezhdenija/modulnye-fap.html (from Austria). Allocation of 12 months essential medicines, consumables, and reagents is included in the project and will be monitored by AICM. Accountability to the affected population will be ensured by a monthly report to AICM from each PHU. Monitoring Interviews of local patients about the quality of medical services will be provided in the PHU. The assistance will be tailored to the needs of elderly population with chronic diseases such as hypertension, cardiovascular pathologies, diabetes, obesity, menoposis and pulmonary defects, who do not have the possibility to access to the nearest hospital due to bad road conditions, high cost/poor public transportation network and lack of access to ambulances. Monitoring and evaluation of the project will be done with the local medical staff and through AICM project visits, planned for the first 12 months of the project. This project takes in account the COVID-19 situation by the provision of PPE.
