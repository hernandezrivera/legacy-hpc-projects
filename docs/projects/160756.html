Project Summary

The project seeks to support the provision of emergency shelter and life-saving Non-Food-Items (NFIs)-in-kind to vulnerable populations in Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut, Manyo, Melut and Fashoda Counties of Upper Nile State. The core of this intervention is to improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable protracted IDPs, returnees and host communities / affected non- displaced who are unable to meet their S/NFI
According to the IRNA conducted in Mandeng in Nasir county from 23rd to 27th March 2019 where IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities are in S/NFI needs due to inter communal fighting and huge influx of returnees which puts Nasir in IPC phase 3 (Crisis) or 4 (Emergency) according to NBS projection for January – March 2019.
PEPO (Poverty Eradication Programme Organization) has been active in the field locations of Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut and Fashoda counties where they took part in joint response with partners on the ground such as Nile Hope, ADA, UNKEA especially in S/NFI assessments, Verification of S/NFI beneficiaries, Distribution and PDM in all the locations, PEPO also took part in a joint S/NFI verification exercise in Manga ten in Juba central Equatoria state organized by HDC in May 2019 and is going to participate in the current flooding in greater Upper Nile in the locations of Ulang and Nasir on humanitarians basis in S/NFI supported by OCHA.PEPO also has static presence in Melut, Fashoda, Nasir and Ulang and where it was previously being funded by UNICEF in education and C4D.
The latest IRNA conducted in Ulang County from 8th to 13th May 2019 puts the population in need to over 20,000 beneficiaries. According to ROSS records, the number of South Sudanese who returned from Ethiopia to Ulang County between 17 December 2018 and 3 March 2019 is 50,846. it is estimated that there are around 10,000 to 15,000 returnees in Ulang County, PEPO is planning to respond to 2,000 beneficiaries in Ulang, 1,600 beneficiaries in Nasir County, 4,000 beneficiaries in Maiwut, 3,000 beneficiaries in Manyo, 3,200 beneficiaries in Melut and 5,000 beneficiaries in Fashoda totaling to 18,800 beneficiaries in all the locations.
According to ROSS Sobat, the total population of Returnees to Nasir County amounts to 64,966 persons or 12,000HHs between December 2018 and 3 March 2019. According to the IRNA conducted in Maiwut from 25th Mach to 1st April 2019 the food security situation for Maiwut county at the last IPC shows a deterioration from phase 3 (crisis) in January to a phase 4 (emergency) by the depth of the lean season. In the locations of Maiwut PEPO will target: 2000 beneficiaries, Manyo: 1500 beneficiaries, Melut: 1600 beneficiaries and Fashoda: 2500 beneficiaries totaling to 18,800 beneficiaries in all the PEPO targeted locations.
Manyo County has been cut off from humanitarian intervention throughout 2014 and 2015 HRP which has resulted to absence of humanitarian intervention in the area except occasional mobile food distribution. Manyo remains an area of high risk for the spread of disease outbreak. According to rapid Assessment conducted by SSUDA staff in Adhidwoi Payam in November 2015, there are an estimated 5,000 IDPs in Adhidwoi center alone. More IDPs are spread to other adjacent payams. The IDP population (estimated 70% women and children) has been integrated into the host communities causing serious burden to already vulnerable host population. Most IDPs share Household items with host communities as they lack resources to purchase them while markets are also dysfunctional.
PEPO will in do coordination with other humanitarian actors especially in Kodok and Mandeng in upper nile states and will work hand in hand with state focal persons and the other actors to do Joint needs assessment to ensure that there is needs bases interventions. PEPO will work with the local authorities to identify the vulnerable populations in the hard to reach areas.

1 - Needs

The internal conflict in Ethiopia, inter communal fighting among communities in Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut counties of Upper Nile state has triggered displacement of refugees and IDPs. Hence this ongoing influx requires immediate intervention.However, due to the huge influx of Returnees, IDPs and more vulnerable Host Communities in those locations there is dire need for intervention in S/NFI. Reference to the above revelation is from the inter agencies/NGOs IRNA report of Mandeng (Nasir County) conducted between 23-27 March 2019. In February 2018 violent clashes in Nasir led to swelling numbers of IDPs especially women, children, elderly persons and persons living with disability to Bittugok in Ulang county. Some displaced families decided to stay back and moved stay in hard to reach areas such as Jikmir and Kechkuon in order to avoid subjecting their children to the tough situation in the refugee camps in Ethiopia. The fighting that broke out in March 2018 in Uror, Nyirol and Akobo Counties also forced huge populations, mainly women, children and elderly to look for safety in villages in Ulang and Nasir counties, which are considered to be relatively secure. According to the South Sudan 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, fresh waves of displacement has added to the already big numbers of South Sudanese who have fled their homes, as families are repeatedly uprooted and remain on the move. Protection concerns related to conflict and violence continue to be the primary drivers of displacement. Yet, poverty is so extreme that one lacks the means to provide for oneself and hunger are increasingly being cited as reasons for leaving. The destruction of shelters, farms and stored food items by criminals from communal feuds further aggravates the worsening situation. In the target counties of Nasir, Ulang and Maiwut violence against women and girls continue to increase especially rape and other forms of GBV; in most cases they go unreported. The level of access to services has deteriorated with insecurity and economic decline that has affected the entire community in all the states of South Sudan. The economic crisis has continued to deteriorate as there is high inflation in the market prices, causing food insecurity across the country leading to malnourished children and women the war affected population. A recent hostility between the host community and refugees in the Kule Refugee camps in Ethiopia has led to refugees returning back to South Sudan especially in Nasir and Maiwut counties yet in Ulang, Melut and Fashoda counties of Upper Nile there is ongoing intercommunal fighting adding to the volume of IDPs in those locations of Ulang with 2000 beneficiaries, Nasir with 1600 beneficiaries, Maiwut with 4000 beneficiaries, Manyo with 3000 beneficiaries, Melut with 3200 beneficiaries and Fashoda with 5000 beneficiaries totaling to 18,800 beneficiaries and equivalent of 3134 H/Hs. This comprises of IDPs 10,204 beneficiaries, Returnees 5,422 beneficiaries and Vulnerable Host communities 3,174 beneficiaries.GBV and protection issues would be integrated into the shelter NFI activity cycle so that the vulnerable categories like women, children, elderly and disabled are targeted and this will be done with involvement of partners, stakeholders and actors.PEPO will undertake S/NFI sectoral analysis around the agreed-upon population groups and geographic locations, Conduct severity analysis and mapping that draws on the indicators, scales, and thresholds applied and any additional sectoral indicators, Relate the S/NFI sectoral needs to needs in other sectors, coherent with the inter-sectoral relationships and experiences from previous years’ cross-sectoral analysis, Identify S/NFI monitoring data, indicators and other information that the sector will regularly collect, analyses and share for joint inter-sectoral and sectoral sectoral monitoring on humanitarian needs and Highlight how S/NFI sectoral needs respond to priorities explained.

2 - Activities

1.1.1 Initial Community Engagement to understand the community and enhance their capacity to participate in the project. Training and orientation sessions on the processes of engagement will be emphasized Do no harm, protection and gender considerations. A conflict sensitive framework for programing will be put in place. 1. 1.2 Training Communities and staff in Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Principles to enhance safe programming and accountability to affected populations will be undertaken. 1.1.3 Strengthen and train the community on the formation of Beneficiary Accountability Committees and other feedback and complaints mechanisms during project implementation (especially during assessment /registration and distribution of S/NFIs). A feedback desk will be setup during distribution. Committees will take into account gender and age, ensuring that committee members are representative of women, youth and elderly as well as people with special needs. 1.1.4 Initial Assessment including conducting needs assessments both direct and inter agency to identify beneficiaries’ needs for Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items paying attention to the needs of women, men, girls and boys. This will be done in Cooperation with state authorities and Beneficiary committees and in consultation with other S/NFI and protection partners. 1.1.5 Carry out verification and registration of persons in readiness for distribution (focusing on the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys). Beneficiary Accountability Committees will be formed and participate. State authorities will participate. 1.1.6 Awareness raising including mainstreaming protection and gender. Beneficiary Accountability Committees formed will be trained on generating and dissemination of key messages. Do no harm principles will be adopted? Awareness creation teams will be made up of local members of the community, trained and led by a member of staff. 1.1. 7 Distribution of Emergency Shelter and Non Food Items as per identified and verified needs of the Beneficiaries. Efficient and equitable distribution approaches will be planned and implemented in consultation with the affected populations through the Beneficiary Accountability Committees and in Coordination with cluster and state authorities. PEPO will liaise with IOM the Lead Cluster at the national level with the support of World Vision International as Co-Lead in the implementation of S/NFI activities. IOM also manages the common pipeline which provides a reliable, cost-effective and steady stream of quality materials to partners for distribution to populations in need. In addition to providing stock for immediate distribution, the pipeline pre-positions materials in strategic locations across the country to further facilitate rapid deployment of items. 1.1.8 Monitoring and Evaluation and Learning including post distribution monitoring. This will be an ongoing activity culminating into post distribution monitoring. This will be done at least 3 weeks after distribution to evaluate effectiveness, appropriateness and coverage. The Beneficiary Accountability committee will take an active role in collaboration with project staff in carrying out monitoring and evaluation. 1.1.9 Reporting to funding partners and Beneficiaries as well as sharing with cluster and implementing partners will be an ongoing activity. PEPO will ensure that women, men and children affected by the emergency are involved in planning, implementing and judging the response to their emergency . This is in line with Humanitarian Accountability Partnership International (HAP) which defines it as “the responsible use of power”. This will be done through Leadership / governance, Transparency, Feed back and complaints, Participation, design monitoring and evaluation. All this will adopt upwards or backwards accountability to donors; horizontal or lateral accountability to peer agencies, and forwards or downwards accountability to those receiving funds.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project is legally binding according to the laws of South Sudan, It targets each segment of the population differently depending on the needs identified. Women and girls are generally affected disproportionately to men and boys and this is taken into account when designing protection interventions. For instance, Women & young girls walking long distances to access water or forage for food are exposed to greater risk of abduction and SGBV compared to men and boys. Protection interventions must be alive to such realities. Further, the aged and disabled are more vulnerable. Interventions are informed by risk analysis that takes into account gender, age and cultural practices that increase or reduce vulnerability to risk this project is designed to consider protection of the beneficiaries from all forms of risks associated with its implementation. This would be and ensuring information acquired from a beneficiary would be kept confidential and could only be released to the third party at the consent of the originator. Parties would be required to adhere to the rules before any intervention is carried out, PEPO in collaboration with Protection clusters/partners on the ground and the local authority will make sure the areas are safe from any danger to the populations/beneficiaries and environment for successful implementation of the project

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

This project will be implemented in close collaboration with the Shelter Cluster, beneficiaries’, community leaders, ROSS/Protection cluster and partners and local authority and project activities will be crafted taking into account the gaps identified through the multi-sector assessment missions conducted. PEPO will dynamically adapt the project to the specific needs of men, women, girls and boys and respond accordingly. With regards to the cluster objective, the proposed project aims to distribute shelter material for setting up or improving dilapidated shelters and NFIs to IDPs, returnees and more vulnerable host communities in Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut, Fashoda and Melut counties to beneficiaries. The target of the intervention will be those Men, Women, Boys and Girls and PSNs that are unable to meet their ES/NFI needs, the shelter materials and NFIs will be sourced from the core pipeline stores.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is embedded into the existing government and community system at all levels from village to Boma, payam, county, state and national levels where PEPO will network with partners, beneficiaries and the informal sector in planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation for community ownership and project sustainability. This will be done in a consultative and coordinated manner for inclusiveness. Selection of beneficiaries will be done using the set criteria so that the right beneficiaries are targeted. Community leaders, local authorities, church leaders will be involved at all levels of S/NFI cycle. Project cycle begins with community consultations where consent is obtained through the community gate-keepers. This serves as the entry point and later the members of the community are involved in identifying their needs, priorities and implementation strategies. This allows the community to be responsible and also to encourage project ownership. Joint project assessment with community representatives as well as interviews with beneficiaries and stakeholders are taken into consideration during project design. Community committees’ representatives participate in the identification and allocation of services to the various stakeholders/persons in need.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The following are the South Sudan National Objectives for S/NFI: •Secure access to civilian populations in need of emergency humanitarian assistance and protection. •Create a safe and secure environment by establishing the rule of law to achieve durable solutions, including return, reintegration and relocation •Implement relief and development programmes in line with internationally accepted human rights and humanitarian principles and standards. •Provide special consideration to conflict-affected persons -including children, women, widows, persons with disabilities, etc. in the provision of public service, including access to health and education services and granting the host communities the same protection and humanitarian services. •Foster community dialogue and reconciliation not only at the political levels but also inter-communal levels. •Implement early recovery and stabilization interventions especially in communities where individuals are returning or being relocated, to include; by restoring the capacity of local governments, rule of law institutions, and creating value chains for sustainable livelihoods. The framework seeks to comprehensively address and resolve internal displacement by assisting and protecting IDPs and host communities; and finding safe and lasting solutions for IDPs, including by supporting their return and the reconstruction of conflict-affected areas. The framework will also enhance lives of the vulnerable people and contribute to stability and peace in the country. Priority actions for achieving the goal is through enhanced coordination within government institutions, at national, state and local levels as well as collaboration with humanitarian and development partners.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Protection is one of the most important aspects considering the emphasis given to it by Humanitarian Coordinator during the HRP development process and presentation. PEPO will mainstream protection in its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls receive protection when they are providing information during assessment and also during distribution of items. Information provided by survivors or beneficiaries are safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm. PEPO will also ensure that the distribution sites are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Our actions will address both basic needs including NFIs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. We will also pay special attention to people living with disabilities, children, child-headed and female-headed households.PEPO will advocate for area-based approaches to programming and coordination’ in recognition of the scale, nature and complexity of emergency in all the field locations because it helps to improve clarity and understanding in programming by providing a clear location and set of actors to engage with, promote the idea of shelter and settlement because it is necessary to consider the wider spatial needs of ‘settlement-based assistance’ and a ‘neighborhood approach’ to engaging with communities, as opposed to being driven by sectoral priorities such as shelter.- PEPO will encourage increasingly more joint missions with partners. For example S/NFI partners can build on cooking coping mechanisms through the Nutrition/FSL cooking projects, as well as contribute to decreased malnutrition through mosquito net interventions. Malaria in children under 5 leads to malnutrition. PEPO’s joint response with other sectors will mitigates misuse of items. For example, some people use fishing nets for mosquito nets and vice versa.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

PEPO will form project management committees at payam level consisting of 10 people whose work is to ensure that project activities are implemented as planned, the committee will be responsible for identification of youth groups, women groups and other stakeholders and will train them on construction of emergency shelter using local materials for resilience. PEPO will carry Due Diligence by research, analysis and monitoring to ensure the protection of HLP rights throughout the shelter program cycle. This requires a double focus: 1) to identify and obtain the agreement of respective owner(s) of land and properties used for shelter interventions prior to any intervention, and 2) to provide the greatest degree of tenure security to beneficiaries feasible in the given context. A Due Diligence approach also ensures that vulnerable groups, especially women, are taken into consideration. Throughout. Failure to conduct Due Diligence not only risks significant HLP rights violations and the breach of humanitarian principles, but can also derail entire shelter interventions. PEPO will nominate (a) member(s) of their team as focal point(s) (FPs) responsible for HLP-related concerns that emerge. The FPs will be trained in HLP and know how to address the HLP issues they are confronted with. The FP position will remain in place throughout the entire shelter project. FPs should also include women. • Identify local community representatives and de facto local authority representative(s) responsible for reporting shelter-related HLP issues to the FPs, if and when they arise.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Main risks stem from affecting variables of the ongoing conflict which continues to divide segments of society, and from leaving some more vulnerable (whether physically, mentally, or economically). The PEPO’s program will build on its acceptance as a neutral, impartial, independent and humanitarian actor, focusing on ensuring the functionality of service provision structures and steering away from institutional/political agendas into which ethnicity is often intertwined, which could represent liabilities to its humanitarian action. The ‘do no harm’ principle imbues all considerations in the planning and project cycle as exposed in the project summary. Nonetheless, risks always remain a possibility, and measures to prevent and mitigate those risks will be taken accordingly. PEPO will adhere to security procedures and updates from relevant authorities such as local authorities in Nasir, NGO forum and OCHA. -Proper coordination with the existing partners on the ground such as SSUDA (NFI), CHADO (GBV), Nile Hope (NFI), ADA (protection) - PEPO has already established local access units, with all the actors, community leaders and the religious groups which negotiate issues of access and security within Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut, Fashoda and Melut., PEPO is able to plan early and intervene on time. PEPO is aware that certain cultural practices and customs may pose a barrier to access and participation of beneficiaries. But this project will ensures that there is a buy-in process that involves consultations with community gatekeepers such as elders, religious leaders and local government authorities for access to agree upon prior to project implementation. It is part and parcel of the entry strategies that the beneficiary project committee develops and implements. Further, the participation and collaboration of community stakeholders who are opinion leaders lends credibility and promotes ownership of the project interventions.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

PEPO will put in place a complaints/feedback handling mechanism that recognizes the importance of the process and the confidentiality required. Complaints are categorized in terms of severity and handled appropriately. Minor complaints are acknowledged and promptly resolved. Most of these pose minimal risk to the organization and beneficiaries. Most significant complaints are acknowledged, an initial assessment is done, an investigation follows and the appropriate remedial action taken. PEPO adheres to set standards as follows: PEPO’s handling of complaints will meet the following minimum standards: 1. All complaints will be acknowledged as soon as possible, and ideally within 5 working days by the recipient. All complainants will receive a copy of our policy. 2. All complainants will receive a response giving the outcome of their complaint as soon as possible and ideally within thirty working days of receipt. If the matter is more complex a further acknowledgement will be sent explaining what further investigation is required to resolve the complaint and the likely timeframe. 3. All complaints should be recorded on a Complaints Record Form. 4. All complainants will be treated respectfully, whether it is felt the complaint is justified or not. A beneficiary committee is in place that collaborates with project staff and local stakeholders to receive and handle complaints. Beneficiaries can also send their complaints or feedback directly to the Complaints Handling focal person, who also visits beneficiaries to listen and receive feedback Specific procedures PEPO also ensures the participation of beneficiary representative at the project review stage PEPO has Feedback/complaint mechanisms tools to measure and collect information on the project progress from beneficiaries and community stakeholders.
