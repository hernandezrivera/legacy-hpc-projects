Project Summary

The overall objective is to address the needs of the most vulnerable communities affected by the crisis in hard-to-reach areas of Bandiagara through an integrated and a protection mainstreaming response.
The first specific objective is to ensure meaningful access to life saving and qualitative primary health and nutrition care for people affected and isolated by the crisis in Bandiagara Circle. PUI will synergize the different modalities of service provision of primary health care in Mali (facility-based services, outreach activities, mobile health team and community-based strategies). PUI will support the salary of key personnel in the health facilities and reinforce capacities of the health work force (community health volunteers, traditional birth attendants, health care workers and health mobile team) to ensure availability of quality health care and provide the minimum package of activities. In addition, free of charge services for all people in hard-to-reach areas will be provided by the mobile clinic and targeted free of charge care for the most vulnerable populations (PLW, children under 5 years of age and IDPs) in the health facilities. In addition to ensuring the access to the minimum service package (curative and preventive), the intervention targets specifically integrated management of acute malnutrition, medical management of GBV survivors and information on GBV services availability.
The second specific objective is to improve food security caused by displacement or insecurity for the most vulnerable people. PUI will provide food assistance to vulnerable households in IDP sites (hosts and IDPs since in Bandiagara IDPs mostly settle in villages) during the lean season, when the risk of food insecurity is higher. An assessment conducted by PUI in October 2021 with the local authorities helped to identify in-kind assistance as the preferred modality to address the needs. However, PUI will also conduct gender-segregated Focus Group Discussions with beneficiaries to confirm the relevance of the in-kind modality and thus reserving itself the right to reconsider the latter to take into account beneficiaries’ preference and capacity of stakeholders in the area to implement a cash or voucher assistance.
The beneficiaries of the project are host populations and internally displaced people of Bandiagara Circle.

1 - Contexte

The crisis that Mali is facing since 2012, has had security, economic, and political impacts including the destabilization of the administrative system, which has led to the rarity of basic public services, particularly in the health and nutrition sectors, which exacerbates the vulnerability of the population. An estimated 2.8 million people are affected by the crisis in Mopti region, of which 1.7 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. In this region, the security situation is deteriorating constantly. According to INSO data, there is a continued increase in incidents linked to armed groups in the region (176 in Q4 2020 versus 192 in Q2 2021). Insecurity has quickly led to a complex humanitarian situation and a raise of the humanitarian needs among Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and the host population. Thus, the focus is on Mopti region for this proposed project since the region hosts the largest number of IDPs (150,746 i.e. 40%) , including 26,161 IDPs in Bandiagara Circle alone.In Mopti in 2020, the ratio was 3.12 functional health professionals per 10,000 people against the 23 recommended in the WHO standards . Due to the worsening of the security situation and the economic degradation, communities face strong barriers in accessing health services in Bandiagara, including lack of quality service provision, absenteeism of qualified personnel, movement restriction and gender inequality, impacting the delay in seeking care. The service utilization rate was 18% in 2019 and decreased to 16% in 2020. In parallel, needs for essential health care are increasing. This instability worsens the nutritional status of the most vulnerable people. The region is one of the most affected by acute malnutrition, particularly among children under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The SMART 2020 survey results show persistent chronic malnutrition affecting more than a quarter of children, even though it was conducted outside the lean season, which registers a usual peak of malnutrition. According to the Burden and Caseload 2021, the prevalence of SAM is 2.2% in Bandiagara Circle, which is above the WHO emergency threshold of 2%. Lack of access to health care services increases the risk of deadly consequences of malnutrition and untreated illnesses such as acute respiratory infection and malaria. Another impact of the ongoing crisis in Bandiagara Circle concerns the food security sector. Analysis of the results of the March 2021 Harmonised Framework reveals a significant increase of food insecure populations in Bandiagara Circle. Indeed, the area is globally in phase 3 (crisis phase), registering 24% of households in phase 3 and 2% in phase 4 (emergency phase). This deterioration of food security is illustrated by the degradation of the food consumption score (FCS) in the circle. The number of people having a poor FCS (<21) hits a sky high percentage of 31.4 in 2021 compared to the 1.84% of 2020. This is explained by the several shocks households experienced in the months preceding the survey (related to insecurity and rising food prices).
