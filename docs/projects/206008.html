Project Summary

The project targets 18,000 individuals in Pibor County (GPAA), with an integrated WASH/nutrition/GBV approach, using in-kind distribution modalities, hygiene promotion and construction/rehabilitation of water points and sanitation facilities, including a Cash-For-Work component (250 HHs). YEDA has static presence in Pibor, with a fully functional operational office and dedicated staff.
YEDA will provide hygiene kits to 3,000 units SAM child-caretaker at nutrition facility/household level, sensitizing the caretakers about items’ use and disposal, including demonstration of the use of chemical products for the treatment of the water inside houses and clearly explain how/when to use the different items. This will be coupled with promotion of basic hygiene practices at household level, including hand-washing at critical times, hygiene and household water treatment, food preparation and safe storage, and household waste disposal. The content of a standard kit includes: (1) one bucket with tap, 20l; (2) one bucket without tap, 20lt or one jerrycan (10 or 20lt), (3) chlorine tablets for 3 months (e.g. Aquatabs 67 mg), (4) Sedimentation/Disinfection powder for 3 months (e.g. PUR) with one piece of filter clothes and (5) soap, 250 grs per person per month for 3 months. The content of the kit might vary according to household size, and could be adapted in case the items are not available in the WASH cluster core pipeline or local market.
Concurrently, access to safe water will be improved in payams/communities with high GAM prevalence related to WASH, by rehabilitating 5 water points at nutrition/health facility level, facilitating nutrition programming and reducing the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission. Other 5 water points and 5 sanitation facilities will be rehabilitated at community level, using a flood-resilient design. This is linked to the Collective Outcome 8.3. “Climate change-related effects including drought and flood displacement are 30 per cent lower in 2025 than in 2021”.
Quality testing and treatment using Del Agua water kits will be conducted on regular basis. After the initial tests, bacteriological testing will be done on a monthly basis, which will determine the water treatment according to the results (e.g. chlorination where bacteriological contamination). The treated water sources will be monitored daily or on a need-basis, measuring that the Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) at point of use is within recommended standards (0.2–0.5 mg/l).
More so, 8,000 vulnerable people (1,334 HHs) will be targeted with distribution of soap paired with hygiene promotion (community sessions) using the PHAST/CHAST approach to improve hygiene behaviours, to reduce diarrheal diseases and encourage effective management of WASH services. Additionally, 50 trained Community Hygiene Promoters (10 per County) will conduct house-to-house visits and hygiene awareness sessions on hand washing, MHM, infant and young child feeding (IYCF), water treatment, food storage and waste disposal, reaching 10,000 beneficiaries. Further, 2,800 women and girls will be reached with distribution of Menstrual Health Management (MHM) kits.
All interventions will be carried out following Covid-19 preventive measures. Key messaging including HIV/AIDS, COVID-19 prevention, EVD and cholera prevention will be passed on during distribution and trainings, as an EPR mainstreaming. Additionally, in line with the Collective Outcome 8.2 “Improved access to a minimum package of program standards for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of the priority locations by 2025”, GBV mainstreaming will be applied throughout the project, ensuring sensitization on prevention, activation of existing mechanisms of self-protection at community level, and referral pathways for safe and timely access to quality case management and psychosocial support.

1. Needs

In 2023, the inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation in South Sudan will greatly affect the living standards and wellbeing of 6.1 million people (24% women, 53% children, 15% PWDs). This is caused by the recurrent conflict and flooding that have been leading to destruction of properties, WASH infrastructures and sources of livelihoods. In GPAA in particular, persistent attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, cattle raids and revenge attacks resulted in the displacement of tens of thousands of people, several killings, increased incidents of sexual violence, abductions and looting/destruction of property and resources (South Sudan HNO, 2023). The economic downturn has also exacerbated the disruption of water services, limiting resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes, while protection concerns have risen (Ibid). Access to water in GPAA is below the national average of 42% of people having no access to an improved water source and 45% needing to walk for more than 30 minutes to access their main water source (South Sudan humanitarian snapshot, July 2023), especially in areas with a high number of IDPs. This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to GBV (2022 ISNA Household survey).Poor access to WASH services and goods, combined with high levels of food insecurity, increases the likelihood of water-borne diseases, malnutrition and school dropout (HNO 2023). According to the IPC analysis Sept2023-Jul2024, in the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2023 to March 2024, 25,000 people are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Pibor County (11,000). The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is reported at 28.1% at national level, and the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate during the lean seasons. Worsening this situation, lack of latrines and/or overfilled ones lead to open defecation resulting in waterborne diseases. Access to improved sanitation is below 10% in GPAA, leading to widespread open defecation, with associated risks of GBV and waterborne diseases. Also, recurrent flooding regularly damages the existing sanitation infrastructures and with frequent displacement, there is great hesitation by the communities to invest in, reconstruct or repair sanitation facilities (HNO, 2023). This increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or waterborne diseases.WASH-related GBV remains a reality for majority of the population, with 16% of both rural and urban households reporting that they felt unsafe while collecting water at the water points. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault/harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines, and 56.7% of households surveyed expressed sexual violence as the main security reason for movement restriction (2022 ISNA Household survey).Finally, hygiene practices, and especially hand-washing at critical moments are reportedly low across GPAA, due to high concentration of the population in specific areas, increasing risk of epidemic outbreaks. This highlights the need of spreading preventive hygiene messages and establishing hand-washing facilities (OCHA, July 2023). The combination of the critical WASH and nutrition situation will lead to high levels of morbidity and mortality if not faced rapidly. In fact, inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreak, but also to increased SAM cases; lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge of hygiene practices contributes to poor feeding habits of children leading to malnutrition. Data from IOM, OCHA and WASH partners revealed that displaced persons in the targeted areas have little access to clean water or improved sanitation, as well as little opportunity to practice proper hygiene in what turns out to be flooded areas for most of the year (IRNA, Pibor, July 2023).

2. Project activities

The project activities will include:(1) Assessment, Analysis and Planning: (a) identify needs and gaps, and market functionality; (b) and investigate community norms and practices related to WASH that may increase the risk of GBV; (c) assess the level of participation and leadership of women, adolescent girls and other at-risk groups in WASH, (d) analyze physical safety of and access to WASH facilities to identify associated risks of GBV, (e) assess awareness of WASH staff on basic issues related to GBV, (f) review existing/proposed community outreach material related to WASH to ensure it includes basic information about GBV risk reduction. (2) Resource mobilization: (a1) Mobilizing items from the WASH cluster core pipeline/field warehouses for in-kind response or (a2) make arrangements through MoUs with local suppliers to ensure availability of stocks and fixed prices for Cash Voucher Assistance; (b) training WASH staff and Project Management Committees/hand-pump mechanics on the safe design and construction of WASH facilities that mitigate GBV risks, (c) targeting women for job skills training on operation and maintenance of water supply and sanitation.(3) Programming: (a) activity planning involving women and other at-risk groups in the siting, design, construction and maintenance of WASH facilities and in hygiene promotion activities; (b)Training of hand-pump mechanics, Water Management Committees (WMCs-60% female) in water resource management/maintenance and HHWT use, and Community Hygiene Promoters (CHP); (c) Hygiene kits’ distribution at nutrition/health facility level and household level for units “caretaker/SAM child”; (d) Hygiene promotion campaigns (house-to-house, community sessions) and distribution of soap; (e) construction/rehabilitation of water points and sanitation facilities using a flood-resistant design, including a Cash-For-Work component; (f) water quality testing and treatment using Del Agua water kits on regular basis; (g) regular community consultations, improving their mechanisms of self-protection to increase the availability and accessibility of water for women, girls and other at-risk groups and strategies to maximizes the safety, privacy and dignity of WASH facilities; (4) Communication and Information Sharing: (a) Consultation with GBV specialists to identify safe, confidential and appropriate systems of care (i.e. referral pathways) for survivors, (b) GBV messages (including where to report risk and how to access care) incorporated into hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.(5) M&E: (a) Monitoring of WASH facility construction/rehabilitation, and monitoring of hygiene promotion activities (house-to-house, community sessions); (b) M&E visits to the field by the WASH and M&E team, jointly with community leaders and partners for quality, measuring results against KAP baseline; (c) PDM 4-6 weeks after distribution; (d) end line evaluation with external consultant to measure impact of the project, documenting best practices while providing evidence-based recommendations for improvement; (e) documentation and reporting to the cluster and UN OCHA.NOTE: At all stages, the CHP, WMC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will be trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies and will sign a Code of Conduct. IEC materials in local languages will be used for sensitization on PSEA, WASH-related GBV and health risks in all areas. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, as well as hand-washing stations. Each activity will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction, PSEA and health risk preventions in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.
