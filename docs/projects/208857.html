Project Summary

Sudan is facing a complex humanitarian crisis, with more than 7.4 million people displaced inside and outside the country since fighting broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) on 15 April 20231. The conflict has spread to central and eastern Sudan, affecting crop production and food security1. Considering the current context in Sudan in particular Central Darfur the project will be implemented in West Jabal Marra (Health and Nutrition) and Zalingei town (WASH). The proposed project will be complimented by other Islamic Relief Sudan (IRS) ongoing projects in the target areas. Due to the humanitarian context, (protracted armed conflict and new conflicts that have led to large numbers of IDPs, deaths and destruction of properties) coupled with hyperinflation and civil and political unrest that have caused adverse trickling effects in the target areas, high cost of living, poor harvest due to failed rains and poor agricultural practices, poor health seeking behavior, the area is estimated to be a high priority in the HRP/HNO 2024.
The proposed project will lead to reduced suffering, increased, and maintained human dignity, and lives saved for 156250 (27,894 men, 47,925 women, 38,629boys, 41,802 girls) out of which 117187 IDPs, 38,063 host community members. The overall project goal is to address the immediate needs of conflict-affected people through integrated health, nutrition, and WASH, as identified during IR Sudan’s Needs Assessment and analysis leading up to HRP/HNO 2024. The project will employ a 3-level implementation strategy whereby IRS will do direct implementation of interventions, work with local partner and line ministries and departments and last one being through working with the affected people to achieve project goal. The interventions will be lifesaving, preventive services, improving the quality of services and improving the living conditions, wellbeing, and safety of the affected people. Insecurity and threat to life of both affected people and staff and loss of property and displacement remains a great risk for the project and IRS will work with relevant authorities to reduce the risks involved by strictly adhering to security advisories, full implementing the do no harm policy, conducting security and conflict sensitivity assessments and updating the Conflict Register on a regular basis. IRS has three sub offices in Central Darfur at the State Level in Zalingei, West (Nertiti) and Central (Golo) Jebel Marra where the project will be implemented. The main office is operating from Gadarif and Port Sudan with over 200 members of staff, 85 (45% Female) of whom are based in Central Darfur. The project staff are based in the West and Central Jebel Marra and Zalingei localities and can interact daily with relevant stakeholders including other humanitarian actors, government departments and ministries to facilitate project activities and ensure continued access. Although IRS will directly implement all activities, the team prioritizes collaboration with local actors throughout the project cycle. IRS’ ability to implement quality SRHR and protection programming in such a sensitive context, access to hard-to-reach and conflict-affected areas, and the large amount of institutional donor funding illustrates IRS is highly qualified and accepted as a leading agency in Sudan. In addition, IRS has well-established community relations through well-functioning community structures that will support the project implementation. Members of IRS staff are from the community and speak local languages which supports IR’s continued access and community-based approach, for example the ability to access the IDP camps and IDP informal settlements in Jebel Marra. IRS is uniquely posited as a faith-based organization, enabling IRS to address deeply held beliefs around inclusivity, gender relations, culture, and religion; thus, opening opportunities to contribute to sustainable change.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

IRW has in place a robust complaint response mechanism (CRM) strategy that is fully implemented during project life-cycle and are culturally appropriate, accessible and effective in line with CHS 5. These allows for timely response and feedback to the affected population. Three levels of monitoring beneficiaries feedbacks will followed for the purpose of quality delivery and continuous improvement of services. These are the establishment of complaint boxes, to enable affected populations to report their complaints and provide feedback, The CRM number and email address is printed on each activity implementation banners in local language to allow beneficiaries to report and CRM mobile number is also provided which can be used by majority of the affected populations. These three levels of obtaining community feedbacks is crucial aspect ensured by IRW staff to manage and respond to feedbacks for improvement purposes.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IRW will create and support a referral pathway to enable survivors of SEA, including children, to receive immediate professional assistance, referring them to relevant service providers within the areas of project intervention. IRW will further work closely with other stakeholders including UN agencies to strengthen SEA mitigation, reporting and provide support assistance to the victims while ensuring safety, security and privacy of the whole process and that of the victims. A dedicated staff will be identified through Protection and Inclusion Department to ensure that all cases are followed and actioned to conclusion and that victims receive timely, effective, efficient and professional assistance. IRW will strengthen complaint and feedback system in the target PHCs and OTP centres and encourage communities and beneficiaries to share their feedback and complaints, ensuring confidentiality and safety of complainants and use the feedback to improve planning and implementation of project activities. All relevant community segments will be encouraged to participate in the project through community mobilization workshops and sensitization sessions. The project will prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm, providing meaningful access, participation and empowerment while also being accountable to the affected communities through counselling, information dissemination and access to services and rights (vulnerable people are supported to improve their living conditions and resilience). This will include information on how to prevent, anticipate, and address risks of violence, discrimination, and marginalization towards vulnerable groups by enhancing awareness and community resilience. Furthermore, through capacity building initiatives, girls and boys will be protected from violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect. IRW prioritizes protection (including Child Protection and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA) and safeguarding against any form of abuse of its beneficiaries (direct and indirect) and staff. IRW through its Gender, Protection and Inclusion focal persons ensures that all project interventions and community engagements are in line with specific needs of men, women, girls and boys including PwD, chronically ill and the elderly and that staff and project interventions are sensitive to vulnerabilities. IRW prioritizes a MEAL system that provides consistent high-quality information, contributes to superior program performance, and promotes quality assurance in a humanitarian response and emphasizes a participatory approach that reflects the voices of all parties involved in the project, while complying with donor’s requirements and ensuring Value for Money.
