Project Summary

The project proposed by IOM intends to address health needs identified by the health sector in Burundi in 2023 through the implementation of the following interventions for the benefit of internally displaced persons, returnees, and host community members:
1. Ensure access to basic health care for IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable populations in priority localities (access to care, pre-positioning of emergency stock for victims of natural disasters, and/or public health events);
2. Organize awareness raising sessions for affected populations to health risks in IDP sites and returnee areas including host community members in priority localities
3. Reinforcement of the early warning system (surveillance) on IDP sites and returnees’ areas including host populations of priority localities.
4. Organization of multisectoral health need assessment in areas hit by emergency events.
5. Provide mental health and psychosocial support for IDPs, returnees and other vulnerable populations and host community members affected by public health events
According to the latest DTM data of October 2022, 89 % of the 75,300 displaced persons in Burundi are displaced due to climate related hazards. Between September 2022 and January 2023 only, more than 90,000 individuals were affected by climate-induced disasters, resulting in internal displacement of more than 10,000 persons.
Climate change also presents significant direct and indirect risks to public health. Many aspects of health are also influenced by climatic and weather conditions, with a direct link to illnesses resulting from an increased frequency and severity of extreme weather events such as precipitation, flooding, droughts, and storms.
More specifically, the ongoing cholera outbreak that was declared on the 1st January 2023 by the Ministry of Health in Bujumbura Mairie has been linked to heavy rains that caused flooding and consequent contamination of water, which allowed the epidemic to spread to Bujumbura and Cibitoke provinces.
Moreover, it has been noticed that mental health and psychosocial well-being of IDPs and returnees can be affected during public health emergencies, exposing IDPs to risks of violence and loss of livelihoods, which can have severe impacts on mental health. When the internal displacement status is combined with the fear of contracting transmissible diseases, the risks of anxiety, depression, sleep disorders, psychological distress and post-traumatic stress disorder will increase and impact well-being and daily activities.
In 2023, 174,034 individuals have health related needs.
IOM's proposed response is in line with the health sector's 2023 strategy and will aim to address objectives 1,5,6 and 7.
Under objective 1 of the health sector strategy, IOM will support the Government of Burundi to provide basic health care to a minimum of 20,000 IDPs, returnees and host community members by constructing basic health facilities on IDP sites, strengthening the capacity of health facilities in host community areas and by pre-positioning emergency kits in health districts likely to be affected by public health emergencies.
Activities to meet objective 5 of the health sector strategy will target a minimum of 30,000 persons and will consist in organizing awareness raising and prevention sessions on public health issues resulting from natural disasters.
In order to meet objective 6 of the health sector strategy, IOM will target 6 health districts and support them to set up an early warning system to detect and deal with public health events on IDP sites, returnees and host community areas. IOM will identify and train community health workers to establish event-based surveillance mechanisms at the community level.
Finally, in line with Objective 7 of the sector health strategy, IOM will collaborate with its government partners and other stakeholders to quickly conduct multisectoral health need assessment in emergency situations, develop and share the report.
