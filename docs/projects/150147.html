Project Summary

The project aims at securing sufficient and safe drinking water quantities for 851 households (4,874
individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “C” for household purposes. This fits and linked with Strategic Objective 1 (SO1) and Strategic Objective 2 (SO2) of HRP as well fits with WASH strategic objective 2. The implementation of the activities will also improve the monthly income by lowering the expenditures of purchasing water.
Throughout the current and previous need assessments and other studies conducted by PHG with the local authorities and community based organizations it was clear that approximately 1,651 households/ families (9,080 individuals) are in need of the below humanitarian needs for the coming 2 years (2019-2020), however, for the coming year our target is to respond 851 households/ families (4,874 individuals – 2,315 females and 2,559 males) with total budget of $450,000 in different selected locations based on the need priorities and emergency.
However, for the second year our target is to respond 800 households/ families (4,050 individuals – 1,925 females and 2,127 males) with total budget of $1,000,000 based on the need priorities and emergency in the following locations: water network in Khalet Abu Alloz (Tammun) in Tubas, water networks in Al Majd, Sikka and Tawas in Hebron Governorate.

Needs

PHG conducted a thorough needs assessment in March 2018 in all West Bank. The assessment covered 115 vulnerable communities in Area C in addition to 2017 need assessment covered 130 vulnerable communities. It was based on; field visits, interviews and questionnaires with potential beneficiaries including Bedouin communities, local authorities and community based organizations including women CBO’s. The assessment was completed in coordination with the PWA and other water related parties. Around 150 women and girls have been included and interviewed within the two assessments.The targeted communities have been selected considering people highly affected by water access (under served and/or un served communities) and affected by forcible transfer. They are those more affected by the impact of drought events, increase of water costs during the dry period and lack of access to water. The targeted households are those who suffer from water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. In addition, part of the proposed humanitarian needs have been inspired and modeled based on previous studies and water master plans such as water master plan for Area C (2017-2035) completed by ACF and GVC in full coordination with PWA. Therefore, it was clear from PHG assessments carried with the local authorities and community based organizations including women CBO’s that approximately 851 households/ families (4,874 individuals – 2,315 females and 2,559 males) are in need of the below humanitarian needs in the West Bank, Below are some of the assessment highlights: - Communities in the northern Jordan Valley (Tubas and western Ramallah Bedouins) are buying water tankers from the surrounding water springs and filling points. On average water cost is as high as 20-30 NIS/m3 and they are using open steel barrels to store water for daily use. This practices exposes the water to contamination. Proposed activity to address the high costs is supplying and installing plastic water tanks of 1.5 m3 capacity.- The available water networks are in need of rehabilitation in Az Za'ayyem (Jerusalem) and Khirbet Umm Athba (Jericho).- Around 2,067 inhabitants located in Area C are unable to access enough water, the use level is well below humanitarian standards (35-55 liters depending on locality). It is therefore suggested to provide them with plastic water tanks of 5m3 capacity as well to provide them with water quantities through mobile tankers to increase their access to water.- A number Bedouins and herders’ communities are not connected to water networks or they are using primary plastic pipes to get water to houses. They are mainly dependent on mobile water tankers to fulfill their needs of water for domestic uses and for livestock. The average water price by water tinkering is 25-30 NIS/m3, water tankers are filled from the sounding filling points existing in the neighboring localities. Suggested intervention to improve water availability and reduce the high cost. All interventions and activities will target communities either fully or partially located in area C and East Jerusalem, while excluding others with less pronounced protection concerns. The targeted households suffer from continuous marginalization, water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. It is worthwhile noting that some interventions are in area B. However their main aim is to serve communities totally or partially located in Area C. This is particularly valid for the following activities: mobile water trucks, water networks construction and rehabilitation.

Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the below parties:- National and international NGOs active in the geographic area - PWA and WBWD- Local water providers (regarding water distribution activity)As to water distribution, PHG agreed to use same procedures and mechanism in line with the PWA/ WASH cluster modality, in case of any activities overlapping at community level PHG is committed to coordinate this issue over families level and beneficiaries with other organization in field.Major activities or outputs of the project are:O1: access to basic water services is improved. A1.1 Supplying and installation of 67 plastic water storage tanks of 5,000 liters for the benefit of 116 households (371 females and 410 males). The water storage tanks localities are distributed as follow: 14 in Al Hadidiya, 21 in Humsa in Tubas Gov., 20 in Ras al Tin, 10 in Wadi as Seeq in Ramallah Gov., tanks suppling and installation will be completed by local contractors and suppliers with the coordination of the project committee and community based organizations.A1.2 2km of water network extensions and rehabilitation for Az Za'ayyem in Jerusalem Gov. for the benefit of 500 households (1,188 females and 1,313 males).A1.3 To install 1.5 km of 75 mm HDPE Water Supply Pipes to connect Khirbet Umm Athba (al-Auja) in Jericho with water from Al Auja for the benefit of 46 households (146 female and 161 Male).A1.4 Distributing 7,720 cubic meters of water by mobile tanks for the benefit of 189 families (611 females and 675 males). The water distribution locations are: 540 m3 in Al Hadidiya, 1,410 m3 in Al Farisiya, 1,200 m3 in Hammamat al Maleh, 920 m3 in Humsa, 240 m3 in Khirbet Samra, 210 m3 in Mak-hul in Tubas Gov., 1,670 m3 in Ras al Tin and 1,530 m3 in Wadi as Seeq in Ramallah Gov. A1.5 Conducting WASH awareness and promotion campaigns to cover all topics related to water handling, water management and saving in addition to training about facilities operation and maintenance as well as to include hygiene promotion topics of the above mentioned interventions., such WASH awareness will enhance water right of marginalized people protected and focused on gender equality and women ’s empowerment in household water management and water sanitation. The other household members (children, youth, men, disabled persons, and elders) have improved their water consumption and sanitation practices due to the increased amount of available water at their houses . PHG is committed to consult women in each targeted household on operational details (e.g. precise location of storage tanks), considering that they will be the primary beneficiaries of interventions. Female PHG staff will be part of the implementation team enabling an open environment for female beneficiaries to voice their concerns. In general women and girls will be affected positively after improving the WASH conditions and facilities on the household level and this will be reflected on reducing daily chores, more time allocated to girls’ education, and improved feminine hygiene. Appropriate WASH facilities should also result in reduced conflict in the household, improving quality of life, and significant decline in health problems (especially for children), time and money saving by the family allowing for greater focus on children's education.Women are more vulnerable to lack of access to water. In the same time inadequate availability of water, collecting water from unprotected sources will expose women, children, and old people in particular to high risks of waterborne diseases and other illnesses. Thus, the project will contribute to secure more clean water and will give more weights to female headed households in beneficiary selection process.

This includes all information, observations that would be gathered during these visits, will be documented, and processed according to PHG internal result monitoring system. d) Inputs and feedback from project Committee meetings on the overall implementation of the project. PHG will document all information, observations that is gathered during these meetings and use it to ensure better informed decision making and project planning and implementation. e) Gathered data segregated by gender and analyzed to assess the level of project impact on gender and to make sure that target number of female beneficiaries are met during the implementation. f) End line data collection and analysis to report on the project impact on the target beneficiaries.g) Monthly meetings with local stakeholders to follow up on the implementation of activities and to address any potential obstacle during implementation.These tools together with baseline and end line data analysis and KAP assessment performed by PHG project team will be used to derive activity monitoring and reporting to management, donors and stakeholders on monthly, midterm and final project reporting periods or as established in the project agreement. PHG team will take all necessary measures to address any potential problem that may arise during implementation and will also liaison with all stakeholders including WASH cluster, PWA, for any arising issue during implementation and will make sure that proper actions is taken to address these problems and mitigate their likely impact on realizing project results.PHG will ensure timely reporting both narrative and financial to donors and relevant stakeholders as per the project agreement and will ensure that reporting quality is met through its quality management system. This will be ensured through the Monitoring and Evaluation officer who will follow up with project manager to ensure compliance with the quality and timely reporting.
