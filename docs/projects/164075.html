Project Summary

In line with the acute needs outlined below and the Sector strategy for 2020, PIN plans to respond to the WASH needs in Northern Syria under two followings results:
Result 1) Deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behavior and practices of most vulnerable people. PIN will focus on both distributions of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion and improved access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services. Interventions will primarily target newly displaced/returned populations in informal settlements, host communities, and camps, based upon coordination with relevant cluster partners to maximize response coverage and minimize gaps. Technical design and proposed level of services provided through these interventions will follow acceptable standards such as SPHERE and those established by the Northern Syria WASH Cluster, as well as taking into account varying needs among different groups in the community, such as women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities.
Result 2) Under the second Result, PIN aims to provide Maintenance and rehabilitation of water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. WASH projects to be implemented will include interventions on water supply systems (pipeline network rehabilitation/construction, water tower/high water tank rehabilitation, pumping station rehabilitation, provision of electric equipment and generators, and rehabilitation/construction of boreholes, etc.).
The following locations will be considered for intervention: Locations with WASH infrastructure damaged by war, locations with high IDP population (at least 30 % of population are IDPs), locations with non-functioning WASH infrastructure indirectly affected by war - unstable electricity supply, lack of maintenance, lack of skilled staff for maintenance, etc., locations where non-existent or malfunctioning WASH infrastructure presents a great risk to environment and public health.
PIN is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. PIN aims to provide most needed assistance to such groups through employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct need, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border, to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of projects.

4 - Needs

The WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destructions or displacement of technical staff (HNO 2020). Both life-saving WASH emergency activities alongside rehabilitation and maintenance of pre-existing infrastructure are necessary to respond to both IDPs but also increasingly overstretched host communities. OCHA estimates that since 1 Dec, almost 350,000 people fled mainly from southern Idleb, moving further north away from the hostilities. As displacement is ongoing and IDPs are prioritizing communities to settle in, there is a need to increase the operational support to existing piped water systems to handle additional population in communities and in surrounding informal/formal settlements and camps. A partner multisectoral assessment (Jan 2020) conducted in 9 sub-districts, where displacements recently occurred, revealed that 52% of interviews confirmed shortage in provision of WASH services, while 89% confirmed that there was no WASH support provided. Based on the KIIs the 3 most urgently required interventions include cash assistance, garbage collection, and sewage repairs. In May 2019, PIN conducted WASH needs assessments in 55 villages in Aleppo and Idleb. The assessments included KIIs with the service department in the LCs and key community leaders, in addition to FGDs with the villages’ residents. During assessments, 41 % of the interviewed LCs confirmed that their villages are in need of rehabilitation and the extension of some parts of the water networks. 64 % said that the water stations in their communities require rehabilitation works, including the rehabilitation of the water wells, installation of submersible pumps and generator and construction of elevated water tanks, and 74 % referred to the need for the support costs to run the water station.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN plans to implement following activities: R1: Distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion - The current hygiene kit composition, based on the WASH Cluster guidance, includes soap, sanitary products, shampoo and dish soap amongst others, however distributions are always followed by PDM conducted by PIN’s (MEAL) team to ascertain levels of satisfaction. PIN will also undertake hygiene awareness activities and specific messages tailored to the context such as identification, prevention and treatment of diarrhea; proper handwashing procedure; potable water safety; human/solid/household waste disposal; Leishmaniosis identification and prevention; and oral hygiene. PIN will also work to improve access to humanitarian lifesaving/emergency WASH facilities and services – through the provision of emergency sanitation (construction of sanitation facilities and emergency water supply). R2: PIN aims to provide Maintenance and rehabilitation of water, sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria. The targeting criteria include vulnerable war-affected communities, where intervention can contribute to a significantly improved access to basic WASH services for the biggest number of beneficiaries. PIN will provide training/mentoring to institutions and will assist in maintenance and technical/management support in order to promote sustainability of service delivery by LA. In villages whose main need includes also funds to support the ongoing operations of the water pumps/networks, PIN will provide subsidies to the service department in order to ensure the uninterrupted operation of the rehabilitated infrastructure and stimulate fee collection in the community. Follow-up monitoring visits after completion will be conducted by PIN and respective LC/MCs to ensure they are fulfilling operations and maintenance duties and support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, which feeds into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)(MM): needs based targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; use of Washington groups Questions (WGQs) and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the LCs.; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, wide advertisements and communication channels for CFRM, 2)R: inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM: Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs 3) R:PSEA, 3)MM: Trainings of all staff; Gender balanced teams, PDM contains protection related questions, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4) R: Distribution sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: Internal distribution procedures in line with Sphere, collaboration with Security teams for timing and place of distributions, vulnerable people are fast-tracked; bypassing queues, distribution sites with sheltered waiting areas, with seating (if possible); Different queues for men/women, PIN home delivers assistance to beneficiaries with mobility/other difficulties if needed; kits are grouped to lighter boxes to facilitate easier transportation. 5)R: WASH infrastructure is not accessible to all/safe, 5)MM: lights installations, locks on the sanitation facilities, WASH facilities are designed to be safe to use for people with disabilities (platforms, ramps) pregnant women, children and elderly, separate facilities constructed for males/females, possibility for disposal of personal hygiene items (including sanitary pads), assessing accessibility of the facilities from the shelters.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation;(MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners.(A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access;(MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs;(A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas;(MM5):If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming.(A6/R6): PIN is able to retain registration in NES under the HAO, in Erbil under the KRI Administration and central Iraqi authorities, and in Turkey; (MM6): If PIN’s registration or visas in cross-border response hubs, PIN would adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal.
