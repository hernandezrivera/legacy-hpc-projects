Project Summary

According to the UNICEF education fact sheet published in 2023, 2.4 million school-age children in Syria are out of school, with 1.6 million at risk of dropping out. Budget allocations to education have declined by 78 percent from 2011 to 2022, and one in three schools is no longer used for educational purposes.
The protracted crisis is the primary factor preventing children from attending school. The devastated school infrastructure, missing facilities, a lack of teachers, and an unfavorable school environment are additional challenges. The micro and macroeconomic disruption resulting from the crisis has led to increased economic difficulties for the vulnerable families, hindering many children from attending schools. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2024 indicates a notable increase in the number of children requiring education and food support.
The education needs assessment in 2023 revealed a national average attendance rate of only 88%, with family economic conditions and child labor being significant contributors to dropout rates within 48% of families. The reasons for non-attendance also included limited learning facilities, lack of interest, or tim.
The Aga Khan Agency for Habitat (AKAH) is already taking steps through schools’ rehabilitation and safety programs to improve school infrastructure and equipping schools with safety equipment in selected governorates (Hama, and Tartous), aiming to reduce dropouts and increase enrollment. AKAH in 2024 propose to fully rehabilitate one school in Hama Governorate. Additionally, through schools’ safety campaigns will equip (training of PTA on disaster preparedness and evacuation, providing first aid kits, fire extinguisher etc.) in18 schools in Hama district Salamiah and in Aleppo 01 school will be equipped.
AKAH through its school rehabilitation and school safety programs will target 10% children with disabilities (CWDs) for inclusion of the children with special needs AKAH will conduct meetings with Parents teachers Associations, school management and conduct general awareness in the program targeted area.
AKAH will establish a robust monitoring and evaluation system will start from project planning (monthly, quarterly and annual work plans), develop LFA (logical frame work analysis) with key indicators to measure the project success, develop system for data collection, establish complaint and response mechanism to get regular feedback form the project beneficiaries.
AKAH to protect students and beneficiaries form PSEA will establish a system around three pillars 1) coordinating with school Parents Teachers Associations (PSEAs), commitment of senior managers to PSEA; 2) engagement with and support of students and other relevant stakeholders through awareness-raising and complaints response and feedback mechanisms; 3) effective prevention mechanisms, including code of conduct, AKAH PSEA policy and strict actions.
The school rehabilitation and school safety program will encourage the retention, bringing back the out-of-school children and contribute to reducing dropout rates. It will play a significant role in promoting the child's right to education, ultimately impacting the overall well-being and quality of life.
