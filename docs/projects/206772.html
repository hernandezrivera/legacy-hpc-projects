Project Summary

South Sudan has one of the world’s most under-developed infrastructures in the world. The country’s road density is the lowest in Africa with 15 km of road per 1,000 km of arable land. Building large-scale infrastructure is also challenging due to South Sudan’s increased vulnerability to devastating climate shocks. For the last three years, the country has been facing an unprecedented level of flooding which translated into the structural change of the wetlands and increasing its surface. In addition, South Sudan has extremely high socio-economic vulnerability and high numbers of displacements due to extreme weather events.
Through this project, WFP will develop critical infrastructures along main supply routes, primary dykes and community dykes in marginalized areas in Jonglei, Unity, and Upper Nile states. These areas are the most vulnerable to climate change and home to nearly 40 percent of the South Sudanese population, which has been isolated from basic needs and services (food, markets, hospitals, etc.). Other locations in Lakes or Western Equatoria States are also included in the plan due to the poor conditions of the roads and increasing humanitarian needs in the area.
Another expected result of the project is to reduce isolation and displacement for communities. By repairing key infrastructure, communities will be protected from future shocks and have continuous access to necessities. Furthermore, it paves a path for already displaced communities to return to their areas of origin, in time to cultivate and rebuild their livelihoods while contributing to long term disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation.
Improving the transportation network in conjunction with other initiatives aimed at building human capacities and labor force skills and strengthening institutions would provide the basis for a transition to economic growth. Through infrastructure projects, local youth stand better chance to get local employments thus increasing family household incomes. Lastly, in addition to facilitating efficient and cost-effective delivery of food and non-food items to various locations, improved road and river infrastructure will improve rural connectivity, promoting equity and social cohesion and ultimately contributing to peace.

1. Needs

South Sudan ranks the highest of all countries in the lack of coping capacity due to weak infrastructure and institutional capacity. It has extremely high socio-economic vulnerability and high numbers of displacements due to extreme weather events. For the last three years, the country has been facing an unprecedented level of flooding. The tributaries that flow into the Sudd/the Nile have overflowed and areas of the country not previously flooded are now underwater and there is an overall expansion in the wetlands surface. The 2022 dry season is expected to be shorter, and some lands are likely to remain water-logged, thus forcing some people into a situation of prolonged displacement until their areas of origin are accessible and lands returned to an arable nature. Additionally, the unprecedented floods have cut off access to many locations forcing humanitarian actors to resort to costly air operations to deliver supplies. Repairing damaged infrastructure will allow communities to return to their areas of origin and rebuild their livelihoods. Infrastructure repairs are critically needed to improve humanitarian access which is now severely restricted. Many stakeholders utilize the dry season (November - April) to preposition supplies prior to the heavy rains before access is further limited. Rehabilitation of roads and opening of river water ways tops the list of priorities for effective prepositioning of relief cargo as well as for emergencies requiring a timely intervention, such as disease outbreaks, rapid missions, or response scale-ups.

2. Project activities

The project will contribute to the resilience of communities by rehabilitating critical roads, repairing dyke breach points, and building dykes along main supply routes and clearing of river routes. This is not only key to ensure access to Greater Upper Nile (Unity, Upper Nile, and Jonglei states) and other locations across the country and pre-position both food and non-food items ahead of the rainy season, but also serves to prevent future flooding, contributing to longer-term disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation. Some of the main project activities will include, among others: • Repair remaining dyke breach points • Drain flood water in anticipation of the next flooding and, where needed, install culverts • Rehabilitate critical roads for the continuous provision of humanitarian assistance across the country • Open up river routes. As part of its vision, WFP seeks to address entrenched inequity and isolation, with the goal of “leaving no-one behind” and fostering unified, interconnected and peaceful communities. To do so, WFP will bring together its portfolio of programmatic tools and operational enablers to address food and nutrition insecurity in ways that create the conditions for jobs, livelihoods, markets and trade growth and eventual economic development in the marginalized areas of the country. Investing in infrastructure networks, along with education and peacebuilding dialogue, and continuing to open up local and cross-border road and river networks will connect communities to each other, to markets and trade. In 2024, WFP plans to rehabilitate more roads and beyond, targeting roads connecting airdrop locations as this has proven to be cost effective. Besides support to WFP operations, the improved infrastructure will strengthen local trade and drive food system transformation by facilitating access to markets and services.
