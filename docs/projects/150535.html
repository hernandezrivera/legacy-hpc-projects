Project Summary

The intervention aims at providing novel ways to ensure that children who entered in contact with the Israeli juvenile justice system receive tailored educational support, with the aim of ensuring access to their right to education. Main target groups are Palestinian children of East Jerusalem who are under home arrests, ex-detainees at risk of dropout/out of school and children in school at risk of drop-out. The project will support them individually or in group through skill based and curricular classes, while training teachers and parents in study skills, inclusive teaching methodologies, adolescence learning profile. Project staff will also develop a series of educational resources to be compiled in a Handbook that will be handed over to the PA MoEHE to encourage the creation of gateways for children who want to be reintegrated in schools. The project is implemented in close collaboration with MoEHE, JDoE and local CBOs through a professional educational mobile team composed by 7 teachers, a lead educator and TDH Italy Inclusive Education Expert. Within 12 months, the project aims at reaching out 200 children, 75 parents and 535 school staff living/working in the most marginalized neighborhoods of East Jerusalem.

Needs

hildren’s basic rights in East Jerusalem (EJ) are compounded by Israeli discriminatory policies, including high rates of arrest and unequal access to education and social services.Since 2016, as part of a larger program, TDH It provides educational and legal support to children in contact with the Israeli juvenile justice system through a Mobile Team of educators and volunteers and a lawyer appointed by Addameer. Educational needs are higher than what TDH It is currently able to meet, as most of identified children need support in subjects which require highly experienced teachers.According to the data collected by Addameer, in 2017 1,138 Palestinian children were arrested in EJ, amounting to 17% of total arrests for the entire Palestinian population for the same year. Given that Palestinians of EJ represent around 6% of the total Palestinian population of the oPt, it is safe to say that EJ children have become a target.Data collected in 2018 by TDH It and Addameer show that EJ children held in Israeli prisons varied from 77 in January to 50 in September. Since May 2016, TDH It Mobile Team identified 81 children (76 M and 5 F). Among them, 45 children are currently or have been under house arrest and 7 experienced both house arrest and detention in prison. Data on children under house arrest are not systematically gathered, so it is virtually impossible to get accurate figures. At the moment of writing, EJ children under house arrest are around 10. Identification is challenging for a number of reasons, among which lack of trust from families and communities and the fact that detention in prison is now preferred to house arrest, which is also usually shorter than in 2015/2016. While formally the Israeli domestic law abides by the international legal framework about child detention, there is a large gap between law and practice, and Jerusalemite children under arrest experience humiliation, intimidation and denial of basic rights. The high majority of arrested children are boys aged 14 to 17. Most affected areas are the Old City, Silwan and Al-Essawiyah neighborhoods.The effects of detention on children are extremely detrimental to their wellbeing and development: 48% of ex-child detainees visited by TDH It drop of school or substantially worsened their school performance and most of them report experiencing feelings of disorientation, anxiety and fear The policies implemented by Israel have dramatic consequences on the education system. Basic education in EJ is fragmented among different actors and drop-out rates are alarmingly high. Direct observation by TDH It shows that children who are detained are especially likely to lose interest in school and low achievers and children who lost motivation in school are more likely to be arrested and detained. Harassment and violations of the right to education cause loss of motivation, in a vicious circle which is difficult to break down, especially as the low quality of secondary education in Awqaf schools, caused by lack of teachers, movement restrictions and traditional teaching methodologies is not enabling schools to meet students’ needs.The drop-out rate of ex-detainee children is exacerbated by the absence of an official system to reintegrate students who missed schooling, nor do schools have tools to weigh the impact of the loss of school days or to evaluate any educational support received from other sources. Decisions are taken case-by-case and most families are unaware that going back to school may be an option for their children.The project aims at providing children (mainly boys aged 14 to 18 – ex detainees, under home arrests, at risk of dropout or out of school) with structured educational support; train school staff on inclusive practices; encourage the creation at MoEHE/JDoE level of a system to reintegrate children in school and value educational support received while dropped out; engage families in meeting educational needs of EJ children.

Activities

1.Group afternoon skill based/curricular classes and recreational activities, to children who dropped-out or still attend school but are at high risk of drop-outGroup classes will address children’s curricular needs and focus on the lack of motivation, including lectures/frontal classes in main school subjects and recreational activities, strengthening children’s interest in education.Group classes implemented by 7 Mobile Teachers and 1 Lead Educator mainly at Dar al Aytam school (Old City). This school faces high level of drop-out and masked drop-out (children are enrolled, but do not attend classes). Classes organized 3 times per week in the afternoon, to better complement and strengthen the regular educational offer.2.Individual educational support to out-of-school children (because of detention, house arrest, learning issues)Children identified coordinating with JDoE, Burj al Luqluq and African Community (Old City) and al Bustan (Silwan). For each child visited at home, an individual student profile will be created. Old City, Silwan and Al-Essawiyah are the areas currently most in need of support, but TDH It works in all EJ in coordination with other actors to promote complementarity of services and avoid overlapping.Individual educational support provided by the project team and tailored on each child’s needs to overcome the gap with the classmates at school and encourage reintegration into the school system.3.Creation of a handbook including outlines on basic curricular requirements and dissemination of deliverables and methodology within JDoE systemBuilding on the educational support provided through group and individual classes, the Lead Educator and the Inclusive Education Expert (IEE) will collect educational resources useful to outline basic curricular requirements. This task will be implemented in close coordination with the Supervisors Dept. at MoEHE/JDoE. Dissemination will be implemented through JDoE in selected Awqaf schools during networking and dissemination events.4.Training of teachers on study skills, individual support to out-of-school children, inclusive teaching methodologies, adolescence learning profileTrainings delivered by TDH It IEE and organized at least twice per month to offer continuous support to teachers at Dar al Aytam school and Mobile Teachers. Training focus on inclusive teaching methodologies, paying special attention to the adolescence learning profile, in order to offer better and tailored support to the children targeted by the project.5.Parent sessions on parenting adolescentsSessions (twice per month) will aim at strengthening the involvement of parents, raise their awareness on the additional educational needs of their children, introduce and promote inclusive practices at home.6.Preventing drop-out and enhancing the re-integration of out-of-school children in the regular school systemComplementing the work that TDH It is doing in EJ since 2012 in partnership with JDoE/MoEHE, the project team will support JDoE in consolidating guidelines and principles aiming at preventing drop-out, providing educational support to children under house arrest and re-integrating out of school children into school according to individual needs and learning pace, assessing the child’s educational profile and evaluating any support received through other educational systems or while out of school. Such support is in line with JDoE and will be provided thanks to the best practices identified at school and field level, which will serve as inputs to figure out how schools could prevent dropout and be ready to fairly reintegrate children. TDH It support will also cover coordination with different stakeholders to consolidate JDoE’s ownership in centralizing the response to children’s needs in EJ as well as its ability to involve as many actors as possible in support of the public Palestinian education system. Throughout the project JDoE will take over the component and its developed approach.

Indicators

All activities to be implemented within the framework of the project will build on TDH It inclusive approach that welcomes diversity, prevents discrimination and makes sure everyone is valued, listened to and protected. Quantitative indicators will be measured through attendance sheets, teachers’, lead educator’s and trainer’s register and reports, class and training outlines. Qualitative indicators will be determined through pre and post questionnaires, teachers’, lead educator’s and trainer’s reports, satisfaction questionnaires to get feedback on the quality of the educational support. A dedicated M&E officer will follow up on the M&E plan and implementation.
