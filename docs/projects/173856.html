Project Summary

Women’s Right to Education Programme is proposing to undertake a child protection programme intervention in Yola North (Yelwa and Rumde communities), Fufore (Fufore and Mayo Inne Communities) and Girei (Girei 1 and Wuro Dole Communities) LGAs of Adamawa State leveraging on its experience and presence in child protection and the culture of the people in North East Nigeria. 21,900 beneficiaries will be supported with specialized technical assistance in case management, multi-sector referrals, livelihood support, and economic reintegration support. These activities are life-saving emergency assistance to displaced persons living in host communities and IDPs camps. WREP will ensure equitable access for those without sufficient contact to appropriate services.
An effective first level of support to children and vulnerable individuals in camps and communities will be developed. 21,900 children will benefit from Psychosocial Support and mental health services provision in safe spaces for children, women and adolescents and child help desk. 10,000 individuals will utilize other community-based monitoring mechanisms to receive more specialized intervention. WREP will also establish Community-Based Child Protection Committees and train them to promote child protection in families and communities. WREP will provide vulnerable children, adolescents and GBV survivors with multi-sectorial case management. 3500 caregivers and foster parents will be supported to start a family business through a micro-enterprise grant, business mentoring and a savings scheme.
Protection, COVID-19 and GBV mainstreaming will be incorporated into every component of the programme. Training on GBV prevention, safeguarding and PSEA would be provided for all staff and field-level volunteers. Community members will receive training on COVID-19, GBV guidelines and gender sensitive processes; encouraged to provide key messages on protection from and response to exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

The conflict that continues to affect North-East Nigeria has caused the displacement of almost 1.7 million individuals in the BAY states that requires direct child protection services. Humanitarian actors especially caregivers are confronted with grave protection concerns. OCHA in 2019 stated that one in four of the IDPs are under five, and 79% are women and children. Child protection remains a serious protection concern in the North-East region of Nigeria. In 2018, the Protection Cluster reported that a considerable number of IDPs and returnees experienced rape, abductions, child and forced marriage, undesired pregnancies, sexual exploitation, physical assault, denial of resources, psychological abuse and sexual assault in the hands of the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) before flight and during captivity. Based on the HRP 2020, protection needs in some LGAs in Adamawa State remain severe, especially for vulnerable groups including female-headed households, unaccompanied or separated children, children living with disabilities and adolescents with protection concerns. According to the Nigeria Child Protection Sub Sector 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan Dashboard, 1.7 million people are in need of Child Protection Services in the BAY states which Adamawa falls under, however, only 1.269 million people are targeted for these Child Protection Services for 2021. The protection sector monthly update showed that in April 2020, 1.8 million people were in need of child protection services, however, only 1.1 million (61%) are targeted and 200,000 (18%) were reached. These means hundreds of thousands of children did not have access to child protection services as the need for protection keeps increasing due to recent increase in activities of insurgency, counter insurgency and the COVID-19 pandemic.Vulnerabilities of disadvantaged groups are further compounded by limited access to limited access to food or livelihood opportunities, and increased fear of being targeted by surrounding violence. Furthermore, the outbreak of COVID-19 has also worsened the child protection services and men, women, boys and girls including people living with disabilities are exposed to the pandemic. According to the HRP 2020, the humanitarian situation has worsened due to COVID-19 with an increase on current projections of people in need of child protection services. The Child Protection achievements from the Child Protection Sub-sector Mid-year review as of June 2020, a total of 25% beneficiaries were reached. A huge gap still stands in the intervention as there currently is limited child protection partners in Adamawa State.WREP is proposing a Child Protection programme that would strengthen and promote the engagement of community-based structures for child protection through life skills and COVID-19 education for girls and boys including those living with disabilities. We would also strengthen the capacity of child protection actors/caregivers to report on quality child protection service delivery including information and case management, family tracing, and reunification services as well as Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) in the selected LGAs of Adamawa state. WREP will build and link on existing capacity it has in child protection and education to provide effective emergency assistance and protective basic services for children in Yola North, Girei and Fufore LGAs in Adamawa State. Access to information is constrained and additional steps need to be taken in integrating CP minimum standards here, particularly in the reintegration of CAAG and SGBV survivors, which have seen limited success in Adamawa State.

2 - Activities

Intervention in Child Protection activities by WREP is specifically aiming to address the gaps in activities and coverage as identified by the Child Protection Sub-Working Group that will use an integrated model of Child Protection which include life skills and COVID-19 education, livelihoods programming and mental health services. Through this approach, the programme will combat trauma, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, stigma, and limited access to opportunity, as well as provide livelihoods and social support for impoverished caregivers and foster parents. To ensure that these activities are fully provided with quality to support the Protection sector strategic response plan to address the most urgent protection needs of our target beneficiaries in selected project communities. WREP will strengthen the already existing community based protection mechanisms with safe spaces and establish community-based protection mechanisms with safe spaces where none exists, and train lay counselors who will provide counseling services for children at the Community Help Desks (CHD) and provide PSS, promoting PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), hygiene, COVID-19 life-saving messages in local languages and be first responders in identifying new referral cases. WREP will identify and strengthen referral pathways, and train caseworkers on the most appropriate course of action so as to provide timely and holistic support for the most vulnerable cases. This will be based on individual need, but dignity kits will be available, as well as the in-house team of Clinical Psychologists, FTR (Family Tracing and Unification) officers with experience in working with foster parents. WREP is proposing a Child Protection programme that would strengthen and promote the engagement of community-based structures for child protection through life skills and COVID-19 education for girls and boys including those living with disabilities. We would also strengthen the capacity of child protection actors/caregivers to report on quality child protection service delivery including information and case management, family tracing, and reunification services as well as Mental Health and Psycho-Social Support (MHPSS) in the selected LGAs of Adamawa state. 8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

WREP will incorporate the protection principles of promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity and rights of women and men, boys and girls and marginalized groups including people and children with disabilities, and reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm.We will bear in mind the concept of equity, providing a level playing ground for people living with disabilities and minority groups bearing in mind their specific needs.Women, girls and the youths are key beneficiaries of the project, therefore they will be engaged in different dialogues at different stages of the project for their inputs. They will also be part of the implementation process. Interventions in the communities will based on their peculiar needs as identified during townhall meetings and other engagements.WREP has a working PSEA policy which describes appropriate standards of conduct, other preventive measures, reporting, monitoring, investigation and corrective measures. There are mechanisms and procedures for personnel, beneficiaries, and communities including children to report SEA allegations and comply with core standards for reporting (safety, confidentiality, transparency, and accessibility) and ensures that beneficiaries are aware of this. The organization’s PSEA policy has assistance and referrals that ensures survivors of SEA including children receive immediate professional assistance referring them to qualified service providers, while the investigation procedures provide evidence that it has appropriately dealt with past SEA allegations if any, through investigation and corrective actions. Awareness creation and sensitization of target groups using our PSEA awareness raising and action plan including reporting mechanisms.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In ensuring feedback on the project, key beneficiaries who will be part of the implementation process will be engaged in different group discussions, community meetings, interviews and phone calls at different stages of the project for feedback. Apart from these categories of people, we will create an environment of trust and flexibility with the larger communities and beneficiaries to give them the opportunity for critical feedback as well as raising points that are critical to them. Focus will be on creating accountability mechanisms which will add value to the project. In receiving feedback, safeguarding measures will be put in place, for example getting consent before collecting feedback from vulnerable groups. WREP’s Monitoring and Evaluation team will constantly monitor feedback.
