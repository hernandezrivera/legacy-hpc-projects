Project Summary

The project aims at securing sufficient and safe drinking water quantities for 2822 households (14,110 individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “C” and “B” of the West Bank for household purposes, including 6,905 women and girls, in addition it aims at improving WaSH conditions in 10 public schools serving 3373 students (2270 males and 1105 females) also improving sanitation conditions for 39 households living in area “C” and "B", and increase hygiene awareness through trainings and workshops for 360 persons (180 females and 180 males) in 3 localities as well as the provision of 150 household hygiene kits for the benefit of 125 households in area “C” and "B". in total, this project serves more than 17597 inhabitants (8975 males and 8622 females) 280 persons out of them are with disabilities.
The initiative responds to needs highlighted in the 2021 HNO and it is also fully in line with the oPt. Response plan, contributing to the WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians communities. Access to safe WASH service is a main concern in the West Bank, specifically In Area C, where around 105,000 people receive less than 50 liters of water per capita per day, while over 50,000 people are unconnected to a water network and over 402,000 people are unconnected to a wastewater network.
The initiative has been endorsed by PWA, it is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and in compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.
Further, the initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of communities and educational institutions in need, increasing water access and availability for unconnected and underserved communities in area “C” and “B” of the West Bank as well as improving sanitation conditions, precisely in Nuba, Adh Dahiriya, Kurza, Idhna, Deir Samit, Beit ar Rush al Fuqa and As Samu in Hebron Gov., Ein Ayoub Bedouins, Ein Jaryout, Ein Qiniya Bedouins and Kobar Bedouins in Ramallah Gov., Iraq al Sakhra, Muhaajir, Wadi Qana, Kifl Haris and Salfit City in Salfit Gov. and in Arab Al Khouli in Qalqilya Governorate.

1 - Needs

Reference to the 2021 HNO, around159,000 people across the West Bank reside in areas that are either not connected to piped water services or are very poorly supplied, With the spread of the COVID-19 since the March 2020, both communities and service providers have been put in a difficult position considering their limited means to obtain necessary water and hygiene material to practice and enforce preventive measures. Recently, in August 2020 PHG has participated in the WaSH vulnerability mapping assessment of 850 communities in the West Bank which was conducted by WaSH Cluster in cooperation with Cluster partners, PWA, OCHA, and PCBS also PHG and ACPP have conducted a thorough needs assessment in 2020 in West Ramallah communities. The assessment covered 30 vulnerable communities in addition to 2019 need assessment in Salfit area jointly with Asamblea de Cooperación por la Paz (ACPP), the Agricultural Development Association (PARC) and the Psychosocial Counselling Center for Women (PSCCW). It was based on; field visits, interviews, focus groups and questionnaires with potential beneficiaries including Bedouin communities, local authorities and community-based organizations including women CBOs. The assessment was completed in coordination with the PWA and WaSH cluster. Around 100 women and girls have been included and interviewed within these assessments. The targeted communities have been selected considering people highly affected by water access (under served and/or un served communities) and affected by forcible transfer. They are those more affected by the impact of drought events, increase of water costs during the dry period and lack of access to water. The targeted households are those who suffer from water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. In addition, part of the proposed humanitarian needs have been inspired and modeled based on previous studies and water master plans such as water master plan for Area C (2017-2035) completed by ACF and GVC in full coordination with PWA. Therefore, it was clear from recent WaSH vulnerability mapping assessment and PHG assessments carried with the local authorities and community based organizations including women CBO’s that approximately 17597 inhabitants (8975 males and 8622 females) are in need of the below humanitarian needs in the West Bank, Below are some of the assessment highlights: - Around 39 Bedouins households in Ramallah, Salfit and Qalqilia do not have their own latrine and depends on open defecation and shared latrines. Proposed activity is to supply and install prefab latrines and to provide dignity hygiene kits for households. - 10 schools in Hebron and Salfit area are in need of rehabilitation and installation of new sanitary units to improve WaSH conditions in schools by rehabilitating existing sanitary units and building new units. - unconnected and underserved targeted communities in Hebron area are buying water tankers from the surrounding water filling points and the available water networks are in need of rehabilitation. On average water cost is as high as 20NIS/m3 and they are using roof tanks, cisterns and on-ground containers to store water for daily use. This practice exposes the water to contamination moreover water consumption is less than 50 liters per capita per day and sometimes less than 30 liters. Proposed activity is installation and rehabilitation of domestic water networks and pipelines with hygiene promotion awareness campaigns. - A number Bedouins and herders’ communities in Ramallah and Qalqilia are not connected to water networks. They are mainly dependent on mobile water tankers to fulfill their needs of water for domestic uses and for livestock. The average water price by water tankering is more than 30 NIS/m3, water tankers are filled from the sounding filling points existing in the neighboring localities. Proposed activity is installation of water networks and pipelines to connect these localities with water to improve water availability and reduce the high cost. All interventions and activities will target communities either fully or partially located in area C and B, while excluding others with less pronounced protection concerns. The targeted households suffer from continuous marginalization, water shortage and cutoff. Many are not connected to a water network. In some locations people are using primary plastic pipes to deliver water to houses. It is worthwhile noting that some interventions are in area B. However, their main aim is to serve communities totally or partially located in Area “C”. This is particularly valid for the water networks installation and rehabilitation.

2 - Activities

All project activities and interventions have been reviewed, evaluated and coordinated with the below parties: - WaSH cluster and other NGOs active in the geographic area – PWA, WBWD and MoHE - Local water providers (regarding water distribution activity) As to water distribution, PHG agreed to use same procedures and mechanism in line with the PWA/ WaSH cluster modality, in case of any activities overlapping at community level PHG is committed to coordinate this issue over families level and beneficiaries with other organization in field. Major activities or outputs of the project are: O1: access to safe drinking and domestic water services is improved. A1.1 To rehabilitate/ extent and install 16 km of different sizes Water Supply Pipelines to connect 2.5km Deir Abu Masha’al Herders in Ramallah with water from Deir Abu Masha’al village, 1.5km Arab Al Khouli/Wadi Kana in Qalqilia, 3km Nuba, 5km Adh Dhahiriya and 4km Idhna in Hebron for the benefit of 2822 households (6905 female and 7198 Male). O2: access to WASH services (Schools, HCFs, QCs) is improved. A2.1 The construction/ rehabilitation of WaSH facilities in schools (handwashing, latrines, sewer system) in 10 public schools, 8 schools in Hebron and 2 schools in Salfit for the benefit of 3373 students (1105 female and 2268 Male) O3: access to sanitation, solid waste, and hygiene services is improved. A3.1 provision of 39 household latrines distributed as follows: 7 Ein Ayoub Bedouins, 4 Ein Jaryout, 2 Ein Qiniya Bedouins, 7 Kobar Bedouins in Ramallah, 6 Arab Al Khouli in Qalqilia, 2 Iraq al Sakhra, 5 Muhaajir, 6 Wadi Qana in Salfit for the benefit of 39 households (91 female and 93 Male). A3.2 The provision of 150 household hygiene kit for the benefit of 122 households in area “C” and 3 households in area "B" distributed as follows: 25 Ein Ayoub Bedouins, 15 Ein Jaryout, 20 Ein Qiniya Bedouins, 15 Kobar Bedouins, 10 Deir Abu Masha’al Herders, 15 Arab Al Khouli, 10 Iraq al Sakhra, 25 Muhaajir, 15 Wadi Qana. A3.3 Conducting 3 hygiene promotion awareness campaigns in Idhna, Adh Dhahiriya and Nuba for the benefit of 360 persons (50% females) in WASH awareness and promotion campaigns to cover all topics related to COVID-19, water handling, water management and saving in addition to training about facilities operation and maintenance as well as to include hygiene promotion topics of the above mentioned interventions, such WASH awareness will enhance water right of marginalized people protected and focused on gender equality and women ’s empowerment in household water management and water sanitation. The other household members (children, youth, men, disabled persons, and elders) have improved their water consumption and sanitation practices due to the increased amount of available water at their houses. PHG is committed to consult women in each targeted household on operational details (e.g. precise location of storage tanks), considering that they will be the primary beneficiaries of interventions. Female PHG staff will be part of the implementation team enabling an open environment for female beneficiaries to voice their concerns. In general women and girls will be affected positively after improving the WASH conditions and facilities on the household level and this will be reflected on reducing daily chores, more time allocated to girls’ education, and improved feminine hygiene. Appropriate WASH facilities should also result in reduced conflict in the household, improving quality of life, and significant decline in health problems (especially for children), time and money saving by the family allowing for greater focus on children's education. Women are more vulnerable to lack of access to water. In the same time inadequate availability of water, collecting water from unprotected sources will expose women, children, and old people in particular to high risks of waterborne diseases and other illnesses. Thus, the project will contribute to secure more clean water and will give more weights to female headed households in beneficiary selection process.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes.PHG and ACPP will secure with community representatives and local authorities safety and protection methods and measures, especially at a time of the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic at the community and school level by adoption of restricted health protocols while providing service, working in the field and during meetings and awareness campaigns including social distancing and prevention tools to reduce The spread of COVID-19.PHG, ACPP and community representatives will conduct a thorough risk analysis for proposed activities in each targeted location especially in area “C” to identify potential risks and propose mitigation measures, including: - Community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. - Community location related risks: to assess the community surrounding risks, like the existence of settlements, military base, firing zones and bypass roads, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will be applicable and operational in such context, not exposing beneficiaries to further risks. - Relations and sensitivity inside and outside the community: to assess the different cultural and social relations inside the community and in its surrounding, to ensure that the proposed interventions will not increase the social sensitivity or tension between the community, households, and with the surrounding communities. - Type of activity: to assess the community capacities and needs to follow up on activities such as the communities’ ability to operate and maintain the water networks which can contribute to the sustainability of the delivered assistance. In addition to doing no harm, the participatory approach contributes to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. In order to further inform its assessments and implementation, PHG continuously works on understanding gender roles and household dynamics. By doing so, PHG believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. PHG has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All PHG staff have the obligation to adhere to the organization code of conduct. PHG will ensure that its activities, especially connecting communities with the pipe water service, will not cause overcrowding of the limited water sources and will not cause competition between the communities regarding water supplies, through the coordination with PWA and water service providers. Moreover, and in cooperation with community representatives, PHG will ensure that the project fully covers the water and sanitation needs of communities and households (each household has the right to have 1 latrine and water connection), avoiding more tensions between the community families and other surrounding communities. Safety of the students of targeted schools will be improved, through increased adequate access to water and sanitation facilities.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The approach of implementation will starts by formulating a project committee that consists of 5-7 person , which will include representatives from the community , women, municipality, local committees, the role of the committee is to assist in collecting contribution if any (local members) , also will facilitate work of the project staff in the field particularly to solve any conflict if raised through implementation period , the women whom representing CBOs and community will be active and expected to contribute equally in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project side by side with the committee members, they will be given a significant role in implementation from the beginning . During the awareness sessions PHG team will ensure that health and hygiene will not be considered as a “women issues”. In this way, men and boys will be encouraged to be actually engaged in their promotion, thus contributing to the improvement of the health and hygiene in their households and communities. The awareness sessions will promote equal knowledge and participation between female and male of good hygiene practices and WASH management in the household. protection approach will be followed throughout the whole implementation phase to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level. PHG staff and project committee role throughout the whole project will that that of guaranteeing compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially for the most vulnerable groups (female farmers, female-headed households, households with people with disabilities). Beneficiaries will be consulted from the project onset regarding their preferred type of assistance and will be invited to participate in fully empowered committees. Awareness raising: in order to maximize the project’s impact, beneficiaries will be informed and sensitized by PHG staff and project committee on the prevention of major health related risks and best hygiene practice
