Project Summary

There remains an acute, unmet need for responsive qualitative R&A that directly supports humanitarian partners’ ability to efficiently advocate for, develop, and implement targeted programming in Syria. Under current conditions, effective programing requires critical decisions, and therefore deep understanding, of issues related to emergency aid, early recovery, stabilization, and durable solutions. However, feedback from partners demonstrates they continue to struggle to efficiently navigate the multitude of dynamic challenges. To be able to effectively provide vital assistance to millions of Syrians in need, partners and donors require a responsive mechanism for tasking and receiving high-quality qualitative R&A.
RCCM aims to meet this need by creating timely relevant R&A to support programming that improves immediate conditions for Syrians and is also conducive to supporting future prospects for recovery and durable solutions. Over the past eight years, iMMAP has developed a nuanced understanding of the problems, needs, and risks faced by humanitarian partners operating in a prolonged conflict with an array of complexities.
In contrast to most reporting in the humanitarian sector in Syria, RCCM will rely on qualitative primary and secondary data collection tools. Primary data collection tools, employed by highly experience and knowledgeable core RCCM team members and area and technical expert STTAs, will include KIIs with local and international stakeholders relevant to the report topic and semi-structured reports based on discussions with key stakeholders and STTA knowledge. Secondary data collection tools will include review of open-source data, including local and international media reports, quantitative reporting produced by operational partners in Syria, and social media. Tools will be employed to ensure robust triangulation.
RCCM is designed to respond to a growing gap in qualitative, responsive reporting and analysis for humanitarian actors, one which has been repeatedly communicated to iMMAP by partners during RCM, the previous iteration of this project. RCCM is not meant to duplicate ongoing quantitative data collection and reporting efforts carried out by others, but to compliment them. Quantitative data collection is often limited in the type of subjects, information and analysis. RCCM is designed, from teaming to deliverables, to fill these gaps. iMMAP has conducted several discussions with partner organizations in Syria on how to successfully pursue a complimentary, coordinated approach.
RCM produced a number of detailed thematic reports that were released just prior to significant discussion on the focus topics, including reports on potential Turkish incursion scenarios in late 2021 and social support systems across zones of control in early 2022. RCM also produced area profiles that provided an updated and thorough look at several focus areas, namely Quamishli and Deir-ez-Zor, and a response report on the ISIS attack on the Ghweiran prison. Repeated surveys show that RCM’s audience overwhelmingly use these reports for their improved understanding of the Syrian context. Over 40% says it used the reports for decision making and planning activities.
Potential report topic examples include: 1) Thematic Reports: Current Conflict Assessment: Overview of Frontlines and Potential Flashpoints Across Syria; GoS-Self Administration Talks: Prospects and Impact of a Bilateral Political Agreement; SSG-SIG/Turkey Relations: Future Governance Scenarios for NWS; Crossline and Cross border Trade in Syria; A Divided Education System: Reviewing Curriculums Across Syria, and 2) Response Report Topics: Expectations and Potential Impacts of the 2022 Wheat Harvest; Status of Kurdish-Kurdish Talks in NES; Responses and Impact to the Mulham Campaign in NWS; Overview and Outlook of GoS Reconciliation Drive in Eastern Syria; Implications of the Revised Social Contract in NES; Status of the Justice System in NES.

4 - Needs

There is an acute, unmet need for responsive qualitative research and analysis that directly supports humanitarian partners’ ability to efficiently advocate for, develop, and implement effective, targeted programming in Syria. With the deteriorating Syrian economy, economic drivers of displacement have been on the rise and fears of further conflict-related violence and displacement, conscription, and abuse have increased. Syrians across the country continue to lack safe, equal, and adequate access to basic services, including power, water, education, health, housing, land, and property (HLP) rights, and civil documentation. Effective programing requires critical decisions, and therefore deep understanding, of issues related to first-line emergency aid, early recovery, stabilization, and durable solutions. However, feedback from humanitarian partners demonstrates they continue to struggle to efficiently navigate the multitude of dynamic challenges. There is a need to add significant value to the overall humanitarian response. Humanitarian programming needs research to reliably addresses issues in a timely manner.

5 - Implementation of activities

RCCM aims to meet this need by creating and mobilizing timely relevant research and analysis to support programming that both improves immediate conditions for Syrians and is also conducive to supporting future prospects for recovery and durable solutions. IMMAP has developed RCCM around three key principles: outreach, adaptability, and timing and all deliverables will provide and facilitate responsive qualitative research and analysis with real world, practical relevance and programmatic applicability for humanitarian partners in Syria, as well as engagement with the humanitarian community to ensure that the information is consumed and mobilized to positively impact decision-making. Topic selection will occur through regular engagement with humanitarian implementors, working groups, and clusters across the four zones of control in Syria and in coordination with donors, and will be examined through two reporting formats: - Response Reports (5-10 Pages, 3-week turnaround) are designed to respond quickly and directly to humanitarian partner needs, and provide qualitative research and analysis on emerging topics. - Thematic Reports (15 to 30 pages, 6 to 8-week turnaround) allow for in-depth examination of key issues and broader topics with detailed qualitative research and analysis. New is that instead of presentations, RCCM will use its reports to facilitate targeted discussions on key topics aimed at generating productive exchange of information with humanitarian actors

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The risk of sexual exploitation and abuse is internally only, as iMMAP does not directly implement humanitarian activities. This risk is mitigated with an internal Global PSEA policy dating from December 2020:a) iMMAP commits to ensure the respect among its personnel and its programs of IASC Six Core Principles relating to SEA and to the Secretary General’s Bulletin on Special Measures for protection from sexual exploitation and abuses (ST/SGB/2003/13).b) iMMAP staff, implementing partners/sub-contractors as well as beneficiaries/hosting communities have the right and obligation to report any claims and suspicions related to sexual misconduct by iMMAP personnel or sub-contractors/implementing partners/staff through the iMMAP Whistle Blower and Complaint Mechanism Policy.c) In both HQ and Field, iMMAP management is responsible for creating and maintaining an environment that prevents sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse. Management shall take appropriate measures for this purpose, including awareness training regarding iMMAP’s Safeguarding and Anti-Discrimination Policy package.d) The violation of Policy principles is considered as gross misconduct and is ground for disciplinary measures, including the possibility of termination of contract.e) iMMAP commits to ensure necessary dissemination of this policy takes place in all field missions and take all necessary measures should any breach of this policy be reported.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the context in the Whole of Syria there is always a risk of a sudden change in the humanitarian and security situation. In addition, the persistent drought in NES forms a risk to the availability of water and food production. This frequently results in groups of IDP's and refugees, who often find themselves in precarious situations with their basic humanitarian needs under threat. There is a risk that due to a lack of solid information, humanitarian programming does not meet the needs of IDP’s and refugees and leaves key vulnerabilities unaddressed or even exacerbated. The risk of new emergencies means that these complex dynamics can change overnight. iMMAP is able to respond quickly and directly to humanitarian partner needs. Response reports provide qualitative research and analysis on emerging topics relevant to ongoing and/or future programming. These reports are developed based on engagement with humanitarian partners who expressed their need for nuanced, qualitative research on diverse topics within a limited timeframe. With topics proposed and select through coordination with humanitarian partners and donors, response reports are expected to encompass rapid area profiles, rapid sector or issue profiles, and requests for information, the latter covering urgent information requests from donors and humanitarian partners around developing issues or incidents impacting the humanitarian response.
