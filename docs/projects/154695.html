Project Summary

The project is focused on increasing access to basic water and sanitation services as per their equity, quality, quantity and continuity for the most vulnerable people in returnee and host communities (OCHA, SO1; WASH Cluster O2, indicators 1,2,3). The project is based on punctual hardware inputs (rehabilitation of poorly functional water and sanitation infrastructures) and software inputs (create sustainability for WASH facilities’ operators at local level and decentralized level) aimed to smooth the transition phase from emergency rehabilitation/repairs to more structured interventions. The project includes also WASH components for health facilites as per PHC, provincial and general hospitals. Geographically, the project will be implemented in the following governorates:: Ninewa (62%), Anbar (13%), Salahaddin (12%), Kirkuk (8%), Diyala (5%). The OCHA SO3 is included as WASH Cluster O3 as per pre-positioning emergency stock for man-made emergencies and public health emergencies, to be deployed by trained staff within 24-36 hours all over Iraq.

Needs

The multinational military operations in Iraq against the Islamic State is at its end but the estimated number of people in need of WASH services all over the country is above 2.3 M person. On November 2018, Oxfam conducted a baseline survey on WASH access and livelihood in Anbar, SAD and Ninewa (22 communities in 13 sub-districts). With regards to households’ status, 10% were IDPs and 2% were from remainees communities. A high number of survey respondents i.e. 87% were returnees; 95% of returnees were displaced for more than 6 months and 5% were displaced between 3 to 6 months. Comparison of income after and before the conflict indicated that conflict has significantly damaged the livelihoods of people. For example, before the conflict, 20% were associated with private work which is declined by 8% and currently only 3% were working on farming as compare to 7% before the conflict. Similar decline was observed in income sources related to livestock as well. Baseline survey respondents were asked to share their average monthly income and expenditures. An average monthly income of survey respondent was 346,650.00 IQD and average monthly expenditures were 544,260,00 IQD. A very high income and expenditure gap of 230,310.00 was noted. Respondents were also asked if their monthly income was enough to meet their daily basic needs. A very high percentage of survey respondents i.e. 87% reported that earned income was not enough to meet their daily basic needs. Access to water as per quantity and quality continues to remain a signiﬁcant humanitarian concern in all the re-taken areas. By the Oxfam survey, 80% of the population reported that water quality is bad (taste, turbidity, salinity) and only 35% reported to buy bottled water for drinking purpose. The water supply (piped water) is working trice/week only for the 61% of the respondents and 23% of the population sample receive piped water twice/week or without any schedule. 44% also reported that they spend between 3000.00 IQD to 30,000.00 IQD/month to get access to any water source regardless its quality. The Directorate of Water is strongly involved in rehabilitation works on the existing water facilities but it still needs a consistent and prompt support by the humanitarian actors in terms ofhardware inputs (water assets and consumables) and software (management capacity of centralized and decentralized systems, water tariff and water revenue management). Electricity cuts remain regular in most parts of the country, and shortage of fuel hampers consistent pumping of water and its treatment. Damages and leakages (up to 75%) in the water mains drastically undermine the water quality reaching the affected population due to cross contamination. Acute diarrhoeal diseases, including cholera, pose a very serious threat to the health of IDPs, returnees as well as the host community. The recent history (2003- 2017) of water borne diseases in Iraq (AWD and cholera) indicates how limited is the safe water and sanitation the affected population have access to. Also, the health services in the country are overwhelmed, with delivery of health care services conducted in poor null hygienic environments which lacks of functioning water, sanitation and waste infrastructures. Medical waste is not managed posing in serious health risk all the operators and population which may get in contact with it from the source to the ﬁnal uncontrolled disposal. Waste water treatment systems cover only 6% of the Iraq population which mainly rely on cesspool which are emptied by the private sector and disposed in uncontrolled sites. The camp population is decreased during 2018 but still about 600,000 IDPs will stay in camps during 2019. Cholera prevention and control is not an issue to be dealt with by the health sector alone: water, sanitation, health and hygiene promotion, mass communication/community mobilization, and camp management andcamp administration are also the important actors in the prevention and control of cholera while meantime reliable WASH services are provided to the IDPs. A rapid responsecapacity needs to be also guarantee to new possible displacement which are not directly linked with the ISIS conﬂict. The capacity of government actors in water, waste water and solid waste management is still limited at central and local level. This is in terms of skills of staff and aged assets/equipment. WASH services billing and revenue collection and allocation are still lacking in policies and regulation. Strong support is needed to particularly focusing on local government service provisions, designing and planning interventions, including capacity building, cost effective, innovative and sustainable strategies. These must prioritize users’ privacy, dignity and security, access for people with special needs/disability and environment health (source of data: assessments conducted by Oxfam and Directorate of Water (in Ninewa, Salahaddin, Kirkuk, Anbar and Diyala) on 2018.

Activities

Activity 1 (SO1, WCO1): Within the targeted areas, support IDPs population in camps in order to provide reliable and dignified water and sanitation services without risk of exclusion of age groups, vulnerable groups, minorities and people with special needs. O&M and monitoring on daily base of WASH (water, sanitation, solid waste) services will be coupled with mass hygiene promotion campaigns aimed to improve hygiene practices, living standards related to the use of the resources provided and HH and camp environment. Activity 2 (SO1, WCO2): Support centralized and decentralized water and sanitation services in returnee areas through the rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities. This will include all the activities aimed to maintain quality, quantity and continuity of the water supply chain from the source to the community (pumping stations, treatment plants, water mains, chemicals). Sanitation intervention (repairs of drainage and sewerage facilities and mains) will be focused on urban areas and education and health infrastructures in urban and rural areas both. Hygiene promotion activities will be integral part of this activity which will include also strong component of domestic and environment hygiene behavioursActivity 3 (SO1, WCO2): Support local and central authorities in the management of the WASH services in order to strength/create capacity on daily O&M of the facilities as well as to define/enforce water and sanitation policies, water metering and billing, water revenue allocations, waste water and solid waste regulation from the source to the final safe disposal. This activity will include plants and services providers at community level as well as policy makers at central and governorate level. Private- public partnerships as well as Build-Operate-Transfer approaches (BOT) will be investigated as per feasibility and piloted. This activity will strength accountability for the services providers (public or private) as well as accountability to the users without distinction of minorities, vulnerable groups, people with special needs (resilience capacity building). Activity 4 (SO3, WCO3): Emergency WASH stock will be procured and prepositioned in strategic areas where localized displacement are likely to happen due to present and future localized conflicts. Outbreak contingency stock will be procured and pre-positioned in hot spots where past records of AWD and water borne diseases have been recorded. This activity is for camp and off camp population. Items will include emergency water distribution points, water treatment chemicals (for water plants and HHs), hygiene and dignity kits, framework agreements for water trucking, desludging services and solid waste collection. Environmental kits (largely adopted by Oxfam since 2016) will be ready for health facilities and schools: these include cleaning equipment and chemicals for environment disinfection, portable incinerators, medical waste equipment and autoclaves for health facilities. Portable toilets and showers will be ready in stock for camp and off camps urgent needs. Hygiene promotion (key messages dissemination and activities are integral part of the emergency response).Activity 5 (SO1, WCO4): More sustainable and durable WASH services will be promoted within the already existing national process of decentralization of services as defined on the Law of Governorates (2005-2008) and Provincial Powers Act (2010). The decentralization process includes water supply, sewerage services, waste management. Moreover, the central government recognized the urgent need for private sector development throughout the entire economy. One way to increase the involvement of the private sector is through Public‐Private‐Partnership (PPP) in infrastructure development given the key role adequate infrastructure can play in improving the business environment, economic growth and stability. Oxfam will support the decentralization process by strengthening local capacity in water governance as well as implementing integrated WASH pilot projects at community (rural) and neighbourhood levels (urban) with the inclusion of all services the community has the right to access in a dignified manner. This will include also the involvement of research centres and universities (e.g. Mosul University with the programme “Community Sustainable Health Outreach Program – CSHOP, the National Solid Waste Management Plan NSWMP) to develop or adopt innovative technologies and approaches and private-public partnerships in solid waste reduction, reuse and recycling and waste water reuse. It is necessary to underline that these activities will involve the formal and informal sector in order to identify and involve (micro-economy, micro-finance) GBV at risk population, elderly, youth and women association as integral part of the civil society’s architecture.

Protection Risk Analysis

General protection risks and context-specific protection risks are taking into consideration in terms of safety and dignity, access, data protection, market impacts, people with specific needs and risks, social relations, fraud and diversion, expectation risk management. The pillars Oxfam projects are based are inclusion of all population to have access to reliable and dignified WASH services which are not only focused to meet the daily needs but at longer term to create a sustainable safe living environment. General protection risks identified are:1. The proposed programme covers IDPs, returnees and vulnerable retmainee communities residing in and out of camps sittings, some areas are concentrated with multi-ethic multi-sectarian communities. There is a risk of tensions between target communities2. Areas with political and high security level might put staff members at risk of being caught in unexcepted clashes, or block access to beneficiaries 3. Engaging in Protection issues my put Oxfam and partner staff at risk 4. Resentment from segments of the community towards families with affiliation to extremist groups may put the target groups at risk of further violations and place Oxfam staff at risk. Oxfam will ensure that its activities do not increase or add to these risks5. Authorities requesting Oxfam staff to share beneficiary data with them Specific WASH key risk identified applicable to Oxfam projects are: exclusion of vulnerable groups (ethnic, minorities, gender) from access to equitable WASH services; expectations from users and communities to achieve durable and reliable services in short time which could create frustration and frictions between service providers and users; protection risks associated to environment degradation (living conditions, environment contamination, water resources depleting) which turns into a public health concern for population with limited financial means to access safe water and basic sanitation facilities and services; risk of accountability and transparency in managing WASH services (affordable pricing and billing policies) and related revenues (allocation, investment plan, equity of salary policies).

Mitigation Measures

General mitigation measures:1. Oxfam will ensure to engage with the communities in the target areas in consultations, the core programme design will be based on social cohesion, will ensure appliance of this approach from the initial stage of implementation to work closely in transparency with the target communities. Will take into consideration the contextual nature specific to the areas of intervention, will have female staff to work with women groups and follow a risk matrix plan throughout the project cycle.2. Oxfam will undertake a though risk assessment to the areas of intervention prior to access. Will negotiating access to civilians with the local authorities, and will ensure to prioritise the safety and security of Oxfam and partner staff and the target communities3. Oxfam will ensure that staff are trained on Safe Programming, incl. conflict sensitivity, Protection principles of do no harm, safety and timely access, accountability, community engagement and empowerment, and to ensure that all activities adhere to the latter to avoid inadvertent harm caused to the communities and Oxfam and partner staff. 4. Oxfam will ensure that all its activities do not increase or add to these risks will work closely with the humanitarian community to promote a safe and dignifying approach towards stigmatised or excluded community groups. 5. Oxfam follows humanitarian principles and acts as part of the humanitarian protection response in Iraq, will continue to reinforce the its stance to local authorities, and explain the principles of confidentially we adhere to. 6. Oxfam Iraq has an established security department, that reviews environmental and hazardous risks, as well as security situation of areas of intervention prior to issuing clearance for access. Specific WASH mitigation measures:Exclusion of vulnerable groups: identification of vulnerable groups and their access to equitable and reliable WASH services by a) community/neighbourhood profiling (ethnicity, gender, people with special needs), b) social profiling, c) services/facility mapping, d) services the population have access vs vulnerable groups having access to the same services, d) identification of special needs and support (financial, assets, consumables, medical, education etc). Communication and early identification of exclusion issues is addressed and managed in a way to maintain data protection, privacy and dignity. Community expectations: expectation of community will be grouped between two categories as per standard approach: tangible expectations (e.g. municipal services), and intangible expectations, (voices and opinions on from community will be heard, acted upon by decision making authorities). A community expectation survey is conducted (and follow ups) at the beginning, during the projects duration and after (impact assessment and sustainability). Environment degradation: The basis of WASH sustainability involves the maintenance of a desired flow of benefits to a particular group or place indefinitely. This involves the maintenance of benefits to the current users, without diminishing the benefits to future users. To cope up with the environment degradation, the projects promote optimum use and management of resources, sustainable development, adoption of green concept and above all community participation in all developmental activities. This includes traditional and innovative mitigation approaches. Risk of accountability and transparency in managing WASH services: Oxfam supports policy makers in the services decentralization process. This includes water governance policies, its architecture and reliability. The water revenues and their allocation are based on population needs, basic equal share, poverty, land area (IBP approach) by using research, reporting and advocacy participation tools designed to support understanding of selected good practice benchmarks for WASH transparency and accountability.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability to beneficiaries is integral component of the projects activities to achieve strategic and clusters objectives as per standard IASC methodology Operational Framework - Accountability to Affected Population – AAP(https://interagencystandingcommittee.org/system/files/legacy_files/AAP%20Operational%20Framework%20Final%20Revision.pdf). The steps are:a) System wide learning and establishing means of mainstreaming and verification (throughout all phases of the program cycle)b) Systematically communicate with affected populations using relevant feedback and communication mechanisms (throughout all phases of the program cycle)c) Ensure that accountability to affected populations is effectively integrated within systems for planning needs assessment and response (before assessment and/or during contingency planning)d) Ensure that accountability to affected populations is effectively integrated within needs assessment methodology, including joint needs assessments (during assessment)e) Ensure that accountability to affected populations is effectively integrated withinsystems for project design and planning (during project design and/or response planning)f) Ensure that accountability to affected populations is effectively integrated throughout the implementation of projects (during project implementation).Accountability to beneficiaries will strongly emphasize accountability for gender equality on both programmatically and institutionally, institutional accountability through a Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Sector-wide Action Plan for Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women (UN-WOMEN). This be focus on: • To identify entry points for gender mainstreaming in the water, sanitation and hygiene sector byhighlighting the intrinsic linkages between gender equality and water, sanitation and hygiene aswell as to highlight good practices and lessons learned;• To strengthen accountability for gender equality and the empowerment of women in the water,sanitation and hygiene public sectors through the introduction and validation of an institutionalaccountability framework;• To enhance women’s leadership, voice and agency in the governance of water and sanitation;• Leveraging water as an asset for increasing women’s employment and access to economic opportunities;• To ensure policy coherence and integration
