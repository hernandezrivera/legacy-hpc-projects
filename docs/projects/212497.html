Project Summary

Needs: In 2024, there are 16.7 million people in Syria requiring humanitarian aid, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of these, 5.5 million are displaced, with over 2 million living in precarious conditions. The situation worsened due to earthquakes in February 2023, which uprooted hundreds of thousands, caused nearly 5,900 deaths, and over 12,800 injuries. The earthquakes further damaged already weakened infrastructure and left many families without their main breadwinner, exacerbating an already dire economic crisis and increasing the vulnerability of millions.
Interventions: Oxfam, drawing on its experience in Syria since 2013, continues to collaborate with local authorities and communities to address water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) needs in vulnerable populations across several districts and subdistricts, including Aleppo, Hama, Idlib, Latakia, Rural Damascus, Dar’a, and Deir Ezzor. This involves assessing needs, prioritizing based on severity, and adapting to emerging needs. Through integrated assessments conducted from January 2022 onwards, covering approximately 50 communities, Oxfam consulted with local and international NGOs, utilized secondary data, and considered the Humanitarian Needs Overview. The targeted areas face significant WASH challenges, categorized under severity scales 3, 4, and 5. In 2024, the project will emphasize life-saving WASH interventions, bolstering existing systems, such as water supply and sewage networks, wastewater treatment, and solid waste management. Additionally, it will integrate public health and hygiene promotion activities, including providing hygiene NFIs. Gender sensitivity is prioritized throughout the project, with a response strategy guided by community engagement and safe programming approaches, tailored to access and security concerns.
Protection Risk Analysis
Risk: Due to access restrictions& limited funds, Oxfam cannot always adopt a blanket distribution, and at the same time is unable to directly identify beneficiaries eligible to NFI in alignment with its vulnerability/selection criteria, which generates a risk of selection bias, inclusion/exclusion errors and community perceptions of unfair eligibility criteria or discriminatory practices. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High MM: community-based targeting and selection; Risk-informed selection criteria; multi-layer verification of the list of selected beneficiaries; Programme data is disaggregated per gender, disability, and status i.e., displaced, returnee, host, so red flags are identified if any. Oxfam, in 2024, will also review its conflict-sensitive framework to ensure building on the connectors and not exacerbating dividers throughout the project cycle. Risk: limited options of sites for distributions & bureaucratic/admin impediments that further condense the timeframe for implementation, could lead to compromising on essential safe& inclusive requirements e.g., access ramps for PWD or elderly, proximate distance from ben. residences, limited number of beneficiaries approaching the site per day (crowded setting can increase the risk of GBV and tensions). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High MM: operational CFM, planning more in advance with considering external barriers, consider CVA modalities to improve dignified outcomes. 0 - Project Summary (extended)

PSEA strategy: Women and girls, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups, face safeguarding risks such as sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse, including VAW and GBV. Changes in home dynamics and gender norms may escalate tensions. Risk mitigation measures include: 1) Working with referral pathways to prevent and manage GBV and SEA. 2) Continuously reviewing referral pathways, especially during outbreaks and pandemics. 3) Collaborating with other INGOs for community awareness on safeguarding. 4) Establishing safeguarding focal points and feedback mechanisms involving Oxfam, partners, and community representatives. 5) Ensuring accessible community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms. 6) Disseminating information about safeguarding to project participants. 7) Conducting gender sensitivity and awareness sessions with project participants and local leaders on GBV prevention and women's protection, household management, childcare and UCDW.Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures: Risk: Tension may arise between communities benefiting from WASH infrastructure and NFIs and those not reached. Probability: High. Mitigation: Prioritize communities with compromised access, coordinate with WASH actors and local leaders.Risk: Men may resent women's participation in hygiene and menstrual hygiene sessions, leading to domestic violence. Probability: High. Mitigation: Engage male allies for consultation, ensure separate sessions, and hold them in private venues.Risk: Increased GBV risks due to lack of segregated toilets or locks in schools and communities. Probability: High. Mitigation: Consult females on toilet location and design, ensure locks on doors and windows.Risk: Failure to control a cholera outbreak becoming an epidemic. Mitigation: Coordinate closely with WASH and health actors, establish cholera preparedness measures, monitor trends, and establish operation and maintenance plans with local authorities.
