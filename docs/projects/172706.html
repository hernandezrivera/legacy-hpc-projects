Project Summary

Access to safe water of appropriate quantity remain a challenge in rural and urban areas. According the ZIMVAC 2020 Rural Livelihoods Assessment report, only 60% of households have basic access to improved water sources. Several districts continue to use unimproved water sources, including surface water; with Binga, Kariba, Bulilima, Mangwe, Gwanda, Chiredzi, Insiza and Muzarabani having at least 20% of the households drinking surface water. This is a cause for concern as it increases the risk of diarrheal disease outbreak. About 51% of the households still travel more than 500 meters to their main water source. There was an increase in the proportion of households travelling more than 1km to fetch water, from 16% in 2019 to 20% in 2020. This predisposes women and children (accounting to 80% with responsibility of fetching water) to protecting issues such sexual exploitation and abuse.
An ongoing drought has caused low recharge for abstraction dams and boreholes. The national dam average as at June 2020 stood at 49.4 against a national average of 70.5% for the month of June . This continues to affect raw water abstraction in urban areas resulting in water rationing. The associated COVID-19 restrictions and lockdowns has also affected local authorities’ capacity to provide WASH services in both urban and rural areas, chief among other things the procurement of water treatment chemicals.
Although the proportion of households practicing open defecation decreased from 33% (in 2019) to 29% in 2020, the figure still remains high. Matebeleland North province proportion of households practicing open defecation is way above the national average at 53%. A number of districts have open defecation rates above 50%, namely; Beitbridge, Tsholotsho, Lupane, Nkayi, Binga, Kariba to name a few. The ZIMVAC urban assessment of 2019 has also noted an increase in open defecation in urban areas, with some areas in Harare recording 19.1%; which is a cause for concern. Compounding this is the lack of hygiene enabling facilities such as hand washing stations. The proportion of households increased from 2% in 2019 to 11% in 2020. This is still low and require to be intensified to curb transmission of diarrheal diseases. The general economic fragility economic fragility, exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, has caused significant erosion of coping capacity of families to purchase hygiene enabling items - among them soap, menstrual hygiene management kits and other hygiene items.
UNICEF is targeting 610,057 vulnerable people in 27 priority districts and local authorities. The estimated budget for this response is US$14,848,252.60. UNICEF and it's partners will carry out the following interventions to achieve the Sector Objectives:
• Strengthening effective coordination and surveillance mechanisms at National and sub-national level, with links to other cluster coordination arrangements on critical inter-sectoral issues
• Restore access to sufficient water of appropriate quality and quantity to fulfil the basic needs of children and women.
• Improve awareness among children and women on safe hygiene, proper solid waste management and sanitation practices, with a focus on community engagement, participatory health and hygiene education (PHHE) and water conservation.
• Provide access to critical WASH-related hygiene kits, with a focus on the most vulnerable families in the targeted areas, including pre-positioning of emergency WASH supplies in critical provinces.
• Strengthen water quality monitoring at the local level during the peak-period so that appropriate action can be taken early before an outbreak.
• To support the Health system to reduce the risk of hospital-associated infections and enhance infection, prevention and control (IPC) WASH related support will be given to health care facilities.
• Mainstreaming Accountability to Affected Populations and other cross cutting issues

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Throughout the project AAP will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the center of our work, and this will be achieved by ensuring the following; 1. Communities participate in the project in a safe and appropriate manner that ensures equal opportunities for all including the vulnerable. 2. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the Partner while working in their community. Community dialogues and inception meetings from District level down to ward level and even village level as the project progresses will be some of the tools to be utilised. 3. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established and utilised throughout the project, to get feedback on experiences and perspectives of the project implementation. Mechanisms such as help desks, suggestion boxes and suggestion boxes feedback sessions will be implemented at a frequency to be determined by communities. All feedback received will be recorded and adequately addressed in a manner that also safeguards the communities. 4. The Partner will also work with other existing WASH partners in the Districts to ensure that communities are not overburdened, and efforts are also not duplicated.
