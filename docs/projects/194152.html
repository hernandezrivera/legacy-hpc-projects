Project Summary

This project aims at increasing access to lifesaving and life sustaining essential health
services for most in need population during humanitarian situations in Sudan, 2023.
The project is targeting Blue Nile, Sennar, Gazira, White Nile, Gedarif, Khartoum, River Nile, North Darfur, West Darfur, Central Darfur, North Kordofan, South Kordofan, and Red Sea.
This project will strengthen health sector capacity to prevent, prepare for and deliver
timely response to population affected by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease
outbreaks. This will include a) procurement, prepositioning and rational use of essential
medicines, basic equipment and medical and laboratory supplies including PPE and strengthening
capacity on rational use of drugs and supply chain management; b) building the capacity
of healthcare providers and community health workers including volunteers on provision of maternal, neonatal and child healthcare services, communicable and non-communicable diseases,
healthcare services and vaccination services; c) integrating, expanding and maintaining disease surveillance system with early warning component including event-based surveillance for communicable and notifiable diseases and strengthening related response (IDSR) and information system; d) strengthening the rapid response capacities to emergencies including setting up emergency health teams, supporting and training RRTs and treatment capacity; e) improving health facility set-up to provide an acceptable quality of health services including the supply of water and power and basic needs of quality of care; f) supporting system for early referral of complicated cases; g) supporting community mobilization and participation, health education and preventive messages. aiming at changing communities towards healthy behaviors.
The project implementation plan will be detailed, implemented and monitored jointly with
stakeholders including targeted communities at national and state levels and local
health authorities. The project will be implemented in 12 months with estimated budget at $16,531,860

1 - Needs

Sudan is currently facing intensive and growing complex emergencies, characterized by wide geographical distribution, severe economic recession and inflation with extremely low public spending on social services, high out-of-pocket payments, growing food insecurity, volatile political context, protracted intercommunal conflicts across several parts of the country, refugee influxes and multiple health system related shocks including flooding, disease outbreaks and pandemics and challenges with health system governance, fragmented financing and needed trained human resources. The interagency Humanitarian Needs Overview 2023 data released demonstrates that the People in Need of humanitarian assistance has risen by 1.3 million from 2022 to a total of 15.8 million people in 2023 representing eight percent increase from the previous year adding to this >1 million persons displaced due to conflicts erupted on April 15, 2023. Whilst there are certainly areas with higher levels of humanitarian need, yet the needs are chronic across the country, reflecting various local drivers, and the explicit impact of country-wide economic and political challenges. Several diseases are endemic in Sudan such as malaria, cholera, dengue fever, yellow fever, hepatitis E and chikungunya with a tendency to cause annual outbreaks across several states. In 2022, at least 2,676 suspected Hepatitis E Virus cases, including 24 associated deaths, were reported across Sudan, out of which 2,052 cases were reported from the refugee camps in Gedaref State. While North Darfur State reported 95 cases it had the highest case fatality ratio CFR of 22.3 percent (HNO, 2023). Flood season is the peak season for water and vector-borne diseases resulting in increased need for health services. 86.7% of population classified as at high risk of malaria, and as of September 2022, 14 states have malaria caseloads crossed the epidemic threshold with a 2.2-fold increase compared to the same period of the last year, and ten states reported measles cases with a total of 886 cases by the same period. The healthcare system remains fragile and continuously stressed by the emerging humanitarian crises including conflicts, civil unrest, floods, disease outbreaks, and deteriorating economy. The economic difficulties facing Sudan with historic levels of inflation across the country, and the effects of COVID19 pandemic have made it difficult to maintain medicine stocks across the country. During 2022, the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 31% in public facilities and 30% in private facilities compared to 43% during 2021. That number rose to 51 per cent in facilities supported by humanitarian actors. This has been especially hard on vulnerable groups including refugees. Children under five years of age are exposed to vaccine-preventable diseases due to reduced coverage of the expanded program of immunization and a high prevalence of malnutrition. In 2022, around 700,000 children missed their routine immunization with the PENTA 3 vaccine and the measles vaccine coverage dropped to 60 per cent nationwide. It is estimated that 3.7 million children under 15 years need to be vaccinated against measles. COVID-19 vaccination remained below the target and by end of June 2022, only 11.65% were fully vaccinated, 19.1% partially vaccinated, and 3.5% received a booster dose, leaving a target gap of 32.9%. In Sudan, 1.5 million women of reproductive age lack access to Basic Emergency Obstetric services. Only 13.4 per cent of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities during 2021, with 40 per cent of the home deliveries not attended by qualified health personnel. Significant gaps in coverage of lifesaving services include EmONC coverage (32 per cent); clinical management of rape (CMR) services coverage is less than 20 per cent and impacted by a weak referral system and lack of qualified staff at the facility and community level (HNO, 2023).

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project will respond to the essential health humanitarian needs including for IDPs, returnees and host communities while ensuring the right to health for vulnerable population. This project will strengthen health sector capacity to prevent, prepare for and deliver timely response to population affected by conflict, natural emergencies, and disease outbreaks. This will include a) procurement, prepositioning and rational use of essential medicines, basic equipment and medical and laboratory supplies including PPE and strengthening capacity on rational use of drugs and supply chain management; b) building the capacity of healthcare providers and community health workers on provision of maternal, neonatal and child healthcare services, communicable and non- communicable diseases, healthcare services and vaccination services, CMR and GBV prevention; c) integrating, expanding and maintaining disease surveillance system with early warning component for communicable and notifiable diseases and strengthening related response (IDSR) and information system; d) strengthening the rapid response to emergencies including setting-up emergency health teams, supporting RRTs and treatment capacity; e) improving health facility set-up to provide an acceptable quality of health services including the supply of water and power and basic needs of quality of care; f) supporting system for early referral of complicated cases including GBV victims; g) supporting community mobilization, health education and preventive messages to changing communities towards healthy behaviors. The planned activities will ensure equitable access to health services by all targeted population mainly the most vulnerable and marginalized women, children, the elderly, and people living with disability

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The climate crisis is an intergenerational child rights crisis, and the greatest threat to children's survival, learning and protection. Save the Children has an Environmental Sustainability and Climate Change (ESSCC) Policy, and is committed to minimizing the negative environmental impact of our programs and operations, contributing positively to environmental sustainability and climate change action, andimplementing interventions which reduce vulnerability to climatic shocks. Environment will be considered as an integral part of the project with a number of factors that will be implemented to improve the environmental friendliness of the health humanitarian response. This will include the coordination among different actors including communities on environmentally friendly health programs, enhancing awareness raising amongst health practitioners and communities on environmental protection. Certain measures will be implemented as an integral part of this project to mitigate possible environmental effects of health interventions such as effective waste disposal, carefulplanning of the emergency waste disposal sites and using appropriate methods, safe and hygienic disposal of needles and other medical supplies, safe management of solid and human waste to prevent spread of communicable diseases.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all programming, Save the Children aims to increase safety, uphold dignity, and ensure equitable access to services, including through addressing the concerns of individuals and groups with specific needs. Program design and implementation is informed by context analysis including local power and conflict dynamics. The project will implement purposeful awareness raising for targeted beneficiaries and communities on the community level protection measures, availability of GBV servicesand importance of timely access to service. GBV survivors will have safe access to health facilities and health providers will be equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Health staff will be trained on first line GBV management and CMR will be conducted in coordination with the protection and GBV. Further, information sharing of violence, IDP movements, and GBV incidents will be shared among sectors to ensure timely response

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Stakeholders’ participation and accountability to affected people is part of SCI’s core values. In practice, our participation and accountability approaches cover the following: a) Regular, timely and accessible information sharing, b) Participation – discussing together, deciding together and working together, c) Proactively seeking feedback from children and communities and handling their complaints, d) Generatedata and evidence for continuous learning. SCI is signatory to Core Humanitarian Standard and considering this SCI Sudan has already established strong accountability mechanisms which include three critical components of information sharing, participation, and complaints and feedback for the targeted communities and children. SCI will provide regular, timely and accessible information to targeted communities about: a) Who we are and the principles we adhere to; b) Project interventions and what SCI intend to deliver (e.g., what people are entitled to receive and when); c) Our staff and how we expect our staff to behave (including our Child Safeguarding policy and Code of Conduct); d) How communities can be involved. This information will be communicated in the local language and medium that are easily understood, respectful and culturally appropriate for different members of the targetedcommunities, especially vulnerable and marginalized groups. To ensure accountability to the beneficiaries during the consultation phase, SCI has will consult and engage the existing community structures/bodies (traditional leaders, representatives from IDPs, returnees, refugees, host communities, youth groups, women groups, and local government authorities). During the implementation phase, SCI will ensure that targeted communities have been provided with opportunities to express their viewsand influence decision-making through discussing together, deciding together and working together. Particular attention will be given to the vulnerable groups to enable for active participation in the implementation of the project activities.
