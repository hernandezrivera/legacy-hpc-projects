Project Summary

The project aims to ensure that 13,333 people prone to flood risks have access to safe clean drinking water, practice improved sanitation and hygiene, and reduced risks of exposure to environmental hazards through the construction of flood-resilient WASH structures and infrastructure in the flood prone areas and contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition focusing on SAM children/caretakers and PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion.
The response shall be through both mobile and static response depending on the accessibility and security context of each locality/payam. The target communities and villages shall be identified in consultation with the WASH Cluster subnational coordinator and with the support of the Nutrition partner, and households with a pregnant and lactating woman, female-headed household, households with a disabled and/or elderly shall be targeted as this will improve access to safe water especially in payams/communities with high GAM prevalence and high vulnerability to WASH. The project shall also assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facility level and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water, hygiene promotion activities and complementary sanitation services where possible in the facility to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission.

1. Needs

Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 6 million people were negatively impacted by inadequate access to clean water and sanitation and poor hygiene practices in 2023.The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. Access to WASH basic services is limited in South Sudan, with below examples:Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns (HNOs).Access to sanitation remains low. Depending on the date of the assessment (from 2019 to 2022), OpenDefecation rate at national level ranges from 80 to 90 per cent! The disaster pattern in South Sudan is characterized, among other by:An estimated 2.2 million people will suffer from acute malnutrition in 2023. Out of 1.4 million childrenunder age 5, 353,600 will suffer from SAM, 1 million will suffer from MAM and 737,800 pregnant andlactating women (PLW) will have acute malnutrition.Over the last four years, about a million individuals have been affected by flooding every year, mainly inJonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states (2019-2020-2021-2022).The total number of people displaced in South Sudan has reached 2.2M individuals during the third quarter of 2022 (DTM), with IDPs settling in settlements/sites or within hosting communities. Flooding, fighting and communal clashes (frequently linked to cattle raiding) continue to trigger new displacements and the situation remains volatile. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. High open defecation rates also expose women, children and people living with disabilities at higher risk of violence and discrimination, particularly in IDP sites and other crowded settlements. The absence, presence or placement of WASH facilities is a driver of population movement and conflict over land and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion. Around 15 per cent of households has at least one disabled family member. After years of conflict, manycivilians have a disability after being maimed, or had an amputation, damaged or destroyed sight and hearing and other impairments.

2. Project activities

The project shall build quality sustainable infrastructures such as flood-resilient facilities water supply systems, hygiene promotion activities, sanitation services awareness and self-sustained maintenance mechanisms through enhance community engagement including persons with disability and ownership by undertaking sand filtration household facility construction to serve 13,333 people. The SAM children and their care takers as well as Pregnant and Lactating women shall be targeted at nutrition facilities with WASH services and NFIs distribution to reduce disease transmission at household level. WASH NFIs, hygiene promotion, emergency sanitation and water supply shall be targeted in communities showing a high risk of outbreak and high malnutrition prevalence. Protection mainstreaming and GBV mitigation measures will be mainstreamed throughout WASH programming.
