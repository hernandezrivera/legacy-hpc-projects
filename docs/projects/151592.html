Needs

Despite improvements, there are pressing humanitarian needs in Somalia according to findings from the post-Gu seasonal assessment conducted across Somalia in June and July 2018.Urgent treatment and nutrition support is needed for approximately 294 200 acutely malnourished children, including 55 200 who severely malnourished between now and December 2018. Integrated support interventions should be sustained to maintain recent improvements as well as prevent further deterioration in the nutrition situation. Over 1.5 million people facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support between now and December 2018. Livelihood support is also required for 3.1 million people who are Stressed (IPC Phase 2)

Activities

1.mass screening of 12100 children 6-59 months (6050 boys and 6050 girls) and 3320 PLWs through OTP Centers.2.Screen, admit and treat 12100 boys, 5370 girls 6730 and 3320 PLW through provision of integrated essential nutrition.3. Provision of micro nutrients including iron folate for 6 months and multiple micro nutrients for the Pregnant and lactating women .4.Provision of routine immunization services to children and pregnant women in both fixed and mobile facilities

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The population of Lower shabelle faces clan conflict and other militia groups might be a threat to the local population. The project will ensure inclusiveness of all social groups, clans and local authority in its implementation

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SHAHRO will ensure the affected population including women have equal access to emergency health assistance in the targeted support to advance gender equality is based on a gender analysis. SHAHRO shall apply gender segregated community consultation meetings enabling women and girls to address their specific concerns, prioritize their needs and appropriately inform the development of proposed activities, measures are also put in place to maintain gender balance in terms of staff recruitment. Equitable and gender-sensitive participation and decision-making approaches will be adopted throughout the project, including women’s participation in the community Health committees and access to gender-specific health and nutrition information. Community consultations through focus-group discussions will take place during the project implementation, and stakeholders will be involved in decision-making processes to tailor the project to the needs of different gender and age groups. Furthermore, data will be dis-aggregated by age and gender in all recording and reporting

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The population of Lower shabelle faces clan conflict and other militia groups might be a threat to the local population. The project will ensure inclusiveness of all social groups, clans and local authority in its implementation and will closely work with the Somali forces and South West Administration to mitigate the impact of armed conflict and violence during the implementation of the project.
