Project Summary

The Gaza Strip is facing immense challenges related to water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), which pose significant health risks to its nearly two million residents and constrain the socioeconomic development of the Palestinian people.
Aligned to the WASH cluster strategic objectives 1, 2, and 3, and in line with the framework of the OPT country 2023 HRP strategy under strategic objective 2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, by the rights of protected persons under IHL”
The project-specific objective is to provide an appropriate, integrated WASH response for Khanynouse area in Gaza Strip to improve access to WASH services that impacted women, children, girls, and boys. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and the WASH cluster.
All project activities will be closely coordinated and implemented in consultation with and approval of relevant stakeholders including PWA, CMWU, and WASH cluster.
By its completion, the project is expected to serve3600 individuals.
The project will also result in establishing, installing, and or rehabilitating sewer networks, in addition to rehabilitation of WASH at the household level in Khanynouse area (Al-Dahra -Qa al Kharaba,and Al-Batten Alsameen locality.

1 - Needs

WASH vulnerabilities in the Gaza Strip have been driven by the ongoing blockade, Israeli restrictions, the internal Palestinian divide, May 2021 and August 2022 escalations along with the consequences of these factors. According to HNO 2023, 47% of the people in need (PIN) in oPt live in Gaza Strip and more than one-third of them are living below the poverty line.Based on the 2022 WASH cluster vulnerability mapping, there are significant shortages of adequate water and sanitation services at the community and household (HH) levels. More than 5,500 households in Gaza Strip are not connected to the sewer networks. 41,000 households are not connected to water networks.The proposed WASH priority interventions were identified based on the 2023 humanitarian needs priorities, and WASH vulnerability index. The identified WASH interventions are classified at 2 levels: community, and HHs levels. follows: Community-level: CMWU and ESDC agreed to rehabilitation and installation of the sewer network in the Khanouynse area, Al-Dahra locality .and at HH level by rehabilitation of 100 household sanitation facilities at HH level in Khanouyse area, Al-baten Alsameen locality.

2 - Activities

The project will respond to the needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted vulnerable areas, including children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Furthermore, the proposed WASH action will be; A 1; rehabilitation and installation of the sewer network in the Khanouynse area , Al-Dahra ,Qa al Kharabalocality. installing of 5 km sewer pipeline and 300 HH connections At the community level. A2, The rehabilitation of 100 household sanitation facilities at HH level in Khanouyse area, Al-baten Alsameen locality.

3 - Indicators

For activity A1: rehabilitation and installation of sewer network, the indicators are # of people with improved access to sanitation services at least 3000 individuals will have improved access to sanitation and sewer network.For activity A2: the rehabilitation of 100 household sanitation facilities at HH level the indicators are # of people with improved access to functional and safe sanitation facilities.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Part of the response framework integrates an existing strategy within its approaches with beneficiaries in alignment with the nature of the WASH response. Further, the impact is sustainable with consideration of security and protection concerns that might arise.Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries, The starting point for the proposed project is the needs analysis (initial diagnosis), which is done before the project is conceptualized. This stage involves the analysis of the actors, their positions, challenges and relationships. It is essential to give some thought to DNH at this stage. This will provide an understanding of local culture and power relations in communities (including all stakeholders, and taking into account discriminated groups, especially women). On the other hand, the initial risk analysis will be conducted, on the risks to beneficiaries, rather than limiting itself to the risks to the project, as is generally the case at present. ESDC already has a significant number of practices highly sensitive to DNH. However, these practices are less often implemented than other practices used in project management. It is vital in the first instance to ensure they are routinely implemented. Mechanisms for managing data protecting beneficiaries, beneficiary feedback (including complaints management) and measures to mitigate negative effects remain inadequate and are among the practices that need to be implemented rapidly.In the end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do-No-Harm principle as outlined in ESDC Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ESDC has developed mechanisms that can contribute to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse such as the signing of the code of conduct by all staff to clarify unacceptable behaviour and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project will focus on the marginalized/economically vulnerable community of the Khanynouse area, Al-Baten Alsameen locality, and Al-Dahra area neighbourhood.ESDC will identify the most vulnerable households based on a socioeconomic household vulnerability assessment. The factors reflecting socioeconomic vulnerability includes socio-demographic and socio-economic data, such as characteristics of the head of household predominantly female/widow/elderly/PWD, income and dependency ratio .by conducting field visit verifications, making scoring system with score matrix and selection criteria. During the implementation phase, it will be announcing events in the target community to ensure deep reach of the target people the target communities will be provided with clear and effective information, complaint, and feedback mechanism about the project, that will be linked to the related municipality, in addition, sharing project information and discussing the pre-identified site-location and works separately through women’s committees will provide a protected space for women to have a strong involvement in infrastructure-decisions related to their communities.
