Project Summary

This emergency recovery and sustainable resilience-building project for the most vulnerable food and nutrition insecure households will be an interwoven response project implemented in a span of 6 months (January to June 2024). The project aim to enhance food and nutrition security and resilience among the most vulnerable food insecure households which promotes sustainability and fosters peaceful co-existence in selected counties in Jonglei, and Unity States reaching out to 5000 households (3000 female, 2000 male). The beneficiaries will be targeted/selected based on a vulnerability matrix tool agreed on with the stakeholders within the project locations during the project launch. Reducing acute food insecurity while building community resilience to future shocks caused by conflict, climate change, and economic instability will be the focus of this intervention.
To drive and sustain emergency food production, main season fast maturing agriculture seeds (both crop and vegetable) in addition to farm tools and ﬁshing gears distributed. These inputs will empower communities to increase short-term food production and improve dietary diversity.
Adaptive and absorptive on-farm trainings and extension support shall be part of this project purposefully to build community resilience. Demonstration plots established at nutrition centers where beneficiaries taught how to prepare nutritious food in a hygienic manner. This will further strengthen productivity and safeguard assets. Community-based early warning systems will be established to improve preparedness for floods, and droughts. Essential inputs, such as seeds will be pre-positioned for rapid deployment following shocks.
To reduce food losses, the project will train beneficiaries on proper post-harvest practices such as drying, sorting, and safe storage methods. Hermetic storage bags will be provided to mitigate spoilage and pest infestations, while community grain banks will be promoted to enhance food security during lean periods.
The project will adopt a participatory, community-driven approach to ensure sustainability and inclusivity. Project management committees established in addition to taking an active role in the beneficiary selection, monitoring implementation and handling complaints and feedback mechanisms and monitoring of the project. Stakeholders will be invited during the launch and all project details will be shared to foster accountability to the affected populations. Training sessions will be delivered by local trainers and extension workers, ensuring the transfer of skills and knowledge. Regular community meetings will facilitate feedback and adaptive management.
SPEDP will strive to integrate with other projects like health, WASH and nutrition through passive referrals and also establishment of demonstration plots at nutrition sites. For instance, SPEDP is running a 5 year Right to Grow project in Leer therefore will link this project beneficiaries to this project for support. Through skills development and provision of livelihood inputs, beneficiaries will be in a position to become self-reliant and develop resilience against shocks.
By combining emergency support with long-term capacity-building and anticipatory action, this project will protect household livelihoods, enhance food production, and build resilience helping to mitigate food insecurity and empower communities to withstand future shocks.

1. Needs

Fangak County, Jonglei State: An estimated 1,112,000 people (53.1 percent of the state’s population) in Fangak County in Jonglei State are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. Fangak County in Jonglei State is one of the four counties classified in crisis level. In the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 1.08 million people (51.3 percent of the state’s population) are likely to face Phase 3 or above, of which 349,000 people are likely to be in Phase 4 and 727,000 people are likely to be in Phase 3. Food insecurity in Jonglei is driven by a complex interplay of factors, including ongoing flooding and localized violence such as raiding and looting. Excessive rainfall has displaced families and reduced crop yields for 32 percent of households, severely affecting food production. These challenges have restricted market access and limited livelihood opportunities. Unusually high food prices, exacerbated by currency depreciation and supply chain disruptions, have further eroded household purchasing power, creating significant barriers to food access.Leer County, Unity State and Ruweng Administrative Area (RAA): The recent IPC report (Sept-Jul 2025) indicate that during the harvest/post-harvest period, food security situation in Unity State and Ruweng Administrative Area will deteriorate affecting an estimated 748,000 people (64.5 percent of the state’s population) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. In addition, 204,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. In Leer in particular, 544,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Equally, during the lean season (April – July 2025 (Projection 2), the food security situation is expected to seasonally deteriorate in Unity State, with Leer being among the 5 Counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Factors driving severe food security outcomes include continuous flooding of farms, depleted household stocks of harvested crops, diminished coping capacities, increased dependency on markets characterized by high food prices and low household purchasing power. Others include; conflict and insecurity associated with cattle raiding, the influx of returnees from Sudan and reduced functionality of markets caused by the degradation of roads and other infrastructure during the rainy season.

2. Project activities

This project proposes a woven approach covering a wide range of activities geared towards meeting the food security and dietary diversity of most vulnerable households in these selected counties. The following are activities planned to be undertaken; 1. Provision of Agricultural Inputs • Distribute drought-resistant seeds and farming tools to support immediate crop production (5000HHs; 3000 female/2000 male). • Provide training on improved agricultural techniques, including climate-smart practices and soil conservation (1200 participants; 800 female/400 male). 2. Fishing Livelihoods Assistance • Supply fishing kits, including nets, hooks, and storage equipment, to enhance fishing productivity (3000HHs; 2000male/1000female). • Train beneficiaries on sustainable fishing practices and fish preservation techniques (600 participants; 400 male/200 female). 3. Post-Harvest Handling and Management • Training on proper post-harvest practices to minimize losses, including drying, sorting, and storage techniques (1200 participants; 800 female/400 male). • Distribution of hermetic storage bags and silos to prevent pest infestations and spoilage. • Support for community-level grain banks to enhance food security during lean periods. 4. Disaster Preparedness Measures • Pre-positioning of essential inputs, including seeds, to ensure rapid response after shocks. • Formation and support of community-based early warning systems for floods, droughts, and livestock diseases. 5. Capacity Building for Livelihood Diversification • Training on income-generating activities, such as poultry rearing, beekeeping, and small-scale food processing (600 participants; 400 male/200 female). To implement the above activities, SPEDP will employ a range of strategies including the following; • Set up of Community Committees for beneficiary selection, a Community based approach used since its effective both in terms of providing a mechanism for implementing a relief operation in a relatively short period, and in terms of meeting the principles of transparency, accountability and fairness. This will have a maximum of 12 members • Selection, registration and verification of beneficiaries done by the enumerators with recommendation from the community committees. Registration lists will be finalized using vulnerability and targeting criteria. • There will also be training of beneficiaries on basic agronomics through demonstration plots and modern methods of fish preservation. Lead Farmers identified and trained, besides, 25% of the beneficiaries trained. - Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring exercises and post-harvest surveys conducted to ascertain the impact of the project at household and Community level and broader economic level
