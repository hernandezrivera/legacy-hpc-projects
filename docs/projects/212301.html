Project Summary

Specifically, the project focuses on the reduction of impacts by explosive hazards, and is a contribution towards a lesser dependency of the population in Al Hasakah Governorate on humanitarian aid and supporting their long-term resilience. The proposed areas of operations are mainly former agricultural areas or contaminated arable land in Al Hasakah Governorate, primarily along the Khabur River, to the west of Hasakah City towards the Iraq border, and to the south towards Deir ez Zour, where limited access to vital assets (e.g. land) due to explosive hazards contamination is limiting people’s abilities to rebuild their lives. Limited operations (especially NTS and/or EORE) are also possible in areas to the north of Hasakah City towards the northern border, security situation permitting. Precise locations, however, will depend on the developments in the field, priorities highlighted by local communities/authorities, and complementary activities of other HMA operators in the region. To remove and mitigate the threats against lives and limbs of the civilian population, ITF proposes an integrated humanitarian mine action approach in close coordination with the relevant local authorities, communities, and other humanitarian actors. The project envisages the continuation of non-technical and technical survey, EORE, and clearance conducted on suspected or confirmed explosive hazards contaminated high-priority areas (schools, infrastructure) or former agricultural land (including agricultural infrastructure, e.g. irrigation channels) in ISIS liberated areas, and return of cleared land and infrastructure to the local population for their safe and productive use. It is estimated that approximately 1.8 million m2 of land in areas assessed as a priority in a participatory process with local authorities and communities will be surveyed and cleared/released; clearance of these areas will make way for possible food security/livelihoods as follow-up response. These activities shall be augmented through explosive ordnance risk education/community liaison, targeting approximately 12,000 at-risk boys, girls, men, and women with safety messaging, and providing the ‘eyes and ears’ for the clearance teams.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

To mitigate the explosive hazards threats in the most cost-efficient and sustainable manner, the parter shall work in conjunction with a national NGO, established by individuals with a track record in HMA and food security/livelihoods. The partner shall support a national NGO through training and building of its organizational and technical HMA capacities, skills and know-how, as well as through extensive QA/QC of its activities and continuous on-the-job training supported by expert staff. The national NGO's two mixed-gender clearance teams, one NTS and one EORE team, with close partner's oversight, shall be the implementing entities on the ground. Thereby, the partner will address several challenges that mark this context in NES, namely 1) risk of death and injury due to explosive hazards, which can compound existing humanitarian needs, 2) lack of access to productive resources, such as land, 3) lack of sustainable national NGO capacities in NES, especially in the HMA field. This intervention shall also restore the social fabric by actively involving women and men, and supporting their equal access to resources. The project takes into consideration the various risks that may materialize during the implementation, and shall adopt the following mitigation measures:R1. Presence of explosive hazards limiting access and posing a threat to the physical safety and security to both those delivering and those benefitting from services; R2. Risk of interventions not reaching all vulnerable groups because of lack of understanding of social norms, gender considerations; R3: Damage to housing, land or properties or eruption of HLP disputes due to survey. R4: Teams exposed to/guilty of GBV, sexual exploitation, and abuse. M1: training all project staff trained on PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and Do No Harm; ensuring access of beneficiaries and staff to feedback and complaint mechanisms. R5: Non-compliance of project personnel with SOPs or lower than expected knowledge and competencies of the staff. M1. Delivery of EORE training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; include first aid component in risk education sessions; delivery of training of trainers (ToT) to ensure large scale awareness and sustainability of efforts; M3. Involvement of community focal points in the planning and delivery of activities; tailoring of messages and EORE materials according to the audience; M3: Clear explanation of intervention to local communities/beneficiaries, and application of participatory processes; M4: training all project staff on PSEA, gender mainstreaming, and Do No Harm; ensuring access of beneficiaries and staff to feedback and complaint mechanisms. M5: Re-training provided to staff failing to live up to the expectations; disciplinary processes for repeated failures to comply; feedback and complaint mechanism in place.
