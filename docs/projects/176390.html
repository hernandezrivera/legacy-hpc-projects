Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Kajo keji, Morobo and Yei counties of Central Equatoria State, Aweil East, Aweil South and Centre of Northern Bahr ghazal State and Uror, Twic East and Pochalla of Jonglei State. Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children.
The project will target over 125,000 individuals, (1) Improve water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and (2) Facilitate hygiene promotion throughout the targeted communities to encourage behavior change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices, ensuring access to safe water through rehabilitation/ construction of water points, water quality testing, provision of WASH NFIs, ensuring access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion.
The project will be in line with, WASH Cluster CO1.1: “Integrated WASH response with nutrition /food security”, WASH Cluster CO1.2 “integrated WASH response with Health”, WASH Cluster CO2.1:“Access to basic services”, WASH Cluster CO2.1: “mitigate Gender-based violence” and WASH Cluster CO3.1: “building WASH resilience”. Tearfund will, (1) Improve water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and (2) Facilitate hygiene promotion throughout the targeted communities to encourage behaviour change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices, (3) construct and installed hand washing facilities in Health, Nutrition facilities to encourage hand washing hence reducing Covid 19 transmissions. The proposed intervention will enable Tearfund to continue providing critical services addressing access to safe drinking water and increased sanitation facilities, and improved hygiene practices to vulnerable communities in Aweil East & Centre, Uror, Twic East, Pochalla, Kajo keji, Morobo as well expand activities to targeted communities in Yei and Lainya. Tearfund has a static presence throughout Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, and Central Equatoria with bases in Aweil East, South and Centre, Uror and Twic East, Pochalla, Kajo keji and Morobo. The proposed project is part of Tearfund’s wider programme, which integrates WASH, Nutrition and Food Security.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 WASH Cluster Population in Need, Uror is predicted to remain at severity 3 in April- July 2021, Pochalla county is predicted to continue being in Severity 4 while Kajo keji, Morobo, Lainya and Yei are in Severity 5. Uror, Twic East and Aweil East, Aweil South are in an Emergency level (IPC 4) in December - March 2021, while Pochalla, Kajo Keji, Morobo, are predicted to continue being in Emergencies level (IPC 4) in tApril 2021 to July 2021. Lainya, Yei and Aweil Centre are in Crisis level (IPC 3). An increase in acute watery diarrhoea and acute bloody diarrhoea is likely as a result of a combination of lack of access to safe water and poor hygiene due to the lack of funding to the cluster in 2020. This affects the nutrient uptake in infants and young children, thereby sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhoea cycle. Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices, low food production and consequently low food reserves are also preventing children from receiving balanced diets. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and creates barriers for Tearfund (WASH partners) when implementing activities. Several boreholes have flooded whilst a significant number of school latrines have collapsed. New health-related WASH needs arose in 2020 as a result of COVID-19 and will persist into 2021 as a vaccination campaign is not expected to reach a sufficient number of people soon, implying a greater need for functional hand washing facilities both art household level and in areas of high density such as the urban areas and IDP settlements. Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhoea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhoea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children. In Kajo keji, Morobo Yei and Uror, poor hygiene and sanitation practices remain prevalent, with increasing numbers of returnees arriving in Kajo keji, Mororbo and Yei, however as its still individual returned its estimated that due to fear to settling in the home village alone, the returnees have opted to set up new settlements without sufficient access to water.

2 - Project activities

The project is in line with the Cluster's HRP strategic directive. The proposed project will carry out the following activities: (1) Rehabilitation of boreholes: This rehabilitation will be carried out in Dakriang FC Uror (1), Kajo keji(3), Morobo (6), Yei (2)and Lainya (5) prioritizing villages with high rates of GAM and SAM. Tearfund will test the water quality to ensure that it remains safe from bacterial contaminants after activity completion. In order to prevent contamination of the borehole after rehabilitation, the WUCs (one at each borehole) will be trained and equipped to fence off the boreholes, in order to prevent animals from accessing them. Pump Mechanics at each rehabilitated borehole will receive technical training including on the construction of platforms and basic repairs in order to ensure they are well capacitated to maintain the boreholes in working order. By deepening their knowledge of borehole repair and rehabilitation, Tearfund is adding to the sustainability of boreholes in situations where overuse frequently causes breakdowns. (2) Water quality testing carried out on boreholes in targeted areas: Tearfund will conduct water quality tests on each of the rehabilitated boreholes to ensure that SPHERE standards for water quality are met. (3) 20 hand pump mechanics trained and equipped: Tearfund has trained pump mechanics linked to WUCs for the last 6 years. This is becoming a sustainable structure for the maintenance of water points, with the pump mechanics embedded within the community (and therefore easily accessible) and WUCs collecting contributions from the community which are put towards the operation and maintenance of the water point. The added value of creating exchanges between new and experienced pump mechanics has become evident from previous/ current interventions. Through this exchange, Tearfund has been able to broaden the knowledge of experienced pump mechanics by introducing them to new topics during the training of the new mechanics. Tearfund will train 10 new pump mechanics - targeting 6 females and 4 males where possible in order to enlarge the pool of pump mechanics in the targeted area and to close the gender gap in WASH. (4) Water User Committees supported: All communities where borehole rehabilitation is undertaken will receive support for setting up WUCs and corresponding training. While additional selection criteria will be defined in consultation with the community, at a minimum, members will be permanent residents of the village, respectful of others and trustworthy. The selected community members will be trained by Tearfund and enlightened on their roles and who they report to and how. Training of WUCs will include the chief and Payam administrator. Trainings will be carried out based on a 14-step curriculum developed by Tearfund’s WASH Advisor, led across a 2-3 day training, based on Tearfund’s experience in Aweil. (5) Construction of households and institutions latrines with hand-washing units addresses issues of open-defecation and the spread of oral-faecal disease. Gender and protection needs will be taken into consideration in the design of the latrines, ensuring safe access for women, girls, men and boys alike. (6) Tearfund will sensitive targeted communities on 5 different areas of hygiene i.e. personal hygiene, food hygiene, water hygiene, domestic or environmental hygiene and safe excreta disposal. This will be conducted through Village Hygiene Promotion Resource Persons (VHRPs). A Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) approach will be applied to address the general hygiene and sanitation issues that affect the targeted area and which increase the risk of fecal oral diseases and, consequently, malnutrition. (7) WASH NFIs will be distributed to households affected by floods to ensure access to basic WASH supplies, including aqua tabs, Menstrual management kits, water buckets, soap, etc, reducing the risk of waterborne diseases and malaria.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Tearfund will strengthen staff capacity on PSEA and will share with beneficiaries information about protection from sexual exploitation and abuse during all project activities. In targeted locations, Tearfund will work with GBV actors and community based protection actors to spread awareness about the GBV referral pathway including a range of services for survivors. Tearfund will collaborate with protection cluster to provide information that seeks to prevent and respond to abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence against women, men, boys and girls, and ensure that a comprehensive complaint mechanism is in place and sagefuarding reports and concerns are investigated. Tearfund will develop Information Education Materials (IEC) and disseminate information to the beneficiaries at the host communities/IDPs/Returnees on the available service providers, locations and services available. The IEC materials will also convey knowledge and skills to young men to adopt gender equitable behavior.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Carrying out need assessment will help us understand Information sharing and feedback mechanisms in order to adapt information to the literacy levels amongst specific vulnerable groups to avoid excluding vulnerable groups. As part of the program design, risk assessment will be conducted to ensure Tearfund work does not cause harm to vulnerable people. The information tools will be contextualized and explained to generate understanding and be relevant. The project will disaggregate data to match the project affected population or sub-groups where practical actions will be embedded into planning and delivery of WASH services to ensure inclusivity and needs of all gender groups. The engagement of the vulnerable groups will help us to understand expectations, involve them in the decision making process and enable us to receive timely feedback. The steps will help address the needs of the most vulnerable in reducing inequalities and help everyone benefit equally from the project.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

To support better performance and WASH outcomes at the community, Tearfund and partners will ground their response in the needs, priorities and expectations of the beneficiaries through a number of approaches for example, by engaging in dialogue and joint analysis with beneficiaries on feedback and complaint mechanisms to empower the communities in ways to communicate their needs and concerns and provide the space to make decisions that affect them to enable beneficiaries to act on their own independently. At WASH facility centres, Tearfund will use notice boards with details about tearfund, contact persons, names and numbers of community representatives in committees, a summary of the project and a map of the project area. Communities will be included in the leadership of WASH response activities in designing facilities processes and community participation in monitoring and evaluating response programs. Complaint help desks will help in providing basic assistance, psychosocial support and counselling to returnees/IDPs/ host communities through the use of community volunteers at the water points and community centres and sites so that the communities can provide regular feedback. Additionally, referral pathways will provide the communities with information on the existing service providers, their services and locations and how to access them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Tearfund will gather data about communications channels in communicating with communities and consider how their rights and entitlements in order to identify feedback mechanisms to be adapted in delivering food assistance in emergencies. Tearfund will analyse information provided by the returnees/IDPs and host communities through the use of field staff and community volunteers to triangulate the information analysis for service improvement. Tearfund will look at other types of information such as, life saving information, where people can access support, people’s rights and entitlements, sexual exploitation and abuse to the communities, security risks, corruption etc to provide adjustments if necessary throughout the project. Information on people’s rights will take into consideration international humanitarian standards (Sphere, HAP, IASC GBV guidelines, Red cross code of conduct) in the provision of impartial and timely access to services and right to protection. Tearfund will ensure beneficiaries participate in decision-making concerning activities and to lead or support the implementation of activities.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To propagate the centrality of protection, Tearfund will train staff and stakeholders to integrate sexual gender based violence, child protection and general protection and social inclusion issues in the planning, implementation, monitoring and review of nutrition activities. To ensure women, girls, boys and men are protected from project related protection risks, Tearfund will train nutrition staff in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse including psychological first aid. Staff will receive training on safeguarding and child protection as part of enhancing our accountability to ensure that our programs and operations do no harm. Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse messaging will be integrated in community meetings including OTP and feeding centre activities. Tearfund will forge linkages with protection actors in project sites to promote referrals to survivor centered GBV and child safeguarding services. Tearfund will use the safety audit findings to determine the timing, venue and location of nutrition activities to minimize safety risks that women, girls, boys and men face when they participate in nutrition activities. All nutrition activities will be conducted in locations with safe, accessible, private, and gender-sensitive institutional latrines, handwashing facilities, and bathing facilities to promote greater safety of women and men, girls and boys.
