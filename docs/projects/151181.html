Project Summary

The project aims at providing life-saving nutrition services for targeted 3960 children (1980 boys and 1980 girls) aged between 6-59 months and 1961 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) that are severely acute and moderately malnourished by providing an integrated nutrition package based on Somali IMAM standards with strong community support through capacity building of nutrition staff, Community Health Workers and Community Nutrition volunteers on case detection through MUAC measurements and weight-height measures to mitigate against sustained and escalating malnutrition caseloads in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts. This will be achieved through: Screening and treatment of severely acute and moderate malnourished boys and girls aged between 6-59 months and PLWs by OTP/SFP program, Prevention and management of common diseases (anemia, diarrhea, malaria etc) by provision of Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, multiple micro nutrients, supplements, iron/folate supplements and dissemination of hygiene promotion messages and practices to the targeted 3960 children (1980 boys and 1980 girls) aged between 6-59 months and 1961 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs), provision of lipid based nutrient supplements to boys and girls aged 6-59 months through health facility and mobile teams alongside food distribution and advocacy for promotion of dietary diversification.

Needs

Past devastating drought that nearly culminated into famine exacerbated by successive seasons of below normal rains has significantly impacted to severe food insecurity, limited access to safe water and sustained critical malnutrition rates among boys and girls aged 6-59 months and PLWs in Hobyo and South Galkayo Districts, Mudug Region, Somalia. The situation is compounded by a number of drought and conflict displaced women, men, boys and girls that have settled in IDP camps in South Galkayo and Hobyo District. Despite above average Gu 2018 rains in most parts of the county coupled with improved food security and reduced mortality rates as well as sustained humanitarian support, nutrition situation in South Galkayo IDP camps and parts of Hobyo District remains high with GAM 16.6% and SAM 3.4% (FSNAU survey September 2018). Critical sustained malnutrition in South Galkayo and Hobyo Districts is exacerbated by limited and poorly distributed nutrition and health centers owing to long distance covered to access the services coupled by poor breast feeding patterns among pregnant lactating women (PLWs),poor diet diversity and complimentary feeding among boys and girls aged 6-59 months as well as limited access to safe water and poor hygiene conditions and practices attributed to frequent cases of water borne diseases that worsen already precarious malnutrition status of Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) and boys and girls aged 6-59 months.The worst affected are IDPs and very vulnerable host communities living under poor conditions and rely on marginal unreliable livelihood strategies with significantly high malnutrition rates having lost all their livelihood the current devastating drought. Nutrition analysis in the project targeted locations showed an identical malnutrition rates of boys and girls at the rate of 50-50% with no clear cut difference in terms of their age as well as Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs) hence need to prevent further escalation of malnutrition

Activities

1. 3980 children (1980 boys and 1980 girls) aged 6-59 months and 1961 PLWs are screened and treated through OTP/SFP program2. Prevention and management of common diseases (anemia, diarrhea, malaria etc) through Vitamin A supplementation, deworming, multiple micro nutrients, supplements, iron/folate supplements and dissemination of hygiene promotion messages and practices to the targeted 3960 children (1980 boys and 1980 girls) aged between 6-59 months and 1961 Pregnant Lactating Women (PLWs).3. Provision of lipid based nutrient supplements to children under 5 (boys and girls) through health facility and alongside food distribution and advocacy for promotion of dietary diversification4. Training of 40 OTP and SFP staff (20men and 20 women) as well as volunteers in case detection through MUAC measurements and weight measures based on IMAM guidelines5. Training of 40 Community health workers (20 Female and 20 male) on nutrition education and to enhance their knowledge in Malnutrition to mitigate increasing malnutrition cases among children aged 6- 59 months and PLW based on IMAM guidelines6. Conducting 12 Community education sessions for 60 community nutrition volunteers (30 female and 30 male) to increase awareness on malnutrition. 7. Strengthening coordination with WASH, Food Security and health partners through participation in monthly cluster coordination meetings for maximum synergies aimed at mitigating malnutrition

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk of deliberate exclusion of marginalized and minority groups during selection of the targeted beneficiaries in to the project through screening. Mitigation measure: Having a representation of all groups selection to prevent risks of exclusion

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

1. Risk of Gender based violence amongst PLWs when covering long distance to access nutrition servicesMitigation: Accompanying the targeted PLWs to nutrition centers of they arrange to go in groups2. Risk of children not completing treatment owing to neglect/failure of mothers to take them for treatment .Mitigation: Follow-up by Nutrition staff using data of the treatment of individual malnourished boys and girls aged under 5

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

1. Risk of PLWs and boys and girls aged under 5 getting caught up in the armed conflict particularly shooting when accessing nutrition services at the nutrition sitesMitigation: Ensuring Nutrition sites are away and safe distance from conflict areas.
