Project Summary

Within the course of the Anglophone crisis since 2016, the WASH conditions for rural communities in Kupe-Muanenguba is a call for concern. With the increasing threads posed by the global health crisis ( COVID-19), and potential cholera outbreak, it is very important for much attention be given towards improving the WASH situation of IDPs and host communities as measure of building community resilience to shocks.
This project is intending to improve the WASH condition of IDPs and vulnerable people in crisis affected and hard to reach communities in Kupe-Muanenguba. This will be achieved through; promotion of good hygiene and sanitation practices (intensive sensitization and awareness raising - including training of hygiene and sanitation promotion agents as focal point for each target community), rehabilitation of water points (including establishment of water user committee), construction of household and gender sensitive emergency public latrines, and also distribution of WASH kits to IDPs and vulnerable people. With an increasing rampant disposal, management of waste in public places like health centers, the project will also have a waste management component through establishment of waste pins and distribution of waste bins to support target population. This will involve solid waste management in towns like to prevent breeding of mosquitoes or to prevent malaria. Waste bins will be donated to health centers, public places and waste pits will be contructed in some health centers.
With the abundance of small streams and rivers flowing from the Kupe-Muanenguba mountains, most communities within Kupe-Muanenguba division still do not have access to clean drinking water. Made up of hard to reach communities/ areas, humanitarian response is still needed in the WASH domain especially targeting women and girls, elderly population and people living with disabilities who face difficulties in terms of moving to long distances to access water facilities. Compared to the needs, what has been done recently was really helpful but from our feedback mechanism, more is still needed to improve upon the hygiene conditions and WASH facilities in many IDPs locations, host communities and public establishments like health centers, and schools.
The lack of good tiolets, pushes most people in these areas to practice open defication. Worse about this is the fact that the few latrines which are manageable are not gender and disability sensitive and almost all even lack bathing facilities. This is quite risky especially to preganant women, children and because such a conditions can lead to cholera outbreak. In this case, households with situations like this will be assisted with latrines ( segregated and gender sensitive) to improve their hygiene condition. Also, sensitisation will be contineous, in target communities on household use of latrines under the best hygiene and sanitation conditions and prevent spread of diseases. Looking at the economic hardship, most women IDPs cannot afford hygiene kits like sanitary pads. Therefore, menstrual hygiene management for women and girls of child bearing age will be a great concern. This will involve workshops, seminars, talks targeting these women and girls
This project is also directly inline with UN SDG No-6 :- clean and accessible water for communities. Therefore, this project will be making an increase in water availability in the most needy villages, communities with IDPs. This will involve, establishing water points in these locations, mobilisation of communities for clean up campaigns of already existing water points, rehabilitating water points and treating some with aquatabs following recommendations from WASH assessment.
Distribution of WASH kits to IDPs and other vulnerable population will equally be a major plan of action for this project. This will be mainly for the needy IDPs and vulnerable population in affected communities.
