Project Summary

The consequences of the on-going crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic in the NWSW Region of Cameroon remain volatile and have forced an estimated 1,033,000 (570,000 from NW and 463,000 from SW) school aged children to be out of school (Safe School Learning, page 1, 9/1/2020). Most students have missed out on the education cycle for over 4 years and suffer exposure to lots of negative coping habits with increasing recruitment into arm groups, early marriages, child labor and lots of exploitations of these vulnerable. Even those that have been able to resume classes in formal schools are studying under stressful life threatening conditions. School aged children in this conflict region, whether out of school, enrolled in non-formal educational or formal setting all need emergency attention.
It is an established fact that children are often caught up in conflict and face violence and insecurity whether they are the direct targets or collateral victims of the conflicts. The grave violations of their rights perpetrated by parties to the conflicts are unacceptable. They have an adverse impact on their capacity to learn, work, build meaningful relationships and contribute to the development of their communities and countries. School aged children in Anglophone Cameroon are generally affected by various forms of involuntary social exclusion. They suffer multiple inequalities, deprivations, and vulnerabilities – in dire need of lifesaving measures as have been deprived of basic human and children’s rights and exposed to loss of human dignity.
What has made this conflict unique in the world is that education was the underlying cause, the immediate cause and remains a target. Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs) continue to use education as a tool to enforce the attainment of their objectives as they continue to enforce school closures. Meanwhile State Security Forces on the other hand continue to massacre school children with targeted shootings/killings, sometimes under disguise as NSAGs. In accordance with UN Security Council Resolution 612, Green Partners Association has been monitoring the gross violations in line with the following six grave violations against children during armed conflict including:
1) Killing and maiming of children,
2) Recruitment and use of children,
3) Abduction of children,
4) Rape and other forms of sexual violence committed against children,
5) Attacks on schools and hospitals, and
6) Denial of humanitarian access.
To respond to the needs of both in-school and out-of-school children, Green Partners Association (GPA) hopes targeting 342,398 children with the support and collaboration from UNICEF and other Partners in 2022 through provision of the following activities;
 Construction of latrines and water points in schools and learning spaces
 Implementation of Radio Education Programmes & digital learning platforms
 Advocacy and social dialogue with stakeholders in the education system (communities, teachers, administrative and school officials, Partners, etc.) to foster access of all children to education, including girls and children living with disabilities
 Training of teachers & supervisors in Psycho social Support, Conflict & disaster risk management & Child Protection
 Purchase & distribution of learning materials (bags, books, pens, pencils, etc.)
 Provision of grants to schools & training institutions & the most vulnerable families & children
 Implementation of a school feeding program
 Training of facilitators of learning spaces & other Education staff in life skills, psychosocial support & other topics related to EiE
 Revitalization of School Management Committees
 Training of school managers in the minimal standards for Protection & EiE, Coordination & Information Management
 Community awareness of the prevention of COVID-19
All these activities will be carried from January 2022 to December 2022 under the supervision of UNICEF and other education partners.

Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs) continue to use education as a tool to enforce the attainment of their objectives as they continue to enforce school closures. Meanwhile State Security Forces on the other hand continue to massacre school children with targeted shootings/killings, sometimes under disguise as NSAGs. In accordance with UN Security Council Resolution 612, demanding partners to monitor and report on the following six grave violations against children during armed conflict, Green Partners Association has been monitoring these gross violations, and more including: 1) Killing and maiming of children, 2) Recruitment and use of children, 3) Abduction of children, 4) Rape and other forms of sexual violence committed against children, 5) Attacks on schools and hospitals, and 6) Burning of homes accommodating children and destruction of livelihoods of parents and carers of these children 7) Denial of humanitarian accessEvidently the abuses are persistent and consistent from both State Security Forces and NSAGs; On October 24, 2020, 7 school children were shot dead and 13 others wounding in school in Kumba (SW region) by belligerents On September 9-10, 2021 9 teachers were abducted by alleged NSAGs in Kumbo-Ndop highway, NW region. The following week a head teacher was slaughtered on his way to school in the same Ndop. On September 15, a three-week, region-wide lock-down halted all services and activities in NWSW On October 1st 2021 children as young as 4 years old carried military parades under supervision of NSAGs On October 14, a 5-year-old girl was shot dead by a gendarme in Buea, on way to school On November 12, police officers in Bamenda shot and killed a seven-year-old girl returning from school.  On November 24, gunmen killed 4 students and a teacher, with 7 other students wounded in Government Bilingual High School Ekondo-Titi (SW region).  The number of teenage mothers resulting from sexual abuses are alarming These abuses continue to exacerbate the continue spread of Covid 19, causing many more children/parents to either refrain from going to school, if not forced to stay at home by enforced school closures. Those that go to school suffer from mental and multiple psycho-social challenges as well as they are exposed to various forms of other disasters. To respond to the needs of both in-school and out-of-school children, in 2022, in collaboration with UNICEF and other partners in the education sector, GPA hopes targeting 342,398 children to provide; Radio Education program to out-of-school children with lessons and protection messages. PSS/C-DRR to both in-school and out-of school children Provide training to facilitators of non-formal learning education Provide financial support to facilitators of non-formal learning education Carry out advocacy against recruitment of child soldiers Carry out advocacy on Safe Schools Declaration Provide vocational training to out of school adolescent boys and girls who refused going back to school  Provide dignity kits, ECD kits, learning material and didactic materials to children and schools Providing WASH facilities monitoring and reporting on the various abuses on children Continuous awareness to mitigate the continuous spread of Covid 19While UNICEF has been supporting local partners to minimize the consequences of this crisis on children the disruption of key learning services leaving children at greater risks of violence, including grave violations, remains so high hence requiring greater and consistent attention through provision of more services.With the support of UNICEF and other partners GPA targets 446,398 children within formal and non-formal education settings in the period January - December 2022.
