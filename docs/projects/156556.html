Needs

Humanitarian need overview for 2019 indicates that 2 Millions internally displaced person (IDP) is living in NW Syria. By the end of 2018, there were 1,448,269 people in need identified, and from of them, 954,271 are children under five and 493,998 are PLW.In NW Syria there is shortage of food due to accessibility, availability, and affordability has compounded nutrition status among the most vulnerable population, which are mothers (including pregnant and lactating women) as well as children younger than 5 years.Disrupted health system and the destruction of health infrastructure have further increased the potential for deteriorating nutrition status among mothers and children. This multi-faceted combination of causes has begun to translate into increased trends of malnutrition latest report of surveillance showed trend portions of malnutrition from 1.33% in January to 2.38% in November 2018 because of the arrival of IDPs from the besieged areas of Ghouta and Homs to Idlib.PAC in 2018 screened in OTP clinics more than 34,837 children under five and ensured treatment of over 74 SAM and 667 MAM children. Also, PAC registered 9,071 PLW and provided service for almost 725 with MAM cases. Also, PAC in 2018 screened in CHWs program more than 70,743 children under five and ensured treatment of over 105 SAM and 627 MAM children. Also, PAC registered 16380 PLW and provided service for almost 1394 with MAM cases. Even though malnutrition rates remain below the international emergency thresholds, the trend of deterioration is rapid, and without proper and optimal interventions, malnutrition rates very soon will hit and exceed international benchmarks. According to the SMART surveys conducted in the the targeted areas, showed high proportion of stunting . there for the affected population should be prioritized for response through expanding the IYCF program to cover all the communities and connecting the nutrition programs with the other clusters "health - child protection - FSL and Wash" that would have a long-term cognitive and productive impacts on children . In response, INGOs and NGOs have launched Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) & infant and young child feeding as well as micronutrient supplementation for children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women programs inside Syria and have received nutrition supplies from UNICEF and WFP. Current levels of nutrition programming are also insufficient vis-à-vis needs on the ground. Geographic coverage of curative and preventive programmes, therefore, should be increased. The huge number of IDPs formed a huge pressure on the health system at hosted communities. The most affected categories are the most vulnerable to such crises are women and children.

Protection Risk Analysis

1- In all locations of the actives , environmental risks still has a fatal effect on implementation of the 1- 1-activities in the case that a major disaster happen in one of the areas of implementation.The Probability of this risk to happen is low. While the impact on activities will be very high.2- Changing in the front lines between the warring parties may compromises the safety for the staff and the beneficiaries .3- Gender-based violence (GBV) and violence against women and girls (VAWG)4- Acts of sexual exploitation and abuse either carried out by staff or not prevented by them.5- access constraints will continue to be the most significant threat to humanitarian operations. 6- Humanitarian operations continue to be in a high risk of corruption and aid diversion or beneficiaries exploitation and discrimination in the delivery of humanitarian assistance.7- The IDPs caseload fluctuations in the targeted regions will remains one of the biggest risks to all humanitarian operations.8- projects may adversely affected by the external incidents " field staff or beneficiaries or physical damages on the properties"

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- For this risk , the SOPs applied in all projects activities should be activated once this risk happened. 2- safety policy which prioritizes the staff and beneficiaries3- regularly convened a wide range of stakeholders to share knowledge and build evidence on what works to address violence against women and girls and to take actions4- ensuring that governing bodies are made fully aware of any serious incidents and are accountable for ensuring appropriate investigation, reporting and response.5- changing the locations according the donor's requirements.6- Accountability to affected populations (AAP) will be strengthened, as well as due diligence and remote monitoring mechanisms.7- maximizes the responses capacity ,Resource Mobilization and advocacy for the case.8- Incident Reports to donors and other and activating the SOPs which related to safety and evacuation
