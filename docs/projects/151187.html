Project Summary

This project aims at providing access to temporary and sustained water supply, safe sanitary facilities and increased awareness on safe hygiene practices to IDPs, host communities and returnees affected by drought, floods and conflict in Middle Shabelle, Hiraan, Kismayo, Baidoa and IDP settlements in Banadir. Water vouchers will be provided to 20,500 people and 23 strategic water points will be constructed/rehabilitated. The water sources will be located 30m away from latrines to avoid contamination. Dewatering of 30 wells and application of shock chlorination will be done in flood-affected areas. To strengthen community ownership and ensure sustainability of WASH assets 50 water management committee members (50% women) will be trained on operation and maintenance. The project staff and WASH committee members will also be trained on DRR and protection mainstreaming. 97 gender-sensitive latrines with hand-washing facilities, raised to avoid flooding and fitted with locks from the inside to curb GBV will be constructed in IDP camps, schools and health/nutrition centers. CLTS approach will be introduced where no latrines will be constructed. Community members particularly women and girls will be consulted in the siting and design of WASH facilities. 5000 hygiene kits will be distributed to targeted high-risk families i.e. families with malnourished children and affected by AWD. 600 hygiene promoters will be trained in the PHAST method and will carry out community hygiene campaigns. WOCCA will continuously monitor the Shabelle river and disseminate early warning and hygiene messages through radio and SMS when floods are imminent. Sensitization meetings will be held at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles. Accountability to the affected population will be enhanced through the provision of a feedback hotline to beneficiaries where they can provide feedback and complaints. To ensure quality in the project outputs the project staff will continuously monitor and report on project indicators. WOCCA will also share GPS coordinates of all WASH structures and monthly project reports with the WASH cluster and donor. Signing off and handing over of WASH structures to beneficiaries upon completion will be done through community elders, government officials and religious leaders. WOCCA will also adhere to the principles of "Do no harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict-oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries.

Needs

Drought, conflict/insecurity and floods have displaced 2.6 million people in Somalia and an estimated 1.5 million Somalis are in need of urgent life-saving food assistance, 58 percent of whom are internally displaced people (HRP, 2018). Floods that occurred in many parts of south and central Somalia between March and June this year destroyed farmland, infrastructure, and roads, washed away shelter and latrines in IDP settlements and disrupted livelihoods in the worst-hit areas. Belet Weyn town in Hirshabelle state was worse affected by floods, with nearly 110,000 people displaced. Another 20,000 people were affected by flash and river flooding in Middle Shabelle region when the Shabelle River overflowed into the Horseed area and other low lying areas within the river basin (OCHA Humanitarian Bulletin, May 2018). The risk of disease outbreaks in these flood-affected areas remains high particularly among IDPs as many water sources are contaminated and sanitation facilities destroyed. Data from cholera situation report by WHO indicates that a total of 6,383 suspected cholera cases including 42 related deaths (CFR: 0.66 %) has been reported from five regions in Somalia since December 2017. The cholera outbreak started in Belet Weyne and has spread to Banadir, Kismayo, Jowhar, Bulo Burto, and Afgooye. During week 38, Banadir accounted for 90% (27) of the newly reported cases which has the highest concentration of IDPs living with limited safe water and sanitation. An assessment done by Medair in May 2018, in Deynille further points out to a general state of despair inside the camps: sanitation facilities constructed by WASH cluster partners were washed away by floods, septic tanks collapsed, some of the existing latrines were filled with water leaving the affected population to practice open defecation and putting women and girls at risk of sexual violence and abuse. The affected population is in urgent need for WASH interventions.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

While serious protection concerns still persist in many parts of south-central Somalia the security situation in the targeted regions has been relatively stable. WOCCA has offices in Mogadishu, Kismayo, Baidoa, Jowhar and Balcad in Middle Shabelle and has implemented several livelihoods, nutrition, protection, and WASH projects within these regions. It has established direct relations with clan elders, cluster partners, and local authorities to gain access, enhance program awareness and monitoring on issues including complaints/feedback about operations. WOCCA closely engages local leaders and government officials to avert any security risk and possible threat. The project contributes towards building household and community resilience and response capacity to climate shocks and stresses by incorporating disaster risk reduction aspects (DRR) in the design and implementation of the WASH intervention. Some structural measures that will be undertaken to build resilient systems include, elevating the height of water pumps, raised platforms and raised latrines in flood-prone areas. Simple water pumping technologies will be selected ensuring that spare parts are available locally, this will ensure that the water system can be repaired after disasters and reducing reliance on external support. Dissemination of messages (early warning and hygiene messages) when floods are imminent, warning on the locations of the floods through radio and/or SMS. Provision of emergency water trucking in water scarce areas and increasing water storage through construction of water tanks. Communities will also be sensitized on water conservation measures and the importance of hygiene. WOCCA will encourage community ownership of the project to enhance sustainability. This is will be done at project inception and subsequently through the formation of engendered beneficiary representative committees that will work hand in hand with the project personnel and the beneficiary community; as well as through the use of Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) approach. WOCCA will train the water management committees (WMC) on operation and maintenance of water systems, maintenance of sanitation facilities, cleaning and chlorination of water in the storage tanks, household water treatment methods and use of aqua tabs among others. It is anticipated that they will continue to run the facilities after the completion of the project. All finished works including but not limited to water provision systems, sanitation facilities are signed off to the community through these representatives and in the presence of community elders, government officials and religious leaders.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs displaced by the recent floods, drought and the ongoing conflict in the targeted regions face serious protection risks particularly women, children, and marginalized groups. They are at risk of exclusion from accessing basic humanitarian services at the camps. Some of the factors that may lead to exclusion of vulnerable and marginalized IDPs from accessing services include; poorly defined vulnerability and targeting criteria of beneficiaries, discrimination from host communities, lack of access to information on the available services and beneficiary entitlement, IDPs lacking opportunities to voice their opinions and concerns which is often done through camp leaders or community elders and might not accurately reflect their views. The project will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Using participatory beneficiary targeting and selection approaches cognizant of age, diversity, disability, vulnerability and gender-specific needs, to ensure the most vulnerable and minority groups are targeted 2. Recruiting individuals from both the host and IDP populations in the implementation of the project in order to benefit the local economy as well as to ensure a non-discriminatory recruitment process (Do no harm) 3. Conducting sensitization meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders including groups at risk of exclusion to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women, girls and other at-risk groups face an increased risk of sexual assault and violence while traveling to water points and sanitation facilities that are located far from homes or placed in isolated locations. Lack of lighting, locks, privacy and/or sex-segregated sanitation facilities can increase the risk of harassment or assault against women, girls and boys. In IDP camps tensions with the host communities may arise over water resources or during the distribution of hygiene supplies, this can lead to violence against women and girls who are often responsible for household water and hygiene. The project will work towards mitigating the above risks by; 1. Ensuring that women, girls are consulted in deciding on the location and design of latrines and washing areas to maximize safety, privacy and dignity. 2. Constructing gender separated sanitation facilities, with lighting and equipped with lockable doors for privacy and safety 2. Establishing water and/or sanitation committees composed of 50% women, to be responsible for the operation and maintenance of the facilities .3. Ensuring water and sanitation facilities are centrally located for easy accessibility by women, girls, the sick, elderly and people with disability.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In conflict areas in Somalia, access to water is frequently restricted because water supply systems have been destroyed, looted by armed groups or located in dangerous areas. Water scarcity heightens competition and conflict over resources forcing people to migrate. People ultimately resort to dirty sources of water with a high health risk. Lack of safe water and sanitation facilities in conflict areas exposes women and girls to violence and GBV. To mitigate the above risks, the project will ensure that the conflict-affected people access emergency WASH services by engaging clan elders, religious leaders and local authorities to gain access, community acceptance, enhance program awareness, protection of staff and to avert possible security risks. WOCCA will employ participatory approaches by engaging the IDPs, host communities and community leaders in order to avoid any unintended conflicts between beneficiaries and the larger community. WOCCA will also adhere to the principles of "Do no harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries. WOCCA is also working with DDG to support processes like traditional conflict resolution mechanisms that are more representative and capable of solving civil disputes and aligning with the formal justice sector in South-Central Somalia. This project targets both police, the armies as well as the community at large.
