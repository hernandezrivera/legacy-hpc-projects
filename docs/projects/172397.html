Project Summary

The proposed project will be implemented in Chipinge and Chimanimani districts and will adopt a holistic approach in addressing food insecurity by providing emergency agriculture support and building resilience to multiple shocks. The project will be targeting 9000 HHs acute food-insecure farmers who are in IPC 3 and 4. The targeted beneficiaries breakdown will be as follows:

The * represents all targeted beneficiaries in the two districts will receive household kitchen gardening support.
The implementation of a wide range of crops and livestock production activities in this project will minimize the risk of further food insecurity deterioration by improving access to basic crop and livestock inputs (Ref Annex 1). The project will enhance access, functionality, and availability of critical community productive assets for crops and livestock production through infrastructure rehabilitation and construction. The project is designed to optimize the benefit of agricultural support through extension and advisory services to capacitate farmers with knowledge on crops and livestock. The food insecurity situation in these districts was worsened by Tropical Cyclone Idai which left a trail of destruction on infrastructure, food security, and livelihoods. According to the situation in the districts, there is a need to intensify climatic smart agriculture techniques, production of drought-tolerant crops and venture into diversified small livestock and gardening activities for improving food production and livelihoods options.

1 - Activities

• Hold inception workshops to sensitize beneficiaries and stakeholders on project objectives ( January 2021)• Provide drought-tolerant seeds for cereals (maize, small grains and legumes) as well as fertilisers (and related trainings)(October 2021)• Provide horticulture seeds and other inputs / materials for household kitchen gardening (and related trainings, cooking demonstrations, etc.) (February - June 2021)• Provide small livestock (restocking, improved breeds) and other inputs / materials (e.g. hatcheries) (and related trainings). ( February 2021)• Provide stock feed and forage seeds. ( September 2021)• Support livestock vaccination, disease surveillance and management. (January 2021)• Provide agriculture or livestock inputs to households while working on diptank rehabilitation / construction. ( September 2021)• Provide training, extension and advisory services to extension officers and farmers focusing on good agriculture practices with emphasis on climate-smart agriculture. (Sepember 2021)• Provide farmers with training to support crop pest management, post-harvest handling, storage and processing. ( January – April 2021)• Provide training on improved pasture and grazing management. ( January-February 2021)• Provide training on and inputs for agro-ecology interventions such as soil conservation practices, soil reclamation techniques and restoration of degraded landscapes (e.g. moringa / acacia trees, soil fertility trenching, solid & liquid organic mauring, soil compositing, diverse cropping patterns such as rotation, companion cropping, alley cropping, gulley reclamation, soil testing kits, water-shed/catchment area management etc.). (October - November 2021)• Provide training on and inputs for water conservation practices (e.g. contour ridging, dead-level contours, infiltration pits, tied-ridging). ( October - November 2021)• Rehabilitation / construction of dip tanks through cash for work / food for work (or assets) activities.( May-December 2021)• Monitor project activities ( January – December 2021)• Conduct exit meetings with project stakeholders. 8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

During the designing of the project, a consultative methodology was used through:• A feasibility study will be carried out first to establish the priorities, capacities and recommendations of the prospective community beneficiaries. • The community will formulate committees responsible for every day running of the project including among others facilitation of distribution of inputs in close collaboration with implementing partners and resolving disputes as they arise with the support of community leaders. • In order to get feedback from the community we propose to conduct post distribution evaluations complemented by hotline, helpdesk and suggestions box.
