Project Summary

This project aims to provide emergency food and livelihood support to close to 210,000 vulnerable households (1,260,000 individuals) in Western and Central Equatoria, Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, and Unity states of South Sudan. The project will adopt a mixed intervention approaches including but not limited to Cash transfers, Livestock support, distribution of farm inputs, knowledge transfer, and cash programming among others.
The project will be implemented in synergy with other sectors within World Vision such as Nutrition, NFI, WASH, Health, and Protection. Needs identified by this project will be referred to the relevant sector for action and support.

Needs

According to the recently-released South Sudan IPC analysis, nearly half of the country’s population is facing food consumption gaps or only meeting their basic food needs through unsustainable coping, despite the availability of some harvests and large-scale humanitarian assistance. Widespread conflict, which has persisted for over four years, continues to cause the loss of life, repeated displacement, disruption of key livelihood activities, and the deterioration of macroeconomic conditions. As a result, less than half of the population is able to produce, collect, or purchase sufficient food to meet their basic needs (FEWSNET, 2018) The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan has deepened and spread over the past year, affecting people in previously safe, stable, havens and exhausting the coping capacity of those already impacted. Since the outbreak of conflict in December 2013, there are 7.5 million individuals in need of humanitarian assistance and protection across the country (South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan, 2018) and 4.9 million people experiencing severe food insecurity (Integrated Food Security Phased Classification, 2018). Prolonged conflict and complex interplays between political, sociological and economic factors underpin a set of inherently huge and relational pressures for its people. The WFP Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS, 2018) reports that 76% of households nationwide are facing moderate to severe food insecurity, which is 67% higher than December 2016 and 71% higher than June 2016. This is the highest number severely food insecure people at this period, this is expected to surpass 6 million in the July/August period 2017. Movement of the war into Greater Equatorial region will result in the 500,000 MT 2016 deficits being surpassed as a result of movement of populations from the previously stable bread basket areas. Fear to cultivate farm lands as a result of violence has resulted in reduced livelihood options, resulting in increased poverty, vulnerability, and food insecurity, negatively affecting the populations' ability to bounce back, in the event of a shock or disaster. Many in the former states of Unity, Lakes, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal have been trapped in renewed and widespread conflict over the past months and were at times cut off from humanitarian assistance. Although South Sudan and Sudan signed a peace deal in September 2018 to end the five-year conflict, it remains unclear whether the political process will contribute to improving the humanitarian situation in the coming months (FAO, 2018).Flooding, prolonged dry spells and pest infestations, like the Fall Army Warm attack in Upper Nile, have also hampered crop production, with only 60 percent of the 2018 national cereal needs met by the harvest. Cereal production has steadily declined since the start of the conflict in late 2013, and in 2017 was estimated at about 765 000 tones – approximately 25 percent below pre-conflict levels. Further complicating an already dire situation this year, an earlier than anticipated start to the lean season, potentially poor harvests in several counties, and possible returns from within and outside the country could put added pressure on already scarce natural resources (FAO, South Sudan in Crisis, 2018). The 2017/18 Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) reports production was below last year and the five-year average due to conflict-related disruptions, crops losses from Fall Armyworm, and the continued outflow of people from South Sudan. CFSAM estimates 2017/18 cereal production at 764,100 tons, 7.5 percent lower than last year and 14 percent below the five year average. The proposed project will strengthen food and nutrition security and resilience among food insecure HHs in Greater Bahr-el-Ghazal, Upper Nile and Greater Equatorial regions in South Sudan, targeting 210,000 individuals.

Activities

Activities • Conduct Cash transfers to the most vulnerable Households, in the targeted geographical regions• Deliberately implement Cash based interventions where markets are functional, for interventions such CFA, and CFT in the targeted locations of Greater Bahr el Ghazal, and Former Central Equatoria states.• Support vulnerable households with seeds, tools and fishing kits.• Conduct market assessments, post distribution monitoring, and inter agency assessments• In collaboration with FAO, conduct livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns.• Train farmers fish folk, women and youth on various agricultural aspects relating to production, preservation and value addition.• Promotion of Vegetable and fruit production.• Promote the rearing of small livestock and poultry especially among women and youth.• Support the rehabilitation of market infrastructure to support introduction of cash interventions.• Gender and protection mainstreaming in FSL activities• Setting up of Community Help desks, to facilitate the complaints and response mechanisms and promote accountability to affected populations.. Ensure synergy with other sectors such as health, NFI, Nutrition, Protection and WASH by going activities in consultation with these sectors. Different needs will be referred to the relevant sectors for their action.. Engage in FSL cluster meetings and conduct project quarterly review meetings with the team and stakeholders
