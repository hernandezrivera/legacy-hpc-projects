Project Summary

The project aims to Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable health services for those most vulnerable and in need, This will be done through specific interventions focusing on:
Increasing access to primary health care through the provision of essential PHC services to cover the health care needs of vulnerable populations in targeted communities, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH), as well as continuity of treatment for CD and NCD. These services will provide through three hospitals, two PHC centers level two and four mobile clinics.
Improving access to secondary health care and ensuring comprehensive trauma services and referral pathways for acute and post-acute management phases through one surgical hospital (Ariha Hospital) located in Ariha –Idlib. In addition; providing gynecological and obstetrics services, address emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) cases, post-surgical care for mothers and other women, and providing incubator services for newborn, These services will provide through; Al-Nour hospital in Talmanes – Al-Mara - Idlib, Save the Soul hospital in Salqeen-Idlib, Al-Ekhlas hospital in Shnan- Ariha-Idlib.
Providing blood products which are very important to save patients' lives who have major surgeries, acute hemolytic anemia, acute hemorrhages, chronic anemia, thalassemia etc, SDI will establish a blood bank in Al-Shifa hospital in Afrin city.
Expanding access to specialized services for hostility-affected populations, including Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services and physical rehabilitation and the provision of assistive devices through health facility named Sawasia center for prosthetic limbs, rehabilitation, and Psychosocial Support located in Afrin -Allepo.
Strengthening and consolidating medical supply chains, ensuring the availability of safe and quality medicines for essential primary health care, trauma, obstetric care, infectious diseases, and chronic conditions.
Continued support of epidemiological surveillance and capacity to detect, investigate, respond to and report on disease outbreaks through early warning system.
Training, re-training and capacity building of health care providers and CHWS to increase public health awareness and address shortages in human resources for health via task shifting.
Disseminate and promote health-seeking and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need. 40 CHWs will provide services at the community level.
Gender mainstreaming in this project will be supported by tools and expertise developed to address gender equality in the specific context of the Arab world and Syrian culture conditions. During designing the project we made sure that gender issue is included in the needs assessment, activities, outcomes and even indicators, the highest priority of the project will be providing the appropriate services to all boys and girls, women, men, including beneficiaries with disability, without any sexual discrimination, among the populations, IDP last resort sites, overburdened communities or areas affected by a high intensity of hostilities as well as recently displaced IDP or returnees, In addition, the host communities will be targeted.
We've already collected enough information about vulnerable groups and according to our experience in health services provision, we always get help by volunteers from the community.
This project is intended to contribute significantly to gender equality as it follows a participatory and inclusive process at all stages of planning, implementation, and monitoring, and aims at empowering vulnerable beneficiaries to enhance resilience.
Additionally, the needs assessment already carried out as part of this intervention aimed to identify the different levels of access or availability of services for men, women, boys, and girls. Project activities are designed and carried out in coordination with the local community.

Needs

Throughout 2018, health needs in Syria continued to be exacerbated by access constraints, damage to health facilities, and insufficient human resources. In addition, many areas that witnessed an escalation in hostilities and changes in control are suffering from the disruption of critical health services, access constraints, and an increased need for humanitarian interventions. High numbers of trauma-related cases continue to be reported across Syria, particularly in north-western and north-eastern Syria. An estimated 10.2 million Syrians are at risk of explosive hazards, further increasing the vulnerability of people to traumatic injury. Further, an assessment among adults in western Aleppo, Idleb and Ar-Raqqa governorates indicated that an average of 45 percent of people surveyed had sustained hostility-related injuries are expected to sustain permanent physical impairment. In addition, in 2019, an escalation of hostilities is anticipated in a number of areas in north-eastern and north-western Syria, necessitating further support to trauma centers.Sub-optimal living conditions and lack of basic services specifically in IDP camps, collective centers, and informal settlements, increase vulnerability to disease outbreaks such as diarrheal disease, typhoid fever, and leishmaniasis. Females make up 72 percent of people in need of health assistance in Syria, out of which 5.2 million women of reproductive age are in need of Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescence (RMNCH+A) services. Attacks on health facilities and health staff have remained one of the main drivers impeding access to health care, impacting the functionality of the health facilities themselves as well as the availability of personnel needed to provide the necessary care. From January to September 2018, the Health Resources Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS) reported that 46 percent of health facilities across Syria were either non-functional or partially functional.The majority of women report delivering in hospitals, where nearly two-thirds of women deliver at home with a skilled attendant. Currently, humanitarian health partners assist with approximately one-third of the estimated 1,580 deliveries occurring each day across the country, with nearly half of the supported deliveries consisting of a Caesarian section. Pregnant adolescent girls and their babies are at high risk of medical complications and death. They constitute a substantial high-risk group in Syria, with early marriage reported to take place in 57 percent of assessed communities, and pregnant girls under 18 representing about 12 to 15 percent of the deliveries taking place in health facilities. (Source HRP2019)Al-Nour hospital for gynecology, obstetrics and child care located in Talmannes provides services to most communities in eastern rural of Al-Mara district, Al-Ekhlas hospital for gynecology, obstetrics and child care located in Shnan provides services to 7 communities in Ariha district and Save the Soul hospital for gynecology, obstetrics and child care located in Salqeen provides services to a lot of communities in Harim district.There is a shortage in basic health services in Al-Mara district-Idlib and Jandiris and Mabatli subdistrict- Aleppo, therefor SDI aims to continue and expand its services in these communities through two PHC centers and four mobile clinics, provide PHC services to 50 communities.In Afrin district, there is no blood bank and the nearest one is too far (about 180 Km in Azaz city), SDI aims to an established blood bank in Al-Shifa hospital in Afrin city In Afrin district, there is a shortage of access to specialized services for hostility affected populations, including (MHPSS) services and physical rehabilitation and the provision of an assistive device, SDI will provide these services through health facility named Sawasia center for prosthetic limbs, rehabilitation, and Psychosocial Support located in Afrin -Allepo.

Implementation of activities

SDI will implement this health programme for people in need in 66 communities; 40 communities in Idlib governorate (25 in Al Ma'ra, 12 in Ariha and 3 in Harim districts) and 26 communities in Aleppo governorate (1 in Afrin, 11 in Jandiris and 14 in Mabatly districts) through four hospitals; three hospitals for gynecology, obstetrics and child care(Al-Nour hospital in Talmanes -Al-Mara - Idlib, Save the Soul hospital in Salqeen- Harim -Idlib, Al-Ekhlas hospital in Shnan- Ariha-Idlib) and Ariha Surgical hospital in Ariha city- Idlib, two PHC centers level 2 (one in Marat Al-Numan and the other in Mabatli-Afrin-Aleppo), four mobile clinics, one health facility for prosthetic, rehabilitation and psychosocial support in Afrin city- Aleppo and 40 CHWsGynecology, obstetrics, and child care hospitals will provide gynecological and obstetrics services, address emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) cases, post-surgical care for mothers and other women, and providing incubator services for the newborn, provision of essential PHC services to cover the health, including Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health (RMNCH), as well as continuity of treatment for CD and NCD through outpatient clinics.Ariha Surgical hospital located in Ariha city will provide comprehensive trauma services and other surgical services( orthopedic, general surgery, ENT, urology surgery), pre and post-surgical care, and outpatient consultation. PHC centers will provide essential PHC services (RH, IMCI, NCD, CD and nutrition services).The four mobile clinics will work in the same activities(RH, IMCI, NCD, CD and nutrition services), each mobile clinic has one doctor, one nutritionist, one midwife, one nurse, and two CHWs, each mobile clinic will cover 12 communities and the duration of coverage for each community is one year.40 CHWs will provide services at the community level, visit homes, schools, and camps, disseminate and promote health-seeking and basic hygienic behaviors.Expanding access to specialized services for hostility-affected populations, including (MHPSS) services and physical rehabilitation and the provision of assistive devices through health facility named Sawasia center for prosthetic limbs, rehabilitation, and Psychosocial Support located in Afrin -Allepo, monthly SDI will produce and install about 25 prosthetic limbs.Providing blood products to patients who have major surgeries, acute hemolytic anemia, acute hemorrhages, chronic anemia, thalassemia etc, through establishing a blood bank in Al-Shifa hospital in Afrin city, the center will provide blood products to patients in Al-Shifa hospitals and other facilities in Afrin district. SDI Continue in support of epidemiological surveillance and capacity to detect, investigate, respond to and report on disease outbreaks through the early warning system.SDI will conduct two training for 60 health care providers and CHWS to increase public health awareness. SDI will use health cluster criteria and forms to register the beneficiaries, all beneficiaries will be registered.SDI will comply with health cluster reporting requirements, including:4W matrixMonthly reports on performance indicators.The project is effectively and efficiently will implemented, manage, document and report by a good administrative staff(female 60% and male 40%)The M&E team will implement the monitoring and evaluation plan of the project to measure the project progress and to evaluate the impact of the project

Protection Risk Analysis

1- Attacks on health workers moving between camps and houses Likelihood: medium, Impact: High Mitigation measure: The CHWs will be chosen from the same-targeted communities and each two CHWs will work together, coordination with local authorities to facilitate the movement of staff and work as a team 2-Attacks on health staff and beneficiariesin the facilities, and losing follows up of beneficiaries.Likelihood: Low, Impact: HighMitigation measure: SDI will set up health facilities in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries, and the project includes 4 mobile clinics and 40 CHWs to facilitate access to services and mitigation risks of movement with the prospect to relocate health facilities in relatively safe areas. In addition; applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, continuous reporting on attacks against health, and sustained advocacy for respect of (IHL).4- Gender-inequitable access to health services. Some women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated, such as divorced women and widows, may not be able to access basic services. Access may be further limited by safety concerns and/or custom and traditions that restrict women from leaving the home in order to access health care.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: HighMitigation measure: more female CHWs will be trained to provide services at the community level and mobile clinics will visit the targeted communities to facilitate access to services and refer cases to receive appropriate and specialized care should beneficiaries’ access to be constrained at the health facility level In addition; promotion awareness about necessary for visiting the health care center to receive basic health care and vaccinations.5- Risk related to GBV on CHWs when visiting the homes, also risks of GBV on beneficiaries and health staff in health facilities Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High, Mitigation measure: The CHWs will be chosen from the same-targeted communities and each two CHWs will work together, coordination with local councils to facilitate CHWs works, training of health staff on providing first-line support, identification of GBV cases, safe referrals, and ensuring access of the affected population to the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) for reproductive health, including clinical management rape,Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor, and the female staff is about 60% of projects staff, that increases access of women and girls to health services 6- Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers of women and girls at the facility and community levels.Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Mitigation measure: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, including victim assistance, train staff on the code of conduct, including the code of conduct of all staff contracts and address any SEA complaints.7- Risk of discrimination or if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. Likelihood: Low, Impact: HighMitigation measure: Ensuring that health project design encompasses accessibility of services by vulnerable groups (physical accessibility, disaggregated areas for women and girls, availability of female staff, Signboards) with effective community-based complaints mechanisms.In addition; to mitigate these risks and ensure adherence to humanitarian principles, the project adopted a “Do No Harm” strategy while providing health services SDI will regularly review risks and mitigation measures make necessary adjustments to programming modalities, and methods of implementation will depend on the dynamics on the ground.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk and assumptions:1-Regular and timely provision of drugs and medical consumables.2-Availability of and access to community health facilities,3-Unimpeded access to assess the needs of affected populations is available. 4-No further deterioration of the security situation.5-Technical skills are available6-difficulties of transportation7-Attacks on health facilities and health workers during moving between camps, communities, and houses.Mitigation measures:1-SDI will set up health facilities in safe and accessible locations that can be easily reached by beneficiaries, and the project includes 4 mobile clinics and 40 community health workers to facilitate access to services and mitigation risks of movement with the prospect to relocate nutrition centers in relatively safe areas.2-More non-health and health workers will be trained to provide appropriate services 3-Stock prepositioning in multiple, safe, and close locations to delivery sites and secure enough drugs and medical consumables to avoid stop the treatment of patients 4-coordination with local authorities to facilitate the movement of staff to provide services to beneficiaries
