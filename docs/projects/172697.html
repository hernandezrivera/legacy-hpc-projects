Project Summary

To support humanitarian logistics and supply partner organizations in addressing challenges, the Logistics Sector provides:
● A dedicated coordination cell to strengthen horizontal cooperation among agencies and the synchronization of logistics efforts.
● A forum for discussing Sector-specific logistics operations and requirements and facilitate access to technical expertise in order to improve existing processes.
● Web-based platforms for information sharing among humanitarian organisations in order to minimise overlaps, reduce duplication of effort and enhance resource utilisation.
● Fully managed storage and GIS mapping services availed to all humanitarian partners.
● Support humanitarian agencies to build their operational capacity through training, simulation exercises and advisory services.
● Support and advise other Sectors, the ISCG and the Government of Bangladesh on the logistics-related implications of cross-cutting issues and advocate for streamlined policies and processes that improve logistics performance.
● Tools designed to collect, consolidate and make available timely information and logistics-related intelligence, such as market capacities, asset capacities and access constraints.
● Approaches for overcoming systemic constraint – and as a last resort - assess gaps, design and implement common services for the handling of humanitarian cargo.
● Measures to reinforce operational resilience by prepositioning standby operational capacity and augmenting agency preparedness activities with technical support to mitigate logistics disruption.
● In efforts to minimize the humanitarian footprints and have a smooth access to the camps Logistics Sector to continue the Humanitarian Access Project with a fully digital system for validation of approvals of humanitarian vehicles reducing delays and bottleneck caused by manual checking at the checkpoints.

1 - Identified Needs

There is a general shortage of available warehousing within the congested camps, limited commercial facilities to manage demand fluctuation in the Cox’s Bazar District and high utilization of existing emergency and disaster management infrastructure.With the majority of in-camp warehousing being partner-managed, there is a continuing need for accountability and effective service response through capacities of national and international logistics staff. There are sufficient quantity and availability of trucks on the local transport market, however, the need remains for quality of service to ensure transporters adhere to contractual terms, safety, price control and coordination including camp access through increased government checkpoints and restrictions. Cox’s Bazar District experiences some of the highest annual rainfall in Bangladesh, and there is potential for severe flooding during the monsoon seasons (April to October). Bangladesh is also at risk from tropical storms and cyclones that form in the Bay of Bengal. While most storms which directly impact Bangladesh typically make landfall along the coastline east of Chittagong, cyclones have also affected the Cox’s Bazar District directly over the last decade. Damage to infrastructure from high winds can be severe, and intense flooding can limit movement and restrict road access. Based on the above analysis, the major logistics gaps and constraints limiting humanitarian operations in Cox’s Bazar District are: • The need for continued coordination, centralisation and sharing of logistics-related information with increased involvement of national partners to avoid duplication and identify and resolve constraints through a collaborative approach.• Capacities and expertise of field and management level national and international logistics staff to manage an accountable and efficient response.• Preparedness and response capacity to scale-up coordination and required logistics services through communication and information sharing, strategically located common storage space, access to rapid deployment logistics assets and mapping services.
