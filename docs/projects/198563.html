Project Summary

This project focuses on addressing the emergency WASH needs of 72,575 IDPs and
returnees in Borno State and to ensure their dignity, welfare and save their lives. The project will be implemented in Bama, Magumeri, Monguno, Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs where INTERSOS is already established and is implementing WASH activities. INTERSOS has continued to provide WASH needs to the people Magumeri, Bama, Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs, through integrated WASH/Health/Nutrition/Protection/Livelihood projects. The needs for WASH in the mentioned LGAs, are still high as there is a perpetual influx of new arrivals and returnees into these locations, from other adjacent locations. Currently, in addition to INTERSOS, there are 5 agencies carrying out WASH activities in these target locations i.e., DRC, FHI 360, IOM, Mercy Corps and UNICEF.
This proposed project for Magumeri, Monguno, Bama, Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs will lead to reduction of the incidence of water related diseases within locations and general well-being and behaviors change of the targeted 72,575 individual and indirectly to many more people within the target location. The following is a summary of the targets and outputs of the proposal
• Rehabilitation of 30 water collection points facilities to standard and Rehabilitation of 2 water source.
• Replenishing of 10,000 hygiene kits to households especially new arrivals who might not have benefitted before and the most affected
• Chlorination of 15 water points in IDP camps in Magumeri, Monguno, Bama, Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs
• Intensive hygiene promotion targeting the host communities, new arrivals and over congested camps, primary schools and MCH facility who/which are currently underserved by existing WASH resources within INTERSOS
• Rehabilitation/construction of 350 shared household latrines with equal number latrine cleaning kits
• Construction/rehabilitation of 50 emergency shower facilities in the IDP camp and focusing on new arrivals and transit center
• Desludging of 320 emergency latrines in the IDP camps in the target locations focusing on new arrivals
• Provision of 350 units of cleaning kits and hand washing stations
• Training for 100 CHV and 30 Chlorinators and 50 community leaders as well as 75 WASH committees’ members to ensure the knowledge is impacted to the key people in the LGAs and contribute to sustainable management of developed WASH facilities.
.Introduction of local content in WASH.(Biofilter).
These proposed activities contribute directly to the sector objectives as described in the WASH sector’s 2022 HRP plan and directly contributes to the global mission of providing humanitarian assistance to over 2.8 million individuals that needs WASH assistance in the North East Nigeria crisis

1 - Needs

The 2022 MSNA revealed that despite humanitarian response to the protracted crisis, an estimated 2.8 million people across North east Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services. ACCESS TO SAFE CLEAN WATER: - 1.02 million people receive less than 15 litres per person per day while about 1.06m people have intermittent water access with over 500 meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queuing time at water points. an average of 40-60% of beneficiaries confirm to having access to an improved water source. The level of service and gains need to be maintained for the operation and maintenance of the facilities. A significant (40) percentage still use insufficient water (below 15 ltr/person/day) which results in households using water from unprotected sources (rivers, open channels etc). Frequent breakdown and vandalism of facilities requires round the clock maintenance for improved functionality. The highest proportion of beneficiaries using unsafe water are mainly from the LGAs; Gwoza, Bama, Dikwa and Marte, as a result of long walking distances as well lack of adequate water sources. ACCESS TO SANITATION: - Overcrowding in camps, resulting in reduced space to put up sanitation blocks and land owners not willing to grant access to their lands for the construction of new sanitation facilities has constrained access to sanitation, with limited access to handwashing with soap and water and no personal hygiene materials. High sanitation needs are persistent, with existing ratios of above 1 latrine for 20 persons still largely exceeded. Lack of space, adverse weather, wear and tear of emergency design of facilities and high dislodging have forced vulnerable persons to resort to open defecation, crowding and associated protection issues around facilities. New arrivals (with the closure of camps in the state capital and environs) increase pressure on existing facilities in camp and host communities, while, continous a lack of policy shift and investment to improve designs have left thousands of sanitation facilities not functional. The fecal sludge management is poor, with low infrastructure to support liquid waste from desludging activities, which is considered as one of the reasons for yearly outbreak of cholera. Additionally, flooding is a major concern as most of the fecal sludge worsens with households facing exposure of untreated waste. The high need for WASH services amongst IDPs and returnees has had an increase from last year, mainly due to continuous displacement and the high volume of expected returns. HYGIENE PROMOTION AND WASH NFIs: Knowledge of disease prevention and practices associated with improved hygiene is good, however the lack of resources hinders implementation at household level. Hygiene awareness remains a gap in locations without WASH partner coverage. Besides, the constant need for NFIs due to factors of availability and capacity of IDPs to provide for self in the settlements come with negative coping mechanisms. Women and girls face critical shortages in MHM kits (Menstrual Hygiene Management) including soap for personal hygiene. The WASH core pipeline has further supported access to lifesaving NFIs but gaps remain in areas with high needs. WASH sector continues to work with all partners to ensure that IEC materials for environmental and personal hygiene awareness are available to the population. New reported acute diarrhea cases, with average bi-weekly data of 1,303-4,873 individuals amongst populations further increases the needs category. While there is high vulnerability across all groups, available data shows that majority of women and children (over 51%) have reported severe needs for water, hygiene and sanitation. According to the last gap analysis of the WASH sector in Bama, Dikwa, and Ngala LGAs current average water consumption is lower than 15L/p/d and the emergency ratio of 50 p/latrine is not secured.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

All groups including women, children and elderly are engaged in gathering data on their specific WASH needs. Concerns of all affected people, including vulnerable groups are factored in to the programme design ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. The programmes include estimated sex and age disaggregated data for the target groups based on the findings of the need’s assessment. For latrines, gender segregated sanitation location, design, safety and appropriateness and convenience of facilities will be decided in consultation with the users, particularly women, adolescent girls and any persons with disabilities to ensure the protection of affected people. All sanitation facilities, including latrines, will be constructed near residential units to ensure the privacy and security of women and children and easy access for elderly. Project will ensure that separate lockable, well-lit latrines are constructed for women and children and the criteria and details of activities will be explained in the community for better understanding. Project will ensure women's participation while selecting the sites for sanitation facilities, water points and timing of hygiene promotion sessions and WASH committee meetings. A close supervision that is also adaptable to the evolving needs of the target beneficiaries will be carried out by INTERSOS to support the community members to maximize their knowledge and promote their engagement to prevent and address child protection concerns. First, the training modules will be adapted to the needs of the audience and will use interactive activities, visual material and as far as possible key actors working with children will be invited to stimulate beneficiaries’ understanding, participation and interests. Before and after each session group play activities will test participants’ understanding on specific issues, if needed, topics will be re discussed. After the training cycle is completed, INTERSOS staffers initially will facilitate regular meetings with the community members and the subsequent design of an action work plan and afterwards to follow up on the action plan and its results. INTERSOS has a strong reputation on protection and GBV prevention and risk mitigation, as supported by the international protection coordinator and GBV/PSS specialist. In addition, INTERSOS protection staff work alongside the health, WASH and nutrition teams to ensure mainstreaming of protection and GBV issues across all activities. Furthermore, all INTERSOS staff is bounded to INTERSOS Code of Ethics, an official document that sets the rights, duties and responsibilities for all INTERSOS operators, collaborators, partners, advisers and members of the statutory bodies to which it is addressed. It also reaffirms the observance of values and ethical principles common to every human being, such as honesty, loyalty, probity and those pertaining to its nature as a humanitarian organization: humanitarian relief, neutrality, impartiality, independence, non-discrimination, transparency. Moreover, protection will be mainstreamed in this project as per the following consolidated procedures:- Prioritizing safety and dignity, and avoiding causing harm, by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of activities;- Ensuring access, by arranging for beneficiaries’ easy access to assistance and services, and paying special attention to individuals who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services;- Participation and empowerment, by supporting the development of self-protection capacities and assisting beneficiaries to claim their rights through their full involvement in activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

INTERSOS embraces participatory community-led approaches, that involve target beneficiaries, |Project Management Committees (PMCs), and key local leaders and influential persons to understand the project targeting criteria as well as expected contributions from the community, for instance social capital and indigenous knowledge. Community entry messaging include information like source of funding (donor), selection criteria, targeting criteria and expected humanitarian support among others. As part of the CHS commitments, INTERSOS establishes functional complaint and feedback mechanisms in areas of project interventions. A compliant help desks are set up in INTERSOS supported safe space (funded by OFDA) where people can report their complaints or give feedback about the project. Beneficiaries feedback will be gathered through several sources, including: face to face with field staff, WhatsApp, suggestion boxes, help desk, and email. Feedback received from adults and children in the community is gathered and organized by project and sector. This information is sent to the INTERSOS office in Maiduguri, where it is added to the Complaints Response Mechanism Register and referred to the most relevant person. In addition to the aforementioned set-up, the community-based approach, through active involvement of men, women, boys and girls, is at the base of the project design. Since the onset of INTERSOS action in the region, it has become well-known and respected within the targeted communities, positively engaging with both the hosting and IDP communities. INTERSOS conducts regular focus group discussions with target beneficiaries to improve its programming, taking into consideration the suggestions coming directly from the affected communities.
