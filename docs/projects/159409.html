Project Summary

Palestinian population living in Area C is composed of some of the most vulnerable families and communities in Palestine. More than 40,000 Palestinians reside in communities built entirely in Area C. Their living conditions in terms of social indicators are the worst; they live in remote areas with lack of access to services such as health, WASH and education. Furthermore, they are rarely granted permission to upgrade their homes or invest in agriculture or in any other infrastructure that can support a better living standard. According to the PWA Az-Za’ayyem is one of the high priority communities that require WASH interventions due to rising health risks and environmental risks that are affecting them severely. The project aims to address the humanitarian crises in Az-Za’ayyem community focusing on enhancing the health and the environmental situation in the short term and support sustainable future intervention in the long term. The suggested interventions focus on completing the sewage collection network and constructing a small wastewater pumping station (300 m3/ day). The project has been designed to address an emergency context, tackling resilience strategies to combine a short-term solution to the humanitarian crisis due to the lack of sanitation services and health risks, but aiming at generating also long-term and sustainable improvements in the livelihood of the community as a whole.

1 - Needs

Based on a recent assessment conducted by the PWA; Az-Za'ayyem is one of many communities in Area C that are in urgent need of humanitarian interventions. The results of the rapid assessment have indicated the following: 1- The sewage collection network installed in 2004 covers less than 25% of the targeted area. Furthermore, the network pipes diameters are not sufficient to collect the sewage flow from connected HHs. Also due to damages over the years, there is high leakage in the network resulting in water contamination. In conjunction, in the winter season, the area faces major flooding since the network is not sufficient to handle sewage and storm flows. The new design will be sufficient to cover the area of sewage collection needs until 2050. 2- The rest of the area (75%), discharge wastewater into cesspits. When filled, Waste Water is pumped by tankers and dumped in nearby valleys creating major health and environmental risks. The suggested intervention will significantly decrease environmental hazards and protect the valleys in the area. Furthermore, the intervention will support better hygiene practices and will improve the health standards of different age and gender groups. The targeted area has witnessed a growth in the population accompanied by uncontrolled urban expansion. In conjunction, due to the natural flow, all domestic effluents reach the lower wadi area between the main street and the new buildings and mix with stormwater. The current situation is negatively impacting the health and wellbeing of the residents and affects the livelihood income of farmers (olive trees, bovine, and ovine farming). Combing those challenges with the building and development limitations imposed by the Israeli authority and being constrained by the separation wall and the apartheid road, humanitarian intervention in Az-Za'yyem is crucial. Taking into consideration the main community need fo basic sanitation services for multiple families, each target group suffers from insufficient wastewater collection, disposal, drainage. Men and women have their own lands and properties contaminated from different wastewater flows. Urban development limitations and lack of basic services from the municipality providers might cause chronic diseases, high risk of contamination, and might force families to abandon contaminated lands from agricultural activities. Children and adolescents suffer from the harmful environment and lack of proper open areas around their houses and are more exposed to potential diseases. A hazardous environment might affect physical and social development, and pushes young people to leave and abandon their native lands for safer or more served area. The project is in line with the WASH Cluster strategic objectives (2-3). Furthermore, the intervention supplies a responsive solution to unserved and unconnected target areas in Area C, where building constraints and risks of demolition prevent them to install proper treatment facilities or equipment. Because of such technical limitation, the project is also intended to provide a remedial and responsive action against potential health, sanitation and natural disaster on the surrounding lands where raw wastewater is currently flowing. The new sewage line and pump station will serve as structural intervention to reinforce the sewage system of the target village for future developments, more specifically for supplementary house connections, for a stronger response capacity in case of extraordinary stormwater flooding, and for the future possibility to provide a proper treatment facility, system or equipment having all wastewater collected together and directed to a single effluent line. Furthermore, the suggested intervention will improve the vulnerability of the local community against social threats such as internal disaggregation, depopulation, violence, illegal and hazardous practices and will significantly improve the health standards for residents.

2 - Activities

Global Communities will partner with PHG and Overseas to design and implement the suggested intervention. Due to the large scale of the intervention and since GC and Overseas are committed to supporting the same area; it has been agreed to collaborate together for completing the project. The overall required intervention is installing 7 KM of sewage collection network installed. Global Communities in partnership with PHG will work on installing 4 KM (2.4k / 12-inch diameter and 1.6 KM / 10-inch diameter) while Overseas will be working on completing the other required 3 KM. In conjunction; GC will work on installing a small pump station (300 m3/ day) to support future interventions; mainly for the construction of one WWTP instead of two. 1- Installing 4 KM of WW collection network; 1.6 KM of 10” diameter and 2.4 KM of 12” diameter. Overseas will be working on installing WW collection network 3 KM; 1.2 KM of "10" diameter and 1.8 KM of "12" diameter. The activity includes: Excavation works, to be performed with rented equipment or manually according to the morphological features and risk prevention; installation of pipelines, both "10” and 12”, including collectors and estimated 250 inspections manholes connection to the existing effluent line in the Northern area of the village. ARABESC (Arab Engineering Services and Consultations) is working on finalizing the design for the network. Global Communities and Overseas will be coordinating their interventions according to ARABESC design and PWA recommendations. Furthermore, the design has taken into consideration the installation of the HH connections. Since the intervention is mainly focused on installing the network, GC and its partner will fully coordinate with the village council and residents for installing HH connections in the future. Additionally, a new small pumping station of 300m3/ Day capacity will be constructed to pump collected sewage to a planned Waste Water Treatment Plant. The pumping station is required to direct the sewage outflow to one location where the treatment plant will be built instead of having to construct two plants. The Village Council will benefit from technical training on the job during the implementation of the activities. 2- Constructing a small pump station (300 m3/ day) to unify the outlet of the newly constructed network. According to the Rapid assessment, it is expected that the area will face in the future major problems with discharging raw WW. Hence, it is crucial to support future interventions for building a WWTP. The pump station will unify the outlet of the network so that there will be no need to build two WWTPs. 3- Community mobilization and hygiene promotion for behavioral change. The goal is to raise awareness within the local community regarding the importance of health and sanitation risk prevention linked with the protection of agricultural and farming areas surrounding the village as a tool for stronger resilience and response capacity against all constraints and limitations from the military occupation. The targeted location will receive community awareness sessions on WASH-related issues to deliver the main key messages (safe water handling, storage, water demand management, sanitation practices, and good personal hygiene practices) in order to influence good WASH practices. Specialized IEC (Information, Education, and Communication) material will be adopted –including responsibility calendars, informative stickers and follow up sheets. In collaboration with the village council, target families and educational institutions will be involved to share the results and propose ideas to involve young students, children, families in the process to support future interventions and rehabilitate flooded lands. The overall intervention will strengthen the capacity and knowledge of the technical staff to control, maintain and manage the sewage system and connections in the target area, and serve as a base for future developments.

3 - Indicators

Global Communities has extensive experience in monitoring WASH programming activities. The Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEL) Plan outlines the anticipated results and the tools and mechanisms through which Global Communities and implementing partners will monitor and evaluate progress over the life of the program. This illustrative plan will provide information about the overall approach to performance monitoring, a description of the proposed methods for collecting, analyzing, and reporting high-quality program data. Global Communities’ proposed plan will ensure that gender and youth are fully integrated into the MEL system. Further, an illustrative learning and adaptive management plan will be put in place to promote stakeholder collaboration and capitalize on best practices and lessons learned. Monthly written reports will be submitted by the partner to the Global Communities will be involved in developing both the interim and final reports to the donor. In order to assess the appropriateness and effectiveness interventions in line with the overarching goal, Global Communities has designed a holistic MEL approach that employs rigorous quantitative and qualitative methods to inform an array of performance indicators that include: index/composite indicators, indirect/proxy indicators, complexity-aware monitoring, standard foreign assistance indicators, as well as several custom indicators to monitor the program’s success in designing and implementing an inclusive approach to achieving sustainable WASH outcomes. Regular field visits and on-going monitoring will be conducted during the implementation of activities and will be verified through PHG progress reports, in addition to partner's daily and weekly reports. In regards to the supplies, they will be purchased directly by Global Communities based on the list reviewed and shared by WASH Cluster. The installation work will be carried out by contractors (selected through competitive bedding) that will be supervised by Global Communities and its partner site engineers on a daily basis in the field. Further, Global Communities will monitor the project activities on a regular basis to ensure that the needed equipment and materials are delivered as planned. In addition. Global Communities will closely coordinate with the Wash Cluster and other stakeholders to ensure no overlapping occurs and enhance coordination with other relevant agencies working on similar interventions. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

People have a right to have their voices heard in judging our response to their emergency. Asking for the views of the affected population can help us understand the difference we are making during the course of the response, and not just at the end of a project. Complaints will be processed by our MEAL Officer who has suitable qualities and is trained and supported to deal with them. They should be confident in directing complaints through the right channels and judging the sensitivity of complaints. Even less serious complaints demand greater or lesser degrees of sensitivity or a more objective view. A bi-monthly meeting will bring key staff together. Regular progress reporting and decision-making forums will be used to raise issues and complaints from the community and to determine the necessary actions and by whom. Where appropriate, local government, community leaders and other stakeholders should be involved in the investigation and resolution of complaints. It is important to make sure each complainant receives a response and appropriate action, and those similar complaints receive a similar response. The complainant will receive a response that comprises a clear answer and explanation (even if no action will be taken) as well as an indication that the complaint has gone through an established process. People need to know they have been heard and answered. We at Global Communities believe that receiving a clear explanation satisfies the complainant. In other instances, if the complainant is left unhappy with the result, here the complainant should acknowledge that they have understood the answer, and know that if they do not accept it they can complain again.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The project will ensure the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries by ensuring the availability of safe water supply and creating a clean living environment in the targeted locations. The project services will be allocated based on the number of beneficiaries. Target beneficiaries will be informed of the project objectives and its impact on the allocated quantities of water to be supplied to their respective areas In addition, areas to supplied by the selected wells are known to be highly vulnerable and groups like people with disabilities, the elderly and women with young children will highly benefit due to minimizing the burden of buying and attaining un-piped water. According to the recent assessment, as mentioned earlier, there are risks that could impact the population; the Eastern area was first identified as priority for a WASH intervention but the environmental impact on surrounding lands was still too heavy for a positive outcome, while the proximity with the main highway was a political hazard in terms of Israeliintervention and limitation toward the village itself. Taking into consideration the North-West side, in accordance with the Village Council was agreed to reduce the area of operation in order to prevent additional risks and potential blocks from Israeli authorities. Finally, the secondary activities of land rehabilitation are still to be properly defined. The implementation of the activities will be tailored in collaboration with all stakeholders involved to ensure the best results while protecting the community's well-being. It is also worth mentioning that the participation of primary stakeholders and beneficiaries will be essential in the monitoring and evaluation of the project, and will have an active role in defining relevant indicators. The main expected benefits of Do No Harm relate to beneficiaries: improved accountability towards beneficiaries, relations with beneficiaries, understanding the context and community dynamics in which projects take place, and acceptance in an intervention area. Global Communities has a specific security manual that includes safety and security guidance for working in Palestine and details the processes, procedures, and risk mitigation measures staff are required to take to ensure their and beneficiaries’ safety and security. All staff expected to work in or travel to the targeted locations will receive security training from Global Community Operations staff. We have established coordination and security protocols working closely with Global Communities DCbased international security team and other organizations, such as UNRWA, on an ongoing basis to understand changes to the security and operational context in West-Bank and advise staff accordingly, including in real-time as needed. In the event of a security incident that affects the project, Global Communities will work closely with Wash Cluster, international and local entities such as the UN, OCHA, AIDA, NRC and local partners to respond accordingly.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Global Communities understands the interlinkage in human rights and that social inclusion stretches beyond focusing on the direct needs of marginalized groups. Promoting the rights to access safe drinking water and sanitation, directly impact the rights of education, health, women‘s right, child rights and the rights of People with Disabilities (PwDs). The unserved and underserved communities in Palestine fall under three main social inclusion barriers; Attitudinal: stigmatization and misinformation including the different roles between men and women, which negatively impacts the decision-making process. Environmental: issues in accessing infrastructures and facilities and lack of communications. Institutional/ organizational: lack of policies, strategies, skills, and information to support social inclusion. While designing the technical intervention, GC was committed to using a participatory approach and non-discrimination principles to empower the marginalized by addressing WASH barriers. To ensure successful intervention, cultural sensitivity matters were taken into consideration and were incorporated in the monitoring and evolution plan so that unforeseen obstacles can be dealt with in an effective and efficient manner. Participation of stakeholders at the appropriate time during the program cycle is imminent; it enhances ownership, power relationships, accessibility, accountability, and sustainability. All target families and landowners, in coordination with the Village Council, will be involved in a number of meetings to share their input on the design and implementation of the works. Furthermore, the beneficiaries will be involved in the planning and connection of each house to the mainline; the Village Council will coordinate the connection of all private buildings together with the beneficiaries, who will contribute to the purchase of material or the realization of the work after the completion of the project. The community will be involved, according to specific needs of different age and gender groups, in informative and awareness activities in order to provide hygiene basic tools and practices at the domestic level, water-saving practices, and instructions to keep grey and black water separated in order to preserve the sewage network and potential contamination. GC ensured that identified WASH stakeholders in general and beneficiaries in specific are fully engaged in the program design. Further, GC is committed to disseminating information to all stakeholders throughout the program to ensure informed decisions are adopted.
