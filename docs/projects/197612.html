Project Summary

Project summary:
Gender-Based Violence (GBV) is life-threatening and a human rights issue that has devastating impacts, especially on women and children, as well as families and communities. The insurgency in Borno State has lingered for over a decade, and displaced people in the Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) camps and those living with relatives in host communities are particularly vulnerable to GBV. The passage of the violence against persons prohibition law (VAPP Law NO.03.2022 in January 2022 by Borno state Government has prompted HECADF to partake in Awareness Creation, Capacity building of stakeholders( survivors, GBV perpetrators and indeed the general public) on the existence of such law with a view to preventing and responding to GBV challenges which are prevalent in the IDP camps and its environment.
The purpose of the proposed project is to provide GBV centered services that would build the resilience, dignity and coping mechanisms of survivors and other vulnerable individuals through survivors centered management, Mental Health Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), economic empowerment of the target population to prevent vulnerability to GBV.
HECADF is proposing to work in two local government areas of Borno state namely: Bama and Dikwa, where it has experience in GBV work. HECADF was supported by CARE International to manage four safe spaces in IDPs and Host communities and also carried out community house-to-house awareness, dialogue sessions among women, men, girls and boys on GBV, and also provided Psychosocial Support (PSS), dignity kits and cash support to the most vulnerable individuals/survivors. The HECADF GBV project ended in March, 2022, nevertheless, HECADF has continued with GBV Awareness creation through the community volunteers, GBV assistants, and case workers on a small-scale to ensure project sustainability. It is in the course of this, that HECADF discovered the need to expand the existing project with regards to awareness, facilities and GBV services in the two LGAs. The daily return of IDPs/Returnees to Bama and Dikwa local Government Areas (as they are both at the international borders) from Cameroun have increased the population of IDPs thereby putting pressure on the existing facilities and service providers on ground, which are now is inadequate. Hence, the need for funding to continue with GBV prevention and response in these two LGAs.

1 - Needs

The proposed project seeks to prevent the rampant cases of GBV by addressing the issue of lack of information, knowledge and consequences of GBV on the physical and mental health of the victims, their families and the society. This will be carried out through advocacy visits, sensitization and awareness programmes. Training will also be provided for security operatives handling GBV cases. Case management of GBV survivors and perpetrators is also another area the project will focus to ensure that GBV perpetrators are made to face the consequences as provided by law while the survivors are given both physical and mental health care and support. Vulnerability assessment has shown that women and girls without any means of livelihood are the most vulnerable; therefore the project proposes to provide empowerment for women and girls with livelihood & entrepreneur skills that will make them self-reliant and thereby reducing their vulnerability potential. Safe spaces for women and girls will be established with the aim of providing a center for skills development, physical and psychosocial support.

2 - Activities

Information dissemination through advocacy, community dialogue, radio programmes and jingles is important in ensuring that people are aware of the menace of GBV as well as reporting procedures. Some GBV survivors and their families are not aware of what to do when incidences of GBV occur. Some GBV perpetrators feign ignorance of the consequences of their actions, therefore, sensitization and awareness creation could also help in preventing cases of GBV. Training for security operatives who handle GBV cases will assist in ensuring that they stay up to date with the current laws on GBV and the actions required of them. Visits to religious and community leaders will be carried out to get them on board in the fight against GBV. Women and girls in the IDP camps are usually lack means of livelihood and therefore depend on men who have means of livelihood for help and support, this exposes then to GBV; therefore empowering the women will reduce their vulnerability and ensure that they are able to cater for their own needs rather than rely on people that can take advantage of them. The safe spaces will be a center where women and girls will be given all forms of support they need to ensuring protection and resilience from GBV.
