Project Summary

Across Sudan, girls and boys and their families face risks associated with conflict, violence and displacement including family separation, Sexual Gender-based Violence (SGBV), mine risk, psychological distress, and child labour, especially during the current Sudan Emergency. According to Sudan Situation Report (OCHA) of January 21th, 2024, about 7.6 million people have been displaced inside and outside Sudan since fighting broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces on 15 April 2023. More than 13,000 people have been killed and another 26,000 injured since April 2023, according to ACLED and Federal Ministry of Health respectively. More than 9,700 suspected cholera cases, including 269 associated deaths, were reported as of 16 January 2024. Health partners provided 2.6 million patients with medicines and 1.8 million out-patient consultations across Sudan between 15 April and 31 December 2023.
Plan International in Sudan (PIS) will ensure the provision of timely and rights-based child protection services to the most deprived and marginalized girls and boys, especially those affected by conflict and displaced from their homes. These services will include case management; prevention and response to the needs of child victims of Sexual and Gender Based violence (SGBV); material support, service provider training, support services, and life skills training for extremely vulnerable children and adolescents affected by conflict; and safe places for play, recreation and the provision of psychosocial support to children displaced or affected by conflict.
To prevent and mitigate child protection risks in communities affected by conflict and displacement, PIS will strengthen the capacity of social workers, teachers, and law-enforcement staff, as well as investing in capacity building of the States Councils for Child Welfare and other humanitarian partners. In order to reinforce the resilience of communities and strengthen capacity of child protection institutions, Plan will support community-based child protection mechanisms and civil society organisations to disseminate information on relevant services and contribute to advocacy and the referral of child protection cases; and to promote safer environments for children, especially girls and those with disability.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed to consider protection issues and concerns for those that have specific needs or higher levels of vulnerability. Protection programming aims specifically to support access to services, increase safety and uphold the dignity of identified vulnerable children and women. Protection mainstreaming is also integrated across all project activities. To mainstream gender in this project, the IASC Guidelines for integrating Gender-based Violence interventions in Humanitarian Settings are adopted. There will be a reference document for ensuring quality in planning and coordination and staff will be trained on the guidelines. Plan International in Sudan will strive to ensure that the proposed interventions will minimize unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population which could potentially expose them to further physical and psychosocial risks. Gender, age and disability sensitive measures will be adopted, including adapting meeting/service schedules and meeting venues to ensure equitable participation, using single sex discussion groups, considering ways to support the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries, and ensuring facilities are physically accessible to all. As appropriate to the specific context, activities to support highly vulnerable individuals or those that may be targeted by members of their communities, will be organised in a way to reduce further stigmatisation or exploitation, e.g. providing services within multi-purpose centres, with internal private spaces, may better serve SGBV survivors as they are less identifiable. The protection awareness raising and capacity-building activities will enhance the capacity of communities through formation and training of community-based structures to understand the protection risks and differential needs of children and other vulnerable people within the communities. The project considers aims to be conflict sensitive by analysing the situation, examining how each activity may influence conflict dynamics and working to maximize the positive and minimize the negative impacts of the proposed interventions. In particular, risks. PIS will work closely with multiple stakeholders (community members, government partners, local NGOs) to avoid exacerbating existing community tensions. Effort will be made to consider and respond to existing power dynamics within communities. Protection staff will work alongside teams from other sectors to raise awareness on protections issues and mainstream protection across all programming, for example supporting training on referral pathways, conducting protection audits, and advising on actions to make non-protection specific programming accessible and appropriate for all depending on age, gender and disability. All PIS projects are assessed according to a gender and age marker prior to proposal submission, and efforts made to promote projects that are gender transformative.

Plan International will ensure accountability to affected people, as well as the donor, local partner, relevant government stakeholders. The project will involve the target community members, where appropriate working through existing community structures whilst ensuring representation of women, youth, people with disabilities, elderly and the related CBOs, in the project cycle management. Target groups and other related project stakeholders will be fully oriented on the project objectives and deliverables using safe methods and forums such messages to ensure that, a sense of ownership is created among target communities and increased accountability towards the beneficiaries targeted by the project.
