Project Summary

This project will enhance and sustain emergency food production through provision of complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits agricultural extension support, and training package on sustainable agroecological farming practices and climate smart agriculture including soil and water conservation techniques. The project also intends to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs by supporting and strengthening households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that builds the resilience of households. The project is designed to enable IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable members of host communities to recover from crises. This will be done by seeking more durable solutions and building the resilience capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks as well as build and strengthen resilience to future shocks. The project will build on interventions that CAFOD and its implementing partners Caritas Diocese of Malakal (CDoM) and Africa Development Aid (ADA) have been delivering in these counties. The communities in greater Upper Nile region have endured food insecurity caused by a complex interplay of factors including climate variability induced conditions such as droughts and floods, and man-made conditions such as influx of returnees and refugees from neighbouring Sudan, localised inter-ethnic conflict and livestock raiding, and worsening economic situation across the country. The project will provide Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPC) to most vulnerable members of the community to stimulate local markets as may be informed by assessments that CAFOD and its partners will carry out. Light livelihoods activities, particularly kitchen gardening, will also be introduced to the communities. The project will be supported by community-based agents who will be trained to cascade extension services to target communities. Lead-farmer approach will be adopted for this project in a ratio of 1:15 (i.e. one lead farmer will be responsible for supporting and guiding 15 followers’ farmers. The capacity of lead farmers will be strengthened to enable them to provide tailored technical support to follower farmers. Post-harvest handling techniques will be imparted to the community-based extension agents, to the lead-farmers, and consequently to other farmers. This will focus on seed selection, preservation and storage to strengthen seed security and seed system in the community. The trainings will also include improved fishing practices, hygienic handling of fish and fish preservation for fisher folks. The project is intended to reach 15,000 individuals IDP, returnee and host communities (5,000 per County). The community will be involved in selection and targeting of beneficiaries. Project management committees will be formed to lead in the targeting process. The committees will include all sections of the community (IDPs, returnees, host community, women, men, youth, and persons with disabilities). A clear vulnerability targeting criteria will be agreed all these groups. CAFOD will also deploy its SADI(Safety, Access, Dignity and Inclusivity) Framework to ensure inclusion of vulnerable and often hard to reach groups such as female headed households, disabled and elderly persons. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will ensure that at least, 10 percent of the benefiting households have a person/s living with disabilities. The project will collaborate and work with other agencies working on Food Security and Livelihood intervention to achieve synergy and avoid duplication, utilize their expertise, work with health and nutrition actors and Ministry of Health to support households with malnutrition cases with a basket of interventions.

1. Needs

According to the IPC results (Sept 2024 – May 2025), between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analysed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The population in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) includes 10,000 people in Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and an estimated 31,000 South Sudanese returnees who have fled Sudan because of the ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. FEWS NET report projected that from October 2024 - May 2025, South Sudan’s acute food security situation remains severe, with 12 counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and some households facing Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Malakal of Upper Nile, Abyei, and among returnees in transit areas. FEWS NET also projected that through January 2025, the number of counties in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) will remain the same but may shift geographically depending on expected duration of own food stocks and access to additional food and income sources. Some households are expected to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in Malakal, Uror, and Pibor, as well as among returnee populations. FEWS NET projects that 7-8 million people, or 65-70 percent of the population, will require food aid by May 2025. The severity is due to the impact of flooding and sporadic conflict that have disrupted access to own production often in areas that have faced years of asset erosion. High returnee and refugee burden, and severe deterioration in purchasing capacity amid worsening economic conditions also culminated to longstanding impacts of conflict and flooding. One year of war in Sudan has resulted in an influx of over 840,000 returnees and refugees and is aggravating already poor food security conditions. Most of these returnees and refugees are arriving into acutely food insecure areas, with already scarce resources and are heavily dependent on relatives and host communities for their food needs. Moreover, many of these populations have limited to no assets to re-start their lives and livelihoods. According to SOUTH SUDAN Floods Snapshot, as of November 2024, about 1.4 million people are affected by flooding in 43 counties and the Abyei Administrative Area, with Jonglei and Northern Bahr el Ghazal states comprising more than 51 per cent of the affected population. Over 379,000 individuals are displaced in 22 counties and Abyei. in Upper Nile State's Panyikang County, assessments have revised the number of people displaced by conflict and flooding in Tonga, increasing the caseload from 3,500 to 5,707 individuals. These people are in need of life-saving assistance. A surge in malaria has been reported in flood affected counties, particularly in Jonglei, Unity, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria states, overwhelming the health system and exacerbating the situation and impact in these flood-hit areas. This situation worsened the existing humanitarian crisis in these counties causing extensive damage to homes, crops, and critical infrastructure, and further disrupted education, nutrition, and healthcare services for vulnerable communities, exacerbating their hardships. In Panyikang County for example, floods are the main drivers of the current displacement. This comes in as a shock on an already Emergency Phase 4 food insecure County. The disaster has crippling routine livelihood activities in the entire County and displaced people to higher grounds. The crop farms and business centres are inundated. 2. Project activities

This project will seek to address the food security needs highlighted in the Upper Nile counties of Renk, Panyikang and Fashoda. It will focus on the provision of nutrition sensitive vegetable production targeting households with access to cultivable land. The project will provide training on vegetable production to diffuse better vegetable production and post-harvest management skills among the targeted participants. The training will cover topics on use of agricultural inputs, soil fertility management, seedling preparation, row sowing, watering frequency, Integrated Pest Management, weeding and harvesting of products, preparation and utilization of vegetable diets (vegetable hygiene, handling, cleaning and preparation) and marketing. The project will support households participating in vegetable gardening with basic seeds, farming and post-harvest tools to improve both productivity and post-harvest handlings and marketing. The support includes provision of seeds such as Amaranth, Collard, Eggplant, Okra, Onion, Pumpkin, Tomato, Watermelon and tools (Hoes) as well as post-harvest inputs such as vegetable collection baskets and crates. Communities will receive critical equipment, including two boxes of hooks (100 hooks per box), one coil of monofilament and two spools of twine. Canoes will also be provided to support both movement and fishing activities. Fisher groups will be trained in proper fish production, processing, and preservation methods to improve yields and reduce losses. These practices will incorporate soil and water conservation techniques. Post-harvest handling techniques will be taught to community-based extension agents, lead farmers, and subsequently other farmers, focusing on seed selection, preservation, and storage to strengthen seed security and the seed system within the community. Training will also cover improved fishing practices, hygienic fish handling, and fish preservation for fisherfolk. The project will leverage community-based agents, who will be trained to deliver extension services to target communities. A lead-farmer approach will be adopted, with one lead farmer supporting and guiding 15 follower farmers (a 1:15 ratio). The capacity of lead farmers will be strengthened to enable them to provide tailored technical support. To support household resilience, the project will implement income-generating activities (IGAs) through skills development and the provision of startup capital. Vulnerable members of the community will be supported with cash based emergency lifesaving assistance. The project aims to reach 7,500 individuals—IDPs, returnees, and host community members—distributed evenly across the three counties (2,500 per county). CAFOD sees its localization approach as a key technical advantage. As such, the community will play an active role in the selection and targeting of beneficiaries. Project management committees, including representatives from all sections of the community (IDPs, returnees, host community members, women, men, youth, and persons with disabilities), will be formed to oversee the targeting process. Clear vulnerability targeting criteria will be developed in collaboration with these groups. CAFOD will also apply its SADI (Safety, Access, Dignity, and Inclusivity) Framework to ensure the inclusion of vulnerable and hard-to-reach groups, such as female-headed households, persons with disabilities, and elderly individuals. At least 10% of the benefiting households will include persons with disabilities.
