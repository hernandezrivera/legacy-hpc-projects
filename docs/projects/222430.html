Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to enhance the protection of civilians and resilience of communities in South Sudan. Civilians throughout the country have been severely affected by the existing humanitarian crisis resulting from recurrent violence, insecurity, and natural disasters. To alleviate suffering and increase the protection and security of affected and vulnerable communities, NP will directly implement activities utilizing a participatory model that seeks to further build on local protection capacities, social cohesion, and resilience as well as prevent and respond to pertinent protection concerns. All project activities will be designed to best respond to the immediate security needs of beneficiaries and support the resilience, adaptive capacity, and recovery of communities from violent conflict, insecurity, and environment shocks utilizing NP’s Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) methodology. The project’s three programming components which will center upon: 1) General Protection 2) GBV and 3) Child protection.
Nonviolent Peaceforce (NP) will utilize a participatory approach in order to prevent and respond to protection needs, including gender-based violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) concerns. All project activities will be designed based on NP’s methodology of Unarmed Civilian Protection (UCP) to respond to the immediate protection needs of affected populations as well as support the resilience, adaptive capacity and recovery of communities from violent conflict and insecurity.
Project activities are implemented in both static locations, as well as through mobile response missions. NP also recognises the specific and unique needs of women, men, boys, girls, elderly and Persons with Disability (PwD). As such, NP has made use of the community feedback/information in the design of the proposed project. During project implementation, concerns of all community members will be captured and disaggregated by age, sex and PwD, and will be constantly reviewed to adapt the project.

1. Needs

Despite the implementation of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in 2018 and the formation of the Transitional Government of National Unity (TGoNU) in 2020, South Sudan continues to face significant challenges. Intra- and inter-communal violence, clashes between armed forces, and ethnic tensions remain persistent threats to the protection of civilians. Recent incidents, including unrest involving the former intelligence chief and ongoing divisions among the security forces, highlight the fragility of the peace process. The postponement of elections to December 2026 further underscores the political instability and challenges in implementing critical reforms. The humanitarian situation remains dire. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 9.3 million people are in need (PIN), with 5.4 million targeted for assistance, requiring $1.71 billion in funding for 2025. This includes severe food insecurity, driven by conflict, climate shocks, and economic decline, leaving millions on the brink of famine. Protection concerns are high, with vulnerable groups such as women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation. Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to rise, exacerbated by entrenched gender inequalities and harmful cultural practices, such as child marriage, which is often used as a coping mechanism in times of economic hardship. Flooding, described as the worst in 60 years, has displaced hundreds of thousands, destroyed livelihoods, and increased the prevalence of waterborne diseases. These climate shocks compound the already critical humanitarian needs, with many flood-displaced individuals unable to return to their communities due to ongoing violence. The lack of unified security forces and continued ethnic tensions further increase the vulnerability of displaced populations. Economic instability is another pressing concern. A depreciating currency, declining oil revenues, and the taxation of humanitarian aid have led to unpaid salaries for civil servants and reduced the ability of humanitarian actors to provide much-needed assistance. Youth disenfranchisement, high unemployment, and lack of livelihood opportunities have also contributed to violent strikes and protests against humanitarian organizations, hindering access to affected populations. Gender disparities limit the participation of women and girls in societal processes, exposing them to violence and exploitation. Harmful gender norms and the militarization of masculine behaviors contribute to cycles of violence and further entrench inequality. Survivors of GBV, including men and boys, face stigma and a lack of survivor-centered services, creating additional barriers to accessing support. The delay of elections to 2026 perpetuates political tensions, instability, and volatility, while recurring crises and protracted emergencies continue to strain resources and exacerbate humanitarian needs. Violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and restricted access hinder humanitarian response efforts. Spillover effects from the ongoing Sudan conflict further destabilize South Sudan and exacerbate vulnerabilities. Limited political will and weak institutional structures hamper the effective delivery of services and reforms. Climate change continues to amplify the frequency and intensity of natural disasters, compounding the country’s vulnerability. Addressing these interconnected challenges requires a multifaceted approach that combines immediate humanitarian assistance with long-term development and peacebuilding efforts.

2. Project activities

Nonviolent Peaceforce (NP) proposes a comprehensive protection program to address General Protection (GP), Gender-Based Violence (GBV), and Child Protection (CP) needs of vulnerable populations, including refugees and returnees from Sudan. Leveraging NP’s expertise in protection monitoring, coordination, and capacity-building, the project aims to reach 12,855 targets across 15 counties through mobile and static protection services. CO1: Deliver tailored mobile and static protection services to mitigate risks, focusing on age, gender, and disability.CO2: Strengthen coordination mechanisms and promote human rights through effective protection monitoring.CO3: Transition from emergency assistance to sustainable systems that address vulnerabilities and exclusion. General Protection (GP): NP targets 5,500 through:1. GP1: Conduct regular protection risk monitoring using Protection Risk Monitoring System (PRMS) tools. Activities include protective patrols, key informant interviews, focus group discussions, and observational assessments in high-risk areas. Target: 150 protection monitoring reports.2. GP3: Implement 50 activities on awareness raising and information sharing related to community-based protection activities, including psychosocial support (PSS), Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), peacebuilding initiatives, Early Warning and Early Response (EWER), and community resilience-building. Target: 50 Activities3. GP4: Identify and refer individuals with protection concerns, such as survivors of violence, unaccompanied minors, and persons with disabilities, through static and mobile teams. Target: 5,300 participants. Gender-Based Violence (GBV): NP targets 3,255 through:1. GBV1: Conduct 90 awareness sessions (6 per county) targeting women, men, boys, and girls. These sessions will address prevention, risk mitigation, and available GBV services. Target: 90 activities.2. GBV4: Deliver psychosocial support services, including recreational and social activities, trauma healing sessions, and peacebuilding initiatives, with 6 sessions per county (30 participants per session). Target: 2,700 participants.3. GBV8: Establish and update GBV referral pathways to connect survivors to multisectoral services, including health, psychosocial support, and legal aid. Target: 15 referral pathways (1 per county).4. GBV9: Train community leaders, humanitarian workers, GBV partners, and government staff on GBV prevention and response, focusing on survivor-centered approaches, referral mechanisms, and accountability frameworks. Target: 450 participants (1 session per county, 30 participants per session). Child Protection (CP): NP targets 4,100 through: 1. CP4: Facilitate child-safe spaces (both static and mobile) to promote psychosocial well-being, play therapy, and resilience among children affected by displacement and conflict. Target: 4,100 children2. CP5: Conduct awareness sessions for children and caregivers on child protection, counseling, and positive parenting, with a focus on high-need areas like Jonglei and Upper Nile. Target: 50 sessions/Activities.3. CP 6: Women and men (community leaders or humanitarian, CP partners and government social welfare staff) trained on Child Protection in Emergencies (CPiE). Target 100 Staff. NP will implement the activities using 11 static teams and 4 mobile teams to ensure access to underserved and high-risk locations. Operational guidance from the Juba office will support timely execution, while data collected through protection monitoring will inform evidence-based programming and advocacy. Note: Targets in this proposal are activity-based indicators, not solely beneficiary numbers. For example, referrals are counted as the number of activities, not beneficiaries, while participant numbers are calculated for activities like trainings. This approach is in line with sector targeting modality to maintain uniformity.
