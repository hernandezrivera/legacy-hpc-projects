Project Summary

The project will provide WASH assistance at IDP in camps and community level as well as in health facilities and schools thereby enabling beneficiaries improved access to safe drinking water, enhance their knowledge on good WASH practices to reduce the prevalence of diarrhoea and water-based diseases that have detrimental impacts on nutrition security. This will be achieved through a combination of interventions such as construction/rehabilitation of water points, formation and training of WASH COMs and building capacity of government authorities to effectively provide lifesaving assistance and have improved capacity to meet the immediate needs of newly displaced or returning populations. The provision of WASH emergency services will prioritize a gender-sensitive approach and include the establishment of safe water supply to affected populations; establishing emergency sanitation and essential items (hygiene kits) that are established in tandem with hygiene promotion sessions. Throughout the project, the active participation of women in the design, implementation and sustainability of interventions will be ensured and protection needs of women and girls will be identified and integrated into programming. ACF WASH Rapid response mechanism is cover all accessible location in Borno and Yobe state according to the emergency needs and request. But consolidated package of WASH will be targeted in Jere, MMC, Mongnu, Mobbar(Damasak), Kakga and Damboa with remote management for Nganzai and Megumari in Borno state and Yunusari, Geidam, Gujba and Ngngare LGAs for Yobe state.

1 - Needs

An estimated 3.5 Million population are in WASH needs (HNO 2020). The displaced population reside in makeshift structures, within overcrowded settlements, which have inadequate drainage systems and latrines making them prone to outbreak of water-borne diseases, especially during the rains and seasonal floods. According to the August 2019 Interagency Flood Assessment report, 10,490 shelters and 550 WASH facilities were damaged affecting 21,056 households in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe states which include loss of food, agriculture and livestock. IDPs will require long-term support to establish permanent latrines and sanitation structures. In Borno State, the withdrawal of security forces to consolidate and form super camps at key positions has left areas that were once accessible exposed to attacks by Non-state actors and groups (NSAG). The new security development is likely to cause an influx of IDPs from areas that are no longer under the protection of security forces. The number of IDPs in Borno has increased by 15,658 people and Maiduguri, that host the most IDPs, experienced a 7% increase of IDPs since the May 2019 IOM displacement tracking matrix (DTM ). In Borno state, the IOM Emergency DTM recorded the largest movement of 986 individuals on 10th September, which included departures from Magumeri, Monguno, Gubio, Nganzai, Diffa, Kondguga, Gubio, Bauchi, Magumeri, Geidam LGAs. Moreover, protection and gender concerns are likely to heighten as settlements get more crowded. According to gender assessment conducted by CARE International, the SGBV against women and girls, has increased by 7.7% since the conflict began. It further reveals that sexual violence occurs at the toilet or latrines (21.8%), while travelling outside of the camp/community for wood collection (21%), at the water point (11.9%) and on way to/from schools (15%). These risks are predicted to heighten if super camps are established, increasing the number of people and competition of needs within settlements and camps

2 - Activities

Activity 01: Improved access to safe drinking water supply for the affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state for 70,000 individuals.In close coordination with the Government and the WASH sector-working group, the response will integrate protection concerns and adopt a gender-sensitive approach to adequately determine the needs of the male, female, boys and girls. Populations will be provided with access to a safe water supply (water trucking /quick repair of water points with chlorination, new drilling and rehabilitation of 50 boreholes with solar pumping system/hand pumps) that prioritizes first distribution to vulnerable groups (women, children and elderly people). Water quality testing including for residual free chlorine will be undertaken for all water points to ensure safe quality water sources. Where every possible 100 WASHCOMs will be formed and trained with 50% of females in the committee to ensure the needs of the women are considered during the implementation. Activity 02: Improved access to sanitation service for the affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state. Multi-unit 200 block communal latrines will be constructed/rehabilitated in IDP camps. While in communities where returnees, host communities and IDPs have been displaced for extended periods, the project will focus on 3400 household latrines. The approach will ensure that community mobilizers and project staff integrate gender-related dimensions to ensure women and girl’s participation while developing community action plans. Beneficiaries will commit their resources, including locally available materials to construct latrines. Local artisans and skilled technicians will be trained to build standard structurally sound latrines. The design will ensure that pour-flush/VIP latrines for health facilities and schools are accessible to people with disabilities and take into consideration the needs of women, children and elderly. The structure will include ramps, be child-friendly, have hand railings, be lockable, gender-segregated and have containers for menstrual hygiene management. Activity 03: Improved access to hygiene promotion and 7,000 gender-sensitive hygiene kits for the affected population in host communities and IDPs camps in Yobe and Borno state.The interventions target hard to access areas with new arrivals in locations with limited coverage of critical lifesaving WASH services, emergency hygiene and sanitation sessions conducted to demonstrate measures such handwashing, good hygiene practices and the safe disposal of waste, to mitigate against the spread of waterborne disease. An experienced consultant and trained ACF staff will undertake a study on the barriers communities face to carry out hygiene practices. This will inform the selection of messages highlighted in the Behavioural Change Communication strategy that will be launched in partnership with the Rural Water and Sanitation Management Agency (RUWASA). Handwashing stations will be established next to constructed latrines and utilized during emergency hygiene sessions covering personal hygiene, domestic hygiene, the proper use of 7,000 hygiene kits and WASH infrastructure. The project will also commemorate world days of significance such as Global Hand Washing Days, Menstrual Hygiene Management Day, World Water Day and World Toilet Day.Rapid Response Mechanism:The RRM will be activated for the provision of emergency WASH support in a scenario where existing partners are unable to provide critical lifesaving emergency support as well as when there is a surge of new arrivals reported in the IDPs camps. In close coordination with the Government and the WASH sector-working group, the RRM will be deployed.
