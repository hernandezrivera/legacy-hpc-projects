Project Summary

Michika and Magalai Local Areas are among the areas affected by the ongoing fight between Nigerian Army and non state actors since 2009. Michika and Madagali were overran by the suspected boko haram members in September 7, 2014 and since then children, young girls and boys, adolescents boys and girls, women, men and the elderly were affected psychologically. The greatest percentage of those affected are children and adolescent, young girls were forced into child labour and are being abused sexually, many are out of school and are still coping with the trauma they experienced while fleeing from the boko haram attacks.
Since the liberation of the two local governments prior to 2015 general election by the Nigerian army, many families has returned home but the trauma of the experience they have gone through is still affecting them; this necessitate the need for intervention in terms of s structured psycho social support services in other to help them cope and regain their resilience.
Children have been forced to flee all that is familiar to them and have experienced violence, fear and loss. Without appropriate care and psychosocial support these children will be forced to process their conflict experience alone, which may prevent them from being able to fully engage in daily activities such as playing, learning, or basic social interaction. Conflict affected and displaced children have been exposed to physical threats as well. Without protection and support their physical survival is threatened, and their status puts them at high risk of abuse, exploitation, forced labor, abduction, or recruitment into armed forces.

1 - Needs

This project will address the following assessed needs of Children in Michika and Madagali Local Government Areas of Adamawa State:i. Those children that are traumatically affected will receive a structured psycho social support services and counseling to help them cope with the situationii. Out of school children will be enrolled back to school through the structured psycho social services to be providediii. Children's resilience will be improved on iv. Children who are survivors of GBV and SGBV will receive a comprehensive case management support based on needsv. UASC will receive comprehensive case management supportvi. One stop centers to be established to cater for the health needs of survivors

2 - Activities

• Provision of focused, age-appropriate psychosocial support services: Recognizes the need to prioritize the provision of comprehensive, empowering, community-based, psychosocial support services, including community-based age-appropriate activities support, the promotion of positive coping mechanisms and resilience, and counseling. Experienced consultants, volunteers/actors on GBV will promote the increased availability of community-based psychosocial support services for GBV child survivors at dedicated private spaces, and with appropriately trained and resourced staff, in line with the guiding principles and adequate case management as part of the response. Individuals shall be supported with case management and referrals and mainly women and girls receive a range of skills building and basic livelihood support services. Psychosocial interventions shall be standardized and context-specific informed by the minimum standards set forth in both the IASC GBV Guidelines and the IASC Guidelines for MHPSS in emergencies. • Facilitating social accountability mechanisms with law enforcement agencies, legal and justice actors to remove barriers to accessing justice for GBV and SGBV survivors and promoting the accountability of perpetrators• The rights based systems approach will be implemented in a manner that integrates programmes to holistically address the development needs of children. • Obtain, furnish and maintain “One-Stop Care Centers”. In recent years, the ‘one stop centre’ model for attending to survivors of violence against women and girls has garnered attention in development settings as an exemplary approach to facilitating comprehensive and ethical care for survivors. The “One-Stop Care Centers” (minimum initial service package (MISP) also appears to have accelerated as a method for scaling up quality services during post-conflict reconstruction and recovery.
