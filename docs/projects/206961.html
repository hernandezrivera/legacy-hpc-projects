Project Summary

This project will be targeting 20,500 vulnerable children, directly and indirectly, from IDPs, returnees, and the host community from the Southwest region. It aims to support children and young people affected by armed conflict and will contribute to the Cluster Objectives for 2024, taking into consideration the Cluster Response Strategy priorities for 2024. Children bear the brunt of the ongoing humanitarian crisis in the North-West and South-West regions. Psychosocial distress and mental disorders and family separation continue to be the main child protection concerns as per the HRP 2023., Early and forced marriage has increased during the conflict as families use it as a coping mechanism to alleviate economic pressures. With the disruption of routine activities, change of environment, dropping out of school and missing out on friends, distress increases among both boys and girls. Therefore there is a need for psychosocial support using creative structured age and sex appropriate activities. This project will alleviate the impact of the conflict on children within the south West region of Cameroon through the provision of targeted psychosocial support, family tracing and reunification services targeting children and caregivers with an aim of rebuilding ability to reconnect and cope positively. Caritas Kumba proposes to build the capacity of 120 (60 female) community social services providers, including local authorities and community leaders (teachers, social workers, clergy, etc.) and strengthen their ability to protect, prevent, and respond to the core rights violations of children, especially those affected by the conflict, and support their participation in peacebuilding activities and community-based referral pathways. There shall be the provision of comprehensive case management for 250 of the most vulnerable children, including UASC, CAAFAG, children suvivors of GBV etc. Provide parenting skills session to caregivers and community members based on existing tools: Conflict-affected and displaced children risk becoming involved in the worst forms of child labour such as child trafficking, hazardous work or begging in the streets. Through training foster caregivers, Caritas Kumba will increase the level of children care and protection. This is in realization of the fact that children outside parental care are at more risk of exploitation. Support community-based child protection Mechanisms to engage in child protection activities:The project has considered gender and age in planning and implementation. Boys and girls with special needs are targeted. The project will provide life skills and resilience programming to adolescent girls and boys who will be supported to organize peer support activities with children. Women and men will be duly represented in all decision-making organs to provide avenues for maximum input from all groups who will be encouraged to participate in every component of the project with the dynamics of the gender roles in mind.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The crisis continues to have devastating effects on the people of North-West and South-West regions especially boys, girls, and their caregivers. According to the latest displacement figures, some 831,138 individuals are displaced in both regions due to insecurity and violence. While most of the population in the region is vulnerable, children are particularly exposed to different types and forms of protection risks. Most importantly, trauma and the psychological impact of the violence is directly affecting their emotional and cognitive development and remains one of the highest priority needs in 2024. Most localities have had their councils moved from the villages to central towns, or simply disappeared. People remaining in those villages cannot access registration services for civil documents such as birth certificates for children or identity cards for youths. Consequently, many children do not have birth certificates. This may be because of birth registration workers having abandoned their duty stations in rural areas for safety in urban centers. Additionally, civil documentation such as birth certificates may have been burnt during attacks against their homes by parties to the crisis, or lost while fleeing to safety. Family separation is a major threat. Given the fact that education is one of the contested issues by NSAGs, and the consequent closure of several schools, many families decide to send their children to urban areas, where they can more easily access education. This choice exposes children to further protection risks. Among the displaced children reported in 2022, 2,919 were unaccompanied and 19,092 separated, with little alternative care solutions in place. In addition, children engage in dangerous child labor activities, such as underwater sand harvesting, street hawking, and working as farm hands in agricultural plantations for economic purposes. Some are exploited and coerced to engage in transactional sex, early marriage so that the dowry obtained can help the parents make ends meet. Some are recruited by NSAGs or decide to join them because of lack of alternative socio-economic opportunities to survive. There is call for immediate efforts to respond to the situation, including psychosocial support services, case management services and the strengthening of community-based structures to prevent and respond to child protection risks The project will ensure quality programming, inter-cluster synergies, Accountability to Affected Populations, regular context analysis and advocacy on environmental conservation, ensuring conflict-sensitive programming and adhere to the principle of Do No Harm.
