Project Summary

The overall health situation in South Sudan continues to deteriorate, especially in conflict-affected and seasonally hard-to-reach areas due to persistent drought, continued insecurity and other underlying factors. This project will strive to address emergency health needs of marginalized and underserved communities in Panyijiar which includes Ganyliel and Nyal payams with an aim of targeting 19,847 people (25% of 79,388 - the population in Panyijiar). The project aims at improving access and utilization of integrated primary healthcare services, including reproductive health and immunization coverage through support of static facilities with mobile response. Mobile service delivery will concentrate in areas not currently served by static facilities, thus increasing access to health services for marginalized communities. The IRC has been one of the largest providers of aid in South Sudan, delivering emergency relief and post-conflict assistance for over 25 years.
IRC is supporting healthcare services in Panyijar. Under the proposed program, two static facilities (a Primary Health Care Centre (PHCC) and a Primary Health care unit in Nyal) and two mobile teams (one in Nyal, one in Ganyliel) will be supported. Each mobile team will be composed of one Clinical Officer, one Nurse, one Community Health Worker (CHW), one Maternal and Child Health Worker (MCHW), one Drug Dispenser, one Card Registrar and one EPI vaccinator. The Clinical Officer and the Nurse will be delivering health care services to patients while the CHW will be involved in health awareness, case identification and referral in the community. CHWs will also conduct community dialogues to identify key health issues in communities. Community dialogues will target different age groups within the communities and at different times. Critical patients attending the mobile clinics and pregnant women with obstetric complications will be referred and managed at the PHCC. Pregnant women in need of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CEmONC) will be referred from the PHCC in Nyal to the IRC-run Reproductive Health Unit in Ganyliel. These facilities are supported through HPF funds except Nyal PHCC.
Support for Nyal PHCC and PHCU under this program may include, but not be limited to recruitment, payment and training of healthcare workers, provision of essential equipment and supplies, and regular supportive supervision. The CHW will be involved in identifying vulnerable and needy community members with other non-communicable diseases including mental health needs and referring them to the nearest point of care. The IRC will integrate prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) of HIV/AIDS and syphilis testing during antenatal care (ANC) visits .All HIV positive cases detected during PMTCT services will be recorded and referred appropriately.
Gender-based violence is linked to a variety of negative health outcomes for women, including severe reproductive and maternal health consequences, like unintended pregnancies, abortions, maternal and neonatal mortality. This program will provide services to GBV survivors through a survivor-centred approach where the survivor’s rights, needs, and wishes are prioritized and respected at all times. A key means to addressing these issues is the support of functional referral systems and training of health providers, ensuring privacy and confidentiality. The expected increase in service provision is likely to increase the amount of waste, mostly sharps. Facilities have sharps boxes, and the 2 major referral facilities have adequate final disposal systems for infectious waste. IRC will ensure that all facilities dispose of sharps and other waste correctly and guidelines are in place and followed. This will also be integrated into supervision support. The mobile team’s medical waste will be taken to the nearest PHCU for incineration, while non-medical waste is disposed of as appropriate.

Needs

Humanitarian needs in South Sudan have been driven by a variety of hazards, including armed conflict, drought and economic and political instability. This has led to poor access to basic health services and infrastructure, recurrent disease outbreaks, food insecurity and high malnutrition rates, lack of safe water supplies and significant rates of population displacement (Source: South Sudan Health Cluster Response Plan for 2018 HRP – January to December, 2018). These continuous and emergent crises negatively affect health outcome indicators, which are some of the worst globally. These include a maternal mortality ratio of 730 per 100,000, infant mortality rate of 75 per 1,000 and under-5 mortality rate of 104 per 1,000 live births. Only 6.2% of children under 5 years of age are fully immunized. This is compounded by lack of health facilities, with only 44% of the population having access to health services. In Panyijiar the health system's capacity to respond to crises is limited as a result of a deficit in central government support due to the county being held by the opposition forces, coupled with already inadequate infrastructure, lack of quality delivery, emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. Over half of the women in South Sudan deliver at home without skilled birth attendance. Health system challenges affecting maternal care include shortage of qualified health workers, lack of supplies and equipment, poor health worker competencies and weak referral systems. Socio cultural and economic barriers also limit access and utilization of primary healthcare services. In addition to the above, the ongoing conflict has disrupted routine vaccination programs leaving large populations unvaccinated, thereby increasing the risk of measles and other communicable disease outbreaks. The consequent limitation in access to health services and ongoing displacement has further led to the diminished vaccine coverage and subsequent increase in associated disease infections and mortalities. In Panyijiar it is estimated that only 20% of the population lives within 5km of a health facility with some communities traveling 7 hours to access a health facility. Coverage of treatments for the three main childhood diseases is extremely low. The increasing number of IDPs cut off from service provision has worsened this already dire situation: most in Panyijiar live on islands with limited access to potable water, latrines, food and shelter. Existing poor water, sanitation and hygiene practices continue to increase cases of sporadic, endemic or outbreak diarrheal diseases.

Activities

• Outreach mobile teams in Ganyiel and Nyal: Staff will be recruited and engaged to provide integrated outreach health and nutrition services in Ganyliel and Nyal payams (one team per payam). The staff will include ( 2 Clinical Officers, 4 Nurses, 4 Dispensers, 4 CHWs, 4 MCHWs, 2 Clerks, 4 EPI Vaccinators) • Static facilities: IRC will engage two midwives at Nyal PHCC which provides CeMONC services and 2 EPI vaccinators at the PHCU in Nyal. This staff will support the existing services being provided. • Procurement of pharmaceuticals including reproductive health commodities and laboratory supplies: IRC will procure essential supplies and drugs to enhance quality of care. These will include health supplies and maternal and newborn care supplies to support provision of antenatal care, basic and comprehensive obstetric care. • Prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV – Support to HIV-positive pregnant women through provision of information and HIV care and support and referral where appropriate for further management  Train 10 midwives on PMTCT: Training will reduce maternal and neonatal mortality. Conduct community dialogues before, during and after outreach to identify key health issues in communities and to address barriers to access. These community dialogues will target different age groups within the communities and at different times: married youth, unmarried youth, religious leaders including opinion leaders, women and men, boys and girls. To maximize mortality reduction and improvements in health outcomes, supply-side intervention need to be complemented with demand-side intervention. Hence community strategies will improve knowledge and address social and cultural barriers.  Training of CHWs (20) will be conducted on defaulter tracing for immunization, antenatal care clients and clinical care for sexual assault survivors (CCSAS). Community health workers (CHW) will be trained to track and support pregnancies and to refer for complications and delivery and they will also be involved in promotion of health education at the community level to contribute towards increasing demand of sexual and reproductive health services (SRH). Importance of CCSAS will also be emphasized.  Quarterly joint supportive supervision : The IRC and MoH will strengthen supervision, mentoring and follow-up to ensure that new knowledge and skills are put into practice, together with training and support for implementation of wider quality improvement interventions. Strengthening use of data to monitor and improve quality of care: Routine quality of care assessments will be conducted at the facility level. This data will be channelled through the MoH. Key staff will meet monthly to review data, follow up on progress towards implementation and to highlight any issues that need to be discussed. Exit interviews will be conducted to identify whether services are meeting the needs of clients who attend the facility and to identify areas to improve clients’ satisfaction. Furthermore, this information is required for monitoring the scale of services, and to assess the client profile by collecting more socio-demographic information about clients such as the level of education, marital and employment status, so as to better understand the type of client seeking different services and who is accessing the services to be able to see if there are client types (e.g. poorer people) that the IRC could try to target as well as assessing the extent health services are reaching underserved populations. This is one of the forms of getting feedback from the beneficiaries in order to improve quality of health services.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Information, education and communication materials developed will be translated in the local language and target specific age groups and gender. Hence messages will be tailored to target women, men boys and girls.

All staff will undergo an induction on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and will be required to sign a code of conduct that mandates they report any PSEA cases

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Consultations have been done with community volunteers and community health workers who are community members. Further consultations have been done with targeted groups within the communities, including women and men.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Room will be given to clients to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get the feedback from the community.

Community Engagement?

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Internally displaced persons and vulnerable groups stand the risk of not affording high quality health care because of their poor socioeconomic status, long travel distances, lack of properpublic transport system, insecurity and marginalization as a result of local clan dynamics. IRC will ensure services are provided to IDPs and vulnerable groups impartially and at no cost.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The main barriers to accessing healthcare in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health services.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Women and children remain the most vulnerable to health and nutrition shocks because of their multifaceted physiological needs. IRC’s interventions are tailored in such a way that they will address specific needs of women and children. Reproductive health services will be tailored specifically for women of reproductive age while EPI services will be for both children as well as pregnant and lactating women.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes health service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls and boys, without discrimination.
