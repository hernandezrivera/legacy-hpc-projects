Project Summary

The restrictive Israeli judicial system have not only directly contributed to the difficult living situation of Area C residents, but also make it difficult to carryout projects designed to alleviate these communities’ most pressing needs. For example, the construction or expansion of schools or medical clinics, the construction of suitable shelters, and the rehabilitation of water infrastructure all require that communities. It seems that defending those land through this system have reached to place where synergies between planning and legal aid is inevitable. Since the new planning and construction regulations that were adopted in 2020, continued like all the previous regulations to prevent Palestinian development and to steal Palestinian lands, using laws, military regulations, and procedures to stop future planning and to implement demolition orders. The Israeli authorities exploit their complete control in the field of planning and construction to almost prevent Palestinians completely from building in Area C in the West Bank. Saint Yves plans through this pilot intervention to enhance its legal aid success rate through providing urban planning to address household (HH)-level protection as complementary to its provision of legal assistance on house and land properties (HLP) to Palestinians affected or at risk of displacement in the WB area C. St Yves will hire an urban planner who will prepare urban building plans (UBPs), those plans can be used in court to assist Saint Yves legal expert in providing legal aid for people with demolition orders. Those plans are now a need as the Civil Administration (CA) – the branch of the Israeli military designated to handle civil matters in Area C – refuses to prepare outline plans for the vast majority of Palestinian communities there and the plans they usually approve were drawn up without consulting the communities and do not meet international planning standards.
St. Yves will aim at preventing the affected communities and structures from being demolished or confiscated. This will be reflected through providing legal aid including legal representation and urban planning to Palestinian residing in Area C of the West Bank, with the focus on the Southern parts of Area C. St. Yves will dedicate its personnel capacities and resources to provide legal aid to its beneficiaries while through this project particularly will assist in providing urban plans to specific cases which has been requested by the court for better appealing the demolition orders. These services will be provided to Palestinian displaced persons (IDPs) or /and refugees who are at threat of displacement and have financial hardship to afford legal representation and urban planning related to HLP violations.
This Action aims at providing protection and assistance to Palestinians at risk of forcible transfer in the West Bank due to recurrent violations of international law. Building on risks and vulnerability analyses, the Action addresses the protection of needs of persons and communities of concern through a multi-sectoral approach that reduces vulnerabilities, builds capacities and responds to threats. Through this Action, legal aid through submitting urban plans for threatened structures will be provided to 50 persons. St Yves will be the sole entity that will offer to cover the cost of urban plans for Palestinians through this pilot project. Through its assessment St Yves has been exposed to the fact that nearly no other organisation is offering the Palestinian people any contribution in solving the crisis of planning in order to help enforce the position of the Palestinian in Israeli court through appealing against demolition orders. thus, Saint Yves have decided in this project to pair its legal aid for against demolition order with urban planning for the purpose of defence.

1 - Needs

According to Restricting space report: The planning regime applied by Israel in Area C of the West Bank – OCHA special focus; In the majority of Area C villages, which have no Israeli civil administration ICA plans, limited Palestinian construction is theoretically permitted, but must conform to the narrow building possibilities allowed by the Mandatory Regional plans of the 1940s, which zone the majority of Area C as an ‘agricultural’ zone and are inadequate to deal with current needs. The Israeli authorities’ restrictive interpretation of these plans makes it virtually impossible for a Palestinian to obtain a permit based on them. According to Saint Yves assessment which was based on analysing the data base of demolition cases in area C:(2115) open cases in Saint Yves and the one-to-one interviews with the beneficiaries; all the data were coherent with the above-mentioned report that the odds of a Palestinian receiving a building permit in Area C – even on privately owned land – are slim to none. Given the futility of the effort, many Palestinians forgo requesting a permit altogether. Without any possibility of receiving a permit and building legally, the needs of a growing population leave Palestinians no choice but to develop their communities and build homes without permits. This, in turn, forces them to live under the constant threat of seeing their homes and businesses demolished.Furthermore, Palestinians in Area C face many difficulties to obtain adequate housing for themselves and their families, because of difficult financial situation and would be impossible to urban plan their area by themselves, According to MSNA 2022, 82% of people in West Bank stated that the reason for demolition is lack of permit. These difficulties in life for the Palestinians are evident through the multiplicity of bureaucratic systems that deal with it, including the legal system. The Israeli authorities alter planning and building laws and planning procedures in the West Bank to serve to prevent development, halt future planning and implement demolition orders; It does all of this as part of a political plan that aims to enable Israel to harness the maximum possible area of West Bank land for its needs, and in return it does everything in its power to reduce the land resources allocated to serve the needs of the Palestinian population.As the event “Impact of forced displacement on Palestinian women” held on the margins of the 66th Commission on the Status of Women (CSW) was convened virtually, Vice-Chair Gertze stated that “women bore most of the collective burden of such policies and practices, which resulted in forced family separation, displacement, threats to their physical integrity, arrests and detention of their children, as well as discrimination in almost every aspect of daily life. According to OCHA during the year 2022 until October 697 structures were demolished (including 105 donors funded) displacing 836 people (including 203 women and 407 children). Such measures had a disproportionate impact on Palestinian women who are the traditional caretakers of the home and the backbone of Palestinian family and community life, in violation of international law and UN resolutions, including Resolution 2334 and 1325,”. It is very important Women and girls in communities impacted by conflict and displacement, i.e. Gaza, Area C and East Jerusalem, tend to suffer from higher incidence of GBV, including more frequent incidents of intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and exploitation, and forced marriage. These communities also tend to be characterized by limited availability of and access to multi-sectoral protection services.According to the latest Humanitarian Needs Assessment (HNO); an official data released by the Israeli authorities indicate over 11,000 demolition orders – affecting an estimated 13,000 Palestinian owned structures, including homes – are currently ‘outstanding’ in Area C of the West Bank. These orders heighten the vulnerability of thousands of poor Palestinian households, some of whom are at imminent risk of forcible displacement. The New Planning and construction regulations that were adopted in 2020, continued like all the previous regulations to prevent Palestinian development and to steal Palestinian lands, using laws, military regulations, and procedures to stop future planning and to implement demolition orders. The Israeli authorities exploit their complete control in the field of planning and construction to almost prevent Palestinians completely from building in Area C in the West Bank. In approximately 60% of this area, about 36% of the total area of the West Bank, Israel imposes this prohibition by defining large areas as "state land", "survey land", "firing zones", “nature reserves" and "national parks". This is done by including them within the spheres of influence of the settlements and the regional councils, or through the restrictions enforced on the lands seized by Israel between the separation wall and the Green Line. The odds of a Palestinian receiving a building permit in Area C – even on privately owned land – are slim to none. Given the futility of the effort, many Palestinians forgo requesting a permit altogether. Without any possibility of receiving a permit and building legally, the needs of a growing population leave Palestinians no choice but to develop their communities and build homes without permits. This, in turn, forces them to live under the constant threat of seeing their homes and businesses demolished. In conclusion, the winning time tactics that once helped the Palestinian in their demolition orders in Israeli court are of no use since 2020. Urban plans might be the substitute for that if Palestinians had the financial ability and legal aid expertise. Saint Yves has been providing the legal aid for decades and providing people with urban plans might be the missing piece of the puzzle to have better defence in court.

11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

St. Yves assist everybody with no discrimination to gender, race, colour, ethnicity, etc. While implementing any program of thematic work St Yves takes into consideration the instability in the political, economic, and social situation of our targeted areas. What St. Yves aims for is to benefit and value all men, women, and children, through preserving their rights As Saint Yves works with themes that are related to the personal life of the Palestinian people in Jerusalem; whether through their residency rights, freedom of movement or their rights of housing, land and property, St Yves deals with many household and personal issues which touches their life deeply and have a greater value as agent of change. St Yves works with the problems that varies from files related to family unification, child registration, residency, freedom of movement, home demolition and land confiscation, we are targeting everybody, with a major aim of preserving the rights of Palestinian inhabitants and strive towards fostering the development of a Palestinian civil society, that stands against the unjust legal system imposed by the Israeli government on Palestinian population in East Jerusalem and Southern West Bank. As an active member of the Legal Task Force (LTF), a sub-cluster AoR under the Protection Cluster we are able to flag humanitarian needs beyond the scope of our organizational mandate and the boundaries of the proposed intervention, as the LTF systematically relays developments and trends that we monitor in the field to the Protection Cluster, to assign to other AoRs (i.e. GBV, Child Protection & MHPSS), as well as other Clusters (i.e. Shelter, WASH, Education, etc.). Moreover, when a multilateral response is required, we will ask that the issue be dealt with the ICCG, and when legal / planning remedies are nearing exhaustion, we will ask the HCT AWG to engage in advocacy. Hence, via the LTF, we are able to maneuver the UN Cluster System, and navigating the humanitarian architecture in the oPt is essential to ensure that we can provide a comprehensive protection response for our beneficiaries. Persons with disabilities in Palestine face disproportionate socio-economic marginalization, resulting in poorer health and medical treatment, lower quality of education, limited employment prospects and generally broad-ranging restrictions on their community participation. These negative outcomes are exacerbated by barriers to access to justice specifically experienced by persons with disabilities. Access to justice, as a fundamental right in itself and as a precondition of the enjoyment of all other rights, is especially crucial for this category of vulnerable persons, and provides a unique tool to counter the discrimination. Paradoxically, however, those who need effective access to justice most are the ones most frequently encountering barriers to itOur focal point who will be attending all disability inclusion training and sessions will take in consideration that lawyers involved in providing legal assistance and representation can effectively contribute to defeating any barrier to access of justice. Protection of the rights of persons with disabilities needs to be incorporated explicitly among the core inspiring principles of any policies. Accordingly, lawyers involved in advocacy and law reform will have a moral and legal obligation to sponsor this approach.
