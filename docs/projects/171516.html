Project Summary

Under this intervention, ACTED will assist 300 households, representing 1,410 individuals, with emergency shelter interventions, including demolition response and shelter rehabilitation. As a result, the proposed intervention is in line with the Shelter Cluster’s Objectives (2) "Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity," through the shelter rehabilitation and winterization for 100 households; and (3) "Increase capacity to respond to natural or manmade hazards in a timely, efficient and effective manner," through the provision of demolition responses for 200 households. Both Shelter Cluster Objectives are directly linked to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objectives 2 and 3.
In light of fast increasing demolition trends, the COVID-19 pandemic and worsening weather conditions in Palestine due to climate change at a global level, ACTED proposes to cover emergency shelter needs of 200 households in the West Bank including East Jerusalem. The intervention is complementary to other existing programs as mentioned below and will ensure ACTED’s capacity to cover any gaps in emergency responses for demolition and other man-made or natural disasters in 2021.The action will follow the same processes as those established under the West Bank Protection Consortium’s emergency response mechanism, which has been led by ACTED since 2015.
Additionally, ACTED will target the 100 most vulnerable households in 10 communities located in the Ramallah governorate with emergency shelter rehabilitation assistance. The 100 households will be selected out of the 225 households showing bad shelter conditions as per Shelter Cluster’s August 2020 assessment, to ensure they can safely live through harsh weather conditions and can quarantine in shelters up to minimum standards in times of COVID-19 movement restrictions and lockdowns.

1 - Needs

While the COVID19 pandemic has tremendously slowed down all aspects of daily life and institutions’ operating pace throughout the world, it has not led to a decrease in demolition incidents. On the contrary, despite lockdowns, movement restrictions and curfews in both Israel and Palestine for the largest part of 2020, demolition incidents have been steadily and fast increasing. As of 15 October 2020, 290 demolition incidents were recorded, with over 560 structures demolished and over 3,600 individuals displaced or otherwise affected. If demolition trends continue at a similar pace, or further increase as they usually do in the second half of each year in comparison to the first half of the year, 2020 will become the year with the most demolition incidents ever recorded, far ahead 2016, which is considered as the historical peak of demolitions in the occupied Palestinian Territory. Due to the tense and fast-evolving geopolitical context, it is expected that such trends will continue to increase in 2021, thus reaching record-breaking levels of demolitions. As technical lead for demolition responses within the West Bank Protection Consortium and lead of the Demolition Working Group under the Shelter Cluster, ACTED expects that in the average case scenario in 2021, there will be 574 demolition incidents, affecting 1,123 structures and 15,243 individuals (including 3,085 individuals displaced). The current displacement rate following demolition incidents is reaching an alarming 20% of all affected individuals, leading to a need to provide rapid and quality emergency responses to cover affected households’ most urgent shelter needs. In addition, although no large-scale natural disasters occurred in Palestine since 2015, harsh weather conditions have been regularly damaging or destroying sub-standard shelters in the most vulnerable communities of the West Bank including East Jerusalem, especially in Area C. It is also anticipated that a major natural disaster would occur on the territory in the coming years, thus encouraging all implementing emergency partners to be prepared for the occurrence of such events. In August 2020, ACTED participated in collecting data for the shelter vulnerability assessment conducted by Shelter Cluster in order to update figures from the 2018 assessment. The assessment found that in the 18 assessed communities in the Ramallah governorate, 225 households were living in bad shelter conditions and needed urgent shelter rehabilitation interventions to ensure their shelters can resist to harsh weather conditions. These 225 households are located in 10 communities: Dar Faza’a, Al Karameh (Beit Nuba), Deir Abu Mashal Herders, Ein ar Rashash, Ein Ayoub Bedouins, Ras al Tin, A Mu’arrajat Centre, Deir Nidham Bedouins Al Hossa, Khallet al Maghara and Wadi as Seeq. COVID-19 further deepens pre-existing vulnerabilities for households who reside in substandard shelters (i.e. overcrowded, not respecting safety or dignity standards for households), as they are unable to quarantine in proper conditions during COVID-19-related lockdowns. Due to the COVID-19 crisis, which further worsened the most vulnerable households's economic situations, these HHs are lacking sufficient resources to cover their shelter needs. Ahead of the coming winter, in the most vulnerable communities, shelters are not prepared for harsh weather or poor households risk not securing tenancy through the winter. For lack of income, most families can neither upgrade their shelter to prepare for the cold weather, nor even afford basic shelter maintenance. Without timely emergency assistance, this could result in life threatening risks during the winter for the most vulnerable groups, in particular the elderly, the sick and infants. This situation constrains the most vulnerable households to resort to negative coping strategies, eroding their resilience over time, including resorting to debt to pay utility bills such as electricity, not paying those bills at all anymore, and failing on rent. Women and children are disproportionately affected by those vulnerabilities, as they impose additional protection threats on them. In particular, living in overcrowded shelters or in shared shelters constitute recognized risk factors of domestic violence. Similarly, failure to fulfill culturally sanctioned roles, such as housekeeping for women, heightens the risk of gender-based violence. For children and elderly people, poorly maintained shelters increase risks of diseases; while shelters with inadequate facilities further isolate people with disabilities and elderly people. In order to ensure fast and quality emergency responses to natural and man-made disasters in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, in a context of increasing demolition incidents and worsening weather conditions due to global warming, ACTED will respond to the most urgent shelter needs of 200 households under this Action, as a complementary intervention to other ongoing and planned programming in 2021. In order to address pre-existing vulnerabilities of the most vulnerable households located in the 10 above-mentioned communities of the Ramallah governorate, ACTED will assist 100 households with the rehabilitation of their sub-standard shelters. In total, the action will target 300 households, representing 1,410 individuals, with emergency shelter assistance.

2 - Activities

As technical lead for natural and man-made disasters within the West Bank Protection Consortium, ACTED will benefit from the contingency measures, community knowledge and strong cooperation developed through other programming to ensure this action’s efficiency, quality and sustainability. The strong synergies between the proposed intervention and existing mechanisms implemented by ACTED under WBPC will ensure full operational capacity from the first day of implementation. Activity 1: Rapid assessmentWithin 24 hours of a natural or man-made disaster, HH-level assessments will be conducted (with 1 male and 1 female to ensure specific needs are taken into account in the emergency response), following the same mechanisms as the demolition response. The assessment will complement already accessible information rather than duplicate it. In particular, it will focus on identifying the type of resulting humanitarian needs on the different affected groups (women, men boys, girls and the elderly) and will include a systematic risk analysis to ensure that assistance does not expose beneficiaries to further harm (demolition, flood prone area, legal risks, etc.). Household-specific socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities will be assessed through the technical assessment, thus identifying affected households under three categories: vulnerable households, at-risk households, and highly vulnerable households. In all cases, a specific attention will be paid to let women, children and the elderly and vulnerable groups express their specific concerns. Activity 2: Emergency Response to natural and man-made disastersACTED will respond to all eligible affected HHs within 7 to 14 days, based on identified needs. In terms of cash assistance (when in-kind is not feasible, ie high security concerns): - Beneficiaries who sustained minor damage to their shelter will be eligible to cash assistance for emergency shelter self repair up to a ceiling of 1,952 USD per HH; - HHs who lost their primary place of residence due to the shock will be assisted with cash assistance for rental subsidy for up to 3,366 USD;- HHs who lost personal belongings in the shock will be assisted with cash for loss of personal belongings based on the size of the HH, for up to 1,428 USD.Vulnerable HHs will be eligible for 50% of the total amount per category, at-risk HHs for 80% and highly vulnerable HHs will receive 100% of the amount. In terms of in-kind assistance, ACTED will provide eligible HHs with caravans, residential tents, residential metal sheds, block houses or other structures based on type of structure damaged/demolished and on type of structures present in the community. All designs were developed in close collaboration with the beneficiaries and to respect all HH members' privacy and security (especially women, children). Activity 3. Shelter rehabilitation in the Ramallah governorateBefore starting the activity, ACTED will hold meetings with local authorities of each community to present the project and ensure the local authorities' support. ACTED will then conduct information sessions in target communities, to explain the project's aim and activities to community members. Ahead of each technical assessment, ACTED will hold gender- and age-sensitive focus group discussions (with men, women, children, elderly people, and people with disabilities) to ensure differentiated needs are taken into account while designing rehabilitation works. Based on the Shelter Cluster’s August 2020 assessment, 225 households live in sub-standard shelters in the Ramallah governorate. ACTED will conduct technical and socio-economic assessments with these households to identify the 100 most vulnerable households. ACTED will then develop Bills of Quantities with each household, to identify needs to be addressed and set up a work plan for rehabilitations. At least 1 male and 1 female member of each household will participate in the development of BOQs to ensure that all specific needs are taken into account during rehabilitation works. Upon completion of the BoQs and signature of agreements with each HH, the 100 selected households will be provided with cash assistance (amount based on BOQ but estimated to 2,000 USD in average) in 3 instalments to proceed to the rehabilitation of their shelters up to minimum standards. Cash assistance is beneficiaries’ preferred method, especially women, as it provides them with more choice and flexibility, while safeguarding their dignity. In addition to being a cost-efficient method for humanitarian actors, the multiplier effect associated with injecting cash within the local economy will participate in restoring the local economy in the aftermath of the COVID-19 crisis. The first instalment (40%) will be released to all beneficiaries upon completion of the technical assessment. The subsequent instalment will be conditional on the effective implementation of the rehabilitation plan agreed with the beneficiary at the appraisal stage. The third instalment (20%) will consist in reimbursing funds to be advanced by beneficiaries, in order to increase leverage and control over cash usage. In exceptional cases where the beneficiaries would not be able to advance 20% of the needed funds, the project team could decide to provide all the needed cash in only 2 instalments (40%, 60%). The correct implementation of the workplan will be monitored through regular visits to each household by ACTED field engineers (3 visits minimum), who will review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel subsequent instalments. Those visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance. A Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted at the end of the project to assess beneficiary satisfaction (1 male 1 female interviewed per HH, to ensure differentiated perceptions are reflected).

3 - Indicators

1) Post intervention monitoring3 monitoring activities will be conducted, 15 days, 3 months and 6 months following the response. Each assisted HH will be visited by the project team field officers to assess the adequacy of the assistance to affected groups (women, men, boys and girls, and the elderly) needs and identify any new outstanding needs and risks that may have arisen from the incident. Specific attention will be paid to the concerns and needs of women and gender disaggregated data will be collected and analysed. 2) Post distribution monitoringACTED AMEU will conduct a post distribution monitoring after the shelter rehabilitation activity to ensure beneficiaries’ satisfaction and incorporate feedback and lessons learned in future interventions. 3) Complaint Response MechanismThe intervention will be supported by ACTED’s country wide Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) compliant CRM accessible to beneficiaries and non beneficiaries through several channels, including a phone hotline. The CRM will ensure that all complaints are recorded, responded to and handled according to their sensitivity. Beneficiaries will be sensitized on its function, process and usage, and will be encouraged to rely on it without fear of any adverse consequences. 4) Quality ControlThe action will incorporate strong quality control mechanisms at all stage of implementation. Random technical checks will be conducted to ensure items comply with the highest standards of quality. Field officers monitoring the delivery of items will be trained at identifying any quality issue, triggering an ad hoc inspection from ACTED’s chief engineer. Random post installation technical checks will be conducted on a regular basis. Monitoring outputs:The AMEU will be in charge of gathering and analysing all monitoring data in order to provide the following information products, in addition to measuring progress against indicators: 1) Humanitarian coordination mechanisms – All relevant data will be shared with relevant agencies involved in man-made and natural disasters monitoring and response, as well as existing monitoring frameworks (OCHA, Clusters, PA) 2) Yearly report the AMEU will produce a yearly report analysing trends and humanitarian impact of disasters, as well as lessons learned, success stories and recommendations. This report will be shared among humanitarian actors, in particular OCHA and the Shelter Cluster. 3) Referral mechanism: All outstanding needs not covered by the project will be referred to relevant clusters, through the channels and modalities established within the partners’ demolition response programme. This includes in particular victims of major damage, victims of livelihoods structure damage and vulnerable households unable to conduct self repair. 8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The project will be underpinned by ACTED’s programmatic approach of meaningful community engagement and participation of the population, in line with the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Five Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), namely (1) leadership/governance; (2) transparency; (3) feedback and complaints; (4) participation; (5) design, monitoring and evaluation. More specifically, AAP will be ensured throughout the intervention by taking account of, giving account to, and being held to account by the affected population as follows. ACTED will be held to account by the beneficiaries in line with Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) principles, by using ACTED’s Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM is overseen by ACTED’s AME Unit, which is independent from the delivery of activities. Beneficiaries can access ACTED’s CRM through a dedicated phone line, whatsapp, email address and complaints boxes as well as directly to ACTED staff during field visits. Through this CRM, beneficiaries can raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED and co-applicants’ work in a secure and confidential way, and receive a response in a timely manner. ACTED’s AMEU is in charge of handling this mechanism to provide timely response or adequate action to address any concerns. Having a well-designed and well-managed mechanism for handling beneficiary complaints will improve the quality of the action, enhance the trust and confidence of beneficiaries and identify areas of the project which need to be improved, and ensure that ACTED teams learn from the feedback provided through this process

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In all programming, ACTED ensures respect and promotion of the rights of all individuals and communities involved, in accordance with the relevant bodies of law (international human rights law and international humanitarian law). In order to take account of affected populations, the action will also take strong consideration of the needs and interests of beneficiary communities, particularly in regard to designing activities with their safety and dignity in mind. As part of protection mainstreaming, ACTED analyzes protection risks; anticipates possible consequences that may arise; and ensures that issues can be reported/referrals can be made to appropriate agencies/host government structures if required. ACTED is firmly committed to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality, and strives to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities do not inadvertently worsen tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance. To this end, all staff will be made aware of and in adherence to the Do No Harm principle as outlined in ACTED’S Code of Conduct and related policies including Child Protection; Anti-Fraud, Bribery & Corruption; Anti- Terrorism; and Policy Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Under this framework, ACTED has developed mechanisms that can contribute to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse such as: signing of the code of conduct by the staff in order to clarify unacceptable behavior and in particular sexual exploitation and abuse; information to the beneficiaries of existing remedies (Complaints and Response Mechanisms); implementation of training and awareness sessions in order to train and empower the teams and identify risk factors on an ongoing basis. Finally, all activities will be conducted in strict adherence of COVID-19 mitigation measures including social distancing and personal protective equipment (PPE) to minimise the risk of COVID-19 transmission among beneficiaries, staff and stakeholder alike. ACTED developed strong global tools for cash programming, ensuring transparency/accountability within all implementation stages, including beneficiary selection with clear criteria; distribution; and M&E. ACTED is currently implementing several projects through cash-based programming during the COVID-19 crisis and has acquired lessons learned and clear SOPs to proceed in a safe manner during the crisis.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Demolition response:The proposed action is based on a well-established mechanism that provides assistance to all eligible households affected by demolition or man-made disasters. As part of the mechanism, ACTED, along with other WBPC partners, has developed an in-depth socio-economic and technical assessment which allows to determine the vulnerability of each affected household, and needed assistance to be received based on the type of incident that household was affected by, and based on specific needs of the household. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting 3 monitoring activities with each beneficiary household to ensure quality of the response, beneficiary satisfaction, and record any complaints or comments from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries within communities of intervention. Shelter rehabilitation:In order to maximize the reach of the intervention, the project will adopt a deeply integrated protection approach (IPA) throughout all stages of the project cycle. ACTED will ensure impartial access to assistance through a strictly needs-based approach. After all, different needs lead to different dimensions of risks and resources for various groups, thus resulting in the necessity of a more comprehensive analysis. ACTED AMEU will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to needs, bearing in mind differing power dynamics. For instance, to ensure the agency of beneficiaries and further local ownership of project outcomes, the affected population will be actively consulted during the assessments to ensure BoQs and workplans are responsive to their self-identified most pressing needs. Where applicable, at least one male and one female per HH will be consulted to ensure gender-specific needs and concerns are taken into account, as well as those of boys, girls, PwDs, the elderly and other marginalized persons to ensure assistance reflects their unique vulnerabilities, including the design of shelter repair and/or adaptation works. Protection and privacy-related items will be prioritized for the shelter repair and/or adaptation works and shelter NFI and/or winterization assistance, taking particular consideration of those exposed to safety and security concerns. Items related to women and girls’ safety and privacy will be also prioritized. In addition, ACTED staff will be present to ensure the integration of HH concerns into BoQ and workplan design. In the event beneficiaries are dissatisfied with their respective BoQ and/or workplan an appeal mechanism will be offered through ACTED’s CRM. If ACTED staff consider the request justified, a new BoQ and/or workplan will be produced by a different engineer. All staff involved in the assessment process will undergo extensive refresher training in protection and shelter SPHERE standards prior to the assessments to ensure equal and impartial access to assistance. Detailed data disaggregated by gender, age and diversity will be collected to ensure a gender mainstreaming approach and measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups. ACTED staff will ensure that information is provided through accessible channels, formats, languages and community support networks to ensure that the beneficiary community are kept fully informed of the assistance available under the project. ACTED’s AMEU will also ensure equal and impartial access to aid by overseeing the CRM, as well as by conducting various verification exercises. For example, ACTED’s AMEU will conduct a verification exercise/post-distribution monitoring to ensure satisfaction, and all community members will be provided with the means to raise concerns regarding the selection process through ACTED’s CRM.
