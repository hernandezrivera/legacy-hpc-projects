Needs

The drought crisis has subsided, but the impacts on affected populations persist as living conditions for displaced families in sites continue to decline. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited as many displaced families have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services, which are concentrated in urban areas. People displaced to informal sites are not having their basic needs met either due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support due to poor targeting of sites. Site level coordination and management systems are required to ensure equitable access to services and protection for displaced persons.

Activities

Objective 1: Strengthen the predictability and effectiveness of multi sectorial services at site level through improved site management and coordination • Maintain CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterpart• Represent CCCM in relevant coordination forums• Ensure through CCCM coordination that relevant responders are mobilised towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable• Ensure the relevant Protection and Shelter responders are aware and take action with regards to HLP issues in sites, specifically regarding evictions. • Train partners and government counterparts on CCCM principles Objective 2: Improve living conditions of displaced people through site development, care and maintenance• Advocate with local authorities for public land for displaced people Objective 3 Support opportunities for displaced people to achieve durable solutions• Facilitate intention surveys and conduct through relevant CCCM partners and other clusters to ensure the intentions of populations are understood and supported• Ensure representation in the Somalia Durable Solutions Initiative and other similar coordination fora• Engage government authorities and community governance structures to ensure that the affected population are informed about their options on durable solutions

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The CCCM cluster recognizes the need for coordination and strategic complementarity between humanitarian partners, local authorities and development stakeholders to ensure that humanitarian programming links relief, recovery and development. IOM, as CCCM cluster co lead, will continue working closely with development actors at both national and field level to ensure that emergency projects support longer term solutions for both displaced and host communities. In particular, the cluster will create synergies with programming related to the Somalia Durable Solutions Initiative, as well as related consortiums, that aim to complement the humanitarian response with resilience programmes and longer-term actions that target IDPs as well as host communities. The cluster will work closely with relevant authorities and community governance structures to ensure that affected populations are informed about options for durable solutions and receive support to attain their plans.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The cluster will also ensure that partners develop and support diverse community governance systems to ensure the participation of all groups in decision making. The cluster will advocate for community cohesion projects that support host communities to ensure that humanitarian responses do not cause tensions between displaced and host communities.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The cluster will work closely with the protection cluster to develop strategies to mitigate exclusion in service delivery. The cluster will ensure that partners develop monitoring and complaint and feedback mechanisms to mitigate exclusion based on ethnicity, age, disability or gender

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The cluster will advocate for sustained humanitarian responses for populations displaced by conflict in the instances that durable solutions cannot be found.
