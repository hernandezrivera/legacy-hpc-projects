Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community
members) in Northeast and Northwest Syria. Through contractors, ACTED will rehabilitate private off-camp private shelter units in Ar-Raqqa, Aleppo and Idleb governorates. Ar-Raqqa governorate will also be targeted with Cash for repairs interventions to conduct small rehabilitation works under ACTED supervision and support. In addition, ACTED will address the needs of displaced households in IDP and last-resort sites, including newly displaced households, through the provision of tents, emergency shelter kits and tent insulation kits. Under this intervention, households will be provided with tents across Northeast Syria; while emergency shelter kits will be distributed in Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates. Idleb governorate will also be targeted with the distribution of insulation kits across the northern and western camp clusters, to better equip the residents for winter. In addition, NFI kits, composed of tarpaulin, jerry can, mattresses/sleeping mats, blankets (single and double), solar lamps, floor mat, stove, cooking pot, bowl for food, and cutlery, will be distributed across Idleb and Northern Aleppo governorates, as well as winterization support kits, including heating fuel, starting in December 2021. The intervention will target a total of 139,361 beneficiaries under shelter activities, and 231,665 beneficiaries under NFI activities.

4 - Needs

In 2020, over 2.2 million Syrians were in need of SNFI support in NWS and another 800,000 in NES, with high levels of need both in and out of camp. Due to lack of access to income, camp residents are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, particularly with regards to non-food items (NFI) and winterization, as limited purchasing powers means households must prioritize food and water at the expenses of NFI and shelter. As access to NFI markets has become increasingly challenging due to the Covid-19 crisis and depreciation of the SYP, with displaced populations among the most impacted groups.93% of shelters in communities surveyed by ACTED in NWS are damaged. Shelter destruction poses dangers to IDPs, especially in winter periods, as well as to residents and returnees who lack the economic means to conduct necessary repairs. Living in unsafe, overcrowded shelters pushes out-of-camp residents to resort to relocate to camp settings. In turn, extensive shelter damage massively discourages in-camp residents from returning to their location of origin. As existing camps face critical capacity issues, newly displaced households turn toward informal settlements, where access to NFI items is the most limited. This is compounded by continued displacement trends, particularly across NES. This highlights the need for both in-camp and out-of-camp SNFI support.IDPs in camp settings in NWS endured an exceptionally harsh 2020-21 winter. Recurrent heavy rain resulted in widespread flooding throughout NWS, affecting an estimated 122,953 IDPs. More than 300 camps and sites across Idleb and Aleppo were affected by the floods with 8,400 shelters destroyed and another 13,800 damaged.

5 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will implement a multi-faceted intervention. On the first hand, ACTED will focus on provide core and winterization SNFI items to conflict-affected IDPs and residents. ACTED will distribute heating fuel to some 150,000 camp residents across Areesheh and Serekaniye camps in NES, as well as the A’zaz camps and Idleb camp clusters in NWS. 77,500 residents across the western and northern camp clusters in Idleb will receive insulation kits. In addition, ACTED will provide emergency shelter kits and winterization NFI kits to 45,000 recently displaced IDPs in A’zaz and Idleb. On the second hand, ACTED will provide shelter rehabilitation support out of camp in order to provide residents with safe and dignified places to live. In NWS, ACTED will provide off-camp private shelter repair works across Aleppo and Idleb governorates, reaching some 1,250 beneficiaries. In NES, ACTED will provide home and shelter repair support through sub-contractor and cash-for-repairs modalities, reaching some 8,900 beneficiaries in Ar-Raqqa and Al-Hasakeh governorates. These activities will be supported by tent distributions for 240 recent IDP households in informal settlements across NES.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups’ needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach. ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct covering Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV), and ACTED implements Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects. Risk 3: Eviction of beneficiaries from the household upon the completion of the rehabilitation works. Likelihood: Medium – Impact: High. Mitigation Measure: A strong HLP due diligence process and MoUs with the rehabilitated buildings' owners will mitigate eviction risks and related conflicts. During the selection process, if the beneficiary household is the owner, ownership is verified by community verification and documentation if available; if beneficiary household is the tenant/hosted, the beneficiary household is the rightful tenant with rental contract or written hosting agreement; the ownership claim of the landlord is verified by community verification and documentation if available; and the landlord provides written consent for repairs and tenancy duration of at least 1 year from completion of repairs. Where possible, ACTED will intend to extend the duration further to ensure sustainability of the assistance, by piloting tailored design of MoU with landowner in order to reinforce sustainability of the agreement.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. In-camp, information on the CRM will be communicated to camp managers and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents; off-camp, to community leaders, local representative councils, and as much as possible directly to communities. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Theft: SNFI kits procured for the project could be stolen in transit to or from warehouses. Ground level storage spaces below residential properties are used as and when possible, warehouses are protected by a security guard 24 hours a day and keys to warehouses are solely in the possession of the Storekeeper who is responsible for all stock movements. Warehouse locations are carefully considered and when possible located outside of the town perimeter and near checkpoints for additional security. - Diversion of assistance: ACTED has robust and established SOP for its activities to track the assistance to ensure it reaches the targeted beneficiaries. In the planning of distributions, ACTED and partners will consult actively with key community leaders and local authorities to define an appropriate time and location for the distributions. ACTED and partners’ security teams will conduct thorough security assessments in each target area to identify secure distribution sites. Distribution schedules will be set up to ensure that there is only a small number of beneficiaries per day and the location of distribution site is close to the area where beneficiaries are. Additionally, a project development unit records beneficiary satisfaction and provides beneficiaries with the opportunity to report any instances of aid diversion or theft.- Continued price inflation: ACTED closely monitors the prices of SNFI kits components on a monthly basis, and uses USD when contracting with suppliers.- Covid-19 risks: In case of major outbreak in a specific location, ACTED activities will be reviewed, all non-essential activities will be put on hold, while contingency plan will be activated in areas where life-saving services are provided.
