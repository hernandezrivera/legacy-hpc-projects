Project Summary

This project aims to enhance access to legal and civil documentation (LCD) in Geidam, Gujba and Potiskum local government areas of Yobe State by offering information, counseling, and legal assistance (ICLA), and GBV services. The goal is also to strengthen the capacities of relevant government registration bodies to safeguard the rights of their respective communities. GSF as a lead shall implement the HLP component of the project and will target 10,000 directly with HLP services, 2000W, 3000M, 2000B, 3000G, moreover, PLCI as a sub-grantee will implement the GBV aspect of the project, targeting 400 survivors with case management services, 200G, 100B, 60W, 40M, more so, 6000 people will be reach with GBV awareness and outreach, 2000W, 2000M, 1000G, 1000B across Gujba, Geidam and Potiskum LGAs. moreover, counseling will be provided to those who self-identify as needing further support after the information sessions. Legal assistance to obtain LCD, including ID slips and birth certificates, will be facilitated by providing logistical support to the National Information Management Commission (NIMC) and National Population Commission (NPC). While NIMC currently operates in Geidam, Gujba and Potiskum, though, NPC operates only in primary healthcare and general hospitals, which are available in the proposed areas. The initiative aims to contribute to the sector response by improving access to LCD, thereby enhancing the protection environment for immigrants from Niger Republic to Geidam and internally displaced persons (IDPs) from outskirt communities to the towns of Buni yadi in Gujba and Potiskum LGAs. GSF current strategy is to be more responsive to displacement needs and provide services that support initiatives to build community resilience. A secure legal identity is considered a pivotal right for socio-economic development, guaranteeing the exercise of fundamental rights such as freedom of movement and access to basic services while reducing exposure to risks like harassment and extortion.
Although there are no formal IDP camp settings in Yobe currently, there are numerous IDPs and immigrants in camp-like settings and host communities, especially in Geidam and Gujba, requiring multi-sectorial services. For the proposed Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) project, GSF will maintain a continuous focus on increasing service provision by enhancing coordination among partners in sites facing significant gaps. Special attention will be given to ensuring that these sites are covered by GBV services. The response will also focus on supporting community engagement and governance structures to increase the meaningful participation of all community groups in decision-making.
Aligned with the Housing, Land, and Property Area of Responsibility (HLP AoR), GSF proposes establishing a community center to address out-of-camp displacement gaps through an area-based approach. The community center will serve as a platform for beneficiaries living outside camps to receive services and be referred to relevant service providers. Additionally, GSF will deploy a mobile outreach team to conduct awareness campaigns, referrals, and collect and receive feedback in different settlements. GSF in particular, will focus on building preparedness to prevent and respond to any reported outbreaks in sites where little or no support has been provided.

1 - Needs

Following a decade of conflict and displacement, Nigeria’s emergency, and recovery needs are extensive, complex and dynamic. The Northeast Nigeria crisis has largely been triggered by a regionalized armed conflict, and is first and foremost a protection crisis. Now coupled with the climate change impact especially recent flood that affected most of the communities in Geidam, Potiskum and Gujba and national economy inflation which led people to be too vulnerable and stranded to survive this difficult time. Although, there is influx of more than 5000 IDPs in the proposed locations while more than 2000 immigrant in Geidam were reported which makes them the LGAs with the highest number of IDPs living in an informal sites. These IDPs, many of which are new arrivals fleeing from conflict-affected areas, are usually living in vulnerable housing such as makeshift shelters, unfinished and abandoned buildings, sharing rooms with other families or renting houses. These communities who sometimes suffered a secondary displacement are scattered in the urban landscape, mixed with the host communities, and are more difficult to identify. They are therefore more vulnerable due to lack of services and protection in those areas. Currently there is no presence of any organizations working in those areas of HLP, with critical gaps particularly shelter, legal assistance including also significant protection concerns related to housing, land and property (HLP), gender-based violence (GBV). For these reasons, IDPs are also particularly vulnerable to extreme weather conditions such as cold or rains and floods, spread of communicable diseases, including Cholera, diphtheria, and HLP abuses. Pre-existing obstacles to birth registration and acquisition of national identity documents have been exacerbated by the ongoing crisis compelling populations to flee leaving behind or losing personal documents.There is the need to create conducive environment to enable access to legal and civil status documents to prevent human rights violations emanating from existing gaps such as the risk of statelessness and lack of access to basic social services including education, employment, access to financial institutions and other cash-based services. Solution-oriented actions aiming at strengthening the capacity of responsible Government entities (NPC and NIMC) for more sustainability in ensuring that no IDP, returnee and other affected population are left behind in the pursue of the sustainable Development Goals 16.9.Moreover, integrating 30% of GBV into this programming shall allow GSF and PLCI to address prevalent of GBV issues in Geidam, Gujba and Potiskum LGAs respectively, as there was report showing GBV cases in those camp-like locations led to menace of GBV concerns.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GSF and sub-IP will ensure that the project integrates protection measures across various sectors, aligning with the guidance from the Global Protection Cluster. In the health sector, efforts focus on ensuring non-discriminatory access to healthcare and integrating protection into health programs, particularly addressing gender-based violence and child protection. In education, protective measures will be implemented in schools, addressing issues like gender-based violence and discrimination. Livelihood and economic empowerment programs integrate protection considerations to mitigate vulnerabilities, especially for women and children. Shelter and Non-Food Items (NFIs) interventions prioritize safety and address specific protection needs related to housing. The Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) programs will integrate protection measures to address gender-based violence and ensure equitable access. Food security initiatives incorporate protection considerations to mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, especially for marginalized groups. Community engagement and participation will be promoted to ensure the active involvement of the community in decision-making, fostering empowerment and ownership. This holistic approach aims to comprehensively address the diverse needs and vulnerabilities of the affected population.
