Needs

White Nile State: WNS is one of the least developed states in Sudan with an estimated 56% of the host community living below the poverty line. Seventy-seventy percent of South Sudanese Refugees (SSRs) in WNS are female headed households forcing women to assume the double of caring for their family and providing an income for their families. While 64 percent of SSR in WNS are children under the age of 17 years. According to a UNHCR Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS) completed in April 2018,households’ main source of food security for refugees in the camps in WNS is general foodassistance which is provided on a monthly basis. The assessment found that the nutritional status of SSR in four refugee camps was ‘critical’, with the Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the 15 percent emergency threshold, while the nutrition status in the reaming camps was ‘serious’. The assessment found that dependency on negative coping mechanism is high across all the campswith between 83.6% to 97% of all SSR surveyed reporting that they were engaged in one or morenegative coping mechanisms. Agricultural production and livestock husbandry was the primary livelihood activity for most SSRresiding in WNS before their displacement. While SSRs are permitted to work, their access toland is limited and must be mediated through the host community. In addition, SSR women lives are constrained by cultural and social norms which restrict their mobility, access to information and services, and control over key decisions. Gedaref / Kassala: According to UNHCR more than 107,000 Eritrean and Ethiopian refugees are residing in Eastern Sudan including 89,000 living in nine refugee camps across Kassala, Gedaref and El Gezira States. The remaining 18,000 live in urban settings in Kassala, Girba, Gedaref and Port Sudan. The majority of refugees are Eritreans who fled to Sudan during Eritrea’s war of independence. In 2017, an average of 970 individuals per month arrived in Eastern Sudan. A needs assessment of Ethiopian and Eritrean refugees in Eastern Sudan completed by UNHCR in2017 found that almost half of camp-based refugees lived in poverty. Similar to the host community, the challenges protracted refugees face stem from harsh environmental conditions and limited opportunities to establish meaningful self-reliance. Specifically, refugees residing in camp settings lacked access to paid work. Refugees reported that employment opportunities were mainly open to nationals or that refugees were unable to satisfy the qualifications and certifications required. Limited access to microfinance is a further constraint on the development of sustainable livelihoods. Self employment opportunities were insufficient because the previously-available limited loan ceiling under the village savings and loan scheme had been suspended in the camps due to the repayment rate dropping below the 95 percent stipulated by UNHCR global livelihood minimum standards.

Activities

White Nile State: The proposed project will work on strengthening the livelihoods of the targeted groups to gradually reduce dependency on humanitarian aid and assistance with a view to increase the economic capacity of SSR households and support local integration. Efforts will be made to reduce vulnerabilities, increase and diversify sources of income, prevent negative coping strategies, facilitate access to the labor market, and create an enabling environment for livelihoods. Market driven vocational training; business start up support; women / youth business incubators; VSLA strengthening; vegetable farming; and solar drying initiatives will be piloted. Gedaref and Kassala: Mercy Corps will undertake a food security and livelihoods program that targets Ethiopian and Eritrean refugees and vulnerable host community members. This project will include market-driven vocational training opportunities, the provision of start-up kits/cash grants, basic business mentoring and market linkages.
