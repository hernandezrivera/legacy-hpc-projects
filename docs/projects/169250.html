Project Summary

The blast which ripped through Beirut has forced 300,000 people from their damaged homes. These individuals have already been strained with the multifaceted crisis facing Lebanon. The high levels of unemployment, depreciation of the Lebanese Lira’s value, and COVID-19 associated burdens, keep the devastated affected persons in no position to repair their homes when they are already having difficulties covering daily food and hygiene expenses.
Anera has set shelter rehabilitation as one of its priority responses to the blast, and proposes to rehabilitate the shelters of 4,000 vulnerable households. Anera’s proposed project will provide cash for work opportunities to youth to conduct HHs assessments. The project aims at responding to shelter needs at two levels. The first level targets 2500 FAMILIES living in apartments/houses lightly damaged with broken glass, doors, locks, collapse of false ceiling. The second level covers the repairs of 500 ASSESSED HHs requiring moderate repair (level 2) through targeting families living in apartments houses that are either not habitable or habitable with safety and security of the premises compromised)-non-structural damage repair. Based on Anera’s primary assessments, this project will fill a needs gap in the targeted zones of Burj Hammoud, Mdawwar, Bashoura, Ahsrafieh, Sin el Fil and Rmeil to ensure the equitable delivery of services to affected families.
The “Minor and Moderate (Level 1 and level 2) Rehabilitation of vulnerable Households in response to Beirut Port Blast” proposed project will complement Anera’s “Beirut Homes Rehabilitation Project” which includes level 1 shelter rehabilitation of 800 HHs, located in Bourj Hammoud, Karantina, Bashoura, Mdawwar, and Rmeil. The shelter’s emergency team has been able to assess the damages of 400 households, and repair 50 households few weeks after the blast. “Beirut Homes Rehabilitation Project” is jointly funded through Anera's private funding, through Anera's Youth cash for work program implemented in partnership with UNICEF (where youth surveyors, social workers, and construction graduates, are hired and have accomplished assessments under supervision of professional contractors, team leaders and technical experts), and through a generous grant from Muslim Aid USA. As the emergency teams have identified a high need for shelter repair, a higher number of financial and human resources are needed to fill the house rehabilitation gaps in the identified zones.
Anera’s early response to shelter rehabilitation through its well-positioned emergency and procurement teams ensure the efficient, and optimized workflow of the proposed 3-month project. The experienced shelter emergency teams including surveyors, foremen, technical advisors, and coordinators, are deployed to efficiently assess, implement, and monitor the shelter rehabilitation project. Anera’s shelter emergency teams will refer the identified damages in 500 water tanks to the WASH team. All shelter rehabilitation activities will be implemented in accordance with shelter sector guidelines, SPHERE, UNHCR SoPs, and other shelter standards. Through this project Anera ensures that the rehabilitation process restores the houses to their initial state to provide the affected families with privacy and emotional security.

1 - Needs

The August 4 explosion in Beirut forced some 300,000 people from their damaged homes. Over 6000 are injured, and more than 160 are dead, with hundreds people still unaccounted for. Damages to greater Beirut are currently estimated at upwards of $15 billion USD. Hospitals, already taxed by a spike in COVID-19 cases, have had to turn the less injured away. October 2019 also marked the beginning of economic and political crises in Lebanon that continue to this day. Since March, the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and its related economic challenges have further exacerbated the economic crisis in Lebanon. Due to these concurrent crises, Lebanon’s inflation rate has risen by 89.74% since October, the unemployment rate is estimated at 55%, and 52% of Lebanese and 83% of Syrian refugees are now below the poverty line. Anera’s emergency team has conducted a quick impact assessment to identify and target the most urgent needs facing the victims of the explosion. Anera has prioritized home rehabilitation, as one of its early response actions, and this is supplemented with a UN analysis, which states “displaced persons suffer significantly higher rates of mortality than the general population. They also remain at high risk of physical attack, sexual assault and abduction, and frequently are deprived of adequate shelter.” The overwhelming majority of internally displaced persons are women and children who are especially at risk of abuse of their basic rights.” According to Anera’s assessment, areas including Burj Hammoud, Mdawwar, Bashoura, Ahsrafieh, Sin el Fil and Rmeil have families who were already economically vulnerable prior to the explosion. These vulnerable individuals, already affected by unemployment, the depreciation of the LBP, and the reduced imports to Lebanon, are in no position to pay for repairs to their homes, especially with the need to cover daily food and hygiene expenses. Accordingly, Anera ensured to select the zones in these areas which have not been covered by other NGOs during the initial shelter response to fill a needs gap at the two levels: minor and moderate damages. Anera has initiated its shelter needs response through The Beirut Homes Rehabilitation Project, which is Phase One of Anera’s two-phase program to address short- and long-term housing needs in Beirut.Anera’s shelter emergency teams have been able to assess the damages in 400 HHs, and repaired 50 houses out of an 800 total target. As there is a high need for households’ rehabilitations at different levels in the different identified zones, this project is proposed to meet the needs of a higher number of affected HHs with a need to a higher number of financial and human resources. With over 300,000 people made homeless on August 4, the immediate need to provide as many IDPs with safety and resources to rebuild their homes as possible is paramount. This prioritization will address immediate security and safety concerns related to the displacement. Anera’s approach will maximize the number of people who will be able to return to their homes quickly, reducing the risk of opportunist developers trying to tear old buildings down. It will also lower the spread of COVID-19 during this current uptick in cases by getting people out of overcrowded shelter and host homes, lessening the pandemic’s strain on Lebanon’s inundated healthcare system.

2 - Activities

1. Identification of HH's: Anera will be identifying HH's through two modalities, (i) door to door or HH visits in pre-identified vulnerable neighborhoods and (ii) referred lists of HH's provided by different sources including local authorities, community members, and agencies belonging to different sectors. 2. Needs Assessment: Anera will be conducting a Needs Assessment covering socio-economic and shelter needs of HHs (RNA and NMSA). A total of 20 teams composed of youth trained surveyors and/or social workers, and construction workers will be conducting assessments simultaneously in different selected zones for over 300 HHs per day, over an average duration of 15 days. Being a UNICEF partner, Anera conducts its assessments in alignments with UNICEF's PSEA (prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse) policies, through ensuring that teams consist of both male and female members to emphasize protection against SEA. The needs assessment will be monitored by 10 YOUTHS (SITE SUPERVISORS) hired by Anera over a duration of 3 months to ensure the accurate and efficient flow of work. 3. Contractors assessments:10 Pre-qualified contractors will be selected, to conduct verification assessments together with their teams, following the needs assessment for proper verification of assessments and measurements. Also, contractor teams will be assessing the presence of any structural damage especially within level 2 repairs. 4. Preparation of BoQs: Anera's technical experts will develop BoQs based on assessment results and share them with the contractors for procurement of supplies; 5. Implementation/installation of repairs: taking into consideration the urgency of the situation, direct repair works of assessed HHs, will be launched directly (new HHs will be assessed while repairs for assessed HHs are being conducted at the same time). Shelter repairs will be conducted to meet humanitarian standards (in accordance with shelter sector guidelines, SPHERE, UNHCR SoPs and other shelter standards) and will ensure that the house will be restored to its initial state to provide families with safety, privacy and emotional security; contractor teams will be installing repairs and will be monitored by 15 youth formen (site supervisors) hired by Anera over a duration of 3 months to ensure proper implementation and safety and security measures; 6. Evaluation/HHs satisfaction: Upon completion of repair work Anera's MEAL team will conduct an evaluation with all HH's, through HH visits and surveys, to ensure that all needs were met up to the agreed on standards and provide additional support where needed.ANERA HAS AN EXTENSIVE EXPERIENCE AND CAPACITY IN REHABILITATION AND CONSTRUCTION INCLUDING: REHABILITATION OF HAIFA HOSPITAL, REHABILITATION OF 100 HOUSES IN NORTH LEBANON WITHIN ITS CASH FOR WORK PROGRAM FOR CONSTRUCTION TRAINEES ( PROJECT FUNDED BY UNICEF), CONSTRUCTION OF SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT FACILITIES IN BEKAA, CONSTRUCTION OF FOOTBALL COURTS IN DIFFERENT AREAS. IN "REHABILITATION OF BEIRUT HOUSES PROJECT" THE ASSESSMENT AND IMPLEMENTATION OF ACTIVITIES ARE DONE BASED ON THE PLAN SET WITH MINIMAL CHALLENGES THIS IS MAINLY DUE TO : - ANERA'S EXPERIENCED ASSESSMENT TEAMS AND SUPERVISORS WHO ARE ABLE TO MAP IDENTIFIED ZONES, AND DISTRIBUTE TASKS EFFICIENTLY, REACHING THE DAILY ASSESSMENT TARGET ( 15 HH/TEAM/DAY). - ANERA'S ACCESS TO A HIGH NUMBER OF CONTRACTORS (WHO HAVE PROFESSIONAL TEAMS) DUE TO PAST EXPERIENCES IN REHABILITATION - ANERA'S EXPERIENCED CONSTRUCTION YOUTHS (SITE SUPERVISORS) WHO HAVE PREVIOUSLY REHABILITATED SEVERAL HOUSES , AND ARE FAMILIAR WITH ALL THE PROCESSES NEEDED TO PROFESSIONALLY HANDLE THE REHABILITATION PROCESSES.
