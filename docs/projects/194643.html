Project Summary

Protracted crises in South Sudan, including climactic shocks and continued conflict, have resulted in 8.9 million people projected to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022. As a result of the shocks, such as flooding, inter-communal violence (ICV) and ongoing armed clashes, the majority of the population are facing increased food insecurity, lack of access to basic services, and protection risks. As per the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in South Sudan will negatively impact an estimated 6.1 million people in 2022.
Since 2006, Solidarites International has become a key WASH actor in South Sudan, implementing emergency lifesaving responses across the country and providing integrated WASH services to crisis-affected populations in areas of static presence in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Western Bahr el Ghazal. Since 2012 SI has played a key role in the Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) strategy in South Sudan, implementing multi-sectoral assessments and delivering emergency responses to crisis-affected populations all over the country.
In 2022, SI will continue to provide emergency live-saving WASH responses across the South Sudan to restore basic access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation while ensuring equitable access, safety, and accountability to supported populations. Through its roving Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) team, SI will deploy to emergency-affected locations to provide first phase and second phase responses including distribution of household water treatment items and soap, emergency provision of safe water, and rapid repair of emergency-affected infrastructure.
At the same time, SI will expand its presence in Upper Nile, Unity and Jonglei to provide basic integrated WASH services that target the most vulnerable, including those suffering malnutrition, food insecurity and protection threats (including GBV survivors). Static programs will focus on providing community-based solutions for sustainable WASH services that ensure that improvements in access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene are durable and can be maintained independently by communities. Engagement and joint programming with Protection and Nutrition actors will ensure that WASH services can be accessed by the most vulnerable and will have a tangible impact on reducing overall priority humanitarian needs. The increased integration of protection, inclusion, and conflict sensitivity analysis in all programming will ensure that WASH services and resources can be safely accessed by all in the community. Greater engagement of communities and local markets will support the design of long term and flood resistant WASH infrastructure and supply chains to ensure that interventions produce durable results for communities.

1 - Needs

Access to basic services, including safe water, hygiene items, and sanitation is extremely limited in many parts of the country, especially in Upper Nile and Jonglei states. In many areas, underground water presence is limited, leaving to the communities no alternative apart from surface water. In other areas with access to boreholes, rehabilitations typically have limited sustainability due to the impacts of flooding, emergency nature of repairs, and lack of maintenance. Economic vulnerability of communities and lack of functional supply chains prevents full handover of both surface water and groundwater supply infrastructure to communities. Sanitation facilities are very rare, with the majority of the population practicing open defecation. Rates of good personal hygiene practices are low, and low access to soap contributes to high diarrhoeal diseases.In Fashoda, Manyo, Melut, Baliet, Panyikang, Canal-Pigi, Fangak and parts of Malakal Counties, between 60% - 100% of the population report relying on surface water as their main source. In Fashoda, 80% of the population report relying on surface water, and safe water is primarily provided through humanitarian organizations. In terms of sanitation, almost no households use latrines in Fashoda, and each communal latrine in Aburoc camp is used by at least 94 persons on average. In Manyo and Canal-Pigi, virtually all of the population rely on surface water. A joint needs assessment conducted by SI in Malakal, Baliet, and Panyikang in December 2021 found that insufficient water points and distance to water points were key barriers to access to safe water, while in Panyikang assessment respondents reported that in the absence of water points the population is collecting untreated surface water. In all locations, respondents reported a lack of or no latrines. There were also protection concerns about accessing the few latrines there were because of no gender segregation. As in other parts of South Sudan, the majority of the communities use open defecation and lack of money and access to market prevent most households to have soap. In all the aforementioned counties, flooding since 2019 has resulted in displacement of households and reduced access to basic WASH services as well as increased protection risks. Water points and sanitation infrastructure have been submerged, contaminated, and damaged. Assessments in Fashoda, Manyo, and Melut since October 2021 found that SWAT systems had been submerged by flooding. Even in areas with normally better water supply, flooding and displacement have had significant effects. An assessment by SI in Terekeka (Eastern Equatoria) found that most boreholes have broken down due to flooding, with a total of 92 non-functional boreholes. Flooding is seasonal in South Sudan and will further impact already affected-populations.On top of the needs identified in 2021, major clashes have been reported in SI area of intervention in 2022 (namely Fashoda and Panyikang/Malakal counties), with around 40,000 people displaced by the clashes. WASH related needs are huge, as newly displaced people are completely lacking of access to basic wash services (i.e access to safe water and ensuring the environment is free of faeces).This lack of access to safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene and continuing shocks has increased the risk to vulnerable populations of water-borne diseases, in turn influencing rates of malnutrition (affecting especially children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women). The lack of access to WASH services, in particularly the distances to water points increase the risk of GBV for women and girls, as well as increasing their burden of work. People with specific vulnerabilities, such as People with Disability, also face many additional challenges to accessing WASH services.

2 - Project activities

SI’s 2022 strategy will focus on 3 Axes:1)Short-term response to reduce mortality and morbidity for the most vulnerable population in priority and WSC level 4&5 locations and in IPC4+ counties through static and roving EPR emergency responses (corresponding to WASH objectives CO1,CO2.1,CO2.2)2)Ensuring medium term safe, equitable and dignified access to essential WASH services to enable populations to meet their basic needs in locations of WSC 4&5; (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1,CO2.2)3)Identify and implement long term solutions for sustainable community-based WASH services through piloting innovative solutions and engagement with communities and local markets, and increased flood resilience of infrastructure (corresponding to WASH objectives CO2.1,CO2.2,CO3) In the first Axis, SI will implement the following activities in Priority 1 areas and WSC 4 and 5 locations:-Distribution of essential WASH NFIs to children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) suffering from malnutrition through referral from nutrition centres-HP and IPC trainings at nutrition and health facilities-assess the WASH conditions at nutrition facility level and support nutrition partners with the provision of safe water in the facility to facilitate nutrition programming and reduce the risk of Hospital Acquired Infections (HAIs) transmission-Distribution of MHM kits following referral from Protection services-Distribution of hygiene items and essential hygiene messages to vulnerable households affected by emergencies. Using CVA modalities where possible.-Emergency water supply: WASH NFI kit distribution, water trucking, SWATs, and emergency repair and chlorination of water points in areas affected by emergencies. Using CVA modalities where possible.In the second Axis, SI will implement the following activities in WSC 4 and 5 locations:-operation and maintenance of water supply infrastructure in SI areas of operation-Improved rehabilitation and upgrading of SWATs and water points to reduce operational costs and maintenance-Rehabilitation/Construction of communal latrines for densely populated areas with high risk of disease outbreak-Supported construction of household latrines for the most vulnerable households in communities.-Engage with vulnerable members of communities (e.g. women, girls, people with disabilities) through joint activities with protection partners to implement WASH infrastructure solutions that increase safe access.-Community hygiene promotion campaigns-EVD preparedness and Response following the Wash Cluster EVD preparedness and response plan-improve access to safe water in communities with high GAM prevalence and high wash vulnerability-ensure to contribute to the Accountability and Quality Approach (AQA) and to use of systematic basis the tools recommended by this working group.In the third Axis, SI will implement the following activities in relevant WSC 4 and 5 locations:-Pilot alternative means of localized and sustainable safe water supply with communities that provide improvements in access to safe water, e.g. adapted shallow wells, sand filters, and non-chemical household water treatment.-Long-term capacity building for WMCs with community engagement on contributions to WASH service management-Identify methodologies for construction of community-led household latrines using only local materials-Assessment of and support for local markets to supply WASH items and materials for activities. Use of local labour and contractors in infrastructural works-Rehabilitate and construct water points with flood resilient designs-Incorporate flood resilient designs for all construction of communal and institutional latrines. WASH safety audits and AAP consultations will be conducted before all interventions. While specific high priority counties have been identified by SI as key locations for intervention, SI will have the flexibility to respond to emergencies in other locations through the EPR team.

3 - Project indicators

SI implements an extensive framework of tools for the monitoring and evaluation of the activities conducted. All the indicators are designed in order to closely monitor the meeting of the project objectives. Key indicators for this project will include:-Number of SAM children/caretakers and PLW provided with standard kits paired with hygiene promotion-Number of women and girls referred through protection mechanisms (including GBV survivors) provided with Menstrual Management Items-Number of people having access to sufficient and safe water for domestic use-% of affected population disposing of their faeces safely every time they defecate% of target population with adequate WASH services and hygiene practices-% of beneficiaries (disaggregated by sex, age, and disability) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable, and participatory manner-% of affected population with access to climate resilient sanitation services Safety audits and protection analysis will be conducted at the start of each project. FGDs on AAP are conducted before, during, and after implementation, and AAP committees are established in project locations to ensure ongoing feedback from communities. These activities allow SI to adapt activities in between and assess the level of AAP as per the beneficiaries’ perception following AAP framework policies. KIIs and FGDs will be conducted in the targeted areas at different stages of the project in order to assess the distribution modalities and adapt them to the targeted population needs. SI will carry out PDM surveys covering all distributions, to gather information on satisfaction of beneficiaries in terms of timeliness and relevance of input distribution, quality and quantity distributed, overall organization and modalities of distribution including security and protection issues. A baseline Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) is done at the beginning of all projects, to assess the KAP of the communities in each area and adapt the activities and messages to the needs and behavioural indicators of communities. An endline survey is conducted at the end of the projects to support the final reporting and assess level of indicators achievement, satisfaction of beneficiaries and lessons learnt of the project to be shared with the donor for further projects. SI will apply the following actions and tools to ensure that the objectives are reached and needs covered: Activity Progress Update (APU) tools and MEAL plan; Follow-up of the different assessments and interventions implemented by the project teams; Weekly update to state level Clusters on achievements and challenges; Monthly reporting to the Clusters through 5Ws; Routine field visits by the Coordination teams. At SI coordination level, a program coordinator and a MEAL assistant will monitor the implementation, achievement of indicators, and quality of the program using the APU, and with support from SI HQ. Lastly SI is a founder member of the Accountability and Quality Assurance (AQA) Initiative, which is a key initiative of the WASH sector roadmap 2020-2025. The main objective of the AQA Initiative is to reliably deliver principled, people-centered assistance that meets quality and accountability standards and commitments. SI will contribute to the AQA Technical Working Group (TWG) in participating to the TWG meetings (once per month) and in sharing relevant data from our project to produce cluster infographics and snapshots.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

SI will do a market assessment before any project, tailored to the context and the area. SI can carry out rapid market assessments to complement longer term market assessments to determine if the market is functional enough to absorb cash and voucher mechanisms and avoid creating shortages in the market or inflation in the areas. Assessments will also try to identify vendors willing and able to accept the vouchers and the items that can be found on each market. SI will also ensure appropriate training to vendors on handling voucher systems. SI is taking part of the Joint Market Monitoring Initiative led by REACH every month. SI will link with market information management systems to monitor the price of items. SI aims to reach at least 15% of people (approximately 15,204 people) targeted for WASH NFI and hygiene items to receive those items through MBP/CVA approaches such as restricted vouchers for local market vendors or voucher fairs. SI also aims to increase use of MBP approaches in activities for construction, rehabilitation, and operation of water supply infrastructure, including engaging people from the local population in Cash for Work activities for activities related to construction of water infrastructure (approximately 10923 people), (to be combined with training), and where possible providing market support to local markets to enable SI to purchase supplies for WASH infrastructure from them. SI will also develop procurement plans at the start of each project to anticipate supply mechanisms. This will allow for detailed cost comparison between local and non-local markets (taking into account transportation costs) and for consultation with local suppliers on their prices, supply capacity, and delivery lead times to supply the desired items.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash and Voucher assistance in the WASH sector can be very useful for the following reasons: - To develop or reinforce markets indeed, injecting cash and having people buying WASH items on the market will encourage traders to buy and sell more WASH items to ensure availability and accessibility. It produces effects at medium/long term.- Using vouchers for supply of materials (especially local materials) increases the independence of and accountability to beneficiaries as they have more control of the quality of materials they receive.- Supports the formation of the habit for people to purchase hygiene items.- Increases the autonomy and independence of beneficiaries from humanitarian support.- Reduced transportation costs as transportation by traders more likely to be in bulk and therefore more cost-effective.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

AAP FGDs with communities at the start, during and end of projects will be segregated into different groups, such as women, people with disabilities, youth, and elderly, to help ensure that they are able to contribute to discussions and give their feedback. These FGDs will include questions asking each group their preferred methods of giving feedback and complaints and what actions can be done to ensure that they feel comfortable giving their feedback, including sensitive complaints. Each CRM committee is selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society and particularly women with specific needs, children, People living with Disabilities and individuals who are not literate have a representative. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. If the beneficiaries cannot write their feedback, they will be able to reach program staff and particularly community volunteers who will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. At distribution and activity sites, complaint boxes and desks will be set up. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism. For internal monitoring and to ensure the quality of programs, SI will integrate the WASH Cluster safety audit, AAP checklist, and Disability Inclusion checklists into the program monitoring tools as essential sources of verification to ensure that the needs, views, and feedback of the mostly vulnerable members are registered and acted upon.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During AAP FGDs and community meetings at the start of projects in each location, SI will inform the community about the planning, activities and objectives of the project and take feedback from the community about their preferred mechanisms for feedback and complaints. Based on these discussions, SI will adapt the feedback and complaint mechanisms. Further meetings will be held to inform the community of the selected mechanisms for providing feedback and complaints. Communities will be reminded of these mechanisms regularly during project activities, for example at distributions, during hygiene promotion sessions, and in further FGDs and AAP meetings. In all project locations, CRM committees will be selected with the communities directly and ensuring all groups of the society, particularly women and PWD, have representatives. Each CRM committee will be trained in the standard procedure for taking complaints and feedbacks. Program staff and particularly community volunteers will also receive CRM training and will be available to receive complaints and feedback during collective activities and household visits. Additionally, offices and distribution sites complaint boxes and desks will be set up. On a regular basis, the CRM committee will gather the complaints received by this mechanism and by the community volunteers, and forward to the coordination at field and Juba levels. Each CRM committee will share on a regular basis directly with the Field Coordinator and SI coordination office the complaints and feedback received as well as the measures taken at field level to be remediated. The community leaders and RRC will also be involved and sensitized to this topic, especially for very sensitive complaints that could concern staff members and sharing information to be handled with respect to confidentiality. SI provides accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and affected populations over information provision.SI takes a zero tolerance approach to any allegation of sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment, and any other form of misconduct from its staff members. In South Sudan, SI is part of the NGO-F PSEA Working Group and the PSEA Taskforce in Malakal County (for Upper Nile State).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During FGDs and community meetings on AAP at the start of projects, SI will start by presenting the principles and mechanisms for which SI are accountable. For complaints made in terms of the PSEA and CP policy, when CRM committees can be created, one man and one woman will be selected by the community as PSEA focal points. They will be considered focal points for the community to confidentially report complaints to. At each activity, awareness will be raised with regards to their availability to receive particularly sensitive feedback, especially regarding the behavior of project staff. They will receive specific sensitivity training and direct communication line with the SI MEAL and Coordination team. This will ensure all PSEA and CP policy complaints reach the coordination offices without program staff on the ground being aware. SI will also work with any existing external complaints structures, such as CCCM committees to ensure other partners are aware of complaints mechanisms.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

At the start of each project and in each location, SI will hold AAP FGDs with different groups (including separate groups with women, men, youth, people with disability, etc.), community meetings, and meetings with community leaders and local authorities such as the RRC. During these meetings SI will explain the planning, activities and objectives of the project and take feedback from the community. The beneficiaries’ opinions will be crosschecked with findings of the assessments conducted at the beginning of the project. This feedback will be used to adapt project planning and activities. to contribute to the adaptation of the project. During these meetings SI will also ask the communities about key topics such as their perceived levels of vulnerability to define selection criteria, and preferred mechanisms for feedback and complaints. Safety audit assessments and protection analysis will also be carried out at the start of each project, before the construction of any infrastructure, and before selection of distribution sites, to ensure beneficiaries have the opportunity to raise concerns about protection risks and to enable SI to adapt the activities. Throughout the project, SI will conduct regular FGDs with communities to take their feedback on the progress and quality of activities. Moreover, SI Program Managers report quality checks based on beneficiary feedback to the Coordination level on a monthly basis. These quality checks are based on the 9 commitments and quality criteria of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS) developed by CHSAlliance. Each project is analyzed through this quality check and followed up in the internal monitoring tool (Activity Progress Update). Based on the findings, Coordination and field bases determine to what extent it is necessary to adapt the project to the needs. Should any issue raised exceed the projects scope, SI will advocate this situation to the relevant Clusters (at National, State, and County level), local authorities, and other humanitarian partners to tackle the issues and ensure needs can be covered. In addition, SI will use the WASH Cluster AAP checklist in all projects to ensure that key AAP actions are being taken and meet the required quality standards.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The mitigation measures that will be taken are:Risk 1. and 2. Through integrated program activities and protection partners, SI will conduct protection and conflict sensitivity analyses to identify potential risks and mitigation measures. At the start of projects and activities, SI will hold consultations with the targeted beneficiaries about the activity locations, especially distribution points, latrines, and water points, to tailor the implementation and provide closer access to services. Distributions planning will ensure smaller distributions are held in more areas and close to beneficiaries’ houses, and take into consideration the time of the day for implementation of activities. WASH infrastructure will be implemented as close as possible to the households. The design of infrastructure will take protection risks into consideration, such as gender segregation of latrines and positioning of doors for segregated latrines. During the consultation, FGD are organized with separate specific groups (FGD with women representatives, FGD with youth representatives, FGD with boys, FGD with girls, FGD with groups with disabilities). Risk 3: SI has strong needs assessments, community consultation, selection criteria, and selection matrices to ensure that beneficiary selection is independent and impartial. First, community perceptions of selection criteria are discussed with the communities in separated FGDs. Based on this as well as needs assessment and situational information (e.g. type of emergency), SI prepares a criteria and weighting for selection. These criteria are then discussed again with communities and local authorities and finalized with their feedback and agreement. Then, depending on the situation and agreed mechanisms, selection maybe done either by direct household assessment and registration by SI enumerators and staff, through community committees with representatives from different groups, or through community leaders that provide a list of identified potential beneficiaries. In the case that selection is not done directly by SI staff, the list provided is checked by SI team to make sure the identified beneficiaries meet the decided selection criteria. Household registrations are also entered into a beneficiary database that includes scoring against the vulnerability criteria and weighting to ensure selected households meet the criteria.SI has regular discussions with different stakeholders, informing them of all the steps and achievement of the project, joint monitoring and reviews of the project with beneficiaries and community leaders. SI select locations selected for building water points based on needs, but taking into consideration land occupation for the WASH Facility buildings are taken into account. 8.9: SI keeps close relationship with authorities to be quickly informed in case of security incident in any of SI areas of interventions. Before the project implementation, at design phase, SI takes into consideration the relationship between different groups, such as cattle keepers and farmers, for example when determining the locations of water points. SI makes sure authorities provide land use authorization if necessary before starting an activity.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Through SI’s longer term activities, SI will construct or rehabilitate flood resilient water points and latrines based on approved adapted designs, in consultation with the WASH Cluster. The provision of these infrastructure will reduce the impacts of flooding and reduce displacement of communities, by helping to ensure that they can still access basic WASH services in their communities. In addition, while piloting community-based means of safe water supply and household latrines, SI will take flood risk considerations into account. For example, designs for local water infrastructure such as shallow wells may include means that allow the infrastructure to be located further away from swamps and river banks, while still collecting sufficient water to serve communities.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project will contribute slightly to the package of services for response to GBV by providing women and girls with essential hygiene and menstrual hygiene items upon referral from Protection services. Kits will also contain items like solar torches which can help feelings of safety and prevention. SI will work closely with protection partners, including a number already identified, to ensure protection led programmatic design and implementation and to address additional adaptations as they arise.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Ensuring access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene will support the reduction of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and food insecurity. Reducing the burden of malnutrition and disease can support vulnerable households to begin to recover their livelihoods and reduce their use of negative coping strategies. In addition, high rates of IPC and malnutrition can be a trigger for response by the EPR team. All the activities aim at giving access to basic services to vulnerable populations in these areas. All activities will prioritize the most vulnerable households.
