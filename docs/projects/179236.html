Project Summary

Telecommunications infrastructure throughout Syria has suffered significant and ongoing damage due to a decade of devastating conflict. While Damascus maintains good coverage, other locations including Aleppo, Deir Ez-Zor, Hama, Homs, Qamishli and Tartous require continued upgrades and maintenance to improve its coverage. In 2021, the ETC will maintain and enhance operational capacity of the six UNDSS-managed Security Operation Centres in Syria and expand the VHF/HF/UHF radio network in common operational areas.
Reliable connectivity remains a major challenge throughout the country. In 2021, the ETC will continue to provide data connectivity services in Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, Hama, Homs and Qamishli while also identifying improvements to ETC data connectivity services in all interagency hubs where these services are provided.
The fuel crisis and lack of electricity remains an urgent issue for the whole country and regular power outages impact the entire humanitarian communications network in Syria. The ETC plans to install a power backup system to support ETC data connectivity services in interagency hubs in Homs and Aleppo and to install solar power solutions in all six SOCs to provide a reliable power supply.
There is a need for the continued provision of shared connectivity and telecommunications services to facilitate cross border movement and operations in neighbouring countries where Syrians have been forced to flee. The ETC will continue to provide dedicated ICT helpdesk support personnel in the inter-agency hub in Gaziantep, Turkey, and provide technical guidance and support to the response community in Turkey, Jordan and Lebanon as required.
In 2021, the ETC will build the technical capacity of national humanitarian ICT staff through the delivery of two planned training courses on disaster management and security communications/ICT.
As the COVID-19 crisis continues to escalate, there is a need to scale up the inter-agency response to meet the increasingly complex and severe needs of Syrians in affected communities throughout the country. The ETC will continue to assess the needs on the ground to support and enable humanitarian activities where and when required.
Contingency resources may also be required to support new common operational areas or the extension of common services, based on assessments conducted periodically throughout the year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In the context of Syria, the provision of reliable inter-agency communication services is key for the delivery of humanitarian assistance. In the case of underfunding, the ETC will be unable to fulfil its mandate to provide all operational areas with vital communications services, which will seriously hamper humanitarian operations in Syria and will affect staff safety in the field. Capacity building is a critical element in Syria, as some areas where it is difficult to deploy international staff rely entirely on skilled national staff. A shortfall in funding would restrict the ETC’s ability to organize training for national staff in 2021.
