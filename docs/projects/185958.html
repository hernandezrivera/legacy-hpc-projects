Project Summary

North and West Darfur nutrition situation is projected to remain at the current levels of >15% GAM rates or deteriorate further given the projected worsening in prevalence of risk factors for malnutrition (limited access to health services, increase in food insecurity, limited access to WASH services, increase in displacements of IDPs due to flood/crashes, poor /low coverage of infant and young feeding practices particularly complementary feeding, economic shocks exacerbated by high inflation etc). Nutrition-cluster of Sudan estimated bout 1,867,821 in need of nutrition services (SUD-HNO-HRP-22). Chronic conflict in Darfur region continues to impact on the nutritional well-being of children and women of reproductive age and people with disability. GAM rates of above 15% WHO thresholds continue to threaten lives and nutritional well-being of millions children below five years. Continued Food insecurity, poor feeding practices, Internal displacements, poor WASH and health services, inflation exacerbated by COVID-19 pandemic have remained the major drivers of acute malnutrition in North and West Darfur, with more than half million vulnerable communities (IDP, Returnees and Refugees) in need of humanitarian assistance. Acute malnutrition increases among children and PLWs and PWDs in distressed conditions (IDP, returnees, refugees etc. Children in such circumstances are likely to miss lifesaving nutrition services exposing them to increased malnutrition, morbidity, and mortality risk. Currently, HPDO with support from UNICEF, WFP, FMOH/SMOH working in collaboration with other nutrition and cluster partners (WASH, FSL, Protection and Education) proposed provision of integrated nutrition services for treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition to conflict affected-most vulnerable children and women of reproductive age in Umbaro, Kernui, Kabkabyia ,tawila, Al malha, Elfasher, ag geneina, beida, jebel moon localities. North and West Darfur through a multi-sector approach ensuring local capacity and linkage strengthening in social behavior change communication (SBCC) towards positive mindset change for nutrition program suitability. Our proposed integrated approach of nutrition-sensitive for treatment (both out-patient treatment and in-patient) of severe and moderate acute malnutrition (SAM) and MAM combined with the nutrition-sensitive interventions nexus with WASH, FSL, Protection among other will enhance the quality of nutrition services for resilience to nutritional shocks. Child protection services –ensuring mothers exclusively breast fed and provision of complementary diets and dietary consideration and needs of PLWs are well known at households and community levels to prevent violence against children and women; and any cases of such nation should be identified and refereed timely by the service providers at all levels.

Nutrition-cluster of Sudan estimated bout 1,867,821 in need of nutrition services of which 245,288 from North and West Darfur (SUD-HNO-HRP-22).The increasing number of areas/locations affected localized violence in recent past have heighten malnutrition and risk of mortality and morbidity among vulnerable population in local communities with most in need (IDPs, Returnees and Refugees) in Umbaro, Kernui, Kabkabyia ,tawila, Al malha, Elfasher, ag geneina, beida, jebel moon localities are North and West Darfur. HPDO with support from UNICEF, WFP, FMOH/SMOH working in collaboration with other nutrition and cluster partners (WASH, FSL, Protection and Education) proposed provision of integrated nutrition services for treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition to conflict affected-most vulnerable children and women of reproductive age and people with disabilities in Umbaro, Kernui, Kabkabyia ,tawila, Al malha, Elfasher, ag geneina, beida, jebel moon localities. North and West Darfur through a multi-sector approach ensuring local capacity and linkage strengthening in social behavior change communication (SBCC) towards positive mindset change for nutrition program suitability. Our proposed integrated treatment and prevention mainstreamed with SBCC for positive mindset change is aligned with Cluster framework of Sudan and will target severely and moderately malnourished (children U5s and PLW) including people with disability (PWDs) in the same localities. The nutrition services will include nutritional assessment of children under 5 years, pregnant and Lactating Women, SC/OTP and TSFP services (nutrition-specific), infant and young child feeding (IYCF), WASH promotion and nutrition-FSL sensitive approach of backyard vegetable gardening for dietary diversification. Child protection services –ensuring mothers exclusively breast fed and provision of complementary diets and dietary consideration and needs of PLWs are well known at households and community levels to prevent violence against children and women; and any cases of such nation should be identified and refereed timely by the service providers at all levels. The quality of and access to integrated nutrition services for at-risk groups will be improved by strengthening local capacity and linkages for provision of quality nutrition services and supporting community-based nutrition services at scale to the reach the last child in last miles of this project. The project areas' outcome will be more equitable, accessible, and higher-quality-high impact nutrition services for most at at-risk groups such as poor mothers and children and PWDs.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The Project is designed to deliver results under four areas: a) Lifesaving interventions through treatment services (SC/OTP and TSFP) provided through the functional health facilities; b) Strengthened IYCF through mother-support groups/other changes powered by cultural sensitive SBCC messages in translated pictorials; c) Support strengthening integrated IYCF-WASH-FSL-Protection services at household and community level ; and d) Strengthening community-facility health linkages and capacities improved coverage and quality of integrated nutrition services. The vulnerable communities will be at canter of the HPDO delivery model towards resilience building
