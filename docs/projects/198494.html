Project Summary

The proposed project aims to overcome barriers related to the shortage of qualified teachers in LGAs of Magumeri, Mafa in Borno State, and LGAs of Potiskum, Fika in Yobe State, in order to improve and rehabilitate existing infrastructure, WASH facilities and build new ones to meet the gender needs of boys and girls at selected project sites and to provide classroom furniture. The project will target 300 teachers in selected LGAs in Borno and Yobe State to alleviate the shortage of qualified teachers and build the capacity of the teachers on teaching methods and Psycho-Social Support to improve the retention of children affected by conflict at school, and train 40 School Base Management committees (SBMCs)/Community Base Management committees (CBMCs). The project will also provide learning materials to 3,450 children affected by conflict so that they can stay in school to assess qualitative education and provide vocational training kits, recreational materials, and provision of chairs to the Ministry of Education.
Project Objectives
1. Introductory Visit to Project Communities and To Conduct Education Needs assessment, Joint Monitoring Visit, and Capacity Building to MOE Staff on EIE activities.
2. To strengthen and build the capacity of teachers in Potiskum, Fika and Mafa, and Magumeri LGAs Yobe and Borno State.
3. To improve existing infrastructure to ensure that children affected by conflict have access to quality education in a supportive and comfortable environment in Potiskum, Fika, Mafa and Magumeri LGAs Yobe and Borno State.
4. To support the conflict-affected children by the distribution of PSS Material, Provision of Vocational Training kit and Mainstreaming GBV in Potiskum, Fika, Mafa and Magumeri LGAs Yobe and Borno State

1 - Needs

Generations of children have been left without learning opportunities and are even more vulnerable to the crisis. Education emergencies working group strategy North-East Nigeria 2021-2023 education situation reports indicate that 611 teachers were killed, 19,000 were displaced, 910 schools were damaged and destroyed, and more than 1,500 schools were forced to close since the conflict erupted in 2009. This has resulted in 900,000 children losing access to learning, and 75% of children in camps not attending school. There is a 70% dropout rate among girls of primary school age in Borno State the highest rate in the country. Of those who do attend, over 70% cannot read by the end of grade six, and only 35% of girls and 46% of boys are literate. While education is a fundamental human right in many internationally recognized conventions and declarations, the efforts of all stakeholders to ensure safe, quality access to education and greater equity and opportunities for lifelong learning are still inadequate, especially for girls and young women. making them more vulnerable to exploitation and sexual abuse. The challenges affecting the education system in Northeast Nigeria are a Less secure learning environment; an insufficient number of teachers, inadequate infrastructure (high student-to-classroom/toilet ratio), and Inappropriate school supplies, including dignity kits for adolescent girls and young women. Low community ownership (including parents) and low participation in education, Distance from school, and frequent assaults/kidnaps at school. In addition, economic needs at the household level seem to outweigh the importance of girls' education, with parents preferring to send their sons to school over daughters often being seen as a source of income when they married out. The lack of access to comprehensive quality and gender-responsive, safe, and protected environments continues to exacerbate the ability of girls and young women in the Northeast (Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe) making them vulnerable to gender-based violence. With parents unable to afford education fees and levies, poverty remains the greatest barrier to accessing education. Lack of classroom furniture, WASH facilities, and the poor quality of the infrastructure are also critical barriers. Schools and learning centers have also struggled to retain students due to a shortage of qualified teachers and a lack of teaching and learning materials. The HNO 2021 estimates that over 1.23 million children will need basic quality education. In terms of the number of People in Need, 32% are internally displaced, 31% are returnees, and 37% are members of the host community. Another 1.2 million people are estimated to be in inaccessible areas with about 60% anticipated to be school-aged children. The 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan estimated that approximately 1.9 million children were in need of education support, while 23,664 teachers required capacity building and training in improved teaching and learning approaches in North East Nigeria and 5,129 classrooms are in need of rehabilitation and reconstruction.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Establishment & running of complaint and response mechanisms (CRMs) To increase accountability and community engagement and improve response to Gender-Based and human rights incidents, the project shall establish/strengthen complaint response mechanisms in the target communities. A dedicated CRM officer will manage complaints desks weekly in each community. Experience has shown that the complaints desks, which provide beneficiaries with the opportunity to provide feedback face-to-face and to receive an immediate response, are the most popular and effective. Besides, complaint boxes will be placed in each community. All complaints and responses will be collected in a dedicated tracker, including referrals made.
