Project Summary

WHO will continue supporting primary health care activities in northwest Syria to maintain access to healthcare for those in need and leave no one behind with a focus on the most vulnerable groups; Thus, through integrated PHC networks that focus on ensuring the delivery of Essential Health Service Package (EHSP) and last resort coverage for top priority health facilities that are located out of the network geographical coverage. Estimated number of health facilities to be covered 600 fixed and MMUs along the country. It is imperative to enhance national health resilience and to reactivate public health partners by supporting the rehabilitation of non-functioning public centers , to repair extensive damage to crucial infrastructure, support ongoing medical training for human resource for health, sustain availability of essential medicines and equipment and to undertake ongoing needs’ assessments to fulfill gaps on the mid- and long term; with focus on advocating and expanding standardized PHC service package along the county. WHO will provide the technical expertise to ensure the implementation of the essential PHC essential health service package to address humanitarian needs and to improve the health system resilient to situations of crisis through comprehensive integrated health services at static and community-based interventions, with a focus on strengthening the capacity for human resources for health as a key point. This includes prevention of diseases, early detection and standard management for NCDs high risk groups, early essential new-born care services, maternal and childhood care, infection prevention and control at the health facility and community levels, through the provision of continued education and training, endorsement of standardized clinical guidelines, treatment protocol. Furthermore, the project aims to support safe health care service through delivery of basic infection prevention and control training, provision of technical support, provision of PPE supplies, and the needed resources to provide IPC standard measures in the context of the COVID pandemic and other outbreaks to ensure the quality-of-care continuum. Within the integration approach to essentially service delivery the access to quality care and referral to specialized health services will be introduced through implementation of this project, which includes dialysis, cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation, including provision of life-saving medical supplies and medicine.
WHO field presence in Gaziantep will promote the INGOs' engagement in the health sector to ensure smooth phasing out of the service delivery in case of the non-renewal of the UNSCR.
Objectives:
To ensure system strengthening the following programmatic areas will be in focus of the implementation of this project:
- Ensuring Access to Integrated Health Service Delivery & Community health
- Support referral system
- Capacity building such as infection prevention and control, facility cleanliness and patient safety, pharmacy & warehouse management, facility management, peace building and standardized reporting through DHIS2.
- Enhance Health Information Systems
- Quality monitoring and improvement of service provision
- COVID 19 response and continuity of essential health services
- NCD integration
- TB integration
- Nutrition linkage focusing on prevention of malnutrition for Pregnant & Lactating Women and U-5, health promotion acclivities, community-based intervention
- GBV first line support is part of the integratory services provided at PHC and community.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022; 12.23 million people need health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33 million children under 5 years; including an estimated 503,000 live births expected and 3.38 million women of reproductive age (15-49years). An additional 550,795 older people will require inclusive health services, as well as those with early onset non-communicable diseases which are estimated to account for 45% of all mortality in Syria (WHO, 2016). Youth and young persons, who comprise 1.98 million of the health sector PiN, are also reporting increasing stress and mental health concerns. In 2022, it is critical to ensure investments in health service delivery are not lost, efforts are required to protect vulnerable groups; to improve equitable access to quality health care for all affected populations and reproductive health and safe delivery, child health including routine and expanded immunization; care for communicable and non-communicable diseases; mental health and psychosocial support; should be followed by the improvement of the referral system, emergency services including trauma and referral; and physical rehabilitation must be maintained at every level of the health system and address chronic health worker shortages and turn over. WHO will continue the processes of integration of TB care, community health, EPI, and other stands alone programs into the delivery of services through integrated service delivery network.
