Project Summary

The project aims to provide emergency shelter and lifesaving NFI assistance to the newly displaced persons, returnees and host communities in Leer, Mayendit, Panyijiar, Koch, Mayom, Nyirol, Ayod, Fangak, Maiwut Malakal through integrated approaches. It will support and improve S/NFI- conditions of 3000 households (7000 women, 4500 men, 3500 girls and 3000 boys) of most vulnerable IDPS, returnee and host community to positively contribute to the reduction of protection concerns ,livelihoods opportunity building and reduce exposure to diseases attributed to poor shelter conditions and lack of nonfood items.
The project will consistently engage and coordinate with other cluster partners such as CCCM, WASH, Logistics, Health, protection and Education to ensure services provisions are delivered to the most vulnerable IDPS,returnees and host communities in dignified manner. The humanitarian hub in leer & Bentiu will be used as center for coordination with other cluster and platform for information sharing in regard to this project. The project will sufficiently address needs of protracted IDPS in secondary displacement, who have no means to meet their S/NFI needs in the targeted locations. It will support returnees who are returning helpless and homeless with appropriate S/NFI assistance in order to rebuild their lives and achieve resilient. HRSS will effectively and efficiently engage with the with stakeholders such as; local authorities, chiefs, herdsmen and S/NFI cluster to ensure lands reused and occupied are recovered back to the rightful owners through due process of law. The project will organize HLP participatory training sessions with the beneficiaries and local authorities on IDPS HLP rights awareness to ensure that HLP rights are adequately realized throughout the project period.
HRSS will conduct need analysis in three state's with smooth coordination with partners and S/NFI cluster. This need analysis will inform the team to identify, verify vulnerability and establish project beneficiaries. During this analysis, the project will also conduct broader consultations using FGDs and KIIs with all stakeholders, persons living disability, chiefs, local authorities and representatives of the affected households to understand community dynamics. It will involve other cluster in the analysis such as; protection, CCCM, WASH,FSL LOG and S/NFI static partners on ground, as part of joint mission.
Through this project intervention, HRSS will distribute numbers of 3000 plastic sheets, 800 rubber ropes, 3000 kangas, 3000 mosquito nets, 3000 blankets, 3000 kitchen sets, 3000 solar lamps and other essential items necessary to the project beneficiaries. These items will be requested through S/NFI pipeline with transportation completed by logistics cluster.
The project will conduct post distribution monitoring with ES/NFIs standard monitoring tools, and conduct a joint PDM 1 month after the intervention by the HRSS M& E team, Program team, RRC,leaders ,AAP and the beneficiaries to measure the impact of the intervention. The guiding principle of this intervention is “Do no harm’’ and it prioritizes the protection, safety, dignity and rights of beneficiaries while delivering services and assistance. The project will ensure participatory inclusion and seek to build capacity to assist affected individuals to claim their rights. Proposed intervention will abide by humanitarian principles by ensuring free access for population groups ; women, PSNs,girls and children are protected from all forms of sexual gender based violence and SEA. Platforms such as open community dialogues, complaint and feedback mechanisms, community consultations, Focus Group Discussions and meetings will be employed in the project to ensure community members views, feedback, suggestions, ideas and concerns are received, acknowledged and attended to on time to safeguard their rights and entitlements. The APP committees will work hand in hand with the project teams.

1 - Needs

The formation of the Government of National Unity in February 2020 has created optimism among displaced people both inside and outside of South Sudan. According DTM data round 9 shows that 87.7 per cent of IDPs (1,403,069 people) and 85.5 per cent of returnees (1,316,232 people) live in rural areas with host community settings.According to Humanitarian Need overview 2021, there will be continue to exist for vulnerable displaced IDPs and returnees due to the lack of access to services; flooding; sub-national violence in areas of displacement and return; and challenges in accessing homes or property after prolonged displacements in POC sites/IDP camps. Bor, Bentiu, and Juba PoC sites have recorded outflows as people concerned with potential insecurity in transitioning or transitioned PoCs return to their areas of habitual residence or move to secondary displacement locations. With an increased population mobility from PoCs to rural areas due to perceived insecurity in PoCs arising from the withdrawal of the UN Police, the needs for shelter solutions in rural or urban areas receiving PoC residents are increasing according to the DTM round 9 . There will be a need for better shelter options in IDP camps instead of the widely used plastic sheets. The transition offers an opportunity to decongestant PoCs and takes incremental steps towards meeting minimum shelter standards in terms of living spaces (3.5 square meters per person) since the government will be responsible for camp administration.In 2021, the need of emergency shelter and non-food items will affect the living standard and well being of an estimated 2.1 million people. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers are also identified with shelter and NFI needs. The number of people in need will increased by 100,000 in 2021, compared to 2 million in 2020, due to an increase in the number of returnees. At the time of writing, approximately 1.65 million women, men, girls, boys and older persons live with inadequate shelter and NFIs According HNO 2021. Without shelter or having a strong social support network, women and children are especially vulnerable due to their roles in society and their health, well being, safety and security are at risk. It is estimated that more than 70 per cent of newly displaced people outside PoC sites and collective centre's are unable to meet their basic household needs and will require a humanitarian intervention. Emergency shelter and NFI needs are greatest in Upper Nile, Unity, Jonglei, Lakes, Warrap, Western Bahr Ghazel, Central and Western Equatoria. Lifesaving access to emergency shelter and NFIs needs to be provided immediately to displaced people to prevent further loss of life and increased vulnerability. Access to naturally grown shelter materials has continued to be a challenge due to recurrent sub-national and localized violence, compounded by seasonal flooding, particularly in Jonglei, Unity and Upper Niles states. The top three needs of people are access to emergency life-saving shelter, access to lifesaving NFIs and access to housing, land and property including support to recovery and resilience building.Displaced people and returnees are experiencing challenges with regard to access to their lands and houses, HLP property documentation, security and protection concerns. IDPS living in designated and PoC sites need S/NFI helps.

2 - Project activities

HRSS will under take the following sectoral Objective by improving access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and lifesaving NFIs to newly displaced or populations in Providing shelter and non-food items for displaced populations in situations of emergency and transition by undertaking the following activities to achieved her Objective.Activity 1.1.1: Need analysis and beneficiaries verification:HRSS will conduct rapid need analysis with static partners to identify vulnerability and project beneficiaries. This need analysis will help the HRSS team to understand beneficiaries dynamics. HRSS team will conduct consultations with population groups using FGDs and KIIs with community leaders in consultation with local areas authorities to reduce any further tensions that may possibly arise. Activity1.1.2: Beneficiaries’ registrations and verification 3000HHS: HRSS in collaboration with AAP committee and trained enumerators will conduct comprehensive beneficiaries’ registrations and verifications of 3000HHS of IDPS, returnees and vulnerable host communities through effective coordination and equal consultation with affected population, local authorities and protection partner on ground. These beneficiaries’ registrations will be door to door exercise taking into full consideration the most vulnerable persons. Activity 1.1.3:Distribution of S/NFI supplies:HRSS will distribution of S/NFI supplies for period of one week or two weeks. The S/NFI will engage with affected population, trained APP committee, enumerators, local authorities on ground, crowd controllers and protection partners on to ensure that most vulnerable persons are prioritized. Community engagement will continue throughout the project time frame to mitigate risks of violence or feelings of discrimination between project beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. Crowd controllers will be recruited and interviewed and must be a persons with high integrity in the community. They will be taken through S/NFI critical criteria of S/NFI on how to verify tokens, crowd control, splitting of supplies and helping of most vulnerable individuals such as; elder persons, children, persons living disability in carrying home the items. And this will be done in compliance with WHO, MOH UNICEF/Protection cluster CD COVID-19 set protocol. The entitlements that will be provided to the beneficiaries will be as follows:1- Plastic sheets 1per households2- Blankets 1per households3- mosquito nets 1per households4- sleeping mats 1 per households5- solar lamps 1per households6 kitActivity 1.1.4:Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM)HRSS will conduct a joint post distribution monitoring with ES/NFIs standard monitoring 1 month after the intervention. The PDM team will consist of Programmed Team , RRC leaders and AAP to measure the impact of the intervention. 20 beneficiaries will be selected from various types of beneficiaries will be asked about the standard of methodology employed, quantity and quality of supplies per households as well as if the supplies distributed adequately addressed their needs. Random sampling methods of selection among the various categories of beneficiaries and stakeholders, will be used in selecting respondents will be used to ensure each beneficiaries has equal opportunity of being identified to provide opinion and feedbacks. The PDM will be conducted three times during the project implementation. And each PDM report will be shared with key stakeholders, ES/NFI SFP at state and national level respectively.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HRSS will use Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) approach which is a system blending both formal and informal community structures, where individuals will be able and encouraged to safely report incidents of SEA. Local communities will be involved in developing and approving the CBCM so that the structure is both culturally and gender-sensitive. The mechanism should have multiple entry points, allowing both beneficiaries and staff the opportunity to report at the organizational level – internally through the network’s and field agency focal points at the community level. The primary Concern of the mechanism is to aid known and potential SEA survivors, and also fulfill a prevention function through awareness-raising efforts. A PSEA CBCM will not be a separate, parallel system to other complaints and feedback structures but rather link to and build on existing structures to create one system for handling feedback and complaints. HRSS will also ensure that a person who receives assistance as part of either emergency relief or development aid through assistance programs. Persons under this title include members of affected populations including refugees, internally displaced persons and other vulnerable individuals, as well as host community members.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Communities will be provided with suﬃcient information on their rights to access assistance, submit complaints and receive feedback. Also, the intervention has mainstreamed protection of women and girls from any form of Gender Based Violence. Information and training on GBV mitigation, reporting and referrals will be avail to staff and AAP committee to refereed beneficiaries to other service provider within the county when needs a raised. AAP and community leaders will ensure the rights of women and girls are observed. This can ensure the continuity of the project outcomes. Accountability will be taken into consideration during the proposed action to ensure that the points of view of the affected population and IDPs are considered. They will be involved all along the project, from the assessments, to the monitoring of activities, including beneficiaries’ selection and composition of kits and implementation of activities. HRSS staff will be well trained to ensure the needs of the children are assessed. Before the intervention, HRSS will organize a minimum of 3 focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for the community consultations to ensure community inputs. This feedback will be used to adapt the activities, improve the mobilization and participation of the population, monitor and improve the quality of the work. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. For each location, HRSS will also create an implementation committee and a complaint committee with members of the community. They will be responsible for the link between affected households and HRSS. They will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community.Hope Restoration will collaborate with the local community, local authority, the government and other security agency partners on the ground while implementing the project. We will sensitize the community and the stakeholders about the viability of the project. Involving all the stakeholders in equitable distribution while prioritizing the most vulnerable and information sharing. The cordial relationship between the target community and HRSS with a big asset in mitigating this risk. 8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will pay keen attention to the provision of inclusive services to the affected women, girls and boys, persons living HIV&AIDS, persons without effective community links with a without disability, lactating mothers, pregnant mothers. unaccompanied elderly persons of 60+ and unaccompanied children under 18 years. This will be done ensure that there is participatory accessibility throughout the project implementation. The project will consult with local leaders and community groups during need assessment, beneficiaries’ registrations and verification and during distribution in order to overcome any considerable disruption associated with any potential risks to the project beneficiaries. Distribution of emergency shelter and NFI will be conducted at the agreed sites or center by all members from population groups. Registrations will be carried out by trained AAP committee or enumerators selected by local authorities in each location targeted. While distribution of NFIS will be conducted by HRSS in consultation with populations groups and local leadership in the broad daylight. the time and venue agreed bilaterally will help women and girls to return homes in a daylight and avoid further attacks any GBV perpetrators. Complaint feedback mechanisms will be established or strengthened to assist community relay their concerns to the project team regarding their perceptions about the response. The project will introduce PSEA to all project and incentive staffs to inform all members of the organization that, during this response and henceforth, any further imbalance between project beneficiaries and humanitarian workers including members of the organization and enumerators will be treated with zero tolerance. NFI response will be done with full participation of protection partner to look into key protection issues in and around the project locations. Need analysis, registration and distribution items. It will be done in coordination with all stakeholders including protection partners to monitor the protection concerns of the beneficiaries and inform the project whether there is a need to increase protection or not.
