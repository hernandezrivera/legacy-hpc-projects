Project Summary

In 2023 ADRA is planning to intervene in several types of activities, such as water system rehabilitation, boreholes rehabilitation and solar systems installation (in locations where household-level access to water is limited due to electricity outages), WASH in schools' repair and debris removal in areas where it poses a significant health and protection risk to the local population. Due to the emergence of cholera disease there, there is an increased need for good drinking water quality and isolating the water source from all types of contamination, especially in Deir-Ez-Zor and Aleppo, where most of the cholera cases were located. Therefore ADRA assessed several sewage pumping stations, water treatment plants and sewerage systems that are damaged and pose a significant risk to the local population, as well as WASH assistance in camps and distribution of hygiene kits.
This project will improve access to safe water and sewage services for approximately 560,000 beneficiaries (85000 households). Male (0 - 17) 109,242 Female (0 – 17) 129,109 Male(18-59) 153,204 Female (18-59) 152,940 Male(>=60) 7,048 Female(>=60) 8,457. The targeted category includes newly returned returnees, IDPs and vulnerable host communities across the governorates of Homs, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Daraa, and Deir-Ez-Zor. The beneficiaries will be selected on a detailed needs assessment, and those with the highest need and underserved by the humanitarian community will be prioritized for assistance.
The project will use a participatory and integrated approach to ensure that the needs of men, women, boys and girls are taken into account from the project design stage, throughout the implementation and during the post-implementation monitoring of the work. Children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will be solicited during the initial needs assessment for the project and in the post-implementation stage. In addition, each age and gender group will be able to provide timely feedback to ADRA on the intervention through regular contact with ADRA monitoring staff.
The needs of young children, children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults and older adults will also be considered, as children, adolescents, and middle-aged and older adults may require more water for cleaning and cooking.
ADRA has already secured 60% of the estimated budget for planned 2023 WASH activities.

3 - Needs

The WASH needs in Syria increased from approximately 13.2 in 2022 to 13,551,861 in 2023, and it remains critical across Syria (HH_Severity_WASH_HNO2023). It is estimated that 58% of the Syrian population remains at WASH severity levels 3-5. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, causing a lack of power to function the water sources and decreasing the pumping hours to the public network. (HNO 2023 draft), posing significant health and protection risks to men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and people with disabilities (PwDs).Based on ADRA's needs assessments, ongoing presence in the area and current interventions, priority WASH needs were identified in Rural Damascus, where some communities still rely on water trucking (which sometimes comes only every 15 days) or private boreholes with unverified water due to a limited power supply or damages to the network. In Deir Al Zor, Based on the need assessment ADRA found needs in Al Bou Kamal where the water pumping station needs rehabilitation and a Solar system to provide clean water for 80,000 individuals. There is low water pressure at the household level, the network is damaged in DeZ city, and in Mayadeen villages. For Araqa ADRA found needs in Al Sabkha where the water system is partially operational due to mechanical problems. In Daraa, ADRA assessed the Al Sanamein area and found a high need for boreholes rehabilitation and Solar system installation.

4 - Implementation of activities

ADRA will implement life-sustaining and life-saving WASH activities in close coordination with multiple stakeholders, including the WASH sector, SARC, other humanitarian partners and the Ministry of Water Resources, the Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and local authorities such as the mayor, and the Head of the local water unit. ADRA's social and technical staff will conduct needs assessments in the targeted areas, including interaction with the community, to ensure the participation of beneficiaries and will select activities based on identified needs for the vulnerable communities. Activities will include life-sustaining works such as the rehabilitation/expansion of water and sewerage networks and the distribution of water tanks. Water and sewerage network rehabilitation will include works such as the spot repair or replacement of damaged pipelines in water networks, the provision of power sources for boreholes such as solar panels or a generator or connecting to electricity, replacing or repairing damaged sewage lines, desludging sewage lines, and the provision of manhole covers to avoid protection risks. Also, rehabilitation of WASH facilities in schools for highly vulnerable groups and distributing hygiene kits to support the prevention of the COVID-19 and cholera pandemic. Since 2019 ADRA has opened an office in Deir Ez Zor where all the studies assessment and monitoring works are being conducted For Ar Raqa, AL Mayadeen and Deir Ez Zor.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards2. Target areas are perceived to prioritise and empower one group over another or unfair selection of communities, causing community tension.3. Vulnerable community members, particularly women and children, are asked to provide services in return for humanitarian assistance.4. Exposure to UXOs causes injuries during the implementation of solid waste management posing protection risks to men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and PwDs.5. Child labour used on project sites increases the risk of school dropout and protection risks such as physical injury in addition to GBV.Mitigation: 1. Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source.2. Conduct detailed assessments at the local level to ensure assistance considers community dynamics, and explain selection criteria clearly to Ministries.3. Train staff in PSEA and closely monitor all staff and contractors working in target areas. Ensure complaints' feedback mechanism is available to beneficiaries.4. Conduct hazard awareness training with staff; ensure areas are cleared by the governorate before intervention.5. Explain ADRA Syria's policy on child labour to the contractor prior implementation of works and close monitoring of staff on project sites.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks:1. COVID-19 and AWD / Cholera outbreaks spread significantly, impeding implementation and posing health risks to staff, contractors, and beneficiaries.2. Cash funds transfers into Syria remain challenging, impacting the timeliness of intervention.3. The devaluation of the Syrian Pound.4. The security situation in the targeted areas deteriorates, impeding access to the site for intervention.5. Delays in receiving approvals impact the timeliness of the intervention. 6. Qualified staff cannot be found to implement the planned activities.7. There is the potential for corruption or fraud, particularly during procurement.Mitigation:1. Ensure staff and contractor use of PPE during implementation and compliance with all WHO and ministry of health recommendations.2. Continue to try alternative transfer routes into Syria.3. Monitor the exchange rate closely; use open and competitive procurement processes.4. Close monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, and staff. 5. Close follow-up with ministries regarding approvals throughout the project period.6. Recruit qualified staff early in the project and conduct regular capacity building.7. Adhere strictly to ADRA Syria's Procurement Policy and Finance Policies and ensure staff are aware of and understand the policies.
