Project Summary

Based on the “Minimum Package of Essential Health Services for Primary Healthcare Facilities in Refugee/FDMN camps, Cox’s Bazar” and the “Standard Operating Procedure for Patient Referrals to Secondary and Tertiary Healthcare Facilities for Refugees/FDMNs, Cox’s Bazar,” the project will offer primary and secondary healthcare services to refugees and the host communities in the surrounding areas in Cox’s Bazar. The project will be aligned with the 2022–2024 Cox's Bazar Health Sector Strategic Plan. A total of 10 primary healthcare centers (PHC), two health posts and give drop-in centres serving the key and gender-diverse demographics will all provide services as part of the project. One specialized hospital in Ukhiya for service delivery will be incorporated into the project and handed over to the Government. PHCs situated in the camp will include two in-patient mental health facilities to provide care to severe mental health cases. Persons with disabilities (PWDs) and the elderly will be able to access health facilities with accompanied referrals from supported households. PWDs will receive services tailored to meet their needs, including access to appropriate equipment. Primary healthcare facilities will provide and incorporate mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS). New evidence-based psychological counseling methodologies will be implemented at the community level. Care will be more locally accessible through trained para counselors and community psychosocial volunteers from the community. Strengthening integration of MHPSS with WASH, nutrition, protection, GBV and other sectors is planned. The project will cover the cost of referrals of emergency cases from UNHCR’s areas of operations, in addition to ambulances for its partners. A robust community health system will ensure disease surveillance, early detection, and response to health hazards such as diseases with epidemic potential, as well as health promotion which will contribute to community resilience. Through the coordination of the Community Health Working Group (CHWG), partners in the response will ensure harmonized community-based health interventions. The CHWG will also provide toolkits, IEC materials and capacity building for community health workers (CHWs). As part of the project, one infectious disease treatment center (IDTC) will remain on standby at Ukhiya Specialized Hospital to support case management of outbreak prone diseases. Stockpiling of medicines and medical supplies and support to emergency medical teams will be ensured. During monthly facility-based health forums where the community has the opportunity to provide feedback, the project will incorporate refugees in the provision of service delivery. Camp health focal points will continue to collaborate with different camp actors to plan activities at the camp level and offer support supervision to health services offered in the camps and healthcare facilities, which will be achieved through utilizing existing UNHCR and Health Sector monitoring tools. Monitoring of performance through age and gender-disaggregated data collection and analysis systems e.g., the community-based health information system, UNHCR integrated refugee health information system (irHIS), compliance with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare’s (MOHFW) District Health Information Systems -2 and the health sector’s 4Ws, Early Warning Alert and Response System etc. will be undertaken and data used to generate evidence to guide interventions. The project will coordinate with the Health Sector and all associated working groups such as the working groups for sexual and reproductive health (SRH), epidemiology and MHPSS, as well as the Emergency Preparedness and Response Committee. Integration of services with other sectors will continue, aiming for partners to implement integrated health and nutrition services by the end of 2025. Integration will be fostered with the WASH, protection and GBV sectors.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

While stable, the health of Rohingya refugees and surrounding host communities remains vulnerable to emergencies and requires improvement. As of September 2023, the crude mortality rate in UNHCR's operational area comprised 0.27/1,000/month. However, the rate of maternal and newborn deaths in the camps is unacceptably high; according to the Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) Working Group, as of October 2023, 68 maternal deaths had been reported while 21% of all mortalities comprised stillbirths and neonatal deaths (Epidemiological Working Group). As of September 2023, 510,884 consultations were attended by UNHCR-supported health facilities, with skin disease accounting for the majority of morbidity. A total of 12,229 cases and 16 fatalities as a result of unprecedented dengue incidence in 2023 were reported as of September 2023 (Health Sector Monthly Bulletin, September 2023). Skin conditions increased significantly, especially scabies, with 39.9% of the camp population suffering from the clinical prevalence of scabies. The Community Health Working Group (CHWG) will play a crucial role in mass drug administration at the household level through direct observation therapy utilizing the community health workforce. Sporadic outbreaks of dengue, diphtheria and cholera were reported throughout 2023. To limit the spread of communicable diseases, there is a constant need for community engagement, as well as collaboration with WASH to promote prevention and response, while case management capabilities must be strengthened. Prioritizing vaccination access for children under five years old is crucial given that inadequate vaccination coverage raises the risk of diseases which can be prevented by vaccination. Statistics show how important the community health system is: as of September 2023, the community health workforce in all camps had referred some 1,048, 449 refugees for medical care, including 261,715 for the treatment of skin diseases and 17,532 with dengue symptoms. To boost the uptake of family planning services, there is a critical need to prioritize the use of the community health workforce in distributing family planning commodities in line with the family planning strategy. Additionally, it is imperative that CHWs are trained to measure blood pressure and refer patients to healthcare facilities to improve the identification and management of non-communicable diseases. Maternal mortality is disproportionately high even though the portion of women giving birth in health facilities comprises 82% as of September 2023. There is a gap in identifying high-risk pregnancies, contributing to a high number of maternal deaths in healthcare facilities, even though 78% of ANC 4 coverage demonstrates the high uptake of ANC services by pregnant mothers. Reducing the number of maternal and neonatal deaths requires capacity building as well as continuous on-the-job orientation and training. In order to pinpoint causes of death and lower maternal and newborn mortality rates, it is essential to prioritize action-oriented recommendations and place a strong emphasis on timely audits of maternal and perinatal deaths. In the camps, referrals represent one of the most significant issues. In September 2023, UNHCR supported 5,910 referrals for life-saving interventions, referring patients in need of secondary and tertiary healthcare to facilities outside the camps. Access to secondary and tertiary care is restricted to chronic cases due to resource constraints, while there is a lack of services in the local public health facilities. By September 2023, over 6,230 people had benefited from clinical mental health consultations in UNHCR-supported facilities, psychologists provided counseling to 3,985 individuals and 214,718 people had been reached through community-based MHPSS activities, including through 2,133 follow-up visits. Going forward, maintaining continuous support supervision and addressing the technical capacity gap will remain critical.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project adheres to the outcomes of the 2022 Health Sector rationalization exercise, which entailed closing four healthcare facilities in order to reduce the overall number. In the upcoming years, more efforts will be undertaken to achieve integrated service delivery, which represents a more cost-effective and sustainable model through the streamlining of services. Two additional health facilities will be closed as part of this exercise. Through planning the implementation of these services within a camp by one partner - or by supporting partners who implement both health and nutrition together - the project will continue to promote the integration of health and nutrition services (Camp 4, 4Extension, 3, and 2 West, 1 West, 1 East). Key initiatives promoting the integration of health and nutrition services for children and pregnant and lactating women will be included in the workplans of all health and nutrition facilities in order to support this strategy. Regarding community health, the project will advance the multifaceted community worker model, through which health workers offer nutrition counseling in addition to health services. This project will continue to support the distribution of general health card linked to UNHCR’s registration team to support rationalization throughout the camps.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will ensure that mental health and psychosocial support services (MHPSS) are available in the areas where not only health, but also protection services, are delivered. This includes community-based protection, gender-based violence (GBV) services such as clinical management of rape in women's and girls' safe spaces and child protection services. Health facilities will promote accessibility for persons with physical and mental disabilities through service provision and infrastructure enhancements. The project will ensure that religious and faith leaders, GBV case workers and child protection staff are engaged in order to support vulnerable populations. To link registration services with health services, the project will utilize the global distribution tool for health services when advantageous, such as for the distribution of general health cards. The project will ensure that the different needs and diversities of the population, including people with disabilities, are taken into account in planning and service delivery through community-based feedback mechanisms.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will provide services for specific age groups, disaggregating data based on the various age groups. Specific needs of different age categories will be considered when planning health services, including separate consultation rooms and in-patient facilities for children and adults. Adolescent sexual and reproductive health services will continue at health facilities and adolescent-friendly spaces will be maintained to support responsiveness to the needs of adolescents. The community health workforce will support older persons needing healthcare services by escorting them or arranging for transfer to health facilities and back to the household. Programs for women of reproductive age (15 – 49 years) will be strengthened, to ensure that the reproductive health of women is safeguarded. UNHCR is committed to preventing child abuse and exploitation of refugees; all partner staff will be trained on child safeguarding principles in order to understand the standards and conduct expected from all staff, volunteers, and contractors working in health facilities. Messages to improve the prevention of any form of abuse among the beneficiaries of all ages, boys and girls aged under five years, adolescent girls, and women of reproductive age will be included in health education sessions to increase awareness. Reporting any form of child abuse or behaviors violating the safeguarding principles will be monitored by UNHCR and partners, and immediate actions to resolve the issues identified will be followed up.The project will additionally provide services to people with disabilities at the health facilities including eye care, hearing losses, physiotherapy among other. The health facilities will be disability friendly to facilitate access of services to this vulnerable group.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR will monitor the feedback and complaints mechanism put in place by the partner to ensure that they adhere to the acceptable UNHCR standards, including capacity enhancement of all programme and clinical managers to ensure the AAP mainstreaming as a continuous monitoring point (with support from AAP focal point in UNHCR).Accountability to the affected population will be ensured throughout the project cycle, and participation of the refugees in the different stages of programming through consultations will be implemented. UNHCR will ensure that partners establish feedback and complaints mechanisms in all the centres with a clear plan to address any issues that arise in an integrated manner. This will also include the provision of different methods of communication with refugees, who will be engaged in awareness raising efforts with regard to the different programs, new programmes or any changes that may have an impact on their access to health services.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In 2023, the health sector carried out the GBV safety audit for health facilities across all the UNHCR-managed camps. In, 2024 UNHCR will strengthen the implementation of recommendations for improvements of the sites and training of staff on GBV, referrals, and linkages with relevant sectors for additional support. Assessments of partners' understanding of key protection principles and mainstreaming will be jointly implemented by UNHCR's protection and health units. All UNHCR health partners including the staff in the field have been trained on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), gender-based violence (GBV), child protection and referral pathways between health and protection in the camps, and the refresher training will be carried out in 2024 to ensure the necessary staff capacity to support refugees to access services in a dignified manner. In addition to the above, other components identified as potential protection risks will be monitored and mitigation strategies identified. Regular monitoring of protection-related issues will be ensured throughout the project implementation, as well as necessary actions to prevent GBV experienced by women, girls, boys, or men who are receiving assistance through health programs.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. Semi-permanent and reinforced structures will be used to maintain health facilities in order to guard against environmental hazards. To lower carbon emissions, the project will use solar power in healthcare facilities. Through their activities, community health workers will improve health and make communities more resilient to pandemic-related disasters. Facilities will continue to have the ability to respond to fires in order to put out any that might start there. In order to minimize negative effects, the project will assist emergency medical teams in responding quickly to health emergencies and hazards.
