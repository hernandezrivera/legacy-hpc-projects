Project Summary

NGO X proposes to provide lifesaving safe water to approximately 851,935 vulnerable individuals in Idleb Governorate, North West Syria. This will be implemented via the provision of essential support to 51 Water Stations (WSs) and 4 Water Units (WUs). NGO X has extensive experience delivering water supply programming in Idleb since 2014. In 2021 NGO X provided clean safe water to the homes of over one million people in Idleb via 66 WS and 4 WU. The proposed project builds upon the experience of having successfully delivered several similar projects funded by OCHA, FCDO, ECHO and BHA.
The aim of the action is to ensure access to reliable, safe, and clean drinking water to vulnerable conflict-affected communities including host and displaced populations in high priority northern sub-districts of Idleb Governorate. Core activities will include procurement of electricity, fuel, minor infrastructure rehabilitation, training to water station operators, coaching to WUs, water quality monitoring and hygiene promotion.
The proposed action will enable people living in the targeted areas to access a sufficient quantity of clean and safe drinking water. Improving the access to safe water will reduce the time spent on collecting water, reduce protection risks and prevent the spread of waterborne diseases, often affecting children. Following WASH cluster standards, between 25 - 30 litres/person/day (LPD) will be piped directly to households (HH) to ensure equitable access for all community members. It should be noted that due to funding constraints and limited water network capacity, it may not be possible to go over 25 LPD.
In target areas, to complement the water supply activities, HHs—including school children, WU staff and vulnerable individuals benefiting from NGO X food or cash distributions will be reached with essential hygiene promotion sessions adapted to COVID-19 best working practices as outlined by the WASH cluster. Themes covered will include water safety, water conservation, COVID-19 and safe hygiene practices.
NGO X will also provide WASH services in camps and IDP sites. NGO X will install water supply infrastructure provide fuel, oil and other consumables to camp water stations. NGO X will extend sewage lines, construct communal latrines and undertake regular desludging services of septic tanks. To ensure adequate and appropriate solid waste collection, NGO X will also install solid waste bins and undertake solid waste collection.

4 - Needs

In December 2021 over 1.1 million individuals in Idleb depended on NGO X supported water networks to obtain free and safe water piped to their homes. Due to a reduction in funding 300,000 of these people are currently without piped water, without additional funding this number will increase. In Idleb governorate 19% of HHs are not connected to the water network and 57% of HHs state that they don’t have sufficient water. 63% use some form of water trucking spending around 4 USD per month with 54% of key informants stating that high water prices are a significant barrier to accessing sufficient water (REACH 2021).IDPs are the most financially vulnerable group and within this group female IDPs, female headed IDP HHs, including widows, heads of IDP HHs with disabilities and recently displaced IDPs are the most financially constrained and often facing higher barriers for accessing income opportunities, which also affect their access to WASH services (HNO 2021). In Azaz camps local councils and camp management have expressed that they do not have the capacity to fund water supply, solid waste and desludging services. Desludging would cost households around 3.3 USD per month, around 3% of the SMEB (NGOX Market Report Aug 2021). In Azaz and Afrin, over 100 Leishmaniasis cases have been reported every week since January 2021 (ACU dashboard). COVID 19 mitigation measures were in place in 49% of communities in November. However, adherence was only reported in 1% of communities.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by NGOX staff of females and vulnerable persons. Mitigation: • Conduct PSEA trainings with staff and inform of sanctions if PSEA policies are violated • Ensure Code of Conduct and PSEA protocols are shared and signed by partners and financial service providers • Consult women, men, girls and boys and persons with disabilities and older people about their access to delivery mechanisms and identify any additional safeguarding measures that need to be put in place for their safe access or identify alternative delivery mechanisms • Conduct awareness raising activities with beneficiaries on entitlements and rights, and NGOX’s feedback mechanism • Monitor the safety of delivery mechanisms and modality through PDMs and the feedback mechanism. • NGOX has a SEA policy in place to address these types of experiences. When reported or suspected, responsible staff will follow procedures in place to investigate and respond to harm caused.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Continued access to targeted areas and vulnerable populations, while security conditions remain stable; - Sufficient and timely access to required inputs through procurement, available transportation remains; - Strong coordination and collaboration with local authorities and humanitarian actors in the AoO prevails; - NGO X's strategies and mechanisms to prevent armed and sanctioned actors from interference in programmes Risks: - risk of international and Syrian staff losing legal status; - sudden border closures disrupting operations; - fraud including banking, cash management, procurement, transport, and programming; - Tools/repair supplies not available: - Fuel theft or misuse at water stations; Aid diversion. - NGO X loses access to some areas in Idleb or Aleppo governorate as a result of shifting political and military control on the ground. Mitigation: - Safety and Security Evacuation Plans and Standard Operating Procedures in place, and an experienced security team on both sides of the border; - Ongoing communication with actors operational across the operational area, and continued adherence to Acceptance Strategies built on community and stakeholder understanding of NGO X’s programming; - NGO X has a strong reconciliation system. Any suspected cases are reported by the WASH team directly to area management team and to SMT to open an investigation.
