Project Summary

The current socioeconomic conditions compounded by the blockade, energy crisis, salary cuts for PA
employees, high unemployment and volatile security situation in Gaza are undermining its health sector.
This has led to high and increasing rates of health problems among the population who have no
alternative means to access healthcare under the prevailing humanitarian conditions. Meanwhile, the
Agency's ability to respond to emergency health needs in Gaza is greatly constrained by the tight
funding situation compromising its ability to respond as needed.
On top of an already on the verge of collapsing public health sector, the ongoing humanitarian crisis
that started on 30 March 2018 has resulted, so far, in an important number of casualties that can hardly
be absorbed. The needs for both urgent treatment and emergency preparedness of the health sector in
Gaza are therefore extremely important.
In light of these conditions, and within UNRWA strategic outcome of 'Refugees' health is protected and
disease burden is reduced' this proposed intervention aims, under the emergency health umbrella, to
support the provision of critical healthcare for (mainly) Palestine refugees in Gaza Strip through (1)
Providing lifesaving and urgent medicines and supplies to refugees, (2) Supporting health interventions
received outside UNRWA facilities, and (3) Supporting rehabilitative care for needed patients (4).

Needs

Gaza population, constituted by 72 percent refugees, 49.5 per cent of whom are females, is relativelyyoung with 42.6 per cent aged 0-14 years old. With 80 per cent of its two million of citizens dependingon humanitarian assistance, Gaza remains under protracted humanitarian crisis. This process of gradual deterioration has left essential services in Gaza barely able to function, thus directly impacting people's basic right to health access, which implies protection concerns. Hence, it is getting more and more complicated for Palestine refugees to access healthcare due to:(1) Restriction in terms of people access: The tightened humanitarian space is making the delivery ofhealthcare challenging. As an example, in October 2017, only 55 per cent of patient applications for apermit to exit Gaza through the Erez checkpoint for hospital appointments were approved. (2) Restriction in terms of good access: For several years, the Strip is suffering from severe shortage inthe medicine supply. In addition, the health care system is suffering from increasing strain due to lack ofspare parts and equipment needed for maintaining and upgrading health facilities. Hence, according tothe Ministry of Health, in the 2018 so far, 41 per cent of essential drugs have completely depleted.(3) Power shortage: Gaza has been faced with a chronic electricity deficit for over a decade that hasfurther deteriorated since April 2017 and has massively impacted the availability of essential servicesparticularly health, water and sanitation services. For instance, health emergency and diagnostic services are at risk, including intensive care operations. In addition, the waiting time for non-elective surgery at Shifa Hospital, which is one of the largest Gaza hospitals, has increased from five months in January 2017 to 14 months in August 2017.Since 30 March 2018, Gaza is witnessing a significant increase in Palestinian causalities in the context of mass demonstrations taking place along Israel's perimeter fence with Gaza. As of 4 October 2018, according to OCHA, more than 21,280 people have been injured - including 4,250 children and 1,950 females - and 11,180 people have been hospitalized in public hospitals. It is expected that the number of people sustaining injuries requiring hospitalization during this crisis and thereafter will grow.In addition, the need of the mental health services in the Gaza Strip have increased significantly due to the Great March of Return demonstrations taking place along the perimeter fence with Israel since 30 March 2018, especially for those who were injured or who experienced the loss of their close relatives. As a result of the current crisis in Gaza, the WHO estimated that approximately 10,400 people (5% of the population) will have severe mental health problems, and 41,700 (20% of the population) will have mild to moderate problems requiring mental health counselling , including 26,000 children. A 2016 survey conducted by UNRWA found that 55% of sampled patients attending UNRWA health centres demonstrated poor mental health and well being, with 70% being identified as being potentially depressed. UNRWA data has further shown that 80% of women who seek counselling are also experiencing GBV. Due to the three problems mentioned above, Gaza's health sector - of which capacities were alreadyoverstretched before the crisis - is struggling to cope with the mass influx of causalities. For instance,according to WHO Health Cluster in the occupied Palestinian territories, as of the end of April 2018, 75essential drugs and 190 essential disposables were immediately needed. Therefore, the needs in termsof supporting the public health sector and scaling up the health services already provided by UNRWA to70 per cent of people across the Strip are very important and should be met rapidly in order to cope withthe ongoing crisis.

Activities

Within the above prevailing conditions, the provision of health care services to Palestine refugees inGaza remains a critical humanitarian response. These services are the minimum required to addresssome of the humanitarian needs in Gaza and their effects. UNRWA provides primary healthcare to over1.3 million Palestine refugees across its 22 Health Centres in Gaza Strip. The deterioratingsocio-economic conditions have seen the UNRWA health system stretched as it tried to provideessential health to refugees. The Agency has had to rely on fuel powered generators to minimizeinterruption of service delivery due to power outages. On the other hand, refugees are becoming lessable to afford the cost of secondary and/or tertiary healthcare provided outside UNRWA, the Agency ishaving increased obligations to support this kind of treatment. This is being provided through coveringthe hospitalization cost of medical treatment received at hospitals with which UNRWA has establishedagreements, or through reimbursing the patients for the medical treatment costs they incur.In response to the ongoing humanitarian crisis, UNRWA is increasing its response capacity in order toabsorb the important number of casualties and to be prepared for a possible escalation of violence.Indeed, as most of the injured people are Palestine refugees, the demand for urgent and short-termhealthcare - including the provision of specific lifesaving drugs - is on the rise and UNRWA is planningaccordingly for further support to treatment received out of its installations. Therefore, UNRWA is also scaling up its primary health care services provided across its 22 Health Centres in order to offer long term and rehabilitative assistance to these injured people. UNRWA’s 22 health centres have recorded an increase in injury-related cases, with patients often presenting severe and long-term needs for injury care, rehabilitation through physiotherapy and mental health support. Consequently, there is need for UNRWA to increase its response capacity in order to absorb the increasing number of casualties presenting short and long tern medical needs. In response, the Agency plans to further support access to medical services through provision of lifesaving medicines to 8,056 urgent case patients, supporting access to hospital services and tertiary medical care as well as 3,960 physiotherapy to patients recovering from injuries sustained during the demonstrations. The mental health interventions at UNRWA health centres support primarily women (90% of cases) including women who are experiencing gender-based violence (GBV) issues (60% of all cases). The intervention is a comprehensive, structured and child/person-centred model of care with the psycho-education sessions being the first line of interventions which is recommended by the mh GAP model and align with the mental health needs for people in Gaza; in addition to group counselling intervention and an individual counselling approach. The health counsellors are critical in supporting patients who present serious mental health difficulties that require more focused and structured counselling, while they are playing a critical role in addressing GBVs.
