Project Summary

Emergency Primary Health Services IDP & Host Communities is one year aimed to provide integrated basic primary care services for IDPs and host communities in Yei and Morobo Counties. The project will be implemented in 2 Primary Health Care units, 1 Primary Health Care Centre and a Hospital county; and 1 PHCU and 2 Mobile Clinics in Morobo County in addition rapid response missions and community level activities, targeting a total population of 23, 265 and 12,109 individuals in Morobo and Yei respectively.
The main objective of the project is to provide integrated, lifesaving emergency healthcare access to vulnerable populations (women, children and elderly) focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality. The overarching objective of the project feeds into 3 strategic Objectives of South Sudan Humanitarian response Plan 2019 which includes; 1- Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys; 2-Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through provision of specialized and integrated services and 3-Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks
Key project interventions include reproductive and child health, disease surveillance and response; and mainstreaming cross cutting health issues which include mental and psychosocial support, sexual and gender based violence, malnutrition, WASH; strengthening of health systems, especially in Health facilities that support. Because of ongoing Ebola Virus Disease outbreak in democratic Republic of Congo; specific intervention to strengthen disease surveillance in the context of EVD some be implemented in project areas.

Needs

Yei and Morobo Counties are one of the worst Counties affected by current Humanitarian Crisis in South Sudan, with health systems severely affected., Only thirteen of the forty health Facilities in Yei County are functional while in Morobo 3 out of 13 health facilities are functional. This limits access to basic health services to both IDPs and Host communities. Meanwhile IDPs and host communities in opposition controlled areas have completely no access to formal health services. While the functional health services do not offer comprehensive and quality services as envisaged in Basic package of Health and Nutrition for South Sudan. Children under 5years and women of reproductive age and the elderly bear the brand of the current crisis. Therefore, only …. Of children under 5 years accessed penta3 and another … were immunized against measles. An estimated …. Children are at risk of measles outbreak. While ….of women of pregnant women accessed ANC 4th visit and …. Were able to deliver with the assistance of skilled birth attendant. Only … Primary Health Care Centers provide Basic Emergency Obstetric Neonatal Services, while Yei State Hospital remains the only facility with Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care in Yei River state. There is virtual no functional referral system for emergency Maternal and Child health care. Other specialized services and cross cutting issues such as Sexual and Gender Based violence, mental health and Psychosocial support, Nutrition, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis are not adequately integrated and or mainstreamed in the health services in the health facilities. This is due to lack of personnel with the appropriate skills, vertically programming and donor interest; where donors limit the services that can be support. Currently sexual and Gender based violence services including Clinical Management of rape (CMR) are only limited to Yei town and the services are provided in only 2 health facilities. While referral pathways and continuum of care after initial clinic visit is limited for SGBV victims and survivors. Since February 2017-September 2018, some 250 (121 rape and 129 other forms of GBV) clients have been managed in the 2 health Facilities in Yei, while there is no comprehensive SGBV services including CMR in Morobo County. However, majority of SGBV cases are unreported and cases seen in Yei State Hospital and St. Barkitha PHCC represents the tip of the Iceberg. Lack of reporting due to stigma, no or poor referral pathways, lack of privacy in health facilities are some of the reasons for which most of SGBV cases are not reported. While, much is not known about cases of SGBVs in areas under opposition control. Disease surveillance, epidemic preparedness and response remains a challenge in Yei and Morobo Counties. Most of the health facilities not submitting weekly reports on time. All the health facilities are not using WARS and therefore detecting alerts early is a challenge. The performance of surveillance is worsened by poor mobile network connectivity in Yei. The situation of Yei and Morobo is complicated by the ongoing Ebola Virus disease outbreak in Democratic Republic of Congo. Yei and Morobo Counties share a long border with Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) with 12 known border crossing points where there is continues movement of people between South Sudan and Democratic Republic of Congo. Control of movement and screening in the points of entry is complicated by the insecurity; and two communities’ exits; those in opposition controlled areas with unhindered and frequent movement to DRC and those communities in government controlled areas that then interacts with the Communities in opposition controlled areas. It is also important to note most of the South Sudanese in opposition controlled areas along the border with DRC are registered refugees in DRC and cross monthly to DRC for their payment and food ratios, posing serious risks of importation of Ebola Virus disease

Yes

The consultative meetings were clustered according Age and gender, this enable provision content to the different age groups and gender, during the meetings, health issues affecting specific age and gender were discussed. Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The identified social barriers include gender inequalities, harmful cultural norms such subordinate position of women in the community, women’s lack of ownership of resources and discrimination of girls from formal education; which will affect their ability to seek health services and take decisions regarding personal health Institutional barriers at health facilities may include; access barriers to people with disability due to poor infrastructure design suck lack rams at service entry points, poor design of sanitary facilities; communication barriers where service providers may not know particular language including sign language; unwillness of some clients to be attended to by service provider of opposite, especially midwifery cadre may deter some the clients from accessing services. Inadequate privacy and lack adolescent friendly space in the clinics may present a challenge to access. Long waiting times at health facilities can deter clients from accessing services The following shall be considered to address the barriers to access;Awareness creation and dialogue on harmful cultural norms which affect access of services by women, boys and girlsImprove infrastructure in Health facilities through minor renovations to ensure service points are easily accessible to people with disable Train service providers to identify needs of disable and special groups such as adolescents, people living with HIV and Elderly. Appropriate recruitments and deployments to ensure challenges with communication do not exist; and appropriate staff are deployed in gender sensitive service points such as maternity ward. Adequate staffing and appropriate management of duty rosters to avoid long waiting time for clientsImprove privacy in service points through minor renovations and provision of screens; and design patient flow in service points. Establishment adolescent friendly clinics in Hospital and PHCCs to provide tailored services for adolescents
