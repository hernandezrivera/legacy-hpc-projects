Project Summary

This project aims to improve education service provision in NW Syria; a geographic location which has absorbed a lot of IDPs as a result of the waves movements during 2017-2018. The overall objective of the project is to increase access and quality of education to conflict-affected children. The specific objective is to scale up safe and equitable access to formal education for 5700 school-aged girls and boys between ages 5 -17 “boys: 2555, & girls: 3145”. Plus, 300 schools’ personnel “166 men, & 134 women”. In addition to what was mentioned, this project will guarantee the continuity of support for the educational process in the areas of intervention.
One of IhsanRD education project is currently supporting ten schools. These schools were fully supported by the following activities:
• School personnel incentives,
• Enroll students in Formal Education,
• Textbooks distribution,
• Conducting PSS activities.
• Establish one CFS in each school,
• Teaching materials distribution for students & teachers,
• PTC,
• Capacity building for the teaching staff,
• Covering the running costs, and
• Providing with safety plans and equipment. Plus,
• Winterization needs will be covered by installing heaters and fuel tanks in addition to winter clothes distribution for all the students.
The current fund for those schools will be ending by the end of Jun 2019. So, the proposed project will guarantee the continuity of support for the educational process in the areas of intervention.
The project proposed activities will be as follows:
- The provision of School personnel incentives.
- Covering the running costs for the proposed schools.
- Establishing/updating the students' enrollment records.
- Procure and distribute school bags for students.
- Establishing one CFS in each school.
- Summer school activities.
- Conducting awareness sessions.
- Updating the schools’ safety plans, and install/renew first aid equipment.
- PTCs.
- Conducting ASER tests.
- Fuel distribution during the winter season.
- Providing/printing textbooks for students.
The locations on the sub-district level are Atareb, Daret Azza, Haritan.

Needs

As the conflict in Syria enters its eighth year, the scale, severity, and complexity of needs across Syria remain overwhelming. Some 13.1 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 5.6 million people are in acute need due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services. Over half of the country’s population has fled; 4.8 million people have sought refuge in countries neighboring Syria and beyond, and 6.3 million people are internally displaced. In many cases, households have been displaced multiple times in response to endless new waves of conflict. Lack of safety and security constitutes the greatest barrier to accessing education, with schools and education facilities seemingly under attack and occupied by armed actors. Humanitarian Needs Overview, Syria, 2018 The current population in the proposed location of intervention “which is Atareb, Daret Azza, & Haritan sub-districts” is around 112,460 according to HNO 2018. In January 2018, the estimated number of IDP arrivals in assessed communities ranged from 13,085 - 15,305 individuals, a 59% increase compared to the number of arrivals reported in December. The demographic map in Syria has faced drastic changes since the beginning of the conflict. This change has been driven by massive displacement waves due to the security conditions. Which has made the demographic map in Syria constantly fluid and subject to change on a daily basis? and the educational condition of their children in the elementary school ages that’s in northern suburbs of Idleb and western suburbs of Aleppo, more than half of the displaced people are children. According to recent assessment that conducted in Aleppo governate by Big Heat Foundation at 02 Jan 2019 showed that Incentives came as first key need with 39%, school supplies came secondly in 26%, and infrastructure rehabilitation as 20%. Additionally, learning materials (like teachers/students’ kits) is hugely needed. And most teachers are working as volunteers without getting paid, a thing that affects their wellbeing and children’s learning attainment. Also, the inadequate support for schools particularly the winterization (like heaters, fuel, class insulation) has had a direct impact on enrolled children's’ willingness to come to school regularly or even drop out. All the ten proposed schools are government buildings administratively affiliated to the Directorate of Education in the interim government. These schools were in-depth assessed, and their locations are as the following: Aleppo – Jabal Saman – Atareb “3 schools”Aleppo – Janbal Saman – Daret Azza “5 schools”Aleppo – Jabal Saman – Haritan “2 schools”Before supporting these schools, more than 80% of teachers and school staff are not receiving monthly incentives and work as volunteers. Also, there was a huge gap in learning materials which affected teachers’ curriculum delivery. The war that has been going on in the region for 8 years has caused massive damage to schools’ infrastructure as well as lack of resources to repair or rebuild the affected schools particularly. Through IhsanRD intervention, these schools were fully rehabilitated and equipped. By continuing the support of these schools, 5700 schools aged boys and girls in addition to 300 schools’ personnel will be still in the education process.

Implementation of activities

The activities will be as the following:Support teacher incentives: IhsanRD will provide incentives to teachers in supported learning spaces based on WoS and Education Cluster guidelines and cluster standardization. Teachers will be supported with regular, monthly salary payments to ensure basic needs are met, and that they are able to focus on providing children with safe learning environments. Covering the running costs for the proposed schools.IhsanRD will cover the costs of water, gas, internet fees… that are needed each month for each school by collecting the needed items, materials, … per month in coordination with the school management.Establishing/updating the students' enrollment records for formal education.IhsanRD will make a registration drive to support enrolment into the proposed schools in the targeted areas. In addition to updating all schools’ students records in coordination with the schools’ management.Distribute textbooks, learning materials, stationery, and school bags: Children in all supported learning spaces will be provided with learning materials such as stationery, exercise books, backpacks, and textbooks in order to promote access, achievement, and retention in education. CFS establishment and equipped: Establishing one child-friendly space for children in each school in order to conduct the PSS activities esp. in during the summer school. Establish or support effective school management mechanisms (PTA Establishment): IhsanRD will support the establishment of gender-balanced Parent-Teacher Associations, including recruitment and training of members in all established learning centers. IhsanRD will support PTAs in the development of School Action Plans which leverage community strengths to address issues affecting educational access and quality. PTAs will also be targeted for information on school enrolment processes. Regular monthly meetings will be supported in targeted program sites.Providing/printing textbooks for students.- After conducting an in-depth need assessment for the shortage in textbooks for the students, IhsanRD will print the needed textbooks, then distribute them.Winterization “Fuel distribution during the winter season”- IhsanRD will contract with a supplier(s) in order to provide schools with the needed amounts during winter season.- The current implemented project received extremely positive feedback from several stakeholders, including the school management, local council, and the education committee of the local council as well. Many lessons learned will be incorporated into the project in order to ensure similar levels of success. Furthermore, IhsanRD currently has several different programs on-going in the district of Jebel Samman. These activities include a child-friendly space, PSS mobile outreach teams, emergency food assistance distribution, and water system rehabilitation. Through these several programs, IhsanRD has been able to build a strong trust with local stakeholders and beneficiaries, enabling a strong presence in the district overall.

Protection Risk Analysis

The number one risk is security, as schools have been one of the main targets in the conflict. Although the situation has slightly improved due to the cessation of hostilities, it is unknown how long it will last. The security situation is not one that IhsanRD can play a major role in mitigating, besides perhaps providing additional safety and security training for teachers to use with their students in the case that security concerns arise. Other mitigation will be the pausing of activities in the case of major conflict escalation including rehabilitation and training (as was previously done in past Ihsan education programs). In the case that schooling is stopped, IhsanRD, in cooperation with the parent-teacher committee will look into alternative mechanisms for education delivery, such as smaller sessions, home-based learning etc. Finally, IhsanRD has chosen a location that is relatively safer and has historically been a location where IDPs flee to, not where people are fleeing from. One risk includes the disinterest of parents to take part in the parent-teacher committees. As the context in Syria is one of war, there are often priorities that families list, and most often they focus on food and health. The secondary is usually education and PSS support. In order to mitigate this risk, the IhsanRD team will have to conduct strong advocacy and outreach during the project set up phase of the project when meeting with stakeholders. Furthermore, the rules and mandate of the committee will be formed by the community itself (with Ihsan field team guidance), so as to encourage ownership and interest in the topic at hand. Risks about procurement under this project, IhsanRD may face many risks such as lack of materials availability, transportation risks, worrying about suppliers’ commitment. IhsanRD will deal with trusted suppliers from IhsanRD vendor list and cooperate with the Local Council to reduce the transportation authorities, in the case of materials lack IhsanRD will import the materials from Turkey.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions 1: changes regarding the security situation in the target areas, including the deterioration of safety and mobility conditions, as well as the displacement of staff and/or beneficiaries, in ways that delay or exclude program access and provision (MEDIUM-HIGH): Mitigation measure: IhsanRD monitors the security situation in Northern Syria, and establish emergency response and contingency plans to deal with changes in stability and access. These plans include relocating program sites if necessary, as well as using alternative modalities of service delivery, such as homeschooling.Assumptions 2: Increased internal displacement, both into and out of the target areas (MEDIUM-HIGH): Mitigation measure: Our monitoring of conflict dynamics includes close attention to migration flows in Northern Syria. In instances where we are unable to meet the educational needs of IDPs arriving at the catchment areas, head teachers and school Protection Facilitators will attempt to link children and their families with other schools in the community. Conversely, when conflict factors cause students and teachers at our schools to flee, we will coordinate with local authorities to fill personnel gaps and continue service delivery, if the security situation is safe to do so. Assumptions 3: Border closures and delays in transporting project goods and supplies (MEDIUM): Mitigation measure: If supply lines are disrupted due to border closures, we rely on alternative channels for procurement in local markets, where possible. As a contingency measure, we also divide the program supply between warehouses in and outside of Syria. Assumptions 4: During closure periods, we also undertake advocacy with relevant parties to appeal for resumed humanitarian access and entry instability of local markets (MEDIUM): Mitigation measure: the high fluctuation of currency and market prices inside Syria can affect the exchange value of program goods and salaries. We monitor exchange rates and market dynamics to anticipate and plan for such changes and adapt budgeting and planning accordingly. Where possible, we set fixed prices with contractors and supply providers.
