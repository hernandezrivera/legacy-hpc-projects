Project Summary

The project intends to support vulnerable households with an unconditional cash transfer to help the households meet their immediate basic needs, provision of training on best agronomic practices, and provision of agricultural inputs aimed to support households to improve production. The provision of conditional cash through cash for work initiatives has a dual objective of supporting vulnerable households to generate income to meet immediate basic needs while undertaking activities that aim to rehabilitate, rebuild and restore community assets. Activities that support the conservation of the environment will be promoted through the cash for work. The village savings and loan association and income-generating activities will support households to build resilience, move toward economic recovery, and gradually become self-resilient.

1 - Needs

Over a decade now, the conflict in northeast Nigeria has taken a heavy toll on the population. Approximately 2,191,193 individuals (445,852 households) remain displaced in North-East Nigeria. According to HNO 2021, the projected figures for people in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 in the BAY states is 8.7 million people (2.4M boys, 2.7M girls, 1.9M women and 1.7M men). Of the people in need, 1.9M are IDPs, 4.8M are host community members, 0.9M are people with a physical disability. 5.1M people need food security and livelihoods assistance. The majority of the people in need are in Borno State, which hosts 81% of the IDPs, of whom 54% stay in IDP camps (HNO 2021). One in four of the affected population is under 5 years of age. Women and children are most affected by the crisis, constituting 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. Borno State is the worst hit and hosts the highest number of IDPs (1,633,829). In Yobe State there are 162,394 IDPs (IOM DTM Round 37, 2021. According to the latest CH report (October 2021) in the projected situation (June to August 2022), there will be about 18,030,674 persons in the combined critical phases of food and nutrition insecurity. This figure is higher than 12,831,034 persons in the same period in 2021(June-August, 2021). The COVID-19 pandemic and the associated containment measures disrupted the household income-generating activities (IGAs) and led to sub-optimal livelihood functionality, reduced income sources while most livelihood activities showed deterioration (FEWSNET, October 2021). It has been projected that several households in Borno and Yobe states are likely to resort to emergency coping strategies that may result in a further collapse in livelihoods in the absence of conscious livelihood intervention stimulus packages. The Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) estimates that more than 453,000 people are displaced in Zamfara, Katsina, Sokoto, Kano and Kaduna by September 2021. In September 2021, more than 150,000 and 140,000 displaced people lived in Katsina and Zamfara as underscored by the DTM – an increase by 14 and 38 per cent, respectively, since June 2021. According to DTM, armed banditry and kidnapping were the most reported reasons for displacement in the states of Zamfara (95%), Sokoto (85%) and Katsina (75%). The majority or 72 per cent of IDPs are children or women, including 27 per cent of the total IDP population under six years old. Only 17 per cent of IDPs are living in camps or camp-like settings. Some of the IDP locations thus remain unknown or not recognised as such by local authorities.The majority of households in the Northwest that are affected by kidnapping, banditry, and herder/farmer conflict, particularly in Sokoto, Zamfara, and Katsina States, will remain displaced and are expected to have difficulty engaging in normal livelihood activities. Additionally, these populations are mainly dependent on limited community support and markets, to access food. They also face limited income-earning opportunities during the lean season. Therefore, these households will continue to face food consumption gaps, hence may require lifesaving assistance.According to the October 2021 Cadre harmonise report, in the projected situation during June to August 2022, 16 LGAs out of 24 LGAs in Sokoto state will be in phase 3 of the food and nutrition insecurity. The report also shows that about 837,640 individuals are in the combined critical phases of food and nutrition insecurity in the current situation(October to December 2021), however, the number will rise to about 1,214,823 individuals in the projected situation(June to August 2022).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed intervention shall take into consideration gender concerns by prioritizing the minimum standards of protection and gender to ensure referral of any protection/GBV concerns received through the FCRM mechanism. The collection of age and sex-segregated data during targeting and registration will ensure that the intended beneficiaries are included and supported with no discrimination on any basis. The project activities shall be shortened in duration to allow women to have time to care for their babies and engage in other productive work. Provision shall be made for child/baby friendly spaces in locations and venues of demo sites, meetings, trainings and distribution points. The distributions shall take place near to the locations of the beneficiary homes to avoid having the PLW walking long distances. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries especially women and girls, vendor locations shall be sited in areas considered to be safe by the community and separate lines for PWDs, elderly and most vulnerable PLW made for priority service. To diffuse possible gender tensions, women and men shall be represented during community meetings and joint decision making on the assistance shall be promoted through sensitization of men and women beneficiaries. Awareness messages shall be tailored in a gender sensitive way that empowers women without causing GBV and household tensions. AAH will ensure that all indicators that could have gender aspects will be age, disability and sex-disaggregated in data collection, analysis, and reporting.To ensure safe and accountable programming, AAH will ensure protection/GBV issues are well assessed, prevented and mitigated both among beneficiaries and among staff. Protection risks at the community level include possible tension between host population and IDPs. AAH project activities shall ensure that beneficiaries are not put at risk due to movement of project beneficiaries from one location to the other. Targeting of beneficiaries will ensure strict inclusion of only households that are residents in the location of intervention.Risk of community transmission of COVID-19 may add to the vulnerabilities of people in the locations of intervention. To mitigate this risk, AAH shall conduct sensitization to beneficiaries and community stakeholders on the pandemic and promote the use of COVID-19 protection protocols such as physical distancing, the use of masks, handwashing and sanitizers by beneficiaries, vendors, staff and stakeholders. The collection of age gender-segregated data will ensure the inclusion of women, children, the elderly and PLW and efforts will ensure that no discrimination on any basis. Furthermore, the project will mainstream protection throughout the project cycle by ensuring that protection principles guide the humanitarian assistance AAH is providing. This includes: ● Ensuring access for affected populations to humanitarian assistance based on needs and promoting non-discriminatory principles (Action Against Hunger Charter) within the program, which is also explicitly outlined in the Code of Conduct, which is signed by all Action Against Hunger staff. ● Monitoring access of the most vulnerable population to humanitarian assistance. In the process of validating target criteria, the M&E team is encouraged to crosscheck lists and minimize exclusion errors. In addition, AAH will report internally when check points or blockades are preventing humanitarian assistance. Special measures will be taken to ensure equality of access for affected people in remote or inaccessible areas.● AAH will reinforce the concept of “right to receive humanitarian assistance” within its own staff and when communicating with stakeholders (NEMA, SEMA, etc.).● AAH will focus on IDPs, returnees and host communities promoting the principle of impartiality.● Assistance to IDPs will not be tied to special legal status, but encourage local authorities to recognize and identify the vulnerability and need

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

AAH will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern, both positive and negative, inform protection, assistance and solutions to programming, and that corrective action taken as appropriate. To achieve this, AAH will: • Establish and maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback; • Allocate human and financial resources to ensure that feedback from persons of concern is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner; • Collaborate with partners in feedback referral and response processes (wherever appropriate).
