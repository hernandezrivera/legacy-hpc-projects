Project Summary

WFP is implementing a nutrition response frame with stronger focus on management and prevention of malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding. The program targets all children aged 6-23 months including those suffering from moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), children aged 24-59 months suffering from MAM and Pregnant and Breastfeeding Women and Girls in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, East, North west and south west regions. The program activities include: 1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment; 2. Management of moderate acute malnutrition in children between the ages of 6-59months; 3. Promotion of infant and young child feeding practices; 4. Distribution of specialized nutritious food or cash transfers to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls(PBWGs); 5. Social and behavioral change communication in favor of good nutrition and health practices; 6. Promotion of the use of the supplementary feeding platforms for provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etc. WFP in 2025 intends to assist about 107,112 nutritionally vulnerable persons in the Far North region.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The crisis in the Lake Chad Basin has led to the influx of almost 124,000 Nigerian refugees in Cameroon, 96 percent of whom are in the Far North region of Cameroon. They are fleeing violent conflict between the government security forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in Nigeria since 2014. Additionally, incursions by NSAGs and clashes over natural resources in the Far North Region have led to internal displacements. Furthermore, the Far North Region is prone to extreme weather conditions, such as devastating floods, usually between August and November every year. Between July and September 2024, torrential rains affected more than 365,000 people with 30 deaths. About 56,000 homes were destroyed, about 82,500 hectares of cultivated farmlands were destroyed, and about 5,300 animals were lost. The conflict, exposure to extreme climatic events and other infrastructural problems have led to more than 453,500 internally displaced people (IDPs). The high poverty levels in the region have triggered very high rates of malnutrition. According to the results of the December 2022 SMART-SENS Nutrition survey, the prevalence of acute malnutrition among children under 5 years of age in the Adamawa, East, North, and Far North regions varies considerably, reaching worrying levels in some regions. The Eastern and Far Northern regions, including internally displaced populations, have acute malnutrition rates ranging from 3.9% to 10.1%. The nutritional situation of children under 5 years of age is particularly critical in the Far North region, with acute malnutrition rates reaching 8.0% among host populations and 10.1% among internally displaced populations, exceeding the 10% intervention threshold defined by the WHO. In addition, internally displaced populations in the Far North and North have high rates of severe acute malnutrition, reaching 2.9% and 2.6% respectively, exceeding the WHO emergency threshold of 2%. In the Far North, more than 70 per cent of the total IDPs are living in host communities. Over 37 per cent of them are children under five and the average number of children per household is 5.8. Together with the host population, they are amongst the most exposed to the lack of access to drinking water, poor access to health care, poor hygiene, epidemics, and the consequences of poor crop harvests, which can lead to rapid deterioration of their nutritional status. The indicators related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, with an exclusive breastfeeding rate of 50 per cent, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods of 33.3 per cent. The proportion of children aged 6 to 23 months with minimum dietary diversity is 46 percent; the Minawao camp records the lowest rate, at 14 percent.
