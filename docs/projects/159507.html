Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to provide support at the spectrum of the Somalia food security cluster strategic objectives of:
1. Improved Access to Food and Safety Nets (IASN): SC will provide unconditional unrestricted cash assistance and food vouchers where appropriate to improve access to immediate food needs for the most vulnerable and marginalized households with emphasis on HHs with U5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Cash will be delivered through mobile money platform to minimize protection risks.
2. Protecting and restoring livelihoods related to food security and income sources. SC will provide inputs to the two main sources of livelihoods in Somalia i.e. agriculture and livestock. In the agriculture sector; seed inputs, tools, agriculture trainings, tillage hours, rehabilitating main agriculture related infrastructure e.g. canals will be done while in livestock, livestock treatment and provision of water.
Mobile money is a feasible transfer mechanism for many reasons, including wide community acceptance and use, efficient and timely set up, and safe and secure means for HHs to receive and manage cash transfers. Across Somalia in general, and in proposed program areas specifically, mobile money is a mechanism widely accepted for payments with a broad array of vendors operating as mobile money agents. According to findings from SC post distribution monitoring, shops and cash-out agents are generally available within a 30 minute to 1-hour walk for most HHs, with the exception of pastoralists who might take longer pending their location. Beneficiaries are able to both cash-out their accounts, or but make direct purchases through mobile money without cashing out, depending on their preferences.
Mobile money enables a timely and efficient transfer for cash to target as it restricts the shop/vendor and food commodities to be transferred to beneficiaries. It also offers improved safety and security compared to in-kind or cash transfers, which can expose HHs and women, as primary beneficiaries, to greater risk. It also minimizes unnecessary beneficiary movement and profiling at the point of payment.
SC will mobilize and sensitize mothers through IYCF sessions on how to utilize cash received to purchase quality and variety of foods for better nutrition outcomes for children as the primary cash recipients.
Cash programing will enable HHs to purchase preferred foods (rice, sorghum and wheat) from a wide number of shops and markets while also strengthening local drought-affected shops and markets, with a potential broader impact on markets than voucher programming, which typically works with only a few commodities and or vendors. In addition, cash is most appropriate to address reduced purchasing power through improved food access, while also increasing demand in local markets with resultant stimulation of market growth and increase of trading opportunities.
SC has been monitoring the market situation at key markets (both intervention and supply markets) in all target regions since June 2018.
Throughout the target areas, local markets are well integrated with supply markets with adequate levels of supply and capacity to resupply with increase in demand. A wide array of food retailers and wholesalers are operational in both local and regional markets, which helps assure consistent and competitive supply of commodities and reduced risk of inflation.

Project Activities

1. Unconditional and unrestricted cash transfers targeting 65,657 vulnerable and marginalized people. 1.1 Community mobilization and beneficiary identification. Will entail awareness creation to the target community about the project, their allocation and responsibilities in collaboration with the Village Relief Committees (VRC). A targeting criteria to ensure inclusive targeting of most vulnerable and marginalized groups will be agreed upon. SC will engage a third party to monitor and evaluate the process by conducting interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries to verify fairness of the selection process. Baseline assessment and protection measures (safety and dignity) will be undertaken.1.2 Monthly cash transfers. Based on Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) for communities in IPC 3, 4 and worse, the value will be 100% whilst communities in IPC 3 will be 80%. Payment will be made on a monthly basis through mobile phone as it reduces exposure to protection risks. 2/3 of beneficiaries will be women.1.3 Post distribution monitoring (PDM). Proactive calls will be done after every transfer to solicit beneficiary feedback. Beneficiaries will have access to the project complaint and response toll free telephone number throughout the project period. For transfers more than 3 rounds, SC will commission an independent third party monitoring. 1.4 End line evaluation. Done by SC Monitoring, Evaluation Accountability and Learning (MEAL) team will be undertaken to evaluate the extent the project has achieved its intended outcomes, impact on beneficiaries, lessons learnt and good practices. 2. Provision of agriculture and livestock seasonal inputs. The project will support poor pastoral and agro pastoral communities to protect and recover their livelihoods and increase income through providing livestock and agriculture inputs. 2.1. Beneficiary selection: Community based targeting will be pegged on cash supported HHs, those in IPC 3and 4, high Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates and villages not targeted by other organizations.Beneficiaries will be registered using KOBO, a mobile phone data collection platform.2.2. Livestock treatment. This will be done though a voucher system. Ministry of Livestock veterinary teams will be deployed to each selected sites and beneficiaries will use the vouchers to access treatment services. The treatment will be conducted during in Xagaa season (July to September). 2.3. Post-Distribution Monitoring. SC will conduct post-treatment monitoring at least one month after the treatment which will assess the treatment process, performance indicators such as satisfaction with beneficiary selection process, treatment modalities, treatment amounts, fraud etc. 3. Provision of agriculture inputs. 3.1. Beneficiary Selection: Vulnerability criteria will guide in selecting beneficiaries prioritizing HHs with low purchasing power or those who have no access to humanitarian support and are likely to cultivate less land or fail to plant at all due to lack of production inputs. Categories that will be prioritized include female-headed HHs, HHs headed by elderly people, poor returnees, and HHs headed by children or disabled people. 3.2. Provision of seeds and tools: The crop production kit will be aligned to the recommended packages outlined in the guidance note on the provision of agricultural inputs in Somalia provided by the Food Security Cluster . In urban areas where farmers are closer to town, there are enough suppliers to source seeds from while in rural areas, SC will identify competitive suppliers who will deliver seeds/tools to the sites and distribute to the community. Commodity vouchers will be used to redeem items to increase accountability. 3.3. Agriculture training. Training on dryland farming techniques (i.e. conservation agriculture (CA)), in-soil water capture, and post-harvest handling of crop produce will be conducted to smallholders, mainly in Bay, Bari and Awdal. Mitigation Measures

SC will ensure that beneficiaries will not be exposed to danger when receiving assistance including landmines or explosive remnants of war. SC will not be asking beneficiaries to travel to far places in order to receive assistance. In addition, SC will provide assistance where communities are, cash will be delivered via mobile phones where beneficiaries will not need to travel to far places to receive assistance. To minimize the risk of making humanitarian assistance as a pull factor for people to access it where it is being offered, SC will employ geo targeting where support will be channeled to places where the need is greatest e.g. areas of IPC 3 or worse and to IDPs particularly. SC is aiming to cover as many beneficiaries as possible so that those left will not have to migrate. In addition, one of the criteria of admitting beneficiaries to the program will be that the person receiving assistance is from the target community. Lense 2: Exclusion and marginalization IDPs and minority groups are also marginalized. Clans who believe are better and more powerful take the lions share if not all of the resources. Host communities or gatekeepers in the IDP camps may sometimes manipulate IDPs. In both IDPs and host communities, families headed by categorically vulnerable and weak individuals including the elderly, children, women and disabled are marginalized and their rights are taken by so called more physical and/or societally powerful. The project will deliberately prevent risk of exclusion of most vulnerable and marginalized groups (based on gender, age, minority clans, IDPs, returnees, persons with disabilities, etc) by taking the following:• Awareness raising and sensitization: SC will start sensitizing stakeholders including community leaders and local authorities on the objective, processes and targets of the project. The organization will than meet people in target communities in a public places where we will ensure all those who can attend come to the meeting. In these meetings, information about the project including objective, entitlements, duration, targeting criteria, targeting approach will be shared. • Project relief committee: after sensitization, communities will be asked to establish project committees. To do this, communities will be asked to establish them in public and endorse the committee in public. The committee will represent all different groups and age brackets (excluding children but youth), and any other groups including women and minority. SC will sign an MoU with the committee which will detail the role of committee and beneficiary’s selection criteria. • Setting selection criteria: SC will, in consultation with the local authorities where possible, and by endorsement of the community, establish a selection criterion which includes but not limited to: Very poor HHs with limited income, few assets, and no external support, IDPs and newly displaced or evicted HHs, high number of malnourished children under 5, child headed HHs, female headed HHs, elderly-headed HHs, disabled HH members, pregnant and/or lactating women among others. This will help us ensure minimize age based inclusion and exclusion. • House to house and public verification: Community committees will do the selection. Once the list is submitted by the committee, SC, committee and local leaders will take a sample (10% -12%) from the list and verify them physically, after which a public verification will be conducted. • Setting community response mechanism: The action will establish community response and feedback mechanism. A hotline will be provided and time will be given before registration. During verification and mobilization, SC MEAL team will provide a help desk. SC will mitigate the impacts of conflict and violence by targeting IDPs and returnees including the ones evicted from urban areas. Please provide further explanation

The project will enable IDPs/returnees and vulnerable host communities to meet their immediate consumption needs and then thus reduce distressful coping strategies exposing them and their children to protection risks. Save the Children will collaborate and link IDPs/returnees to long-term food security and livelihoods projects (implemented by SC or partners) in order to enable them settle/stabilize and build their resilience. The project will be linked to SC and other partner’s long terms resilience building project i.e. BMZ in Awdal, BRCiS in Southern States and RESTORE in Bari and Nugaal.

Caseload

- The Somalia Food Security Cluster (SFSC) aims to be the primary source of information on the humanitarian response by addressing food insecurity, and by providing its members with a strategic vision and guidance in their response to the acute and underlying causes of crisis. As per the rationale of the Global Food Security Cluster, the FSC aims to promote responses that are: •Proportionate. The FSC will promote proportionate needs-based responses and advocate adequate resources and support. •Appropriate. The FSC will promote seasonal and livelihood specific responses. •Timely. In Somalia in particular, the FSC will aim to improve the timeliness of responses in order to provide a more effective and cost-efficient response.
