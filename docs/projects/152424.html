Project Summary

According to the just concluded education cluster countrywide assessment, over 2.8 million girls and boys between 3 and 18 years of age in conflict and crisis-affected areas are estimated will not have access to pre-school, primary and secondary education in 2019. In addition, some 42,902 teaching personnel and members of school management committees are in need of humanitarian assistance as they are crucial to delivering education services during emergencies. The number of boys and girls in need represents a 67 per cent increase from 2018. Plausible reasons include deterioration of systems and services whereby the resilience of households to cope with the economic downturn is seriously tested and collapses in the absence of support, especially in opposition-controlled areas such as in the Upper Nile State. Ulang and Nasir counties, where this project will be implemented faces a myriad of challenges including lack of teaching and learning supplies, inconsistencies in the provision of teacher incentives as the opposition government does not have the resources to pay teachers and other essential-services personnel including health workers, non-availability of school feeding programmes, poor infrastructure and insecurity. These challenges continue to strangle the provision of safe and quality education. Socio-cultural and economic realities increase the vulnerability of the community, more so for girls who are more likely than boys to be excluded from education.
The project is designed to create and support safe and quality education to the children and youths affected by conflict in six (6) most-affected schools in the two counties of Nasir and Ulang.
The intervention will contribute to the following HRP strategic objectives:
1) Save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys,
2) Protect vulnerable women, men, girls and boys through the provision of specialized and integrated services
3) Support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to recurrent shocks.
In pursuit of the cluster objectives, the project will:
a) Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms
Temporary learning spaces will be rehabilitated in affected areas (in line with INEE - Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies). This will lead to improvement in the number of children and youth (3-18 years old) accessing and attending learning spaces/schools in affected areas.
b) Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion. This will go a long way in enhancing dignity and protection.
c) Provide emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational material
This is aimed at improving protection through learning, recreational activities and cognitive development of children, youth and adolescents
d) Provide young children with ECD kits aimed at improving protection through learning, recreational activities and cognitive development of young children aged 3-5 years.
e) Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children.
f) Undertake training for Educators, community members and authorities on psychosocial support
g) Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance.
These strategies/activities are derived from the recommended cluster objectives, which in turn speak to the 2018 cluster HRP strategic objectives. In particular, the project pursues the following cluster strategic objectives:
1) Increase access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (formal and non-formal) for children and youth affected by emergencies
2) Improve the quality of education to ensure continuity of relevant education services for children and youth in emergencies
3) Strengthen the response capacity of communities and education actors to mitigate the impact of emergencies on children and youth
With over 14 years in programming, 5 of which are in Integrated Education in Emergency response, TADO is, without doubt, a significant contributor to the Education landscape in South Sudan, more so in the Upper Nile State. TADO has hands-on experience working in some of the most volatile and vulnerable areas, working with hard to reach people. TADO has set footprints in areas such as Ulang, Nasir, Gogrial East and Ikotos Counties in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Warrap which are admittedly, high-risk security zones and managed to forge close working relationships with the local communities, other partners with a presence in the region and county government administrative structures on the ground.
As a learning organization, TADO operates an open-door policy when it comes to community engagements, where the members of the beneficiary community are not mere spectators of events taking place, but key architects in the conceptualization, implementation and evaluation of the interventions targeting them. Accountability to the beneficiary community and all other key stakeholders and funding partners is a key pillar underpinning all our engagements.
TADO will bring on board as part of this intervention her experts and advisors on areas that the project team has inadequate capacity. A safety and security advisor, a gender consultant and a Protection focal point person will be seconded to the project to ensure that the intervention benefits from an integrated approach, ensuring that all engagements with beneficiaries adhere to the "Do no harm" principle.

Needs

During the past five years, South Sudan has experienced a series of devastating conflicts and widespread insecurity leading to the worsening of an already shattered education system across the country. According to the education-cluster needs assessment of 2017, most counties are affected by violence with 41% EMIS listed schools rendered non–functional. Only 59% of schools were reported operational but facing challenges ranging from; lack of /or dilapidated learning spaces, inadequate capacity of education facilitators, inadequate school supplies, inadequate WASH facilities, lack of ECD in schools, inability to offer psychosocial support to conflict-affected children, to insufficient support and participation by the local communities. Children and youth aged between 3-18 years are the worst affected. Education cluster needs assessment 2018 projected that the number of vulnerable boys and girls would increase to 67% from the 2017 figures due to acute economic turndown. An assessment conducted by TADO and partners in the two counties placed education among the worst affected service in the counties despite that it is a lifelong investment and lifesaving. Continued fighting by the warring parties at the Guelguk/Mathiang/Pagak corridor cut partially the economic supply route for the counties as market could not access commodities from Sudan and Ethiopia. The fighting also led to a huge displacement of populations to interior towns of Mading, Kieckuon and Wanding in Nasir, and Makak and Wechbuorni in Ulang Counties leading to a surge in enrolment in the few existing schools. A recent assessment by REACH conducted in September 2018 put the two counties under IPC 5. This is due to cut off from accessing markets from Ethiopia and Sudan, the commodities supply routes. The food shortage has affected learning because many children remain home, as they could not endure hunger. A surge in the number of IDPs that came to Ulang and Nasir because of the February 2018 fighting in Uror and Nyirol counties of Jonglei state worsened the existing precarious situation. (IRNA report of partners in Ulang/Nasir February 2018). Since the counties are largely controlled by the opposition forces, payment of volunteer teachers continues to be a great challenge as authorities here cannot raise the wages. The conflict has led to increases in insecurity, surge in population displacements in relatively secure areas especially in Ulang and Nasir Counties, Upper Nile State. Despite the great strides made, challenges exist in the provision of quality education and in the provision of safe spaces for conflict-affected children and the community to thrive in peace. Increase in the number of IDPs and IDPs children in the area has further squeezed safe learning spaces for children that is already inadequate. Capacity enhancement for existing stakeholders in the school system is low and could not adequately support the new challenges that include increase to high level class enrolment that need more trained teachers to support upper classes, create resilience to communities that are exposed to all life risks levels brought by the conflict such as GBV, trauma and hunger. Education and recreational supplies to support education is critical to ensure continuity and to support the economically and conflict hard-hit households that could not support their children's educationTADO has been intervening in integrated Education-in-Emergency project for the past 2 years in Ulang and Nasir County supporting 24 schools in partnership with UNICEF.

Activities

a) Establish and rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms. Temporary learning spaces will be rehabilitated in affected areas (in line with INEE - Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies). This will lead to improvement in the number of children and youth (3-18 years old) accessing and attending learning spaces/schools in affected areas. Existing TLS that need repair and rehabilitation will be identified through the participation of the SMCs and PTA. Communities will be challenged and motivated to participate in the rehabilitation of the learning spaces using local materials and labour. Building materials will be bought on a needs-basis to supplement the available local materials.b) Provide emergency-affected children with quality education and recreational material.Aimed at improving protection through learning, recreational activities and cognitive development of children, youth and adolescents. TADO will access and distribute education supplies to the identified beneficiary schools, children and teachers. c) Provide young children with ECD kits.Aimed at improving protection through learning, recreational activities and cognitive development of young children aged 3-5 years. TADO will access and distribute education supplies to the identified beneficiary schools, children and teachers.d) Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children. Incentive-supported teachers will be trained on EiE, basic pedagogy and learner-centred methodologies. TADO has internal capacity in the Education Department to undertake most of the training and coaching of the teachers. Where capacity is inadequate, consultants will be brought on board to help.e) Undertake training for Educators, community members and authorities on psychosocial support. Capacity building will target teachers, community members (SMC/PTAs) and education authorities to enhance their capacity to respond and offer psychosocial first aid including referrals of specific cases for better case management. Further, the interventions will seek to create community-led women and girl-friendly spaces.f) Equip SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management and governance.Community education committee members and Local Education officials will be trained in school management, monitoring and record keeping and followed up with coaching and support to ensure that schools are better managed and community ownership of education is enhanced.In addition, Advocacy and social mobilization campaigns among local authorities, community leaders, business community and religious leaders will be conducted to: ensure more girls access and finish basic education, extract renewed and sustained commitment to education, back to school efforts and gender equality including on how the community and local authority can mobilize and utilize education resources at county level more effectively and efficiently and creating safer education environment by discouraging school occupation and armed elements.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

TADO has in place a complaints/feedback handling mechanism that recognizes the importance of the process and the confidentiality required. Complaints are categorized in terms of severity and handled appropriately. Minor complaints are acknowledged and easilty and promptly resolved. Most of these pose minimal risk to the organization and beneficiaries. Complex and significant complaints are acknowledged, an initial assessment done, investigation follows and appropriate remedial action taken. TADO adheres to set standards as follows: TADO’s handling of complaints will meet the following minimum standards: 1. All complaints will be acknowledged as soon as possible, and ideally within five working days by the recipient. All complainants will receive a copy of our policy. 2. All complainants will receive a response giving the outcome of their complaint as soon as possible and ideally within thirty working days of receipt. If the matter is more complex a further acknowledgement will be sent explaining what further investigation is required to resolve the complaint and the likely timeframe. 3. All complaints should be recorded on a Complaints Record Form. 4. All complainants will be treated respectfully, whether it is felt the complaint is justified or not. A beneficiary committee is in place that collaborates with project staff and local stakeholders to receive and handle complaints. Beneficiaries can also send their complaints or feedback directly to the Complaints Handling focal person, who also regularly visits beneficiaries to listen and receive feedbackSpecific procedures include PTA/SMCs to sign handover letters on activities conducted to ensure completion and acceptance of services and posting suggestion boxes to all support providing centers. TADO also ensures the participation of beneficiary representative at the project review stages

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is aware that certain cultural practices and customs may pose a barrier to access and participation of beneficiaries. The project ensures that there is a buy-in process that involves consultations with community gatekeepers such as elders, religious leaders and local government authorities. Consent for access is sought for and agreed upon prior to project implementation. It is part and parcel of the entry strategies that the beneficiary project committee develops and implements. Further, the participation and collaboration of community stakeholders who are opinion leaders lends credibility and promotes ownership of the project interventions. Further, hard to reach people such as the elderly, people with disabilities and pastoralists will be targetted with flexible activities that can easily adapt to their specific situations. Further, the building of technical capacity is targetted at local actors / service providers further building the resource base of the community to respond and make access easier. Beneficiaries are selected by need and need only. All stakeholders are represented at the decision making body

Yes

Project activities are specifically targeting to address gender inequality and gender-based violence in all its forms as manifested in the target community. How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

TADO Human safety guidelines acknowledge the importance of human rights, seeks to empower vulnerable people to claim their rights and emphasises the need to protect respect and fulfil people’s rights.
