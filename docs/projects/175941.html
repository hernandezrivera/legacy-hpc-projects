Project Summary

The goal is to contribute to the reduction in malnutrition related mortality and morbidity, and improve access to high quality lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions for the most vulnerable populations notably U5 children and pregnant and lactating women affected by floods in Duk County.
In spite of ongoing intervention, the proxy GAM rate is still at 26.61 %, (NC, 2021) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention.
These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 month; therefore, treatment of SAM and MAM for children below five years as well as PLW continues to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk in Jonglei State.
This project intents to reach 2,176(M=1044, F=1,132) SAM cases and 4,288(M=2,058, F=2,230) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 3,012,MIYCN 18,838, VAS 27,508 and Deworming 24,757(PIN 2021). These targets are focusing on the vulnerable people among IDPs, returnees and host communities who have no access to Nutrition interventions.
The proposed project objectives will be:
1. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventive nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in Jonglei by the end of 2021
2. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in Jonglei by the end of 2021.
3. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information in Jonglei by the end of 2021
4. To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in Jonglei by the end of 2021.

1 - Needs

Duk County is among the severely flood affected counties and are inhabited by extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, disable and elderly people who are physically and psychologically exposed to multitude of stressors who are displaced by flooding since July 2020 and disease burden in current COVID 19 Pandemic. The proxy GAM rate is still at 26.61 %, (NC, 2021) which are above the WHO threshold for emergency intervention. It is estimated that a child with severe acute malnutrition is 12 times more likely to die and a child with moderate acute malnutrition is 3 times more likely to die than a well-nourished child. The COVID-19 situation, violence and floods that led to population displacement, coupled with a decrease in coverage and uptake of nutrition services exacerbated the nutrition situation.The county is also among the counties under IPC Phase 4(Emergency) (FSNMS round 24) and yet water levels are expected to rise in the coming months causing severe deterioration in food security for the flood affected communities. Farmers are facing challenges in crop production which have adversely affected their ability to access food from own crop production. Climate change is a major element which has affected farmers with agricultural seasons being characterized by long flooding which lead to crop loss. The depletion of food stocks and the early onset of flooding have cause agricultural disruption leading to increase in malnutrition cases. The increased numbers of admissions are attributed to increased food insecurity, displacements and movements, poor WASH services and disease burden of population with 40,000 people displaced by flooding. JDF has also observed key social and cultural determinants of malnutrition to children under the age of five years such as food sharing, introducing water to babies younger than 6 months old, and polygamy families with limited resources to look after family. Rapid needs assessment (IRNA, August 2020) of flood impact on nutrition services indicated that damage to infrastructure of nutrition sites that would require rehabilitation. Some other sites, though functional, have become not accessible to beneficiaries, requiring for outreach services with local transport solutions using canoe. In addition, only limited quantities of supplies could be pre-positioned at nutrition sites, due to inadequate secure storage facilities, hence limited storage capacity, and risks of looting. There are also high risks of damage to the supplies if heavy rain continues resulting in roof leakages of storage facilities, flooding and collapsing of storage structures. There is also high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (80%), diarrhea (15%) and pneumonia (5%) in U 5 years which have a direct negative impact on the nutrition status of young children and calls for high sensitization campaigns for timely health seeking behavior, good hygiene practices and public health initiatives (DHIS2 2020). These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 month in 2021. This project targets delivering quality lifesaving management of acute malnutrition to 2,176(M=1044, F=1,132) SAM cases and 4,288(M=2,058, F=2,230) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 3,012,MIYCN 18,838, VAS 27,508 and Deworming 24,757(PIN 2021). Program approaches will include active case detection and treatment of SAM/MAM and preventive intervention through community education, community mobilization MIYCF, Mother MUAC Approach, deworming campaigns, and mass screening targeting flood affected communities through static nutrition sites as well as mobile services to ensure continuity of nutrition services.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Women, girls, elders, men, people with disabilities will have clear Needs, roles and power dynamics on equal access to equal benefits. The risk analysis emerging from a community- and rights-based approach that is underpinned by a comprehensive system of accountability will inform and shape the overall strategy, enable the design of appropriate preventive, responsive and environment-building interventions by protection actors. The project will ensure an equitable and meaningful access by girls, boys, women and men of all ages and diverse backgrounds to protection and assistance programmes. The project will employ participatory assessments at key stages, including as part of the Joint Needs Assessment, incorporating systematic engagement to identify the diverse perspectives, risks and capacities of groups within crisis-affected communities, including men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority groups, and persons of diverse sexual orientation or gender identity complemented by Do no Harm and conflict analysis. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centres; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian programmes.
