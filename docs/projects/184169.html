Project Summary

Crisis affected populations who are underserved are at great risk of Child violation, sexual exploitation and abuse, and forced/early marriages are usually the consequences in this insecure and unstable setting with weak rule of law, scarce of opportunities to sustain the daily lives and limited access to the quality multi-sectoral services. The project will be targeting boy and girls in hard-to-reach areas including children with disabilities as they are especially vulnerable and report a very high level of unmet needs. They are the most vulnerable, marginalized and at-risk group within Somalian society as a result of the numerous attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers they face. The proposed project will provide of age, and gender sensitive structured mental health and psychosocial support services to girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities, provide caregivers with psychosocial support, parenting messages, positive parenting and other kind support leading to improved relationships with the children under their care, provision of individual case management services that meets their unique needs to the identified girls and boys, including adolescents, children with disabilities at risk, Reintegrate girls and boys released from armed forces and groups with their families/communities and provided with adequate care and services, reunification of identified separated girls and boys with their primary caregivers or in appropriate alternative care, Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM), Training CP actors [social workers, community volunteers, government officials, field coordinators], Community awareness on child protection and Conduct referral mapping. Optimizing Child Protection outcomes through meaningful integration with other sectors and providing various forms of support to caregivers and families of children facing protection risks in order to enhance their capacity to care for and protect their children. Awareness raising activities will target 15,000 community members including PLWDs with a wide range of prevention messages and awareness sessions including on recruitment/CAAFAG, SGBV, harmful practice, family separation, and Children’s rights. The project will be implemented in the hard to reach areas of Bari (Bandarbeyla and Iskushuban) Mudug (Galdogob and Jariiban) Nugaal (Burtinle and Eyl )Sanaag (Laasqoray) and Sool( Taleex and Xudun)

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

n this project TASS programs will seek to improve access to beneficiaries of the proposed interventions by scaling up services to be within a walking distance. in Far and hard to reach areas in rural settings TASS will ensure mobile teams reach in that area so as to reduce risks and hazards faced by these groups when they are accessing services. Additionally There will be a deliberate effort by TASS to ensure accountability to the target population through open, transparent and inclusive project implementation and management processes. The target beneficiaries will be highly involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project activities. In addition, proper feedback mechanisms will be set in place to continuously improve on the quality of programming and ensure that TASS is responsive to the community needs. Additionally a Complaints Response and Feedback mechanism with a hotline is already available for use. The use of this system will be enhanced by making beneficiaries more aware of its existence in the proposed project locations. The protection from sexual exploitation and abuse of children is dealt with under our Child Safeguarding policy and PSEA Policy. This includes direct or indirect beneficiaries of our programming and adults in the wider communities in which we work. For anyone under 18, refer to the Child Safeguarding Policy. The PSEA policy contains essential principles and rules that reflect the behaviors and standards expected from employees, trustees, volunteers, interns, trainees, consultants, partners, and other representatives working for, or contracted on behalf of TASS in any capacity and covers our international development and humanitarian work. TASS is committed to reducing the opportunities for abuse and exploitation to happen through effective prevention, ensuring that survivors are not exposed to further harm through good reporting and responses, and ensuring that survivors receive appropriate care. Effective prevention and providing safe responses require all staff, volunteers, and intern to be clear about their individual accountabilities and responsibilities. It also requires all senior staff and managers to ensure that their working environments minimize the opportunities for sexual exploitation and abuse, and to ensure that reporting and response do not expose survivors or reporters to any further harm. TASS takes the position that early reporting of any concern supports both prevention of further abuse and safe response through the hotline available 24/7. There must be increased awareness about reporting, including the importance of reporting of any and all concerns, however apparently minor. TASS’s commitment to Zero Tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse means all programme design and planning must include a gender power analysis, which also looks at the roles and structure of TASS staff, volunteers, interns and partners and a safeguarding risk assessment TASS has a PSEA and child safeguarding Focal Points, who is responsible for driving awareness of the policy and on how to report, inviting, receiving and collating all causes for concern. When these collected pieces of information generate a picture of a site, or a programme, or a particular person, the PSEA Focal Point must escalate the matter to trigger further inquiry and/ or investigation.
