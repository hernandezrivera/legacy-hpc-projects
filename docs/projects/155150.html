Objectives

1. Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases. 2. Ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone. 3. Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behavior and utilisation of essential service packages among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups. 4. Strengthen health sector coordination and information management with a focus on rational and accountable health service delivery systems, including monitoring of minimum standards and the quality of the services provided.

Needs

Access to life saving health and reproductive health services remains uneven. In Teknaf, refugees interviewed most often received care from local pharmacies without trained medical staff (60%) or from government-run health centers (41% of respondents), which remain overwhelmed and unable to handle the increased refugee caseload. Similarly, RI found many host communities were relying on local pharmacies (73%) and Teknaf Upazila Health Complex for basic, primary health care needs (35%), instead of seeking care from government supported community clinics (12%) (RI Multi-Sector Assessment, April 2018). Refugees and host community respondents in Teknaf both identified transportation cost as the most critical barrier to health access (49%), followed by the cost of medicine (25%) and treatment (11%). Respondents confirmed that primary health services were the most important service for women and girls specifically (66% of respondents), followed by health care services for pregnant women as the second most important.Access to life saving health and reproductive health serveices remains uneven. With high malnutrition rates and less nutrition and health coverage, host and refugee households in smaller settlements remain more vulnerable to disease outbreak. Living conditions are overcrowded and damp, and there is limited access to toilets or improved water sources for bathing and drinking water. The uneven delivery of WASH, nutrition, and health assistance further heightens the risk of communicable disease spread. Furthermore, anticipated flooding over the cyclone season will exacerbate an already fragile humanitarian situation and compounding a potential disease outbreak. The Civil Surgeon and Teknaf Health Complex cited a lack of community outreach and health education within the communities. Unlike Kutupalong, where a large network of Community Health Workers (CHWs) cover approximately 80% of the population, makeshift settlements in the south have limited to no networks of CHWs.

Activities or outputs

- Establish and support two outpatient health posts in Nuralipara and Shal Bagan which will provide life-saving health services as per Health Sector guidance, and the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) to address reproductive health needs. Activities in the outpatient health posts will be in-line with the Health Sector's minimum service package for health posts. - Support disease surveillance and response including indicator based surveillance at the facility level and community surveillance through the training of 80 total community health workers (CHWs). Twenty CHWs will support surveillance and community outreach per facility. - Provide support to two MOH supported clinics in Minabazar (Whycong) and Uttapara (Shah Parir Diwp). -Establish a “one stop shop” to address the immediate health needs of GBV survivors - Establish a beneficiary feedback mechanism in the health posts and MOH-supported clinics, to support stronger accountability to communities As of end of November 2018, RI has secured funding to sustain these health activities for two months until February 2019 and will work to secure support for the remainder of 2019, so that services are not interrupted.
