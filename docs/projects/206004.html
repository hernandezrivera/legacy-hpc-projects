Project Summary

The project will target 20,000 direct and indirect beneficiaries including (5,600 Girls and 5,100 Boys) Children, adolescent children and (4,300 men and 5,000 Women) in Ayod 5,100, Canal/Pigi 5,100 Counties in Jonglei State and Longochuk 4,700 and Maiwut 5,100 while 15% of beneficiaries are persons with disabilities in Upper Nile State, aimed to ensure the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls, and boys in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience caused by the flood and conflict, through provision of timely comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI, Mobile and static protection monitoring and assessment, Psychosocial Support Services, protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging and CBP preparedness and protection risk mitigation.
UNCDR, with community participation, will involve the beneficiaries, local authorities, CBCPN and other stakeholders in project implementation and capacities building on the three approaches that will use to achieve more results as the Current flood and conflicts significantly impact the children’s psycho-social well-being and development, which exposes them to violence, loss of lives, or separation from, family members and friends, deterioration in living conditions, inability to provide for one’s self and family, and lack of access to services that can all have immediate and long-term consequences on children, families, and communities and impair their ability to function and be fulfilled.
UNCDR will provide crucial psychosocial support for children during emergency situations to help them overcome such difficult experiences. These efforts include culturally and age-appropriate, safe and stimulating activities such as games and sports to develop life skills, coping mechanisms, and support resiliency.
UNCDR will also use extensive comprehensive case management forms to document all cases in accordance to the CP principles into the database and report it to the relevant services, this will be done in conjunction with the community-based child protection committees or Networks (CBCPC/N), which will be recruited and trained to provide quality case management and proper coordination of the FTR in the local context. The project will also strengthen the ability of community members to support and provide comprehensive quality of services to the most vulnerable children, UASC, communities and families with their neighbors with keys messages on how to cope with this situations through a variety of channels including the existing community structures, youth centers and use of child friendly spaces (CFS), organize activities to the children and adolescent youths in a safe and stimulating environment where affected communities and children can be supported, provide specialized referral pathways to access the services base on the need, or who may need extra support, so that appropriate networks of care may be engaged.
UNCDR will recruit and train the CP staff on the child protection concerns in order to speed the implementation to provide quality activities and services to support the, identification, registration, documentation of the Unaccompanied and separated children in the selected counties and conduct assessment, planning, implementation follow up and reporting in accordance with the IASC, SOPs and ISPs to improve FTR and case management in line with minimum standards of Child protection in emergencies services.

1. Needs

The children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, continue to be exposed to a wide range of protection risks, such as family separation, recruitment and use of children by armed forces and groups, psychosocial distress, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), neglect, abuse and exploitation. Protection concerns are high, HRP 2023 2023 indicate 9.4 M people are in needs, including 2.2 million women, 4.9 million and 337,000 refugees, 15% persons with disability; with 5.53 million-PIN and 3.15 million children in need of CP services, an increase from 2022. According to the CPIMS+, the number of documented child protection cases including UASC increased from 26,009 (12,128 girls and 13,881 boys) in 2020 to 30,441 (14,185 girls and 16,256 boys) by the end of 2021, with Unity, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Lakes states having the highest number of documented child protection cases, including UASC, child neglect, child labour, domestic violence and child trafficking. This situation has been exacerbated by the protracted displacement, and limited access to basic services which continues to significantly affect the physical, mental, and social well-being of children, adolescents and caregivers (2023-HNO). A recent 2022 FSNMS+ survey results show that 3.7% of interviewed households indicated that their children were missing or not living in the household at the time of the assessment and are at risk of abuse, neglect,, and exploitation. The areas affected include; Ayod, Canal/Pigi counties of Jonglei; Longochuk, and Maiwut of Upper Nile. Children and adolescents also continue to face a multitude of risks, including grave violations, abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. Economic pressures from COVID-19 have forced some families to rely on negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. Decades of insecurity and upheaval have damaged traditional social structures and weakened justice mechanisms, leaving children more vulnerable to protection risks. The prolonged conflict in the listed counties of Ayod, Canal in Jonglei and Longochuk and Maiwut Counties in Upper Nile where it has had a catastrophic effect on the population especially children being the most affected due to increased exposures to protection risks which include separation from caregivers, widespread of GBV concerns, loss of immediate caregivers, forced recruitment and marriage, massive destruction of schools and TLS, abuse, exploitation of the vulnerable children among other protection concerns. UNCDR intends to continue addressing protection risks issues through a response strategy which includes: Provision of comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI to children under support with material and other medical concerns. UNCDR will increase its Static and Mobile respond to reach hard to reach areas with protection monitoring and assessment, strengthening the Psycho-social Support Services and Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging to the affected communities within the selected counties, strengthening CBCPC/N, increase capacity building program to staff and communities to support the children during the exit of the organization, to continue improving the well-being of the children that have gone through difficulty and in the community’s centers in order to complement the current multi-sectoral interventions with mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health and FSL Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were UNCDR operates.
