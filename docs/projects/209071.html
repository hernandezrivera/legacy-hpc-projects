Project Summary

The conflict created massive displacement, with over 6.1 million people forced to leave their homes in search of safety elsewhere. To date, 3 million children are displaced making Sudan the country with the highest number of displaced children globally. In addition, 19 million are not in school. To date, only one state (River Nile state) managed to reopen schools for the academic year (2023 – 2024), with eight other non-conflict states struggling to announce a date for schools reopening. Leading factors to the ministry of education inability to reopen schools includes the use of school grounds by displaced population, and the government inability to pay teachers’ salaries consistently since April 2023. Nearly 9% of schools countrywide are either used to shelter displaced populations in receiving states or occupied by armed groups in conflict affected states.
UNICEF humanitarian response project for 2024 aims to enable 1,681,627 (1,008,976 girls, 672,651 boys) children to access formal and non-formal education opportunities, including early learning. A cornerstone of the response is the establishment of safe learning spaces (MAKANNA), to enable girls and boys, especially the displaced to access lifesaving and life-sustaining learning, skills, and education opportunities. Access to formal and non-formal learning spaces activities aims to be inclusive and targets 168,163 (100,898 girls) children. Furthermore, the project will aim to equip 1,177,139 (706,283 girls, 470,856 boys) children with essential education supplies, including 117,714 children (70,628 girls) with disabilities. The interventions will include supporting local partners and the Ministries of education in safe states where schools are reopened for the remaining of the 2023 – 2024 & and the next academic years. Another element of the project is the provision of technical assistance, coordination, and capacity building to local partners, MoEs, education personnel, and communities to open schools and learning centers, where is safe to do so, and to sustain their operation.
In line with the strategic objectives of the HRP 2024 (SO2 & SO3), UNICEF project will aim to provide 1,681,627 (1,008,976 girls, 672,651 boys) children with Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) training through their learning programmes in schools and safe learning spaces. As well as engage 134,530 (80,718 girls) young people in adolescents led activities on skills development, recreational and learning activities.
The purpose of the UNICEF education HRP project is to provide for every child uninterrupted and sustainable learning and education opportunities in safe and accessible environment. UNICEF education response will build of engagement of children, caregivers and communities in the design and preparedness of all interventions. The project will integrate protection activities, accountability to the affected populations and reporting mechanisms for protection and sexual exploitation and abuse.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

UNICEF education project will integrate protection activities in safe learning spaces, as well as targeted schools. UNICEF will provide basic training on PSEA and children safeguarding to teachers, community members, PTAs and others involved in direct work with children. Additionally, UNICEF will ensure that the implementing partners adhere to UNICEF policies and ensure explicit measures are mentioned in programme agreements. Subsequently, UNICEF PSEA specialist will continue to advice on all interventions design and implementation through various phases. UNICEF will ensure full induction and orientation on UNICEF’s zero tolerance policy to the implementing partner, project and education personnel on-ground (teachers, facilitators, PTAs, headmasters, etc.). Furthermore, establish with the partners complaints and feedback mechanisms. Additionally, materials to clearly reflect safeguarding polices will be shared in an easy-to-understand messages across schools, this includes, reporting and complaint processes. As well as mechanisms to handle and record reported cases. Monitoring and programmatic visits will ensure that protection and safeguarding are assessed and monitored throughout the programme implementation. At local level, community members, PTAs and education personnel will be oriented on protection measurements. Specific roles for coordinating, reporting, and continuously monitoring will be assigned to PTA members as well as mother group members. Implementing partners will assign CSG, PSEA, and GBV focal points/officers, who will explicitly coordinate reporting on the establishment and monitoring of protection measurements and community roles.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

UNICEF’s education project builds on a strong community-level lead interventions to ensure sustainability and continuity of services provision in established and supported safe learning spaces and schools. Across the targeted states, consultation and coordination meetings with children, caregivers, communities, and local partners aims to engage more than 50,000 members in the intervention design phase. Targets for consultation and coordination meetings will be recorded and assessed on on-going basis. Across the targeted communities, community members, including PTAs, mothers’ groups, teachers, facilitators and volunteers manning safe learning spaces will receive capacity building on community-level management for schools and learning spaces. This includes a resilience component, and reporting components to ensure that interventions are context relevant, community driven, and sustainable in the case of prolonged crisis.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Building on UNICEF’s zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse policy, UNICEF’s education project will aim to provide capacity building on PSEA, referral mechanisms and complaint mechanisms. The training will be strengthened by explicit guidance and measures to implementing partners, and communities’ and on-ground personnel leading and supporting the intervention (teachers, facilitators, volunteers, etc.).
