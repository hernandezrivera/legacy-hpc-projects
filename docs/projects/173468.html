Project Summary

The project aims to engage affected communities to combat the trafficking challenges in the camps and host communities. It seeks to strengthening systems and support local institutions; organic structures and other social dynamics to address the prevailing protection challenges. The most vulnerable women, girls, sex workers, and other diverse sexual communities are the primary beneficiaries. As preventive, mitigation and response measures, it aims to promote a community based approach to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services to the persons at heightened risk to trafficking and other forms of violence; monitor and advocate for the access to territory and prevention of refoulement in order to ensure effective targeted protection and assistance to women and girls vulnerable to and victims of trafficking(VoT). The project places affected communities at the centre and ensure community consultation, communication and information sharing in line with AAP principles.
The specific interventions identified are:
• Raising awareness and understanding of trafficking risks amongst and within communities, local authorities, law enforcement agencies, legal structures and service providers leveraging community led messaging approach
• Supporting women groups and led anti-trafficking prevention, mitigation and response initiatives
• Conducting appropriate screening, identification of VOT ensuring dedicated case management support (referrals and follow up) and access to direct assistance including structured PSS services and legal aid and related services
• Supporting VoTs with direct assistance such as, skill development and empowerment programming to improve living standards and living conditions
• Establishing a partnership network and system strengthening for (potential) VoTs identification and referral, linking to institutions other organic structures set up by the Rohingya refugees, local communities, etc.
Since Light House does not underestimate the predominating trafficking challenges and committed to evolving with community led anti-trafficking interventions leveraging its strong presence, replicating best practices and deploying gender and PSEA sensitive staff.

1 - Identified Needs

As of October 31, 2020, it is reported that Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar have become temporary shelter home for 862,277 refugee populations that has added additional multiple socio-economic and cultural challenges and burden to the poverty stricken area which is also home of 472,002 . The challenges crippled and aggravated following COVID-19 pandemic. As per the Joint Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (October, 2020) findings, women and girls living without men or head of the families were identified to be at most risk. The protection related concerns were raised related to robbery, kidnapping, child marriage, sexual harassment etc. The women, girls and children are identified to be at higher risk of trafficking for forced labour and commercial sexual activities. The RGA reveals that the key populations such as transgender persons, female sex workers and other diverse gender communities are the hard hit populations in all aspects of the COVID-19 response and protection (exploitation, abuse and trafficking). As affected population consultation, participation, information sharing to the affected communities are also found limited both in the camps and host communities. As part of prevention, mitigation and response, to reduce the trafficking risk, concerted and coordinated efforts of actors are required. Therefore, the major needs are identified: • Lack of understanding of trafficking risks amongst and within communities, local authorities, law enforcement agencies, legal structures and service providers.• Needs to conducting appropriate screening, identification of VoTs ensuring dedicated case management support (referrals and follow up) and access to direct assistance.• Lack of skill development and empowerment opportunities to support potential victims of trafficking including vocational support to improve living standards and conditions• Lack of partnership and greater networking for victims (potential) VoT identification and referral, linking to institutional frameworks and organic structures.• Lack of access to legal information, aid and counselling to Victims of Trafficking to support resolution including safe shelter for the victims.Light House does not underestimate the predominating trafficking challenges and committed to evolving with community led anti-trafficking interventions both in host and refugee communities in Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts. It plans to cover camps 1E, 2E, 7, 8E,9,11,12,19, 13 and KRC in Ukhiya and 24,25,26,27 and NRC in Teknaf and peripheral host communities. To address the predominating challenges, the defined specific objectives framed in consultation with the affected communities are, 1. Promoting a community-based approaches including women groups to the response, support community self-protection mechanisms and facilitate meaningful access to specialized services for persons at heightened protection risk, including girls, boys, women and men of all ages including those sex workers and diverse gender communities who have diverse needs and vulnerabilities, with the aim of mitigating exposure to protection risks, strengthening the resilience of affected communities in order to build skills for return and reintegration, and placing communities at the centre of the response, as well as by ensuring active and meaningful two-way communication between humanitarian actors and communities of concern, in line with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) principles. (SO1, SO2 & SO4); 2. Improving access to quality survivor-centered services by responding to individual needs, preventing and mitigating GBV risks, and supporting women, girls, boys and men survivors of GBV in Rohingya refugee camps and targeted areas in host communities. (SO1, SO2 & SO3) and 3.
