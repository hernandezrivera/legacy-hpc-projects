Project Summary

This program aims to improve maternal, neonatal, reproductive and child health for children <5 years and women of child bearing age in Somalia by expanding access to essential lifesaving health services for displacement-affected populations. The intervention will reduce overall morbidity and mortality and specifically maternal and child morbidity and mortality. The program also aims to empower communities to manage health at the community level where access to the formal health system is limited and to strengthen the fledgling Somalia health system.

Needs

Somalia is one of the most fragile states in the world, rated second on the Fragile States Index. The survey found that around 1.1 million people have been internally displaced, which has risen to 2.6 million according to current estimates. Average life expectancy is estimated at 56 years. Four out of five Somalis live below the national poverty line. More than 5.4 million people, close to half of the population, need humanitarian assistance and protection.The maternal mortality rate across Somalia is estimated at 732 per 100,000 livebirths. Less than 10 per cent of women deliver in a health care facility. Women have on averagesix children, a direct result of only 15 per cent using modern contraceptive methods. FGM affectsalmost all women and can result in increased obstetric complications. According to UNICEF, themortality rate for children aged under five years is ranked as the highest in the world at 133 deathsper 1,000 live births. Diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough) and tetanus (DPT) third dosecoverage is estimated at 42 per cent.These dire health outcomes require the concerted efforts of the government authorities, international community, and communities to comprehensively address and improve the quality of life for Somalis, especially women and children.

Activities

Primary health care provided to both outpatient and inpatient services.• Rehabilitate and operate static health facilities and mobile clinics• Provide consultations and referrals to vulnerable people in need (men, women, children, host communities, IDPs, returnees and refugees) • Provide pregnant women with free access to ANC, PNC and delivery servicesImprove immunization coverage• Procure and provide essential drugs for the static health facilities and mobile clinics. • Train health workers on stock management and use• Conduct community sensitization and awareness on immunization • Conduct measles and polio campaigns at the health facilities in coordination with Somali MOH, WHO and health partners• Train health workers on EPI planning, forecasting and tools for immunization Improve early warning and surveillance systems• Train health workers on sentinel reporting and response • Train health workers on AWD identification and response as part of rapid response team Community Engagement• Conduct health promotion and disease prevention awareness campaigns• Train community health committees to support management of health facilities and serve as a conduit to better meet community health needs• Train community health workers on integrated community case management of malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea • Link traditional birth attendants to qualified midwives at the health facility and train TBAs on danger signs of pregnancy Systems Strengthening• Support the training and mentorship of local, district, regional, and state health officials• Establish referral pathway from community to PHU, health facility, referral health center, to tertiary health center

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

This project specifically targets displacement-affected communities. The goal is to bring quality health care as close to the beneficiaries as possible by reducing physical, financial and attitudinal barriers to uptake.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Maternal and child health is a main focus of the project. Recognizing the vulnerability of these groups: children and women of child bearing age, the project seeks to improve their access and uptake of health care services. The project also targets men to encourage their support of and championing of their families' health needs.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project takes a community approach to mitigate against the impact of armed conflict and violence. Through the iCCM and CHC approaches, the communities will feel ownership over the health care services provided and work to protect the gains made.
