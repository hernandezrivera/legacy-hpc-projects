Project Summary

This project will seek to build on gains made through programming in 2015–2018, meeting the needs of approximately 115,813 conflict and famine-affected individuals in South Sudan through an integrated Nutrition, Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL), and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) response. To increase adequate access to clean water, hand pumps will be rehabilitated, a manual drill will be utilized to drill additional wells, existing water points will be upgraded to solar, water-management committees will be formed and hand pump mechanics will be trained. With regard to efforts to improve health and hygiene practices, communal and HH latrines, and gender segregated bathing shelters will be constructed, and trainings will be conducted on better health and hygiene practices.
The areas of South Sudan targeted for this WASH project include: Mayendit, Abiemnhom and Mayom Counties. In addition to their levels of food insecurity, each area has been identified due to a lack of access to clean water, high rates of malnutrition, or overall poor health and hygiene practices.

Needs

An estimated 5.3 million South Sudanese will need WASH services in 2018. In partnership with USAID/OFDA, SP has been providing WASH activities in Mayendit, Abiemnhom and Mayom since 2014. To address the urgent need for access to clean water, wells have been drilled, hand pumps have been installed and repaired, and committees have been formed and trained on Water Management as well as Health and Hygiene. With regard to Sanitation and Hygiene activities, latrines and bathing shelters have been built, hygiene kits have been distributed, and waste disposal pits have been dug. Great gains have been made under these USAID/OFDA supported programs. However, the need for access to clean and safe water, sanitation, and proper hygiene remains urgent. Results from a 2017 baseline study conducted by SP for WASH activities indicated that many HHs – 96% in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 71% in Mayendit – collected water from improved water sources (i.e. tap stands, hand pumps, rainwater collection). However, there were still significant WASH needs, with 36% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom stating it took longer than 30 minutes round-trip to collect water; 31% in Mayendit stated the same. According to Sphere guidelines, it is recommended that each person have access to at least 15 liters of water each day. Only 20% of HHs met this threshold in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 48% in Mayendit; 49% of respondents indicated they did not have enough containers to carry or store adequate amounts of water. Furthermore, only 7% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 19% in Mayendit treated their water to make it safe for drinking. In addition to the poor water access markers, sanitation is also a concern, with 40% of HHs in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 62% of HHs in Mayendit using an improved sanitation facility. Furthermore, 61% of the respondents in Abiemnhom and Mayom, and 62% in Mayendit acknowledged practicing open defecation (OD).

Activities

SP intends to do the following: 1) Establishment of 10 new water points manually drilled and fitted with hand pumps. 2) Establishment and training of 66 Water Management Committees (WMCs) for community management of the new water points. And, 3) Training of 66 hand pump mechanics to maintain and repair the new water points.Furthermore SP will do the following in Abiemnhom, Mayom and Mayendit: 1) Rehabilitate hand pump/shallow wells 2) Solar installation to an existing water yard 3) Install appropriate water drainage systems at existing and rehabilitated water points 4) Establishment of water management committees 5) Training of hand pump mechanics 6) Construction of institutional latrines 7) Construct shared household latrines 8) Construction of gender segregated bathing shelters 9) Refresher trainings of Health and Hygiene Committees 10) Distribution of Non Food Items (NFIs) 11) Conduct trainings on solid waste management at the household and community level 12) Conduct community led debris clean up campaigns to ensure appropriate waste management.

This approach also aims to minimize unintended negative effects that may increase people’s vulnerability (both physical and psychosocial).
