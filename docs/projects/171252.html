Project Summary

The proposed response aims to alleviate poverty and food insecurity for the most marginalized farming households in Area C and focused on vulnerable Farming HHs with demonstrated protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities from a pool of 40 communities from 6 governorates (Tulkarem, Tubas, Qalqilya, Jenin, Nablus, Jericho), with a particular focus on herders and livestock bearers.
The intervention components respond to the 2021 HRP SO3, in addition to the FSS O1, and O2 through its integrated approach to strengthen resilience of agricultural based livelihood by addressing community scale response components in conjunction with individual support of vulnerable farming households to deal with protracted livelihood and protection threats.
Overall it is expected that the project in its various components will benefit 3465 vulnerable farming HHs including ( 366 Female headed) thus directly benefiting 16,979 individuals ( 4629 men, 4491 women, 4006 boys, 3842 girls, including from all 339 PWDs)
The intervention will achieve the aforementioned through three main components that respond directly to the identified 2021 FSS priorities of i) Restoration of agricultural assets and productivity through multi-faceted approach. ii)provision of cash assistance for people in criticial consequence on food insecurity status. Through:
• provide support related to water management, through increase efficiency of water usage, increase profitability of farmers and decrease deficiency
• to increase the productivity, production and competitively of agriculture in the local market and it is contribution in support the food security for the farmers’ families through introducing new varieties of cash crops.
• enhance and build the capacity of the farmers including women in the irrigated crops planting, water resources management and the food processing through providing technical trainings.
In particular, the proposed response will include the following activities:
• Rehabilitation of 7 ground water wells, and installation of solar units and pumps.
• Rehabilitation/provision of 6 water harvesting/collection facilities including Installation and/or rehabilitation of 36 km of water carrier lines and connecting irrigation networks and water storage for 1000 donums
• Provision of inputs for rehabilitation, production and protection of 400 underproductive lands ( new varieties of cash crops in which can compete in the production and marketing fields.+ land protection inputs for farmers ar risk of settler violence)
• Assisting needy and vulnerable households through conditional and restricted CFW
• Promotion of technical capacities of farmers in particular females through 30 technical trainings.
As noted, MA’AN’s flexible approach through including a wider array of locations will allow to be agile and responsive. Some of the wells identified are in “Area B”. These wells have a somewhat lower-level of demolition risk. MA’AN would rehabilitate the wells then rehabilitate carrier-lines to small agricultural communities in Area C to improve water access. Note, that sometimes “B” and “C” areas are a mere handful of metres apart. Water carrier lines do not require large-scale machinery to be installed.
MA’AN has designed and proposed the above intervention to serve an aid gap in FS sector at the community level and further to complement the current and foreseen work of partners and other FS aid actors in their interventions at the HH level.
Further the proposed interventions at the community level aim to provide sustainable solutions for productivty of assets for vulnerable farming HHs and complement other MA’AN response submissions including a coinciding response proposed by the consortium of ( OXFAM, MA’AN, ESDC, and LRC).

1 - Needs

In March-May 2020 MA’AN Development Center conducted an overall needs assessment as part of the design of its Humanitarian Strategy in Area C. the needs assessment includes updated statistics from the Palestinian Authority (PA) Ministry of Agriculture in addition to updates and additional information based on MA’AN’s direct engagement in the Food Security Sector. The assessment was further informed by MA’AN during the first 3 months of implementing the project funded by the Canadian Government in 2020 The need assessment included interviews, meetings, field visits and focus groups with beneficiaries, stakeholders and the FSS,. MAAN’s own findings from previous field assessments (e.g. those of previous projects funded by the Canadian Government Funded projects- GAC, HF/OCHA, AMENCA3) are also included. In particular, MA’ANs assessment of 96 communities revealed: • 145 of artesian wells need rehabilitation by replacing their old equipment with new ones in order to improve their capacity and to increase the production and efficiency of water extraction. Therefore, this rehabilitation is expected to contribute to an increase in the quantities of water available for irrigation by about 6 million additional cubic meters of water if all are rehabilitated;• 111 artesian wells need to be connected to solar energy in order to reduce the cost of extracting water available for irrigation by about 12 million additional cubic meters, thus ensuring free pumping of water expected for a period of about 20 years;• 5 artesian wells needing to desalinate irrigation water in Jordan Valley, in addition to constructing 20 injection wells for groundwater (feeding wells) in Jordan Valley and Jenin• There is a need to replace and install 995 km of carrier water lines to increase the efficiency of water transmission and distribution by 17% through reducing water losses due to old and damaged lines• 5,260 dunums are available for the cultivation of semi-tropical crops such as Guava, Mango, Avocado, Custard apple and lychee, which will contribute to increasing the Palestinian agricultural production of tropical fruits by 25 tons annually.The needs of Vulnerable farming households in area C communities remain vast, and disproportionately addressed by aid actors ( as stated in HNO 2021 and MAAN findings from previous fields assessment: the main cause of food insecurity in the oPt is limited economic access to food, which is driven by unemployment and poverty. These two indicators have been on the rise in 2020 due the overall slowdown of the economy, following the restrictions imposed to contain the COVID-19 pandemic) The restraints in sustenance of livelihoods and needs include: • • Constrained productive capacities due to damages of vulnerable farmers Agricultural assets resulting from settler violence and other occupation regime; • Subjection of lands nearby settlements to a regime of permits and stop work order and movement restrictions • ; Shortage of water for agricultural purposes and lack of networks that facilitate access to water in communities; • Lack of farmers’ access to agricultural lands due to inadequate agricultural roads and/or presence of land in “no-go” zones; • However, these restrictions have had a severe disruptive impact on the entire food system, including producers, processors, marketers, transporters and consumers. Small producers have been particularly hit by the reduced demand, the unprecedented challenges in securing basic inputs, and the impediments in accessing credit and markets. • Further the COVID 19 pandemic caused significant increase in food and inputs prices and caused incomes’ shrinking and high unemployment rates. This led to heavy indebtedness and falling into the poverty trap specially with the absence of job opportunities and the possibility of being self- reliant families. • Disruption of producers access to basic agricultural inputs with foreseeable adverse impacts on agricultural productions. Several farming HHs have suffered from shortages and limited access to and increased prices of agricultural inputs, particularly fodder. • The demand by suppliers for upfront cash payments instead of credit on inputs and services further exacerbates their socioeconomic disadvantage in such times. This in turn supports the adoption of negative coping strategies for vulnerable HHs in general and farming HHs in particular where daily portions of meals were reduced and rationed carefully and available HH food stock is reduced due to financial burdens as well. The activities being proposed, are aiming to improving food security of marginalized farmers and herders families in Area C through an integrated approach of modalities to contribute to sustainable increase in capacities at the community and HH level to cope with prolonged threats to their livelihoods and living conditions. With considerations to the risks of work in Area C, MA’AN has adopted a pooled approach to targeted localities from 6 governorates, thus allowing planning for community level interventions subject to damages and confiscations to be implemented in nearby Area A&B localities with extensions of inputs into Area C to serve vulnerable farmers.The final selection of where community level interventions will be implemented from the 40 target locations will be conducted in partnership with the Ministry of Agriculture and in consultation with, PWA, WASH cluster and legal aid actors on community level interventions proposed to reduce risks. This approach to selection of target communities will allow MA’AN to be agile yet responsive in light of restrictions and risks in working in Area C communities. MA’AN will ensure ownership of assets and existing licensing of community infrastructure component, and when needed, will conduct community works in Area B lands adjacent to the targeted Area C locations.

2 - Activities

1.1 Rehabilitation of 7 under-capacitated agricultural ground water wells, and installation of solar units and pumps:The project in close coordination with the MoA,PWA, DoA and local councils and municipalities in Tulkarem, Tubas, Jericho, Nablus , Qalqiliya and Jenin. identified artesian wells need rehabilitation by replacing old equipment with new ones in order to improve capacity and increase production and efficiency of water extraction, connecting to solar energy will reduce cost of extracting water available for irrigation and to increase water output and accessed quantities by farmers, in JV particularly wells are in need of desalination. identified wells are owned by cooperatives, private, or local councils, and drilled in the sixtiesare now in a dilapidated condition. The deteriorated pumping equipment, increased price of fuel, drought, and Israeli restrictions have all lead to a reduction in the production of these wells. The project through this activity will improve the water supply to 350 farmers and their families :1. Replacing the old deteriorated pumping equipment of the targeted wells with new ones. This includes the change of motor, turbine, rising pipes ,gear box, and other fittings2. Well’s pumping equipment will be changed from the diesel to electrical/ solar power. This include electrical cable, electrical transformer, electrical motor, rising pipes, electric control panel, turbine, and other fittings3. Re-activating the non-functioning well (which is suffering from pumping equipment failure) . This include test the pumping capacity of the well, replace the old submersible pump by new one with the needed electric cable, rising pipes, control panel, discharge orifice and the plate, other needed fittings, and rehabilitation of well buildingwith the above, it is expected to have increase in the water quantity supplied to agriculture of 180,700 m3 which will be used to increase the irrigated area by 1000 dunums.1.2 rehabilitation/provision of water harvesting/collection The activity targets 420 poor farmers and their families living in Tulkarem, Qalqiliya, Jenin, Tubas, Jericho, Nablus and will improve their agricultural productivity by increasing the access and quantity of water available to them.farming HHs in target communities are dependant on wells in operating and producing water, the system depends on water carrying line connecting the wells with the agricultural lands, the carrier lines end in irrigation metallic pools ( 150 -200 m3) and these pools are connected to the farms through water pipe lines.This project is going to construct a new water system to deliver water from the water storage tanks with storage capacity from500-700m3 to the farm with minimum losses using a closed system of PVC carrier lines and drip irrigation pipes. The estimated total water losses during the old system are 15%,therefore the replacement of the old system with a closed water pipeline will save 5% of the total water which equals 120,000 m3.The activities will include:1.Provision of 6 water tanks of 500-700 water storage capacity, this will increase the quantity of water accessible to 70 farmers and their families for each tank, so that they can irrigate their agricultural land.2. Installation / rehabilitation of 36 km of water carrier lines, this will lead to a reduction of agricultural water losses by 5% which is equivalent to 120,000 m3 of additional water, an increase in the cultivated land of 2100 dunums (350 dunums for each new built tank)3. Installation of new drip irrigation system for 420 poor farmers, CfW scheme will be used to implement the component. This activity will also lead to an increase in employment for farm workers and unskilled laborers in the targeted areas and the increased income from this work. 1.3 Provision of inputs for rehabilitation, production and protection of 400 underproductive lands for vulnerable farming HHs in Area C prone to settler violence and affected by occupation regime Tulkarem, Qalqiliya, Jenin, Tubas, Jericho, Nablus:( Provision of seeds, fencing inputs for protection. CFW, and extension services) 1.7.a) Provision of new varieties of cash crops in which can compete in the production and marketing fields. 1.7.b) Provision of land protection inputs (fencing inputs to Farmers whoms land are in close proximity of settlements to protect from settler attacks and access of animals to the land that might ruin the crops. 1.4 Promotion of technical capacities Technical trainings on the best practices of cultivation new varieties of cash crops and the modeling irrigation units 1.8. a) Conduct 30 awareness-raising workshops to enhance women households’ knowledge in cultivation of cash crops 1.8.b) Provide extension services on good agricultural practices and rain-fed crops and rangeland management to the beneficiaries by MoA.1 Assisting needy and vulnerable households through- cash-for-work for Beneficiaries benefiting from inputs for land rehab (Activity 1.7, and irrigation networks A1.5 implemented through conditional and restricted Cash for Work CFW modality with each of the selected farmers ,including female workers and headed households. where cash assistance will be provided based on level of achievement of milestones by beneficiaries related to utilization of inputs provided by the project or reimbursement of approved agricultural inputs (individual case basis). 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

- Some communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN , in alignment with the “Do No Harm” Principles for humanitarian assistance depends approaches to reduce risks to beneficiaries as a result of intervention. MA’AN adopts key principles that serve to reduce unintended negative impact of the project; these principles include: Effective feedback mechanism:, strong linkages with beneficiary communities, with a feedback mechanism that has allowed targeted communities to participate actively in the design of the project, and will further allow them to participate in the beneficiary selection process, and determining needs per each beneficiary. Further, MA’AN will conduct a Risk assessment for each component of the response in partnership with the MoA especially for those at implemented at the community. In cases were implementation in Area C for works is risky, MA’AN and MoA will seek nearby localities in Area A&B for implementation of community inputs with benefit to the targeted Area C population ( such as wells rehab, carrier lines, modeling unit, waste water treatment…etc) Feedback/complaints, and suggestions of targeted communities will be documented and taken into consideration. The project staff will further hold regular meetings with villages’ representatives and will update them about the progress and workplan of the project. Further, MA’AN will implement the project in a transparent manner where beneficiary list will be made public and reason for any person not being selected will be stated. The community will have a period to object to the list. MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources. MA’AN has been working for many years in the target , including for support to livelihoods, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. Aligning with the “Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the land rehabilitation, MA’ANs technical team will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).for each phase of the proposed intervention, MA’AN will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessment and community consultations(FGDs).for each phase of the proposed intervention, MA’AN will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessment and community consultations(FGDs)To mitigate management of the CFW approach with beneficiaries, MoUs for cash assistance will be signed with beneficiaries and cash to beneficiaries will be provided in return for labor in their land based on achieved milestones that will be verified and documented by the project coordinators and agricultural engineers. 9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

- The intervention is designed to promote and help protect and the rights of people who have historically been marginalized or discriminated against, such as women, children, the elderly and PWDs practicing agriculture and herding in Communities in area C. For target HHs, the selection criteria is designed to be transparent and ensure Equal and more importantly equitable access to aid. The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households as well as on the vulnerability of the land and rehabilitation needs and the gender make up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in the household will also be an important part of the application process. As women-headed households are disproportionately affected by poverty. The needs of both genders are addressed equally. The entire family, regardless of sex, age, and disabilities will equally benefit from the intervention. As a positive measure towards women, the project team will take all necessary measures to assure equal access of women farmers to benefit from this project. These measures will include but are not limited to ensuring women participation in project orientation session, and monitoring activities. All beneficiaries will be treated with dignity and respect.Considerations to the needs of the elderly and PWDs are taken and will ensure that special arrangements are made to rehabilitate or construct green houses to them. At the start of the project, the project will hold an orientation workshop in each location to explain the project and tentative time frame. In order to involve the affected population in the project implementation. Community Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) will be formulated to assist in the project implementation and to ensure accountability and transparency with beneficiaries as it is part of MA'ANs community integration and ownership approach to community development. The (CBPSCs) will consist of members of the targeted communities, ,Municipality representatives, representative from the Directorate of Agriculture -DoA and representatives from local CBOsand the project team. Women and people with disabilities will be encouraged to be part of the committee. The committee will assist in the beneficiary selection according to the set criteria and provide information to the project management as needed on any special cases or circumstances. The (CBPSCs) will supervise the public announcement of the project among the inhabitants of the targeted communities. Representatives of the communities will participate in the application process. Women of these communities will play an integral part in the selection of beneficiaries through participation in the application selection process. A comprehensive analysis of the candidates will be conducted to select final beneficiaries of the land rehabilitation components. (CBPSCs) and beneficiaries will be encouraged to provide feedback about the process of application, selecting beneficiaries by contacting the field coordinators and/or the project manager. All suggestions and complaints will be taken seriously and needed action will be taken accordingly. Furthermore, the (CBPSCs) will also be involved in monitoring the project through conducting regular visits with the project staff, monitoring materials delivery, and conveying feedback on progress of rehabilitation to the project team- Further In the project deigned, MAAN considered the women and men different capacity. For example, the designed activities: management of new veriaties farms and the modeling irrigation units took into consideration the different in capacity between women and men in dealing with the established models.
