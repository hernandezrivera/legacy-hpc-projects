Project Summary

CCC Child Protection program will continue to provide comprehensive protection response services and create a safe and protective environment for children who are at risk of or have experienced abuse, exploitation, neglect, and violence as a result of recurrent crisis and conflict, assisting them in healing, recovering, and feeling safe again while collaborating with their caregivers and the local government and community-level prevention mechanisms in accordance with the Minimum Standards for CP and national level SOPs, guidelines, and protocols. The CCC CP program will reach 8150 people through an integrated response of static and mobile response approaches in the CP AOR priority locations (severity 3) of Juba (IDPs Camps) and Terekeka Counties in Central Equatoria State.
The proposed child protection responses are linked with the strategic objectives of the cluster and three domains of the CP AOR for HRP 2025. Case management services will be provided to children who have concerns about their safety. FTR services, alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), and provision of cash to meet the basic needs of the children. The Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) service will be provided for children and caregivers with psychological distress using a combination of approaches such as the Child Friendly Space (CFS) and schools for Social and Emotional Learning, and positive parenting sessions. Finally, the community-based child protection (CBCP) structures will be established/strengthened to the lower structure of the community, community outreach on CP awareness and GBV prevention messages, and capacity building of the frontline staff and community volunteers.
CCC’s Interim care centre will also serve as a point of referral for GBV survivors, UASC, and other OVCs for temporary alternative care arrangements as case management will be ongoing. In addition, the CP team will be part of the integrated mobile team in all locations where CCC is operating.

1. Needs

It has been Six years since South Sudan's revitalized peace agreement was signed, but child safety has deteriorated, increasing the number of children at risk of violence, displacements, abuse, and exploitation who require critical child protection services from 2.9 million children in 2022 to 3.7 million children in 2024 (SS HNO, 2024). The country continues to be vulnerable to cyclical and protracted risks, such as food insecurity, flooding, intra-national violence, and disease outbreaks. The child protection needs in the CCC proposed locations Juba (IDP Camps) and Terekeka are classified as severity three, and the needs of the children are very enormous. According to CCC’s team's observation, majority of the IDPs were children, including unaccompanied, separated, child-headed House holds, orphaned, and children with disabilities who experienced emotional abuse. The current South Sudan Humanitarian situation has increased children's needs for social-emotional skills development and psychosocial support (PSS) in order to strengthen their resilience capacity to overcome adversity . The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on 15 April 2023 furthermore led to an influx of people fleeing the country which has affected South Sudan significantly, with over 794,000 refugees and returnees, including 413,382 children (207,150girls and 206,232 boys), comprising of over 60% South Sudanese returnees. Under these conditions, children continue to be disproportionately affected by the crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence, exploitation and displacement. As of December 2024, there are 180 (76boys, 104 girls) children with various protection concerns, including unaccompanied and separated minors in alternative care arrangements, child neglect, child labour, domestic violence, and sexual exploitation in Juba (Urban and IDP camps) and Terekeka counties in CCC’s case management caseload that need continued support in line with their respective case plans (SS CPIMS +, December 2024). CCC interim Care Centre also received over 40 cases of children referred by CP partners or self-referral for protection from early forced marriage and child survivors of sexual violence, children transiting for family reunification (FTR) , abductees and abundant children, for protection and comprehensive case management services in the past 6 months which indicates an increase in child protection concerns. CCC is proposing to maintain its specialized child protection programming approach and mainstream child protection measures across other humanitarian sectors/actors (Wash, Education, Nutrition, Health, food security and GBV) to prevent, mitigate and respond to protection threats facing children affected by the humanitarian crisis in South Sudan. To reduce the incidences of child abuses, the project will empower frontline workers in child protection through a mix of interventions at family/community and institutional levels. This project will be implemented in Central Equatoria State specifically in Juba (Urban and IDPs camps), and Terekeka Counties. Currently, there is insufficient support/services for child protection in Central Equatorial State mostly in Juba, and Terekeka counties. While in the selected counties which fall in severity three of CPAoR, to meet the individuals needs of highly affected children and the broader needs of affected children within the community, CCC will render CP servicers such as Case management, PSS, FTR, MHPSS services for minors for one year in the selected counties. CCC therefore aims to prevent and respond to immediate life- saving child protection and MHPSS services to vulnerable children and their families in Juba, and Terekeka Counties of Central Equatoria State.

2. Project activities

CCC'S proposed child protection interventions are in line with the Protection Cluster objectives, particularly for the three main focus areas of the child protection AOR, such as: Provision of mental health and psychosocial support services; strengthening case management approach, and community-based child protection (CBCP), capacity building, and outreaches. The CCC will reach 8150 children and their care givers most affected and vulnerable populations, including UASC, GBV survivors, CAAFAG, and children with disabilities from the IDP, returnees, and host communities of Juba (IDP Camps) and Terekeka counties. CCC will continue creating a safe and protective environment for children and providing quality protection services to empower vulnerable girls and boys and work with their caregivers, while strengthening the capacity of the local government and community level prevention mechanisms in line with applicable standards, including the Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action, IASC Guidelines for MHPSS in Emergencies, and Core Humanitarian Standards. In addition, the CCC will use standards and guidelines developed and adapted by the Child Protection AoR such as CP Comprehensive Case Management SOP; Information-Sharing, and Data Protection Protocol (ISDPP); Guidelines on Alternative Care Provision for Vulnerable Children in South Sudan; and Guidance Notes on conducting Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR) in SS. The proposed CP activities include (a) Comprehensive Case Management: The CCC will continue to provide case management services including FTR to the most vulnerable children, including children with disabilities affected by the flooding, conflicts, food insecurity, neglects, exploitations and abuses as per the South Sudan vulnerability and risk assessment criteria. The CCC will use paper-based inter-agency case management forms and the system-based Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS+/Primero) to manage individual cases, conduct child and family assessments, develop case plans, provide direct and indirect services to meet needs identified in the assessments, conduct case follow ups as per the plan, and close cases when goals have been achieved. The caseworkers and supervisors will be trained on Child Protection Case Management, MHPSS, GBV and PSEA. Besides, the existing service mapping and referral pathways will be updated in all the operational areas in collaboration with the CP working groups in all the Counties. To ensure dignified and safe service delivery, CCC will prioritize cash assistance for children identified for case management support as per their child case plan and the cash. CCC will cooperate with UNICEF and UNMISS when CAAFAGs are released for reunification and economic and social reintegration in the community. (B) MHPSS: CCC will also provide PSS services through a variety of methods, including Child Friendly Space (CFS), PSS clubs in schools, a child protection help-desk, and Positive parenting skills training. The rehabilitation of existing centres and the construction of new CFS will create a safe environment for children to attend PSS activities. Teachers, PTAs/SMCs and CFS facilitators will be trained on UNICEF’s PSS manual . Furthermore, schools will be supported with the establishment of CP help desks, PSS training for the teachers, and provision of playing materials for clubs as part of the PSS activities. (C) CBCP: Finally, the CBCPC will be better equipped to prevent and respond to child abuse, neglect, exploitation, and violence. The CBCPC members will be trained on CPiE, case identification, and referrals to the CP actors. In addition, continuous community awareness of CP prevention messages will be conducted using different platforms.
