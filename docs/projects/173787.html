Project Summary

CPPLI is proposing a 9-month project that aim to enhance the protection and well-being of boys and girls in Adamawa State. The project will be implemented in 3 communities through community-based approach that geared towards strengthening and supporting effective delivery of lifesaving Child Protection services. CPPLI will:
1. Promote access to integrated case management services of girls and boys facing protection risks: case management services will be provided for UASC as well as other children at risk of abuse, violence, exploitation and neglect including children with disabilities. Through coordination with the MWASD, alternative care arrangements with prioritization of family-based solutions will be provided to children deprived of parental care, or whose parental care is accessed as inadequate. CPPLI will respond to the needs of UASC through coordination with the MWASD and ICRC for family tracing activities with the view to the best interest of the children. Other vulnerable children will access specialized services. Referral to inter-sectoral and other specialized services will be facilitated through coordination with other humanitarian actors on ground including health, education, nutrition etc. CPPLI will recruit 6 community-based caseworkers (2 caseworkers per community: 1 male & 1 female) to ensure case management services in line with minimum standards. Case management service is targeted to reach 260 children and 40 children with disabilities. Also, 6 community volunteers (2 volunteers per community: 1 male and 1 female) will be engaged to support caseworkers on case management process.
2. Support community-based reintegration of CAAFAG and other children and young people affected by grave child rights violation. CPPLI is already implementing CAAFAG reintegration programming in Adamawa under the ProSPINE+ consortium as partner to Plan International Nigeria. Leveraging on the existing programming, CPPLI will support the protection and education needs as well as vocational training and income generating opportunities for CAAFAGs. Through case management, identified CAAFAG will receive specialized services and PSS and by collaboration with education authorities, the CAAFAG will be enrolled back to school and supported with educational materials. CPPLI will also work with vocational authorities to integrate 90 CAAFAG (33 girls & 57 girls) into 3-4 months long vocational training where start-up kits will be provided to the children after graduation to begin working. Existing community-based child protection mechanisms will be strengthened and supported to raise awareness on CAAFAG stigmatization and prevailing child protection issues to the wider community members. Awareness raising activities is targeting to reach 6000 beneficiaries (2355 girls, 2055 boys, 796 men and 794 women).
3. Enhance the well-being of conflict affected children, adolescents and caregivers through provision of quality PSS services: through coordination with the existing Community Based Mechanisms, structured Child Friendly Spaces will be established to provide age-specific creative, recreational and friendly social activities for children including adolescents. CPPLI will construct 3 child friendly space centers (1 per community) for structured recreational and creative activities where 30 CFS facilitators (15 males and 15 females) will be engaged and train to facilitate CFS sessions and CFS is expected to reach 3000 children (1620 girls and 1380 boys) across the 3 locations. Adolescent girls attending the child friendly spaces will benefit from life skills education to build their self-confidence, self-reliance and decision-making skills to serve as influential members to their peers. This project is targeted to reach 250 adolescents (125 boys and 125 girls). In addition, positive parenting sessions for caregivers of vulnerable children/vulnerable caregivers as well as foster parents will be established targeting 180 caregivers.

1 - Needs

Madagali, Michika and Mubi South in Adamawa are part of the LGAs most impacted by grave child and human rights violations such as arbitrary detention, children association with armed forces or armed groups, child labor, targeted violence, killing, violence, exploitation, abuse among others. This severely exacerbate on the ability of the affected population (IDPs, returnees and host communities) to cope positively and meet their basic needs including protection and physical well-being. Children made up 93% of the most affected population in these local government areas and most of whom have been exposed to violence, exploitation, neglects, abuse, loss of friends, family separation, stress, deterioration of living conditions and lack of access to basic services. These conflict related experiences have a significant impact on the psychosocial well-being and development of the children and adolescents and have both immediate and long-term consequences for children, adolescents, families and communities. The 2021 CPSS response plan shows that 52,866 people in Madagali, 106,781 people in Michika and 84,515 people in Mubi South are in needs of child protection assistance where 41,299, 88,498 and 29,499 people were respectively targeted for the child protection assistant. There exist lots of gaps in the current child protection interventions as the child protection sub-sector is not able to meets all its indicators, hence, the need to scale up child protection responses to be able to provide case management services, reintegration and psychosocial support services that meet best practices in line with minimum international standards. Within the targeted LGAs, there are limited child protection actors responding to the needs of unaccompanied and separated children and other children at risk of abuse, violence, exploitation and neglects. This further exposed the children in needs to harm and aggravated their existing child protection concerns. Without adequate support and response, the lives of children in needs will continue to be at risk.

2 - Activities

CPPLI is an active member of the CPSS in the Northeast Nigeria and based on the severity of needs in Adamawa as described in the 2021 response planning for the sector, we proposed to achieve 3 main outcomes with clear detailed activities below: 1. Boys and Girls exposed to protection issues access comprehensive case management services in line with minimum standards. CPPLI will support FTR through coordination with MWASD and ICRC and other vulnerable children faced with protection concerns will receive quality case management services. Service mapping will be conducted on quarterly basis to update referral pathway and referral of vulnerable children to access inter-sectoral and other specialized services will be facilitated through coordination with the actors on ground including health, education, nutrition etc. CPPLI will recruit 6 community-based case workers (2 caseworkers per community: 1 male and 1 female) to provide comprehensive case management services. The caseworkers will be trained on interagency case management and receive continuous coaching and mentoring support from the CP officer. CPPLI will engage 6 community-based volunteers (2 volunteers per community: 1 male and 1 female) to support the caseworkers on case identification, care plan implementation, referrals and follow up. 2. Boys and Girls faced with grave child rights violation are reintegrated back to community through community-based reintegration services: CPPLI will provide case management services and structured recreational and creative activities for PSS to CAAFAG and other children faced with grave child rights violations. The children will be enrolled back to school and supported with educational materials to support their school retention. CPPLI will reintegrate 90 CAAFAG (33 girls and 57 boys) into Income Generating Activities (IGA) where start-up kits will be provided to the children after graduation. CPPLI will also conduct labor market assessment across the 3 project locations to identified the most suitable IGA to engage the CAAFAG with prioritization of their best interest. Master trainers will be identified who will support in training the children. Also, existing community-based mechanism supporting prevention and response on Child Protection issues will be strengthened and supported with materials to support their roles on awareness raising on prevailing and broader issues of child protection including stigmatization on CAAFAG reintegration. In total, our awareness raising activities will reach 6000 beneficiaries (2355 girls, 2055 boys, 796 men & 794 women). 3.Conflict affected children, adolescents and caregivers receive community-based quality psychosocial support services. Through coordination with the existing Community Based Mechanisms, 3 structured Child Friendly Space (CFS) centers will be constructed to provide age-specific creative, recreational and friendly social activities for children including adolescents. The CFS centers will be constructed in line with global standards having clear disability inclusion. Also, adolescent girls and boys attending the CFS centers will benefit from life skills education to build their competence, resilience, self-esteem, self-reliant and decision-making skills to serve as influential members to their peers to support peer-to-peer network. To ensure gender responsive programming, 250 adolescents (125 girls and 125 boys) for life skills and 3000 children (1620 girls & 1380 boys) for CFS are targeted. CPPLI will coordinate with the CPSS to adopt manual for adolescents life skills education. In addition, positive parenting sessions targeting 180 caregivers will be established. Through coordination with child protection sub-sector, CPPLI had adopted positive parenting manual of Better Care Network which is currently in operation under the NHF funded project in CPPLI. This manual will be used for the positive parenting lessons for this proposal.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Project staff including volunteers and facilitators and the existing community-based structures are trained on gender roles, gender inclusion and influencing, gender specific violence and violence against women and girls, Do No Harm principles, child and youth safeguarding as well as PSEA so as to ensure effective protection of beneficiaries during implementation. CPPLI will ensure that beneficiaries are not force to take any action that may expose them to further harm and information of the beneficiaries and informants are kept in strict confidentiality so as not to violate their rights. CPPLI has PSEA focal point who is also the safeguarding advisor for the organization and whose contact will be shared with the project beneficiaries in communities to give feedback or complaints on any sexual exploitation and abuse during the implementation of the project, where any incident of SEA is reported, CPPLI through the PSEA focal person will investigate the situation and appropriate action will be taken based on the safeguarding policy of the organization. CPPLI will also coordinate with the child protection sub sector to further train the PSEA focal person to be part of the North-East PSEA network managed from Maiduguri, Nigeria. All assistance to be provided during the project, CPPLI will ensure that no direct or indirect discrimination is put in the beneficiary selection, providing services and other intervention and any deliberate attempt to exclude any part of the community will be challenged accordingly. CPPLI will ensure close consultation with the relevant groups in the communities such as men, women, girls, boys, elderly, lactating mothers, pregnant women etc in all the stages of the implementation.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

The designed of this project is based on the 2021 Child Protection Sub-Sector Response Planning. For the 2021 HNO, 484,356 people are in need of child protection assistance in Adamawa with children covering 93% of the total population. A total of 1.25% are children with disabilities and 53% are all females. The severity of this needs have cut across all the 3 population groups with a total target of 12% on IDPs, 48% returnees and 40% host communities. Adamawa State continues to experience increased child protection needs driven by conflict, attacks by non-state armed groups and other insecurities such as communal clashes, kidnappings, banditry, theft among others. Insurgent attacks continue to pervade the state with a series of armed attacks in communities around the fringes of the Sambisa forest. The growing insecurity continues to hinder access to vulnerable returnees in Mubi South, Michika, Madagali and some parts of Hong and Gombi LGAs. Attacks by the insurgents were recorded in recent times in Adamawa in both Madagali and Michika towns leading to loss of lives and destruction of properties. Occasional infiltration and attacks by AOGs in communities bordering Borno from Adamawa were witnessed thereby exacerbating the needs of children and other vulnerable population in the targeted communities. There exist gap in the current child protection intervention in Adamawa hindering the achievement of the sector’s targeted indicators. Thereby, there is need to scale up live-saving responses through child protection services in Adamawa.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will establish feedback and complain mechanism across the entire locations. This will leverage on the feedback and complain mechanism survey conducted by Plan International Nigeria in Adamawa under the ProSPINE+ consortium project of which CPPLI is an implementing partner. Appropriate feedback box will be provided to the communities and the community members will be enlightened on how to give feedback with regards to the project programming. Also, monthly focus group discussion will be conducted with children (segregated by age, sex and disability) and other project beneficiaries. Help desk will also be established in child friendly space centers where in collaboration with the community structures focal persons will be identified and trained particularly on confidentiality, feedback mechanism awareness focusing on strategies of receiving and responding to feedback. In the course of the project implementation, feedback will also be received via dialogue sessions and focus group discussions at the mid-term stage of the project implementation which will be used to ascertain the satisfaction of the beneficiaries on the project programming and understand the needs of adjustments where necessary. During implementation, CPPLI will starts with effective information sharing about the project activities and programming in a timely, accessible and inclusive manner thereby putting affected communities in a position to understand and shape decisions that impact their lives. CPPLI will ensure that people receiving support participate in all decision-making process of programme cycle including baseline and end line assessment, beneficiary selection, implementation, monitoring and evaluations.
