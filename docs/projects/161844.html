Project Summary

CIDAR is currently implementing Emergency WASH interventions for vulnerable and affected population in 18 IDP Camps and 13 Host communities in four LGAs of Brono state in partnership with UNICEF Nigeria for a period of 12 months (12-Sep-2019 to 11-Sep-2020):
Jere LGA – 6 IDP Camps and 6 Host pockets
MMC LGA – 5 IDP camps and 4 Host pockets
Dikwa LGA - 6 IDP Camps and 5 Host pockets
Konduga LGA – 1 IDP Camp and 4 Host pockets
The major activities of UNICEF support Project of USD 1,727,026 (includes UNICEF WASH Supply as well) are:
- Operation and maintenance of 51 solar water facilities
- O&M of 24 hand-pump water facilities
- O&M of 20 Solar hybrid water facilities for extra hours during off-sun
- Upgrade of 6 hand-pumps to Hybrid Solar water facilities with hand-pumps
- Daily chlorination and free residual chlorine monitoring (FRC) of 679 water points (SMBHs, HPBHs, MBHs, Underground reservoirs receiving water frpm Urban Water Treatment Plan or centralized mega-borehole) covering total population of 311,400 in 13 Wards of Jere and MMC LGAs
- Repair/Rehabilitation of 50 Blocks of latrines in Custom House-1 Camp
- O&M of 118 Blocks of sanitation facilities in Teachers Village Camp to cover daily cleaning, repair/maintenance of sanitation facilities by beneficiaries through WASHCOMs
- Intensive Hygiene Promotion in focal Camps covering house-to-house visits, focused group discussions, hand-washing at critical times, safe handling of water and food, etc.
The UNICEF funded proposal was approved prior to WASH Sector’s ‘One Camp – One Actor’ Rationalization processes, which in fact, a brilliant idea as far as accountability of WASH Actors and effective resource utilization are concern.
This camp rationalization has created some gaps in almost every Camp and host community where one or two (max.) WASH partners will implement Complete WASH Package.
CIDAR’s case is not different from other partners and through proposed proposal for funding request to potential Donor(s), we meet the gaps filled in WASH Sector allocated/proposed Camps.
Proposed Project will reach additional 201,995 people through following WASH interventions in those Camps and host communities where CIDAR is implementing similar activities with another donor support:

A. Providing additional 560.6 m3 potable water for drinking and other domestic use per day from Solar motorised boreholes and Hand pumps in:
A-1 Upgrade of 12 broken-down Hand-pump boreholes to Hybrid Solar motorized Boreholes with Hand-pump (174 m3 water per day):
A-2 Rehabilitation and Operation & Maintenance of 45 broken down hand- pump boreholes in Focal Camps and Host Communities (256.6 m3 water per day):
B. Construction of 40 blocks of sanitation facilities in WASH Sector allocated rationalised camps in Dikwa, MMC and Jere LGA
C. Rehabilitation, repair, cleaning and maintenance of 120 blocks of toilets in allocated camps to CIDAR.
D. Operation, maintenance and daily cleaning of 250 blocks of sanitation facility by engaging beneficiary committees in focal Camp
E. Daily Chlorination and FRC monitoring of 1,000 community water collection points (covers - surface / uderground storage tanks, overhead tanks, - not water facilities) 17 Wards identified by WASH Sector as Cholera Hot spots Wards in MMC and Jere LGAs
F. Distribution of 12,000 replenishment kits in CIDAR allocated camps

1 - Needs

The ongoing armed conflict in North East Nigeria which started in 2009 has left an estimated 1,936,748 people (382,296 households) displaced and Largest IDP populations are located in Adamawa (10%), Bauchi (3.42%), Borno (73.69%),Gombe (1.89%), Taraba (4.44%) and Yobe (6.56%) as per as per Round XXVI Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) conducted from 20th November 2018 to 20th January 2019. According to the DTM, 745,397 displaced individuals are residing in 237 camps and camp-like settings (camps, collective centers and transitional centers) and 690,420 displaced individuals are residing in host communities. 56% of the IDP population are children (0 - 17 Years). Borno State has been disproportionately affected by the crisis and is the epicenter of military operations and displacement of civilians. Many LGAs are partially accessible and three LGAs are not accessible yet. The mobility within Borno state is limited as humanitarian organization can’t travel without military escort. The incidents of attacks increased recently on many roads/locations. Prior to conflict, access to safe drinking water and improved sanitation facilities in the North East has been generally poor for years; the conflict and the population displacement has significantly worsened the situation – particularly for IDPs and host communities. While low levels of WASH access existed pre-crisis, it is estimated that 75 per cent of WASH infrastructure, especially in local government areas (LGAs) affected by conflict, was destroyed during the crisis (Recovery and Peace Building Assessment, 2016). A significant influx of people returning from various locations within the north east of Nigeria and bordering countries have been recorded in Borno state. According to DTM Nigeria-Baseline Dashboard - Round 26 (January 2019), over 13,502 Nigerian refugees came back mainly to Banki, Pulka, Gowza, and Bama from neighboring countries. This increase has put an additional pressure on the existing WASH services. The ongoing rainy season doubled the likelihood of water borne diseases due to limited WASH infrastructure and poor hygiene practices. According to the 90-day Emergency Response Plan, new waves of displacement is taking place. The most affected areas of this influx are camps in Maiduguri, Konduga and Monguno. There is an immediate need for humanitarian intervention in the affected areas. With camps and communities running over-capacity there is a dire need for a quick response. As of January 2019, 43,244 new arrivals have moved to the three above mentioned LGAs. It is estimated by April 2019 (summer) this number has increased to 85,800 individuals. With this addition, the current responses majorly lack on-ground capacity to facilitate these demands. There is need for increase the water availability of an additional 1,247 m3 per day, construction of 500 sanitation facilities and distribution of approximately 23,355 Hygiene kits. There are some water distribution systems constructed and or rehabilitated but the actual water demand is higher than the production capacity, resulting in limited or sub-standard access to potable water. It was also observed by CIDAR that the frequency of water systems breakage is very high. The water systems are not being treated/chlorinated in camps. The overflow of sanitation facilities was also observed in many camps. Hygiene practices of IDP are very poor, and sanitation facilities are lacking in camps, where toilets exist but hygienic condition are very bad. There is need to establish a comprehensive operation and maintenance mechanism to ensure the water systems functional at all times and IDPs have access to potable water.

2 - Activities

The upcoming rainy also poses a challenge of water related disease outbreaks, hence the need to improve on water quality through treatment with chlorine as Borno state has been affected by cholera in the recent past. The proposed project focus on following key areas; i. -Operation and Maintenance of all existing water facilities including chlorination, monitoring of residual chlorine in 18 IDP Camps in Dikwa, Konduga, Maiduguri MC and Jere LGAs ii. -Upgrade of 2 high yielding hand-pump boreholes to hybrid (duel operation) solar powered motorized borehole with 10 m3 water chlorinated storageiii. -Upgrade of 4 high yielding hand-pump boreholes to simple solar hybrid water facility – direct water delivery to buckets/jerricans of 4 solar powered motorized boreholes in 2 LGAs of Borno stateiv. -Hygiene promotion in 18 IDP Camps in 4 LGAs of Borno statev. -Hygiene Replenishment Kits distribution to 22,668 households in 18 IDP Camps The proposed project will ensure linkages with Nutrition, health and education programme.Operation and maintenance of connected water supply will be ensured in CMAM/ UNICEF run health clinics and temporary learning centers/schools which exist in the targeted camps. Existing water points are located in different camps and host communities.O&M of Solar MBHs in Camps: 51 solar boreholes (includes 6 proposed upgraded hybrid SMBH & HPBHs) will be targeted for operation and maintenance in camps and host communities with IDP population.Extra Hours of Running of 20 Water Facilities in Camps: A total of 20 Water Facilities- 17 solar powered motorized borehole water facilities to run extra hours on generators and 3 fully generator powered MBHs to be covered under operation and maintenance (O&M) in camps.O&M of Hand pump Boreholes in Camps: 24 hand pump boreholes will be targeted for operation and maintenance in camps and in host communities.Reticulation to reduce waiting time and water spillage around collection points:To reduce waiting time at few over-crowded water collection points reticulations will be done in two water systems to add extra water collection point. Provision of supplies, materials and labour and all that is required to make the job complete in every part and detail to construct new water collection point, operate and maintain throughout the period of project. Activity-1.2-a: Upgrade of 2 hand-pump boreholes to hybrid (duel operation) solar powered motorized boreholes with 10 m3 water storage capacity 02 high yielding hand pump boreholes will be upgraded to duel operation (hybrid) solar motorized borehole and hand-pump at the same system to operate before solar powered pump starts and after it stops during the day. Water system will consist 10 m3 storage capacity and a water collection point with 6 taps, drainage and soak-pit or animal watering trough (community preferred).Activity-1.2-b: Upgrade of 4 hand-pump boreholes to hybrid (duel operation) solar powered motorized boreholes and handpump for direct water delivery to bucket Keeping the concept of cost efficiency, 04 high yielding hand pump boreholes will be upgraded to duel operation (hybrid) solar motorized borehole and hand-pump at the same system to operate before solar powered pump starts and after it stops during the day. Water system will deliver water direct from the borehole to buckets solar powered pumping and when it works as manual hand-pump. System is low-cost pumping device with handpump platform (UNICEF/WHO type), drainage.
