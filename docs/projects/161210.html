Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (ACF) has been operational in what is now South Sudan since 1985, responding to both chronic and acute humanitarian needs. Our strategy addresses the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition in children U5 by linking nutrition with WaSH and FSL interventions to foster a meaningful impact upon communities’ resilience as well as prevent childhood morbidity and mortality. Currently ACF is implementing program activities in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap and Jonglei states with established field offices in Malualkon, Alek, Old Fangak and Paguir Payams. In 2020, WaSH projects will be implemented to ensure that host communities, IDPs and returnees have safe access to water and sanitation facilities with improved hygiene behavior. Proposed activities will improve the nutritional status of children U5 and pregnant and lactating women through an integrated WaSH and Nutrition response aimed at breaking the diarrhea – malnutrition vicious cycle. This project will ensure the effectiveness of addressing malnutrition in the proposed target area by reducing disease burden in communities as well as availing safe, potable water for use in appetite tests at nutrition sites. To achieve the overall project objective and objectives of the HRP, we will provide critical services to 120,000 individuals, including 100,000 at the village level and 20,000 (non-double counted) beneficiaries in targeted Nutrition Sites and SCs. These 4 main activities include: 1) Providing timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected women, men, girls and boys. 2) Enabling vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender-appropriate sanitation services (household and site level latrine construction). 3) Providing a WaSH minimum package of interventions, adapted to the specificities of the related outbreaks, at targeted nutrition sites and catchment areas. 4) Increasing knowledge of appropriate hygiene and sanitation practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WaSH-related diseases. ACF will implement the above in the catchment areas of its 36 nutrition facilities in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap and Jonglei states. Baseline KAP surveys will be conducted in all target areas to identify the WaSH-related knowledge, attitude and practices of the communities. Detailed needs assessments will be conducted to assess community access to safe water sources and sanitation facilities. The implementation strategy will align with the needs identified in assessments and KAP surveys at project start. Additionally, the ACF multisector emergency response throughout the country will include a WaSH component to complement its nutrition work. The proposed program strategy takes into account specific gender-related needs during surveys and activity implementation to ensure the equitable participation of all gender and age groups.

1 - Needs

South Sudan faces a protracted economic crisis and physical insecurity exacerbated by civil conflict, which began in 2013. Over 4 million were forced to flee their homes, with 2 million internally displaced and 2.2 million residing as refugees in neighboring countries. Despite the September 2018 peace agreement, implementing the transitional government is delayed and severe needs persist. Per the IPC analysis for August 2019 to April 2020, the situation in South Sudan has improved slightly compared to 2019, with 6.35 million (about 54% of the population) estimated to be severely food insecure (IPC 3,4 & 5). The 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan projects 5.2 million persons to be in need of WaSH assistance. ACF’s 2019 SMART surveys in its operational areas of Aweil East, Gogrial West and Fangak, specifically, show GAM rates of 15.5%, 20.1% and 16.2%, respectively. While demonstrating marginal improvement from previous years, this is still above the WHO threshold of 15%. One of the key drivers of malnutrition is WaSH-related diseases due to poor hygiene and sanitation practices, and limited access to clean water. Fangak County makes the top ten in the WaSH Cluster’s severity mapping of “WaSH and Nutrition”, “WaSH and Health” and “WaSH Severity,” while Aweil East is part of the top ten in the “WaSH and Nutrition” and “WaSH Severity” mapping. Additionally, REACH (May 2019) reported that Gogrial West has the worst WaSH-based mortality rate (38%) nationally. ACF’s 2019 baseline KAP and SMART surveys found that in all three areas, more than 94% of the population practiced open defecation, and very few washed their hands with soap/ash and water (<10%) or had knowledge of 3 or more key times for handwashing (<25%). South Sudan’s State of the Environment report (2018) found that the country is also starting to see the effects of climate change, with a 2-degree centigrade increase in temperature and a 10-20% decrease in rainfall since the 1970s. Rains are becoming less predictable, perennial rivers are becoming seasonal, and more water is being lost to evapotranspiration. These changes are increasing stress on existing limited water infrastructure and supplies. Support for WaSH programs is necessary to reduce water-borne diseases, cut off the vicious cycle between diarrhea and malnutrition, and ensure that communities have safe and equitable access to water and sanitation facilities and practice good hygiene behavior. This is equally important in emergency response by ACF’s Multi-sectoral Emergency Teams in hard to reach areas of South Sudan. Also with the threat of EVD from DRC in addition to common WaSH-related diseases, WaSH will play an important part in infection protection and control in institutions and communities. Unusually severe flooding in 2019 exacerbates this situation in some parts of the country and is likely to have a negative short-term as well as medium-term impact. Finally, despite persistent needs, improved security and economic activity as a result of the peace agreement have warranted opportunities for transitional as well as emergency assistance.

2 - Activities

The project has four aims. 1) Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected women, men, girls and boys. 2) Ensure that SAM and MAM children and their caregivers have access to WaSH services at the static and emergency nutrition sites, including safe water for drinking and conducting the appetite test. 3) Increase knowledge and practice of good WaSH behaviors among vulnerable and affected populations. 4) Address emergency WaSH needs through multi-sectoral emergency team (MET). First, in partnership with respective state water ministries, ACF assess and map existing functional and non-functional water points in the target areas, and update relevant databases. We will also analyze catchment communities’ practices for safe water sources and compliance with Sphere Standards for water access. Data will be used to strategize the rehabilitation of existing nonfunctional water sources and installation of new ones, including boreholes, at targeted nutrition sites and catchment villages. In partnership with local state ministry and State Cluster Pump Mechanics Associations, pump mechanics will be identified and trained on borehole rehabilitation to ensure sustainability. Second, to ensure WaSH access at nutrition facilities, gender-segregated latrines will be constructed for caregiver families. The project will also provide a WaSH minimum package, adapted to the specificity of any related outbreaks, at targeted ACF nutrition facilities (safe sources of water, gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, and soap-equipped handwashing stations). Access to hygiene items and their proper usage are key to breaking the diarrhea-malnutrition cycle. As such, ACF will distribute hygiene kits to caregivers of SAM and MAM children. Female sanitary items will be included in kits for pregnant and lactating women and adolescent girls. Caregiver families in nutrition facilities will receive regular hygiene promotion sessions, including instruction on the proper use of kits. Third, the project aims to enable vulnerable and affected populations to maintain safe, sanitary and hygienic living, and increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices. As part of its sanitation strategy, ACF will launch CLTS/anti-ODF campaigns in target areas of Aweil, Gogrial West and Fangak to provide secure, dignified and gender-appropriate sanitation services. CLTS is the primary component of South Sudan’s Environment and Sanitation Strategy for SDG 6. Household self-construction of latrines ensures equitable access of women, which will further help in addressing GBV and protection issues at community level. Through PHAST and CHAST approaches, ACF will train and build the capacity of local communities and schoolchildren for improved hygiene behavior. Student Hygiene Clubs will be formed in targeted schools where children will be trained to practice and promote good hygiene behavior at school and community level. Children will work as agents of change, while their elders will be included through the PHAST approach. Participatory Rural Appraisal (PRA) tools will be used to motivate communities for proper hand-washing at key times, safe water handling and drinking, environmental hygiene and household hygiene practices. ACF will also respond to EVD and WaSH-related disease outbreaks with appropriate WaSH infection prevention and control, in coordination with other partners. Fourth, our countrywide multi-sector emergency team (MET) provides response to recurring acute malnutrition crises. As part of their mandate, the MET undertakes borehole rehabilitation, delivery of a minimum WaSH package to nutrition facilities, hygiene promotion, hygiene kit distribution, and building capacity of local partners. Finally, ACF is a part of a multi-nation 18mo study into the influence of WaSH factors on the incidence of relapse in cases of SAM.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Entitlements will be legally recognized as far as humanitarian assistance is recognized under the laws of South Sudan.Local authorities are involved in project discussions. MOUs are signed with communities and local authorities about the right for all to use the communal infrastructure provided by ACF’s WaSH program.ACF tries to build communal infrastructure on communal land; when this is not possible (e.g. needing to build a water point on higher land to mitigate flood risks), the community and local authorities are involved in negotiations with the private land owner and an MOU is signed between all stakeholders allowing all community members, without discrimination, to utilize the infrastructure.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Where displaced affected communities are identified during the project identification, they will be incorporated. According to the latest IOM DTM, approximately 30% of the NBeG and Warrap populations are internally displaced persons. These persons will be involved into community action planning processes to incorporate their perspective and plans for the future. ACF includes the input of all groups, including displacement-affected people, in the targeted communities through focus group discussions and pre- and post-intervention KAP surveys. Projects are also supported by community management committees, which are comprised of representatives from both host and displaced populations. ACF has MOUs with the communities which specify the non-discrimination of certain populations for use of communal infrastructure provided by ACF’s WaSH program. Hygiene promotion and sanitation education is given to all in the community, while hygiene kits and other distributions are based upon the nutrition programs’ beneficiaries. These include host community members, displacement-affected people (IDPs, returnees, refugees, etc.), and anyone else in need of nutrition support.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Nationally, ACF works in collaboration with the relevant line ministries of the government of South Sudan, including the South Sudan Rehabilitation and Recovery Commission (RRC) and the Ministry of Labor. At national and state levels, ACF continues to coordinate with the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry and the Ministry of Water and Irrigation, especially in the mapping of water points and follow-up monitoring to villages declared Open-Defecation Free under the CLTS approach. ACF links all its WaSH work with the local authorities. In addition, at the community level, ACF facilitates the creation and training of Water User Committees and Sanitation Committees. These committees link the projects to the targeted communities by fostering local ownership of supported WaSH facilities. Project management committees made up of women and men community leader networks facilitate the implementation, monitoring, and accountability insurance of all project activities.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with National WaSH Cluster Priorities: WaSH Cluster Objective (CO1): Integrate WaSH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package. - This is a primary activity of ACF WaSH Program which is tightly integrated with ACF’s nutrition program.WaSH Cluster Objective (CO2): Integrate WaSH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level. – ACF is prepared to do this as needed by drawing on its experience with integrating WaSH into ACF’s multi-sectoral emergency teams.WaSH Cluster Objective (CO3): Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees - ACF conducts outreach into the villages in the catchment areas of its nutrition sites targeting all parts of the community (hosts, IDPs, returnees, etc.). ACF rehabilitates or builds new water points, engages communities through CLTS and has community volunteers doing hygiene promotion. WaSH Cluster Objective (CO4): Mitigate WaSH-related gender-based violence. – ACF is mainstreaming GBV prevention into all its work, and all its infrastructure is designed to minimize GBV risk.The project is also in line with the PARTNERSHIP FOR RECOVERY AND RESILIENCE FRAMEWORK IN SOUTH SUDAN Pillar 2: Reestablish access to basic services. – The ACF WaSH project is ensuring targeted communities have access to proper water and sanitation facilities and services, and that their sustainable management is embedded into communities.Finally, the project is rooted in the priorities of the South Sudan WaSH’Nutrition Strategy, for which ACF led development and rollout.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project protection environment is stable: beneficiaries are able to move with relative security. Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Warrap, and Jonglei States have enjoyed reduced armed violence since 2016. Warrap in particular has been affected by inter-communal conflict in previous years, however witnessed significant improvement in 2019. If the protection environment were to become unstable, ACF will immediately cease program implementation and work to relocate and evacuate staff as needed. ACF’s WaSH programs have not faced any such situation since 2016, and do not project major instability in 2020. Given high rates of SEA in South Sudan, ACF will also conduct periodic GBV safety audits to ensure GBV risk mitigation. The WaSH Program will further contribute to maintaining a stable protection environment by: 1) Providing more functioning improved water points, thus lessening stress and tensions at the existing functioning improved water points. 2) Decreasing the amount of time women are vulnerable while fetching water by putting improved water points closer to communities. 3) Reducing the risk of GBV due to open defecation by facilitating the construction of household latrines.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

ACF prioritizes building resilience and locally led sustainability in its target populations through its community based, scalable approach. ACF focuses on beneficiary leadership in all activities to promote sustainability and risk preparedness. Work in communities relies on community based water user and sanitation committees and community volunteers. ACF establishes water user committees for installed or rehabilitated water points, and trains local pump mechanics to do pump rehabilitations. Most of the work is conducted at the household level (e.g. household latrines and hygiene promotion), and is designed so that the household can continue it without outside support. Hygiene promotion is conducted by locally recruited Community Hygiene Volunteers, using door-to-door and other methods. Therefore, the knowledge and skills on hygiene remain in the community with the volunteers and those who received the hygiene promotion outreach even after the program ends. Moreover, our pump mechanics trainings ensure that technical capacity for major water point repair is locally available after the project’s end. Pump mechanics are further invited to join the local Pump Mechanics Associations, for which ACF collaborates with the local Ministry of Water and Irrigation to facilitate registration and technical support. For household latrines and communal infrastructure ACF signs MOU’s with the relevant parties spelling out responsibilities and handover to the relevant parties. Institutional water and sanitation infrastructure are handed over to the nutrition or health program of the partner managing the supported facility, which often may be ACF’s nutrition program.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ACF maps available protection services in the area and visits protection service sites to learn more about available services. ACF also liaises with local protection actors to identify services and contact information. ACF trains all field staff on GBV concepts and Psychological First Aid, and promotes awareness of the availability of referral services where they exist through IEC materials and direct sensitization of beneficiaries during hygiene awareness sessions. Survivors will also be supported to pursue appropriate services, as ACF’s prevailing motto is that the survivor’s best interest will always take prominence. To ensure protection and GBV related considerations are integrated into implementation strategies, we involve women and girls in project committees and consultations, and conduct safety audits to capture any outstanding safety issues and suggest prevention/mitigation measures. Men and boys are also sensitized to improve the GBV environment. GBV indicators are included in WaSH program proposals to ensure that GBV threats and hazards are considered. All supported latrines are sex-segregated and lockable. Water sources are developed at appropriate places in consultation with the community, especially women and caregiver members, so that all gender and age groups can safely access the facility. ACF often faces pressure from local authorities when identifying locations for WaSH projects. To mitigate this pressure, ACF meets with local authorities and communities to explain selection criteria and receive input from local authorities and communities on priority needs. ACF signs MOUs with communities and local authorities that spell out the selection criteria and stakeholders’ roles and responsibilities. Ultimately, ACF makes its decisions impartially and based on need. It helps that ACF WaSH is closely tied with the ACF nutrition program and its beneficiary selection program is based on the needs data from the Nutrition program.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Community project management committees, alongside CV selection and activity facilitation, are additionally responsible to receive complaints, and address them or pass them on to ACF management staff if required. Committee membership is equally represented by women and men, and includes the membership of varying age and ability groups, as well as displaced populations. Women and men community leaders are involved in the assessment of the CV network, helping to monitor community activities. Their feedback also ensures the accountability of activities to meeting community expectations and needs. Mass awareness campaigns and CV household visits also inform communities on the intervention being delivered. All messages are tailored to the context and the gender and age groups involved. ACF has 5 mandatory feedback mechanisms: Monthly staff meetings (where frontline staff report issues faced by project beneficiaries), sex-segregated and mixed-sex FGDs, Individual interviews (KIIs), Comment Boxes present at base offices, and an anonymous Hotline and Email address. Depending on urgency, resolution of feedback is communicated to beneficiaries immediately or monthly. Specific responses are discussed bi-monthly during site level FGDs. Visual IEC materials, including text in English and the local dialect, posted at all project sites inform beneficiaries on available feedback mechanism modalities. ACF conducts Safety Audits for all supported WaSH facilities, in which they are assessed for protection risks and presence of mitigation measures. Part of the Safety Audit, direct conversation with beneficiaries who use the facility captures their perception of the facility’s quality and associated risks. At project start and end, varying gender and age groups are invited to participate in sex-segregated and mixed-sex FGDs which assess whether activities are meeting specific needs and not exacerbating protection risks.
