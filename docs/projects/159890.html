Project Summary

The emergency support project is a 1 year response plan to assist vulnerable IDPs and host community comprising of 10166 households and 61000 population in Yale,Tinagau ,Nyang ,Pagarau and Adior payams of Yirol East county of Lakes state .The intervention aims to ensure that lives are saved and human suffering is reduced through provision of emergency dry and main season vegetable, crops and fishing kits for vulnerable households and livestock interventions.
The prioritized objectives to achieve the overall goal will include:
Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support.
Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks.
The main activities to be carried out to realize this objectives and achieve the overall goal will include:
CO2: livelihood support: crop & vegetable seed kits, tools, fish kits and livestock vaccination and treatment in-kind except nutrition vouchers (cash + poultry + goat) and seed trade fairs (vouchers);
CO3: local procurement of seeds & cereals (aggregation centers); value chain addition and training: improved agronomic practices, post harvest losses.
WAV needs assessments, as well as Post Distribution Monitoring from ongoing programs, have identified significant unmet needs for both IDPs and host families who have lost productive crop agriculture or fishing assets due to ongoing conflict or displacement and as a result has reduced purchasing power and incomes that has hindered food security. It is critical to support these households to begin to restore lost crop agriculture or fishing livelihoods, thus enabling them to have increased food availability at personal production level, as well as small income for increased food access. The project will distribute fishing kits and then train fishermen on the usage of the new fishing equipment. Early distribution of fishing kits will support fishermen to take advantage of the fishing season. Similarly the project will distribute vegetable seeds and tools to beneficiaries (farmers) during early February for planting in the same month. In addition, the project will do post planting monitoring and provide periodic technical advice and follow up as necessary. During April and May, WAV will distribute staple crop seeds and tools to farmers. The beneficiaries of staple crops and tools will take advantage of planting season and will do the planting between May and ­June, 2020 The majority of IDPs in Yirol East traditionally men take care of the livestock and they are also involved in the fishery business, while women do most of the agricultural work. Due to these cultural and traditional approaches, the majority of agriculture beneficiaries will be female headed HHs.

1 - Needs

Recurrence of violence Intra and inter communal conflicts and instability in parts of Lakes state has had detrimental effects on the food security and livelihood situation affecting mainly women and children. 5.5m people in South Sudan are currently food insecure (IPC August 2019) a trend expected to rise due to seasonal forecast in locations prior to multiple crises, already below thresholds in food security, WASH, Health, Education and Nutrition . The county targeted in Lakes is Yirol East in Yale,Pagarau,Adior,Nyang and Tinagau payams currently in IPC 4 and jointly have a populations of 61000 people with 5% of the population currently IDPs. (IOM DTM, ’18). These County host the IDPs integrated into the host community without access to work, with food distribution as the main source of livelihood (WFP,NP and CRS 2017). FSNMS round surveys indicate that at least two thirds of respondents in Yirol East County reported having received some humanitarian assistance. Multiple displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecurity at households (43%) than residents (35%). Female headed households also has higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male headed households), a possible indication of gender based vulnerabilities. Significant decrease in the amount of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor passed from 20% to 30% to merely 6% to 8% in Yirol East county as a result of multiple displacements. Populations are now engaged in alternative coping livelihoods options such as sale of crops, livestock and unsustainable natural resources (poles, grass, firewood, charcoal) and/or begging. National production is expected to be below the five-year average, which will lead to low domestic supply in 2020 and a continuation of extremely high food prices. At the household level, food availability is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to crop damage from Fall Armyworm infestation. Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence rates are above emergency levels malnutrition thresholds (GAM >15%) in the targeted payams of Yirol East county. Disease outbreaks such as cholera and other AWDs have had significant effects on food security. Populations have resorted to less preferred dietary alternatives due to loss of livelihoods to both natural and conflict fueled causes, crop failure and multiple displacements. Prices of commodities have presently increased by over 200% due to local currency depreciation. Inflation currently stands at over 600% (SSNBS, 17). With over 61000 people said to be flood prone in the targeted county (52% Female) alone,this has affected road accessibility within the aforementioned county causing food prices to skyrocket in the local markets. Livestock conditions have also been impacted negatively due to changed migration patterns to cattle raid conflict, insecurity, and concentration of animals in small areas has raised concerns about disease outbreaks, reduced access to resources and veterinary extension services, as well as increased tensions between herders and farmers. Importantly, the terms of trade between livestock and cereals have drastically declined across all markets, undermining the purchasing power of aggro-pastoralists in Yirol East county.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

YES-Prior to this application participatory assessment through focused group discussion (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) was conducted in Yirol East with 30 participants involved drawn from different ages ,gender and diversities to ensure there is commitment in placing accountability to people affected by disaster and conflict at the core of its emergency policy and practice, from preparedness and the onset of an emergency throughout the phases of the programme cycle.The current FSL concerns being faced by the vulnerable marginalized groups (women headed HHS,people living with disability, elderly women and pregnant and lactating women) patterns and the risk factors of food insecurity have been identified. WAV will continue to ensure that this project utilizes a people-centered approach, sensitive to the dignity of all human beings, the varying needs of different segments within the community, and the importance of ensuring that women, men, girls and boys can equally access and benefit from FSL services provided . WAV will strengthen the existing community volunteer structures of farmers and fish folks groups in Yirol East to support programming and activity implementation through trainings for 1 year to enhance long term sustainability.During the selection of the beneficiaries there will be community consultative meetings to identify the vulnerable beneficiaries to be considered for support with the vegetable and fishing kits. Furthermore,WAV will build up this project based on the already existing knowledge of farmers and fishermen,and provide them with training's on improved vegetable production technologies and use of new fishing kits. WAV will follow participatory assessments to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. The criteria will prioritize vulnerable people, including IDPs, host communities and female headed HHs.

The 1st objective: To enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable,crop seeds, fishing and livestock support is in line with strategic objective 1: To save lives by providing timely and integrated multi-sector assistance and services to reduce acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys .The 2nd cluster objective is to Reduce dependency on food and agricultural input to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks which is in line with strategic objective 3: To support at-risk communities to promote and sustain their resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

YES-WAV will continue to enhance the capacity of staff and community volunteers on the integration of 4 protection principles (meaningful access, safety and security, accountability to affected population and participation and empowerment) in their work. WAV has developed guidance notes tailored to South Sudan context. During the workshops with community volunteers and front-line WAV staffs will be oriented on the 4 protection principles to enhance safety, dignity and meaningful access to the services provided. The recommendations developed will be supported to ensure quality service is provided for all based on age, gender and diversity. Safety and dignity will be promoted in the following areas: dis-aggregation of gender in distribution centres,distribution of the cop,vegetable and fish kits, installing locks in latrines, ensuring confidential spaces for reports, and building ramps to ensure people with disabilities can have meaningful access to the services in question. Furthermore WAV will carry out awareness raising to the community, local authorities and ministry of gender and social development at county level on protection principles and protection mainstreaming to raise awareness and influence changes in practices, policies, and procedures and to assist in solving the protection concerns through service delivery.”DO NO HARM” principle will be applied within all proposed interventions. WAV will work closely with other actors especially UN OCHA to ensure meaningful access is negotiated and provided to ensure smooth implementation of activities and underpin neutrality of support. Additional efforts are in place to work with marginalized segments of the population (adolescent girls, female headed households, widows, pregnant and lactating women, people with disability and elderly women, child headed households) to strengthen the key principle of empowerment and participation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

YES-During the preparatory phase of the project design community entry processes will be initiated through sensitization at the county level.There will be a brief overview on project goals,objectives,activities and monitoring and evaluation to gain the buy in from the stakeholders and to promote active involvement and ownership for the project. Sustainability of the project will depend on the beneficiaries at large who will continue with the support of the same activities and cascade to other communities .Capacity building of the skills of the community volunteers to conduct fishing and agricultural activities will support in smooth implementation of activities and also promote sustainability when the project comes to an end.Promotion of linkages and partnership to private and public institutions to support fishing and agricultural activities will enhance sustainability of the project even after the exit. WAV will work closely with the MOALF and also map out other humanitarian actors implementing fishing and agriculture interventions and collaborate with them throughout the project cycle
