Project Summary

Improving access to formal education and strengthening the resilience for the crisis affected students in 9 schools through an integrated innovative approach and better learning environment in Western Aleppo Countryside and Northern Idleb Countryside.
The project attempts to target both male and female children equally
The project supports formal education with the following activities:
- Rehabilitation of schools
- Teachers will be paid a monthly stipends.
- Each student will receive two meals weekly.
- Fuel will be supplied to schools for heating purposes
- Teachers will be trained on teaching methods and classroom management. Teachers will be observed during classroom throughout the second semester to assess their teaching techniques and classroom management pos-training to ensure the deliverables of training have been met.
- Catch up classes on Arabic, English and Mathematics will be available to underperforming students based on a selection criteria.
- Students will be supplied with stationery kits
- Schools will be supplied with the necessary classroom stationery, exam papers and printing materials.
Students will receive awards and certificates upon completion of school year; exam papers and printing materials will be covered.
- Students will be provided with PSS activities (4 modules from "I deal" curriculum)
- Students will be provided with hygiene promotion sessions on topics of oral hygiene, prevention of head lice and hand-washing techniques. Each student will be provided with the necessary hygiene items.

Needs

We expect to deliver educational support to approximately 8200 students aged 6-17, grade 1-12.After seven years of war in Syria, the educational infrastructure has been severely impaired. Schools buildings have been deliberately targeted by aerial bombardment and those that are still standing do not qualify as conducive learning environments.AN will work in Western Aleppo Countryside and Northern Idleb Countryside, where there is a large population of IDPs. Schools in the area lack the capacity to meet the needs of the IDPs.The project will address the limitation in financial assistance to teachers, the lack of quality of education, the gap in student learning and being able to fully integrate in schools and the lack of incentive for students to attend schools. Protection Risk Analysis

1. Risk: Changes in security situation, particularly in project target areas which will affect student attendance, distribution of planned activities and movement of staff. This includes risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/communities and the risks of airstrikes and cross fire. (High) 1. Mitigation: AN will suspend activities until fighting stops and the situation becomes clearer. AN staff will hibernate until further notice. This will be followed up by closely monitoring the security situation through liaising with local actors and authorities on the grounds. Examine the possibility of adopting different modalities to implement the project. All important documents will be saved as soft copies and evidence of hard copies will be destroyed immediately. Communication with donors will be initiated immediately to consult on possibility of shifting project activities to a safer location if needed. All schools will be supplied with hand held device to monitor the security situation as well. Schools will be trained on emergency drills and devised evacuation plan. 2. Risk: Train teachers and education personnel on classroom management and teaching techniques. There would be a limited number of beneficiaries in the program causing tensions in the community and pressure on implementing staff. Full attendance not achieved due to deterioration in security on some days. Limited number of experienced trainers able to provide the requested training. (Medium)2. Mitigation: Selection criteria and lack of resources is clearly communicated to local authorities and schools.Suspension of training and compensation for missing days when situation re-stabilize. Training is planned well ahead to ensure identification of sufficient trainers. 3. Risk: Provision of teacher incentives to a limited number of beneficiaries may cause inter-community tension and pressure on implementing staff. Tension/competition over salary/incentive due to different scales, interruption of payments, etc. Education staff switching schools due to different scale (High) 3. Mitigation: Liasion with the right teacher recruitment authorities for their selection criteria. Salaries should be approved and accepted by them. Standardize teachers incentives at the levels of ESWG/cluster and other major donors. 4. Rejection of activities by target beneficiaries, local and national authorities- Early consultation with the relevant authorities and clear communication of methodologies; consulting different segments of affected population on needs, type of assistance, modalities of intervention most suitable for them- MOUs signed with relevant authorities- Continuous review and reflection on the modality in which assistance is provided and adjusting the form if needed. - Feedback mechanisms and remedial action

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1-Fighting between opposition groups:AN will suspend activities until fighting stops and the situation becomes clearer. AN staff will hibernate until further notice. 2-Government forces shelling:AN will freeze all activities immediately. All staff will hibernate at the safest and nearest location until further notice.3-Local hostility towards INGO’s:AN will immediately suspend activities and hold discussions with local actors and authorities on the ground.4-Specific targeting of Arche nova staff members: AN will suspend activities immediately and discuss the situation within the organization and with the local actors on the ground. 5. Catch up classes not suitable for students with special needs. AN will refer any students with special needs to the right services and will be focusing more on them during PSS classes. Special activities can be designed by teachers with the assistance of AN staff and consultants.
