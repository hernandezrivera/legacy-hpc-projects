Project Summary

This project will ensure that the newly displaced and most vulnerable populations affected by conflict or disasters related to natural hazards with specific needs have timely access to Emergency Shelter (ES) and Non-Food Items (NFIs), Communal Shelter, Tents, and Cash or Cash for Rent assistance. This project contributes to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) Strategic Objectives 1 (provide timely multi-cluster lifesaving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity). IOM proposes to purchase, preposition, and distribute 40,000 NFI kits to reach 200,000 individuals, and 10,000 ES to reach 50,000 individuals. IOM will also provide 10m x 24m communal shelters for 1,000 households (HH) reaching approximately 5,000 individuals, distribute 5,000 tents reaching 25,000 individuals, and provide cash support or cash for rent to 2,000 HH reaching approximately 10,000 individuals as a preparedness and response measure for addressing the needs of newly displaced and vulnerable people with special needs and from protracted displaced populations in different states. Each NFI kit will have seven items: (1) two (5 by 6) meters plastic sheets, (2) two jerrycans, (3) one kitchen set containing 36 pieces including items such as pots and utensils; (4) three blankets, (5) three sleeping mats, (6) two mosquito nets and (7) one solar lamp. Each ES will have materials inclusive of bamboo sticks (30mm / 50mm), grass mats, wooden poles (Y poles), rope (1kg), tarpaulins/ plastic sheet (4m x 6m). The communal shelters will measure 10 meters by 24 meters and tents will be family size measuring 23 square meters inclusive of materials and necessary tools for installation. IOM will distribute cash for rent for approximately 2,000 vulnerable HH for a duration of six months as part of rental subsidy support. The assistance will reach approximately 10,000 beneficiaries considering a HH has an average of 6 people, based on Cash Working Group (CWG) standard calculation of an average number of people per household. Currently the most effective modality is cash in hand or using mobile money and IOM will continue to explore other options depending on the security situation and bank capacities such cash transfers using bank applications. To mitigate potential housing, land, and property (HLP) issues, IOM will facilitate a rental agreement between landlord and beneficiaries only and will hand over the monthly rental amount to the landlord in presence of the beneficiary. ES/NFI needs, market as well as feasibility assessment missions will utilize data from IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM), ES/NFI cluster, and the CWG to select the most vulnerable households to be assisted. IOM will transport the ES/NFIs, communal shelter materials, and tents to the distribution locations and will also make arrangements for cash support or cash for rent to be distributed to the most vulnerable populations in selected states. The distributions will be conducted in close coordination with ES/NFI sector lead, cash working group and partners and relevant stakeholders to ensure consistency in provision of assistance and to reduce possible duplications. As a final stage, IOM will undertake post-distribution monitoring (PDM) missions to monitor usage and relevance of the assistance two to four weeks after the distributions. IOM will adapt its interventions to the current context, ensuring it maintains safe access to services for beneficiaries and supporting staff to be able to deliver in-kind goods to locations that are secure especially with regards to cash distribution where added security measure will need to be in place. IOM will mostly target newly displaced persons but also vulnerable members of the host community eligible for assistance to mitigate risks of community tensions.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM plans to address the critical humanitarian needs through delivering timely emergency assistance to newly displaced persons including in a protracted displacement situation, refugees, returnees, and host communities. Focus will be on severe affected locations reported from the needs assessments within the country and along border points with Egypt, Chad, South Sudan, the Central African Republic, and Ethiopia where the displaced are facing harsh conditions, enduring extended waiting periods due to border crossing complications, with scant or non-existent infrastructure and services, as host populations were already overstretched due to climate change and food scarcity. The ES/NFIs will include certain items - plastic sheeting, plastic mats, jerry cans, solar lamps, blankets, and cooking sets in the kits provided to help restore a minimal sense of dignity and protection against exposure to the elements, mitigate health risks, and provide some privacy and security for those affected. In addition, IOM will establish communal shelters and distribute tents and provide cash support or cash for rent to the vulnerable affected populations. This will be critical to ensure privacy and safety, mitigate GBV risks, safeguard displaced populations from harsh environmental conditions to preserve security in displacement and their health amid the upcoming rainy season. IOM will conduct specific assessments with the support of relevant staff including the ES/NFI, WASH, CBI and protection staff including rapid needs assessments, door-to-door household assessments, focus group discussions, briefings with community leaders, field observation in target locations to prioritize the most at-risk households within each community including and people with specific needs (PWSN), and gender considerations related to the participation of men and women in the distribution process. Further screening and verification will be conducted by the field teams in the form of specific needs assessments to identify gaps and additional vulnerabilities of the target population DTM data and beneficiary lists will be shared with ES/NFI, WASH, Protection Clusters, CWG, and other partners, following IOM Data Protection Principles, to ensure there is no duplication in assistance and the most at-risk beneficiaries are supported including persons with special needs such as single parent households with multiple dependents, unaccompanied/ separated minors, elderly, pregnant and lactating women, women at risk, people with chronic illnesses, and persons with disabilities.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will apply principles ensuring accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase – registration of newly displaced persons and other affected populations, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected populations to the implementation of the interventions. Beneficiaries will be identified in consultation with community leaders, local authorities, IOM national partners on the ground and when practicable, IOM will also receive referrals from cluster partners. IOM will perform field visits to check the cases to measure the minimal shelter living standard and assure protection of beneficiaries before the enrollment for rental assistance. IOM will apply PDM to track the implementation of project activities and conduct interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on ES/NFIs, communal shelter, tents, and cash support or cash for rent quality, appropriateness, and utilization as well as any complaints including alleged SEA, which will be reported using the appropriate pathways. PDM will also assess the short-term effects of ES/NFIs, communal shelters, tents and cash support or cash for rent in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group, and CWG at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. A PDM plan will outline data collection methods, i.e., household interviews, focus group discussions, and observations.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

IOM will adhere to its gender mainstreaming, PSEA and accountability to affected populations (AAP) guidelines, as they are aligned with the “do no harm” principle and IOM's Institutional Framework for Addressing Gender-Based Violence in Crises. The needs of individuals in extremely vulnerable situations will be particularly reflected in the needs assessments, programme design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. In these areas, the sector will ensure the following elements are considered: (1) safety, security, and dignity; (2) equitable access; (3) accountability through community-based feedback mechanisms, with information collected based on on-site and PDM. This project will prioritize households headed by females, children, and elderly to receive ES/NFI assistance, communal shelter, tents, and cash assistance. All members of the community including people with additional vulnerabilities, as well as women and girls, will be separately consulted and encouraged to be fully involved in the consultation, distribution, and post-distribution processes. Beneficiaries will also be involved in assessing their access and safety around accessing services, e.g., identifying the locations of distributions points, and identifying appropriate risk mitigation measures that ensure adherence to “do no harm”. IOM has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA, which is in line with the UN policy, and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA. Staff will be trained on protection topics including how to treat GBV disclosures ethically and appropriately, as well as using the referral pathways and provide essential support to community members to access these pathways. IOM will work with staff and suppliers to strengthen the implementation of the PSEA through awareness raising and delivery of PSEA face-to-face or virtual training in line with IOM PSEA guidelines.
