Project Summary

The worsening economic, political and COVID-related crises in Lebanon have had devastating impacts among vulnerable communities and particularly for women, girls and boys, and other populations such as the Lebanese host community, migrants, Palestinian Refugees (PRLs) and people with disabilities. The International Rescue Committee (IRC) proposes a coordinated and holistic response to provide Gender Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) services to reach vulnerable communities most in need.
Based on the IRC’s experience in Lebanon, it is evident that reported GBV incidents do not provide a full picture of GBV that is occurring. GBV survivors are often unlikely to report GBV due to the fear of continued cycles of violence, shame, stigma or lack of information about available services. It is essential to improve access to specialized GBV prevention and response services that increase safety, mitigate harm and reach diverse groups of women and girls who have experienced GBV or are at risk of GBV. The proposed project will utilize approaches (mobile or in person) to increase accessibility to GBV services including the provision of mobile safe spaces and focused psychosocial support (PSS) activities.
Mobile safe spaces will serve vulnerable communities with limited access to services. IRC’s mobile teams will conduct outreach to introduce vulnerable women and girls to services. To engage the community in a holistic GBV response, the IRC will conduct sensitization activities for caregivers, men, community leader and local authorities about the importance of utilizing services and the consequences of GBV. Beneficiaries will receive non-focused PSS services and information and awareness sessions tailored to build the trust with the community. PSS activities will include Arab Women Speak Out and Emotional Support Groups for women, Life Skills through Drama, Girl Shine, and Early Marriage Tailored Package for girls. Women and girls will be free to voice their concerns related to safety and GBV risks through Focus Group Discussions (FGD) and other activities. Beneficiaries will receive appropriate referrals, and safety plans will be developed through GBV case management sessions.
Based on the IRC’s experience in Lebanon, it is evident that Lebanese and other children are not adequately accessing services provided by NGOs including social protection, judicial protection, mental health support, and PSS activities. Families also need assistance to reduce negative coping mechanism including violent discipline, and child labor. To address the needs of vulnerable Lebanese, migrants, and PRLs, the IRC proposes to implement activities in regions that have extremely high levels of socio-economic vulnerability which drives protection risks for Lebanese and migrant children and their families.
The main goal of IRC’s CP interventions is to increase access to protection pathways to prevent, reduce and respond to the risks and consequences of violence against children. The IRC will provide lifesaving services including tailored case management, focused PSS and risk mitigation activities for children at risk of any kind of abuse or exploitation. Prevention activities targeting boys and girls, caregivers and other community members will include the provision of community-based PSS, parenting skills classes, awareness sessions and community events aimed at promoting positive practices, behavioral change and reducing child labor and violent discipline.
Children will be identified through community-based outreach and community events designed to engage participants, identify specific vulnerabilities including children with disability, identify emerging CP trends, and find ways to address evolving challenges. IRC will coordinate with key stakeholders such as municipalities, Social Development Centers and Community Based Organizations to stimulate referrals from other sectors and increase outreach to vulnerable and at- risk children.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

1.1: Outreach activities: conducted with community members (caregivers, men, community leaders and local authorities) on the importance of the available services and the consequences of GBV. Non-Focuses PSS and awareness sessions: conducted to build trust, respond to the needs and raise awareness. GBV risk assessment activities: conducted with women and girls to provide them with a safe space to voice out concerns related to their safety and exposure to GBV risks within their communities.2.1: Provision of Case management and specialized services for children at risk using the National SOPs. Emergency assistance, referral to specialized services including cash assistance and alternative care will be considered.2.2: Provision of FPSS to children showing signs of distress, exposed to extreme neglect, abuse and exploitation as well as working children and parenting sessions for caregivers. curricula will be adapted to cater to the evolving needs and expectations of the target groups.3.1: Provision of community-based Child Protection activities including PSS sessions for girls, boys, and parenting sessions for caregivers. 3.2: implementing child protection initiatives to mitigate child protection risks in communities based on active participation of beneficiaries in identifying needs and designing activities.4.1: Activities include quality GBV case management, focused psychosocial services (PSS), and necessary referrals using a survivor-centered approach.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Several risks might influence the project implementation including health risks, security risks and socio-economic risks. IRC’s Mitigation strategy focuses on the adaptation of activities to avoid disruption of service delivery through the application of remote modalities (remote case management, remote counselling, and remote PSS) using guidance adopted by the sector. The adaptation of activities ensures compliance with COVID-19 safety and infection prevention control. Through the Humanitarian access and security department, IRC will maintain regular analysis of the security situation and potential risks; safety procedures will be circulated to staff to ensure their own and beneficiary safety in areas of service delivery. IRC will rely on its contingency plan to mitigate the risks that would affect the activities implementation namely the lack of fuel, power and internet. IRC agreements with supplier include clauses ensuring the continuation of supplies provision in case of crisis.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IRC’s dedicated MEAL team ensures that feedback from beneficiaries is mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. The MEAL team collects and analyses feedback from beneficiaries on a regular basis to inform project adaptations. IRC also utilizes a national feedback and response mechanism (FARM) for adults and children. Feedback communication channels (hotline, WhatsApp, feedback boxes, email, approaching Feedback Officer, help desk and SMS) and materials are used by beneficiaries to voice their suggestions, feedback, and complaints to an independent accountability team. After the onset of COVID-19, the Accountability Team adapted its outreach method from face-to-face sessions to two videos, one targeting children above six years and the other targeting adult beneficiaries. Regardless of the modality of implementation, IRC teams communicate constantly with beneficiaries explaining their rights, communicating hotline numbers and receiving their feedback on IRC activities.
