Project Summary

This project has two components of the protection, the first component is the general protection (Serief, Kabkabiya, Kernoi, Kutum, Tawila, Um Baru and Dar Alsalam beside Port Sudan, Sawkin and Hya from the Red Sea) includes supporting the persons with specific needs with referral to specialised services providers , support the communities and stakeholder in legal assistance and specific groups that will be supported through psychosocial support, the project will also include the cash support for identified with special needs to get their needs, this project activities will also train community groups aiming raising their protection awareness associated with community based structures establishment including renconcialion committees and youth networks where government authorities and peace partners.
The second Components(GBV): The proposed activities for GBV will complement HDPO’s interventions on protection issues with implementing awareness raising campaigns on GBV and its consequences on women and girls and the whole society in six localities in North Darfur: As Serief, Kabkabiya, Kernoi, Kutum, Tawila, Um Baru. Also, through these campaigns you will focus on raising the awareness on available support and services for GBV survivors in the targeted localities. The campaigns will involve large number of people and will include public events that engage men and boys in preventing GBV. The HDPO will also use social media, radio and TV to reach various segments of the community. It is expected to reach at least 6000 persons through this activity directly and indirectly.
In addition, HDPO will implement community sensitisation sessions in the targeted localities. These activities involve a limited number of people (less than 50) on topics like GBV and health, domestic violence, child marriage etc. It is planned to reach 2000 people through this activity.
Furthermore, HDPO will establish 6 community based protection networks in the targeted localities with attention to gender balance (15 member in each CBPN). The CBPNs members will be appointed in consultation with the community and will be directly involved in the awareness raising activities and referral of disclosed GBV cases to available GBV services. The 90 members will receive trainings on GBV, safe referrals of survivors and basic psychosocial support (PFA).

1 - Needs

The major causes of vulnerability in North Darfur, Red Sea, and South Darfur are conflict-driven, economic crisis, and family instability. These have resulted in the migration of millions of inhabitants from the place ton other to camps in urban areas' outskirts, with minimal livelihood resources. Many IDP camps have existed for over 15 years, becoming increasingly violent and volatile with reports of various human rights violations. Further movement of populations is likely to occur due to political tension and instability in hotspots like Darfur. This increases civilians' vulnerability, particularly women and children, to protection risks such as SGBV, family separation, child recruitment, and psychosocial and physical harm. Perpetual risk of hunger caused by food insecurity and flooding may lead to more child abuse, neglect, and exploitation. Children are forced out of school to fend for families through petty trade and domestic/unskilled labor. Displacement will also further contribute to the breakdown of the community self-protection systems. The conflict recently broke out In Eastern Sudan and mainly in Port Sudan, leaving serious protection issues, putting extra overburden on the lives of the vulnerable people especially people with special needs across community structures; hence this project aims to support those categories and provide the essential interventions.Humanity for Development and Prosperity Organization (HDPO) works in (red Sea, North and South Darfur ) with IDPs, returnees, and host community. Services in the IDP camps are severely limited, particularly regarding education. The majority of children can be found wandering within the camp, leaving them more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks like exploitation and child recruitment. Per unpublished records from agencies in affected areas, children in 4 out of 5 families are subject to violence, abuse, and/or harmful cultural practices, such as child abandonment, neglect, child marriage, corporal punishment, and SGBV. Effects for girls can be as serious as unwanted pregnancy and miscarriage, child mortality, STIs, and serious injuries. Children are also less likely to be sent to school when they must travel through highly insecure areas daily, coupled with transportation costs and the undervaluing of girls’ education. 58% of community members do not know the exact age to get a girl married. Moreover, the majority of Sudanese nationals do not have identification documents like birth certificates. Gender analysis reflects the change in gender roles and labor division due to the camps' lack of work opportunities. Many men leave the camps to look for money to support their families, resulting in women assuming households’ heads' responsibility. The number of female-headed households among IDPs is about 31%, much higher than that of returnees (22%) and settled communities. As a result of these pressures, many women find it difficult to function psychologically and socially. In cases of child-marriage, women, as well as the children, do not have decision-making powers. 70% of female respondents mentioned that the decision to marry depends completely on the father or other male family members, leaving out women, who are most likely to know the interests of the child in terms of needs, challenges, and priorities, or even approximate age of the girl child.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Objective 1 Provide protection services to vulnerable crisis-affected people, particularly those with special needs;The following activities will be mainly in port Sudan • Identification and referral of the persons with specific needs within Port Sudan to appropriate services and assistance.• Conduct psycho-social support and awareness sessions targeting people affected by conflict such refugees, host communities focusing on the elderly, People with Special Needs.• Mapping out specialized services provided by the humanitarian agencies through community centres.• Case management of those expose to risk with focus on women • Targeted individual assistance to participate those who can’t hear or blind to get access to services. Objective2: Strengthen community-based protection mechanisms to prevent and respond to protection risks where feasible;• Establish and train women, adolescents, children and religious leaders committees in North Darfur localities including villages.• Conduct training on Human Right in general and group wise rights targeting local different community structures and CBOs in North Darfur.• Arrange awareness session and social dialogue on various protection issue to promote community-based protection mechanism and improve social cohesion between refugees and affected host communities. • Conduct monthly dialogue with the trained institutions to monitor their adherence to guidelines, procedures regarding the protection and human rights standards to increase immediate response to protection concerns.Objective 3: c) Contribute to a favourable protection environment through engagement of and support to authorities, humanitarian and peacebuilding partners.• Negotiation, evidence-based advocacy and capacity building of service providers and relevant stakeholders for protection mainstreaming at the community level in all localities • Monitoring of protection environment in settlements and villages through various monitoring methodologies and tools by engaging relevant stakeholders and produce protection situation reports and sectoral reports on a monthly basis • Conduct regular protection-related/scheduled to increase positive interaction, involvement, and effective coordination in monthly protection activities. • Support the state level protection units in policies and guidelines developments. • Disseminate the sector documents and guidelines to the communities structures . • Involvement of the peacebuilding elements in protection through ensuring the women and young leaders participate and benefit from the opportunities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

This project will maintain the following druing the different phases: 1. Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. 2 – Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. 3 – Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. 4 – Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacitiesand assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.

Critical activities include running awareness-raising sessions with women, girls, boys, and men in communities of concern and other stakeholders (including implementing and operational partners, government officials, civil society organizations, and companies in the private sector). To prevent SEA effectively, all staff and humanitarian workers must make sure that PoC is aware of their rights and entitlements. PoC should know that they are entitled to assistance, that they should never be expected to provide any form of sexual favor, and that demands for such favors are strictly prohibited. They should also know how and where to report SEA incidents safely and confidentially when they occur.
