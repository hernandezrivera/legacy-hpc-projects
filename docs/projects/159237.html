Project Summary

6.3 million Somalis are acutely food insecure through December 2019, a 36% increase since late 2018. This includes 2.1 million Somalis in IPC 3-4 (double an average year) and 4.2 million in IPC 2 (the highest on record). The situation deteriorated most significantly in rural areas, where the population in IPC 2-4 doubled and IPC 3-4 tripled. Since 2016, six out of seven rainy seasons have been poor, including Gu 2019 which yielded Somalia’s lowest cereal harvest in decades in southern Somalia. Pastoralists also struggle to recover from significant livestock losses and debt incurred from the protracted drought.
FAO’s project will directly assist 150,549 households (903,294 people) and deliver countrywide veterinary services, contributing across FSC’s three strategic objectives (SOs).
To improve immediate access to food (SO1), FAO will deliver cash assistance to 20,000 households through cash-for-work (CFW) to repair community infrastructures and 71,758 households through integrated cash and livelihood programmes (Cash+). Beneficiaries will receive 3-6 monthly cash transfers, based on duration of the lean season and their vulnerability. To support transition out of emergency cash programmes, FAO will offer lighter and longer-term cash and livelihood assistance to 4,500 chronically vulnerable households in districts with high recurrence of IPC 3-4. FAO’s cash programme assists severely food insecure people to meet their food immediate needs, while giving them choice and re-injecting cash into the local economy. FAO delivers cash through mobile money operators and money vendors, adopts rates recommended by the Cash Working Group and regularly monitors the availability and prices of selected food and non-food commodities in local markets through FSNAU.
To protect and restore livelihoods (SO2), FAO will provide quality production inputs and training to Cash+ beneficiaries and an additional 31 791 vulnerable households. Tailored livelihood support packages will enable farmers to secure a Gu/ Deyr harvest, pastoralists to enhance milk production and coastal communities to improve fish catch and processing. The project will further enhance production and promote climate smart practices through field school approaches, targeting 2,500 farming, pastoral and coastal households that will pass on the knowledge to their wider communities. To protect livestock assets, FAO will conduct country-wide livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns to protect 17.5 million animals; and deliver emergency water supply and animal feed in the case of severe drought (20,000 vulnerable households).
To build resilience and mitigate new shocks (SO3) at a community level, the project will improve water management and related infrastructure to protect rural populations and livelihoods against flooding (repaired river embankments) and cope with drought (fenced water catchments, animal troughs, canal sluice gates). FAO will also support government and community led efforts to control plant and animal pest and disease outbreaks. This includes (i) stocking State Ministries of Livestock and Livestock Professional Associations with veterinary drugs, water bladders, vector control treatment (in cases of severe riverine floods), PPR vaccines and related test kits to advance PPR eradication in Somalia; (ii) developing an Antimicrobial Resistance Strategy with the Ministry of Livestock; (iii) supporting Desert Locust Units to collect data on locust prevalence during the breeding season; and (iv) mobilizing Ministry of Agriculture extension staff and NGO partners to roll out large field trainings with farmers to control Fall Armyworm in key cereal producing areas.

Project Activities

FAO will directly assist 903,294 rural people in need (150,549 HH), while delivering veterinary services to 17.5 million animals, belonging to 2 million people. 1. CFW/UCTs: 20,000 HH. Households with members able to work will engage in 24 weeks of paid labour to rehabilitate community infrastructure (e.g. water catchment, canal) or undertake work to improve their productive environment (Prosopis clearing, contour bunding). Households unable to work (pregnant, elderly, ill, disabled people, etc.) will receive UCTs of the same amount and duration. 2. Cash+ Livelihood Packages: 71,758 HH. FAO will provide 3-6 monthly cash transfers and tailored livelihood packages for food insecure farmers, pastoralists and coastal fishers, including: (i) Cash+ Agriculture for 47,758 HH (cereal, legume, veg seeds, tools, tractor/irrigation hours, fertilizer), (ii) Cash+ Livestock for 21,900 HH (mineral blocks, deworming, mazzicans); and (iii) Cash+ Coastal Fisheries for 2,100 HH (e.g. boat, gear, safety supplies, cooler, handling & cooking kits). Households without land/livestock assets may receive UCTs alone. FAO will provide all beneficiaries training in climate-smart practices, complemented by sessions in nutrition (infancy to old age) under Nutrition Cluster efforts. 3. Longer-Term Cash & Livelihood Support (L-TLC): 4,500 HH. Already being piloted with USAID funding, L-TLC will enroll the most vulnerable segment of beneficiaries from recently concluded emergency cash interventions, where IPC 3-4 is recurrent. Each HH will receive (i) extended cash assistance (monthly UCTs of USD 35), (ii) livelihood support (e.g. bee-keeping, dairy, fruit & veg), (iii) guidance in savings schemes. 4. Gu/Deyr Crop Production Package: 31,791 HH. This package includes the same inputs, services and training provided under Cash+ Agriculture, but does not integrate cash transfers. Where possible, FAO will partner with NGO Resilience Consortia to deliver integrated assistance. 5. Countrywide Livestock Vaccination & Treatment Campaign: 17.5 million livestock. FAO will vaccinate 14.5 million goats against CCPP and treat 3 million livestock (parasites, wounds, respiratory infections). 6. Emergency Livestock Supplies & Services: 20,000 HH. FAO will provide supplementary feed (range cubes) & truck water in cases of extreme need. 7. Farmer, Pastoral & Coastal Field Schools: 2,500 HH, 88 schools. FAO will enhance production and promote climate-smart practices through field schools tailored to (i) pastoralists (value addition; fodder production, processing & storage; disease surveillance); (ii) farmers (diversified food & cash crop production, marketing, value addition); and (iii) fishers (ecological approaches, non-destructive fishing methods including regulated net mesh size, new fishing technologies, dried fish products). 8. Plant Pest Surveillance & Control: FAW & Desert Locust (DL). FAO will support DL Units in Somaliland and Puntland to collect data on locust prevalence during breeding (Oct-Mar) for early warning & action. To control already high & damaging FAW prevalence, FAO will train and mobilize 300 MoA staff and NGO partners to conduct large extension programs to monitor & control FAW with farmers in 15 cereal producing districts. 9. Livestock Disease Surveillance & Control. FAO will stock Ministries of Livestock and LPAs with veterinary drugs (3 million animals), water bladders (600 units), vector control treatment (in case of floods) as well as PPR vaccines (3 million doses), solar fridges (60 units) and test kits (1,000 units) to advance PPR eradication in Somalia. 10. Enhanced Community Infrastructure for Flood & Drought Resilience. FAO will contract works to repair river breakages (18) and improve the efficacy of infrastructures already repaired by communities through CFW, including water catchments (20 enhanced with fencing & troughs) and irrigation canals (10 enhanced with sluice gates, pumps, supported canal committees).

Mitigation Measures

2a-b. Exclusion and marginalization: FAO Somalia verifies and ensures that beneficiaries identified in the tripartite (NGO, elders and council members) participatory selection process meet the selection criteria (female, male, age, IDP, household heads, etc.). NGOs hold sensitization and communication sessions with all targeted communities about the selection criteria of the project. The same info is broadcasted through local radio before activities start. FAO selection criteria ensures that at least 30% of beneficiaries are women-head households for each programme activity. Moreover, FAO has formal complaint and feedback mechanisms in place, including a Call Centre and a toll-free Hotline. Third Party Monitors provide further opportunity for beneficiaries to report on the selection process, provide feedback and complaints. 3b-c. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection:In addition to the above, FAO heavily engages with district, state and federal authorities to facilitate and solve issues of interference by external stakeholders. FAO triangulates information on alleged cases of interference and seeks mediation from relevant parties (elders, NGO staff, local authorities, etc.). If the case cannot be resolved, FAO reprogrammes activities to other districts. All contracts with FAO implementing partners include a clause on zero-tolerance of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), and obligate partners to report such cases. NGOs receive dedicated trainings on AAP, Prevention of SEA and gender inclusion principles of FAO activities. 4 b-d. Protection mainstreaming: In addition to the above, FAO mitigates these protection risks by avoiding large gatherings of beneficiaries, through conducting registration and input distribution at village level. FAO has also developed a mobile payment system that mitigates protection risks related to travel, extortion and intimidation, among others.

Caseload

- The Somalia Food Security Cluster (SFSC) aims to be the primary source of information on the humanitarian response by addressing food insecurity, and by providing its members with a strategic vision and guidance in their response to the acute and underlying causes of crisis. As per the rationale of the Global Food Security Cluster, the FSC aims to promote responses that are: •Proportionate. The FSC will promote proportionate needs-based responses and advocate adequate resources and support. •Appropriate. The FSC will promote seasonal and livelihood specific responses. •Timely. In Somalia in particular, the FSC will aim to improve the timeliness of responses in order to provide a more effective and cost-efficient response.
