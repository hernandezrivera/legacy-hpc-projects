Project Summary

WASH infrastructure requires significant support and substantial investments. Reliability and efficiency of water systems have sharply decreased for the first time since 2016, with nearly 2 million fewer people using water networks as the only household water supply
modality, in comparison to mid-2020. This means up to 47% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet or complement their water needs (vis-à-vis 38% in mid-2020). Investment in sanitation infrastructure is critical, as at least
70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. In Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor, for example, pretty much all the untreated raw sewage is discharged into the Euphrates River, which also serves as the source of drinking
water to a vast majority of the population. In NWS, wastewater from huge camp clusters is openly discharged, forming sewage rivers. Similarly, in NES, no wastewater treatment exists for the IDP sites, resulting in environmental pollution and increased disease
prevalence. Over 30% of garbage is inappropriately disposed or collected with low frequency, while the dumpsites often pose serious health, environmental and protection risks.
Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a challenge for more than 2 million people in IDP sites, highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance. Up to 96%
of interviewed residents of IDP sites received some WASH humanitarian assistance, but 44% were not fully satisfied with it, mainly due to insufficient quantity or quality of assistance. This is potentially linked with shortage and/or discontinuity of funding. Water
trucking services are the only source of water for 73% of IDP site residents and only 24% households are connected to (simplified) sewer networks, indicating a continued need for medium-term investment in IDP sites infrastructure to provide more sustainable,
affordable, and safer way to supply water and dispose wastewater. Despite the sector assistance, 24% of IDPs in sites couldn’t access one or multiple hygiene items, 23% faced barriers to effective handwashing. Also, 35% of households reported issues with toilet
functionality or wastewater disposal. Disruptions and poor quality of services in IDP sites have also direct negative physical and mental well-being and protection consequences, notably on women and girls.
The 4 Ws indicators are consolidated with WASH sector. The project will also cover the monitoring of safe drinking water quality for 11 camps and 93 IDPs shelters with estimated targeted population of 600,531 individuals in NES GOV.
Currently, WHO has 250,000 USD committed towards WASH programming in 2023. WHO is also actively fundraising with donors and its leadership to support the total WASH needs of 10.2M USD.

3 - Needs

Critically bad sanitation conditions, poor hygiene practices and deterioration of water quality have a direct impact on increasing public health risks, including AWD/Cholera outbreak, COVID-19 and malnutrition, as well as an impact on the livelihood and protection of households. During the 2022 cholera outbreak response, a detailed WASH services assessment was carried out in Assad Hospital in Deir Ez-Zor and Aleppo University Hospital considering those two governorates were highly affected. The situation inside the hospitals was found to be catastrophic and high-risk for further spread of disease. Additionally, drinking water samples collected as part of WHO’s water quality monitoring activities in Sep – Dec 2022 showed high contamination with faecal coliform bacteria in Aleppo, Deir ez-Zor, Al-Hassakeh, Rural Damascus and Dar’a which leaves these areas at high risk for outbreaTo ensure the safety of drinking water for vulnerable populations, WHO will continue to conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water to regularly monitor chemical and biological contamination of different water sources. WHO will perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in primary healthcare and will enhance medical waste management.In addition, WHO will enhance medical waste management, which is key to preventing any nosocomial infections among healthcare workers and patients through capacity-building along with provision of healthcare waste management containers, vehicles, incinerator

4 - Implementation of activities

• Conduct surveillance of the quality of drinking water by regularly measuring PH, ATP, chlorine residual of water samples from various water sources (e.g. water networks, ground wells, reservoirs, water tanks, jerry cans) and culture to identify pathogens. These activities will cover all governorate with special focus in Rural Damascus, Aleppo (Rural), Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor, where the severity scope is over 3, by deploying WHO focal points to conduct testing in collaboration with Ministry of Local Administration and Environment and WASH partners.• Perform laboratory analysis of contaminated water samples • Support MoLAE in provision of laboratory equipment and supplies and sterilizing the contaminated water sources by distribution of chlorine tablets to households and Directorates of Health.• Perform light rehabilitation of WASH facilities in PHCC in Homs, Hama, Aleppo, rural Damascus and NES.• Enhance medical waste management through procurement of 5000 containers to be equipped in PHCC. • Monitoring Visits to the project sitesIn hard to reach, cross line areas, WHO is working closely with other UN agencies and partners to achieve access. The first XL mission to RAA in more than 2 and is working now with OCHA to organize assessment mission based on this WHO will shape its approach. Provision of RCCE materials and water purifiers to hospitals and communities is envisioned in coordination with WASH Sector.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WHO has established mechanisms to treat reports of SEA as a priority. Based on the gravity of proven cases of SEA involving WHO staff members, and in addition to any criminal verdict that may result from legal proceeding for cases that are referred to national authorities for criminal prosecution, or other measures taken against the staff member concerned, WHO will apply disciplinary measures pursuant to Section 11 of the Staff Rules, including as applicable summary dismissal. Disciplinary sanctions will also apply to staff members who are proven to have failed to report SEA or to have encouraged or condoned SEA to happen. Malicious reporting of SEA without evidence or reasonable suspicions with the intention of harming another person’s integrity or reputation amounts to misconduct and is subject to disciplinary action. This is distinct from reports of suspected wrongdoing made in good faith based on the judgment and information available at the time of the report, which may not be confirmed by an investigation
