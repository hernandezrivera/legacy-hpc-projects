Project Summary

Guji Zone experienced a significant increase in internal displacement since 2018 as a result of ethnic conflict, conflict between armed insurgency and Ethiopian Defense force. The humanitarian situation in Guji Zone continues to deteriorate due to the compounded effects of multiple crises, in particular on-going security operations by security forces against unidentified armed groups (UAGs) and the triple threat of drought, COVID-19, floods and desert locust. The nature of the conflict is continuously evolving particularly in Gumi Eldelo, Liben, Goro Dola and Seba Boru Woreda’s, prompting the confinement of entire communities and internally displaced persons. Reports indicate that there is continued displacement as a result of security operations. After months of displacement and limited humanitarian assistance, the population remains affected by insecurity, restricted movement, and lack of access to basic service and livelihood. Humanitarian space continues to shrink and compromise the safety of the population and aid partners operation. Since April 2021, the security operations further intensified in Gumi Eldelo, Liben, Gorodola and Seba Boru Woredas’ with the reported allegations of violence, arbitrary detentions, and other human rights violations against the population by all parties in the conflict. According to OCHA’s June 2020 report, 74,916 population displaced from their original homeland in four woreda’s (Gorodola, Gumi Eldelo, Liben and Seba Boru). Moreover, OCHA report shows that from January to April 2021, the displaced population reached 75,800 . In the same way, Guji zone DRMO report of May 2021 indicates that there are 97,580 internally displaced people (46,246 female and 51,334 male) in 20 IDP/Returnee and host communities of Liben, Gumi Eldelo, Goro Dola, Saba Boru districts. The above-mentioned report reveals that the incidence is going on and intensified. This project aims to provide integrated child protection (CP), gender-based violence (GBV) and psychosocial support services to conflict and drought affected communities, and displaced persons facing
protection risks in Liben, Goro Dola, Seba Boru, and Gumi Eldelo districts of Guji Zone, Oromia
The general objective of this project is to provide emergency, responsive protection support mechanisms to address the immediate needs of victims of conflicts, displaced persons and / or other affected populations.
The specific objectives of the project are to:
1. Improve access to child protection prevention of and response including standardized case management services for 1,000 UASC, CHH and other vulnerable children/women with additional protection concerns.
2. Improve access to risk mitigation, prevention and response interventions addressing gender-based violence (GBV)
3. Create access to psychosocial support services for Children, women, GBV survivors and other people with MHPSS concerns
Direct beneficiaries: Total 21,110 (Women 6474, Girls 6060, Boy 4650, Men 3926); (59.37% girls and women, 40.63% boys and men); IDP/returnee: 14,777 (70%) host community 6,333 (30%)
This project proposal focuses on integrated child protection (CP) and gender-based violence (GBV) and psychosocial support services to conflict and drought affected communities in Liben, Goro Dola, Seba Boru, and Gumi Eldelo districts of Guji Zone, Oromia. The affected population is 97,580 internally displaced people (46,246 female and 51,334 male) in 20 IDP/Returnee and host communities. From the total affected population, the project targeted 21,110 (Women 6474, Girls 6060, Boy 4650, Men 3926) IDP/Returnee and host community. The target expected results, for each of the project objectives, are provided in the log-frame annexed in excel spreadsheet.
