Project Summary

The project aims to support a self-sustainable protective environment for the community in northern Syria through the provision of a wide range of protection activities for host community members and IDPs in, Idleb governorate and Aleppo governorate.
The entire community will have access to community-based protection services, including awareness raising over general protection concerns, through awareness raising campaigns on different topics such as; child protection, housing land and property (HLP) issues and civil documentation. Also, case management services and psychosocial support (PSS) activities will be provided in the community center, outside of the camps. In addition, community protection networks will be established and supported by the protection team.
The project will target (27.500) girls, (27.500) boys, (16.500) adult females, (16.500) adult males, (3000) elderly female, (2000) elderly male. Including (500) persons with disabilities.
A mobile outreach team will serve people through awareness raising on child protection concerns, gender-based violence (GBV), and the importance of civil documentation for IDPs and hosts. In addition, the outreach team will do basic case management to identify, refer, follow-up cases to appropriate services, will conduct PSS activities and establish community protection networks.
The project will provide technical and vocational training which allows the youth to be able to find a job easier.

Needs

Protection issues and needs in Syria remain prevalent and continue to affect all population groups. The protracted nature of the crisis has led to the emergence of increasingly complex and inter-connected protection issues and needs, in a continuously evolving environment. While the overall protection situation remains dire, a diversity of situations emerged. In some areas of Syria, in particular the North West and North East, the civilian population is still exposed to hostilities, leading to civilian casualties and forced displacements in seek of safety. The prolonged and widespread use of a range of weaponry has left behind a range of explosive hazards, each posing their own risks. Different mitigation strategies are needed to reduce their impact on the civilian population and humanitarians delivering aid and relief to those in need. In a number of locations across the country, IDPs still live in sites or collective accommodations, sometimes subjected to security screening procedures and deprived from their freedom of movement, hindered in accessing services, livelihood opportunities and dignified life conditions. Numerous situations of protracted displacement are reported, including in areas enjoying relative stability, with families living in sub-standard conditions and struggling to find resilience mechanisms. As a result, resorting to harmful coping mechanisms (including child labor, child recruitment, different forms of exploitation and child/early marriage) is a persisting trend. Finally, the current and constantly increasing scale of IDP returns, adds an additional layer of needs that should be taken into account, not least in the fields of civil documentation and housing, land and property (HLP). Beyond residual situations of direct exposure to hostilities and forced displacement, the protection environment in Syria should also be understood in relation to the impact of eight consecutive years of crisis. In addition to physical destruction, the protracted displacement has contributed to a steady erosion of coping mechanisms of individuals and communities, generated high levels of psychological distress, disrupted services available to support the most vulnerable population, and has put a major strain on in the everyday lives of communities and on community support structures. Despite a continued increase of protection interventions in Syria over the years, the scale and complexity of protection needs remains a major challenge to the sector, in a context where access and operational constraints are still present. Capacities of protection actors remain insufficient in certain domains and locations. According to the findings of the available assessments, the protection environment in Syria remains characterized by the occurrence of several protection issues selected for the analysis, with varying frequency of occurrence across locations and population groups, as well as some constant patterns. in 48% of assessed communities the occurrence of at least four protection issues was reported by the consulted population. In parallel, qualitative protection sources are a crucial source of information to indicate how different protection issues are inter-linked and should therefore be addressed in a holistic manner. Civil documentation, housing, land and property issues and freedom of movement remain major and countrywide protection issues, reported by respondents as occurring in 59%; 47% and 46% of assessed communities respectively . According to qualitative sources, the incidence of these protection issues may be even higher . The occurrence of these issues derives directly from the effects of hostilities and forced displacement, which remain the main drivers of the protection crisis in Syria.

Implementation of activities

Through our community centers, the communities as a whole will be able to access community-based protection services. These include awareness raising about general protection concerns; case management for follow-up on vulnerable cases; supported by staff psychologists for mental health referrals; psychosocial support; and finally, case management service will be provided by our community center. Children of One World will establish new (five) community centers in (five) different locations (communities). Psychosocial support activities conducted within the center follow a structured model using “War Child’s IDEAL curriculum and the Red Crescent Palestinian curriculum (primarily for children)” for one month and vocational training as PSS activity for youth and adults will be conducted inside the ten centers. PSS activities conducted outside the center in both locations are generally recreational activities directed towards children. Awareness raising is conducted outside and inside the center; when inside the center, they are conducted by the legal counselor or case management team. Awareness raising is conducted on a variety of topics including child protection, gender-based violence, family separation, mine risk education and civil documentation. We will provide vocational training for men, women, boys, and girls in the community centers. The training aims to prevent and respond to protection risks including child recruitment and child labor, which are exacerbated by the high poverty rate and number of child-headed households. Vocational training will reduce the resort to negative coping mechanisms by providing specialized skills related to income generation. In addition, the training will include psychosocial support activities to raise behavioral awareness. It will educate young people about risks and safety in specific work environments, allowing them to avoid risks in the workplace. Areas of training will be determined after consultation in focus group discussions with key stakeholders and may include, for men and boys, business, blacksmithing, plumbing, electrical work, haircutting, mobile phone maintenance, auto repair, and agriculture. According to community feedback, women and girls may be trained in the following areas: business, nursing, sewing, hairdressing, secretarial skills, computer skills and language skills. COOW will use a variety of tools to monitor the effectiveness of our proposed interventions, In accordance with COOW's MEAL policy and procedures and IASC AAP standards, including surveys, observation, document review, Structured interviews and FGDs. COOW Complaint Mechanism will be applied in the ten proposed locations.
