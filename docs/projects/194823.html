Project Summary

Through this project, Dorcas and its partners will be providing food vouchers, food parcels and healthy meals to vulnerable population in Aleppo, and Idleb.
The food vouchers will be provided to 400 HH in 2023 from Aleppo and these families will get vouchers for 3 months, in addition Dorcas through its partner MSJM will provide 1,000 household with food parcels in 2023.
Moreover, Dorcas will continue to provide daily healthy meals to more than 5,050 students attending private schools in Aleppo and activities at the 2 community centers run by the partner in close collaboration with Dorcas team.
Dorcas is currently negotiating an agreement with one donor related to livelihood support for agriculture and livestock. The project will help to re-establish both urban and rural livelihoods for conflict-affected households in Aleppo to revitalize local markets devastated by the conflict. Also, iI will provide awareness sessions for the farmers and livestock owners on protection topics such as awareness on the gendered discriminatory practices, laws or cultural norms, etc.

3 - Needs

The project is a continuation of the previous year project, Syria continues to face complex socio-economic challenges. According to the 2023 HNO, 12.1M people are estimated to be food insecure. The WFP remote food security monitoring and recent food security and livelihood assessment in Syria (FSA, FSLA) estimates that 7 in 10 Syrian families is food insecure with the highest concentration in Idleb (74%), Quneitra and Al-Raqqa (68%), Aleppo (61%). All population groups have all been affected by the complex and deteriorating socioeconomic challenges that continue to negatively impact the whole country. The war in Ukraine had an immediate and direct impact on the worldwide supply of grain and vegetable oil. Syria is especially vulnerable to changing global dynamics since it is a net food importer. In September, the national average price of the WFP's standard reference food basket set new highs since tracking began in 2013. It has climbed by 52% since the beginning of the Ukrainian crisis, & by 91% since the same time last year, reaching SYP 357,593. As a result, the average price of a food basket soared by 87%. The project will provide food vouchers, food baskets & healthy meals to more than 2,200 HH and 5,050 students enrolled in schools. Dorcas will provide agriculture and livestock support as well for vulnerable to meet immediate humanitarian needs of vulnerable populations and contribute to sustainable livelihoods recovery.

4 - Implementation of activities

Dorcas will also continue to provide food assistance through food vouchers in Aleppo and food baskets in Idleb depending on the area to be covered since the needs for food assistance remains high.In Aleppo city where the local markets are functional 1,233 families will be provided with 3 times vouchers to purchase food items, the preference for food vouchers is noticed due to difficulty in providing cash assistance because of the current limitations in the country mainly in cash withdrawal but also needs for approvals needed from authorities. This modality is chosen in response of the wish from the beneficiaries to choose the food items according to their preference and needs. In Aleppo city the food vouchers will be distributed through 6 different centers which are run by the local partners of Dorcas (3 times). These FBOs select the most vulnerable people from the community (according to pre set selection criteria) and are able stay in contact and follow up with the beneficiaries on their needs. In Idleb, Dorcas will support food insecure families by access to food. In 2023, the intervention will cover 1,000 food baskets for families that are mainly female headed and has no bread winner or source of income. In addition, Dorcas will continue to provide daily healthy meals to more than 5,050 students enrolled in schools. Dorcas will provide support for agriculture livelihood through direct provision of inputs to farmers and livestock owners (inputs, seeds, incubator...)

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project will continue the work that Dorcas has been doing through its community centers. Dorcas’ community centers/partners are well-known & trusted; individuals and families understand that Dorcas has the ability to provide a multitude of protection services, as well as many humanitarian services like hygiene items and food parcels. Working in conversation with the community, Dorcas develops many proposals and activities based on information received from its feedback systems and the needs of the community expressed by those coming into the center. Dorcas’ two-way beneficiary feedback system was established in the community centers to ensure that beneficiary populations have a voice in the planning, activity implementation, and evaluation of the project. Feedback and suggestions are listened to as part of the protection risk monitoring and subsequently included in the implementation. This also includes a process to confidentially handle allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in an effort to ensure that beneficiaries are protected against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Dorcas ensures beneficiaries have multiple ways to provide feedback, including a hotline, text messages, emails, & box established within the centers. Dorcas’ long term presence within the community also ensures that the most vulnerable, such as the elderly, children, PWD, and widows are reached through its services. Dorcas also has been supporting its partners to implement PSEA strategy.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Beneficiaries and staff are at risk of covid-19 or cholera when implementing the activities. Dorcas/partners taking measures to prevent risks through provision of PPE materials to staff and beneficiaries but also awareness campaign, posters, pamphlets...Inflation is also a main risk. Dorcas will be reviewing the amount of the voucher after getting the donor'sapproval, the amount can change in order to be in line with the recommendations of the working group, this also happened in one of Dorcas project in 2020 when the amount was reviewed and adjusted. Assumptions: Enabled access to targeted areas is maintained with acceptable security conditions during the intervention. The political and economic situation of the country remains at a level allowing effective humanitarian action. The Covid-19 pandemic remains contained where the implementation will take place. Risks: Restrictions imposed by the Government of Syria on humanitarian interventions, including humanitarian access. Civil unrest in project locations prevents full geographic coverage as anticipated. Confirmed increasing number of cases and/or potential outbreak of Covid-19 in project locations. Significant in-flux of conflict affected populations into project locations leads to over stretch of project delivery. Mitigation: Dorcas/the partners will closely monitor safety & security concerns in targeted areas and act upon its Risk Mitigation Plan. Dorcas/partners will ensure strong participation in field coordination
