Project Summary

NRC’s shelter project objective is to adopt a participatory-based approach to improve the living conditions, dignity, and physical protection of the most vulnerable people affected by conflict in targeted areas of Northeast of Nigeria. In response to the protracted nature of the crisis, NRC will increasingly work in an integrated manner, bringing in Shelter and any other relevant competencies to collaborate on providing more durable and holistic solutions to displacement and return. Considering the complexities of displacement and return in both urban and rural areas, NRC will adopt a flexible approach to target specifc needs with adaptable shelter options and incorporate safe programming principles in order to mitigate and respond to any potential adverse effects, which might result from the proposed shelter solution. The following include the overall area for the focus of the project: 1. Emergency Response: Responding to the urgent shelter/NFI needs of IDP and returnee population occurring due to unforeseen reasons such as security incidents, communal clashes, natural disasters (flooding, fire, etc) or premature return or resettlement; 2. Protracted Displacement Response: Improving the shelter conditions in camps and camp-like sites by decongestion of camps and improving the emergency/transitional shelters habitability conditions; 3. Return Context Response: Contribution and facilitating safe and dignified return of IDPs by provision of adequate shelter conditions and exploration of diverse shelter modalities in host communities and areas of return. Based on its mandate, NRC will target the people directly impacted by the conflict, especially the most vulnerable IDPs, returnees, and host-communities living in inadequate dwellings. Based on humanitarian analysis and internal assessment results, NRC in collaboration with the shelter sector will select areas of intervention using defined specific selection criteria, which will consider physical access and security in addition to the presence of IDPs in the area, and the areas offering opportunity for safe and voluntary return. NRC will target and select beneficiaries using a community-based approach, to promote the commitment of leaders and involvement of the host community and target groups.

1 - Needs

The deteriorating economic and security context is further complicated by resettlement and returns of IDPs proposed by the Borno State Government. A premature move towards resettlement could see vulnerable individuals’ safety placed at risk and gaps in government and humanitarian actor service provision. Nevertheless, the protracted nature of the crisis does require a movement towards early recovery and durable solutions, in line with the humanitarian-development-peace nexus approach. In addition, the Borno State Government’s ambitious plan for the massive resettlement and return of 50% IDPs and returnees in 2021 has not been coordinated with humanitarian actors and insufficiently focused on durable solutions. Many IDPs, returnees and host communities continue living in inadequate shelter conditions such as makeshift shelters, sharing accommodations with relatives and friends, living in damaged buildings or crowded collective centers. The living conditions in the IDPs sites are also very poor, with half of the camps in Borno state being overcrowded. Land allocation for new IDP camps or the expansion of existing ones remain extremely low. The informal and spontaneously settled camps are also severely congested and underserved due to lack of space and resources as well as complications relating to private land ownership. This level of congestion continues to exacerbate risks and impact of fires. Overcrowding is also a particular protection concern for women and girls, and negatively affects the elderly and people with disabilities. Such ongoing issues, in addition to seasonal threats of rain and wind, make the construction of new shelters and repair/rehabilitation of existing shelter stock an ongoing need. The need for adequate shelter and living conditions stay urgent with many IDPs resorting to negative coping mechanism such as using makeshift shelters, sharing accommodations with relatives and friends, living in partially damaged shelters, in public buildings and collective centers. Similarly, the shelter and NFI needs among returnees are also urgent. NRC assessment conducted between March and June 2021 in Borno state, shows that almost one-third of all returnees are currently living in either damaged or self-made shelters. Returnees living in return areas with a high number of returnees and living in partially burnt houses or in self-made/makeshift shelters are the most vulnerable. An NRC report in 2017, "Not Ready to Return" indicated that even though an overall 45% of IDP households believed to be able to re-inhabit their homes in their villages of origin, only 27% reported that their previous homes were undamaged. This indicates that appropriate shelter conditions upon return will depend on repairs and reconstruction.

2 - Activities

Emergency Shelter Kits (ESKs): NRC will distribute Emergency Shelter Kits to support beneficiaries in building basic emergency shelters using traditional construction techniques. The ESKs will be provided as necessary as identified by the RRM assessment.It is a flexible approach, fast, simple to implement, and cost effective. It is used to construct temporary shelters or to repair or upgrade existing shelters in order to address the needs of newly arrived IDPs or affected people living in dilapidated tents or partially covered spaces and makeshift shelters either in transit centers or in IDP sites. TemporaryShelter Construction (BAMA Shelters): NRC shelter construction preference for 2021 is transitional shelter (Dikwa shelters). However NRC will still build a number of Bama shelters in 2021 as contribution to sector’s decongestion plans and prototyping of sectors’ staged shelter concept and in emergency context where construction of shelters are necessary. Transitional Shelter Construction (Dikwa Shleters): In new displacement areas where land is accessible and tenure secured enough and also where IDPs live in makeshift shelters or their existing shelters have been destroyed (more than 70% damaged) NRC will provide complete transitional shelter solutions. The design and composition of Dikwa Transitional Shelter model is in line with sphere standard and approved by Shelter sector and provides a higher level of privacy and protection against elements and other hazards.Temporary/Transitional Shelter Repairs: For those protracted displacement areas, where communities have lived in temporary or transitional shelters for several years, without an opportunity or permission to build more permanent structures, NRC will provide repair kits. For repair, in-kind or voucher modalities might be used to allow more flexibility to shelter owners to prioritize some of the upgrading components. Otherwise, NRC will provide in-kind building materials and pay for labour fees to support the construction of the repair process. Permanent Shelter Rehabilitation: For returnees living either in damaged/abandoned building, their own property or a rented house, NRC will provide shelter rehabilitation assistance. In the cases where returnees are paying rent, Legal agreement between Landlord, NRC and the HH will be developed by ICLA prior to the intervention. Free occupation or rent freeze is negotiated for a period of 6 months or 1year. The rehabilitation includes doors and windows installation, roof and wall repairs.The activity will be coordinated with ICLA/HLP team to maximize programming effectiveness. Permanent Shelter Construction: NRC shelter team will design and develop a complete permanent shelter solution (mud shelters) in 2021 in response to the needs of return context and also government shift in use of more durable construction materials even in displacement context. The design and composition of the permanent sheltersolution will be done in consultation with community’s representative and in line withSphere standards with specific attention to local standards, in order to maintain cultural harmony and easy maintenance. Shelter design will look at maximizing local knowledgeand construction techniques for producing and using mud blocks. Design and prototypeof the permanent shelter solution will be presented to the community, local authority representatives and shelter sector partners for technical feedback and approval. ImprovedNFI kit Distribution: NRC will ensure that all targeted shelter beneficiaries in all three above categories, would also receive adequate household items during the shelter handover process. When applied, NFI kits will be distributed to all targeted shelter beneficiaries through direct distribution and/or through an electronic-voucher system according to market capacity. NRC will ensure that NFI kits follow Shelter/NFI/CCCM guidelines and are adapted to the cultural context.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In all the shelter activities, NRC will look into mainstreaming gender and protection principles. This would be achieved through different mechanisms and approaches.NRC will ensure the participation of all beneficiaries in the decision-making process by establishing a committee at the outside of any shelter activity in every community. The committee is comprised of various members of community, representative of both genders and different age groups. In addition, it will be ensured that minority groups and populations with disabilities are also represented. Specific sensitization conducted by NRC staff and community mobilizers will facilitate social mobilization and strengthen the capacities of communities to participate and take ownership of a ctivities. The committee will also assist NRC team in identifying vulnerable households and assess the capacity level of the community. NRC Shelter’s ultimate goal is that beneficiaries become self-sufficient and equipped to repair community infrastructure, their homes, or pursue livelihood activities. On-the-job and workshop trainings provided will allow for improvement of capacity and skills development within a short period-of-time. NRC construction activities, along with IGAs developed in the form of cash-for-work, will be provided to youth as a way to reduce their exposure of being recruited into armed conflict for militant activities due to lack of economic opportunities. NRC will also attempt to include whenever possible vulnerable female headed household in need of livelihood opportunities in its construction-related capacity building training and cash-for-work opportunities. In addition, NRC shelter team will ensure to seek the feedback of various community members in form of focus group discussions in the process of design and improvement of shelter modalities. Specific attention needs to be made to ensure the needs of such highly vulnerable population are responded to by following gender and protection mainstreaming principles. This could include provision of internal partitions with lockable doors and windows in shelters whenever possible. In cases where emergency shelters kits are distributed, technical and labor assistance should be provided to highly vulnerable population who might not have the physical capacity or social network to construct their shelter themselves. Paying special attention to providing transportation, labor and technical assistance to highly vulnerable households such as female-headed households, child-headed households, elderly and people with disability. Inclusion of female beneficiaries in construction-related capacity building-activities. Focus group discussion with different age and gender groups in order to mainstream gender and protection principles in emergency and transitional shelters design and improvements. Providing internal partition in emergency and transitional shelters and locks for all doors and windows. Alongside all shelter and settlement activities, awareness-raising and trainings will be provided to maximize the project output and strengthen this intervention. Specifc training and awareness sensitization will focus on habitability measures and environment to promote healthier living surroundings. Following the training, communities will easily integrate proper designated places for animals and people, respectively and will ensure safe healthy kitchen spaces is available.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

NRC is committed to setting up systematic and effective mechanisms to collect beneficiary and beneficiary feedback, and to provide on-going feedback to NRC during its projects. NRC takes guidance from the Core Humanitarian Standard definition of Accountability and understands Accountability to Affected Populations asa process of using power responsibly, taking account of, and being held accountable by the people we seek to assist. As with other NRC projects, a Complaint and FeedbackResponse mechanism (CFRM) integrated in this project will ensure that beneficiaries and communities have the opportunity to register feedback and complaints. This mechanism includes the provision of a complaints/suggestion boxes at project sites, and dedicated complaint and feedback hotline, email, and face to face sessions with staff. NRC uses this feedback to resolve any issues that may arise, and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. This CFRM will be managed by a dedicated team, and report directly to senior management to provide additional accountability towards targeted communities and ensure that complaints are discussed on a senior level within the organization where appropriate. Furthermore, populations served by NRC staff through this project will be informed on NRC’s Code of Conduct and how to report any breaches of conduct. Post distribution monitoring will also gain feedback from the target population
