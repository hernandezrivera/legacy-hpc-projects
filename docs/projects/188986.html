Project Summary

The main goals of the project are: to increase equitable and dignified access to quality malnutrition-specific curative services for children and women, while enhancing integrated sustainable preventive measures to halt prevalence of acute malnutrition among children and women in Ikotos Count.
The specific objectives will be; To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific service delivery for children, adolescents and women in Ikotos county by the end of 2022; To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in Ikotos county by the end of 2022; To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2022 and to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups in prior.
Nutrition service delivery will prioritize provision of comprehensive and timely community management of acute malnutrition at both Nutrition centres at Boma levels and in hard to reach locations without static centres to access service. The project will as well emphasize on promoting breast feeding and optimal feeding practices among children and women through scaling up of MIYCN counselling services. Nutrition for farmers will focus on raising awareness on the importance of producing and consuming specific food items: households will be sensitized and trained on the use of new vegetables and crops that will be selected together with the communities in a participatory process; focus groups discussions will be held with the farmer groups in order to discuss the importance of diversifying the cultivation for nutrition purposes. With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID-19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines, and approaches such as family MUAC shall be adhered and implemented in 2022 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services in the context of COVID -19. Given the multi causative contributing factors to malnutrition, the project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programming with other relevant sectors such as WASH, health, protection and food security through effectives coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to multi-sectoral basic service which will further reduce prevalence of acute malnutrition and eventually reduce morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable groups (children and women).
Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that programming of nutrition services approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation to ensure that the intervention is responsive enough to the arrays of the changing needs. Consideration gender and age variation of the vulnerable groups will be prioritized as well for efficiency of the response more so safety audit will be conducted at every nutrition center to ensure.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. More people are likely to experience severe food insecurity in 2022. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May-July). In the Greater Equatoria Region, it’s estimated that 1.41 million people (40% of the region’s population) will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity during the period of April to July 2021 this includes Ikotos county.An estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment in 2022 the highest number in the past four years.The key drivers of acute malnutrition are multifaceted, including severe acute food insecurity, sub-optimal feeding practices, high prevalence of disease, inadequate sanitation conditions and hygiene practices, and limited access to health and nutrition services driven by sub-national violence, floods, and COVID-19 related disruptions.There is no current county base data for late 2021/2022 since the FSNMS R27 is still on process. However, from the FSNMS R25 and 26 prediction, the situation continued to worsen in 2021 indicated GAM rate for Ikotos county at 16.77% which above the WHO classification threshold. The major contributing to acute malnutrition in Ikotos includes high prevalence of communicable diseases (up to 36%), worsening of food security situation thus poor quality and diversity of food (Minimum Acceptable Diet: 7%, Minimum Dietary Diversity: 15%) and poor food consumption score (48%). COVID-19 related disruptions, and restrictions also compounded to existing food insecurity Covid-19 pandemic as well interrupted smooth nutrition program implementation, e.g., changes in SAM and MAM referral protocols for children have exacerbated lack of access to services.

2 - Project activities

The main project activities will be:1 - Capacity building: recruitment of nutrition workers at nutrition centers and community nutrition volunteers, training of health and nutrition workers/community volunteers on growth monitoring; Integrated Malnutrition prevention package and integrated package on prevention of malnutrition and training of health and nutrition workers/community volunteers on maternal infant and young child counselling.2 - Curative services: routine static and mass community screening of children U5 and PLWs screening and referrals, Treatment of both moderate and severe acute malnutrition at nutrition sites and outreach locations in hard-to-reach areas without health facilities3 - Nutrition education and awareness: conduct routine MIYCN counselling to mothers/caretaker of children under five at both Static and in the community; conduct routine Integrated socio-behavioral change communication campaigns on nutrition, hygiene and health promotion including global breast-feeding week campaign; conduct cooking demonstration to mothers and caretaker of children under five on nutritious dense meal at both nutrition sites and in the community. 4 - Micronutrient supplementation: facilitate Vitamin A and deworming campaigns in the county, promotion locally produced fruit trees and diversified micronutrients rich food crop e.g., orange sweet potatoes.5 - Integrated nutrition sensitive activities: malaria testing for children admitted in OTP; provision of LLITN to children admitted in OTP program; distribution of soaps and WASH kits to children admitted at OTP; distribution of hand washing facilities at all OTP sites; establish kitchen gardens at all the nutrition centers and in the community to promote household’s production of diversified source of diets.6 - Provision of safety net: provision of food assistant to mothers/caregiver of children admitted in SC, and linkages and referral of mothers to children in OTP/TSFP to GFD. 7 - Protection mainstreaming: Conduct GBV safety audits at all nutrition sites; training of health and nutrition staffs on GBV/protection; training of health and nutrition staffs on ECD; distribution of ECD kits to all Nutrition sites.8 - Monitoring: provision of monitoring/evaluation tools and national guidelines to all nutrition sites and to community nutrition volunteers; conduct routine on job mentorship and supervision of nutrition workers at nutrition sites by qualified nutrition officers; conduct performance scoring for all nutrition sites on quarterly basis; facilitate quarterly joint (SMoH, CHD, UNICEF, WFP and AVSI) support supervision in all the nutrition sites; facilitation of carry our periodic beneficiary’s and satisfactory assessment in all nutrition sites; conduct quarterly programmatic visits and monitoring of nutrition sites; establishment of feedback/complaints mechanisms at all nutrition sites in form suggestion boxes and AAP committees as a means of community engagement; timely and regular nutrition information reports submission from all the nutrition sites into cluster nutrition dashboard; actively support and facilitate national surveys (FSNMS).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AVSI has in place a tested system of yearly multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 400 questions (including question on FSL, nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a large number of interviews (more than 400 HHs) capable of providing detailed reference at the subcounty (payam) level, an analysis centred on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge is interiorized by AVSI staff and becomes the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. In the last 12 months AVSI has produced 4 of such surveys which, along with other research aimed at monitoring the results of AVSI’s projects, are accessible on the “Assessment” section of the Humanitarian Response’s website. The questionnaire used for the Multi-Sector Survey can be accessed at this webpage https://ee.humanitarianresponse.info/x/#Yztl. Beside these surveys, AVSI has a tested system of detailed post distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community level. During each field visit, AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concerning protection, AVSI Foundation will work collaboratively with other actors (UN agencies, NGOS, FBOs and CBOs) involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities. AVSI will be accountable to the beneficiaries by enabling them to participate in the project at the planning, implementation, and evaluation phases. Moreover, appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up. On regularly basis, AVSI conducts community engagement meetings such as awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows open participation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted. AVSI embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analysed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention and beneficiaries barriers to access services. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AVSI Foundation will place protection and gender issues at the centre of the intervention by engaging directly with the IDPs, returnees and host communities’ children and CWDs so that their needs, priorities, rights are addressed within the scope of the project. AVSI Foundation will always create conducive and interactive environments that encourage them to express their needs, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rights holders in the project, AVSI Foundation will ensure that the duty bearers(Government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effective role to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Activities will be developed with the active participation of women and parents in all stages of the implementation process, to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken into consideration and that WaSH infrastructures meet the right gender standards (IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and aiding recovery). Moreover, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and community). On the other hand, safety audits are conducted, and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity will be strengthened through specific PSEA trainings.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The capillary and continuous presence that AVSI has in the communities targeted by the intervention (AVSI has more than 80% of its staff, including senior positions outside Juba), and the extensive use of staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities, ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the implementation of the project. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnees’ children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. Thanks to a very localized approach with AVSI staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Furthermore, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. Yearly, AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma and sub Boma Chiefs). During these events, AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. AVSI extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where AVSIs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/ intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, AVSI’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: Nutrition sites will be established equitably in all the boma at PHCU which is easily accessible by the local communities, and in boma without static nutrition services the project will ensure vulnerable groups are reached through mobile services; Reducing waiting time of beneficiaries at nutrition service point by adhering to the effective flow in service provision as per the guideline and ensure every nutrition sites has got adequate staffing (nutrition workers) attending promptly to beneficiaries. In areas where case of violence is high ration provided to beneficiaries will be adequate enough to reduce on frequency of follow up visits at nutrition sites. Engagement of local and traditional leaders during mobilization to provide security in their area for their people accessing services and protection of service access points; Women are always more exposed than men, therefore service provider will make sure women are encourage to be accompanied by their husbands while accessing services. • Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.): the project will ensure initial involvement of women and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and in planning activities implementation; Nutrition facilities will act as a valuable entry point for disseminating information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women and girls about their needs/safety concerns; the project will ensure CNVs are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathway. With support and coordination with GBV specialists from available local actors, household nutrition screenings will be used as a modality to proactively disseminate information on available GBV services. Health and nutrition workers/volunteers will be equipped with pocket cards containing relevant contact information; Representations of the GBV referral pathways will be posted in all nutrition sites.• Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: AVSI managed to install suggestion boxes in all, the nutrition sites however very few feedbacks were received majorly because majority of beneficiaries are illiterate (not able to write). Beneficiaries access to information in form communicating complaints and receiving feedback will be enhance through formation of AAP committees at Boma level with representatives from each village who will be in touch with beneficiaries to receive compliant and feedback. AVSI will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time (Monthly); On quarterly basis there will be a help desks at Nutrition sites attended by one personnel from AVSI at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries.• Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: AVSI will ensure Local stakeholders are oriented on the program in term of its objective/purpose, targeted beneficiaries and justification of selection criteria such as those with acute malnutrition (children and PLW); Involvement of Local stakeholders and community organs/committees or groups and tradition leaders in selection procedures e.g. vulnerability assessment; Visual presentation of targeted beneficiaries and their entitlement at distribution points/service access points in form of charts, poster; Engagement of community committee/groups or local leaders to participate, supervise and monitor distribution; Distribution points should be centrally and equitably allocated

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

AVSI’s Foundation shall ensure that the assistance during the project does not harm the safety, dignity and integrity of women, men, girls and boys receiving it, and is provided in a way that respect their rights. Protection sensitive services will as well be mainstream in the project, in fact the activities will include: facilitation of child stimulation and ECD in all nutrition sites, GBV/PSEA awareness to workers and beneficiaries, referral of GBV cases to access service at the management sites. Likewise, AVSI will be making sure that trainings will target at least an equal number of female and male, that the monitoring will include sex and age disaggregated data, that the project personnel is trained on gender/protection topics including GBV identification, handling and referral. Equal participation will be ensured through the involvement of women and men, equally in decision making right up from induction meetings, and routine focus groups discussions. For instance, Inclusive selection process with set % for specific population components typically victims of inequalities (min 60% of females in project implementation, inclusion of elderly and people with special needs, and returnees). AVSI’s projects include activities of livelihood and capacity building especially tailored to women, individually and as women group, and is matched by educational projects that always include GBV and peace building sensitization for pupils and training on GBV and referral pathways for teachers and PTA/SMCs members. Finally, AVSI operates through numerous permanent bases distributed across the targeted communities and serves an adequate number of service sites equally distributed though out the counties to avoid crowding of beneficiaries per sites, reducing distance to access services, and thus diminish the gap for remote, inaccessible communities. Finally, AVSI will have a gender & protection specialist, a gender officer and a protection officer dedicated to the analysis, supervision and adjustment of activities from the gender perspective.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project will contribute to the collective outcome 3 acting on different levels. The emergency activities will focus on the most vulnerable, among which flood related IDPs represent a priority group; these activities, such as emergency distribution of agricultural inputs and basic trainings, will help IDPs in finding relief from the emergency situation while building the basis for a new system of self-reliance. On the other hand, AVSI will work with Host Communities to strengthen the support network that take care of those displaced ensuring they have all the support needed. AVSI intervention aims at building the collaboration between host community and displaced people as a synergic tool to ensure the growth of the whole community and the reduction of internal conflicts over the use of natural resources.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Both in Lakes and Eastern & Central Equatoria State, AVSI will focus its intervention in the field of food security and livelihoods with the main aim of reducing the proportion of population facing famine, emergency or crisis situations. All AVSI activities will be oriented to delivering basic agricultural assistance to the most vulnerable while building the capacity of the communities towards an ever-growing resilience and independence from humanitarian aid. To improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding, the project will integrate nutrition to food security component, establishing sustainable solutions as demonstration kitchen gardens at household level, as well as at nutrition sites, schools, institutions (as County, Payam and Boma-based offices). AVSI will continue engaging in cereals largescale production through progressive farmers’ groups and enhancing the mechanization of cereal production in the targeted areas with common/group farms of above 20 feddan of dimension and supporting in production management and post-harvest methods. AVSI will also facilitate cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers, and caregivers on how to prepare nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be link to community general food assistant to improve on household food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance.
