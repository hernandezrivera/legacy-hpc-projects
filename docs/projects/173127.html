Project Summary

REACH, created in 2010 as a joint initiative of ACTED, its sister organisation IMPACT Initiatives (IMPACT) and UNOSAT, has been providing assessment and information management services to humanitarian actors in Libya since 2011, establishing a full-time presence in 2015. In light of the dynamic context in Libya, it is necessary to maintain the flow of updated evidence in order to develop efficient and adequate programming to address the needs and vulnerabilities of affected populations. Critical information on the humanitarian situation is provided through:
Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA): The basic methodology is expected to stay consistent in 2021 as previous years to ensure the comparability of data. REACH will conduct quantitative data collection followed by a qualitative data collection exercise expecting to target IDPs, returnees and non-displaced. REACH will consult and coordinate with the sectors (IMWG, ISCG and HCT) around the details of the methodology, including geographic coverage and the choice of indicators. Once the survey data has been collected and analysed, presentations of preliminary results will be given to the sectors. Subsequent presentations including more advanced analysis will then be organised in time to feed into the 2022 HNO/HRP process. Written outputs will include a final report, plus a set of factsheets for each sector (6 in total).
Rapid Assessment output: Conducted at the onset of humanitarian emergencies in Libya, to inform effective, rapid humanitarian action, directly benefitting the humanitarian community and ultimately vulnerable communities in need of assistance. REACH will activate its network of Key Informants to collect information and release findings in the form of a report and accompanying maps on an accelerated timeline after sudden crisis. These findings will provide humanitarian rapid responders with a timely and up-to-date situation overview, as well as information on priority needs, to allow implementation of an informed response.
Two Protection Assessments: Conducted to fill an urgent information gap pertaining to the main needs of returnees and IDPs as well as inform programming aimed at strengthening the protective environment for refugees living in urban settings in Libya. This will include assessing infrastructural and housing needs; looking at access to services, markets, and livelihood opportunities; and examining prevalent safety and security concerns. Alongside this, it will also consider local conflict dynamics in each assessed location, to create an evidence base that can help support social cohesion and reintegration. It will also assess potential protection risks migrants face as well as their level of integration and the acceptance of the host community.
Joint Market Monitoring Initiative (JMMI): through monthly monitoring and analysis of prices and market systems, the JMMI improves the understanding among humanitarian cash actors if markets in Libya are functioning and reacting to various crises. It also supports and facilitates the planning, implementation, and evaluation of cash-based interventions (CBI) by providing accurate information on the prices and availability of key food and NFIs.
Sectoral Support: technical support and capacity building will be provided to the humanitarian sectors as they plan, design and implement their own assessments in 2021. This will help ensure that the assessments produce high-quality, relevant and actionable outputs, as well as to strengthen the sectors’ long-term capacity to collect, analyze and share data. The goal will be to assist the humanitarian sector in producing a more diverse pool of up-to-date data and analysis to draw upon when planning and implementing activities in Libya. This technical support is not meant to replace the sectors’ role in conducting these assessments, but rather to enhance it by providing sectors with resources that help them achieve their assessment objectives.

1 - Needs

Crucial humanitarian information gaps remain in Libya: the political, economic and social landscapes are constantly evolving. To ensure that programming quickly responds to the rapidly changing needs and environment on the ground, it is thus crucial that timely and up-to-date information is maintained and reinforced. REACH proposes to conduct assessments (MSNA, rapid assessments, protection assessments, JMMI and support to sector-led assessments) for an informed and coordinated programming of the humanitarian response. Data available on the humanitarian situation in Libya beyond the REACH MSNA often relies on key informant rather than House Hold (HH)-level surveys, and frequently does not have the comprehensive geographic and thematic coverage necessary for making decisions about the allocation of scarce resources. The MSNA intends to complement these and other sources of information such as the IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM). The proposed 2021 MSNA intends to provide an overall understanding of HH vulnerabilities, their most pressing needs and their severity, both within each sector and from a cross-sectoral perspective. Furthermore, in the Libyan context, there have been periodic outbreaks of armed conflict which have spurred population movement, a pattern which is anticipated to continue into 2021 due to 2019-2020 escalation of the conflict in Tripoli. This conflict has now spread to Sirte and other areas between Libyan National Army (LNA) and Government of National Accord (GNA) forces. To enable an informed and rapid response, information on the situation needs to be collected in a timely matter. Currently, many of the humanitarian sectors in Libya rely heavily on data from the annual MSNA. However, the MSNA is not intended to provide an exhaustive analysis of all dynamics and vulnerabilities for every sector. Rather, the MSNA should serve as one of a range of information sources that sectors rely on when deciding where, how and for whom to target their resources. To help make this a reality, REACH proposes in 2021 to provide technical support to sector-led assessments. This would include support in research design, analysis and final output production, as well as capacity building. REACH is uniquely positioned to assist the sectors in this regard, and to help the sectors achieve quality assessment outputs that strengthen evidence-based decision-making in the Libyan humanitarian response.

2 - Activities

MSNA: conducted through a quantitative data collection exercise (i.e., a HH survey lasting around two months), followed by a smaller qualitative data collection exercise (lasting around 2-3 weeks) that will triangulate and build on the survey’s findings. REACH will collect data for the MSNA and Rapid Assessments through a combination of trained enumerators (gender-balanced as far as possible) and local data collection partners (typically local CSOs). Once the MSNA data is collected, it will go through several rounds of analysis and presentations of results (i.e. sectors, ISCG, HCT), for data to feed into the HNO/HRP process. Rapid Assessments: Upon the onset of a humanitarian emergency in Libya, and in coordination with humanitarian coordination structures such as the ISCG, REACH will activate its network of Key Informants to collect information and release findings in the form of a report and (if relevant) accompanying maps on an accelerated timeline after sudden crisis. Sectoral Support: Upon request from the sectors, REACH Assessment Officer will help sectors plan, design, and implement assessments, including support in assessment data analysis and final output production. If needed and requested, the Assessment Officer may also provide tailored, complementary support to build the sectors’ capacity around assessments and data analysis.JMMI: REACH proposes to strengthen and expand its existing price and market monitoring system, thereby improving the understanding among humanitarian agencies of how markets in Libya are functioning and reacting to the various crises, whilst facilitating cash-based programming. This initiative will support CBI activities by providing accurate information on the prices and availability of basic food and NFIs on a monthly basis. 2 Protection Assessments: REACH will conduct individual interviews, FGDs, KIIs with refugees, returnees and IDPs to understand their main needs in the urban environment and what protection risks they face.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

As REACH is not a direct service provider, the primary risks associated with these activities relate to the ethical practice of research, exposure of COVID-19 transmission, and the protection of respondent anonymity. R3: All enumerators are selected in line with understood community dynamics. All conflict sensitivity considerations are mainstreamed throughout assessment design (sampling, respondent selection, questionnaire design, dissemination strategy).R4: Due to the ongoing COVID-19 outbreak, REACH has decided to conduct remote data collection as primary modality through phone calls and online focus group discussions. Any in-person data collection is in line with WHO recommended guidelines on social distancing, hygiene practices, wearing masks, and ensuring ventilation. R6: REACH relies in all its data collection activities on Libyan respondents, and the data collected may include sensitive information such as household income and medical conditions. If this data were not kept anonymous, it could lead to negative repercussions for the respondents. REACH-led data collection is undertaken within the framework of a do-no-harm approach, and in accordance with IMPACT’s policy on the collection, storage and dissemination of personally identifiable information. All enumerators receive training on IMPACT’s policy and procedures around data collection. Also, any information that could potentially violate respondent anonymity is removed from datasets prior to publication. Respondent contact information is not shared with any other actors unless there is a specific, pre-planned purpose and the respondent household has given informed consent – and even in such cases, only the absolute minimum data is shared. R15: Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) number is provided to every respondent household and individual and it continuously monitors the quality and effectiveness of project deliveries including by collecting feedback from beneficiaries directly.. This is further detailed under 6.1. In addition, REACH conducts spot-check phone calls from field managers to respondents to ensure numbers have been provided, and to provide opportunity for reporting on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) cases. Through the CRM, beneficiaries can anonymously report any instances of misconduct on the part of ACTED staff or partners. REACH will also conduct Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training for its field managers and staff, in order to mitigate the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse occurring as a result REACH activities in Libya. .R20: Regarding field staff and enumerator safety, enumerators receive training by ACTED on both their and REACH's responsibilities around ensuring safety and managing travel as well as mine risk education awareness trainings. Throughout all data collection exercises, REACH remains in daily contact with enumerators and local partners. In the event of any security incident or potential security risk, REACH closely consults with the Tunis-based ACTED Security Manager and conducts site security assessments for all in-person visits as well as the affected local partner and other local sources, to determine how to react, and whether it is safe to continue. In particularly volatile areas, REACH may periodically suspend data collection in reaction to upticks in fighting.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Libyans who serve as respondents in REACH-led data collection activities may provide feedback on their experience through the ACTED-managed CRM continuously monitoring the quality and effectiveness of project deliveries including by collecting feedback from beneficiaries directly. REACH successfully rolled out the use of the CRM with the 2020 MSNAs, providing CRM cards for every respondent household and individual. REACH will continue and build on the integration of the CRM into data collection activities in 2021. The CRM ensures that the proposed action remains relevant to the needs of beneficiaries throughout the project implementation. The CRM is managed by ACTED’s Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation (AME) staff to ensure independence and for which confidentiality is a fundamental principle. Complaints are received via a dedicated CRM phoneline and in the field are then recorded and transferred to the CRM confidentially and registered in a password-protected database, categorized based on the level of sensitivity. Information is shared on a strictly need-to-know basis and restricted as much as possible. The CRM Focal Point responds to all individuals who have made a complaint to inform them of the outcome of their complaint. This serves as an active line of communication for ACTED to receive any grievances from community members regarding the implementation of the project activities or any feedback and suggestions for changes. These calls therefore directly translate into recommendations to programs for improvement of programming, and the resolution of such complaints communicated directly back to the beneficiaries.
