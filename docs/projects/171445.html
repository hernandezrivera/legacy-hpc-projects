Project Summary

This project aims at providing access to temporary and sustained water supply, safe, sanitary facilities, and increased awareness on safe hygiene practices to IDPs, host communities, returnees, and persons with disability affected by drought, floods, and conflict in Middle Shabelle, Hiraan, Kismayo, Baidoa and IDP settlements in Banadir. Temporary and sustained access to water will be delivered by providing water vouchers and rehabilitation of strategic water points. Hygiene kits that include soap, aqua tab, bucket, Jerrycan, and sanitary pads will be distributed to targeted high-risk families, i.e., families with malnutrition children, affected by AWD cholera, and persons living with disability. The project will also ensure that distribution points are close and can be accessed by people with disability. To promote good hygiene practices, hygiene promotion campaigns will be done in schools, water points, and health/nutrition centers in disaster-affected areas. The design of hygeine campaign materials such as IEC materials will consider the needs of visually and hearing impaired people. Disability disaggregated data will be collected throughout the project.To strengthen community ownership and ensure the sustainability of WASH assets, Water management committees (WMC) will be established; women, marginalized people, or those living with disabilities will have equal representation in these committees. Shock chlorination and dewatering of wells in flood-affected areas will be done to ensure water is safe for use again and to prevent further spread of AWD outbreaks. Gender-sensitive latrines with hand-washing facilities raised to avoid flooding and fitted with locks from the inside to curb GBV will also be constructed for the IDPs/returnees and dislodged. Siting of WASH facilities will be discussed with community members, particularly women and girls, to ensure optimum use and safety for vulnerable groups and people with disabilities. The design of WASH facilities will take into consideration the needs of disabled persons and the elderly. CLTS approach will be introduced where no latrines will be constructed to encourage good hygiene and sanitation behaviors aimed at achieving ODF free villages. Due to recurring floods in the Middle Shabelle region, WOCCA will use early warning systems to provide early warning messages in case of flood risk and create an effective response. The project will ensure that people living with disabilities will be included in every phase of the project. All WASH facilities will be located at accessible distances to women, girls, people with disabilities and vulnerable people in order to reduce time spent waiting and collecting water and to mitigate incidences of violence. The project will also ensure that women, men, girls, boys and people living with disabilities will participate equally in the design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation phases of the project.To ensure an integrated approach to WASH programming and GBV risk reduction, the project staff, and WASH committees will be trained on basic issues related to gender and GBV, including where survivors can report risk, access services, and referral mechanisms.
WOCCA will hold sensitisation meetings at the inception of the project with all project stakeholders, including groups at risk of exclusion, to inform them about the project objectives, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiary entitlement and roles. Accountability to the affected population will be enhanced by providing a feedback hotline to beneficiaries to provide feedback and complaints. To ensure quality in the project outputs, the project staff will continuously monitor and report on project indicators. Signing off and handing over WASH structures to beneficiaries upon completion will be done through community elders, government officials, and religious leaders. WOCCA will also adhere to the principles of "Do no harm.”

Project Activities

OC1: Increased and sustained access to safe water for the disaster-affected populations. Act 1.1: Provision of water vouchers to 15,000 people. 1.2 Construction of 20 new wells and rehabilitation of 15 wells as per the cluster standards. Wells in locations with a large population will be complemented with a solar-powered elevated water tank and water kiosks. All water facilities will be at accessible distances for people living with disabilities, women, and girls in order to reduce time spent waiting and collecting water and to mitigate incidences of violence. 1.3 Establish and train 105 Water management committee (WMCs) members on O&M of water systems. Women, men, and people living with disabilities will be equally represented in these committees.OC. 2: Provide access to adequate sanitation facilities and reduce the risk of violence against women, girls, and vulnerable people. The design of the latrines will consider the needs of people living with disabilities and the elderly Act.2.1: Conduct CLTS triggering campaigns in 20 villages where no latrines will be constructed. 2.3 Construction and desludging of 250 gender-sensitive lockable latrines with handwashing facilities and security lights in IDP camps, 2.4 Construction of 35 gender-sensitive lockable latrines with handwashing facilities and security lights in 7 IDP schools and nutrition/health centers. OC. 3: Reduced number and impact of AWD outbreaks and other WASH-related diseases in targeted communities and institutions Act.3.1 Pre-positioning of hygiene kits 3.2: Distribution of 3,500 hygiene kits to the most vulnerable households. Distribution points will be at accessible distances to the communities and people with disability 3.3 Establish school WASH clubs in schools where latrines will be constructed. Girls and Boys will be equally represented in these WASH clubs 3.3: Train 250 hygiene committee members (at least 100 are female) on the PHAST method and protection mainstreaming. 3.4 Chlorination of 100 wells in case of AWD or heightened risk of AWD 3.5: Link with Health Cluster actors for referral of AWD cases. OC. 4: Improve and make use of the early warning system in flood-affected areas. Act.4.1: Continuous monitoring of the river and disseminating early warning and hygiene messages through radio and SMS when floods are imminent. Act 5: M & E- Continuous monitoring and reporting on project indicators, providing a feedback hotline to beneficiaries, signing off, and handing over WASH structures to beneficiaries upon completion. Disaggreagted data of people living with disabilities will be collected.

Mitigation Measures

• To reduce the risk of violence during distribution, WOCCA will avoid centralized distribution of hygiene kits to minimize queuing time and risk of looting. Distribution areas will be close to the settlements to reduce travel distance for beneficiaries• To ensure marginalized groups are not excluded, WOCCA will sensitize and engage all community groups during the response mobilization phase. WOCCA will also carry out community consultation to identify vulnerable, under-represented HHs, with specific attention to age, gender, minorities, disability, and clan representation• WOCCA will ensure that land tenure security, certified by relevant local authorities, is provided and guaranteed for a significant amount of time (at least 5 years) before any construction activities.
