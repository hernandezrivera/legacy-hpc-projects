Project Summary

The Health and Nutrition Cluster partners estimated that 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza (HNO 2020). The partners identified that within Gaza, the population requires humanitarian interventions for reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health services including nutrition, treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs), including mental health and psychosocial support. Meanwhile, the health system is struggling to cope with the ongoing influx of conflict-related trauma casualties each week, as part of the Gaza Mass Demonstration. However, infrastructure, human resources, and the referral system remain only partially functional after 12 years of blockade, inadequate to cope with the growing needs.
The project aims to ensure access to essential life-saving health services of the most vulnerable affected population in Gaza through provision of essential drugs and medical supplies and implementation of early essential newborn care. A total of 188,000 men and women, including 5,000 newborns and their 5,000 mothers in the Gaza Strip will be targeted by the provision of life-saving medicines and access to interventions in emergency departments and maternities. Additionally, the WHO will initiate a robust supply monitoring and emergency storage management to inform humanitarian decisions and ensure better monitoring of donated supplies.

1 - Needs

Increasing attacks against healthcare, violent trauma, especially in the context of the ongoing Great March of Return (GMR) demonstrations in Gaza and accumulating mental health disorders, alongside chronically depleting medical supplies and dwindling number of specialist health care staff, have left a deteriorating and fragmented health system. Lack of medicines is particularly severe in the Gaza Strip, where 42% of the items on the essential medicines list are completely out of stock on average in the first half of 2019. As a symptom of the degrading health system, out-of-pocket expenditures for healthcare as a percentage of current health expenditure have increased to one of the highest in the region at 46 percent, affecting the welfare of households, disproportionally impacting the poorest.The Health Cluster estimates that 1.3 million people are in need of humanitarian health interventions across the oPt, of whom two out of every three are in Gaza (HNO 2020). Noncommunicable diseases (NCD) remain the leading cause of mortality in the oPt, accounting for more than two-thirds of all Palestinian deaths. According to the HNO 2020, within Gaza, one out of six of the adult population requires treatment for NCDs. However, recent acute emergency events have required prioritization of emergency interventions at the expense of the management of chronic diseases. The lack of medical supplies, including diagnostics and therapeutics, and referral restrictions, are main reasons for delays and interruption in the treatment of chronic diseases. Untreated NCDs can lead to serious complications, such as renal failure, disability, and amputations.The Gaza Central Drug Store (CDS) reported that at the end of September 2019, 246 essential medicines out of the total 516 essential medicines list (48%) were at less than one month’s supply and 227 essential medicines (44%) were completely depleted at the MoH store in Gaza. In addition, 235 essential disposables out of the total 853 essential disposables list (28%) were at less than one month’s supply (CDS September Report on Drugs and Disposables Shortages, 2019). In 2018, more than 57% of the total financial cost of pharmaceuticals supply in the public sector in the Gaza Strip (18.5M USD out of a total of 32.1M USD) was provided by different donors, including WHO (MoH Annual Report, 2018). Still, there was a gap of an estimated 5.0M USD even considering a 9.00M USD shipment coming from the CDS in Nablus. Due to this great reliance on donor supply, the need emerged to establish an efficient and systematic monitoring process to monitor the supply and distribution of pharmaceutical supplies (drugs and medical disposables) in public health facilities, in order to avoid duplication of supplies and prevent diversion of donations.In Gaza, the neonatal mortality rate is estimated to be 14 per 1,000 live births and has not significantly declined over the last ten years. Newborn deaths currently represent 68% of infant deaths – highlighting that the newborn period remains the period of highest risk for all infants. Ensuring access to respectful maternal and newborn health services and commodities during humanitarian crises is even more critical toimproving the outcomes for women and their newborns. The Health Cluster estimated that up to 15,000 newborns in Gaza will be in need of humanitarian interventions (HNO 2020). Those most vulnerable newborns are at higher risk of dying as they were born before the term and born through C-Section. There is a need identified by the cluster partners to focus on supporting good quality life-saving intrapartum care through the implementation of early essential neonatal care, including the protection of breastfeeding from the first minutes of life.

2 - Activities

This project is fully in line with the needs identified by the Health Cluster and expressed in the HNO for 2020. WHO aims to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability, and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza through the provision and monitoring of essential drugs and medical supplies as well as ensuring child survival from the first days of life (HNC Objective 1). The activities in this project are in line with two Health cluster activities under the Strategic Cluster Objective 1:* Provide essential medical supplies (medicines, disposables, blood components, etc) for acutely vulnerable NCD patients; and * Ensure that people have access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality.The project will address the life-saving needs of the most vulnerable population identified by the Health cluster partners. With this project WHO will target up to 198,000 affected people, including an estimated 101,000 females. These include patients at MoH hospital emergency departments - an estimated 98,000 people and those who have non-communicable diseases (NCD) - an estimated 90,000. An additional 5,000 neonates born through C-Section and their mothers (a total of 10,000) will also benefit from the availability of essential basic supplies and improved clinical practices. WHO will implement the following activity:Support life-saving health interventions for the most vulnerable population in the Gaza Strip:a) WHO will procure and deliver the essential medicines and medical supplies to ensure the ability of health care services to diagnose and manage life-threatening non-trauma conditions in the most vulnerable groups, including the most vulnerable patients attending the emergency departments and the NCD patients, including at PHC level. The procurement will be done in close consultation with the MoH based on their monthly needs and availability of drugs/disposables. Cluster partners will also be consulted to avoid potential duplication in procurement. Only existing gaps in essential drugs/disposables will be covered. WHO will target and estimated 188,000 affected people, including an estimated 96,000 females and an estimated 9,500 PwD – those attending EDs and patients with NCDs.b) WHO will work with the health authorities in Gaza, the MoH in Ramallah and partners through Health Cluster to develop a robust medical supply monitoring system in Gaza to ensure impartial identification of humanitarian needs, adequate storage of emergency health supplies for key partners and improved information system for humanitarian decision making. All humanitarian partners will benefit from better planning, including the health authorities.c) WHO will scale-up early essential newborn care (EENC) in Gaza to ensure child survival of the most vulnerable newborns. This will focus on the implementation of key evidence-based life-saving interventions in key maternities across Gaza. The EENC is the basic care required for every baby that should always be prioritized in a humanitarian setting (WHO, UNICEF, Save the Children, 2017). Adoption of these practices, elimination of harmful practices and revising newborn NICU admission criteria will prevent and manage many cases of sepsis, asphyxia, and complications in children born through C-Section. WHO will work with the staff of the maternities to improve their skills during the C-Section that would improve child survival. A limited procurement will also be undertaken to ensure the availability of baby cups and wraps to dry the babies. WHO will only target the most vulnerable 5,000 boys and girls who are born through C-Section (50% of all who born in four target hospitals with C-Section). Their mothers would also benefit, including from skin-to-skin contact that contributes to improved delivery outcomes for mothers. The activities of this component will be closely coordinated with UNFPA, UNICEF and MAP-UK.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

WHO places centrality of protection at the core of its response by identifying who is atrisk, how and why, taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these risks, including those experienced by men, women, girls, and boys, and groups such as Palestinian refugees, internally displaced persons, older persons and persons with disabilities. This allows WHO to plan, collectively with the affected population, the set of interventions that are culturally appropriate, based on the needs, relevant, timely and are in line with humanitarian principles. A feedback mechanism will be established to ensure that assistance is delivered in adherence to the principles of “do no harm”.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

WHO identifies the needs and monitors projects taking into account the specific vulnerabilities that underlie these needs, including those of men, women, girls and boys, and groups such as Palestinian refugees, internally displaced persons, older persons and persons with disabilities.
