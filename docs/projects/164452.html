Project Summary

DDD intervention will improve access to essential health services and psychosocial support for approximately 508,000 individuals in northwest Syria (catchment population as of September 2019).
The intervention will contribute to improving access to comprehensive and inclusive healthcare services and to increase well-being of the conflict-affected population in Idlib and Aleppo Governorate, focusing on areas densely populated with IDPs, and continuously receiving new IDPs following conflict escalation in January 2020.
Direct Implementation by DDD:
In northern Idlib governorate (Harim & Idlib Districts):
- 5 PHCCs (Qah, Al Doaa, Al Jolan, Al Dana, Al Salam)
- 2 part-time PHCCs covered by 1 mobile team (Morek Alez and Deir Hassan)
- 2 mobile PHCCs available to respond to new health needs, currently responding to emergency needs in Sarmada and Kili.
- 1 mobile outreach team providing specialized home-based medical services for vulnerable households near DDD fixed PHCCs in Qah, Dier Hassan Cluster, and Al Dana town.
In northern Aleppo governorate (Afrin District)
- 1 PHC clinic in Jinderes;
- 1 PHC clinic in Afrin City;
- 1 PHC clinic in Celmeh, including normal delivery services (starting in second quarter of 2020)
Partners
DDD will work closely with partners to complement DDD direct implementation:
- Idlib Health Directorate – 2 PHC clinics in Idlib city (Al Thawra and Al Jameea)
- Aleppo Health Directorate – 3 PHC clinics (Kafr Noran, Kafr Naseh, and Kosnia), until March 2020.
- Hand in Hand For Aid and Development – From January to March 2020, full support to Atmeh Maternity and Child Hospital. Starting from August 2020 until December 2020, support to 3 mobile clinics in northern Aleppo.
DDD and its partners maintain a flexible approach and adapt intervention according to priority needs defined in coordination with the Health Cluster. Emergency response to most vulnerable groups will be prioritized.
Between January and December 2020, DDD aims to provide a total of 387,320 consultations, consisting of 306,480 PHC consultations, 77,976 SRH consultations, and 2,864 MHPSS consultations.
A total of 215,098 beneficiaries are expected to benefit from the project. In Idlib, it is estimated that about 75% of beneficiaries reached by the project are displaced individuals (living either in camps or out of camps) while 25% are part of the host population. This is due in part to the fact that certain health facilities are serving IDP camps or urban areas with strong presence of IDPs. In Afrin, beneficiaries of the project will be mainly host communities and IDPs living in informal settlements.
DDD recognize the specific needs of certain groups who face higher health risks or might have limited access to health services. In line with HNO 2019, these population are identified as children under five, pregnant and lactating women, at-risk pregnancies, individuals living with war-related injuries and disabilities, older people, people with functional difficulties, people living with chronic conditions and people affected psychologically by the conflict.
DDD, as a member of Medecins du Monde (MdM) international network, has a proven track record in providing emergency and long-term medical care to conflict-affected populations in northwest. MdM has been responding to the Syrian crisis for since 2012, progressively increasing funding dedicated to the response. DDD currently runs one office in northern Idlib, located in Qah. The main operational base for DDD operations in NWS is in Antakya, in Hatay province.
The intervention is in line with the Humanitarian and Do No Harm principles and are adapted to comply with the EPHC Package developed by WHO and the Health Cluster.

4 - Needs

Needs identification guiding DDD’s activities in Syria is based on a combination of primary and secondary level data. - According to HNO 2019, the main health gaps in Syria include essential health service provision for patients living with chronic conditions, access to essential primary health care services, sexual and reproductive health and MHPSS services as well as limited availability of quality medicine and qualified health staff. Health service delivery and infrastructures in Syria have been critically undermined by ongoing attacks on health care. - HNAP estimates that up to 30% of the households in NWS include a head of household with a disability and that at least 27% of the population (aged 12 years and above) in Syria have a disability. - In Afrin District, an important gap in access health care was identified, with 20% of surveyed households reporting having limited access to health care. One in five (22%) household surveyed was not able to receive treatment after getting sick in the 3 months in Afrin district, reaching one in four households in Afrin City. This gap is increasing with important arrivals of new IDPs in the area living in inadequate and substandard living conditions in informal settlements and unfinished buildings. - In Dana sub-district, although coverage for regular primary health care services is adequate, ongoing arrival of new IDPs to the already overcrowded camps is increasing pressure on existing service providers and poor living conditions is increasing health risks for residents. Specialized services for persons with disabilities and older persons are lacking.  HNO 2019 Disability: Prevalence and impact, HNAP, October 2019 Situation Report No. 6, UNOCHA Community multi-sectoral needs assessment conducted jointly with HIHFAD – Northern Aleppo (Afrin, Azaz, Al Bab, Jerablus), November/December 2019  Community multi-sectoral needs assessment – Dana sub-district, November 2019

5 - Implementation of activities

DDD health response is in line with the EPHC Package developed by WHO and the Health Cluster, with a specific focus on NCD, Mother and Childcare (incl. MUAC screening) and MHPSS (incl. Mh-GAP). Response to GBV cases will be provided through CMR and MHPSS support. DDD will provide a comprehensive package of PHC, SRH and MHPSS services to conflict-affected population in DDD PHCCs and through implementing partners in Idlib and Aleppo governorates. DDD will provide health promotion activities by CHWs in all areas of intervention and home-based specialized medical services by DDD multi-disciplinary outreach teams near DDD fixed PHCCs in Qah, Dier Hassan Cluster and Al Dana town. Referrals provided from DDD clinics will be done in coordination with Syria Relief and Development (SRD) Referral System. DDD contributes to 4W reporting and reports weekly to EWARN. M&E: Regular monitoring will be carried out by DDD staff including cross-border monitoring visits, implementation of robust data collection system and monthly exit interviews at health facility level, monthly health committee meetings, regular needs assessment for new response and complaints and feedback mechanism. Early Recovery and Resilience: Resilience-building activities will be conducted such as health promotion activities by CHWs, psychosocial group sessions and awareness raising sessions on topics related to healthy lifestyles, participation of communities in implementation, capacity building of health staff and rehabilitation of health facilities to improve accessibility for persons with disabilities and reduced mobility. Supplementary budget information: Cost effectiveness will be ensured through prioritization of costs directly benefiting individuals in Syria, including balancing provision of general PHC services reaching larger number of individuals and essential specialized services such as MHPSS services. Breakdown on staffing budget line can be shared separately due to space limitation.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Attacks on health facilities, health staff and beneficiaries: DDD has a regularly updated contingency plan, Security protocols and Duty of Care Policy which cover the mitigation measures in case of impact on activities. Regular security trainings are provided to the team. DDD Duty of Care Policy covers response in case of kidnapping/detention/evacuation, including salary advance. Gender and disability mainstreaming are key components of DDD response. GBV: DDD staff provide safe referrals of GBV through inter-agency referral pathways to relevant protection actors in coordination with Protection Cluster and medical services by DDD medical staff (CMR). Ongoing capacity building of staff is taking place to improve response capacity to GBV. Privacy and data protection:• Private consultation rooms with adequate level of privacy for examination and counselling and a confidential data recording system.• Gender and cultural sensitivities are considered as priorities during recruitment process and service provision. Prevention against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse:• PSEA Trainings is mandatory for all staff and part of induction• Awareness raising with Health Committees • Beneficiaries have access to complaints mechanisms information which are available in the waiting areas and can be processed safely through the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. • Support is available if needed for people with disability and illiterate beneficiaries Equal and impartial access to assistance and services:• Community-based outreach visit activities (home-based medical specialized services and PSS group sessions in schools) to increase the reach of intervention beyond the clinics and enhance meaningful access to health care.• Rehabilitation of health facilities for accessibility.• Focused service provision for children under 5, women in reproductive age, persons with disabilities, older persons, persons living with chronic conditions and IDPs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS• DDD health staff are able to continue working in the areas of intervention• DDD partners continue working in the areas of intervention• DDD health staff are able to conduct cross-border monitoring visits• The Government of Turkey continues to allow DDD to deliver medical supplies from Turkey to Syria• Population has access to health facilities, despite restrictions of movement• Mobile teams are able to reach IDP camps• Data collection at health facility level is feasible RISKS• DDD and/or partners are forced to interrupt activities because of the volatile security context;• Difficulties related to payment transfer affecting salaries and running costs• Main cross-border points (Bab Al Hawa and Bab Al Salam) closed at the same time by the Turkish authorities• Access to areas of intervention within Syria are restricted due to high level of conflict, change of controlling entity or siege of certain areas• DDD areas of operations are targeted by hostilities• Health facilities, DDD offices, patients and staff are targeted• Fraud and Aid diversion• Partial/total diversion of health shipments by rebel/armed groups/local stakeholders• Increased displacement due to fighting impacting needs and extension of support required• Large-scale population displacement, further straining the resources and absorption capacity of host community Mitigations• Standards Operating Procedures for security are in place and staff is trained on how to act in insecurity situations• Contingency Plan including actions in the potential scenarios is available and continuously updated.• The EPREP is developed in coordination with all departments• Duty of care policy is developed and implemented for staff inside Syria• Coordination with key stakeholders in Turkey and inside Syria is maintained to ensure acceptance for DDD operations inside Syria and safety for staff and beneficiaries• All cross-border personnel attend Safe and Secure Approaches In Field Environment
