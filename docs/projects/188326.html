Project Summary

The NRC protection response will be two-fold: (1) the direct provision of information, counselling and legal assistance (ICLA) to PACD and (2) increased knowledge and capacity to respond amongst duty-bearers. In addition, ICLA will identify emergency response capacities and multi-sectoral integration with NRC Shelter, WASH, Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS), and Education programmes. NRC will prioritise IDPs, returnees, host community members as well as Iraqi refugees, Palestine refugees, stateless individuals and individuals at risk of statelessness including third-country nationals (TCN). Through the delivery of a multi-faceted response, NRC will focus on the protection of rights to legal identity and civil documentation (CD); housing, land and property (HLP); employment and labour; and access to essential services. Geographic areas will be targeted based on the severity and scale of needs, inter-agency coordination at hub-level, and access.
(1) Provision of Direct Assistance: For 2022/2023, NRC direct services will be aimed at PACD who face legal challenges in relation civil documentation, HLP, employment procedures and accessing essential services. Key needs include the issuance of basic civil documentation such as birth, marriage and death certificates which are necessary in order to access education, health care and humanitarian assistance. NRC will further prioritize the most vulnerable, taking into account gender, age, disability, ethnicity and/or religion. This includes a focus on children and youth without proof of legal identity and female-headed households (FHH) with missing or deceased spouses who require confirmation of their legal status and that of their children. HLP information and counselling will aim to protect security of tenure and legal assistance will seek to prevent forced evictions. Information and counselling activities may involve home visits to reach beneficiaries who are otherwise inaccessible to increase inclusivity for differently-abled persons. NRC will increase its support to key community stakeholders, including local civil society actors, on collaborative dispute resolution (CDR) techniques, such as mediation and negotiation. NRC will continue to carry out HLP due diligence to support Shelter and WASH, Education and Livelihoods interventions. NRC will launch interventions related to community awareness and training arounds the rights of children.
For all NRC and other humanitarian activities, ICLA will advocate and emphasize that civil documentation should not be considered a prerequisite to receive assistance. (2) Increased Knowledge and Capacity to Respond: NRC will continue to undertake legal analysis and research and will additionally provide technical assistance to community leaders, duty-bearers and humanitarian partners through training modules on legal identity, HLP, collaborative dispute resolution (CDR) and employment law. Community leaders who will benefit from capacity building interventions may include formal and traditional authorities such as Mukhtars, Sheikhs, lawyers/jurists, female leaders and representatives of vulnerable groups. NRC will continue its support for the resolution of HLP disputes through CDR techniques such as mediation and negotiation, and will explore the potential for such techniques in employment or labour disputes. Guidance and advice on HLP due diligence procedures will be provided to other NRC programmes, such as Shelter and WASH, as well as the broader humanitarian sector through continued leadership and coordination in in the HLP Technical Working Groups. Across interventions, particular emphasis will be placed on the rights of women and girls, as well as the rights of persons with disabilities. NRC will continue to input activities into 4Ws, as often as requested, to ensure accurate protection service mapping. NRC will further review all awareness raising and training materials on a quarterly basis to update as needed.

4 - Needs

Long-term conflict in Syria lead to access to civil documentation being severely curtailed, leaving an entire generation potentially undocumented or under-documented and unable to effectively enjoy their right to legal identity as well as to services. According to the 2021 HNO, access to legal identity and civil documentation was reported as a protection concern by 61% of communities while restrictions on freedom of movement were reported by 65%. Births frequently go undocumented, leaving children at risk of statelessness and at heightened risk of trafficking, recruitment and sexual exploitation. The main barriers to documentation include security concerns, access to government-controlled areas for persons residing outside of them, fears of conscription or arrest, costs and lack of necessary prerequisite documentation. Most communities report lack of access to legal aid. HLP issues were identified as a protection issue by 61% of the communities surveyed for the 2021 HNO, with the primary concerns related to damage/looting of property, lack of HLP documentation, secondary occupancy, forced evictions and UXOs. Women face additional barriers to HLP rights, especially when divorced/widowed. In the midst of the ongoing economic crisis, 50% of the Syrian population remains unemployed while overall poverty is at 90% (HNO 2021) making it critical for Syrians to be able to safeguard their employment rights, claim unpaid wages and avoid unfair dismissal from work.

5 - Implementation of activities

NRC will provide direct assistance on accessing rights and procedural requirements in relation to legal identity, HLP, essential services, employment laws and returns-related issues. In relation to HLP disputes, NRC will utilise CDR techniques to seek resolution. Training sessions will be conducted on CDR, HLP and employment for duty-bearers, humanitarian partners and community leaders. NRC will lead national and hub-level coordination and engagement on HLP and CD through the relevant Protection and HLP humanitarian fora, and by coordinating with partners, in particular CSOs at local level. CSOs and other community-based structures will receive training on collaborative dispute resolution methods, expected to contribute to a better protection environment and increased social cohesion. Further awareness and training subjects will include the rights of women, legal protection of children and rights of persons with disabilities in Syria. Priority will be given to disseminating and expanding HLP due diligence guidance across the humanitarian sector. Legal research and policy analysis undertaken by NRC will feed into programme design and advocacy messaging. Information and feedback collected from communities will be used to inform programme, advocacy and preparedness. Detailed M&E plans will be implemented Activities for Damascus hub: awareness-raising, capacity building, coordination and research.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are mainly identified during the beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities as well as confidential complain and feedback channels available to them and makes sure gender balanced teams register and deliver assistance to communities whenever possible. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases. Efforts in 2021 to strengthen child-friendly CFM started and are expected to conclude in 2022.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. NRC staff are injured, killed, or suffer psychological trauma due conflict: Mitigation: Security briefings, HEAT and HEIST trainings, briefings, more support from UNMAS.2. Fraud/corruption/bribery. Mitigation: enforcing NRC's policy of zero tolerance through strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the "whistle-blowing" policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. 3. NRC is unable to timely operate due to the impact of internationals sanctions. Mitigation: request for derogation, looking for different banking channels, advocacy.4. NRC is unable to timely implement due to delays in approvals from Government/local authorities. Mitigation: Advocacy plan in place, set the lead time for approval submission to be 2 weeks with a close follow.5. NRC is unable to timely deliver the planned activities due to rapid inflation and change of prices. Mitigation: review budget and targets on a quarterly basis, communication with the donors, find more convenient payment terms to suppliers.6. Donor policies, including overly restrictive redlines and perceptions, affect the ability to implement and scale-up programs. Mitigation: Engaging with donor, Joint advocacy efforts with DINGOs, Intensify the negotiations with local partner and MOUs, Long terms programs and reconstructions, media coverage in private and public advocacy.C-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during C-19; adapted implementation modalities.
