Project Summary

The project will work on (a) strengthen and scale up access to case management, psychosocial support, and referrals for survivors of GBV and other protection violations; (b) strengthen the capacity of current child protection partners to provide quality services for child survivors; and (c) raise awareness to affect behavioural change to increase acceptance of services, to reduce stigma, and to prevent GBV.
The focus population is women, girls and boys affected by armed conflict
(including host communities, internally displaced, returnee, migrants and refugees).

1 - Needs

Seven years of conflict in Libya and widespread violations of International Human Rights Law, International Humanitarian Law and International Refugee Law have resulted in severe protection needs for IDPs, returnees, affected host communities, refugees and migrants. Women and girls have traditionally had limited participation in the public sphere and continue to face discrimination in law and practice. Gender-based violence is reportedly widespread; 40% of respondents in a 2017 survey stated that GBV was common, 87% of women and girls reported that they needed permission from their husband or parents to seek health care. Humanitarian access for assessment, intervention and support to GBV survivors and to ensure access to basic services and referral pathways continues to constitute a major challenge due to the security context in Libya. Women and girls are often afraid to come forward and report cases of GBV or to access the limited available services for various reasons, including stigma, fear of retaliation by the family, lack of services and lack of awareness and information. Libya has long been a transit and destination country for individuals fleeing conflict and persecution, or seeking to escape from extreme poverty, lack of access to economic, social and cultural rights, and other human rights violations. While official statistics are not available, it is estimated that there are at least 670,920 migrants in Libya. Of these around 10% are children (67,920 children), of which 65% are accompanied and 35% are unaccompanied (in total 23,482 UASC). Approximately 9% of the adult population is made up of women. Due to security concerns, humanitarian access to affected populations in urban settings continues to be a major challenge. National partners have significant capacity gaps to respond to GBV cases, to ensure proper prevention and response, including a lack of functional referral mechanisms. In addition, communities lack trust and are often unaware of the importance of reporting and requesting support as a result of cultural, contextual and security reasons.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Provide age-appropriate, survivor centered, community-based psychosocial support, case management, and other specialized services for survivors of GBV(Through establishment and strengthen partnerships with two national partners and two INGOs who will also provide capacity building and coaching for national partners, which include different services within community and girls centers) Activity 2: Support sustainable and community-based GBV awareness raising programmes to affect behavioral change to increase acceptance of services, to reduce stigma, and to prevent GBV(Awareness raising will be done through sessions to different stakeholders, children awareness days in different community centers, develop community-based approaches to promote awareness about how and where to access services through consultations, with partners, conduct community awareness activities (including group discussions, dissemination of information, etc) with GBV messagesActivity 3: Provide capacity building for government and NGO partners to build skills and capacity on GBV, especially care to child survivors, clinical management of rape, and PSS.(Capacity building conducted with UNICEF partners and UNICEF-led sectors on GBV risk mitigation to make programming safer and in line with recommendations from the IASC GBV Guidelines and/or sector-specific guidance that already integrates such recommendations, also, strengthen UNICEF partner and GBV sector partner competencies through training (Case Management, Caring for Survivors of Sexual Assault, Caring for Child Survivors, etc.)Activity 4: Strengthen capacity across sectors to integrate GBV and reduce risks of GBV, including PSEA

5 - Mitigation measures

UNICEF will allocate designated emergency capacity to closely follow emerging trends and will build internal emergency readiness, in continuous consultation with conflict analysts. Enhance coordination mechanism and de-confliction through Humanitarian Access mechanism. Raise awareness of national and international partners on prospective risks and threats and undertake regular security risk analysis

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNICEF will ensure commitment to accountability to affected populations by ensuring transparent feedback and accountability mechanisms are integrated into project proposals, monitoring and evaluations, recruitment, staff inductions, trainings, partnerships et. This will ensure that the affected community accesses timely sufficient information, guidance, and support that enable the target population to understand and influence key decisions in the project to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices through active participation and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and its affected populations over information provision. Field monitoring is key for timely and informed decision-making, and UNICEF is committed to its accountability and transparency to the affected populations, government and donors. The volatile situation, constraints posed by security and lack of access, however, prevent UNICEF staff from undertaking regular field monitoring visits. UNICEF Libya, therefore, engages the services of a third-party monitoring organization to conduct programmatic visits. In addition, UNICEF Libya maintains regular communication with its implementing partners and governmental actors to ensure timely and updated information on the situation of children and women inside the country
