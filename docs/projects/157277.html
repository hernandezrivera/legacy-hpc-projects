Needs

ACH has conducted a needs assessment in the States of White Nile and Red Sea during the period of November 21st to December 18th 2018 and the assessment has used different methods to collect data: 1)Literature review and existing information compilation.2)Semi structured interviews with key informants in the different phases of the assessment. The identified key informants delivering information on the situation and main actors, these interviews leaded us to new interviews (cascade effects). 3)Focus group discussion. 4)Interviews with affected populations in White Nile and red Sea. 5)Rapid health facilities assessment. The assessment has indicated that in White Nile the available nutrition information dates back from 2013 surveys, and since then, the situation has changed dramatically because of the arrival of thousands of new refugees, rendering those results useless for our analysis. New results for host communities will be available in early 2019. The analysis of the 2018 CMAM database (complete until November) shows that the information system lacks information from many OTPs and SCs in several localities, disparities between localities are big, and that coverage rates are very low in most localities. Moreover, there is little support from external actors to public health and nutrition services in host communities. The capacity of the staff is low, and allocated resources scarce. The assessment recommend to provide a technical support to State MoH and reinforcement of CMAM Scaling-up in health facilities and community level in key identified localities. Nutrition causal analysis, WASH in nutrition and behaviour change at community level. Contingency planning for seasonal peaks of acute malnutrition. Global wasting rates are around 6%-10% in White Nile State (within “medium” threshold), but some localities in White Nile are over 10% (“high”) such as Kosti, El Jabalain, Umm Renta and El Gutaina. Exclusive breastfeeding in children under 6 months in White Nile are around 50%, lowest rates are found in Rabak and Guli. ACH assessment indicated that WASH in Health centers; nearly inexistent or in poor conditions in most of the health units and health centers. Sometime, there is a storage tank for drinking water with variable quality and treatment, and some of the health centers do have sanitary infrastructures but with poor quality (simple pit not ventilated) with no segregation by sex.ACAPS new report 13 February 2019 indicated that households across Sudan, including most IDPs, are struggling to meet their basic needs as staple food prices for wheat, millet and sorghum increased by 150-200% in 2018.Limited access to food, reduced purchasing power, and few livelihood alternatives for farmers have left an estimated 5.76 million people facing severe levels of food insecurity, especially in Khartoum, Red Sea, Blue Nile, White Nile, South Kordofan, Kassala, Gedaref and Darfur states.

Activities

1. Technical support to the White Nile State Ministry of Health in surveillance of severe acute malnutrition, strategic planning and management of scaling-up of essential nutrition and primary healthcare services and community approaches. Improved data collection and analysis, both quantitative and qualitative, will contribute to informed decision-making at State and locality levels. Underserved localities with high rates of acute malnutrition (according to the 2019 March S3M) will be prioritized.2. Capacity building, group problem solving and supervision to improve quality of facility-based nutrition and health services, including outpatient and inpatient management of severe acute malnutrition, growth monitoring, integrated management of childhood illnesses, infant and young child feeding counseling, antenatal care and postnatal care.3. Support and follow-up of a WASH minimum package in the targeted health care facilities. This will include construction and rehabilitation of WASH infrastructures; set-up, follow-up and training of persons in charge; distribution of hygiene kits to health facilities and caregivers of children with severe acute malnutrition enrolled in OTP; hygiene sensitization.3. Community mobilization via local organizations, community health workers and community volunteers, to promote behavior change around hygiene, infant and young child feeding, appropriate nutrition of children and pregnant and lactating women, and health care seeking behavior.4. Targeted food distributions during the lean season to improve/allow food access and to address socioeconomic situation due to economic crisis of the most vulnerable households.5. Blanket supplementary feeding programme targeting children U5 and pregnant and lactating women to prevent undernutrition. 6. Vegetable production to address low dietary intake and low diet diversity.
