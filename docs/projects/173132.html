Project Summary

The project seeks to deliver quality life saving and life sustaining, inclusive and gender responsive interventions to 160,000 crisis affected populations in need across East and South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. This will be achieved through the following thematic areas;
• Provide crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic water services.
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic/limited sanitation services
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services
• Provide Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected population access to minimum WASH for Nutrition package
• Provide outbreaks affected and ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health package
The response targets five localities in East Darfur (Abu Jabrah, Abu Karinka, Bahr Al Arab, Shia'ria and Yassin), six localities in Blue Nile (At Tadamon, Baw, El Damazine, Al kurmuk, Ar Rusayris and Geisan), eight localities in South kordofan (Abu Jubayha, Abu Kershola, Abassiya, Kadugli, Ar Rashad, Talawdi and Um Durein) and thirteen localities in South Darfur (Beliel, Buram, Damso, Al Radoum, Kas, As Salam, Mershing, Nitega, As Sunta, Kateila, Kubum, Sharg Al Jabal and Um Dafoug)

1 - Needs

2020 has been a difficult year globally and Sudan was not spared especially at a time when the country is going through a fragile transition period, amidst a myriad of multiple shocks. The protracted sanctions led to a crippling economic crisis characterized by high inflation, rapid currency depreciation, fuel shortages and a strained supply of basic goods and services. This was further compounded by the COVID 19 pandemic which introduced further economic constraints, with 18,254 cases confirmed in the country by 30th November 2020, with 1265 associated deaths, CFR of 6.9%. Pockets of conflict especially in the Darfur region and South Kordofan led to displacement of people from their homes to rescind in IDPs. The country further went through one of the worst floods in decades within the months of August to October 2020, affecting 885,000 people with 143 associated deaths and leaving over 80,000 homes damaged. Post flooding outbreaks like Malaria, Chikungunya and Ndengue fever put extra pressure on an already strained and weak health care system. These multiple shocks severely impacted livelihoods, escalating the vulnerabilities of the crisis affected populations. This response project targets severely ranked localities in East and South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states where the bulk of the people in need rescind. These four states were in protracted conflict for about 17 years and therefore also host about 50% of IDPs in Sudan. According to the HNO 2021 vulnerability mapping, less than 7.5% of households in majority of the targeted localities in these four states have access to hand washing with soap and water. Similarly, less than 7.5% of households in targeted areas of East Darfur and large areas of South Kordofan have no access even to limited sanitation while in most targeted localities of South Darfur and Blue Nile states, access to limited sanitation was found to be between 25-50%. Access to basic water services as better but still low ranging between 35-65% across the targeted localities in the four states. Status of WASH in schools was also found to be stressed or severe in these localities according to a study done by the WASH cluster. Protection risks abound, especially for women and girls who bear the burden of facilitating access to WASH services to their families. This is more pronounced among displaced populations in camp settings who face unique challenges with regard to access to services. Access to dignity kits among adolescent girls in IDPs and other remote and hard to reach areas is a major challenge which often forces to use old pieces of cloth, sometimes leading to infections. When not available, some girls end up missing school every period cycle during the menses days. Water quality challenges have led to increased SAM cases among U5s and increased WASH related morbidity among affected populations over the years. Faced with these multiple shocks, eighteen million people are in need of humanitarian assistance to access WASH services across the country. The sector has targeted 4.7 million people in need of urgent WASH services, among which World Vision proposes to provide life saving and life sustaining interventions to reduce suffering, improve health status, dignity, safety and overall wellbeing 160,000 vulnerable people spread across the four states targeted.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Uncoordinated use of natural resources can have detrimental impacts on the environment further contributing to climate change. Climate change has become one of the major issues threatening the sustainability of WASH Infrastructure development in the world. Changes to weather patterns, including the reliability and predictability of seasonal rainfall and the impacts of extreme events, now place unprecedented pressures on water resources, especially in flood- and drought-prone contexts like Sudan. Climate change threatens water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities and services, as these are intimately linked to the water cycle and are vulnerable to changes in the quantity and quality of available water resources. Floods and droughts, which pollute and reduce water delivery respectively, have now become a perennial issue to deal with in Sudan. We evidently witnessed the destruction of WASH infrastructure when flash-floods ravaged across many states in Sudan in recent months.One of the Five Principles of Sustainable WASH is the element of environmental sustainability which entails placing WASH interventions in the wider context of the natural environment and implementing an approach of integrated and sustainable management of water resources. This is the approach proposes to use in this response to protect watersheds and over-exploitation of ground water. Through IWRM, WV will ensure harmonization of water use with land use to enhance the resilience of water and sanitation services to disasters as an effective strategy to reduce disaster impact, enhance ecosystem services, minimize pollution hazard, minimize over-pumping of groundwater aquifers, Promote the sustainable use natural resources for WASH infrastructure, and source materials as sustainably as possible. Borehole sitting will be done through geophysical surveys accompanied with Environmental and Social Impact assessments to ensure correct siting. Through a participatory approach with the target beneficiaries, potential environmental impacts from proposed interventions will be identified during ESIA processes and mitigation plans developed, implemented and monitored. Green energy solutions like solar pumping and gravity schemes will be optimized as much as possible. Ground recharge technologies like sand dams will be adopted where feasible. Use of locally available appropriate materials will also be promoted for sanitation facilities as opposed to transported brick and mortar.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into sectoral activities. As mainstreaming issue, the project will ensure that each activity will not expose vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conducted throughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality to protect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support including referral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be ensured protection to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, community discussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space for everybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be held separately from the ones for adults. Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministry of social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. The safety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable and project will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staffs or project stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the day with the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While project will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in project activities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does not create another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities on beneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within the communities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian Accountability Standard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other means preferred by the communities. Program staff will use their current safeguarding and accountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staffs will include Code of Conduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries. Sanitation facilities in schools will ensure gender segregation, be located in well lit safely accessible spaces especially for girls and be designed and constructed to offer safe and private dignified access to boys and girls. Care givers will be educated to ensure that children only carry age appropriate jerricans when they have to help in fetching water.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups and accountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries will similarly be based on MoU with group members and regular joint review of progress. Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVI represented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. The MoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution of the communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI to communicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and project locations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUs have enabled WVI hold communities accountable for commitments they make at the onset of projects. Participation: This program will be designed in consultation with IDP and host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regular consultation as part of ongoing interventions, IDP , host communities and government leaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries and prioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level will be facilitated project staff to further publicize the program and communicate opportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institute feedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and ‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully in Darfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularly women – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamper participation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanisms and / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will work towards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuring bens have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied with the opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consult with beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, and communicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it can address. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner that prioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages.
