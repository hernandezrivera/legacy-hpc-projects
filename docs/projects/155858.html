Needs

The northern rural of Aleppo and Idleb governorate has wide agriculture lands. Prior to the crisis, the main sources of income for most (estimated 70%) of the population surrounded agricultural production and livestock production. However, after eight year of conflict, the prices of agricultural inputs such as seeds for cultivation, basic tools, and water for irrigation became difficult to purchase due to rising costs, and extreme inflation. This leaves many households without adequate means to support themselves. Poverty and empty fields, and garden plots are now a prominent feature of the landscape in this rural area. While men of working age are indeed affected by these decreases in agricultural production and livelihood strategies, the most vulnerable groups (Including female and child headed households, pregnant lactating women, boys and girls, the elderly and disabled) are further impacted. This is due to the lack of access to livelihood mechanisms which can be performed near home as well as decrease in the availability of fresh produce in the locality and the subsequent resilience on food aid which does not provide adequate micro nutrients content. Through supporting he most vulnerable households with appropriate agricultural inputs, this will support resilience, and have an indirect positive effect on the surrounding community in each of the target areas. All potential beneficiary households will be assessed to determine vulnerability, willingness, and ability to conduct small scale food production activities.Based on the Food security sector HNO 2019 that, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity.- From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies.- Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations.- With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines, and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. - The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with the lowest wheat production at 1.2 million metric tonnes since 1989. There is a shortfall of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes.- Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies.Agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25/30 percent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen resilience of Syrians.RF proposal planned. Protection Risk Analysis

Risks - Low garden yields - Border closure, or restrictions leading to delays or inability to deliver humanitarian materials. - Lack of beneficiary knowledge of gardening practices. - Lack of beneficiary interest or participation in gardening activities. - Diversion of assets (including water provided) - Revoked access to land/assets (particularly in informal camps and settlements) - Risk to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households - Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection - Inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions, - Inclusion and exclusion during distribution leading to sexual exploitation of females. Mitigation strategies - Diversity of seeds provided, proper monitoring and follow up, specialist consultants available to troubleshoot and mitigate. - Liaise with local and international stakeholders in advance to establish access. - Provide guided community based training activities for all selected beneficiaries. - Arrange community based incentives such as gardening contests, prizes, etc. - Post distribution monitoring and periodic community based monitoring of beneficiary gardens. - Liaise with all local stakeholders prior to providing inputs to those living in camps, informal settlements, rented accommodations, or those squatting. - Community education and training efforts (especially for community leaders, local councils, husbands, and fathers) Community based beneficiary selection, clear selection criteria, accessible complaints mechanisms such as - public-ally posted anonymous comments and complaints email and phone numbers/Whats App. - Recruit female staff, all staff vetted and trained on code of conduct and preventing GBV.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

During the project implementation all the targeted beneficiaries will be ensured equal access to the provided assistance. They will be duly informed about the dates and locations where the inputs will be distributed and the technical support provided. Distribution locations will be selected in order to ensure the safety and security of both the beneficiaries and the project team. At the distribution site the necessary arrangements for each specific targeted group will be ensured (elderly, women etc.). In case some of the beneficiaries cannot access the distribution point due to their extreme vulnerability alternative ways of distribution will be identified as much as allowed by the security situation in the targeted areas. Throughout the project implementation Do No Harm will be a guiding principle for the project teams. For this purpose, they will be trained on humanitarian principle, human rights and other approaches that will ensure the successful implementation of the planned activities. In case any protection incidents occur during the implementation process, they will be duly reported to stakeholders.A focus on building partnerships with communities, beyond the direct provision of aid inputs, is a guiding principle in all work RF do. RF implements a framework for Accountability to Affected Populations that prioritizes leadership/governance; transparency; feedback and complaints; participation of communities and beneficiaries; and a design, monitoring and evaluation approach that engages affected populations and feeds learning back into the organization. Do No Harm framework that IRF adopt holds the safety of staff and beneficiaries, as well as accountability to beneficiaries, as key operating principles. RF will promote the dignity, agency, and safety of beneficiaries at all times. Protection considerations – mitigating the risk of harm, abuse, discrimination, exploitation, and neglect of beneficiaries – will be overriding components of all activities as well as a specific objective of some activities, which will be implemented in line with SPHERE guidelines and other international standards.Beneficiaries selection is based on a community mobilization mechanism where the criteria of selection shared with all stakeholders and the project advertisements will be published in the community. After conducting introductory workshops for community to explain the project outputs. Also, the project will complement the agriculture and livestock input activities that are targeting the same intervention areas. RF has a community feedback mechanism to receive all complains from local community. Giving the vulnerable households the ability to resilience will increase the opportunities of children to learn. RF through the field team will advocate to this among the targeted beneficiaries and further data will be collected in the endline to have indicators.
