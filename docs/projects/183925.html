Project Summary

The project will build the capacity of community governance structures as WASH committees, train and equip committees/communities to continue to operate and maintain WASH infrastructure, conduct water chlorination, and monitor chlorine levels. Also will conduct training to strengthen multi-sectoral approaches such as WASH in Health Facilities, WASH in schools, and WASH/GBV. NPO will preposition supplies for disasters like floods, epidemics, and other conflict-related displacements. Cash transfer modalities will be explored and piloted to cut logistical costs of transporting supplies in collaboration with other sectors according to the agreed point via the WASH sector.
The project will intervene with health lifesaving interventions for outbreak control of cholera, typhoid, hepatitis E, COVID19, and other vector control in communities, this is more than 20% of the health facilities do not have sanitation facilities in place, so the project will provide technical support and carry out health interventions prioritizing common diseases as cholera treatment centers and COVID-19 isolation centers upon specific demands and investigations.
The project will integrate all three components of WASH to deliver a complete response in IDPs, returnees and host communities in Elfashir rural, Tawilla, Kutum, Melit localities North Darfur in addition to affected by crises population and refugees in Gadaref and Khartoum. WASH interventions will be implemented for those affected by the risk of epidemics, and floods. All will contribute to curbing the morbidity and mortality amongst the targeted population.
The project will provide WASH services in the target localities to organize water existing sources management, rehabilitate some water points, constriction for new water yards, provide water containers with avail weekly water by tankers at schools and public places. Also, the project will conduct hygiene promotion for communities focusing on women & children and will provide shared household and public latrines.

Due to Humanitarian Response Plan 2021, WASH sector estimates that about 8 million people need water, sanitation and hygiene services. WASH survey in according to UNICEF report showed an increase in access to water by only 6% , Still, around 12 million people do not have access to safe drinking water and are at risk of disease. Children and their families are practicing open defecation, with the majority of the population being deprived of their basic rights. Around half of the schools in the Gadaref and North Darfur access to water to hand washing facilities is limited to only around 10% of all schools. Most of the schools have the facility, but no running water or sustainable supply of soap for hand washing . So the project will ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all for by 2030 to reach Sustainable Development Goal continue to strengthen systems and empowering local communities. The project will focus on the key priorities to sustain and increase access to basic water supply with a focus on the most vulnerable people in North Darfur and Gadaref States, expanding WASH services in all target state and engaging them with WASH technical. Also to sustain basic water supply with focus on water quality, water safety.Most WASH needs continue to be for protracted displacement, and host communities. With the highest severity rankings across most localities and camps in the targets states. Most of the community are access to pumped water, with a proportionate increase in its price, and the migration to unclean water sources, with clear public health implications. Unable to pay for water, families rely on public water sources or unreliable water sources.Water systems and chlorination: As cholera is spread through the consumption of contaminated water, ensuring water was safe to drink was critical in keeping community members healthy. Sanitation and hygiene is highest in need to ensuring that sanitation is available and that communities both understand and practice safe hygiene was critical in reducing the spread of cholera amongst communities, soap and hygiene kits also need to distribute to vulnerable communities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will consider the natural disasters and conflicts that had led to mass-movements of people, which has direct and indirect environmental consequences. Communities and their livelihoods are vulnerable to shocks such as drought or conflict when environmental resources are scarce and/or unevenly distributed. Integrating environmental issues into humanitarian programming is essential to improve outcomes and create multiple benefits. Reducing the pressure on natural resources can support livelihoods through job creation opportunities, nutrition and protection (by reducing lengthy walks for firewood collection), safeguard drinking water quality and quantity and provide shelter building materials. Human impact on the environment in Sudan is most visible in the areas of deforestation/ desertification, overcultivation and over-grazing, and over-extraction of water.The project will continue promoting trees planting, replace the use of fired bricks, awareness raising on environmental protection and support Sold Waste Management schemes, and train communities on Biodegradable material, include usage of fuel efficient cooking techniques (e.g. pre-soaking beans, sheltering cooking fires) , enable National authorities to take the lead, and coordinate with WES on Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM) on monitoring of water levels.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project will consider :- Consider installing lights near the latrines, especially is these are communal or away from homes or alternatives such as providing torches for each household. - Do not place facilities near possible perpetrators. N.B. The police and armed forces are often seen as perpetrators of violations. Whether they provide a reassuring feeling or instill fear depends on the location. It is important to consult the community and potential beneficiaries about their preferences.- Consider ways to reduce/manage/cope with these threats, including location, accompaniment and arrange appropriate policing if required (as water monitors)- If an individual does not feel safe or dignified in using the latrine, (s)he may go elsewhere, potentially exposing themselves or others to harm.- Physically separate and label the latrines “male” & “female”- Ensure latrine design accounts for children (size of pits may present a safety risk for children)- It is preferable that latrines and showers can be locked from the inside to ensure privacy. - Consider that women and children are the most likely to be collecting water, and might be waiting in the sun for hours for a turn to fill their jerry can. Establish safe and shaded waiting areas where possible. - Schedule water collection times to limit overcrowding at the water points; collection in shifts.- Children should not carry jerrycans. However, assuming they will be forced to do so, make sure that jerrycans are not so large as to cause injury. - Ask individuals collecting water when they would prefer taps/pumps to be open. Recognize that different people have different work schedule, women and girls who are typically the water collectors have specific times when they are busy making meals.- Ensure that you have discussions on latrine/shower design with various groups and adapt the design
