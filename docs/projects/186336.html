Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to safe and quality education for crisis-affected children, with a particular focus on girls. The action will facilitate access to education for out of school children and adolescents to enable them to obtain the basis for integration into the formal education system. It is urgent to re-engage out-of-school children with the education system since, as these children grow up, their chance and ability to resume school decreases. The project will address gendered barriers to education and sensitization on gender-based violence, through teacher training, awareness campaigns and the promotion of equal and safe education for girls and boys, regardless of their status (IDPs, returnees or host communities.) To achieve this, NRC will ensure close collaboration with the decentralized services of basic education in Cameroon, and will ensure a conflict-sensitive approach while ensuring respect for Do No Harm principles. The project result will be achieved through activities aimed at strengthening the capacity of education actors at the community level and ensuring the protection of children and adolescents. To ensure a consistent and comprehensive response, the project will be developed through the lenses of gender and conflict mitigation, which are two concomitant perspectives to the dissemination of the principles of protection in the project. At the end of the action 30 000 persons including IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community’s children will benefit the above package of education assistance in order to scale up their access to qualities education opportunities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Access to quality education: Cameroon is under a lot of pressure due to the devastating effects of three ongoing humanitarian crises. The mutant and rampant insecurity in the Lake Chad Basin region affecting Far North region of Cameroon, the socio-political instability in the Central African Republic that maintains refugees in Cameroon, and the violent conflict in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon. Violence, attacks and threats against education, population displacement, and limited capacity of the school system and of communities to absorb extra student populations has placed almost 1.9 million school aged children in need of some form of assistance in education in Cameroon. The COVID-19 pandemic further worsened an already precarious education situation and created severe child protection risks including for the youth.During the month of September, the rainy season in Cameroon caused mass flooding in various regions, mostly in Far North. This trend is expected to worsen over the coming years, hence preventing access to some schools. Cameroon is dealing with multiple crises, hosting large numbers of refugees and internally displaced people, facing food insecurity and being exposed to climate, socio-economic, and political shocks. The Covid 19 pandemic, and containment measures, will trigger secondary ‘shocks’ that negatively impact educational continuity and access particularly for displaced people .The Far North region is the poorest of Cameroon with the lowest education rate. The primary completion rate is estimated at only 58 % . The Region has been classified by the Government of Cameroon (GoC) as a priority education zone. Needs of school infrastructure and equipment :The International Organization of Migration’s March 2021 Displacement Tracking Monitoring estimates that nearly 514 741 people have been displaced due to conflict in the Far North Including 341 535 Internally Displaced Persons, 124 310 Returnees and 48 902 Out-of-camp Refugees . 62% of these population are children with several & critical education needs. Due to the ongoing crisis, classrooms have been destroyed due to vandalism and obsolescence of functional schools are overcrowded, which are suboptimal for teaching and learning. Following the Cameroon HNO 2021, In the Logone et Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga divisions, 62 schools are still closed, and 50 others have been destroyed for years and have never been rebuilt, affecting about 35,000 students due to the security context. In many of such schools, lack of infrastructure and teachers are reasons for some children to leave school. Needs of teachers: There are currently 281 schools with just 01 Teacher. This translates to a pupil ratio of 1 Teacher /162 Children. This situation has led to a number of children and youth being out-of-school for several years. As these children grow up, their chance and ability to resume school decreases. Communities used to hire local teachers to complement the insufficiency of trained teachers. However, these locally hired teachers lack the technical background and skills to deliver quality teaching for primary school pupils and are regularly underpaid by the community. Need of alternative Education program: There are few flexible, alternative education opportunities that accommodate those who do not fit into the formal system. There is no data reflecting the extent to which protection risks, security issues, and gender-based violence (GBV) affect education; however, an education assessment conducted by NRC in the Far North found that cultural factors combined with the stresses of displacement exacerbate the risk of child marriage, especially for girls.
