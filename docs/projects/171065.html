Project Summary

Verified documents and evidence based reports indicate that protracted conflict crisis and repeated natural disasters lead Somali children to unknown future. About 1 million and half Children have been out of school from March to August 2020 as a result of COVID-19 preventative measures enforced by the Government of Somalia (GoS). Somalia has one of the world’s lowest school enrolment rates. Only 30 per cent of children aged six-13 years are enrolled in primary education and only 26 per cent of children aged 14-17 years are enrolled in secondary education (Somalia Education Cluster Needs Analysis Note, October 2020). FENPS team in the field reported 9 schools that hosting 2653 children (1460 boys and 1193 girls) were submerged by flood in Beletweyne in September this year and children fled from the area with their parents. Hiran Multi-cluster Joint Assessment for Shocks-affected People September 2020 reported that 39% of school age children don’t go to the schools due the different factors including: 39% of the children in the age of school don’t go because they can’t scholastic materials even if the school is free, 34% due to fees being unaffordable, 24% due to the long distance of the nearest school and 3% due to the security concerns. On the other hand, Bandir Education Cluster Partners reported that Corona Virus lockdown has affected 147 schools which hosting 30,944 children (16,255 boys and 14,689 girls) and 454 teachers (327 male and 127 female) ( Situation Report on Education in Emergencies (EiE) in Banadir region, June 2020).
Across the country children experience violations and protection issues against their basic human rights. The Report of the Secretary-General on Children and Armed Conflict 2020 states that in 20194, 1,442 children (53 girls) were recruited and/or used mainly by non-state armed groups, that 227 girls reported sexual violence and that 1,158 children (including 93 girls) were abducted. From January to June 2020, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified 1,184 grave violations, registering the recruitment of 546 children (30 girls), the killing and maiming of 469 children (107 girls) and 29 attacks on schools during the reporting period5. The majority of these incidents are attributed to non-state armed actors. Furthermore, children with disabilities meet timely harassment while small number of them has access to education in Somalia. Rapid assessment about the status of children with disabilities in Somalia that commissioned by the government of Somalia indicate that children with disabilities experience different violations, including physical assault, being pelted with stones, being called names, insulted and generally treated with disrespect, being cursed or being prevented from playing with other children. Somalia Education Cluster Needs Analysis Note 2021 also indicate that an estimated 1.4 million school aged children (45% girls) are in need of humanitarian assistance to retain children in schools or enroll in schools.
Education is lifesaving and protects children at risk of abuse and exploitation. The purpose of the proposed program is to provide 5,000 (2,500 girls and 2,500 boys) vulnerable children at the crisis affected areas in Hiran and Banadir regions of Somalia with safe and inclusive educational assistance that contribute to the protection and safeguarding of children through rehabilitation of 60 classroom with friendly and gender responsive WASH facilities, distribution of friendly teaching and learning materials, provision of school feeding and hygiene promotion supplies, supporting 100 teachers (60 male and 4o female) and 70 CECs (35 female 35 male) to improve teaching and school supervision work and establishing child protection mechanism in all targeted schools.

Mitigation Measures

In order to address possible risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, FENPS will do the following important things (1) establish girls’ empowerment clubs in each school, (2) developing and confirming that each school has exercised available Codes of Conduct for safe guarding and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), (3) conduct monthly forums/ awareness campaigns for prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), and (4) establishing school based mechanism/system for reporting sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) incidents, if any that is secure and accessible to all in each school.For the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities, FENPS will (1) conduct regular mentoring and consultation with marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls,(2) schools, WASH facilities, hygiene promotion supplies, teaching and learning materials and other furniture in schools will designed and distributed in child-friendly, inclusive and gender responsive methods, (3) regular assessment will be conducted to identify address individual needs of project beneficiaries, and (4) referral pathway mechanism will be established for the affected people targeted

Please provide further explanation

The project will give highest consideration to the children with disabilities and other people at risk. FENPS will make sure that schools are friendly and inclusive environments through establishing classrooms that easy to access by all children, designing and distribution materials that are responsive and acceptable by all, mentoring and guiding children with disabilities and their parents, raising the awareness of the community on the importance of supporting children with disabilities and also training teachers on best methods to mentor and teach children with disabilities. FENPS will do regular monitoring and supervision of the program activities. Data of beneficiaries will be collected and recorded properly through Dis aggregation of gender, disability, location, group, etc. Progress reports, lessons learnt and feedback from the phases of program interventions will be documented shared widely.
