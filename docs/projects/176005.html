Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to prevent famine and provide life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihood protection and Nutrition support through enhanced food production for 55000 individuals at risk of food insecurity (IPC 3 and 4) in Lainya and Morobo Counties.
ACROSS proposes to distribute emergency food production kits (vegetable seeds, cultivation tools and fishing kits) to approximately 10000 food insecure households in Lainya and Morobo Counties. The counties are currently experiencing IPC Level 3 and 4 food insecurity, the vegetable kits will support food production during the onset of rainy season. The kits will greatly enhance food consumption and dietary diversity and boost livelihoods for targeted population, and contribute significantly towards efforts to prevent famine. This project is designed to target areas that have not received any support and mainly IDP returnees from neighbouring Uganda; in this counties we will target 50% IDPs and 50% host community households as beneficiaries.
In addition to distributing emergency food production kits, ACROSS staff will provide a series of tailored and integrated trainings in vegetable cultivation
2000 people will participate in vegetable farming training. Trainings will be delivered to both men and women, and also integrated messages on nutrition and cooking, hygiene promotion including latrine use, safe drinking water and prevention and treatment of malaria and diarrhea, gender equality and protection principles.
With the aim to reduce suffering, morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition among targeted beneficiaries, ACROSS through Nutrition component will increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative and curative nutrition-specific services for children under 5, and women in both Lainya and Morobo, hence contributing to the overarching strategic objective of the humanitarian community. Linkages and integrated approaches are essential for addressing the key drivers of acute malnutrition.
ACROSS will also have coordination and joint programming with other partners offering Health, WASH, Education and Protection and this will be an enhanced for nutrition-sensitive interventions that will ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services, further decrease the prevalence of acute malnutrition, and, ultimately, reduce morbidity and mortality.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian needs in South Sudan are largely stirred up by the impact of conflict, impact of climate change and recently by the outbreak of the Corona Virus pandemic (COVID-19) which has left communities overwhelmed with complex socioeconomic consequences including severe economic reductions, rising in prices of basic supplies, loss of livelihoods, and lack of nutrition in young children resulting to negative impacts on their physical and mental wellbeing, as such, they are prone to and more vulnerable to common and infectious diseases like acute respiratory infection, measles, Cholera and diarrhea. Around 1.4 million South Sudanese children under age 5 and 483,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be extremely malnourished in 2021 (South Sudan HNO,2021).The December, 2020, IPC indicate an estimated 686,000 individuals in Central Equatoria state facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity of which 480,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 206,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. While Morobo and Lainya Counties the intended project locations were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity respectively. The food insecurity in Morobo and Lainya Counties has resulted from the insecurities experienced in the locations. Furthermore, movement restrictions linked to the COVID-19 pandemic with its consequential negative impacts on markets led to the deflation of the local currency resulting to high prices of food, as well, the excessive rainfall causing unusual flooding and thus impacted negatively on crop yields. The enforcement of the COVID-19 control measures has continued to affect livelihoods and markets. Therefore, affecting household incomes, reduced buying power, and limiting access to food which is a serious threat to the wellbeing of the population and a likely cause of Malnutrition.With the expected deterioration in the food security situation from April to July 2021, with an estimated 778,000 people (51.6% of the population)-(IPC, December 2020), likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute insecurity, Morobo County classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while Lainya classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, 551,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and thus 227,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. This is projected to the typical lean season factors such as depletion of harvest stocks, high food prices, likelihood of insecurity in these locations (Morobo and Lainya). Therefore, the dire need for this intervention to improve the livelihoods of the beneficiaries as well improved nutrition needs of children, pregnant and lactating mothers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will establish a feedback and compliant mechanism that will enable beneficiaries and other stakeholders to identify and report on feedback and complaints regarding the project to enable early addressing. the FCM system will set in accordance to the beneficiaries preferred modalities of voicing their pinons. The FCM will follow a simple process whereby the project team raise awareness among stakeholders about the need and the reason for establishing the FCM, all the stakeholders with beneficiaries being at the centre agree on the method for collecting feedback, and then Chose a FCM Focal Person. The complaints handling mechanism will then be set up in manner that acknowledges a complaint made or a concern raised, which are eventually entered into the complaint and Feedback database which makes sure that each complainant receives a response and appropriate action.The nutrition intervention will follow the CMAM guideline to ensure the community is at the heart of the intervention embracing the community engagement model, emphasizing participants’ contribution in refining the program through the Complaint and feedback mechanism. Capacity building and mobilization in communities by strengthening mother to mother to support groups (MtMSG), locally recruiting and supporting community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) to promote increased participation and best practice of desired behaviors, keeping in mind the need to connect and establish a reciprocal sense of trust and respect among community members. This encourages multi-channel communications to inform, sensitize, and motivate key groups with information about priority Nutrition behaviors and services. Program EC material will be designed to be culturally relevant, and acceptable in accordance with the community norms.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Our greatest asset in risk management is the strong levels of community acceptance we have built in greater Yei. This acts as a powerful protective factor for our personnel, facilities and assets. ACROSS does not take security risks lightly, will engage with NGO Forum, INSO and other mechanisms for keeping up to date with security alerts and warnings. We have radios and satellite phones to maintain communication in areas where the network coverage does not reach, and have included a few lines in our budget for security-related costs. ACROSS will recruit project staff from within the localities who are aware of security situations of the their area and are often among the first to hear about clashes. ACROSS is aware of the current insecurity along some of the roads leading out of Lainya and Morobo and also within the surround areas, however will plan around these routes so as to not endanger staff. Staff in field and headquarters have undergone recent security training, and we are currently updating our policies and procedures to ensure duty of care to our staff.The project will used the local people to be a registrar together with the local chief in identifying the affected population and register them. The distribution will be done at the center of the communities in which the local chief will provided the team that will organized the distribution center, there will be no private distribution between the beneficiaries and distribution team. All invited participants will attend and complete the training and that the communities will remain peaceful and stable. In our experience delivering participatory and practical trainings, attendance rates are usually quite high, if conducted in accessible areas, and mobilization is done well. Assume that people will implement the skills they learnt at the training. In our experience, this is quite a small risk, it can be reduced with adequate sensitization. Additionally since people have been receiving this kind of assistance over the years, they have changed their mindsets. Delivering kits to beneficiaries in a timely manner assumes smooth logistical support and minimal disruptions from weather and insecurity/conflict along the roads.
