Project Summary

The project will utilize the humanitarian pipe line for crops, vegetables and fisheries kits from the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). Where markets are functional market based interventions such as direct purchases and or seed and tool fairs will be sought as informed by respective market assessments. These activities will be complimented by capacity building/training modules in the specific livelihoods so as to improve the targeted farmer production, productivity capacities and improve yields and returns. The fisheries training will enable target beneficiaries utilize their kits in the proper way and also improve their knowledge and skills in fish processing, preservation and marketing. The crops and vegetable production training will be based on the crop production seasonal calendar which includes land preparation, planting weeding, pest and disease management, harvesting, post-harvest management and marketing. Using the training of trainers (ToT) approach, the project will target 7,500 crop/vegetable farmers and 4,500 fisher folk for assorted livelihoods inputs support as well as training and engaging community based extension workers to provide the technical support to these farmers while they replicate the training to the other beneficiaries. The proposed interventions will also train targeted households with trainings on approved agronomic practices, pests and disease control, post-harvest handling and value addition and linkages to markets and other value chain actors In addition, 1,500 HHs will be supported through unconditional/conditional cash transfer programming especially in flood and conflicted affected areas. The action will also target 1250 HHs with small ruminant (goat/sheep) or poultry restocking through livestock voucher modality, 750 women, youth and men will be engaged in VSLAs/IGAs while 450 HHs will be engaged in natural resource management and climate adaptation activities.
To ensure integration the intervention will target nutrition & health centers, schools and mother garden’s locations conveniently with/without of access and proximity of a water source. Additional community gardens will be promoted at locations such as schools, health facilities where they will be jointly tended by students and community volunteers. The FSL interventions will be designed taking into consideration and incorporating mainstreaming of protection, safety, GBV prevention, Prevention of Sexual Abuse & Exploitation and conflict sensitivity analysis. The project teams will work closely with local authorities to identify persons with disabilities both men and women so that they are supported. This number is not expected to exceed 5% of the 10,500 HH targeted. This ensures participants are selected based on needs alone, a rigorous community-based targeting process with participation and inclusion of vulnerable groups such as female and child headed households, disabled and the elderly. The proposed project will also take into consideration cross-cutting issues like environmental protection, HIV/AIDS, community-based participatory planning, gender, nutrition will be considered during project implementation.
All agricultural and fisheries activities will be implemented in collaboration with the State Ministry of Agriculture in order to ensure that government extension workers benefit from and also contribute to the technical components that will be offered by the project by so doing building capacities that will continue to be utilized after project closure. Community based extension workers such as lead farmers will be trained to continue supporting other farmers during and after project closure.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of the population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021 (2021 FSNMS+HNO). The situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks resulting in the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country, as compared to 1.7 million in 2020 (DTM Round 11). Climate change continues to impact different sectors, particularly agriculture, which is one of the most climate-sensitive economic sectors (Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Recovery). Above normal rainfall for the third consecutive year in 2021 led to prolonged flooding. An estimated 835,000 people were affected by severe flooding between May and Dec 2021, and 80 per cent of those affected were from Jonglei, Unity and Upper Nile states. Warrap, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei states were the most affected in terms of crop and livestock production. FAO assessment indicated that 65,107 hectares of land planted with cereals had been damaged, with an estimated loss of 37,624 tons of grain in the flood-affected areas (FAO South Sudan, Impact of floods on food security, 20 December 2021). The cumulative impact of recurrent flooding contributed to the destruction and damage to water facilities, leaving vulnerable people in urgent need of water, sanitation and hygiene services, and intensified existing vulnerabilities of affected people, which include high poverty rates, limited access to basic services including health and education, high prevalence of disease outbreaks, and widespread displacement. An estimated 8.3 million people are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the2022 lean season as shocks appear to be intensifying. There are 13 counties with extreme levels of food insecurity in 2022, compared to 6 in 2021. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. Compelled with response to flooding, humanitarian organizations had to re-program in-country support resulting in a reduction of food assistance to internally displaced people, refugees and communities in acute food and livelihood crises. These unmet needs increased vulnerability for populations in 2022 likely to result in severe food insecurity. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season representing a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million in 2021. An estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women are expected to be at risk of acute malnourishment. GAM prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15 % was reported in people in twenty-six counties in six states (FSNMS+ round 27 results). A continuation and increase in sub-national violence reported in many states across the country will likely continue to disrupt livelihoods and trade and contribute to an increase in humanitarian needs in 2022. GBV remains a threat; women and girls are exposed to risks when carrying out their routine activities, which is further exacerbated due to flooding that limits livelihood opportunities (2021 FSMNS+). Many violations go under-reported. Women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities (PWDs) are particularly vulnerable, exposing them to risks of exploitation (FSNMS+ 2021).

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The proposed intervention will use cash and vouchers where local markets arefunctioning and able to meet demand. Cash transfers serves as an appropriateand powerful alternative to in-kind direct distributions of food and non-fooditems. Cash and vouchers are also more dignified and flexible form ofassistance, since beneficiaries have the freedom to choose what they need.Cash/voucher can also be delivered in a timely and cost-effective assistance tocrisis-affected populations, while at the same time supporting the localeconomy.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

NRC conducts market functionality assessment prior to designing any cash orvoucher interventions. Cash and vouchers will only be considered by NRC where localmarkets are functioning and able to meet demand. NRC also conducts securityrisk assessment and price monitoring in addition to market functionalityassessments prior to any cash programming interventions. In locations wherethere is no market integration and where markets are not functioning NRC willnot conduct cash/voucher transfers in the locations. NRC has comprehensivecash based intervention standard operating procedures with guidelines ondecision making process for cash interventions. Teams work with protectionstaff to conduct a comprehensive protection risk analysis prior to any cashprogramming, especially to vulnerable people in meaningfully accessingdistribution sites (including safe return from distribution points, and ability tosafely store cash). Protection analyses, based on consultation with affectedcommunities and especially vulnerable people, will seek to balance the needs totarget the most vulnerable, especially women, with the risks of offering themcash resources. NRC teams and other protection partners will work withcommunities and beneficiaries to establish plans to mitigate and addressprotection risks.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Reducing vulnerability, ensuring safety and dignity of program participants is key to NRC’s activities across South Sudan, including in this project. It is why protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and child safeguarding comprises core of its accountability standards. To that end, the project orients all staff and volunteers on core PSEA and safeguarding principals, with any major breach grounds for disciplinary actions, including termination and criminal reporting, where deemed appropriate. At the programmatic level, a key initiative will be NRC’s linkage with the GBV sub-Cluster to jointly develop the toolkit and implement (via theM&E team and Protection staff) randomized GBV safety audits across program sites in all NRC’s sectors to mitigate GBV risks in programming. Modalities will include checklist and observational approach, safety mapping, and focus group discussions to identify safety risks and concerns of women and girls, including site location, timing of activities, threats during travel, availability of gender friendly spaces (including WASH facilities) with adequate protection, and overall treatment by staff and volunteers. Further Do No Harm actions include engaging with community leaders and local authorities to ensure safety during distribution process, ensuring girls and women do not face domestic threats due to participation in program activities, and ensuring activities are not conducted in cattle grazing areas. Targeted protection interventions include Psychological First Aid (PFA) training to all health and nutrition staff to serve as effective first responders during the immediate aftermath of conflict (inter-communal violence) or disaster (flooding) for displaced people (especially women and children) affected by acute shock and trauma to reduce initial distress and nurture short- and long-term adaptive capacities. Additionally, through collaboration with the GBV sub-Cluster, staff will be trained on rape and SGBV case identification, reporting and handling, with active referral network established for comprehensive MHPSS treatment and case management. Equal and impartial access to assistance will be done through having rigorous targeting criteria that selects participants based on severity of needs and existing vulnerability, using trained and impartial selection committees. The project targets participants along pre-defined age and sex disaggregated criteria with deliberate focus given to include women and child headed households, persons with disabilities, IDPs, and returnees. Project-level data will also be collected along that disaggregation, which will allow NRC to assess whether individuals withparticular vulnerably were reached as envisioned within the initial design. All project participants will be rigorously oriented on community feedback and complaints response mechanism (CRM) and encouraged to use it not only for feedback, but in particular to report cases of abuse, and other related cases of right-violation. With CRM cases disaggregated along sensitive and non-sensitive in nature, NRC will support communities to ensure protection and seek redress for violations of rights, including through linkage and collaboration with protection cluster focal points.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

NRC will continue participated in joint/multi-sectoral (IRNA) assessments in various locations affected by floods with reports shared with respective clusters for response planning. The NRC emergency mobile team will also continue monitoring the situation and displacement country wide through clusters, partners, ERRM consortium (NRC, Medair & DRC), REACH, SFP, partners on ground, etc. NRC mobile team then responds for WASH, EiE, SNFI and FSL based on the displacement triggers through conducting a rapid assessment followed with a response based on the assessment findings. Through Another immediate support provided by NRC has been through multi-purpose cash transfer to cover basic household gaps to among others flood affected communities. In terms of medium to long-term flood mitigation interventions, NRC programs works closely with Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR) mechanisms to map out communal vulnerabilities such as floods, identify areas or locations that are at most as risk come up with local response and mitigation measures. These measures include using CFW/FFA approaches to construct flood control dykes, tree planting, shallow wells and road dyke construction among others. The community is also trained on various aspects related to floods such as causes, community DRR plans, response and mitigation measures. In addition, NRC builds capacity and promotes climate adaption practices such climate-smart agriculture, such as agro-forestry, tree planting, minimum tillage, zero tillage, mulching among others all geared to mitigating floods.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

NRC in collaboration with WFP will provide food assistance to ensure assistance to the most at risk populations according to the IPC acute or emergency phase classification, specifically population classified under IPC 4 & 5. Returnees, IDPs and host community populations that are classified in IPC 3 and 4 that have access to land for farming will be targeted with crop and vegetable production, as water is readily available to support production. Farmers will be trained on vegetable production, irrigation techniques, and making compost manure and fruit tree production. NRC will promote horticulture through extension support, farmer field schools, and demo plot approaches. Households will be encouraged to begin fast maturing vegetable gardening around their homestead during the rainy season. Men will be encouraged and consulted to ensure that they will support their women counterparts in nursery management and vegetable gardening. NRC intends to leverage experience on integrated pest management interventions, including the current and previous projects to tackle newly reported desert locusts’ infestation. On the other-hand the fisher-folk will be supported with fishing gear composed of twine, hooks and monofilament in addition to training on fish preservation, processing and hygiene. Livestock farmers will be supported with livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns as well as capacity building and installation of the cold chain for the Community Animal Health Workers
