Project Summary

This project is a multi-pronged approach, serving at both individual and community levels in both basic sectors and Livelihood Support for the target beneficiaries and Early Recovery by stimulating the economic growth of the target communities.
The project is going to be implemented within 12 months to achieve:
The Overall Objective is To reform and strengthen the performance of the local economic system and scaling-up the community resilience in NW Syria.
BINAA will work to achieve this objective through four main interventions:
1st: Providing an MSME grant of 1,000 USD to 500 beneficiaries to establish new business projects or repair or resume existing ones that are heavily affected by the war or the current Covid-19 pandemic.
Initial beneficiaries (M/F), women-headed households, PWDs, and other vulnerable segments such as families having child-labour are a priority, to be targeted, where 2-day sessions, be conducted to develop the Business Plans "BPs" and apply to the grant, and then, BINAA team be evaluating the submitted proposals, select the best projects to be supported, and identify the final beneficiaries of the grants.
Afterward, providing to the finally selected beneficiaries a 5-day Training Program on Entrepreneurship Skills to build their capacities in managing and sustaining their businesses.
The grant is to be provided in the form of CASH for assets and into several payments to ensure that the beneficiary is committing, achieving good progress, and being businesslike to receive the next payments. Additionally, BINAA, to provide 200 USD to the most successful 50 projects, (representing 10% of BNFs), as additional monetary bonuses, be given based on hard work and noticeable progress.
2nd: Providing Vocational Training (VT) for 300 males and females and PWDs, paid for three months, and then, providing Internship Training within the private sector, also paid for 3 months, to link them to the market.
The appropriate professions and training are according to two groups:
1. The professions do not require any qualifications e.g. Hairdressing and Sewing.
2. The professions require relevant experience e.g. Computer Maintenance and Web Development.
Three months paid internship are to be provided by contracting with business owners in the private sector, who are willing to accommodate the trainees and provide them with the Internship and BINAA will provide allowances to the beneficiaries for three months of VT and three months of Internship, $120 a month (totaling $360), which is empowering the beneficiary to gain the trust of the employer and gain practical experience.
Within the continuous Covid-19 pandemic, BINAA be supporting and ensuring all precautionary measures through disinfecting meeting venues before and after each meeting, distributing masks to attendees, printing guiding instructions and caution signs everywhere, and also, selecting suitable venues that be primarily accessible by women and PwDs and having all comfort facilities for both genders.

3 - Needs

According to HMAP Report in 2022, the median value of daily per-capita income is less than 20 USD a month. This is extremely concerning, given that this would be a daily income rate of less than half of what is usually considered as being in extreme poverty - which is defined as living on less than $1.90 per day. (76% MHHs, 52% FHHs) of interviews mentioned that they suffer from a lack of employment opportunities. (46% MHHS, 54% FHHS) of respondents said that there is a lack of skills.BINAA carried out an ERL Need Assessment in November 2022, it was found that the most requested professions are programming, sewing, home electricity, office work, and mobile maintenance. Lack of capital and COVID-19 are some of the most influential factors causing the shutting down of businesses. 50% said that their communities have a hardship in insuring expert manpower.BINAA also implemented a market needs assessment in November 2022, all KIs reported that there are roads that need light repairs and that some roads are unusable.Regarding the livelihood condition, more than 85 % of IDPs face poor economic conditions (their monthly income may not reach even 20 USD/360 TL). In turn, 98% of respondents report livelihood support as a top priority. More than 90 % of Women and 98 % of PwDs have less opportunity to integrate within the local markets where they have no access to livelihood to run a profitable business and rely only on humanitarian aid.

4 - Implementation of activities

First, BINAA to coordinate with the ERL Cluster. And mapping all stakeholders, their roles, influences, and impact to set necessary participatory mechanisms.During implementation, active community mobilization. To this end, Need assessment is essential. Then, conducting introductory workshops aiming to share information, and sign MoUs outlining the roles and responsibilities of all parties and how the LCs can support smooth implementation.BNFs selection is based on community mobilization mechanisms and focuses to target vulnerable segments including women, elders, child vulnerability, and PWDs. The beneficiaries are delivered the grant in CASH for Assets modality and be in two phases, half number in each, and monitoring the local market regularly to ensure Do No Harm if injecting cash in one go, and developing the second phase based on lessons learned to support the most appropriate projects or professions. The VT BNFs are delivered vocational training (theoretical and practical) for 3 months within equipped VT centers for the prioritized professions. Post VT, trainees be connected to the private sector and receive Internship training paid for 3 months to ensure employment and sustainability.MEAL, to monitor and evaluate this project based on the work plan including logical framework, M&E Plan, Baseline, Endline, PDM, and indicators performance tracking table (IPTT) with disaggregated data by gender, age, and PWDs, all are to evaluate the overall impact of the project.
