Project Summary

The project will contribute to improved aid coordination and delivery (both humanitarian and longer-term recovery planning). To continue informing the humanitarian programming cycle with field evidence, REACH will provide information management and assessment support to the humanitarian coordination architecture. Capitalizing on the work implemented in 2016, 2017, 2018, and 2019, REACH will provide the inter cluster coordination group and humanitarian partners with support for multi-sector needs assessment analyzing gender, ability and age disaggregated data in both government and non-government controlled areas, in addition to continuing the provision of ad hoc information management technical expertise.
Initial findings from the Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2019 indicate a downward trend in the frequency of security related incidents and conflict related civilian casualties in 2019, most likely linked to increased political engagement between Ukraine and the Russian Federation. Overall indicators in the target areas showed slight improvements compared to 2018. The proportion of HHs reporting shelling as a protection concerns in the 5km area dropped from 86% to 60% and the proportion of HHs that are severely or moderately food insecure in the same geography went from 13% to 9%. However, some areas are showing continued deterioration as in 2018, specifically healthcare where the proportion of HHs reporting challenges in accessing health facilities increased from 43% to 54%. These overall trends did not change the geographic difference found in previous years. Using a multi-sector needs indicator inspired from the Joint Interagency Assessment Group, the proportion of HHs in need in rural areas within 5km of the contact line was close to 2 times higher than those in urban areas further away (44% vs 23%). It is crucial that timely and accurate information about the severity of these needs continue to be made available to aid actors.
In line with commitments from the Grand Bargain, the Ukraine humanitarian community with support from REACH has been able to rely on ‘Improved joint and impartial needs assessments’ to develop the humanitarian needs overview and humanitarian response: to further increase the localization of assistance REACH will continue to strengthen local level capacities in information management and Geographic Information Systems through its ‘IM_HUB’ which is run out of three technical centers in Slovyansk, Severodonetsk and Mariupol.
In line with Accountability to Affected Populations commitments, REACH will also collect data on the knowledge about/availability of complaints and response mechanisms in conflict-affected areas where aid actors operate, and maintain its efforts to ensure survey participants can raise complaints to an anonymous hotline with the phone number distributed after all surveys.

1 - Needs

Humanitarian funding keeps being steadily reduced, with only 42% of the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan funded as of October. In order to target the needs of the most vulnerable including the elderly, (single) female headed households and families with children, interventions in 2020 will have to be guided by solid evidence.The 2019 HNO has shown that after 6 years of active conflict along the contact line, people in eastern Ukraine continue to face conflict related challenges. Continued conflict poses major protection risks to populations living closest to the line of contact. Restrictions of movements between non-government controlled and government-controlled areas continue to disrupt access to services and markets and limited perspectives for a peaceful resolution to the crisis have created a protracted humanitarian situation. While there have been encouraging signs of political engagement between Ukraine and the Russian Federation lately (exchange of prisoners, agreement to the ‘Steinmeier Formula’), the likelihood of a short to medium term resolution of the conflict remains minimal. Based on preliminary figures, as of October 2019, there remain more than 3 million people in need. Access to reliable and timely information is critical for empowering aid agencies to meet the multifaceted and evolving needs of women, men, girls and boys affected by conflict in both GCA and NGCA. Since 2015, REACH Ukraine has supported clusters (Shelter Cluster, 2015 Shelter & NFI Needs Assessment; Protection Cluster, 2019 Protection Assessment of Isolated Settlements Along the Line of Contact ), UN agencies (UNICEF, 2016 WASH Assessment), and the HCT (REACH, Interagency Vulnerability Assessment 2016; Multi Sector Needs Assessment 2016, Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2017, MSNA 2017, TA 2018 and 2019) in designing and implementing multi sector needs assessments to inform strategic level documents including UN, donor and NGO strategies. This was achieved due to REACH’s comparative advantage in collecting primary data and capacities in the analysis of secondary data in both GCA and NGCA. The need for detailed, gender and age disaggregated data will persist in 2020 especially given the persisting vulnerability of specific household categories (single headed female, elderly, people living close to the line of contact, and people that cross the contact line). Only through the implementation of large-scale household surveys using similar indicators as those calculated annually since 2016 can the humanitarian situation be monitored accurately and reliably for the benefit of aid programming. This approach will complement programmatic local level assessments funded outside of the HRP. The project will continue engaging with the inter cluster coordination group and NGO partners to ensure that the data collection tools are adjusted where necessary.These assessments will also provide significant field evidence with regards to accountability to affected populations. Based on agreed indicators from the inter-agency standing committee (IASC) on AAP, REACH assessments will continue monitoring the implementation of accountability mechanism in the field as per good practice from 2017, 2018 and 2019. REACH will also continue to monitor beneficiary preferences for different modalities of assistance – especially cash and whether it continues to be an appropriate modality to meet the needs of the most vulnerable in conflict-affected areas close the contact line.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Humanitarian aid actors have improved access to reliable evidence to inform their programming Activity 1: Implementation of two comprehensive multi-sector needs assessment in government controlled and non-government-controlled areas. Capitalizing on the assessment work completed in 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019 REACH will conduct follow up assessments using comparable indicators and sampling methods to inform strategic level aid programming. In the GCA REACH will replicate the sampling strategy of 2018 to include 7 strata using a household survey representative at a 95% confidence interval and 5% margin of error. The strata will cover areas within 5km of the LoC, areas between 5 and 20km and areas beyond 20km of the LoC disaggregated by large urban, urban and rural settlements. Given the HCT’s commitment to accountability to affected populations, REACH will keep a module on AAP which focuses on satisfaction of aid provided, communication with communities and interaction of aid workers.REACH will also seek to implement a specific study of IDPs living in the large urban areas of Donetsk Government Controlled areas to address an ongoing information gap. While macro-level data is available through the IOM National Monitoring System and the World Bank Study of IDPs and Veterans, city level information on the challenges IDPs face in accessing housing, employment and social support are seldom available. Capitalizing on research efforts undertaken by the Joint IDP Profiling Service in 2019 in Luhansk oblast, REACH proposes to implement a similar activity in the large IDP hosting cities of Mariupol, Kramatorsk, Bakhmut and Severodonetsk.In NGCA, REACH will continue its area-based type assessments by gathering information through 6 different data collection methods including: household surveys, multiple types of key informant interviews and focus group discussions. The assessment will have a significant component targeting the 600,000-individual people who cross the LoC on a monthly basis to access administrative, social and financial services. This will provide information to support cross contact line programming, including for actors who may be able to access NGCA population and provide humanitarian assistance (e.g. psychosocial or legal support). Activity 2: Building field level capacities of local and international partners in GCA. REACH will continue capacity building workshops for NGOs and local authorities on 3 information management topics: i) mobile data collection through Kobo Collect ii) data analysis using Microsoft Excel and iii) geographic information systems using Quantum GIS. Following the 3 core principles of the IM_HUB (using open source/widely available solutions, small training groups and applied learning methods), REACH will strengthen IM skills of organisations and authorities supporting the response in eastern Ukraine. REACH will also provide standing capacities for ad-hoc partner requests. The protection of all respondents is REACH’s first priority. All assessments will be designed, implemented and reviewed through multi-stakeholder engagement to ensure the highest quality possible and respect of AAP commitments. Qualitative data collection methods will be employed in a gender sensitive manner to ensure that women of all ages and other vulnerable groups are comfortable sharing information on their perceptions, needs and priorities. All data collected will be disaggregated by gender, age and household indicators to identify vulnerabilities for women, elderly, households caring for persons with disabilities, and IDPs. REACH will ensure that enumerators and participants in IM_HUB trainings are trained on gender/age disaggregation and gender sensitive information collection. REACH will also ensure fair targeting of women under its IM_HUB activities by collecting gender disaggregated data of its participants.

3 - Indicators & targets

i) # of multi sector assessments available to the humanitarian and development community (target: 3); ii) % of humanitarian partners that are satisfied with REACH multi sector assessments products (target: 90%); iii) % of REACH Assessments with gender and age disaggregated data (target: 100%); iv) % of respondents who are satisfied with the quality of the survey they have participated in, disaggregated by gender and age (target: 90%); v) # of NGOs and local authorities trained through the IM_HUBs (target: 12, 7 NGOs and 5 local authorities); vii) % of training participants who show improved scores between the pre-training and post-training tests (disaggregated by gender and age) (target: 80%)
