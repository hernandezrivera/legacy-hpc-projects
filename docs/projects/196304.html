Project Summary

The ongoing conflict in Syria has resulted in a critical lack of access to essential services, particularly education, for many families and children. Forced displacement to camps has led to high rates of school dropout and child protection concerns related to exploitative child labor and child marriage.
The proposed project aims to address this issue by providing access to education for out-of-school children and children with disabilities in Syria. The program focuses on non-formal education through the support and empowerment of community members to offer classes in camps and through back-to-school campaigns. Additionally, children with disabilities will be referred to special education services to develop and promote their academic and life skills.
The project targets out-of-school children, particularly those in hard-to-reach areas such as camps, with a focus on primary key stage 2 (ages 9-14) and children with disabilities (ages 6-12). Specific activities include a Back-to-School campaign for 5,000 children ( 2,500 boys- 2,500 girls) in Idleb, non-formal education for 3,000 children ( 1,500 boys- 1,500 girls) in Idleb, establishment of 10 Temporary Learning Spaces in camps for 200 children ( 100 boys- 200 girls), and education for 10 children with disabilities ( 5 boys- 5 girls) at HRS' center in Idleb.
Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) activities and procedures are an essential part of the proposed project to ensure that it is implemented effectively and achieves its intended objectives. The M&E activities and procedures will include the following:
1. Planning and Design: The M&E team will work with project staff to develop a detailed M&E plan that outlines the objectives, indicators, data sources, and timelines for monitoring and evaluating the project. The plan will also include a description of the data collection methods, data analysis techniques, and reporting formats.
2. Data Collection: The M&E team will use a variety of data collection methods, including surveys, interviews, focus group discussions, and observations, to gather data on the project's progress and impact. The data will be collected from project beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders, and will be analyzed to determine the effectiveness of the project.
3. Data Analysis: The M&E team will analyze the data collected to determine the project's progress and impact. This will include comparing the results to the project's objectives and indicators, identifying any gaps or challenges, and making recommendations for improvement.
4. Reporting: The M&E team will produce regular reports on the project's progress and impact, which will be shared with project staff, stakeholders, and the funding agency. The reports will include key findings, recommendations, and action plans.
5. Quality assurance: The M&E team will conduct regular quality assurance activities, such as data verification and validation, to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the data collected.
6. Evaluation: The M&E team will conduct an end-of-project evaluation to assess the overall effectiveness of the project and its impact on the targeted beneficiaries. The evaluation will include a review of the project's objectives, indicators, and outcomes, and will make recommendations for future programming.
Adaptation: The M&E team will continuously monitor and evaluate the project progress, and will make adjustments and adaptations as needed based on the findings of monitoring and evaluation activities.
Overall, the proposed project aligns with the HRS 2023-2027 strategic plan to contribute to making a child safe, well, and educated, through creating pathways for out-of-school children to have access to appropriate and safe learning opportunities and improve the learning outcomes and resilience of children.

3 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria has had a devastating impact on the education of children, with a significant proportion of school-aged children left out of school. This disruption to their education not only puts their future prospects at risk, but also exacerbates the already-fragmented education services across the country. The Covid-19 pandemic, economic crisis, insecurity, and displacement have further compounded the education needs of children in Syria. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, there is an estimated 1,026,533 children in need of education in the Idleb governorate, with 15% of them not attending any form of learning. Children in camps in Idleb continue to face difficulties in accessing and continuing their education due to a lack of schools in these camps or unstable learning environments. This leads to poor education outcomes and exposes them to child protection risks, robbing them of their right to education and a brighter future. To address these pressing needs, the proposed project aims to support these vulnerable groups as per the objectives 2 and 3 of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2023. The project will focus on providing education services to out-of-school children and children with disabilities, particularly in camps, through innovative and inclusive non-formal education after school hours and back-to-school campaigns.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to support out-of-school children and children with disabilities in Idleb with comprehensive activities, including:1.Conduct a Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaign targeting 5,000 children (2,500 boys and 2,500 girls) in the primary key stage 2 (9-14 years old) in Idleb. Additionally, provide non-formal education to 3,000 children (1,500 boys and 1,500 girls) in Idleb, through supporting teachers with incentives and capacity building, as well as supporting schools with running costs and winterization. This will ensure that the 3,000 children who are out of school in Idleb have access to non-formal education as an after-school club.2. Establish 10 Temporary Learning Spaces (TLS) in camps in Idleb, to reach 200 children (100 boys and 100 girls) with non-formal education of ages 6-12 years old. These TLSs will be tents in camps and the project will continue to work with 50 teachers ( 25 male- 25 female)who are working voluntarily in these camps to provide them with incentives and capacity building.3. Reach 10 children with disabilities (5 boys and 5 girls) of ages 6-12 years old in the HRS center in Idleb. After these children are identified through the case management team, provide them with life skills education in the HRS center in Idleb, to ensure that they acquire the necessary academic and essential life skills to enroll in basic PSS and education activities. Provide these children with an inclusive and equitable opportunity for education, among their peers.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk/Threat: Out of school children not being able to enroll in schools.Mitigating Measures: To address this risk, the project will work closely with the case management team of HRS to identify and remove any protection-related barriers that may be preventing children from accessing education. Protection Risk/Threat: Children with disabilities facing barriers to accessing education.Mitigating Measures: To mitigate this risk, the project will conduct assessments of schools using a safety checklist to identify and address any barriers that may be preventing children with disabilities from accessing education. Protection Risk/Threat: Violence, physical punishment, or bullying in schools.Mitigating Measures: To address this risk, the project will provide sessions to children on appropriate behaviors from staff and how to report any misbehavior and implement a safe staffing process. Protection Risk/Threat: Students not being sufficiently protected.Mitigating Measures: To address this risk, the project will implement a safety checklist and establish an e-system for receiving and managing complaints of PSEA. Protection Risk/Threat: Cholera outbreak.Mitigating Measures: To address this risk, the project will conduct awareness and preventive measures sessions on cholera and its symptoms, provide safe water and sanitation facilities, and work closely with local health authorities to identify and respond quickly to any cases of cholera in the area.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Harassment/abuse by HRS or school’s staff: The proposed project takes a proactive approach to addressing any potential incidents of harassment or abuse by HRS or school staff. A well-established safeguarding allegation system is in place and accessible to children, families, and community members. This system includes trained Safeguarding and Gender Officer (SGO) in targeted locations, centers, or schools where the project activities will take place. Complaint boxes are distributed and the system is set up for the schools' managers to follow up on cases and provide proper intervention in the community.Each education activity, whether inside or outside of the schools, is assigned a safeguarding officer from the school or from HRS. Beneficiaries, including caregivers or children, are made aware of how to report incidents through multiple methods such as an online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff, and complaint boxes.HRS has a policy of PSEA which addresses complaints of PSEA as part of child safeguarding complaints (through direct reporting to the child safeguarding focal point). The complaint is then managed as an investigation and allegation following the policy. In addition, HRS shares with beneficiaries brochures issued by the PSEA network which contains the contact of the FP from the network to report any complaints.HRS adopts a survivor-based approach in all stages of the investigation and response in all PSEA issues.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

● Risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium)HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locators.● Security incidents (Medium to High)HRs has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitor the security situation and put in place prevention and response plans. All staff receive safety training and all facilities and schools in the program are screened and provided with first aid and fire extinguishers in addition to a selected safe room and evacuation plan.● Interference or influence by armed groups (Medium)HRs has strong relation with the community leaders and has been successful in the last 5 years in mitigating the risk and using such bodies as a buffer. In addition, HRS has a clear SOP on how to behave in such incidents and clear disengagement criteria in case the risk could not be mitigated. ● Potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium)HRS’s finance team limits the cash on hand to a certain ceiling that minimizes the risk and relies on an established network of vetted Hawala offices. Each office is equipped with safety measures such as security cameras, guards. Etc.
