Project Summary

The Purpose of this project is to conduct explosive ordnance clearance (EOC) by deploying 1 manual clarence team in Terekeka County conduct clearance of Landmines and UXOs and 2 EORE teams on each location of Terekeka and Tambura to endure provision of explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to affected communities, IDPs, Returnees and Refugees. aimed to protect tehm from the dangers of unexploded ordnance (UXO) and landmines, promote safe land use, and facilitate socio-economic recovery.
The purpose is to enhance Safet by reducing the risk of injury or fatality among civilians caused by UXO and landmines, ensuring a safe living environment, Restore Livelihoods through Clearance operations aim to reclaim contaminated land, allowing communities including refugees, IDP’s and returnees to return to agricultural activities, build infrastructure, and safely access resources.
Increase Awareness and Preparedness through EORE and educate communities about the dangers of UXO, thus empowering them with knowledge on how to identify and avoid risks. This aims to foster a culture of safety and preparedness and finally Support Sustainable Development by ensuring safe land use, the intervention contributes to broader recovery efforts, facilitating economic development and community resilience.
The intervention will focus on specific areas known for previous conflict, where UXO and landmines are likely present.
Intervention Components
A. Explosive Ordnance Clearance
• Assessment and Survey: Conduct detailed surveys of contaminated areas using a combination of community reports, historical data from IMSMA, and technical assessments to determine the scope of clearance work.
• Clearance Operations: SLI-SS will Utilize trained Manual Mine Clearance teams equipped with appropriate tools and techniques to safely remove and dispose of ordnance
• Post-Clearance Verification: Conduct verification surveys after clearance to ensure the area is safe for re-entry and use by the community.
B. Explosive Ordnance Risk Education
• Conduct EORE targeting at risk communities, IDPS returnees and Refugees through a direct presentation using a tailed EORE messages
• Conduction EORE in schools targeting school children
• Conduct EORE training of School teachers, to ensure integrated approaches to school curriculum and sustainability of EORE awareness in schools
• Conduct training to community focal persons to ensure continuous EORE awareness and briefing to returnees and new comers to communities informed and aware of the danger pose by Explosive |Ordnance.
• Continuous Engagement: Establish ongoing communication with communities to keep safety at the forefront and update them on clearance efforts and safety protocols.

1. Needs

Decades of conflict, still ongoing, have littered vast swathes of land with explosive hazards and 39.2 million square meters are thought to be contaminated by such hazards. The full extent of contamination is unknown with large areas of the country requiring survey. Approximately 150 previously unknown hazardous areas are discovered each month. In addition to the threats posed to the safety of conflict-affected communities, explosive hazards prevent the safe movement of the population including those who must flee active conflict. Explosive hazards preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid and inhibit socioeconomic development. There are currently an estimated 5.34 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across the country and nearly 3.5 million people displaced, including 1.6 million people who have fled to neighbouring countries and approximately 1.58 million people who remain internally displaced and some are sheltering at UN Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) protection of civilian (PoC) sites across the country. Due to the continued conflict, the contamination from the Explosive remnant of war has increased and affecting the livelihood and security of women, children and that requires a special intervention approach to mine action especially Explosive Ordnance clearance through Manual mine clearance includes safe removal of mines and UXO and provision of Explosive Ordnance Risk Education ensure continuous and sustainable Explosive Ordnance Risk Education for all age and generations. In this case SLI – SS will seek to remove through clearance of Explosive ordnance by deploying one Manual clearance team to Terekeka County together with 2 EORE teams to disseminate Explosive Ordnance Risk Education across Terekeka (Central Equatoria State) and Tambura (Western Equatoria State) to increase the safety of the most vulnerable Returnees, IDP’s and Refugees population

2. Project activities

Activities will include the following: Capacity: a) Recruit a total of 12 Technical staff for manual clearance team includes (6 Female & Male 6) that will form 1 Explosive Ordnance Clearance team (EOC) and 8 EORE technical staff includes 4 male and 4 female that will form 3 EORE teams managed and monitored by an experienced Project Manager. 1 Technical Field Manager and 1 EORE Technical Advisor. The teams will conducta) Conduct Manual Mine Clearance training and accreditation conducted by UNMAS/NMAA for the Explosive ordnance Clearance team and deployed to Terekeka after tasking by UNMASb) Conduct Manual clearance in according to SLI-SS SOP’s and NTSGc) Conduct training for 2 EORE/CL and accredited by UNMAS/NMAA, deploy to the project areas. d) Development and distribution of EORE materials and messages to support EORE delivery Explosive Ordnance Risk Education/Community Liaison (EORE/CL)e) Delivery of EORE sessions to 40,000 vulnerable populations at the communities, returnees and IDP camps and Refugees f) Organise and conduct training for 100 (Men 50, Women 50) Teachers in 40 Schools in order to deliver and integrate EORE in Schools (EORE School based program me)g) Organize and train 200 (Men 100, Women 100) Community focal persons in various locations of the project in order to coordinate dissemination of EORE and any reporting’s of hazards h) Organize Mass media campaign by organizing 10 Radio broadcast in order to target 80,000 indirect beneficiaries. i) Community liaison to gather initial contamination information for non-accessible conflict affected areas j) Collect and reporting of Hazardous items using IMSMA reporting forms (20 HA forms through data gathering by the end of the project. Quality Management System (QMS):k) Conduct internal Quality Assurance Monitoring at least once in six weeks as per National Technical Standard Guidelines (NTSG) and SLI - SS Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Mine Action Reporting:l) Reporting of Daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly EORE/NTS progress reports to UNMAS NMAA, protection cluster and donors
