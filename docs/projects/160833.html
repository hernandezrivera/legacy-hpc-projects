Project Summary

Since the signing of a renewed peace agreement in September 2018, active conflict has been noticeably reduced and an increase in returns has been observed. Humanitarian organizations continue to provide support and assistance as prospects for peace and development improve and confidence is generated for durable solutions, including returns, relocations, or local integrations. Unfortunately, a lack of accommodation, work space and other key facilities often hinders humanitarian operations and a sustained presence in key strategic deep field locations.
In response, IOM will establish humanitarian hubs to enable the expansion of operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. Based on lengthy consultations with field offices and State Inter Cluster Working Groups (ICWG), sixteen potential locations were proposed, of which eight have been selected as proposed locations, namely Jiech, Kajo Keji, Kodok, Leer, Mandeng, New Fangak, Pieri, and Raja.
The eight priority locations would provide field working facilities, accommodation and warehousing to enable agencies to maintain a continuous presence on the ground and international staff to overnight in these locations, as well as allow care of overflow of staff who are unable to find any other accommodation.
IOM will work closely alongside NGOs that will assume responsibility for the running of the hubs. IOM will hand-over the management of the hubs and work on building the capacity of NGOs and local partners to ensure sustainable management of the humanitarian hubs subsequent to the conclusion of the intervention.

1 - Needs

Since the signing of a renewed peace agreement in September 2018, active conflict has been noticeably reduced and an increase in returns has been observed. However, the cumulative effects of years of conflict have translated to sustained poverty and persistent humanitarian and protection needs for more than 7 million people in the country. This is particularly the case in the Equatorias, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Upper Nile and Unity, where drivers and multipliers of crisis have persisted. As of September 2019, new research finds that 1.5 million individuals are internally displaced in South Sudan and more than 6.35 million people are severely food insecure (Source: OCHA South Sudan Situation Report, 11 October 2019). Humanitarian organizations continue to provide support and assistance as prospects for peace and development improve and confidence is generated for durable solutions, including returns, relocations, or local integrations. Unfortunately, a lack of accommodation, workspace and other key facilities often hinder humanitarian operations and a sustained presence in key strategic deep field locations. Consequently, UNOCHA in South Sudan approached IOM to request IOM to take the lead in upgrading and/or establishing additional operational centres/humanitarian hubs. The establishment of additional humanitarian hubs will serve to enable humanitarian and development actors to set-up a permanent presence in these strategic locations with a long-term perspective. Supported by CHF (and later OFDA), IOM assumed responsibility in South Sudan to establish and run three Humanitarian Hubs in Malakal, Bentiu and Bor. Since then the hubs have remained key in facilitating the provision of humanitarian services in PoCs and other interventions in the surrounding communities. The Humanitarian Hubs currently serve 57 unique organizations on a permanent basis in one or more of the three hubs; 34 agencies in Malakal, 33 agencies in Bentiu, and 5 agencies in Bor. In addition, several organizations are served on short‐term visits.As lead under this project, IOM South Sudan aims to ensure that structures are in place to facilitate and support the effective, targeted delivery and monitoring of services to displaced populations. To facilitate and meet this objective, IOM will establish eight additional humanitarian hubs in South Sudan. The requested humanitarian hubs will enable humanitarian agencies to rapidly establish or re-establish presence in areas in which people have been heavily affected by the ongoing conflict and subsequent outcomes, enhancing overall efficiency by providing stable and secure living and working conditions. Establishing the hubs will support the entire humanitarian community, providing common spaces to enable agencies to establish a sustained presence and deliver services to the most vulnerable. Additionally, the hubs will offer cost-effectiveness through reducing transportation and other costs.The proposal to develop humanitarian hubs is aimed at enabling the expansion of operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. Based on lengthy consultations with field offices and State Inter Cluster Working Groups (ICWG), sixteen potential locations were proposed, of which eight have been selected as proposed locations, namely Jiech (Ayod County), Kajo Keji, Kodok (Fashoda), Leer, Mandeng (Nasir), New Fangak, Pieri (Uror), and Raja (Raga). The eight priority locations would provide field working facilities, accommodation and warehousing to enable agencies to maintain a continuous presence on the ground and international staff to overnight in these locations, as well as allow care of overflow of staff who are unable to find any other accommodation.

2 - Activities

As above, IOM will establish humanitarian hubs to enable the expansion of operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. The project will be undertaken in four phases, namely 1) Mobilization and Technical Scoping; 2) Hub Establishment; 3) Operationalization and Management of Humanitarian Hubs; and 4) Handover.1) Mobilization and Technical ScopingSites at the eight locations have already been identified, with a focus on existing humanitarian sites, which will be upgraded and rehabilitated, depending on the site. IOM will assume responsibility for engagement with the local government and partners, as appropriate, as well as the design of the humanitarian hub site itself. Each humanitarian hub will provide sectioned accommodation for women and men, warehousing, communication services, security and office space to humanitarian workers. All humanitarian agencies and workers will have access to full accommodation, generators for power and electricity, latrines and ablutions, water purification, bunkers, fuel storage, a common kitchen, and sufficient office space to maintain communications with colleagues and co-agencies.Each upgraded compound converted into a Humanitarian Hub is envisioned to host and accommodate up to an additional 20 humanitarian workers, and continuous rotation will allow the entire humanitarian community to benefit from the establishment of such hubs.2) Hub EstablishmentIOM will facilitate local procurement, logistics, and construction of the humanitarian hubs. 3) Operationalization and Management of Humanitarian HubsUpon the completion of the humanitarian hubs, IOM will assume responsibility during the initial phase for administration, management, and maintenance of the Humanitarian Hubs. The Hubs will eventually be operated on a cost recovery basis, and all agencies accommodated at the hubs will sign a standardized Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with IOM. However, at the initial stage of operationalization and thereafter, running costs will require support from donors. It is foreseen that IOM will manage each Humanitarian Hub for a period of approximately six months, during which, they will provide ongoing training to those agencies selected to assume management of the Hub. Therefore, the budget as submitted alongside this proposal includes all costs associated with start-up, running, and maintenance of the Hubs for a six-month period.4) HandoverOne Agency per site will be selected as Humanitarian Hub Manager, and will assume responsibility for administering, managing and maintaining the Humanitarian Hub. IOM will launch an open call for interest among humanitarian organizations. All applications will be reviewed by a technical team – encompassing IOM, MSB, and UN OCHA – to determine if the capacity of the applicant is in line with the requirements to administer, manage and maintain the Hub. Agencies will be prioritized based on prior experience/presence in targeted counties; logistical capacity to set up and maintain the Humanitarian Hub; and administrative capacity to manage the Humanitarian Hub, such as resource management and staffing levels.During handover phase, IOM will sign an MOU with the selected Organization, detailing roles and responsibilities during the handover phase, as well as subsequent to the Organization assuming sole responsibility for hub management. IOM will subsequently partner with the respective Organization at each hub location to capacitate on hub management and prepare the eventual handover of the hub management, which will principally take the form of on-the-job training. If required, IOM may request the secondment of standby personnel, including MSB technical staff to support organizations taking final ownership of the hubs during the first three to six months of hand-over, to ensure proper management of the hubs and long-term sustainability herein.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The humanitarian hubs will enable humanitarian agencies to rapidly establish or re-establish presence in areas in which people have been heavily affected by the ongoing conflict and subsequent outcomes, enhancing overall efficiency by providing stable and secure living and working conditions. Establishing the hubs will support the entire humanitarian community, providing common spaces to enable agencies to establish a sustained presence and deliver services to the most vulnerable. They will serve to lessen the scope for communities to leave their habitual place of residence for the purpose of receiving assistance, as humanitarian and development agencies will be able to expand operations across hard-to-reach areas of South Sudan through an increase in static humanitarian presence in key strategic deep field locations. This will also serve to lessen the exposure of women and girls to sexual violence in relation to access to humanitarian assistance, and more particularly as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services.

The Humanitarian Hubs will constitute a cost recovery model of providing field working facilities, accommodation and warehousing to enable agencies to maintain a continuous presence on the ground and international staff to overnight in these locations, as well as allow care of overflow of staff who are unable to find any other accommodation. Ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable community members will be the responsibility of organizations operating from the humanitarian hubs. This notwithstanding, the establishment of the humanitarian hubs will enable these agencies to best reach particularly vulnerable community members - such as individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, and women and girls who may otherwise face risks of sexual violence in accessing humanitarian assistance - through enabling sustained operations on the ground.
