Project Summary

UNHCR strategic intent in 2024 is to ensure the provision of life-saving shelter support through timely, targeted, and appropriate emergency shelter, while in parallel, improving resilience and the protection environment through housing and infrastructure projects at household and community levels.
Needs
In urban, peri-urban, and rural housing contexts, the series of earthquakes in Feb 2023 led to significant deterioration in shelter and housing in affected locations, worsening the already dramatic condition; according to 2024 HNO, of the 21.3 million people in housing/apartments (finished or unfinished), 38% are estimated to live in partially safe or unsafe conditions. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, no insulation from heat and cold, leakage from roof and ceiling are some of the major shelter adequacy issues identified, affecting the ability of households to access safe and dignified shelter and housing. Moreover, HLP issues across Syria are considerable, with 50% of the populations experiencing concerns.
Findings indicate that the quantity of assistance provided continues to be insufficient and they reflect the dire need for hundreds of thousands of housing-related humanitarian interventions in a context where donor funding to the shelter sector continue to decline. The range of shelter needs is diverse, while an increasing number of IDPs and refugees are returning to their original houses/apartments.
Intervention, Implementation of activities
Experience to date in engaging with return communities has demonstrated that an integrated, area-based approach is the most effective to ensure the viability of returns. UNHCR will continue its current response to address the need to voluntarily return IDPs and refugees in their initial reintegration, through shelter rehabilitation and community infrastructure repair, in partnership with other humanitarian and recovery actors as well as with the local authorities. UNHCR is planning to support 22,400 families with the rehabilitation of damaged houses and minor repairs in Lattakia, Hama, Homs, ldleb, Raqqa,Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Daraa, Qunitra and Deir Ezzor, about 10 of them through cash for shelter. Particular attention will be given to persons with specific needs. The rehabilitation program will be sustained by the upgrade of 400 common areas in the damaged buildings, , the repair of 50 structurally damaged units in Aleppo, Hama, Lattakia urban area, and the repair of key necessary infrastructure, in order to ensure access to basic services and a safe environment in areas of return, including solar streetlights debris removal and access to energy.
Emergency shelter preparedness and response capacity will be enhanced with UNHCR taking the lead during emergency response. The main interventions will be tent distribution and replacement in 11 camps of Northeast, and northwest Syria, with an expected target of 30,000 HH assisted between 2024 and 2025: at the same time UNHCR, jointly with Shelter sector, will explore options for a more robust and sustainable shelter type in the camps, Gaziantep operation will support with about 2080 transitional al shelter. As part of our preparedness, a contingency stock of family tents will be held by UNHCR to cover the needs for 2024/2025. Continuous support will be provided to protracted or new internal displacement all over Syria, including the necessary assistance that will be provided for the maintenance of 4 existing collective shelters (if approved by the authorities).

Operational and organizational risks might be: Limited ability to conduct projects monitoring resulting in potential loss of resources, or planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation (price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply), or delay with government clearance of shelter projects. Mitigation measures: strengthen risk-based monitoring tools and establish remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access - support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems - strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms - early release of resources to partners to allow for timely implementation - flexibilities to market changes, such as allowing for increased price or reduced target - advocacy through UNCT, Sectors or UNHCR Rep with government counterparts to expedite the process.
