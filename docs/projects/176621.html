Project Summary

This project is designed to provide health assistance to U5 children and their caregivers affected by conflict in Cabo Delgado, settling in Monapo, Nacaroa and Murrupula districts of Nampula province. The key interventions include:
(i) Train CHW integrated into health committees on community/based maternal, newborn, child health, covid-19 prevention, referral and counter referral, home visits using dialogue based counselling. Support health facility staff to conduct monthly supervision visits to health committees/CHW, (ii) support CHW integrated into health committees to be linked to nearest health facilities and to report their activities on monthly basis, (iii) support CHW to identify U5 children with diarrhea, malaria, ARI and mange as per MoH protocols, (Vi) Support CHW with basic material (Job aid, monitoring tools, and transport) to enable them to carry out their activities at the community level, iv) Support health facilities with covid-19 prevention messages (masks, gloves and hand sanitizer) and to implement monthly mobile brigades/ immunization action plans, (Vi) IEC on appropriate child health practices, recognitions of danger signs in pregnancy, labors, danger sign in newborn and promotion of integrated child health care, hygiene and sanitation practice.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

World Vision Mozambique, a child focusse organization, is working in Nampula province, in the districts of Monapo, Muecate, Nacaroa and Murrupula. In these districts, child-well being integrated intervention are being implemented, focusing on health, education, livelihoods and child protection in collaboration with the local government and communities.The escalation of insurgent activities in Mozambique’s northern province of Cabo Delgado, the humanitarian situation of affected populations is deteriorating. More than 11 of the 16 districts in Cabo Delgado, have witnessed incessant attacks, about 350 since October 2017, with a significant rise in the death toll (ISS 10/01/2020). The number of displaced people has risen from around 1,000 in March 2019 to over 115,000 in March 2020. Over two million people are affected by the crisis overall (ECHO 14/02/2020). Displacement has risen rapidly as violence has escalated, with 211,485 people now estimated to be internally displaced in Cabo Delgado, according to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM)’s data. While violence was previously confined to smaller villages, attacks on district capitals have increased in 2020, leading to larger displacement flows from Cabo Delgado to the Nampula, Zambezia and other provinces. Although there are no exact figures on the total number of displaced people from Cabo Delgado to Nampula province and Zambezia provinces, the INGC points out over 300 thousand people who are currently being welcomed in Nampula and Zambezia provinces. These people take with them children under 5 years. Experiences from other conflicts, children are one of the the most vulnerable groups during conflicts, and are prone to illness and death as a result of lack of access to health care, nutrition and hygiene.• This project is designed with the objective to provide health assistance to U5 children affected by conflict in Cabo Delgado settling down in the districts of Monapo, Nacaroa and Murrupula of Nampula province. The specific objectives are: (ii) empower CHW to provide community-based health services to U5 children and their caregivers and (ii) strengthen health facilities to provide quality and high coverage preventive and care health services to U5 children and their caregivers. The target population is composed of 202,946 people , being 78,157 from Murrupula end 124,789 from Monapo & Nacaroa.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

To ensure that the rights of all project participants are respected, the project will implement the Mozambique guidelines and norms for ethics in project implementation. All community health workers and volunteers will be trained on ethical aspects to be respected during the fieldwork, and confidentiality will be assured during data collection. The participation of every mother and child individual in the project will be strictly voluntary. Before initiating certain activity that will require family participation, CHW will explain the purpose, benefits, and risks of the activity and request informed consent from adult participants.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

The project will ensure that equal opportunities are given to female candidates in staff recruitment process. The project will promote active participation of children of both sex in nutrition screening, home visits to families with malnourished children. The project acknowledges the role of men in decision-making and will ensure that girls and boys are engaged through to influence decisions in a positive way for men and women during IEC promotion. Structures that will be set up to coordinate this project will respect the principles of gender participation and sensitivity. In addressing the primary target – women and children - training activities, staffing and other opportunities for involvement, will be strongly encouraged and supported to ensure equitable gender involvement. In the project area women do not own resources and are unlikely to have input in decision-making. Traditionally they perform the most important tasks relating to the household food supply, are responsible for water provision and food preparation and are fully responsible for agricultural production as well as caring for children. Targeting women therefore is designed to relieve constraints on a particularly disadvantaged group and have maximal impact on livelihoods. Behavior change interventions will proactively address gender by empowering mothers and caregivers to take charge of their destinies whenever possible, by giving those skills, confidence, positive self-belief and other critical attitudes that will continue to support and protect them long after the program has ended. All health facility staff and health volunteers to be trained and supported by the project would have equitable representatives of women and men at all levels.All interventions will be bound by a clear requirement that they address gender issues and especially the direct involvement of girl children and women in their implementation. Women will benefit from the project in terms of improved skills and knowledge, improved nutrition and greater capacity to manage their own resources. Training events of community volunteers will be participatory and will encourage communities to challenge long held views of the roles of women and men, girls and boys. Monitoring of the project will be done from a gender-based approach, as will be the collection of information for program management, which will be disaggregated and analyzed by gender whenever possible.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The project will work to ensure inclusive health, meaning that all children will access health care irrespective of impairment, gender, age, color, race, religion and socioeconomic status. To ensure this, the project will work with health facility staff and community based health care providers (health community volunteers, CHWs) to have positive attitudes towards disability and people with disabilities and have appropriate skills, e.g. communication skills to accommodate the needs of people with different impairments. Activities planned to ensure inclusive health are:•Build the capacity of health facility based and community based health providers (health community volunteers, CHWs) to engage with issues of people with disabilities.•Series of processes to raise awareness about inclusive health and education Communities learn about how abstract human rights can be translated into concrete commitments by their government under national law – i.e. right to health for people with disabilities.•Staff lay groundwork to mobilize communities towards ensuring that these rights are respected

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The environmental impact of this project is not likely to be significant. For the disposal of used medical equipment such as syringes, needles, gloves, masks, the project will provide guidance to health facilities on ways to improve disposal of medical waste as a mitigation measure to reduce environmental hazards.
