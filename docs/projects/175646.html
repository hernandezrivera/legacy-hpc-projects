Project Summary

YEDA will target 26,600 indivuduals with cash and in-kind modalities in Morobo, Yei and Mundri East Counties, and only in-kind in Terekeka, Pibor and Nagero, due to lack of functional markets and access constraints.
The response modality is informed by YEDA’s previous experience, and the need to scale-up CVA as an avenue to recovery&resilience. All interventions will be carried out following Covid-19 preventive measures; social distancing, use of PPE and preventive messaging through IEC. YEDA will maintain static presence in Mundri East, Pibor and Yei Counties, which will also allow delivering assistance to the other targeted areas through mobile response within two weeks from alert.
Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs (30%), high number of dependents, multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, GBV and COVID-19 risks associated with SNFI needs.
The response will improve access to safe and appropriate S/NFIs to 12,000 new displacements/people with new vulnerabilities, improve the living conditions of 4,800 vulnerable individuals in protracted displacement/host populations through ES upgrade and NFI replacement and 2000 through CVA, support vulnerable 5,400 returnees and affected host community with ES upgrade and NFI replacement and 1,800 with CVA, and assist the 600 most vulnerable returnees/host communities with durable solutions through construction of 100 transitional shelters, and increase the ability of people to respond to new shocks through CBIs, including CFW and multipurpose cash vouchers.
For in-kind response, pipeline items will be requested upon approval of SNFI analysis report by the SNFI Cluster coordinators, and delivered in areas without access to local market/shelter material. The CVA will include the creation of a multi-purpose cash fund for procurement of shelter materia/NFIs from the local market, allowing vulnerable families to repair their homes, and ensuring appropriate shelter, protection and dignity. Since in remote areas framing material is not accessible due to protection concerns, hence the project will include a small CFW component targeting 200 individuals for training in shelter construction by the YEDA local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). Risk of conflict over land will be mitigate by identifying land ownership/tenure arrangements and local mechanisms for settling land disputes, involving all relevant local authorities and local stakeholders to handle eventual cases of disputes, and training staff, volunteers and communities in HLP access rights.
Multi-purpose cash vouchers will be distributed in two istallments of $70 and $30, allowing households to purchase SNFIs as per their priority. FSPs will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by YEDA staff. Female heads of household and PWDs will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction.
To measure the project success and take evidence-based decision on the course of action, continuous monitoring and end evaluation will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders as part of a Response, Feedback and Complaint Mechanism, on weekly and monthly basis.
Coordination with the SNFI cluster through the SFPs on by-monthly basis, and with other humanitarian partners utilizing humanitarian hubs in Mundri, Pibor, Yambio and Yei on monthly basis in order to take part to joint responses, will minimize duplication, and promote integrated response, gender and protection mainstreaming, GBV referrals, risk mitigation, and incorporation of HIV/AID and COVID-19 prevention messages. As alternate WES SFP, Greater Mundri SiFP and DFC focal point for UN OCHA, YEDA will lead humanitarian interventions through the cluster methodology, lead humanitarian coordination and refer need/gaps to ICWG.

1 - Needs

According to the HNO 2021, 8.3 million South Sudanese are in need of humanitarian assistance. The IPC analysis 2020 estimated that 5.82 million people are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worsening acute food insecurity. In 2021 the number of returns from PoCs and outside the Country, due to a new situaiton of fragile peace, is expected to increase, and many will face considerable barriers to return as poor conditions in their settlements of origin; inadequate shelter conditions and construction materials, basic household items and absence of functional markets (REACH, Oct 2020). At the same time floods and persistent conflict and insecurity in CES, WES and Jonglei States have caused significant displacement, disrupting harvesting activities, market access, and livestocks (OCHA, Jan 2020). Affected displaced communities will need S/NFIs support to protect them from weather hazards, vector-born disease and ensure privacy and dignity.According to REACH Situation Overview (July 2020), IDPs and returnees in Western Equatoria appear to face insecure shelter solutions, less solid structures being the main shelter solution, especially in Nagero County where 33% of assessed settlements reported improvised shelters of plastic sheeting and scavenged materials serving as the most common shelter for IDPs (REACH, July 2020). As reported by OCHA humanitarian snapshot of January 2021, 9,000 people were displaced by violence in Mundri East County since the beginning of the year. This aggravated the situation of flooding of the previous months, which created displacement and destruction of farmlands and shelters, similarly to Pibor and Terekeka Counties.In Central Equatoria State, Morobo, Yei and Terekeka are some of the counties encountring the most severe acute food insecurity conditions. Chronic vulnerabilities were exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including atypical floods and movement restrictions related to the COVID-19, which have increased prices of food and other commodities (IPC 2020). Displaced populations are hence in need of shelter and basic NFIs, and in most cases do not have access to markets.In the Jonglei Region, which includes the Greater Pibor Administrative Area (GPAA), a combination of floods and and cyclic violence involving the Dinka, Nuer, and Murle communities led to huge displadcement and disruption of livelihoods. Thousands of civilians displaced from their homes by floods and conflicts in parts of the GPAA are currently in need of humanitarian assistance as they begin returning to their place of origin. While food assistance had started being supplied by air drops, shelter is one of the priority needs, as majority of IDPs and returnees are sleeping under the trees (WHO, Dec2020).At-risk groups include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under 5, the elderly and Persons with Special Needs/With Disabilities (PSN/PWDs), due to their higher vulnerability to environmental hazards related to lack of access to essential SNFIs, inability to collect shelter material, and higher vulnerabilities to protection and GBV risks, especially for women and girls. This justifies YEDA’s targeting criteria of most vulnerable households, considering such groups who cannot afford to access shelter materials and cannot afford to pay comunity members to collect it (labor rates are $6/day for unskilled labor and $8 for skilled). Finally, findings from various FGDs revealed that the extent of damage on S/NFI differs, which will guide the cash voucher value. Multi-purpose cash support will be preferred where possible, as part of a dignified approach to allow freedom of choice in the item selection as per household priority (including personal items like clothing) and for repair kits. Such modality increases the speed of implementation, integrated response and reduces the likelihood that surplus kit items will be sold, for a smaller amount than could have been saved and used for the purchase of other much needed items

2 - Project activities

YEDA will use cash and in-kind modalities in order to target the most vulnerable 20,000 individuals in WES, CES and Jonglei States with ES/NFI assistance. For in-kind assistance, activities will include;(1) S/NFI analysis, including protection risk analysis-involving at least 2 S/NFI partners, one GBV partner, and one health partner -and conduction of community consultations (total duration 2-3 weeks) and sensitization on disaster risk reduction. The analysis will also include analysis of the existing land rights systems in place and risk of conflict over HLP issues, through discussion with local authorities, host community leaders, HLP and S/NFI partners. (2) Community consultations and establishment of CFRM (3) Formation and training of Community Representation Committees (CRCs, including women/youth/PWD representatives, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs; 50% female). Training will be on their role and responsibilities during activity implementation, including beneficiary selection, distribution, CFRM, and mediation in cases of disputes, especially over land and property, using the appropriate channels.(4) Beneficiary registration and verification, including CFRM. (5) Request and transportation of ES/NFI from S/NFI cluster core pipeline (6) Local procurement of framing material (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass) wherever possible (7) Secondary transportation and distribution of pipeline items and shelter material** (8) M&E and reporting: Rapid monitoring at the time of distribution, in order to record feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries concerning the distribution modalities and type/quality of items received, and PDM 4 weeks after distribution.***(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster.For the cash modality, activities will include; (1) Community consultation including protection risk and context analysis, establishment of a CFRM and needs/market analysis, including Covid-19 preventive measures* and sensitization on disaster risk reduction.(2) Beneficiary identification (3) Market/Price monitoring – to update the prices due to currency fluctuation (4) Mapping of service providers for Cash-Based Intervention (cash voucher modality) (5) Community sensitization on cash modalities (vouchers, CFW) supported by community selected volunteers on source of funding, intended purpose, transfer value, CFMs(6) Provision of cash assistance (vouchers) through the selected service provider and then YEDA staff**(7) Training in shelter construction(8) Construction of transitional shelters (mixed modality) and provision of cash under the CFW modality (9) M&E; Rapid monitoring after cash distribution, M&E (after 1st installment) by the M&E staff through household visits and FGDs with beneficiaries and later after a month (final installments) a post distribution monitoring conducted to measure impact and effectiveness***(10) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (YEDA M&E data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries). NOTES:* At all stages, the CRC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will all be provided with PPE (washable masks) and IEC materials in local languages will be used for sensitization in all areas. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, including social distancing and provision of PPE for each head of household, as well as hand-washing stations**Each distribution will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction and Covid-19 prevention, as well asl PSEA, in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.***M&E: YEDA may use third-party monitoring/evaluation, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Market functionality will be considered at all stages of the project cycle: preparedness, planning, design and implementation set-up, Distribution cycle and monitoring, exit and feedback.YEDA has put in place a contingency plan in order to be able to respond rapidly with targeted CVA in cases of chrisis, since this kins of response can be implemented quickly on a large scale. Strategic placement of YEDA’s field offices in Yei, Mundri and Pibor, as well as the preparation of guidelines and templates, and staff training on them, has been taken on during the preparedness phase of this project. The YEDA’s preparedness plan and guidelines are informed by its participation to the national Cash Working Group and the SNFI cluster working group, which together with information collected from field partners, allow us to monitor market functionality in different rural areas.At the planning phase, need and gap analysis will be complemented by a market analysis in all locations, to establish the best way of implementing Cash and Voucher Assistance. The response analysis report will inform the course of action by providing information on; beneficiary needs and preferences for assistance delivery, community and political acceptance of CVA, market conditions and operational conditions, including beneficiary access to the market, and supplier’s ability to restock and willingness to take part to the project with full awareness. Design and implementation set-up will be informed by the response analysis report, which inherently consider market functionality, including all actors involved in the market system where the CVA will be implemented, obstacles to access, protection risks and risk of harm to the local economy by introducing CVA, and related mitigation measures. Community consultations, including representatives of all vulnerable groups, are key at this stage to assure the best outcome of this intervention. Also at this stage, contracted partners and suppliers, such as financial service providers, will be required to establish all relevant operational processes and support mechanisms necessary for successful cash or voucher distribution, in order to ensure timely and efficient delivery. YEDA’s prior experience has shown that it is key that vendors and supplier undergo a creful vetting process and sign a MoU to ensure ability and commitment to provide quality items in a timely manner and at an agreed maximum cost. In this view, a risk analysis must also consider factors that might affect the cash transfer, for instance insecurity on the road and inflation which can cause the cost of items to increase or the exchange rate to be unfavourable. At the stage of distribution, rapid monitoring, monitoring of market prices and the established CFRM will allow a smooth distribution of cash or voucher transfers to beneficiaries and their subsequent exchange/utilisation by beneficiaries to purchase commodities and services. Due to this monitoring mechanisms, it will also be possible to make adjustments to the programme design and implementation processes if deemed necessary and appropriate, after consultation with the SNFI cluster coordinators and UN-OCHA.Finally, through the exit strategy developed during programme planning, YEDA will determine if the project will be handed over to local stakeholders, or formally closed. The exit strategy and outcome of the project will be communicated to the beneficiaries and target communities, and will involverestitution and closure of documentation and related contracts. Success stories and lessons learnt will be shared with all stakeholders, donors and cluster coordinators and will inform future programming.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In South Sudan, CVA has been proved to be an appropriate form of assistance, because the following preconditions are met: cash is a familair form of exchange for commodities and services within the community, and it is used to meet SNFI needs in areas with functional markets, and when accompanied by a careful planning informed by context analysis, cash assistance does not increase the level of risk or vulnerability faced by any recipient.For this specific project, CVA is preferred to in-kind assistance in the targeted areas (Morobo, Yei and Mundri East Counties) where markets are functional, there are low risk of conflict and all target beneficiaries have access to the market. The latter includes absence of economic, social, cultural and physical barriers to access. Since in remote areas framing material is not accessible due to protection concerns, hence the project will include a small CFW component targeting 200 individuals for training in shelter construction by the YEDA local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). Additionally, multi-purpose cash vouchers will be distributed in areas where markets are functional and equal access can be granted, in two istallments of $70 and $30, allowing households to purchase SNFIs as per their priority. Financial service providers will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by YEDA staff. Female heads of household and PWDs will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction. In the targeted areas, CVA can revitalize the local market, creating a multiplier effect that benefits the whole community, and can help restore dignity and coping abilities. It can also ensure more flexibility and rapidity in terms of aid delivery, and can often ensure the provision of higher quality items (for instance local shelter material) compared to the SNFI pipeline items.Evidence shows that in many contexts, cash transfers are more cost-efficient to deliver than in-kind aid as they require fewer staff and lower logistical costs. Additionally, multipurpose cash will give the maximum amount of flexibility to meet the beneficiary needs, as well as choice at a low cost – ensuring value for money. This has been proven by YEDA’s experience in implementing Cash-Based Interventions in Mundri East, Mundri West, Yei and Nagero County, through a combination of Cash-for-Work for construction of shelters for vulnerable individuals by community members and cash vouchers for purchase of locally-procured shelter material. All these interventions empowered people by increasing their purchasing power, increasing their resilience to shocks, rehabilitating community assets such as access roads to the market, and providing them with essential skills (e.g. training in shelter construction) to be employable in the future, either by NGOs or within the community. Finally, careful project and market monitoring, also through the Complaint, Feedback and Response Mechanisms embedded in this project, will allow the YEDA team to ensure that the grants will be directed and used by the intended beneficiaries (e.g. selecting women as head of households/direct beneficiaries for cash delivery), that suppliers are able to meet the supply demands and to stock quality items in time.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Addressing inequality and protection threats is key in this project. YEDA has trained and continuously reminds its staff on PSEA, including GBV referral pathways to ensure GBV risks are prevented and/or mitigated, and it is already in contact with the GBV FP for CES for any referrals. Under this project, any new staff, volunteers and local partners will be trained in gender integration and protection mainstreaming to better enable them apply a gender and protection lens (do no harm principle) throughout program activities. This also includes a zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). A criteria for any activity implementation will consider 3 key components; gender appropriateness, safety and accessibility by all groups. Appropriate, safe and accessible sites and schedules for distribution will be agreed upon in consultation with the elderly, PLW and PSN/PWDs, who normally face difficulties in accessing distribution sites due to distance and SGBV risks, and will be supported by community volunteers for shelter construction and transportation of items to their places.The selection criteria for the beneficiaries will prioritize gender and protections concerns to ensure persons with disability, pregnant and lactating women, female heads of households, and the elderly among other groups with protection concerns receive the assistance needed. Beneficiaries, as well as the community as a whole, will be encouraged to report any suspected behavior/acts of SGBV in their community by humanitarian staff/volunteers, using the confidential CFRM that will be put in place, as well as the different channels (GBV/Protection referral pathways, or PSEA taskforce) for remedial. In order to prevent SEA, YEDA will mostly target female-headed households (widows, single mothers), providing items which are desperately needed to reduce the risk of exchanging sexual favors. During the distributions, the beneficiaries will use separate queues by gender, in order to avoid harassment towards women, while the YEDA volunteers will handpick the most vulnerable people and assist them in receiving and transporting their items. YEDA will also work to ensure that the contents of aid are sensitive to any protection risks, monitored through community assessments and PDMs. YEDA is already in contact with the GBV FP and partners in CES, and referral pathways will be used to ensure PSEA risks are prevented and mitigated, and will collaborate with GBV/protection partners (IsraAID) for joint activity implementation. A GBV/SEA risk analysis will be undertaken on water, sanitation, hygiene, and ES/NFI item distributions to make sure that no harm is done and that a minimum of S/NFI and Protection sphere standards are implemented. This will also allow YEDA to take into account all gender perspectives, ensuring protection of all community members and mitigating risks that the project activities might pose to specific age and gender groups. All activities will include community sensitization on GBV and creation of Community Representative Committees (CRCs), with the aim to reduce dangerous behavior and find common strategies to reduce inequalities and protection threats within the community.Finally, YEDA staff will ensure to inform beneficiaries that the assistance is free of any cost, and confidential complaints, feedback and protection mechanisms are in place to report any violation. Sensitization on GBV, in relation to creating a safe environment for women and girls, will be done with protection partners using gender-tailored messages.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community consultations will conducted at inception phase of the project, as integral part of the multi-sector analysis in the targeted project locations. They will not only include the community leaders, but also Focus Group Discussions with representatives of the vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly and disable persons), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed. Also, in each distribution location YEDA will form a Community Representation Committee, including representatives of women/youth/PWD and other vulnerable groups, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs (50% female). The CRCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM), as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict.The CRCs will work with YEDA in order to identification the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria. The composition of the CRCs will ensure that each vulnerable and minority group is taken under consideration. The CRC will also be responsible for ensuring that IDPs, returnees and host communities are aware of the priorities for the selection of beneficiaries for distribution, and how vulnerable households have been verified. They will also assist the YEDA team in the distribution of emergency shelter material and NFIs (including a complaint desk), they will report to the YEDA staff on a regular basis about in case of any feedback and complaint about the intervention modalities (both in-kind and CBIs), in order for the project team to be able to make due changes in the project activities, if need be. Finally, the CRCs will assist the YEDA team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community consultations will be conducted at the inception phase of the project, including FGDs with representatives of vulnerable groups (women, youth, elderly, PWDs), in order to make sure that views, challenges and needs of the vulnerable groups are addressed. Also, in each distribution location YEDA will form a Community Representation Committee, including representatives of vulnerable groups, IDP/returnee and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs. The CRCs provide great insight for activity planning and project design, Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, as well as context-specific information, changes, and early warning in case of conflict. They will work with YEDA in order to identify the most vulnerable households to be targeted by in-kind or cash-based interventions, based on the shared vulnerability criteria, and will be responsible for ensuring that all stakeholders are aware of the priorities for beneficiary selection, and providing feedback to the community about the project activities. They will also assist the YEDA team in the distribution of S/NFI and assist the YEDA team by mediating in case of discontent among the population in regards to the prioritization of the beneficiaries, quality of items, and any other complaint raised and be part of the project monitoring team. For quality assurance and guidelines’ adherence, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be in place, in consultation with the community to allow beneficiaries to provide feedback on project activities including the conduct of staff. This will be supplemented by continuous monitoring, PDM and end line evaluation (third party monitoring) that will be conducted jointly with community members/local leaders to assess satisfaction, timeliness, quality and impact of our intervention. Data collected will inform action during the activity implementation, while lessons learnt will be shared with the different stakeholders including the S/NFI cluster to inform and improve its future work.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The establishment of a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) as an essential component of this project, through the establishment of complaint desks at every distribution center and of the Community Representation Committees will allow to adjust the internventions in the course of action. Such system will see key stakeholder act as a bridge between YEDA and the beneficiaries, in order to inform the organization about the best modalities for aid delivery, as well as issues, concerns and obstacles to the current intervention. For YEDA it is paramount to ensure effectiveness and efficiency in the aid delivery, and ensure that the interventions do no cause any harm to the local economy, social relations and fabric, and beneficiary wellbeing. To this effect, the response modality will be adjusted according to the speicifc context, upon detailed market, need and response analysis that consider the best approach to be adopted in terms of feasibility, sustainability, sphere standards (external relevance) and preferences of the local population (internal relevance), and ensuring that such approach does not have negative ripercussion on safety&security, access to services, and socio-economic enviroment. This includes minimising risk of conflict between IDPs/returnees and host population, by always targeting 10% of host population as direct beneficiaries, as well as balancing stakeholder representation in the the Community representation Committees and involving members of all groups (IDPs, returnees and host populatoin) as volunteers and temporary workers, to increasy project ownership and sustainability.Rapid monitoring will also allow to determine if the beneficiaries perceive the aid receive to be adequate, catering their main needs and ensuring that all communications are done appropriately, for instance using appropriate IEC, language and communication channels to reach a given aim. Such aim could include explaining purpose of response, targetning criteria and NGO capacity in order to manage expectaion, enforce behavioural change for COVID-19 prevention, effective training in shelter construction, sensitization on the use of NFI and shelter material distributed, explanation on the scope of the response and GBV referral pathways among others. Continuous project monitoring through the collabotation of the CRCs will allow us to determine if the modalities used are appropriate or need to be adjusted to that specific context or situation.In case the feedback received through the rapid monitoring and the CFRM show the need of adjusting the project response, YEDA will review its implementation plan for the location/context concerned, and decide on a different course of action upon approval of the SNFI cluster and OCHA. Changes in the project implementation can include, but are not limited to, provision of additional or different supplies (SNFIs or shelter material) compared to the ones additionally planned, changes in the response modalities such as integration of CVA and in-kind, changes in the number of targeted beneficiaries of a specific area, or in changes of locations (e.g. in case of relocation or unforeseen protection risks hindering access). For instance, a previous project planned by YEDA in an IDP collective center in Tombura County, saw the beneficiaries leaving the site abruptly, due to the fact that FSL partners had stopped providing food ratios. Due to this, YEDA had to change the location of the interventions to Nagero, County, where majority of the beneficiaries had relocated, as well as its approach, from building shelters from scratch in the collective center, to reparing the existing ones with local material in Nagero, integrating a Cash-For-Work component.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To minimize the beneficiaries’ risk of being exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services, all activity locations and other key activity details will be agreed upon the members of the Community Representation Committees. For instance, the distribution locations, time and days will have to be favourable for all vulnerable categories to minimize their protection risks on the way. The CRCs include representatives of vulnerable cathegories, which will be able to advise appropriately on the matter, and local authorities that will negotiate access and ensure that no armed actors are present at the distribution site. The long-standing positive relationship of YEDA with the local authorities will also be helpful to this end. Additionally, the risk of of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance will be minimized by training the staff and volunteers in the PSEA principles, informing the beneficiaries of their rights and referral pathways to GBV partners, and by establishing a Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) embedded in the intervention with the support of the CRCs.Furthermore, there is often a risk of interference from local leaders and sometimes authorities in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries. This will be mitigated by leveraging the long-lasting positive relationship that YEDA has maintained with the local authorities during the years, sensitizing the local population on the need to maintain certain prioritizaion criteria for assistance and on the risk of conflict over resources and negative effects on social cohesion, and by creating project ownership by involving beneficiaries and affected communities at every stage of the project implementation (as local staff, volutneers, trainees, community representatives, etc.). Additionally, data triangulation and third party monitoring, coupled with a carefully implemented CFRM, will guarantee to always target the intended beneficiaries of the project. Finally, the risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area will be mitigated by analyzing the context beforehand, identifying land ownership/tenure arrangements and local mechanisms for settling land disputes, as well as previous incidents of conflict and tension over land. All the relevant local authorities and local stakeholders will be involved from the onset to handle eventual cases of disputes (e.g. returnees finding their land and/or shelters occupied) , while staff, volunteers and communities will be trained in HLP access rights. Additionally, in case of shelter material distribution and shelter construction, local authorities, HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, as well as using an integrated response for camp-like settings in order to benefit groups of IDPs, following the HLP due diligence guidelines.
