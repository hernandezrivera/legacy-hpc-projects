Project Summary

This proposal responds to the identified needs from ApS and its partners, that have solid experience in GBV programming (both jointly and separately). We aim at improving the GBV protection response in Hebron and Bethelem areas (Area C) and Gaza. Our intervention will be articulated in 3 main results with specific activities allocated to each one of them:
R1: Improving access to quality multisectoral services for GBV survivors or women at risk. By providing face to face and/or remote, depending on the situation, quality and specialized services to protect, prevent and assist the survivors. Mobile teams will be mobilized to ensure access for the population living in remote areas, or under mobility restrictions. Furthermore, Cash and voucher assistance will be integrated to the GBV service provision, ensuring the teams have strengthened capacities to apply vulnerability assessment, to conduct specific risk assessment on each case and to decide the best modality for every survivor, mitigating any potential risk and maximizing its positive outcomes.
R2: Improved response from specialized service providers: COVID-19 has brough new challenges to assist the beneficiaries for service providers as they have been forced to implement new service delivery methodologies with scarce resources. In many cases the teams didn´t have enough support and guidelines on how to implement the SOPs and to ensure quality standards when assisting the cases remotely and reported high levels of stress and fatigue. ApS will ensure comprehensive capacity building for service providers on remote response (including revision of SOPs for remote service delivery), will promote the implementation of regular selfcare, compassion fatigue and stress release activities in a systematized and culturally adapted methodology and integrate Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SHEA) prevention and mitigation actions.
R3: Prevention, emergency protection and urgent response: Various contextual factors have worsened the emergency protection and the urgent response for many vulnerable cases. ApS will conduct in-depth quality trainings for non-specialized actors in the communities of intervention allowing CBOs, and other community actors to detect a GBV case to make sure the survivor receive specialized assistance and is referred to the right GBV service provider. It is intended to improve the protection capacity for the emergency shelter in Jericho by providing awareness sessions with service providers to enhance their understanding of the benefits of emergency protection and referral procedures to the shelter and to ensure they inform the survivors about this emergency shelter with no bias o stigmatization, covering fees and expenses for those cases referred to the emergency shelter. Furthermore, ApS and WCLAC will enhance the capacities in the protection networks in Hebron and Bethlehem (setting up the network in this last case). Protection networks are a regular coordination mechanism stablished by WCLAC to improve coordination among official service providers such as MoH, MoSD, Family Protection Unit, public prosecution office.
A wide range of tailored SMART indicators are used to measure the impact of the intervention and to identify any required corrective measure.

1 - Needs

The situation of the occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) it is marked by a protracted humanitarian crisis due to an Israeli blockade and overlapping crisis. A total of 53% of Palestinians in Gaza are living below poverty line, while in the West Bank is 14% . These pressures have been linked to increased incidence of gender-based violence (GBV), school drop-outs and early marriage, while shelters and other service providers struggle to meet the needs with increasingly limited resources. While all Palestinians are vulnerable on account of the occupation and emerging crises, some appear to be perpetually more vulnerable, and systematically at a greater disadvantage. According to the 2021 HNO these groups are: people living below poverty line; refugees; children under five in Gaza; female-headed households; small-scale farmers, herders and fisherfolk in Gaza; pregnant and lactating women in Gaza; bedouin and herders in Area C; women with disabilities; women survivors of GBV and internally-displaced women. The deterioration of the living conditions and its impact on gender and family dynamics, has exacerbated GBV in all its forms. According to the 2019 PCBS survey 29% of Palestinian women experienced some form of violence by their husband ,in Gaza this number increased at 49%. Numbers provided by the GBV Sub-Cluster (SC) in 2019 reveal yet another layer of SGBV and the majority of cases referred were of psychological (39%) and physical abuse (34%), with cases of forced marriage (6%) and sexual assault (5%) . The findings also reveal that the perpetrators of SGBV are usually a partner or a family member. GBV has been reported as a key protection concern in the OPT in HNO (2018), which encompasses the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and the Gaza Strip . In oPt as worldwide COVID-19 has had a disproportionate impact on the most vulnerable groups identified above. Marginalized women in the oPt, including those below poverty line, have become more vulnerable to the impact of multiple, compounded emergencies in part due to their limited access to resources. CVS has potential (integrated with other services) to respond to protection concerns, especially those linked to economic hardship and GBV risks and can contribute to the resilience of vulnerable women. Yet, this is a resource that is not enough integrated in the multisectoral services. A survey conducted in April 2020 revealed, that 47% households reported an increase in violence incidents during the house isolation in WB and East Jerusalem being psychological and economic violence the most reported forms (62% and 70%). As the crisis continues, there is a need to scale up emergency GBV services, build capacity in remote delivery, and support frontline teams with stress management and psychological support. ApS gendered analysis of critical problems relating to protection through a multisectoral lens to assess the impact of the ongoing crisis on vulnerable groups is presented below:• Limited availability and access to multi-sectoral services (i.e. GBV case management, health, legal aid and psychosocial support)• Higher occurrence of home violence and sexual abuse in communities due to the by the outbreak of C-19 and related movement restrictions, school closures, job losses and socio-economic distress.• The already limited freedom of movement further reduced by the pandemic: only a minimal number of medical emergency cases are permitted exit via Israel and internal movement and business operations within Gaza curtailed by a lockdown imposed since late August.• Paralysis of the judicial system and reduced access to primary health care centres and shelters.• Service providers exposed to major psychological pressure and stress, • Concerns on the safety and the security of GBV data collection.• Gaps in the protection and referrals for children facing violence within families or communities. Within the current situation, adolescent boys and girls are especially vulnerable to school dropout and significant protection concerns, including physical and emotional violence, sexual abuse, arrest and detention, child labour and forced marriage• Frequent electricity cuts in Gaza have increased women’s vulnerability. Power outages have also threatened the life and well-being of vulnerable groups, particularly those needing urgent medical care.• Limited access to drinking water, domestic use water and wastewater and solid waste management have significant impact on household spending, health and hygiene.• New urging economic problems rise in terms of inability to provide the essential needs of the homes. As the daily-paid breadwinners cannot leave the quarantine and go to their works.• Violence increased more in households with vulnerable individuals like children, people with disability or people with mental illness. These persons need special care with food, medicine and assistive devices. • Lack of equipment and need for capacity building for service providers to provide safety remote services.• Economic violence reported as the most widespread, including exploitation and blackmailing

2 - Activities

R1: improved access to quality multisectoral services for GBV survivors and people at risk.- (WB,Gaza): Specialized GBV services in Safe Spaces and One Stop Center: Specialist and trained teams will provide services for GBV survivors and people at risk, including MHPSS and legal services, case management, referral and distribution of dignity kits. All ApS partners are active members of the GBV subcluster and ensure the application of SOPs and their teams are trained and deliver service according to international standards. - (WB,Gaza): Mobile teams to reach people in quarantine and remote communities: Sexual and Reproductive Health services are still a valid entry point to reach the communities and detect GBV cases. In coordination with Health Cluster members, mobile teams (including psychologist, social worker, nurse and doctor) will conduct campaigns and provision of services to the remote areas and/or people in quarantine. This activity will be adapted to the specificities of the geographical area and respect all COVID-related restrictions at every moment. - (WB,Gaza): Cash and Voucher assistance (CVA) to increase women´s resilience: ApS has mobilized funds to conduct a research to identify lessons learnt and recommendations for improving the effectiveness of CVA in building women and girls’ resilience in GBV service delivery. Specifically, we expect to obtain evidences on the most appropriate modalities of interventions for the specific context of the areas of intervention and the profile of the rights-holders in the current COVID-19 scenario. Based on the findings from this research, ApS will promote CVA as a tool to build women’s resilience towards GBV through meeting their basic needs and targeted protection services. ApS will ensure capacity building for service providers to ensure the no harm principle and that the selected modality responds to the situation of each case. R2: Improved response from specialized service providers. (WB,Gaza) Capacity building of service providers for remote response (including revision of SOPs for remote service delivery): ApS will support specialized GBV service providers to revise the current SOPs and adapt their internal guidelines to remote counselling and case management. Internal procedures will be discussed and endorsed by the GBV Subcluster and shared with all members of the GBVSC. ApS will ensure that the GBV teams receive regular coaching and support in the application of the revised SOPs and the safety and security Guidelines for remote counselling. The coaching program will include participatory methodologies and a series of webinars and joint sessions where the staff can exchange and share good practices and lessons learnt. Furthermore, individual coaching to support the staff in the daily challenges they may face to implement the SOPs.(WB,Gaza) Implementation of regular selfcare, compassion fatigue and stress release activities in a systematized and culturally adapted methodology: GBV specialized teams will engage in regular and systematic individual stress release programs conducted by specialized external staff and include at least the following: i) individual remote sessions through telephone or zoom. The program includes at least 4 sessions of 1h per year. ii) group self-care sessions and training the teams in the practice of of self-care. (WB,Gaza) Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SHEA) prevention and mitigation: ApS, is applying the SHEA policy that i) aims at creating a safe working culture, ii) taking an intersectional feminist approach. Iii) is survivor-centred, respecting their power and dignity through confidentiality and right to make decisions. SHEA prevention and safeguarding is embedded in every part of our work. ApS will ensure the following actions:A.All contracts, MoU and agreements with partners, staff, volunteers or external consultants, will include a clause on the compliance of SHEA and safeguarding policies.B.All staff and partners will receive a training session on the application of the SHEA prevention approach. C.ApS will directly engage in the PSEA Network. R3: Prevention, emergency protection and urgent response- (WB,Gaza) Training on GBV detection and referral for non-specialized actors: ApS will conduct in-depth quality trainings for non-specialized actors in the communities of intervention allowing CBOs, and other community actors to detect a GBV case to make sure the survivor receive specialized assistance and is referred to the right GBV actor. This process has been ongoing for the last couple of years with the cooperation of UNFPA and other GBVSC members. In 2021 the trainings will be built upon the conclusions withdrawn from an evaluation that is already being coordinated with UNFPA to assess the impact of the training and identify the challenges faced by people trained in 2019 and 2020. - (WB Only) Improving protection response for the emergency shelter in Jericho: ApS, in cooperation with WCLAC will:A.Provide awareness sessions with service providers to enhance understanding of the benefits of emergency protection, referral procedures to the shelter, the services and activities the shelter provides, and how to integrate this resource in the recovery process of survivors. Ensure the professionals inform the survivors about this emergency shelter with no bias o stigmatization when preparing the emergency plans and case management. B.Information sessions and material dissemination (with all respect to safety issues) in women safe spaces and women CBOs. C.Covering fees and expenses for those cases referred to the emergency shelter. D.Equipment, furniture, and material support for the shelter. (WB only): Increasing the capacities for the protection networks in Hebron, Jericho and setting up Bethlehem (this is a regular coordination mechanism stablished by WCLAC including official service providers such as MoH, MoSD, Family Protection Unit, public prosecution).

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

There are different ways in the project to collect feedback from the affected population. There is a complaint box in the premises where multisectoral services are provided. The boxes are easily accessible to the users and anonymous. Each implementing partner has its own system for complaints management and feedback, but all of them are survivor centered and protect the rights of affected people. On the other hand, satisfaction questionnaires are distributed in the sessions and for people receiving services. The surveys are standardized with a set of closed questions that allow assess the level of satisfaction in different dimensions, at the same time that include some open questions to open the space for recommendations. The information collected through these tools is systematized by the programme coordinator and shared with ApS with the internal monitoring reports. The received feedback will be used for: i) programmatic purposes. Identifying any needed corrective measure to improve access, quality or impact. Ii) identification of emergency or non-identified needs. For planning future interventions or for advocacy purposes. ApS will directly engage in the PSEA network, participating as requested and engaging in the capacity program to build a robust complaint mechanism to allow the affected groups to safely report any misconduct or abuse at the same time that to allow the partner organizations to investigate and take immediate action if needed.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

APS has a strong track record in gender-sensitive capacity development interventions and the intervention has been designed, planned and implemented in a gender-sensitive manner, avoiding further damage and suffering as a result of our actions. Our staff recognize the potential negative effects of interventions; taking into consideration not only the target group but also the wider environment; taking a step back from and even questioning interventions;The staff of APS always make sure and is aware:• of the power-relations and conflict dynamics in the contexts in which projects are implemented, • are not creating conflict any harm to the local population and target group,• that conflict analysis conducted and updated regularly,• that we plan, implement, monitor and evaluate our intervention in a no harm-sensitive fashion (including reformulation where necessary) Community participation and self-assessment of the needs of local community is always a part of the intervention, since quality relationships with beneficiaries is integral to do no harm principle. Therefore, APS consults and involves communities from the identification phase of a project. APS project teams have a wide range of participatory tools at their disposal, which are available in several documents. They allow for meaningful consultation with communities at the project identification stage. Methods we use include: • Needs assessment;• Desk research; • Key informant interviews; • Surveys; • Expert interviews; • Community consultations/focus groups; and • Workshops with staff, partners and other relevant actors. Using these methods improves accountability towards beneficiaries, relations with beneficiaries, understanding the context and community dynamics in which projects take place, and acceptance in an intervention area. ApS is willing to integrate the Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) in GBV programming as a resource for service providers to support women exiting the cycle of violence and meet their basic protection needs. ApS will start by conducting an in-depth assessment to determine the contextual and personal requirements and potential challenges for each modality, as well as identify the needs in capacities for the GBV staff to apply CVA in their case management. During the implementation, several practices are implemented as part of project management, referring directly to the “Do no harm” principle:• Measures to mitigate pre-identified negative effects, • Implementation of a complaints management system, • Communication and information shared with beneficiaries, • Implementation of a mechanism to manage and protect data, • Disaggregated data collection according to the most vulnerable categories. In terms of COVID-related prevention and no harm: - ApS is always ensuring that facilities and office arrangements are safe for the target group and staff, and adhere to all minimum precautionary safety measures, including physical distancing and hygiene promotion, as defined by health authorities. All ApS staff has received comprehensive information on prevention and mitigation and are fully updated and compliant with the imposed restrictions and requirements. - ApS Head of Mission is the designated member to follow up and monitor the implementation of hygiene and safety measures within the organization, to establish clear policies and procedures for what must be done when COVID-19 symptoms are suspected or there is a potential exposure to a possitive case, including immediate reporting, monitoring, isolating staff and activate home-working when necessary. - All activities are subjected to modification to adapt to COVID-19 restrictions at any time. Remote services to survivors will be activated as soon as face to face is not advisable, and regular need assessments will be conducted to identify any new emerging need.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The observance of humanitarian principles is a matter of ApS professional duty. Considering the definition of impartiality, APS staff ascertained that aid provided will be allocated in proportion to need and without discrimination. ApS chooses the area of intervention solely on the basis of its own assessment of humanitarian needs, and no distinction is made among the victims on any criteria other than their vulnerability. To ensure that we target those most in need, we continuously build and deepen cooperation with our partners and other local organizations, who share our values. Their contribution is fed into the need assessment done before the project is conceptualized. The needs analysis consists of:• the analysis of the actors, their positions, challenges and power relations,• the understanding of local culture and power relations in communities (including all stakeholders, and considering discriminated groups, especially women),• the training of the institutional capacity of local actors, including their understanding of humanitarian principles and standards. Our staff always makes sure that representatives of all social, ethnic and religious groups in the area of intervention participate in needs assessments. Furthermore, in order to avoid any possible misunderstandings, APS explains to the population that humanitarian aid has to be provided to the most vulnerable as a priority. Here the non-discrimination principle, which implies equal distribution of aid among beneficiaries with similar needs, complements the principle of the equity, so that an assistance has to be given in priority to those who need it most. If the humanitarian needs of one community are clearly more important than those of another, ApS may still consider rolling out activities for each of them to prevent social fragmentation, conflict and reprisals. Another measure to comply with the principle of equality and impartiality involve the APS attempt to build the response on local capacities. We always strive to involve programme beneficiaries in the management of aid delivery and reflect the composition of local society. Ensuring that staff composition reflects the diversity of the operating environment help us to reinforce the local perception of ApS as principled, transparent and accountable. A gender equality perspective will be included to ensure the program is designed and implemented with a “do no harm” approach that it does not set off conflict, exacerbate tensions between populations or subject individuals to danger and/or abuse including GBV.The program has been designed upon the joint experience of ApS and its partners, with a strong knowledge of the sector, the communities and the needs of the people we are assisting. Gender is mainstreamed in all actions and all activities are planned to ensure meaningful access of most vulnerable groups. GBV services, awareness, self-care sessions, and many other actions are conducted taking into consideration the unpaid and care responsibilities of women that are socially assigned, to ensure they can take part in them. Furthermore, special considerations are taken to mitigate any potential reputational or stigmatization risk, that may prevent women to engage in the activities. ApS and its partners are all committed to enhance the participation of people with disabilities, either physical or other type of impairments. When preparing for each activity, the assessment will be conducted to ensure that any corrective measure can be done in time to promote their participation.
