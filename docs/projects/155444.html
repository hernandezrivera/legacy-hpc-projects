Project Summary

Past experience has shown that during emergencies such as droughts, child protection violations become rampant and these are often not prioritized as most interventions tend to focus on provision of material, ‘tangible’ assistance. In 2017 alone through WEI/B support, a total 3,808 (2,500 females and 1,408 males) children were reached with critical protection services whilst 3,808 (2,116 females and 1,692 female) children were reached with welfare services in 10 Emergency districts. Noted cases on the rise included child sexual abuse, child marriages (where parents would marry off their children in exchange for food), child sexual exploitation, neglect and child labour. It is therefore imperative that the National Child Protection System is strengthened during emergencies for it to effectively prevent and respond to child protection violations during emergencies. Against this background, WEI/B hereby proposes a number of activities to ensure the child protection system in the Zimbabwe and indeed in the target districts is well capacitated. WEI/B therefore proposes to strengthen the capacity of all the DSW officers so they can effectively manage child protection cases as they are identified especially in the context of the emergency.WEI/B will also place graduate social worker interns in each of the target districts to support with documentation and case follow ups. Additionally, WEI/B will also support districts with fuel for case follow-ups and child protection surveillance at public places of entertainment and other child rights violations hotspots. Regular technical support and onsite mentorship and troubleshooting will also be conducted during the implementation period.A key component of the NCMS is the community structure, where CCWs are responsible for the identification of child protection violations and routing them to the district offices. In light of this, WEI/B proposes to conduct refresher trainings for LCCWs and selected CCWs on case management as well as CPiE, including the referral pathways and how these cadres will link with other community cadres and structures such as VHWs in order to increase case-findings at various entry points. The training of community CWs will also incorporate a component on data management and real time reporting of child protection concerns at community level. uring the 2017 response, it was noted that coordination of key stakeholders was key to the success of the project. It is in this context that WEI/B proposes to deliberately support bi-monthly coordination meetings at district level, and ensure that the DCPCs are functional in providing concrete solutions and services for children. WEI/B will review the standard DCPC agenda to ensure all issues are captured and actioned upon. In addition, WEI/B will support the districts to develop and track district action plans. This will help in enhancing accountability in provision of services to affected children by the key stakeholders in the districts. Support children’s access to protection services through the transport voucher. Lessons from the previous interventions revealed that the transport voucher works as an effective way of supporting children to access critical protection, health care, PSS, and justice among others. WEI/B therefore proposes to expand the transport voucher system to the other non-CPF ditricts, which are being targeted for the response. Observations from the main CPF programming has shown that other child protection players such as the police, health professionals and other service providers help to create demand for the transport voucher and child protection services when they are knowledgeable on available services. Training of CCWs and transport operators on the transport voucher system will also result in improved access to protection services.
